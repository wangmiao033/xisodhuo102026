# Chapter 10: Her Signature

Lin Mo’s hand stopped above the console.

Wang Hai held his sleeve without pulling, careful of the injured wrist. “Ask first.”

Lin Mo drew back. “Memory synchronization. With whom?”

“The associated person in this segment.”

“You’ll give me her memories?”

“The sample selected by the audience will be applied.”

“She’s in the sample. Who gets changed?”

“The performer.”

Wang Hai’s grip tightened.

“If I leave now?” Lin Mo pointed at Exit.

“This adjustment will not be applied unless you enter the next sample.”

“And afterward, I can leave?”

“After completing a sample, you may leave or continue the retrospective.”

“Can I agree to playback without agreeing to synchronization?”

“This round’s adjustment is determined by audience vote.”

Exit glowed inches from his fingers. Wang Hai shifted his sleeve aside so he could reach it.

“We could look somewhere else.”

“The agreement she signed is here.”

“I know. Anything to do with her, and you’ll try it.”

In the preview, his sister had laid the pen across the paper, a finger holding it still. She seemed to be waiting for an answer.

“She was asking questions, too,” Lin Mo said.

Wang Hai followed his gaze and slowly let the sleeve fall back.

Lin Mo felt for the wood in his pocket. The screw pointed outward; a crack ran through the carved name. He turned it until he found the groove beneath Mo.

“Can we just see Clause Seven?” Wang Hai asked.

“Agreement samples cannot be divided.”

“Then show me. I’ll take his place.”

“This agreement’s associated person is Lin Mo.”

“I’m not trading you,” Lin Mo said. “Listen for me. Find out what she did. If I get it wrong, stop me.”

His throat still hurt. He couldn’t force the words back to their old pitch.

“You missed some of it last time.”

“I won’t argue while she’s talking.”

“You still have to argue when it matters.”

Lin Mo nodded and put his water beneath the chair. Sun Ning turned toward the screen, her uninjured left hand beside her pen.

“I’ll listen to her. You two take the clauses. And back out when you’re done. Don’t turn around. Your legs gave way last time. We’ll be here.”

Lin Mo pulled his jacket zipper up, caught his wrist against the stiff padding, and stopped. Wang Hai tucked the cloth back into his sleeve.

“Don’t lose that. Wood hurts worse.”

“Your left hand?”

“Wrapped. Not using it for now.”

He showed the bandage without removing it, then pointed at the timer.

Four minutes twenty seconds. Lin Mo pressed Continue, waited for Wang Hai to release his sleeve, and returned to the white circle.

> Memory synchronization. Approval of 60% required for implementation.

Voting began. A masked spectator flipped a paddle and pointed at Lin Mo. The person beside him laughed, raised a hand, and sent the count higher. Lin Mo steadied his feet and ignored them.

Onscreen, his sister slid the paper onto the bedside cabinet, weighting a corner with her cup. The pen cap rested between two fingers.

“Everything listed here?”

“Yes.”

She pulled the sheet back, nearly upsetting the cup, caught it, and read slowly.

> All Existence value at my disposal, transferred in full.

The scratching of pens paused in the front row, then resumed.

“What would I have left?”

“Zero.”

“There isn’t much money in my account. He paid my hospital deposit.”

“Existence value is not measured in money.”

At the unseen speaker’s request, she moved the page toward the camera. Beneath her cup lay the scope of payment: her name, her registered connections in reality, the identity allowance available for her own use.

“Even my place here in hospital?”

“Included.”

She wiped water off the sheet and pointed to her name. Could she keep that?

“Full payment does not exclude this item.”

Her nail caught the edge, leaving a small crescent in the paper. She set down the cap.

“You said I could buy him protection.”

“The proceeds purchase Beginner protection eligibility for your designated person.”

“Does he get it before you deduct mine?”

She spoke quickly, swallowing a cough. Directed to Clause Four, she followed the print with her finger.

“Transfer will occur after protection eligibility is established. Protection from what?”

“During the beginner stage, the guaranteed individual’s Existence value cannot be reduced to zero, nor can his identity be permanently canceled.”

“How long?”

“Until he leaves the beginner performance area.”

“What if he doesn’t know? Takes a wrong turn?”

“He will be notified before eligibility ends.”

Only then did she release the corner and smooth it with the back of her hand.

Lin Mo touched his neck. As she shifted the page, another passage came into view: physical fitting and performance penalties were not exempted.

Her finger stopped there. “What’s this?”

“It will not prevent his continued participation.”

“I didn’t ask whether he could perform.”

She bent forward coughing. When she finally straightened, her eyes were red.

“What if I don’t sign?”

“The designated person will undergo standard admission.”

She held the paper without moving. A crease across the screen cut through her hand. Lin Mo followed it to the pen tip, trying to see whether it had touched down.

> watching this again. her bit holds up

> he’s only finding out now?

> someone skipped Clause Seven last time

A spectator read the last message aloud. A piece of chin showed beneath his mask. The person beside him covered his voting paddle, as though warning him against saying more.

The comment slipped away before Lin Mo could turn. He kept quiet and watched his sister turn the page.

“Clause Seven. Cancellation.” She flattened the sheet. “You delete my account?”

“After all available Existence value is transferred, the original identity account is canceled and its associated records in reality revoked.”

“What about my brother?”

“The guaranteed individual’s memories are excluded from this payment. Other connections are processed according to the transfer amount.”

She looked toward the camera.

“He’ll still remember me?”

“Yes.”

She picked up the pen. Her uncertain hand gripped it firmly now.

Lin Mo’s left foot shifted forward. The circle flashed red. He stopped, toes curling inside his sock.

Records in reality revoked. He remembered the nurse asking whether he had the right floor, the old man in bed six beckoning to his family.

“Can I reverse it later?” his sister asked.

“Completed transfers are irreversible.”

“Oh,” she said softly.

“Fifty seconds,” Wang Hai called. “Don’t move forward.”

She reread the first line, pressing her finger against the protected person’s name. She said “Lin Mo,” made the unseen speaker repeat it, then pushed the sheet into position to write.

Wang Hai’s pen stopped. He didn’t repeat the name. He held his right hand toward the circle, palm down.

Lin Mo looked at his feet. His heels had lifted. He lowered them as voting closed at 61%.

Memory synchronization began. His sister’s face was unchanged onscreen. The comment about making him someone she would recognize was gone.

The white light flickered. He was sitting on the hospital bed.

His sister waited by the door, the gray jacket over her arm. He had just paid. The hospital said she couldn’t stay. A voice on his phone said they could send her home.

He picked up the pen and signed: Lin Mo.

His right wrist didn’t hurt. The handwriting came easily. His sister walked over, patted his shoulder, told him not to worry.

His hand rose, fingers curving around a pen that wasn’t there.

“Lin Mo!”

Wang Hai held up his booklet outside the circle.

“The paper’s with her! There’s no pen in your hand!”

Lin Mo opened his fingers. The old scar remained at their base. The red mark across his wrist pulled painfully. He’d been about to say, I signed it. Only a breath escaped.

“She signed,” Sun Ning said.

The pen was still moving onscreen.

He pressed his hand against the gray sleeve. The patch had a stiff edge. The knot joining cream thread to blue caught beneath his nail.

His sister finished the last stroke and offered the pen.

“Done. Is that all right?”

Inside his head, his right hand was still writing. Onscreen, she had let go and pushed the agreement toward the camera. Beside her signature ran the same source account number.

> Payment confirmed.
> Beginner protection eligibility bound to: Lin Mo.

She held the sheet and asked whether they could keep him from seeing this part.

No answer.

“He’ll keep asking. Just let him know he can survive.”

Lin Mo opened his mouth. Nothing came.

The balance at the bottom right changed to 0. She reached for her cup. Her hand faded, and the handle showed through her fingers.

She tightened her grip, loosened it, and touched her fingertips with her other hand.

“Wait.”

Black.

“Four twenty!” Wang Hai shouted.

The circle turned green. Lin Mo stayed where he was until Sun Ning stood, pointing at the floor and calling him back. He backed into Wang Hai’s outstretched hand. His own right hand was still curled.

He straightened it and reached into his pocket for the blister pack his sister had hidden there.

“What did she say at the end?”

“Wait,” Sun Ning said.

“Before that.”

“Don’t show you. You’d keep asking.”

He sat, holding the chair back. Wang Hai brought his water without replacing the cap.

Lin Mo took a mouthful and couldn’t swallow. Wang Hai crouched beside him, waiting.

“She signed it,” Lin Mo finally said.

“I saw.”

He set the bottle between his knees, left hand pressed against the blister pack. In Room 09 he had asked, What did you hide it here for?

Sun Ning sat with her pen cap holding her booklet open. She said nothing. Wang Hai steadied the bottle before his grip could tip it over again.

“I wrote down zero. Her zero.”

Lin Mo turned to him.

“Your protection’s still there,” Wang Hai said. “Yours hasn’t reached zero.”

Lin Mo pulled up his sleeve. The red mark hadn’t faded. A corner of the padding stuck out. He tried several times to tuck it back with his left hand.

“I thought they were keeping me alive so people could watch.”

Wang Hai gathered the cloth without answering. Lin Mo rested his hand on his knee. Eventually he took out the cracked piece of wood.

Part of the carved name was missing. A torn thread clung beside the screw. He pulled it free, and Wang Hai caught it in an empty wrapper. Lin Mo asked for the pen.

Wang Hai handed it over. “Doesn’t need rewriting. I can read it.”

Lin Mo returned it and held the wood in his left hand, slowly passing his thumb across the carved characters.

“Her name’s gone.”

Wang Hai opened his mouth, but nothing came.

“Does canceling an account mean the person’s dead?” Lin Mo asked.

“Cancellation of an original identity does not establish whether the individual is alive or dead.”

“What can she leave behind?”

“Retained materials may be used in subsequent performances.”

“Where do I find them?”

“Search using the current performance number.”

He stood, knocking the chair back with his knees.

> Cognitive overwrite: 30%
> Physical modification: 19%

Beginner protection still glowed above his name. He gripped the chair back and stared at it.

Sun Ning pointed out the lit exit. Wang Hai stood beside him, his booklet against his chest.

“Going to look for her now?”

“Yes.”

“Don’t follow that road in your head again.”

The spectators remained in the back row, blank masks facing the screen. One turned his paddle over to cover his face.

Lin Mo walked between the seats and stopped before him. Wang Hai called out. He went no closer.

“You read that comment. Do you know what they skipped?”

The man didn’t move.

Lin Mo turned out the patched cuff and held it beside the paddle.

“Where’s her next performance?”
