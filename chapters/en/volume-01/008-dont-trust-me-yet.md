# Chapter 8: Don't Trust Me Yet

Lin Mo held out his ID. “You're remembering right.”

The photograph and sex field were unchanged. Wang Hai supported the edge with his thumb, avoiding the photograph.

“Doesn't match the booklet.”

“They printed the booklet here.”

“Filled in the job too?”

“I collected clothes. Didn't apply for a job.”

Wang Hai glanced at the blue skirt. “Expensive work uniform.”

Lin Mo pocketed the card. Ahead, two signs marked a junction: “Rest Area” left, “Staff Passage” right.

He felt he had been down the right-hand passage before. A green door opened into a laundry, with stairs beyond. The light at the bottom was broken; going down, you had to feel along the right wall.

“Which way?” Wang Hai asked.

Lin Mo raised his hand toward the right, then felt the cloth inside his cuff. Seven had just given him that. Before then, he'd only been in the dressing room, Mirror Corridor, and Room 09.

“Wait.”

Below the sessions in his booklet, he added: Green door. Laundry. Stairs.

Wang Hai looked over his shoulder. “You've been here?”

“Feels like it.”

“Have you or haven't you?”

“I don't know.” He touched the entry with his pen cap. “If I suddenly say I know a route, don't trust me yet.”

“Sign says rest area's left.”

“Then left.”

“Voice always sounded like that?”

“They haven't changed it. Not that I've noticed.”

“Face just happened?”

“In the first room.”

Wang Hai stopped comparing him with the photograph. “Still want me to remember Lin Mo?”

“Still Lin Mo.”

“I'll cover the rest.”

He folded the last page inward, hiding sex and occupation but leaving the name visible. When it wouldn't stay folded, he tucked the corner into his envelope, securing it with the remaining clear seal. Lin Mo didn't tell him to remove it.

Shoes scraped briefly in the staff passage. Wang Hai turned.

“Heard that before too.”

Lin Mo added: Voice unchanged. Wang Hai confirmed in person.

The rest area held plastic tables and chairs, a wall clock, and a lit vending machine. No mirrors. Nobody else. Lin Mo leaned the chair leg against a table and read the notice.

> Break between sessions: Maximum ten minutes.
> Claim food and water with this session's settlement record.
> Follow onscreen directions when the break ends.

Wang Hai sat, loosened his belt one notch, and exhaled.

“Does resting count as a session?”

“Doesn't say.”

“What if we haven't finished eating after ten minutes?”

“Take it. Don't stay here to finish.”

“They might charge for takeaway.”

“Find that rule, let me know.”

Lin Mo put his settlement page on the vending machine's reader. A water bottle and packaged bread dropped into the slot. Wang Hai tried his and received the same. He checked both packages' dates.

“Same batch.”

“Expired?”

“No. Can still read that much. They're not charging the audience, are they?”

“Doesn't say.”

“Eat now, make me cough it up later?”

Lin Mo checked the seal and handed it back. “Keep the packaging.”

“Proof, if they do.”

At the table, Wang Hai held his bottle between his knees and unscrewed it one-handed. Lin Mo managed half a turn before his wrist hurt.

“Give me yours.”

Wang Hai opened it too. The cap rolled off the table; he stopped it with his foot, retrieved it, and placed it in the middle. He took two bites of bread before pausing.

Lin Mo stared at his own package. A memory came to him of his sister giving him the same bread that morning.

The ward. His blue clothes. She'd asked why his stomach was still empty when she'd messaged him to eat before coming. Condensation wet her fingers around the bottle. She raised the bed a little and drew breath before speaking.

He reached for the bread. His fingertips touched the package on the table.

Wang Hai called his name. Lin Mo came back to himself, the wrapper crumpled in his fist.

“What happened?”

“Remembered her giving me this.”

“Your sister? When?”

“This morning.”

Wang Hai put his remaining bread into its bag. “You wrote that you got the clothes in Room 09.”

“She mentioned the message too. Eat before coming.”

“You got that message?”

“Yes. But when I reached bed six, there was an old man in it. She didn't give me bread there.”

Wang Hai pushed the water closer. “Eat this one now. Not the one she gave you.”

Lin Mo looked up. Wang Hai pointed earnestly at the table.

“I saw it fall out. Mine too.”

Lin Mo took a bite. Dry. He drank and swallowed slowly. Wang Hai asked no more about his sister. He squeezed a list of the vending machine's contents onto one line in his booklet, then copied the wrapper's barcode below.

Lin Mo recorded the breakfast memory with a question mark, underlining “this morning” and linking it to the clothing collection time on the previous page.

Three minutes had passed on the clock when wheels rumbled outside.

A linen trolley lurched out of the staff passage, one wheel caught on the threshold. Gray and white clothes were piled on top. A garment slid to the floor. Then a hand appeared beneath the frame.

Someone was trapped underneath.

Lin Mo stood, striking the table leg with his chair.

“Don't come over!”

A sharp voice from the passage. The trolley rolled farther, hiding the fingers. Its trapped occupant pushed a foot against the doorway, trying to drag free.

Lin Mo grabbed the chair leg and ran. Wang Hai followed.

Behind the trolley stood the green door. He remembered it. A small button on the left would divert the trolley track. He'd dealt with this before. The button was hidden under paint.

He reached left. Wang Hai caught his arm.

“What are you pressing?”

“Stopping it.”

He'd lifted a cover halfway, exposing a red button and small lettering he hadn't read.

> Forced retrieval.

Wang Hai pulled his hand away. “Stop it? That sends it in!”

Lin Mo's hand hung in the air. He remembered pressing it: lights beyond the green door, trolley moving right, route clear. His thumb remained bent, waiting for the button to spring back against it.

A heel scraped underneath the trolley.

He closed the cover. “Remembered wrong.”

Wang Hai looked at him. “Get them out first.”

Lin Mo crouched and tried levering up the frame with the wood. Too heavy. It barely lifted before his wrist gave.

Once, he could have managed that weight. Now exertion dragged his collar against his neck, and his arms shook after two seconds.

Wang Hai lifted a hanging sheet instead of taking the wood. Two wheels: one braked, one sliding down the slope. He gripped the side and stepped on the pedal, but the threshold blocked it.

“Stop this side! Chock the wheel. Don't lift!”

Lin Mo jammed the chair leg in front of the moving wheel. Its screw scratched the floor. The trolley pressed forward; wood cracked.

“Once more.”

Lin Mo braced his left shoulder against the frame, pinned the wood with his foot, and pushed the wheel back half an inch. Wang Hai used the gap to depress the pedal fully. It locked. The wheel stopped.

The motor still hummed, pushing outward. Lin Mo kept his shoulder there until the vibration eased, then crouched to reach underneath.

A short-haired woman lay cheek to floor, her right sleeve wound around the axle.

“Can you get out?”

“Sleeve.” Her voice was weak.

He tugged the cloth. Pulling would only tighten it around her arm. He retrieved the chair leg. The screw remained at the broken end, but the wood had split through the middle.

He hooked the point into the sleeve, worked open a hole, and tore at it. The woman clutched her right arm with her other hand. Her face whitened. Wang Hai braced the trolley.

A long tear opened. Lin Mo supported her behind the shoulder as she wriggled out. Halfway up, his shoulder weakened again. Wang Hai caught her other side with his right hand.

They dragged her to the rest-area doorway. Lin Mo chocked the wheel again and gripped the protruding end with his left hand.

“Staff passage. Do not remain here,” said the overhead voice.

“She's out.”

“Staff passage. Do not remain here.”

The woman pulled herself upright against the doorframe, breathing hard.

“Not talking to you.” She pointed at the trolley.

A red light beneath its chassis, previously hidden by clothes, flashed. The wheel scraped the wood but couldn't advance.

“I was the one shouting. Thought you'd press it too.” She wiped her face. “Wrong entrance.”

Bruising circled her arm where the sleeve had tightened.

“What were you doing here?”

“Clothes I haven't collected. Someone said I could find them here.”

“Who?”

“Me.” She glanced at the green door. “Remembered coming here before.”

Only then did Lin Mo notice his white-knuckled grip.

“You remember a laundry behind that door?”

She nodded. “Stairs beyond.”

He stepped farther from the button. “Did you press it?”

After several seconds she asked, “You know the one on the left too?”

“Says forced retrieval.”

“Didn't say that when I looked.”

He glanced back. The cover hid the button. A small screen beside the door lit with a side view of the trolley.

> almost pressed it

> what happens if he does?

> ask the one on the floor. she tried

Lin Mo stared at the last comment. The woman hugged her arm and turned away.

Wang Hai folded his wrapper into his booklet. “Photograph the writing next time.”

“No phone.”

“Copy it, then. Call me before pressing anything.”

She laughed softly, then sucked in a pained breath.

Wang Hai pointed inside. “Sit there. Trolley stays out.”

She took the chair nearest the machine. Lin Mo released the wood, checked that she hadn't touched the papers he'd left on the table, then inspected the wheels.

The red light had gone out. The frame shuddered and retracted several inches. He freed the chair leg. Half of it had bent to one side, thin cracks running through his carved name.

He crouched, thumb pressed to the character Mo.

“Broken?” Wang Hai asked.

“Some left.”

He snapped off the loose portion, keeping the end with the screw and lettering. Short enough now to grip inside a pocket.

Back in the rest area, a little over a minute remained. The woman's booklet lay on her knees, “Sun Ning” written on its cover in black ink.

“Remember that yourself?” Lin Mo asked.

“It's on my ID.” She looked at Wang Hai. “He hasn't got one?”

He patted his pocket. “Employee number for now.”

“Fits this place.”

She opened her booklet and pushed it into the middle. Among several notes, “Someone guided me to the retrieval entrance” had been crossed out.

“I don't know if that person exists. A moment ago I knew their face and surname.”

Lin Mo laid his green-door memory beside it. He kept the slip about his sister to himself.

“Where did you go downstairs?”

“To collect a white shirt. You?”

“The ward.”

He moved the books together. Sun Ning touched the button entry.

“I pressed that to clear the way too.”

He circled it. “Don't go back.”

She nodded. The screen lit up.

> Post-session cognitive fitting in progress.
> Lin Mo: Cognitive overwrite 18%.
> Break ending shortly.

Wang Hai studied the number and moved half a step closer.

“Remembering wrong now, or before?”

“That route before was wrong.”

“And now?”

Lin Mo handed him the ID. “Old photograph.” He turned the payment receipt over. “Wrote these lines in Room 09.”

“Saw you with the booklet.” Wang Hai opened it beside the receipt.

Lin Mo took back his ID and marked matching details in all three places with his pen.

“Keep these. If what you saw doesn't match what I remember, tell me.”

Wang Hai nodded and took out his own booklet. Sun Ning's gaze stopped at the receipt's lower corner. Her finger hovered without touching.

“Hospital receipt?”

Lin Mo covered it with his hand. “Seen it before?”

She pointed beneath the hospital name. “More of that number's folded under.”

He unfolded the bottom edge. He'd received this at the real hospital's payment counter, before going up to the eighth floor.

Yet after the digits came faintly printed English letters, matching the source-account prefix he'd just copied from the protection payment record.
