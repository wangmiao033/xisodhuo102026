# Chapter 12: Read Only This Line

Lin Mo touched the screen’s frame.

In the preview, Xiao Lu turned, revealing a pale section at the end of her right eyebrow. She straightened her badge and smiled. Before the smile faded, the program schedule replaced her.

“Play it again.”

No answer. Wang Hai tugged his sleeve and pointed. The index had caught in the slot, a corner almost slipping between two panels.

Lin Mo pulled it free and smoothed the folded photograph against his palm. He could make out the eyes now, but not that pale end of the eyebrow.

“Your sister?”

“Looks like her.”

He’d meant to say yes. His throat tightened around the rest.

“Looking alike isn’t proof,” Wang Hai said.

Lin Mo flattened the corner without arguing. Sun Ning moved beside him, blocking a draft from the passage.

“That was a preview. She couldn’t hear you.”

He nodded and reinserted the page. The same channel’s schedule appeared, with a small button beside it: Live Response.

He showed the others without pressing it. Wang Hai leaned closer, scraped his bandage against the wall, and grimaced. Sun Ning held his clothes and scrolled for him. Lin Mo turned sideways to give them room.

“Respond to whom?” Wang Hai asked.

“Don’t know.”

Lin Mo opened the explanation beneath the button. His current status was performer, making him ineligible for ordinary audience seating. He could apply for a response position in the same show.

Sun Ning brought her settlement page to the reader. A companion option appeared, unchecked.

“Can I sit beside him?”

“Access is limited to companion seats.”

“Vote for him?”

“Voting requires audience eligibility.”

Wang Hai’s factory badge also offered companion access.

“Don’t press it just because it lets you in. Ask what you’d have to do.”

Lin Mo scrolled to the bottom. His microphone would open only on cue. He had to speak the audience-selected text. Changing the words or leaving his mark counted as interrupting the performance.

“So I can’t ask my own questions?”

“This program does not grant two-person conversation rights.”

He showed Wang Hai, who swallowed whatever he’d been about to say and felt for the booklet through his pocket.

“Would we have to read too?” Sun Ning asked.

“Companions do not receive response instructions.”

She selected companion access. Wang Hai waited until Lin Mo had read the remaining conditions before presenting his badge.

“Can they leave?” Lin Mo asked.

The host directed them to the reverse of the eligibility page. Companions could leave freely with their collected materials. There was no guarantee that members of a group could leave together.

“So I can go without taking him?” Wang Hai asked.

“Yes.”

“Don’t need your permission for this part.” He pressed his badge to the reader. “If he doesn’t come out, I’ll wait by the door.”

Sun Ning moved her swollen arm into the light, fabric stretched against it.

“Can I change seats? I can’t have anyone crowding my right side.”

Seats couldn’t be moved. She asked for an aisle. Two vacancies appeared, and she selected the first with her left hand.

“I want the outside.”

“Take it. I’ll squeeze in.”

Looking at the confirmed seats, Lin Mo thanked them.

“Save it,” Wang Hai said. “Let someone twist your arm again and I’ll still give you hell.”

A sound-check requirement appeared: read the illuminated text once to obtain response eligibility.

Lin Mo applied. A white light came on beside the slot, and a small door opened onto a room containing a microphone. Beyond a glass partition, someone was arranging chairs, bracing their legs with a foot to line them up.

Wang Hai set the water beside the console. “Drink first.”

Lin Mo took one small sip. Since his voice changed, even swallowing needed a pause, as though someone had replaced a part in his throat with the wrong size. He capped the bottle without clearing his throat and stood on the white footprints.

> I’m a newcomer making my first appearance. Please take good care of me.

The live indicator in the corner was gray.

“No voting now?”

“Appearance adjustments are not accepted during sound check.”

He reread the line and pointed to “take good care of me.”

“Does reading that mean I agree to something?”

“This segment checks vocal output only. No additional agreement is established.”

Wang Hai wrote it down, dotting his pen beneath “only.”

Lin Mo read. His voice came back through the partition, higher than before, rough at the edges. The chair mover looked up, then returned to work. The microphone light stayed unchanged.

“Move into pickup range.”

He leaned forward, felt his heel reach the footprint’s edge, and stopped. Instead, he pulled the stand closer with his left hand.

Wang Hai started to raise a warning hand. Lin Mo pointed to the instruction to remain on his mark.

“Doesn’t say I can’t move the stand.”

The host didn’t object. Now he could speak without forcing his painful voice louder. On the second reading, the light turned green.

Beyond the glass, the worker pushed the last chair beneath a table and gestured. When Lin Mo didn’t understand, he pointed at the stand’s adjustment knob.

Lin Mo loosened it and lowered the microphone. He’d set it by old habit, half an inch too high.

“Better?” Wang Hai asked.

He breathed out. His lips didn’t touch the grille.

“Better.”

“Keep it there inside, too. Don’t get excited and start chewing it.”

Sun Ning waited by the door. “What are you going to ask her?”

“About the jacket.”

“If she doesn’t know?”

He reached into his inner pocket and stopped at the receipt’s stiff fold.

“The medicine.”

“Ask about a hundred things,” Wang Hai said. “It still won’t let you pick your words.”

While his sister was in hospital, the congee he brought often went cold. Whenever he saw her, he forgot to ask why.

He withdrew his hand empty.

“I’ll listen first.”

Wang Hai steadied the stand. “Can you speak this quietly in there?”

“There’s a microphone. I don’t need to shout.”

“Still hurt when you raised your voice?”

“Yes. Just don’t make me repeat myself.”

“If they can’t hear, make them adjust the volume,” Sun Ning said. “You don’t need your throat adjusted again.”

“Watch the light for me.”

“I can do that. Stay quiet until it comes on.”

He nodded. Wang Hai released the stand; it stayed put. Lin Mo tested an “All right.” The voice returned from beyond the glass without climbing in pitch.

> Sound check passed.

A compartment opened beside the partition. Inside lay a stiff card labeled Response Position. Fine print on the back specified that it was valid for this occasion only and conveyed no ownership of the streamer’s channel.

Lin Mo pocketed the index.

“Let’s go.”

Wang Hai took the water and badge. Sun Ning tucked her booklet beneath her left arm. They followed the illuminated passage toward quiet music, as though the show had already begun.

The audience seating was different here. Transparent panels stood before the stage, each with a person behind it, an account and voting buttons floating across its surface. Some folded their arms; others rested their feet on crossbars. Light from the accounts obscured their faces.

Xiao Lu stood at center stage, arranging a ribbon.

She didn’t smile continuously as she had in the preview. When nobody addressed her, her mouth relaxed and she looked at the floor. Only when the host asked if she was ready did she look up and straighten the ribbon’s ends.

Lin Mo stopped at the entrance. The pale patch at her right eyebrow remained. There was no adhesive on her neck from the IV. Her cheeks were fuller, her hair longer, her chin neater than in the recording.

“Don’t block the way,” someone said behind him.

Sun Ning touched his back. He moved aside. His response card lit a matching number along the stage, and a small light directed him to a narrow space beside the curtain.

Xiao Lu was only steps away. A red line separated them.

> Crossing the line forfeits this round’s choice and triggers the default adjustment.

He brought both feet onto his mark before looking up.

The companion seats lit on the left. Sun Ning sat forward; Wang Hai set the water by his feet and waited for Lin Mo to turn, without calling his name.

Lin Mo held the microphone.

The jacket first. Not her name, or why she didn’t recognize him. But the microphone was closed. A gray notice hung over it.

> Please wait for response text.

Xiao Lu put the ribbon in a box and turned. Her gaze crossed the gray jacket and rested on his face for two seconds.

“New?”

He nodded.

“Don’t speak until the light comes on. They’ll cut it into something else.”

She moved the box farther from the stage edge, keeping her hand behind the red line.

“Keep your cup by your feet. Anything onstage, they can choose.”

Lin Mo signaled toward Wang Hai’s water. Wang Hai moved it farther under the seat. Xiao Lu’s gaze followed, then left Lin Mo.

He pointed at her name badge. She glanced down and tightened its clip.

“Crooked?”

He shook his head and mimed switching on the microphone.

She paused. “You want to ask about the name?”

He nodded.

She turned it toward him. Beneath the plastic was a piece of paper with ragged, badly cut edges.

“I collected this one first. Have to have something for the announcements.”

“Let the newcomer say something,” someone behind a panel called. “All this waiting. Is he mute?”

Xiao Lu clipped the badge back and smiled toward that panel.

“He passed sound check. Please wait for the light.”

Knuckles rapped the panel twice, but the speaker stopped demanding. Lin Mo noted where it stood. Xiao Lu remained beside the box, feet behind the line, while her fingers slowly wound the ribbon’s tail into her palm.

He watched until she pulled it straight again.

The host welcomed the new response performer. Scattered applause followed. Xiao Lu tied the ribbon around the lid; her nail scraped faintly against the paper.

The chosen text appeared.

> I’d like to know what you’ve chosen for me today.

The microphone turned green.

Lin Mo read it, keeping the respectful form of address. He substituted no name or title.

Xiao Lu glanced toward the panels.

“They haven’t finished choosing. You don’t have to say you like it yet.”

Someone laughed.

“Looking out for the newcomer, is she?”

“Have him ask if she likes his face now.”

The next line quickly replaced the first.

> Do you like the way I look now?

Lin Mo hesitated. A countdown appeared beside the microphone. He raised his chin a little, read, and returned the microphone to its stand.

Xiao Lu looked at him.

“They like anything they can change.”

She spoke softly. The microphone caught it, but the host’s music immediately covered the latter part. Lin Mo turned. Two photographs of him appeared before the audience: one with his female face and blue clothes, one from his ID before admission.

He reached for the card in his inner pocket and felt the index paper instead. Someone tapped a button. A thin white line extended between the photographs.

Lin Mo pointed at his sound-check card, then the pictures. The microphone light had gone out. The text was gone.

“Sound check has concluded,” the woman reminded him. “This is the actual performance.”

Wang Hai stood in the companion seats. Lin Mo motioned for him to stay down. He didn’t reach his foot toward the buttons beyond the red line.

Xiao Lu replaced the box at center stage, briefly blocking the screen as though adjusting her position for the camera. She didn’t press Cancel. Her hand passed the photographs and pointed at the floor.

“Get back on your mark.”

Lin Mo looked down. The sock over his left toes was already touching the red line.

“Wait for them to choose. Any farther, and they won’t need to.”
