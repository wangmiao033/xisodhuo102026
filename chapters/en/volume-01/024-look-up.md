# Chapter 24: Look Up

“Look up,” Lin Mo said.

The hand held down the photograph, tapping a finger against the eyebrow on his old face.

“Don't you recognize yourself?”

The voice was low, dropping at the ends of words. Lin Mo's right hand left his pocket, fingers spreading, as they once had when he retrieved something mistakenly handed to his sister. Wang Hai gripped his sleeve and pressed his arm back.

“Keep your hand out.”

Lin Mo turned. Wang Hai was staring at the photograph.

“You think that's the real me too?”

Wang Hai released his sleeve, his face tightening.

“I'm looking underneath. Did you even look down?”

Lin Mo didn't move.

“You recognize my old voice. Not this one?”

Wang Hai rested his right hand on his knee, keeping his bandaged left against his chest.

“Say what you like. There's a metal slot under that photograph. Just big enough for your hand.”

He turned his factory badge over. A plastic corner ticked against a button.

“I've mistaken the photo on this thing before. Now you show me another one. What am I supposed to recognize?”

His name had returned to the badge. He still held it tightly, keeping it from swinging toward the window.

“I didn't ask you to recognize him for me.”

“That's exactly what you asked.”

Lin Mo turned away. Wang Hai said nothing more, shifting aside to clear the narrow passage. He stood a little farther away now. Lin Mo opened his mouth to tell him not to leave. Nothing came out.

A laugh came from inside, still in Lin Mo's voice.

“He recognized you perfectly. Return the paper. Don't make this difficult for him.”

Lin Mo curled his fingers against his white wrist scar, then held his empty palm outside his jacket.

“Show me your face. I've got the paper. It can wait.”

The photograph slid forward an inch, exposing a metal edge exactly where his reaching hand would go. The person ducked lower. Only a strip of skin above the mask remained visible.

Keys clinked against metal. A small door behind the wooden frame opened, and Xiao Lu emerged with a ticket clip and the script she'd retrieved from the prop cart. She put the clip on the frame beside the window.

“Why have you opened already?”

The person neither turned nor withdrew the photograph.

“He came on his own.”

Xiao Lu checked the yellow line, then Lin Mo's socked toes outside it. Tucking her script under her arm, she reached for a wire beside the window. The hand rose to block the connector.

“Your turn's next.”

“My voice is in there too. You're playing it now?”

Lin Mo edged toward her.

“You knew he wanted my receipt? That's why you told me not to reach in?”

She withdrew her hand, looking at his inner pocket.

“I've seen someone's hand get caught. Paper or no paper, I'd have told you.”

He gripped his pocket without answering.

“Did you press Continue?” she asked.

“No.”

“Then don't do the missing actions for him. I'm here for my recording, not to sit in there for you.”

Wang Hai pushed himself upright and moved to the other side of the passage without touching Lin Mo.

“Move. Let me look underneath.”

Lin Mo made room. Wang Hai bent, keeping his face clear of the opening and following the outside light. Xiao Lu turned the clip aside, took the flashlight from beneath it and shone it along the desk.

Under the photograph lay a shallow tray. Four short hooks curved inward from its mouth. The hand concealed the far end, retreating a little from the light.

“You could've said that when you grabbed me,” Lin Mo said.

“I did. You were busy recognizing people.”

Wang Hai held his right hand beside Lin Mo's pocket without reaching inside.

“Lend me the wood.”

Lin Mo pulled out the chair leg. It snagged against his pocket; his left shoulder couldn't take the strain. He supported it with his right hand, wincing as the broken end scraped his scar.

Wang Hai waited.

“Keep the writing toward you. Give me the other end.”

Only when Lin Mo turned it did he take hold. Keeping his hand outside, he laid the wood across the tray's opening and fed the unmarked end inside.

The person pressed down on the tray, trapping the photograph beneath their palm.

“The paper. We don't take that.”

“Then why move your hand?” Wang Hai asked.

No answer. He dragged the wood outward. The tray shifted with it. A rapid clicking sounded underneath, and all four hooks snapped shut, gouging the wood and biting off a chip.

Lin Mo reached to support it. His knees bent first, as though he were picking something up beside a hospital bed. He braced a hand against his skirt, straightened and stopped reaching.

Wang Hai withdrew the wood. Four fresh scores marked its broken end.

“Catch your sleeve in that, you wouldn't have time to get your jacket off.”

Lin Mo stared at the marks. When he took the wood, Wang Hai kept hold. They stood gripping opposite ends. Lin Mo looked up at him, but Wang Hai was watching the tray.

“What I said. I take it back.”

Wang Hai let go.

“Keep the wood out. We'll need it.”

Xiao Lu set the flashlight down, pointing downward. The person tried to push the tray back, but the wood chip jammed it, leaving a paper edge exposed. She caught the corner with her ticket clip.

“Let go.”

“It's his to collect.”

“He hasn't continued the performance.”

She pulled. The paper slid from beneath the photograph, tearing slightly. Lin Mo's name filled the name field. Beside it was printed “Former Ward Patient,” with an empty handprint below. Xiao Lu turned over the folded bottom edge to reveal two lines.

> Original retained. Replacement issued.

She held it before the mask.

“You're not giving him his receipt. You're making him exchange it for this.”

Lin Mo touched the original in his inner pocket without taking it out.

“And then?”

His old voice answered from inside. “Keep it. Don't lose it again.”

Xiao Lu set the clip against the desk edge. The blank handprint lined up with the tray; the four hooks waited where a wrist would rest.

“More underneath,” Wang Hai said, pointing.

Lin Mo crouched, his gray jacket hanging against his long socks. Two tracks ran beneath the tray. One delivered the new paper; the other led inward to blackened rollers. An old scrap was caught beside them, its edge shredded into strips. Of its red stamp, only a curve remained.

He stood, pressing his inner pocket through the jacket.

“Mine goes in there?”

The person fell silent, palm covering the flap before the rollers. Xiao Lu turned the clip toward Lin Mo. He pinched the replacement's exposed lower corner and lifted it to compare with the track opening, leaving the flap alone.

“You keep my original, give me a patient's paper. And I have to press my hand down.”

Xiao Lu released the clip. He set the replacement on the frame. As the handprint left the desk, the person's hand dropped onto the tray.

“Your performance hasn't been continued,” she told them. “Don't use his things to fill the gap.”

Lin Mo looked at her. Without waiting for thanks, she reached for the wire again.

“Disconnect my recording.”

The person released the tray and covered the connector with the photograph. Lin Mo raised the chair leg against its edge. The hand retreated.

So did Xiao Lu.

“What are you doing?”

“Helping you get it.”

“Am I supposed to take it or grab his hand?”

The wood stopped in midair. Beneath the photograph was a small black box. The person held it against the desk, fingers already finding a lever on its side.

All Lin Mo had been blocking was their face.

“Left,” Wang Hai said.

Lin Mo raised the wood higher, hiding the hand behind it. Unable to see the connector, Xiao Lu pushed his elbow. He thought she meant to cross the yellow line and threw his left arm across her path.

“Don't come in.”

“I'm at the side.”

She stopped instead of ducking under his arm. Wang Hai couldn't get around them. His shoe struck the frame; the flashlight wobbled beside the clip.

Too late. The lever went down with a faint snap. A woman's voice said, “Inside the jacket,” then cut off.

Xiao Lu held out her hand.

“Give me the wood. Stop pushing it at him.”

Lin Mo drew it back half an inch. She waited, watching the box. He turned the broken end and offered it. Only after she took it did he withdraw his empty hands.

“How can I help?”

“Hold down the photograph. Not him. I'll reach the connector.”

Wang Hai took Lin Mo's place.

“I'll hold it. Move your hands. You're blocking the light.”

Lin Mo stepped aside. Xiao Lu passed Wang Hai the chair leg. He pinned the old face to the desk. The person tugged, but the photograph stayed put. Xiao Lu pushed the connector sideways with her clip.

The old voice stretched, then stopped.

The person swore. The voice was hoarse and thinner now, without that downward inflection.

Wang Hai didn't look up.

“So you do have your own.”

Xiao Lu caught the box with her clip and dragged it to the desk edge. The wire came free. A green light still glowed on the box; three narrow slots lined its side. She found the middle one and pulled out a thin sliver, keeping it between her fingers.

“This one's mine.”

“How do you know?”

“You haven't heard my lines for the next performance.”

Lin Mo shut his mouth. She held the sliver against the box and pressed a raised spot. Her voice told someone to put their change away. She released it after half a sentence and slipped the sliver into her script.

Lin Mo pointed left-handed at another slot.

“Can you play the one that got cut off?”

She glanced inside the window without moving.

“Who are you asking?”

He gripped his pocket, then let go.

“You. Would you play it for me? Just the sound. I won't take yours.”

Holding her script against her waist, she crouched to inspect the lever. The person reached. She moved the box farther out; Wang Hai kept the photograph pinned. The hand fell short.

“It's broken. I can try holding it down.”

“Please try.”

She looked up. “Why did you suddenly block him?”

Lin Mo watched her fingers on the clip.

“You wanted your recording. I thought he'd trap your hand too.”

“Then ask me. Don't block the opening. I couldn't see which recording he was cutting.”

He nodded without explaining what he'd meant to do for her. She pressed the clip's tip against the broken lever. A faint scraping came from the box.

The woman's voice resumed. “…don't take it out. Just give him the clothes.”

Lin Mo pressed the gray jacket's lining. Half a tablet was hidden there. The writing on the blister pack was his.

He wanted another sentence. His fingers were already inside his left pocket, hooking the lining. Xiao Lu glanced down, and he withdrew them without exposing the pack at the window.

“Heard this before?”

“No. Don't take what out?”

He shook his head and pointed at the broken lever.

“Keep playing. Did they say a name?”

She didn't ask about the clothes again. The clip moved inward. Lin Mo watched her hand. He'd blocked her just behind that clip. Its tip trembled against the break; she adjusted the angle and pressed again.

After a pause, a man asked, “Where's the person collecting it?”

“Not inside yet.”

Wang Hai shifted the wood. The person snatched the photograph free, tipping the black box. The loose wire caught around a desk leg. Xiao Lu's clip sprang away, and the recording stopped.

Lin Mo didn't grab the box. He followed the retreating hand one step, stopping at the yellow line.

Xiao Lu retrieved her clip. The rollers whirred beneath the desk, drawing in the shredded paper. The last trace of red stamp disappeared.

Wang Hai crouched with the wood outside the tray.

“He's packing up.”

Wheels rattled behind the panel. The person dragged the paper trolley backward, its corner scraping a desk leg. Xiao Lu pointed to the small door at the passage's end.

“The paper goes out there. My next performance's starting. I can't chase it for you.”

Lin Mo looked up. Her sliver was secured inside her script; she held the ticket clip in her other hand.

“You got yours?”

She nodded.

Wang Hai handed him the chair leg instead of putting it in his pocket.

“Sun Ning's still waiting. I'll tell her to close the door and where we're going. If you follow him, stick to that route.”

Lin Mo gripped the wood and headed toward the closing door.

“The paper route. I'll wait outside for you.”
