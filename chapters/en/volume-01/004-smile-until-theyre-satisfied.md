# Chapter 4: Smile Until They're Satisfied

Lin Mo pressed his thumb against the corner of his mouth. The skin pushed upward beneath it. When he lowered his hand, his cheeks tightened again, as if thin threads ran from his mouth to the backs of his ears.

In the mirror, he smiled at himself. His wrist hurt. His mouth hurt. None of it showed on that face.

“Please complete the fitting,” the woman said.

He put the blue top on the chair, intending to sit. Both cheeks jerked.

“Collection complete. Changes are not permitted.”

“I'm not changing anything.”

He picked it up again. The pain eased; the smile stayed. Beneath the voter count, a bar ran from red to white. The red filled less than a fifth.

> Fitting approval: 17%
> Submission available at 60%.

Two buttons beneath it: Satisfied. Adjust.

“Where does it change me if they pick Adjust?”

“Please adjust according to audience feedback.”

The messages answered faster.

> put it on first

> can't see anything from back there

> do something with his hair

One demanded he turn around. Another told him not to.

He ignored them and searched the fitting room. Flat shoes on the floor, an unopened pack of long socks, two wall hooks. Nowhere else to put his belongings. He pushed the door halfway closed. It sprang back.

The camera was inside, its black lens aimed at the chair. Closing the door wouldn't hide him. He found a small corner out of shot, removed his trousers, and put on the costume.

When the blue cuff brushed his right wrist, he stopped and pulled it wide with his left hand, easing the injured one through. Tighter than the preview suggested. The hem bunched at his waist; shoulder seams pulled toward his neck. His shoulders had already narrowed in the dressing room. The top was still too narrow across the shoulders.

He folded his trousers and took out his keys and wallet. The blue jacket had two real pockets, but they couldn't hold everything. Half a corner of his ID stuck out when he tried it.

His sister's jacket hung on the chair. He moved the keys, wallet, ID, and pills into its pockets, keeping the receipt with the chair leg in his hands. He couldn't collect the gray jacket yet. He could keep it in sight.

“Please stand in the display area.”

White light outlined a rectangle on the floor. He checked that the collar wasn't pinching his skin and stepped inside. A full-length image appeared beside the mirror.

He studied its smile for several seconds before realizing it was live. A sock wrinkled beneath one knee. He looked down; the figure looked down.

Approval reached 24%, then fell to 22%.

> you call that smiling?

> stop glaring

> straighten the skirt

> shoulders still need a little work for blue

Hearts followed the last message. Lin Mo pulled down both sides of his top. The cuff slid up, exposing the ring around his wrist.

Adjust lit up.

Pressure squeezed his shoulders inward. He inhaled. The fabric tightened, then slowly slackened. He braced himself against the wall beside the mirror. Approval rose to 29%.

The shoulder seams had reached their proper places.

“Stop.”

The pressure continued. He tried switching the hand against the wall, but his left shoulder was trapped in the tightening cloth. He could only lower his arm.

“Stop.”

He raised his right hand. His fingers went numb.

> quit yelling. adjustment in progress

He pinched his right palm with his left hand. The skin was cold. He pinched harder until his little finger moved, then slowly let go. The pressure stopped too. The figure's smile had grown more pronounced.

31%. He straightened. His lips would barely close, and he could only breathe through his nose. All these people had to do was press a button, and he'd hurt again.

The gray jacket was still on the chair. His things filled its pockets. His sister's mismatched thread marked the sleeve.

“Can I submit now?”

“Approval requirement not met.”

He searched for the submission button, lowering his head slowly against the pull in his cheeks. The clear housing beside the mirror remained unlit. The camera had already followed his gaze.

“I haven't touched it.”

“Approval requirement not met.”

“If I have to stand here, you could at least let me breathe.”

He tried breathing out through his mouth. The pull sharpened at once. He stopped and used his nose, keeping his eyes on the gray button, memorizing the distance.

“Sixty's enough?”

“Submission available at 60%.”

He reread the rules. Audience approval. Nothing about making everyone happy.

He moved toward the mirror. The camera followed his face.

“What do you want to see?”

Messages crowded the glass.

> he speaks

> right there. don't move

> move your hands first

He hadn't realized the chair leg was still across his chest. He laid it outside the rectangle, carving upward, weighting one corner of the receipt. Slowly he released his hem and lowered his hands. 34%.

Someone told him to turn. He turned halfway. Fabric pulled across his back, but no new pressure came. 38%, then nothing.

> sleeve

He looked back at the mirror.

“What's wrong with it?”

> turn the other side up. let's see

Thinking they meant the fabric, he lifted his left arm and folded back the cuff. Unmarked skin. Two lines of laughter scrolled past.

> right one

> that red circle from before

He lowered his left hand. The burn on his right wrist had begun to swell. He tested the joint. It moved, sending pain into his forearm. Approval dropped to 35%.

Adjust lit again. He quickly raised his right hand and turned back the cuff for the camera. The entire ring showed: a finger-width band, darker at the back where small blisters seemed to be forming.

He left the skin alone. Someone voted Satisfied. 39%. Another vote.

42%. He lowered his hand, and the number fell. He raised it again. 45%.

His throat was dry. He watched the figure. He couldn't stop the pain or remove the smile. He could choose when to raise his arm, when to lower it. The camera edged toward the wound.

> how'd he get that?

> turned around to pick up someone else's stuff

Some of them had watched the corridor. Then Wang Hai came up.

> what happened to wang something?

Lin Mo didn't answer. He remembered him beside 09, asking whether his badge would still work. The photo had been erased, and he'd still tried holding it against the mirror to get it back.

47%.

> come on, tell us

Lin Mo glanced at the entrance. Closed. He faced front again.

“He went back for his name.”

> did he get it?

“Don't know.”

Hearts drifted upward. 50%. He'd said it smiling, in his ordinary voice. The camera saw only the smile, the clothes, the ring around his wrist.

Someone said he'd adapted fast. His stomach felt painfully empty. His sister had told him to eat, but he'd rushed straight over. He raised his arm a little higher, turning the injury toward the lens.

53. 55. He said nothing else.

An itch started near the shoulder seam. He didn't dare scratch. His cheeks had gone numb from the sustained pull; his breaths came hot and short through his nose.

58%. It stopped there.

Several viewers told him to move closer. He looked at the round Submit button beneath its clear cover. Previously gray, it now showed a faint white glow.

Bracing his left hand against the wall, he stepped forward. The camera followed. 60%.

He pressed immediately.

The cover didn't obstruct his finger. The button clicked home. The camera stopped. Approval fell to 59%.

His left hand stayed pressed against it. A short tone sounded.

> Approval at submission: 60%.
> Fitting passed.

His mouth released. His back slid a little down the wall before he could steady himself. Only then did he realize he'd been clenching his back teeth. He opened his mouth and drew two breaths, then touched the inside of his lip with his tongue.

Split. His cheeks still trembled, but the smile was gone. Someone cursed him for being quick.

He took his hand off the button and reread the notice. Approval at submission.

He tried putting that hand in his pocket. It shook halfway there. He leaned on the wall instead. The message telling him to move closer was still visible, followed by one accusing him of pressing too soon. He opened his mouth toward the camera. Nothing came out.

Eventually he stepped out of the rectangle. A fold in his sock pressed against his toes. Holding the chair back, he slowly smoothed it away. Nobody ordered him back into position.

“Please return your old clothing.”

He waited.

“Personal effects can stay.”

“Yes.”

“My stored jacket. And the handover record.”

Rollers turned beneath the cupboard. A sheet emerged halfway from a slot, print downward. The black thread at the gray collar snapped by itself; the tag fell.

He secured the jacket first, then took the paper. Real paper, still warm. The first line listed one pale gray jacket. Beneath it were long intake and collection numbers. He memorized their last four digits before reading on.

The original holder's field was blacked out. Delivery method: Deposited on another's behalf. Delivery notes:

> Choose something loose for him.
> His right hand's giving him trouble.

He touched his wrist. Injured in the corridor. The tag said the jacket had arrived before this round; by then, he had already been hurt.

But how could his sister know?

Held to the light, the black area had no thickness like tape. No letters showed through the back.

“Who's the original holder?”

“Restricted information.”

“Is she watching this stream?”

“Restricted information.”

He put on the gray jacket and zipped it up. Looser than the costume, its right cuff covered the burn. He restored his ID to the inside pocket, checked the pills and keys, then collected his original black jacket and trousers from the chair.

He searched every pocket again before feeding the clothes into the return slot. The woman didn't hurry him. He retrieved the wood and receipt, then found a pen at the collection desk. A string tethered it; it wouldn't reach anywhere else.

On the receipt's back, he wrote:

> Entered 04 male.
> Collected blue clothes in 09.
> Never wore them at the hospital.

He paused. Where had that last line come from? Facing the camera, he'd briefly felt he'd worn these clothes here from the hospital. His sister had sat on the bed, asked him to turn, said they suited him.

But someone else had occupied bed six. He checked the receipt's date, then the blue stamp on the handover record. Different things.

“Fitting changes have been finalized.”

Two figures appeared.

> Physical modification: 14%
> Cognitive overwrite: 8%

He gripped the pen. The ward grew clearer in his head. His sister straightened his jacket hem and smiled.

“You chose it yourself. Why aren't you happy?”

He traced his last line harder. Never wore them at the hospital. The pen tore the paper. He eased the pressure, added the date, and wrote Wang Hai's name.

A narrow door clicked open behind the cupboard. Darkness beyond.

“Please proceed to the next round.”

He folded the receipt with the handover record. In the mirror, the figure in gray watched him, still smiling. He moved closer.

His own mouth wasn't smiling. The figure mouthed something without sound. He understood.

“You chose it yourself.”

Wiping sweat from his chin with his sleeve, he gripped the chair leg and went through the door.
