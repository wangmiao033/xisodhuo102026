# Chapter 13: Choose the Pain

Lin Mo brought his left foot back inside the mark. The red line hadn’t touched his skin, yet his foot felt cold where the sock had brushed it.

Xiao Lu moved aside. Two options lit between the photographs: Scar Retention, illustrated by a close-up of his right cuff; and Waist and Hip Fitting, with a body outline beneath blue clothes. The latter was labeled Preview: not the current state.

He hid his burned wrist. The photograph stayed.

“No welcome gift you can eat?” Wang Hai muttered. Sun Ning tugged him into his seat.

> One vote per person. The option with more votes will be implemented.
> Ties extend voting by thirty seconds.
> No approval threshold. At least one option must be implemented.

Last time it had required sixty percent. Lin Mo pointed at the final lines. Xiao Lu read them and asked for him.

“He wants to check. This isn’t the same as the earlier approval vote?”

“A choice between two options, not approval of a proposal.”

“How long does it stay?” he asked her quietly, pointing at Scar Retention.

She repeated the question to the host.

“A permanently visible mark will form. No further burning. Eligibility for wrist movement will not be affected this round.”

“Eligibility isn’t ability.” He tried turning the injured wrist.

“He means the joint,” Xiao Lu said. “Will movement be restricted?”

After a pause, the host answered. “No additional restriction this round.”

“It’ll hurt,” Xiao Lu said.

“I know.”

“Different from what hurts now.”

Her gaze touched his wrapping. He didn’t ask how she knew. He raised the cuff, but the cloth stuck. Moving it sent numbness through his fingers. He began loosening the edge.

“Don’t peel it off. Just show the camera.”

He exposed part of the red burn and its lingering swelling. With his other hand he held the jacket closed, keeping the camera out.

Wang Hai pulled the pen cap off with his teeth. Lin Mo waited for his pen to touch paper before turning back.

The host announced two minutes. Votes climbed from zero.

> Waist and Hip Fitting: 73%
> Scar Retention: 27%

Lin Mo pointed toward the exit, asking what would happen if he left before voting finished.

His response position had already entered the welcome round. Leaving counted as interruption and couldn’t revoke its outcome.

“They start before telling you what the welcome is,” Wang Hai said.

Sun Ning showed him the sound-check confirmation page. The welcome round was listed; its content said only To Be Announced Live.

“Next time it says that, don’t fill the blanks with something good.”

Lin Mo nodded. He’d read every line, eager to enter, and still assumed a welcome meant saying a few words. The exit arrow was steps away. Those steps couldn’t cancel the show he’d accepted.

Xiao Lu stepped back, her pale skirt covering her footprints. Instead of leaving, she fetched a placard and stood it beside the box.

“Only the winning option will be implemented. No stacking this round.”

“Who said anything about stacking?”

“Just a reminder.”

A hand hovered over a button, then withdrew. The enlarged fine print revealed a gray default-continuation checkbox behind the fitting preview. It wasn’t selected.

“That?” Lin Mo pointed.

“Reuse the preview next round. It isn’t for this round.”

“I haven’t agreed to another.”

“Keeping it there doesn’t mean you have. Don’t press it.”

She angled the placard to obscure a corner of the checkbox without touching an option. He withdrew his hand. Nothing within reach on that smooth table let him rest easy.

> I’m excited about the gift you’ve prepared for me.

The word “excited” lodged in his narrower throat. The microphone flashed. He breathed in and read the whole line, without smiling.

“Wrong tone. Again,” a spectator said.

The host declared the text complete. The spectator lowered his hand and pressed Fitting again.

> You have already voted.

Wang Hai tapped his pen against the paper. He’d noticed too. They couldn’t replace anyone’s vote, but each spectator couldn’t keep voting to chase him into another change.

No wonder you change your names, Lin Mo mouthed.

Xiao Lu approached the stage edge. Her own microphone had never gone dark.

“There are thirty seconds for feedback after a response. You can speak now. Don’t ask about my private life.”

His light turned yellow. Feedback time appeared below it. He turned his exposed burn toward the camera.

> it’s already there. that’s not a modification

> who wants to watch a scar?

> budget option?

Xiao Lu clipped the ribbon beneath the lid and tapped the table.

“You can’t just tell them what not to pick. Give them a reason.”

Nineteen seconds remained.

“Choose the scar. When I clear the next round, it’ll still be there. Remember who voted for it.”

The fitting count slowed.

“Threatening the audience?”

“I can’t even see your faces. Who am I threatening?”

Wang Hai ducked his head and coughed. Sun Ning covered her mouth with her booklet, shoulders shifting.

“How about a signature beside it?” another spectator called.

Lin Mo looked at the light where the host’s voice came from.

“Two options this round. Names aren’t included.”

“Feedback cannot add implementation content.”

A rejection appeared on the spectator’s panel. He withdrew his hand, seemingly stung by the public refusal, then voted for the scar.

58% to 42%. The scar still trailed.

“Legs would look better,” someone in front said.

Lin Mo raised his wrapped wrist. “Wait for the next show.”

The yellow light went out. He stopped before adding an insult. Even those short sentences had set his throat stinging, his newly steadier voice floating upward again.

Xiao Lu pushed the microphone back within reach and pointed to the voting timer.

“You’ve spoken. Wait. Don’t take it back.”

He moved his mouth away and held his cuff, watching.

At 49% for the scar, Wang Hai stood at the edge of the companion seats. He wasn’t reaching for a voting button. He pushed his water toward Lin Mo, but it caught beneath a chair leg.

“Not yet.” Lin Mo waved him back.

Wang Hai retreated without sitting.

Through the last ten seconds, the lead changed repeatedly. Xiao Lu counted down from center stage, pausing half a beat at three. The host supplied the final two numbers. Xiao Lu held the box still.

> Scar Retention: 52%
> Waist and Hip Fitting: 48%

Voting closed. Both previews dimmed, leaving the wound’s close-up. Lin Mo turned his wrist. The red mark hadn’t changed, though Implementation in Progress already glowed on the stage edge. He steadied his hand without asking to cancel.

As he exhaled, his wrist sank, as though a thread had passed through the wound and tied itself beneath the table. He caught the microphone stand and left the painful hand where it was.

The redness faded. Skin rose along the burn’s outline and tightened, dragging the cloth with it. The original heat had worked inward from outside. Now the whole wrist seemed to shrink from within.

He tried moving his thumb. Tendons shifted, and white flooded his vision. The stand tilted under him. He eased his weight and its foot sprang back with a metallic noise lost in the music. The broadcast continued.

Sun Ning told him to breathe out slowly. He counted to four with her but couldn’t find the second breath. He held the stand until his throat loosened.

Wang Hai reached the entrance to the seats. A light turned red before his shoes.

“Companions may not enter the implementation area.”

“I’m bringing water!”

“Wait until completion.”

Lin Mo raised his left hand to stop him. At chest height, the shoulder shook with fatigue. He lowered it to the stand’s knob. Metal edges pressed his palm. He could tell one pain from the other. He left the cloth on his right wrist alone.

Xiao Lu shifted her box before the camera, hiding his bent posture.

“The implementation area must remain visible.”

She moved it half an inch aside without arguing. Only a ribbon tail remained at the picture’s edge.

Lin Mo held his breath, teeth clenched. Air escaped through his nose. He didn’t want to cry out, but the panels enlarged his tight mouth. Someone suggested choosing crying sounds next time. Another immediately told him to shut up and stop mixing other things into this round.

Lin Mo remembered that second remark.

The pain stopped abruptly. His hand fell onto the stage edge. A pale scar ran along the outside of his wrist. The swelling and redness were gone, but the cloth had become tight.

Xiao Lu produced scissors and laid them flat on the table instead of reaching for him.

“You can loosen the bandage. You can’t cut the wound. Want these?”

He nodded. She pushed them across the red line within his left hand’s reach.

He cut the knot with the points outward, leaving the small piece stuck to the scar. He wound the loosened wrapping back over what the camera had found so interesting.

A scrap clung to his finger. Shaking didn’t dislodge it. Wang Hai placed folded wastepaper on the edge for him to drop it onto.

“Don’t put adhesive on the scar when you wrap it.”

“I haven’t got any.”

“Neither have I. Just in case it offers you some.”

Sun Ning folded the paper around the scraps. Unable to reach with her right arm, she couldn’t align the fold. Wang Hai pressed it flat with his good hand. Once they were free, Lin Mo returned the scissors handle-first.

Xiao Lu wiped the threads from the blades with paper.

“Permanent scar complete. Physical modification: 22%.”

The other figure remained 30%.

Sun Ning pointed at Wang Hai’s booklet. He finished writing, tucked his pen between the pages, and offered the water without touching Lin Mo’s wrist.

“This counts as a modification too?”

Lin Mo couldn’t open the cap. Wang Hai took it back and loosened it with his right hand.

“My body changed. It counts.”

“Very diligent about counting.”

Lin Mo drank without answering the joke. He moved to set the bottle on the table. Xiao Lu pointed at the floor. He stopped and returned it to Wang Hai, who supported his left elbow.

“You can’t stand.”

“Not falling over yet.”

“Then don’t lean on that skinny pole. It won’t pay if you fall.”

Xiao Lu was closing the segment, announcing the next start time and giving the audience a brief bow. Her smile relaxed only after the camera turned away.

Lin Mo used the gap while feedback was being settled. He pointed to the jacket.

“Have you seen this before?”

Her hand stopped on the lid.

He turned out a corner of the cuff, revealing the patch and its white-and-blue stitches, but not the note. She bent to examine the stitches.

“Who gave it to you?”

“The Returns Window.”

“Which number?”

“Seven.”

She pushed the ribbon into the box. The lid missed its place on the first try. She straightened it and closed it again.

“Don’t use it to pay performance fees.”

“I wasn’t going to.”

“If they offer to take it as payment, don’t agree.”

The curtains behind the stage began to part. Someone wheeled in a measuring frame, its casters knocking against the floor. The host announced the measuring event and called the performers forward.

Lin Mo still wanted to ask why, but Xiao Lu had put the scissors away. Passing him, she pressed a rolled corner of the gray jacket’s hem flat with her palm.

“Keep this on properly. Don’t take it off until they’ve measured you.”
