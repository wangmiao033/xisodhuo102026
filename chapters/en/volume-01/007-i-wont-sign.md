# Chapter 7: I Won't Sign

The back of the application was blank. On the front, a signature line waited at the lower right. Beside it lay a slim pen, pointing toward Lin Mo, thin enough to hold without brushing his injured wrist.

He left it there. A black screen opposite showed an open envelope.

> Individual reward awaiting collection.

“Please confirm your application,” the woman said.

He leaned the chair leg against the table. “I didn't apply.”

“The form has been prepared for you.”

“By you.”

“You may collect this session's individual settlement reward.”

The door behind him was closed. A small monitor beside it showed Wang Hai still seated, holding one corner of the payment receipt against his lap so it wouldn't slip.

“I selected settlement representative.”

“The representative has priority collection rights.”

“Priority. Not permission to leave alone.”

The envelope opened farther, revealing a card.

> INDIVIDUAL REWARD PREVIEW
> Restoration of original facial appearance: One use.

Lin Mo went still. A photograph popped out beside the tabletop. Taken after his discharge: slightly longer hair, a black T-shirt, his arm along the back of his sister's chair. Her face had been cropped away. Only her shoulder remained.

He picked it up. A broad jaw, lips set in a straight line, no cut beneath the left eyebrow. His sister had said he looked too fierce that day, told him to stop glaring at the camera.

“You'd restore this face?”

“One restoration of original facial appearance.”

“What about my body?”

“The reward is displayed.”

“My memory?”

“The reward is displayed.”

He put the photograph down. “Settle things for both of us first.”

The envelope closed. After several seconds, the woman answered.

“Your companion's identity is incomplete. Individual settlement is available.”

“His number was verified. He's collected his record.”

“Those without names require further processing.”

“Rule four says we can enter settlement once we've both collected our records. Nothing about recovering his name first.”

He spread out his record and pointed from its number to the monitor. “Two records. Both collected.”

“Please confirm your application.”

“Change it.”

“The application provided is for individual settlement.”

He picked up the pen. Its tip hovered over the signature line. On the monitor, Wang Hai looked down at the receipt, then back toward the door. His lips stayed still. He seemed to think the sound prohibition still applied.

Lin Mo moved the pen to the heading and wrote in a refusal, changing the request to abandon his companion into a rejection of it.

“Do not alter the prefilled application.”

He stopped. “Fine. I'll write another.”

“Do not alter the prefilled application.”

“I'm not.”

He pushed it aside and turned over his settlement sheet. The line about associated information faced him. He glanced at the printer, then put down the pen. This paper wasn't his to ruin. There might be no replacement.

“Give me a blank sheet.”

“No other application is currently available.”

“If I can't change yours, let me write one.”

The viewer count jumped. Only then did he notice that settlement was being streamed. A black screen instead of a mirror; chat tucked into a corner like notifications.

> still hasn't signed?

> sign and he gets his face back, right?

> did he come here to fill out forms?

> seven wasn't enough. now there's another clerk

Lin Mo looked up. “Want it to go faster? Tell her to give me paper.”

> give it to him

> get on with it

> just spell out the reward

The printer whirred. A sheet emerged bearing only a date and an empty box. He caught its edge and waited for it to finish.

“Application for joint settlement,” he wrote, followed by his name. He couldn't read Wang Hai's number on the small monitor. He held the intercom button.

“Wang Hai. Can you hear me?”

The man looked up sharply.

“I'm inside. You can speak now.”

Wang Hai watched the ceiling before answering. “Really?”

“The last rule said until the representative enters settlement. Did you see it?”

He rubbed his throat. “I saw. Didn't dare believe it.”

“Read me the number at the top of your record.”

Wang Hai began after a pause. Lin Mo wrote the letters and digits in sections, repeated one section, and had him lean toward the camera to read it slowly again. Once finished, he asked him to check it all.

“Are you taking the reward?” Wang Hai asked.

“We're getting out first.”

His shoulders dropped, as though he'd finally heard something he could hold on to.

Lin Mo placed the application on the document reader. White light passed over it. No sound. The reward envelope remained onscreen. After more than ten seconds, he asked, “What's wrong with this one?”

“Receipt of the individual reward must be acknowledged.”

“I didn't ask for it.”

“Declining requires confirmation.”

“Where?”

Two buttons appeared: “Collect” and “Decline.” He drew his application back a little.

“Decline the reward, not settlement?”

“Decline this individual reward.”

Wang Hai pressed harder on the receipt. “If you say I'm not with you, can you take it?”

“They want my signature.”

“I haven't got a name. They could justify it if you signed.”

“You have a number.”

Wang Hai wiped his nose with the back of his hand and fell silent.

Lin Mo turned the prefilled application faceup. Fine print beneath the signature line read “Collection constitutes confirmation.” He touched it lightly with the pen. Taking the reward would count, even without signing.

“Declining won't take away the records we just collected?”

“Basic settlement and rewards are processed separately.”

“Put that in writing too.”

No answer. Some viewers told him to choose; others started asking whether there was a deduction. He waited until a note appeared beside “Decline”: “Does not affect this session's basic settlement.”

He replaced the joint application, checked both identifiers, and pressed the button. The photograph darkened. The envelope disappeared.

Green light passed over the two entries on his application.

> Both collection statuses verified.
> Joint settlement approved.

He set down the pen. The monitor changed view as the lock beside Wang Hai released. Wang Hai glanced back at the six boxes, then stood with a hand on his chair.

Inside, he saw the old application. His face changed. Lin Mo covered it with the joint one.

“Didn't sign.”

Wang Hai stared, still gripping the receipt. When Lin Mo reached for it, he took several seconds to let go.

“There's writing on the back.”

“I know.”

“I held it down. Didn't let it fall.”

Lin Mo folded it. “I saw.”

The printer produced two booklets, each less than half a palm in size, with thin pages. Both covers read “Personal Settlement Record.”

Lin Mo's bore his name. Inside: a collection number, Physical modification 14%, Cognitive overwrite 8%. The next page was blank and widely ruled. A short, untethered pen was tucked inside.

Wang Hai's cover bore only his number. He twisted his pen, failed to write, and looked up anxiously. Lin Mo pulled the cap off.

“Thought you twisted it.”

“Going to take it apart and fix it?”

“That scanner really was broken.”

He wrote the first character of his name, Wang, then stopped halfway through Hai. Lin Mo didn't hurry him. In his own booklet he listed the two sessions and what had actually happened: clothes could be changed before collection; submission used the current percentage; Silent Room identities could be verified by number.

At “hospital,” his pen paused. He could still see himself beside his sister's bed in blue clothes. He unfolded the payment receipt and checked his earlier notes before adding: Received blue outfit in Room 09.

“Need to write all this?” Wang Hai pointed beside “Original identity.”

“Name, age, what you've done. Lin Mo. Twenty-five. Living at home since leaving the army. Start there.”

“You were a soldier?”

“Used to be.”

“No wonder you reacted so fast in the corridor.”

“Didn't stop you.”

“You did later. Put that bit of wood across my legs.”

Lin Mo nudged the chair leg farther from his shoe and wrote when he'd left the army. He couldn't recall the exact date. He left a space rather than guess.

Wang Hai saw the blank and returned to his first page.

“Shift handover's at seven every morning. Half past if the next lot are late.” His handwriting grew smaller. “Does that count?”

“Anything you still remember.”

“You told me the name. This, I remember myself.”

Lin Mo drew a vertical line between two entries to separate them. Wang Hai leaned over to look.

“Hospital?”

“My sister was a patient there. Then she was gone.”

“That jacket was hers too?”

“Yes.”

Wang Hai looked at the patched gray cuff. “You're looking for her?”

Lin Mo nodded.

Slowly, Wang Hai finished the second character, underlined his name, and held out the booklet.

“This is me?”

“Yes.”

“What you called me in the corridor?”

“Yes.”

He pocketed it. “Then I'll use it for now.”

A new option appeared.

> ASSOCIATED RECORDS
> Available queries: 1.

Lin Mo raised the handover slip. “Find the source account for the custody channel.”

Three categories appeared: Clothing. Names. Protection.

His hand paused over Clothing. Wang Hai glanced from Names to his own booklet but said nothing.

Lin Mo held the blue stamp toward the screen, then turned the slip back. Seven's old receipt had linked the jacket and protection. Searching clothing might tell him who delivered something. He wanted whoever had paid for his protection.

He selected Protection. A gray avatar appeared beside a source account number, without a name. He copied the full number before proceeding.

> Third-party payment confirmed.
> Original account cannot be restored to use.
> Protection eligibility remains valid. Remaining duration subject to original agreement.
> Full agreement: Insufficient read permissions.

“The account can't be used again, or the person's gone?”

“This record describes account status.”

“Same person who added the note?”

“Custody channel operator identities are recorded separately from source account status.”

“Where do I check the operator?”

The avatar vanished.

> Query used.

He pressed the screen. Warm plastic. Wang Hai stood beside him, leaving his booklet untouched.

“Nothing?”

“A little.”

“If they're still alive, you can find them.”

Lin Mo didn't answer. He didn't want a promise from Wang Hai. He didn't have the energy to explain those distinctions again, either. He circled the number's prefix. Jacket storage, old receipt, protection payment: all the same.

Now he had the complete number.

“Can you use yours to check your name?” he asked.

Wang Hai glanced at the printer. “Didn't see anything about one query.”

The screen still said “Representative's query used.” Two people through; one query. Lin Mo pocketed the booklet and returned to the table.

“Wang Hai has a record. Where's his query?”

“Associated queries are allocated per settlement group.”

“Put that in his booklet. Don't leave him waiting for one.”

After a silence, the printer issued a small sticker bearing the rule. Wang Hai stuck it beneath his number.

“Fine. Now I know.”

A comment said they insisted on collecting every freebie.

“If there isn't one, tell me there isn't,” Wang Hai muttered.

Lin Mo bent for the chair leg. His mouth twitched. This time nobody was pulling it into position.

The door behind the table opened. Wang Hai waited there while Lin Mo separated the handover slip, receipt, and booklet into different pockets. Before leaving, he turned the abandonment application facedown.

Just beyond the doorway, Wang Hai caught his sleeve.

“Why are you smiling again?”

He held out his booklet. On its final page, a joint-settlement comparison chart contained a tiny identity photograph: Lin Mo in the blue stage costume, smiling.

> Recipient: Lin Mo.
> Sex: Female.
> Occupation: Performer.

Lin Mo reached for the ID in his inside pocket.

Wang Hai lowered his voice. “You said you were in the army. Or am I remembering wrong?”
