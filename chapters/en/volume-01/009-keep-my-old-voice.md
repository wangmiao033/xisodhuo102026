# Chapter 9: Keep My Old Voice

Lin Mo flattened the receipt's fold and rubbed the lettering. The ink stayed.

“How did you know there was more?”

Sun Ning indicated its width with her good hand. “Used to submit shop expenses. These receipts leave a line at the bottom. Half was showing.”

“Hospital printed this too?”

“No idea. I could just tell part of it was still folded under.”

He compared it with the payment record. Only the prefixes matched; the rest differed. He'd almost mistaken them for one account.

Wang Hai leaned closer. “Like my badge? Factory first, person after?”

“Maybe.”

He copied both strings on separate lines. Faint letters crossed the rain stain. He remembered the nurse using this receipt to check the bed, but not whether they'd been there then.

The countdown reached zero. Beside the vending machine, a door opened beneath an arrow marked “Waiting Area.”

Sun Ning stood, torn sleeve dangling. Lin Mo put away his papers. Wang Hai took the remaining water and pocketed his empty wrapper.

“Keeping that?” she asked.

“Debt's not settled. Keep the proof.”

Three rows of seats faced a white projection screen. A deep horizontal fold cut through a woman's face. Underneath:

> External item: Associated record playback.

Lin Mo stopped. Not his sister.

A man in short sleeves held up keys. The woman in the projection reached for them. He reached too, but touched only cloth. His fingers lingered.

“Sample complete. Please exit.”

He stepped forward.

“Sample complete.”

He withdrew his hand. The projection died, and he left through a narrow door beside the seats, head lowered.

Wang Hai caught Lin Mo's sleeve. “Nobody behind that cloth.”

“I saw.”

“Say it first.”

Lin Mo looked at him. “Nobody behind that cloth.”

Only then did Wang Hai let go.

Sun Ning scanned her settlement page at the entry desk. A seat light came on. She sat, adjusting her injured arm. Wang Hai scanned his badge and sat with one empty seat between them.

Lin Mo's sheet lit another seat. He placed his water underneath. Two options appeared on the desk: “Wait” and “Associated Record Playback.”

He held his receipt in the light. Its faint letters brightened and appeared on the screen.

> Association mark established on external item.
> Mark locates this session's archive and is not part of the original receipt.

“When was it printed?”

“Once the association was established.”

“You can change papers I bring in?”

“This session only verifies associated materials.”

An evasion. He retrieved the receipt and recorded the explanation. Date, bed number, his handwriting: still there. The faint letters were no longer evidence from the hospital itself.

“I've used my one query.”

“Settlement query complete. Associated record playback is an independent performance.”

“What does it cost?”

> Remain in the marked area during sample playback.
> Audience voting may adjust your performance appearance.
> After each completed sample, you may exit or continue.

Wang Hai stood. “More changes, then.”

“Yes.” Lin Mo touched the receipt's fold. “Can I choose what plays?”

“Supply an associated number.”

He didn't enter it yet. “Watching this doesn't mean agreeing to what follows?”

“Playback does not substitute for signing an agreement.”

“If someone calls me, do I have to answer?”

“Interaction with samples is not required.”

He asked Wang Hai to record both answers. Wang Hai dragged his chair forward until its light flashed red.

“How long will you stand there?”

“One sample.”

“How long is that?”

Lin Mo asked again. Three minutes forty seconds, starting with playback, not selection.

“I'll count,” Wang Hai said. “Come out when it's up. Don't stay for another glimpse.”

Sun Ning opened her booklet. “I'll watch the time. Call you when it stops.”

Lin Mo entered the protection payment's source account, checked each character, and confirmed. The screen displayed “Associated Person Verification,” dated before his arrival. A thumbnail showed a gray cuff.

He gave Wang Hai his booklet. “Don't fill mine in. What you see goes in yours.”

“If it's different?”

“Keep both.”

Wang Hai pulled the pen cap off with his teeth. Lin Mo entered the white circle before the screen and pushed his gray cuff up, exposing blue underneath.

Seats creaked behind him. The empty back row now held several people in blank masks, some with voting paddles. Above them lit the words “Live Audience.”

The circle offered room to stand, none to step back. He spread his feet and lowered his weight from habit, but the blue outfit pulled at his waist and his right knee met the skirt. He narrowed his stance and turned the short wood inside his pocket so its screw stopped digging into his leg.

Wang Hai pointed two fingers toward his feet. One heel already touched the boundary. Lin Mo shifted half a step inward and nodded, keeping a hand on his pocket to guard against another old, longer stride.

The screen lit. Bed six.

The camera tilted, cutting off half the blue bed number. His sister drew the curtain. A missing bottom hook let one corner drag across her cup; she blocked it with her arm.

Sitting, she loosened the gray collar. Her face was thinner than before hospitalization, her chin sharper. A near-invisible section at the end of her right eyebrow made her frowns uneven.

Lin Mo bit the old cut inside his mouth. Pain brought his lifted foot back into the circle.

“My brother might not get here early today.”

The off-camera voice was quiet, impossible to place as male or female.

“Confirm associated person.”

“Lin Mo. You just made me fill that in.”

“Confirm relationship.”

She tugged impatiently at her cuff. “Older brother. My biological brother. Want our household register?”

Laughter from the back row.

Lin Mo watched her fingers pick at the patch. Cream thread joined pale blue at a tight little knot. Unable to undo it, she let the sleeve drop. The thread he'd lifted with his nail in Room 09 brushed her bedsheet.

She tucked a piece of blister pack into the lining. Foil flashed, three crowded pen strokes visible on its back.

“Half a tablet missing?”

“Already split.”

She took it out and turned it toward the camera. Her fingers covered “After food,” exposing the crossed-out line below.

Through his pocket, Lin Mo pressed the same foil. He remembered ink spattering from a pen, Wang Hai beside the bed offering a tissue, his sister wiping the sheet.

He turned sharply. Wang Hai sat writing in the front row, left hand bandaged.

They'd met in the Mirror Corridor. The pen had been dry; he'd only made three scratches on the pack.

Yet Wang Hai's hand remained in that memory, down to the black dirt beneath his nails. Lin Mo squeezed the foil until its edge hurt, refusing to follow the scene farther.

Onscreen, she hid the medicine again and nudged the lining's opening, checking it wouldn't fall. He almost told her not to hide it. The circle stopped the words.

Wang Hai's pen scratched rapidly.

The complete source account appeared at the projection's edge, above “Original holder verified.” Chat swept across it.

> that's the one?

> sent the clothes earlier, right?

> stop blocking the number

Lin Mo craned to see. The circle's front edge glowed red. He stopped, toes curled inside his long socks.

“Turn the comments off.”

“Audience interaction is part of this session.”

“Move them. They're covering the evidence.”

No answer. A comment paused over his sister's face.

> that voice on that face. wrong dub

A voting box appeared: “Vocal fitting.” Approval of 60% at the deadline would trigger execution.

Lin Mo touched his throat. “Sample's still playing.”

“Appearance adjustments do not affect playback.”

“Rules said performance appearance. Appearance is what you see. Voice counts?”

“Performance appearance includes audible presentation.”

“Where's that written?”

No answer. Approval reached 57%.

“I'm requesting verification of that rule. Not fitting.”

The box grayed out.

> Objection under review. Execution deferred.

“One minute left!” Sun Ning called. “Film hasn't stopped!”

He turned back. His sister was speaking; the review alert drowned out the end. Could it rewind? Samples played only once.

The gray box brightened.

“Objection rejected. Appearance is interpreted to include this session's audible performance.”

“You interpret your own rules?”

The votes climbed without an answer. Wang Hai stood, his chair back jolting backward. Sun Ning told him to sit. A light outside the circle turned toward him, catching half his face.

“Stay there,” Lin Mo said. “Write what you hear.”

His sister sipped water and coughed into the back of her hand. When it passed, she asked, “Finish this, and he can come in a little later?”

“Associated person confirmed. Proceed to the agreement.”

“I'm asking whether he can—”

Voting closed. 62%.

His throat tightened. His own gasp drowned out her question. He swallowed against something hard. The shortened hand braced his neck, feeling tiny movements beneath the skin. More laughter behind him. Someone urged him to say something.

Wang Hai spoke, indistinctly. Lin Mo tried to tell him not to stand, but the first word came out thinner.

He closed his mouth, tongue pressed to his palate. He could breathe. Heat climbed his throat with each breath.

“Say something.”

“Had plenty of questions a minute ago.”

He searched for the account number. Chat obscured its final characters. He checked them mentally against the sequence he'd recorded before handing Wang Hai his booklet.

The last letter emerged. He traced it in the air. Wang Hai touched his pen to the end of the entry and nodded twice.

His sister picked up the phone. The image swept past crumpled paper and a pen by the head of the bed. She left the pen alone and turned the camera toward the window.

“Wait. I'll send him a message.”

Its contents stayed unseen. She protected the IV line with her left hand and tapped with her right thumb, then propped the phone against the bed rail again.

“Three forty!” Wang Hai shouted.

Sun Ning pointed. “Stopped! You can come out!”

Sample complete. The circle turned green. Lin Mo stepped backward, knees weakening. Wang Hai was waiting between the seats and caught his elbow.

“Sit first.”

Lin Mo shook his head and pointed to the booklet. Wang Hai showed his own notes, covering the circles above to expose the last line: She said Lin Mo was her older brother.

Lin Mo tried to add something. The pen shook too much.

“I'll do it,” Wang Hai said. “Tell me.”

“Record the voice too.”

Higher now, unsteady at the end. Wang Hai looked up. His pen bled a dot into the page.

“Was fine a minute ago.”

“Keep that version. Don't write as if I've always sounded like this.”

Wang Hai opened Lin Mo's booklet and circled “Voice unchanged.”

“Don't know the proper terms. I'll write what I heard.”

In his own book: Original voice. Low. Spoke slowly. Dropped at the end of sentences. Like Old Li in our workshop.

“Who's Old Li?”

“Forklift driver. Still remember his name myself.”

Beneath it: Voice higher after screening. He left the first line untouched.

Lin Mo tried clearing his throat. It sounded sharper. He stopped.

“Can you speak?”

“Yes.”

“Then stop testing it. Hurts more.”

Lin Mo retrieved his booklet and pushed the other back. “Don't let it scan that page.”

Wang Hai tucked it inside his clothes. Sun Ning brought Lin Mo's water from beneath his chair. He took a small sip; swallowing still hurt.

He looked up at the screen.

> Physical modification: 19%
> Cognitive overwrite: 24%
> Account status: Canceled.

He stared, crushing the bottle noisily. Wang Hai patted his hidden booklet.

“I heard what she said too.”

“I know.”

He remembered the last message she’d sent before she disappeared: eat before coming. He watched where the image had vanished without writing anything. The recording had shown her sending a message, not its contents.

Sun Ning steadied his bottle. “People can leave recordings after their accounts close.”

He nodded. “Exit” and “Continue” lit on the desk. In the next preview his sister held the pen, her cup weighting the paper.

Protection payment agreement.

“Why was her account closed?”

Small text had only reached “Article Seven” when another voting box appeared.

> Next sample: Memory synchronization.

A comment slid across the preview.

> make him look like someone she'd recognize

Lin Mo reached out. Wang Hai pressed his sleeve down.

“This time, don't trust it yet.”
