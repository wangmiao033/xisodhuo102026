# Chapter 26: Words She Never Said

Lin Mo lifted the curtain's corner without pushing through.

Xiao Lu sat behind a low table, her script open on her left, a sheet of paper held in her ticket clip. Before her stood a man in a brown shirt, stubble still rough on his cheeks. He was taking one hand from his pocket. The other gripped his collar.

“I didn't tell you to undress,” Xiao Lu said.

The speaker by the door answered, louder than her.

“Just give him the clothes.”

The man looked at her, his hand pausing at the first button. Someone beside the stage laughed and told him to get on with it. The audience waiting behind him edged forward.

Xiao Lu set down the clip and pointed toward the door.

“Turn that off. That's not my recording.”

Someone pushed the yellow plug deeper into its socket. Instead of switching it off, the trolley attendant turned the speaker toward the audience. Another thin wire connected to the microphone at Xiao Lu's collar. The white box was lit; its light moved only when she spoke.

Wang Hai hooked the curtain aside and pointed at Lin Mo's feet.

A white line led from the entrance to the accompanying performers' seats. Lin Mo stopped outside it, the chair leg upright beside his leg. He didn't charge the table with it.

The man released his collar. Xiao Lu pushed a shallow dish of change toward him. He reached, and another line came from the recording.

“A little farther in. Keep your hand flat.”

Xiao Lu's lips hadn't moved. She glanced at the script on her left and snatched the dish toward herself.

“Take your hand away.”

The man's cuff moved with it. Lin Mo saw a sliver of metal turn outward beneath the table's edge. Xiao Lu stood, reaching toward it with her clip.

“Don't touch it!” Lin Mo shouted.

Her hand stopped. Hearing him, the man jerked his arm backward. His sleeve stretched tight; something popped beneath the fabric. He stumbled half a step, his wrist still caught.

“Who told you to pull?” Xiao Lu's voice rose sharply.

The man tried to retreat again. The table lurched toward him, its legs screeching. Xiao Lu braced a knee against one leg without grabbing his arm. Her script slid off the edge. She didn't retrieve it, only blocked it with her toe.

Lin Mo saw the thin sliver protruding from its pages. His mouth opened. He wanted to tell her to look after her own things first. But she'd already planted her foot on the script, her eyes never leaving the man's wrist.

“Where else is it caught? Talk to me.”

He looked down, fumbling at his cuff with his other hand.

“It's only the fabric. Don't put your other hand in too.”

He stopped and held that hand against his chest, breathing in broken gasps.

Lin Mo moved toward the line. Wang Hai blocked the chair leg. Their shoulders collided; Lin Mo sucked in a breath of pain and stopped at the edge.

Xiao Lu slid her ticket clip under the dish's rim, supporting the trapped sleeve between its jaws.

“Push it back a little. Just a little, so I can lift the fabric.”

The man shook his head, lips pale. The speaker kept telling him to flatten his hand, drowning her words in its own.

Lin Mo turned to pull the yellow plug. The attendant covered the socket with an elbow. A nearby screen lit up, showing Lin Mo's reaching hand.

Xiao Lu called his name.

“I heard you. Can you let me finish now?”

He turned. She held the clip beside the taut sleeve, waiting.

“Go ahead.”

“Block that speaker. Stop shouting at me.”

The attendant still guarded the plug, but the speaker stood on a small stand outside, facing the front row. Lin Mo stepped in front of it and cupped his jacket's lower hem over the grille. Cloth couldn't silence it. The order to move farther in still hummed through, considerably muffled.

Wang Hai came beside him and turned the stand halfway around with his right hand, pointing the speaker at the curtain. The attendant grabbed its edge. Wang Hai let go, staying on the outer side without pulling against him.

“She said block the sound. You want it talking to the wall, or to you?”

Pinned beside the curtain, the attendant couldn't stretch far enough to turn it back.

At last Xiao Lu could be heard.

“Look at me. Push forward and the sleeve loosens. I'm only clipping the fabric.”

Watching her, the man eased his arm forward half an inch. She lifted the clip. His torn cuff came free of the shallow groove, trailing a long thread. Changing hands to pinch the cloth, she waited until his wrist cleared the table before releasing the jaws.

He stood shaking, clutching his arm. A patch on the back of his hand was scraped red, but there was no blood. A triangle was missing from his cuff.

Someone applauded.

“Do it again. I missed it.”

Xiao Lu turned the dish facedown on the table. Coins fell and spun by her feet. She left them there.

“You can look at his sleeve.”

Lin Mo released his jacket. The voice by the door became clearer at once. Xiao Lu's mouth was closed. The speaker kept saying, “Keep your hand flat.”

The man pointed at her.

“You're still telling me to do it?”

“She didn't say that,” Lin Mo said.

“I'm not deaf.”

Lin Mo moved aside, showing him the speaker facing the curtain. Yellow tape showed around the plug. Its wire hung from the socket to the floor.

“Watch her. Wait for her to speak.”

But the man stared at his sleeve, eyes reddening.

“That's what she said before.”

Lin Mo didn't argue about belief. He set the chair leg beside his feet and raised both empty hands, showing he wouldn't touch the table. Xiao Lu glanced at him and nodded toward the microphone.

“I'm going to try something. Don't shout over me.”

She tapped the table twice.

The white light flashed with each tap. No tapping came from the speaker, only the end of the instruction to flatten his hand.

Xiao Lu tapped once more, closed her mouth and raised the ticket clip beside her chin, where the camera could see her lips clearly.

“Put your change away,” said the speaker.

The man looked up. Her mouth stayed still, the clip beside it. His gaze moved from her lips to the doorway and back. His grip on his arm loosened a little.

“Is that from earlier?”

“It isn't what she's saying now,” Lin Mo answered.

Someone in the audience called them a good double act. Someone else said they should at least get the lip-sync right. Two lines scrolled across the screen before the stage.

> Then turn up the live sound.

> Thought this was live. Can they borrow the cashier's mouth too?

The attendant suddenly swung the stand back, aiming the speaker at the front row. Wang Hai had already released it and didn't grab again. Its voice swallowed the click of Xiao Lu lowering her clip. The audience called for her to do something besides keep her mouth shut.

She lifted the microphone half an inch from her collar. Rather than compete with the speaker, she waited until it finished telling people to put their change away.

“This next sentence is me speaking now. Don't reach in.”

The white box lit. Lin Mo watched the yellow connection. It flashed too, a moment later. Immediately the speaker delivered a clear, crisp command.

“Reach in.”

The first word was gone.

Laughter broke from the front row. Xiao Lu set the microphone down with a thud.

“You won't even leave me that much.”

She stopped addressing it. Instead, she faced the man and shook her head. He tucked his other hand against his chest too.

Lin Mo stepped away from the speaker. The evidence was there. Shouting again would only give them another voice to cut up.

Xiao Lu put down the clip and retrieved her script. A corner of the sliver showed between the pages. She covered it with her thumb, keeping it from the camera.

“This is the only recording I need for this performance. Who added the next line?”

The host gave no name, only told her to put the dish back and complete the handover according to the continuation prompts.

Xiao Lu left it overturned.

“Why does this dish trap people?”

“Please complete the actions for this performance.”

The man stepped back onto a coin that was still wobbling. He bent to pick it up, then snatched his reaching hand against his chest again. Lin Mo leaned forward. Across the white line, the coin lay beside the man's shoe. He didn't pick it up for him.

Xiao Lu closed her script and stepped behind her chair.

“He can leave.”

A staff member beside the stage raised a hand, still indicating the undelivered change. The man looked at Lin Mo, waiting, it seemed, for someone to tell him whether to go. Lin Mo pointed to a clear stretch beyond the table.

“No line there. Go if you want. Don't let me choose for you.”

The man retreated along it without looking at the dish. He pinched the sides of his torn sleeve together. A piece was missing; however he gathered the cloth, the hole wouldn't close. Finally, he rolled the cuff over the scrape on his hand and disappeared into the crowd on the other side.

Xiao Lu pushed her chair aside. The host ordered her back. Her performance hadn't been settled.

“I haven't finished,” she said. “But I won't sit there for that voice.”

She approached the side exit. One of its white lights went out. A staff member pushed back a chair provided for departing performers, blocking her way.

Lin Mo's hand moved, then stopped beside his jacket. He looked at her and waited.

She pointed toward the narrow waiting entrance on the other side.

“I'm going there. Taking my script.”

The staff member shifted half a step. Xiao Lu slipped past sideways, clutching script and clip against her chest. Her result on the screen remained blank. Beneath it were the words “Not settled.” A light for resuming the performance came on behind the table. It didn't follow her out.

She paused at the exit, looking back. Her chair was still there. A staff member spread spare script pages at her former place and tapped an empty response field.

“Come back and add one line. Then we can settle it.”

She stayed. Lin Mo thought she was waiting for him to say don't go back. He gripped his jacket and said nothing.

“What line?” she asked.

The staff member switched on a small screen beside the table. It showed her getting up, frozen with her mouth open. Beneath the image were the words “Voluntary continuation.”

“I said take your hand away.”

The staff member tilted the screen down and pointed to her script.

“You can start again from the original.”

Xiao Lu opened the book, pressing her sliver deeper between the pages. She looked for a moment, then closed it against her chest.

“Next time, I'll speak for myself.”

She turned into the waiting entrance. Behind her, the empty chair scraped back toward the table. Only then did Lin Mo follow.

The white line turned at the entrance, separating him from the space inside. Xiao Lu didn't open the door for him. She leaned against the wall, scraping a curled page corner with her clip.

“Why did you keep telling me not to touch it?”

“I saw the metal slot.”

“So did I. I was right behind it.”

He looked at her hands. A brown fiber from the man's sleeve clung to the clip's tip.

“I thought you were going to pull with your hand.”

“You didn't ask.”

Lin Mo propped the chair leg against the wall and began to crouch beside it, then stood again. His knees ached. His soles felt gritty, and stopping only made his balance harder to find.

Wang Hai pushed a low stool up from behind and left it outside the line.

“One of you sit down. With both of you standing, turning my head back and forth is more work than performing.”

Xiao Lu stayed standing. When Lin Mo sat, her gaze fell on the broken casing Wang Hai took from his pocket.

“Where did you get that?”

Lin Mo told her about the rollers beneath the shutter, the broken sliver and the yellow connector. He described only what he'd seen. When he reached the window attendant's escape, he stopped, without claiming that person was behind everything.

“We couldn't save the whole recording. All it plays is those few words.”

“Who said them?”

“I don't know.”

“Did you follow it here because it had my channel number, or because you thought it was me?”

Wang Hai pocketed the casing again. Lin Mo looked up. She was holding her script tighter. The hand she'd extended toward the box had drawn back.

He didn't answer immediately. Another voice onstage told someone to take their change. Fresh footsteps approached the low table.

“Both,” he said.

Her mouth moved, without a smile.

“Was that man someone you're looking for too?”

“No.”

“Then you could have listened to how I was helping him.”

Lin Mo rested his right hand palm-up on his knee, the white scar beside his cuff. Her eyes paused there, then returned to his face.

“Anything else you want to ask?”

“Yes.”

“You can ask. But let me ask you things too.”
