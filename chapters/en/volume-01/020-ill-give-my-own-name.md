# Chapter 20: I'll Give My Own Name

Xiao Lu supported Lin Mo's left forearm as the bottle landed on the stage edge. He reached with his other hand. Cloth brushed the scar on his right wrist, and his fingers recoiled.

She stood the bottle upright before letting go.

“Your arm hurts too?”

“Twisted it in a handshake.”

“Whose hand were you shaking?”

Lin Mo didn't look toward the audience. He took the bottle in his left hand, gripping the middle this time.

“The man who had this index.”

She saw the paper corner protruding from his inner pocket but didn't ask for it.

“What did he make you trade?”

“I didn't give him the jacket.”

She nodded and wiped her badge. The paper beneath was damp, but Xiao Lu remained legible. She removed the clip and tried twice to peel the paper from its plastic. When Lin Mo offered to support it, she drew it back.

“Wait. Don't touch the writing.”

She smoothed its curled corners with her fingertips and clipped it to her collar again.

From across the white lane, Wang Hai told him to finish drinking before he talked. He didn't answer for him. Lin Mo took two swallows, returned the water, and looked at the invitation button.

“I want to ask you to perform with me.”

The cap tilted in Xiao Lu's fingers.

“Perform what?”

“An outerwear demonstration. That's the invitation this program offers.”

“Wanting a particular show doesn't mean you get to put me in it.”

“I know.”

He left the button alone. She straightened the cap, set the bottle by her feet, and held out a hand.

“Let me see.”

He turned the page toward her. Only extensions of the current program were available. The item would be displayed between two performers, then returned. Its custodian had to submit it; neither performer could transfer someone else's ownership.

She pointed at the return condition. “Do we have to show the lining?”

The host said the custodian could limit the display's scope. The audience could still request an appearance vote.

Lin Mo drew his gray sleeve forward, showing the patch.

“Just this outside part. No opening the pockets.”

“Private things stay inside?”

He looked up. Her eyes moved from the bulge in the cloth to the registration page.

“If there are personal things in the lining, don't take them out for the audience to count. I've closed shows like this before.”

He moved his hand away from the pocket. She pushed the page back. The half-tablet still lay against him through the gray fabric.

Wang Hai set down his water and nodded without smiling or urging him to ask anything.

“Only the front of the patch,” Lin Mo told the host.

The woman asked them both to confirm. Xiao Lu didn't sign. Turning the sheet over, she found another blank field.

> Relationship between performers.

“Why is this empty?”

“Performers may state their relationship.”

“Will you fill it in if we don't?”

“Incomplete entries cannot be registered.”

She put the pen down. “What were you going to write?”

Lin Mo held his cuff, thumb on the join between white and blue thread. Two-person livestream access remained unavailable. This project registered only one shared performance, but every backstage camera faced them.

“Collaborators for this performance.”

She pointed at personal confirmation in the heading and picked up the pen.

“Have it printed first. No signing blanks.”

He asked the host to complete the field. The reprinted page said only collaborators for this performance, with no statement about their original account identities. Each confirmed their own side. Neither supplied the other's name.

When she returned it, Lin Mo caught the edge right-handed. His wrist tightened; switching hands let the paper slip. Xiao Lu caught one corner.

“Stop switching back and forth. Which side hurts?”

“Right hurts. Left aches.”

“Then give yourself somewhere to put things.”

She pointed to an empty basket near the crossbar. Before the show began, he could set it inside his mark.

“Part of the display?” he asked.

She shook her head. It was labeled for safekeeping, not a prop for the audience to choose.

“Got two?” Wang Hai asked.

She glanced at his bandaged left hand and pointed behind his seat. He'd been sitting in front of one all along.

“Thought it was a wastebasket.”

“Going to put your factory badge in it, then?”

“I didn't. I'm looking after it.”

Lin Mo put the cooperation page in his basket. Wang Hai moved his water behind his chair, so the cameras couldn't crop either bottle into a shot at will. He finally sat, then looked away as Lin Mo prepared to remove the jacket, checking whether his chair crossed the white frame.

The shared performance began. A bar stood between their marks. Lin Mo eased his left arm out. Padding made the right sleeve harder; Xiao Lu supported the hem while he withdrew slowly.

He untied the broad strip and placed it in his own basket. He left everything in the jacket pockets, including the shortened wood. He kept its screw clear of the bar.

With the jacket hung up, he stood exposed in the blue top, skirt, and buckled shoes. He was becoming used to the heels, though his knees still tried to brace outward as before.

Xiao Lu came along the other end of the bar without putting the jacket against herself. The backs of her fingers touched the patch. The white thread was shorter than the blue; a little knot remained at their join.

Lin Mo waited for her to speak.

She turned the cuff toward the camera. “Here?”

“Here.”

She didn't ask who'd sewn it. The host requested one detail from the custodian. Lin Mo drew the microphone close enough to spare his throat.

“The thread ran out. Had to switch. The last few stitches stayed.”

> who sewed it?

> let the other performer try it on

> how many shows has that old thing survived?

He didn't answer the first. Xiao Lu didn't read it aloud. She kept the patch outside and the lining hidden, her other hand holding the pocket below the bar.

“Trying it on wasn't submitted.”

An appearance-vote request had already lit up: change Lin Mo's short hair to shoulder length. The reason given was that the new face should match the host.

> Executes at 60% approval.
> Applies only to the selected performer's appearance. Item alterations excluded.
> Display continues during execution.

Lin Mo pointed to the exclusion.

“You can't touch this jacket. Or count what's inside it.”

“Items in custody are outside the scope of appearance adjustment.”

“Keep that in the caption.”

The host put the restriction beside the request. Someone said he was better at finding his place than the props. Another asked to change Xiao Lu's name too. That request was immediately rejected: she wasn't the selected adjustment subject.

She looked up without smiling and continued supporting the sleeve.

Lin Mo drew the jacket toward himself. The audience demanded he let go, but if the modification loosened his grip, the garment could fall into the transfer frame on the other side.

“Even your hair's mine to vote on,” someone said.

“Vote on my hair. Don't change my hands while you're at it.”

Xiao Lu raised an unused clip on the bar and fastened the collar. Only when the jacket stopped slipping did he loosen his fingers a little, keeping his wrist on his own side.

> Approved: 62%.

His scalp prickled. He raised his left hand, but before it reached behind his ear, hair brushed his palm and fell against the blue collar.

He lowered his hand. Fine strands slid along his neck with each breath, covering the nape that had been bare. Hair softened his temples, narrowed the face in the camera, and slipped along his jaw to his shoulders.

Wang Hai stood, then sat again, chair legs knocking. He didn't call for Lin Mo to look at him. He only said the jacket was still clipped in place; there was no need to look down for it.

Lin Mo pushed hair from his eyes. Another lock fell forward. With his right hand guarding the garment, he couldn't arrange both sides. Xiao Lu set an empty prop clip within reach on the bar.

“Back there. Not against your scalp.”

He clipped one side, catching a small tuft painfully. Hair slipped inside his collar. He hunched; his left arm knocked the bar and the jacket slid a little.

Xiao Lu supported the sleeve without reaching for his hair.

“It's caught in your collar.”

He reached behind his neck. Afraid to yank, he worked his fingers from the roots down and eased the strands free. He used to reach his nape in one motion. Now he couldn't even wipe the sweat off properly.

Wang Hai told him to keep hold of the jacket, that the collar clip would hold. Xiao Lu shook her head toward it.

“It's holding the clothes. Don't hang your arm off it as well.”

Lin Mo shifted his right wrist, lifting the skin beneath the scar from the hard edge. He released the hair clip and refastened it. The light stopped breaking into pieces behind the loose strands.

Then a rehearsal appeared onscreen. Xiao Lu held the jacket, smiling, inviting him to try it. He stood beside her in blue, hair just as long as it was now. As her recorded hand lifted the sleeve, his own fingers followed, nearly delivering the patch into her palm.

“She didn't wear it,” Wang Hai said.

Lin Mo stopped midway across the bar. He turned toward Wang Hai on his left, drawing the patch half an inch with him. Xiao Lu still stood at the far end, supporting the sleeve without putting it on.

“I know.”

The onscreen Xiao Lu asked again. His throat moved as if he'd already answered. He opened his mouth and met another I know, coming from the screen. He lowered his head, afraid to keep speaking.

Gripping the bar, he counted the final stitches. Off-white first, pale blue after. When the recorded sleeve turned in her hands, the order was reversed.

He flattened the real cuff with his left hand and stopped pushing it toward her.

Xiao Lu saw his trembling fingers and withdrew her palm.

“You don't have to hand it over now. Collect it at the end.”

Her voice came from the microphone in front of him while the recorded question continued. He pointed to the replay. The woman announced synchronized recording; she did not remove the new false memory.

> Physical modification: 44%.
> Cognitive overwrite: 40%.

His toes pressed the edge of his mark. Holding the cuff, he watched his recorded hand rise without following. Xiao Lu supported the other side so the jacket's weight wouldn't hang from his injured wrist.

At the end of the display time, the return light came on. She released the collar clip and slid the jacket along the bar. Only after he took it himself did the settlement tone sound.

Her signature appeared opposite his on the cooperation page. He had the shared performance record. The actual two-person livestream still showed channel confirmation pending.

“This says we performed together,” Xiao Lu said. “It doesn't let you summon whoever you want.”

“I didn't let it confirm for you.”

“Good.”

She retrieved the prop clip. Hair fell beside his face. Putting the gray jacket back on meant drawing the strands out of the collar. He retied the broad strip. The shoes still needed returning; Wang Hai waited by the exit line.

Lin Mo put away the record and opened the formal invitation. He didn't reach a pen toward the empty relationship field. He showed her the limit: collaboration for this performance.

“I'd like the next one with you, too. I won't choose your name. I won't let them take a line of dialogue and say you agreed.”

She paused, looking at the audience's lit voting boards. Some called for her to accept; others complained about all the standing around. She turned down her microphone, keeping their voices beyond the stage.

“Script first.”

He turned the application to the project details. Beneath Two-person Livestream, only his own name was filled in. She reached for a pen without signing her consent.

He moved the pen away from the empty box, still holding the paper between them.

Xiao Lu laid it beside the sheet instead of writing in her field.

“I'll give my own name,” she said. “Leave my box alone.”
