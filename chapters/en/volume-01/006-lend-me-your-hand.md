# Chapter 6: Lend Me Your Hand

Wang Hai recognized the factory badge. He stood and held out his right hand. As his thumb rubbed the blank photograph, Lin Mo unfolded the payment receipt and pointed to the name he'd written. Wang Hai's lips moved.

Lin Mo raised a finger. Wang Hai closed his mouth, nodded, and sat down. More lines appeared beneath the rule on the screen.

> SILENT ROOM
> Entrants: 2
> Return to your seats and await the opening instructions.
> Sounds made before the opening will not count toward this session.

Lin Mo pointed out the last line. Wang Hai shook his head hard. Whatever he'd experienced here, he wasn't risking another word.

Lin Mo sat, lowering the chair leg beside his shoe. An empty chair with a shallow paper tray stood between them. Wang Hai turned the badge over, pointed at the employee number, then at the name on the receipt. He spread his hands.

Lin Mo understood. The name and workplace were there, but Wang Hai couldn't connect them to himself. He folded the receipt to show “Wang Hai” and placed it in the tray. Remembering could wait, provided the name stayed visible.

The white light dimmed. A small green one came on.

> Session begins.
> 1. Deliberate sound is prohibited. Speech, tapping, and clapping count as sound. Normal breathing and clothing friction do not.
> 2. Collect your own settlement record. Do not open another person's record box.
> 3. Verify your identity before collection.
> 4. Once both entrants have successfully collected their records, they may enter the settlement area.

A fifteen-minute countdown started. The opposite wall slid apart, revealing six transparent boxes. Each lid bore a number, with a round opening below. Beneath them stood two card readers with small screens.

Wang Hai caught Lin Mo's jacket and pointed from his mouth to the rules. Lin Mo nodded, patting his hand without a sound.

Wang Hai pocketed the badge and stood. Blood marked the edge of his bandaged left hand; his fingers wouldn't straighten. His loose trousers slipped with his first step. He caught them with his right hand.

Lin Mo produced the belt and held it against his waist. Wang Hai gave him a thumbs-up. Lin Mo crouched to thread it through the loops. At his left side, Wang Hai lifted his arm, caught the bandage against the chair, and winced. Both froze.

Nothing changed onscreen. Lin Mo exhaled, buckled the belt, and tightened it. Wang Hai gripped his shoulder, patted it, then snatched his hand back. The camera swiveled halfway around.

Ordinary movements. Clothing friction. Tapping. Lin Mo pressed his palm against Wang Hai's hand: no more pats. The green light stayed on.

Both readers said “Identity scan.” Lin Mo put down his ID card.

> Real-world identity valid.
> Supply your collection number for this session.

He pulled the handover slip from his gray jacket, dislodging the blister pack. Half a tablet shifted in its plastic pocket. Wang Hai caught it, glanced at it, and handed it back.

Lin Mo returned it to his inside pocket and laid the slip on the reader. The paper overhung the edges. No scanning light moved. A keyboard appeared.

> Enter collection number manually.

The number lay along a crease in the paper. He flattened it. Wang Hai tugged his sleeve. His badge was on the other reader.

> Name missing.
> Provide an associated record.

No keyboard. Removing and replacing the badge made no difference. Wang Hai pointed at Lin Mo's ID. Lin Mo kept his hand on it. Rule three required their own identities; borrowing his would only mix up their records.

Wang Hai withdrew his hand and rubbed the empty photograph. There was nothing to wipe away. Lin Mo set his slip aside and pointed to the narrow barcode on the back of the badge.

Wang Hai flipped it over. The reader scanned.

> Pending collection number found.
> Verify the corresponding record box.

White lights came on at both ends of the row. The last three digits matched; only one character earlier in the numbers differed. Wang Hai started left. Lin Mo stopped him and pointed to rule two.

They compared the numbers. On the reader's thumbnail image, the faint character beneath the barcode was a circle with a line through it, followed by letters and digits. Lin Mo took it for a zero, like the one on the left-hand box.

Wang Hai studied the right-hand box, compared his badge twice, then drew a circle on his bandaged palm. Lin Mo pointed left. Wang Hai shook his head and added a vertical stroke. Lin Mo pointed right. Another shake.

Lin Mo drew a zero in the air. Wang Hai nodded, then immediately shook his head, frustration returning. He drew breath to speak.

Lin Mo caught his arm. Wang Hai let the breath out slowly and tried again: a rectangle drawn with two fingers, a gesture at the scanning light, then the blurred character.

Beneath the image was a small button: “Original.”

Lin Mo pressed it. The enlarged image filled the screen. The line through the circle ran down the entire image. A scratch on the scanner glass.

He shifted the badge and scanned again. The scratch stayed put; the character moved. A capital O.

Wang Hai pointed, eyebrows raised, and gave himself a thumbs-up. Lin Mo nearly laughed aloud. He swallowed it and returned the gesture.

The left box had a zero. The right had an O. Wang Hai held the barcode toward the right-hand lid's round opening. White turned green.

> Owner's identity verified.

He waited before opening it. Lin Mo signaled for another moment and returned to his reader. He entered his creased collection number section by section. A box in the middle lit up.

Only at Lin Mo's nod did Wang Hai lift his lid. A thin envelope lay inside. Lin Mo's box held the same, his name on the front, a blue stamp at the lower left.

He took it out and felt a damp patch. The cloth padding his cuff had slipped. His injured right wrist scraped the clear rim, and he almost jerked his hand away.

Wang Hai pressed it down first. His bandaged hand came forward too, its edge brushing the injury.

Lin Mo clenched his teeth and withdrew his hand slowly. Wang Hai stepped back in alarm.

> Identifying sound.

Both went still. Blood tasted metallic in Lin Mo's mouth. He'd bitten the split inside his lip. Had a sound escaped his throat? The overhead scanning light swung toward him.

Wang Hai clutched his envelope. The red text remained for several seconds.

> Classification: Normal breathing.
> Continue collection.

Lin Mo finished breathing out and leaned against the wall. Wang Hai pointed at himself, then the wrist, and raised his palm. Lin Mo squeezed his uninjured hand. It wasn't his fault.

He replaced the cloth and gestured toward the chairs. Both boxes were empty, but the timer still ran. Eight minutes. Successful collection meant more than taking an envelope.

They set their envelopes in the tray. The camera tilted down. Wang Hai tugged at his seal, failed, and asked for help with a gesture.

Lin Mo reached halfway, then remembered rule two. Did an envelope count as a record box? Each had an identical clear seal with a black square and no name. His own felt unusually thick, like hard plastic. Another kind of lid.

He withdrew his hand and pointed at the rule.

Wang Hai took his envelope back. His left hand wouldn't open; his right had to hold the paper. After two attempts, he'd only torn the corner. His grip loosened, and the envelope fell to the floor. Lin Mo picked it up and returned it to the tray without touching the seal.

Wang Hai stared at him.

Lin Mo laid his own envelope beside Wang Hai's right hand. Then he pinned the lower half of Wang Hai's envelope in the tray and mimed lifting with his fingers. Wang Hai reached for the seal.

Lin Mo shook his head, pointed to where he was holding it, and repeated the gesture. He could hold the envelope steady. Its owner had to open it. Wang Hai frowned, considered, and nodded.

He peeled up one corner. Lin Mo kept his fingers still. When the envelope lifted a few millimeters, he relaxed his wrist and let it settle. The seal came halfway off.

The camera stopped overhead. Lin Mo watched Wang Hai's hand. The rest peeled free without an alert. Wang Hai pushed the envelope open with his thumb and removed a sheet.

Lin Mo let go. The camera turned away.

> Collection complete: 1/2.

Wang Hai laid the sheet on his lap and sagged against the chair. He opened his mouth, then shut it quickly. His turn to hold Lin Mo's envelope.

Lin Mo indicated the good hand and left his own right wrist alone. With his left he picked at the seal. What had looked easy was suddenly too slippery to grip. Wang Hai glanced between his hands. He didn't hurry him.

Lin Mo folded the corner up, trapped it between his left fingers, and slowly closed them. The adhesive lifted a thin white layer from the paper. He paused, eased the pressure, and peeled away the last half.

He withdrew the sheet.

> Collection complete: 2/2.

The countdown stopped with a little over four minutes left. The exit stayed shut.

His record was headed “Fitting Settlement.” Physical modification: 14%. Cognitive overwrite: 8%. The same as the previous session.

Two boxes appeared below. “Original identity retained” was checked. Beside it, “Guarantee valid” was gray.

He touched the gray box. Nothing changed. He held it nearer the light. If the guarantee was valid, why wasn't it checked? On the back:

> Read associated information in the settlement area.

Wang Hai looked worse than before. At Lin Mo's gesture he handed over his record, keeping a finger on the bottom field. There was no name, only a number and “Name awaiting collection.”

The bottom field wasn't blank.

> Collection location: Mirror Corridor 08.

Wang Hai pointed back the way they'd come. Lin Mo shook his head. The bucket, the note, the submerged hand. They'd both seen them. Now an official record was sending him back there.

Lin Mo returned the sheet, unfolded the receipt, and pointed from “Wang Hai” to the new number. Keep both. They weren't through this session yet.

The screen chimed softly.

> Verification complete.
> Select one settlement representative.

Two options: Lin Mo's name and Wang Hai's number. Lin Mo raised a hand, then stopped as more text appeared.

> The representative may enter the settlement area first.
> The other entrant must remain here until settlement is complete.

Wang Hai pointed to Lin Mo. He folded his record into its envelope and moved to a chair farther from the exit. Lin Mo didn't select anything. Wang Hai pointed again, urging him on.

Lin Mo laid the receipt on Wang Hai's lap, with the name facing up. He pointed to the door, then to both of them, and held up two fingers.

Wang Hai watched him and shook his head.

Lin Mo went to the screen. He unfolded the handover slip, meaning to write something, but there was no pen beside the tray. He put it away and selected his own name with his left hand.

The lock on the right released.

> The sound prohibition remains in effect until the representative enters the settlement area.

He looked back at Wang Hai and pressed his left palm downward twice. Wait for me.

Wang Hai nodded, holding the receipt against his record.

Lin Mo picked up the chair leg and stepped through. A printed application lay on a narrow table. The applicant's name was already filled in: Lin Mo.

The request contained one sentence.

> I apply to abandon my companion and claim the individual settlement reward.
