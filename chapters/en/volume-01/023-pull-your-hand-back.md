# Chapter 23: Pull Your Hand Back

“Yes,” Lin Mo said. “For the receipt.”

Xiao Lu's hand remained at the edge of her mark, palm down.

“After you handed over the money?”

“The change came out. The paper was still inside.”

“How did you take it?”

He reached into his pocket, stopping when his fingers touched paper. Beside the microphone, he turned his empty hand palm up to show her. Bending his right wrist pulled the white scar taut. His fingers wouldn't straighten.

Onscreen, the patient reached from beneath the blanket. The bedside receipt slipped down to his fingertips. Lin Mo lowered his right hand and gripped the cloth tie with his left.

“I wasn't injured then.”

Xiao Lu withdrew her hand and pulled her script halfway from its clip.

“In my performance, the paper was held like this.”

She pressed it with her forefinger, thumb hidden behind the stand. He bent toward the clip, his hair sweeping the page. From below, he could see her fingers. When he raised his face, only the back of her hand remained.

“How do you take it while it’s held down?”

“They let go. You have to wait for the person inside to release it.”

The masked spectator tapped his board against his knee.

“Show him. Reach in and find out.”

Lin Mo stopped his left hand between the microphones without crossing his mark. Xiao Lu drew the script toward her. It crumpled against the clip.

“This is my script.”

> you've reached out. don't stop now
> all those answers and he can't manage the hand part
> she said wait for them to let go. what's the rush?

“One twenty,” Wang Hai called.

Lin Mo withdrew his empty hand. “When you held the paper, did the people outside say anything?”

“Some did.”

“Ask you to look up?”

She shook her head. Her finger left the page, which stayed caught in the clip.

“Told me to hurry.”

His left shoulder twitched. The queue at the payment window. Someone pressing his backpack from behind; he'd moved forward, pushing money through the narrow slot. The person inside hadn't looked up. He hadn't waited. He'd turned away with the change, then returned for the receipt.

He lowered his hand without claiming that memory belonged to her performance.

“I told them to hurry too.”

“Then left?”

“A couple of steps. Came back. Forgot the receipt.”

She eased her script from the clip and smoothed its creased corner. Her nail scraped the fold.

He wanted to ask whether she'd been that person. The microphone touched his lower lip. He pushed it down a little and changed the question.

“Did anyone ever ask you to look up?”

“No.”

“In any performance?”

She gripped the sheet. Chairs scraped behind them. Someone stood and shouted for her to check the script. She folded the answer column inward.

“None I remember.”

“Forty seconds,” Wang Hai said.

Lin Mo studied her face. Her eyebrow's outer tip showed clearly beneath the light, making the name forming in his mouth harder to swallow. She held the script against her leg and pointed to his pocket.

“My turn. Do you still want to take that paper out?”

His fingers touched the opening without entering.

“No.”

“You kept wanting to give it to me.”

“I wanted you to recognize the writing.”

“And if I did, you'd recognize me?”

Wang Hai half raised his right hand from the timer sheet, then rested it on the chair back. Lin Mo withdrew his hand too. The cloth he'd tugged up exposed his blue collar.

“I'd still have to ask.”

She clipped her script back without offering its edge to him.

“Where's your next performance?” he asked.

“Backstage. The window.”

“Can I come?”

She tilted her head toward the curtain's left edge.

“Outside the yellow line. Don't follow me inside.”

“Five seconds.” Wang Hai stood.

The woman told them to confirm their answers. As Lin Mo raised his hand, someone called for him to hold up his receipt for a photo and for Xiao Lu to stand beside him.

He pressed his column. She touched hers. The space between them stayed open. The timer reached zero; their breathing cut out of the microphones.

The masked spectator's board remained raised. When its red light died, he scraped a corner against the armrest.

Lin Mo stepped off his mark. His ankle buckled. The onscreen bed was gone, but his knees still wanted to sit on its edge. His socks slipped; his shoulder struck the stand. Wang Hai reached from outside the companion line, caught his upper arm through the jacket and brought him to the stage edge.

“No bed here.”

Lin Mo gripped his sleeve, cloth rubbing his injured wrist. Wang Hai lowered his arm and waited until both feet were steady before helping him toward a stool.

Leaving by the other side, Xiao Lu paused.

“Wait outside the yellow line. Even if someone comes out, don't go in.”

He nodded. She lifted the curtain and disappeared. Her footsteps continued beyond it, fading.

Wang Hai nudged the stool closer. Lin Mo sat and loosened the socks pulling beneath his knees. He almost asked Wang Hai to raise the foot of the bed. The stool legs scraped; he shut his mouth.

“What were you going to say?”

“Nearly asked you to crank the bed.”

“I can crank machinery. Never learned beds.”

He shook the sleeve Lin Mo still gripped.

“Pull your hand back. My sleeve won't survive another round.”

Lin Mo let go. The piece of wood slipped inside his jacket; he clamped his left elbow to the pocket until it settled. Wang Hai crouched and picked up the fallen water bottle, keeping his bandaged left hand off the floor.

“Drink?”

“The window.”

“The water won't take your place.”

“Once her next performance starts, I can't ask.”

“Ask what?”

“About whoever held down the paper.”

Wang Hai held the cap without turning it.

“You asked. She said it was a performance.”

“I want to see the desk. At the hospital it was so low I couldn't even tell if it was a man or woman.”

“Going to duck down and take the paper again?”

His reaching hand stopped beside the bottle.

“This time I'll ask them to look up.”

“And if they don't?”

“I won't reach in.”

Wang Hai put the bottle in his hand, supporting its base.

“Don't forget that when you get there. If you hear me calling from outside, answer. Don't make me crawl in looking.”

“Don't reach in.”

“My left hand's wrapped like this and you're worrying about the right? I'll watch the time and whether anyone comes out. You ask the questions.”

Lin Mo steadied the bottle. Water touched his lower lip, but he remembered a straw beside the bed and didn't tilt it. Wang Hai tapped the bottom. He drank, choked, and hunched into his jacket.

Sun Ning called from the doorway. “You were standing when you went in. You were still on your feet when she mentioned the payment.”

He returned the bottle and looked over. She stayed seated, left hand on the sealed bag, right arm against her waist.

“I remember.”

“You didn't just now. You were waiting for someone to tip the bottle for you.”

“Don't look at me,” Wang Hai said. “He drank it himself.”

Wang Hai capped the bottle and tucked it beside his pocket, its base protruding.

Lin Mo raised his left hand to his chest. In the bed memory, someone had fastened his hospital shirt. A loose buttonhole, a white thread visible when he looked down. His fingers found blue cloth instead. The gray jacket's zipper pressed his hand.

“Did you do up my clothes?” he asked Wang Hai.

“No. Could I? Your one hand's busier than both of mine.”

He withdrew his fingers. Wang Hai bent to smooth the jacket's turned-up hem.

“This is me. Doing it now. Don't remember it as something from before.”

Sun Ning pointed left-handed to her collar.

“With that white shirt, I thought the buttons were wrong. Kept looking. Wasn't even wearing it.”

“You remembered afterward?”

“Now you've asked, I want to button it again.”

She returned her hand to the bag. He watched, then pulled his own cuff out of his palm.

Wang Hai steadied the water bottle and made room.

“Don't want to go, we can stay.”

Lin Mo shifted his socked toes, then turned his heel back.

“She's waiting.”

“She said wait outside the yellow line. Didn't say stand.”

He sat a little longer. The microphones retracted. A worker wheeled a cart from the curtain and collected the clipped pages. Xiao Lu's creased script went into a compartment, its corner still curled.

Lin Mo raised a hand. The worker paused.

“Can you keep that for her?”

“She'll come back if she needs it.”

The cart went to the curtain, unlocked. Lin Mo didn't fetch the script for her. Wang Hai didn't search it either. Its wheels crossed the stage opening; Lin Mo's legs tensed as though someone were wheeling his bed away.

Wang Hai set his shoe across the stool's legs.

“Let it go. Your stool hasn't got wheels.”

Lin Mo gripped the seat without following. When the rumbling stopped, he drew his feet away from Wang Hai's shoe.

Sun Ning pushed the chair by the doorway inward to clear the passage. Lin Mo started to rise, but Wang Hai touched his shoulder and pointed at the screen.

The hospital identity's results page still showed his current face. Two answers sat together: his about collecting the receipt, hers about nobody asking her to look up. The space for their names was blank.

> Ward identity complete. Continue at the payment window.

He pushed himself up and approached the screen. Before he could take out the receipt, Wang Hai blocked his pocket.

“Use the cooperation form.”

Lin Mo took out their confirmed cooperation form, unfolded it to the signatures and relationship field, and held it toward the host's pickup microphone.

“Two people finished. You've put her answer under mine.”

The woman said the results display followed the approved hospital identity. He placed the form against the reader without pressing the payment-window continuation button.

“Her answers weren't in the vote. Put the names back.”

The screen paused. The answers separated; Lin Mo and Xiao Lu appeared beside their own. The continuation button stayed lit. He retrieved the form without touching it.

The bed folded into a sidebar with Xiao Lu's answer. His current name hung beneath the bed section, beside an empty collection box. The worker returned with the cart, pulled out a white-and-blue-striped shirt and held it up toward him to judge the fit.

He zipped his jacket over the blue collar.

“The screen used it. I never wore it.”

The worker draped it back over the cart. A sleeve brushed Lin Mo's sock. He stepped away without picking it up.

“That one looks your size,” Wang Hai said. “Move before he measures you again.”

Lin Mo couldn't laugh. He refolded the form along its creases. Wang Hai waited before clearing the way.

Spectators called for him to continue, calling Xiao Lu's name too. Nothing answered behind the curtain. Wang Hai pushed the stool against the wall and followed him along the stage.

Sun Ning moved the bag off her lap. “Coming back?”

“Yes. He's handed in his shoes. Can't run very fast.”

Lin Mo turned, supporting himself on the wall. “Need water? I'll leave this.”

“Still some by my feet. Don't give your paper away.”

He flattened his pocket, freed his skirt from the stool and followed Wang Hai. Sun Ning settled near the open door.

Beyond the curtain, a narrow passage hugged the wall. Xiao Lu's footsteps were gone. A yellow line ran around the wooden scenery frame to a low panel. Lin Mo stayed outside it, gray sock prints marking the tiles.

Below the panel was an opening lower than his waist. Light shone through. A paper corner curled on the desk; a hand pressed it flat.

Wang Hai crouched beside him without crossing the line.

“Where's the face?”

Lin Mo bent his knees, left hand braced on his leg. The person inside lowered their head too. The panel hid their forehead, a mask the rest. Their hand pushed the paper forward, stopping deep inside the opening.

He didn't take it. His fingers left his pocket.

“Xiao Lu?”

The person didn't look up. Two taps on the desk. A photograph slid from beneath the paper: the face on Lin Mo's ID.

Wang Hai caught his sleeve.

From inside, the person spoke in Lin Mo's unmodified voice.

“Give me the receipt. You've got mine.”
