# Chapter 11: Hold My Hand

The paddle brushed the patch. The man drew it back.

“Finished with the old ones? Watch something new.” His voice sounded muffled behind the mask.

“Where?”

He laid the paddle on his lap and pointed at the gray jacket. “Leave that with me.”

Lin Mo withdrew his arm and addressed the ceiling. “This was deposited for me. Can you take it back after the account’s canceled?”

The man tapped his armrest, demanding an answer to his own proposal.

“Existing deposits and handovers continue through the custody channel.”

“And the note about the cuff? Can somebody add to it?”

“The operator’s identity must be verified.”

“I haven’t agreed to hand it to anyone.”

“The current custodian is Lin Mo.”

He folded the patch inward. The man reached after it. Lin Mo held the wood between them, keeping the screw point clear.

“Do you want the jacket, or me begging?”

“Can’t I have both?”

His neighbor laughed. The man joined in, lifted his mask to expose his mouth, and whistled toward the screen.

The agreement preview vanished. A narrow display lit up beside it.

> Audience interaction request.

He turned his paddle over. Its seat number matched the application number. Two taps, and he offered a tightly folded sheet headed Performance Index. When Lin Mo tried to read farther, a thumb covered the print.

“Keep the jacket, then. Give me one round.”

“Of what?”

“Doing as you’re told.”

The woman’s voice came from beside the screen.

“Interaction event: Absolute Obedience.”

Wang Hai stood at the aisle entrance with Lin Mo’s water. He hadn’t squeezed into the back row.

“Don’t take it.”

The man glanced at the booklet beneath Wang Hai’s clothes. “Your friend likes making your decisions.”

“He’s never pressed a button for me. Unfold that paper.”

“You get it afterward.”

“Blank paper counts as an index?”

The smile faded. He held the sheet beneath the display. Light shone through a folded corner, revealing a short number.

“Reward material submitted,” the voice said.

“I get it after one round?”

“The reward is delivered upon confirmation by the designated spectator.”

Rules appeared.

> Only one instruction takes effect per round, as repeated by the host.
> Until confirmation, no additions or withdrawals are permitted.
> The performer must obey absolutely. The method of carrying out the action is the performer’s choice.
> Confirmation rights belong solely to the designated spectator.

Lin Mo pointed at “performer’s choice.”

“He can’t change this by saying what he meant?”

“The action must remain within the effective instruction’s scope.”

“If it doesn’t specify how?”

“The performer chooses.”

Lin Mo showed Wang Hai the line. Wang Hai read it quietly, his mouth moving without a smile.

“You’ll make him spell it out?”

“If he leaves it out, it stays out.”

“Leave if you don’t like it,” the man said, tapping his knee with the paddle. “Door’s open.”

Wang Hai offered the water. Taking it, Lin Mo realized his left hand was slippery with sweat around the wood.

“One more thing. Until he confirms, I keep doing it?”

“You must maintain the state required by the effective instruction.”

“If he won’t confirm?”

“The round cannot be settled.”

Wang Hai stepped aside to give him a clear view of the exit.

“Your shoulders are done. Moving that cart nearly flattened you.”

“I know.” Lin Mo returned the bottle. “I’ll hear what he wants first.”

The mask turned toward him, as though examining something already delivered.

“Take off your jacket and put it over my shoulders.”

The host didn’t repeat it.

> Please submit one instruction.

Wang Hai snorted. “That’s two.”

The man deleted words on his paddle, then turned it away when Lin Mo leaned closer.

“Haven’t decided yet. What’s your hurry?”

He raised his right hand, palm open.

“Hold my hand.”

The host repeated it. Accept and Exit appeared on the paddle. Lin Mo pocketed the wood with its screw facing inward and pointed to the folded paper.

“That one. No switching.”

“Reward material locked.”

He pressed Accept.

Wang Hai said nothing more. He put the water on an empty chair by the aisle and moved closer. At the other end, Sun Ning stood with her injured arm against her body.

Lin Mo took the man’s right hand in his left.

His hand was smaller. The grip that once fitted around the bases of another person’s fingers no longer worked. Before the man even squeezed, his little finger slipped free.

Wang Hai stepped forward, then stopped.

Lin Mo straightened his wrist and closed his fingers again. The man stroked his knuckles with a thumb. The narrow display cut to a close-up.

“Changed these, too.”

“Move the sleeve.”

Lin Mo didn’t turn for the camera. Gray fabric stayed over their wrists.

The hand was warm, its fingertips rough. The man hooked his thumb against Lin Mo’s fingers and pulled. Lin Mo’s knee struck the seat. Laughter rose behind him.

“Sit closer.”

Lin Mo stayed where he was.

“The instruction is in effect. No additions permitted.”

The grip tightened. “Didn’t tell you to sit? Stand, then.”

He rested his arm on the armrest and pressed downward. Lin Mo bent with the pull, exposing the blue collar beneath his jacket. His shoulders drew tight.

> don’t settle with her yet

> let’s see the outfit first

> left hand? clever

He tried to straighten, but their joined hands were dragged lower. His narrower shoulders wouldn’t give him the angle he once had. The blue top rode up. The padding on his right wrist rubbed the sleeve seam.

“Give me a smile and I’ll confirm.”

“That’s not this round.”

His thin voice seemed to please the man further.

“Then I’ll wait.”

With his free left hand, the man slowly folded the paper, hiding its heading.

Lin Mo watched the corner. The material was locked, but nobody had said it couldn’t be torn. His fingers stirred. The man instantly pushed their joined hands down.

“Keep the other one out of it.”

Again the host warned against additional instructions. Lin Mo lowered his right hand.

“The method is the performer’s choice.”

“How would you like to hold it?”

“I let go after you confirm.”

He spread his feet. Skirt fabric caught his knee; he shortened his step and moved beside the armrest instead of pulling across the chair.

“Don’t stand on the crossbar,” Wang Hai said.

Lin Mo had meant to brace a foot on the bar projecting beneath the armrest. He moved it away and settled beside the chair leg. The man was still dragging their hands downward.

Pulling upward would only tear his fingers loose first. His shoulder already ached.

Their hands never separated.

He pressed his thumb against the base of the man’s, tucked his elbow toward his waist, and turned his palm along the direction of the downward pull.

The man’s wrist lifted clear of the armrest. His shoulder rose with it. The laughter stopped.

“What are you doing?”

Lin Mo waited until his feet were secure before tightening his grip around the bent fingers.

“Holding your hand.”

The man recoiled, but his elbow caught against the armrest. As he tried to pull free, Lin Mo moved a little closer, keeping their hands together.

“Let go.”

The display flashed.

> The current instruction cannot be withdrawn.

“I said let go!”

“Please confirm.”

“You’re hurting me.”

“I didn’t tell you to pull backward.”

His shoulder pressed against the chair back. His breathing quickened. Lin Mo’s left arm shook too. He eased forward half an inch, allowing the man to sit upright without releasing him.

“Stop pulling. Press Confirm.”

The paddle lay beside his left hand. He stared at it before reaching. The paper fell onto his lap. He picked up the paddle, knocked its corner against the armrest, and missed the button.

“No hurry,” Wang Hai said from the aisle. “Door’s open.”

The man whipped his head around. “Shut up.”

“He’s not the performer,” Lin Mo said.

The host didn’t repeat that order.

The neighboring spectator scraped his chair away. The man’s mask slipped. He adjusted it with his left hand, dropping the paddle again.

Lin Mo’s shoulder ached. He maintained the angle at the wrist without twisting farther. Sweat reached the corner of his eye.

At last the man found the button.

“Confirmed.”

> Round complete.

Lin Mo let go and stepped back. His left arm sagged. The wood nearly slipped out of his pocket. He caught it with his right wrist, setting the red mark burning again.

The man hid his right hand beneath his left armpit and snatched the paper with his other hand.

“Give it here,” Lin Mo said.

He didn’t.

“Reward delivery pending.”

He held the corner between his fingers, teeth clenched. The paddle’s light changed from white to red. Heavy breathing came from behind his mask.

He threw the paper down.

Lin Mo didn’t bend for it. The man’s feet flanked the sheet, toes pointing toward him. He moved back one seat. Wang Hai came around from the other side and nudged it into the light with his shoe. Sun Ning picked it up by a corner with her good hand.

“There’s writing.”

By the time the man looked up, Lin Mo was back beside Wang Hai.

“Reward delivered.”

The instruction field cleared. Lin Mo let his trembling left hand hang against his skirt. He left the wood alone.

Wang Hai put the water in his right hand. The bottle was soft enough to hold without squeezing.

“You knew how to do that?”

“My instructor taught me. With these smaller hands, I had to use his strength.”

“Don’t just practice hurting people. You’re wrecking your own hand.”

Lin Mo glanced at the hand hidden under the man’s arm.

“I only wanted him to confirm.”

Wang Hai didn’t smile. He retrieved the cap and screwed it onto the bottle.

Sun Ning unfolded the page. A channel number appeared above a tiny program picture. Someone stood beneath a light, a crease concealing the face.

> Streamer name: Xiao Lu.

Lin Mo’s thumb stopped on the name. It wasn’t his sister’s old one.

He reached, but Sun Ning held on.

“That’s a name. It doesn’t say how she’s related to you.”

“I know.”

Dates of past broadcasts ran down the back. The newest carried the label “Payment source association.” The last characters matched those in his copied source account.

He opened the innermost fold, revealing the rest. His finger tracked each section while Wang Hai checked his booklet and nodded twice.

“This tells you the source,” Sun Ning said. “You haven’t seen the person as she is now.”

Lin Mo nodded. He moved his ID in the jacket’s inner pocket, made room for the index, and headed for the exit.

At the front row, Sun Ning retrieved her booklet. She tried the pocket beside her torn sleeve, failed to get it in, and switched sides.

“Where are you going?”

“To find this channel.”

“I’ll come partway. My white shirt has a custody number, too. At least collecting things here doesn’t mean crawling under a cart.”

“Nobody said you could collect it here.”

“I know. I’m not walking in just because the route feels familiar.”

Wang Hai pointed to her arm. “Hasn’t tightened again?”

“It hurts. There’s no medicine here, either.”

They scanned their admission documents separately. The exit flashed green for each. Lin Mo went last. No notice appeared to end his protection.

Outside, a sign pointed to the Staff Passage and Broadcast Waiting Area. They stopped beneath it. Wang Hai stood on the staff-passage side while Lin Mo put the index into a playback slot in the wall.

It wasn’t swallowed. The same channel number lit along the rim. A small screen came on, labeled Program Preview.

A girl in a pale performance costume stood before a set, hair hanging below her shoulders. She clipped a name badge to her collar. Her fingertips concealed the final character, then moved away.

> Xiao Lu.

She raised her face to the camera.

“Don’t like this name? Vote for another.”
