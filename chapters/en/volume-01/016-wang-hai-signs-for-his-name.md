# Chapter 16: Wang Hai Signs for His Name

Before Wang Hai’s fingers reached the paper, the water moved.

A hand rolled palm-up beneath the slips. A ragged notch along its left edge, beside the little finger, nearly matched the wound under his bandages. He withdrew. The water settled, but the hand remained at the rim.

“Don’t take it,” Lin Mo said.

Wang Hai pressed the envelope against his knee and straightened. “Last time I followed that hand. Never collected anything.”

“You remember afterward?”

“Turning back. Then changing clothes. The bits don’t join.”

Lin Mo didn’t join them for him. The metal plate outside 08 still read:

> Name awaiting collection.

Below it, a hinged sign had a reverse visible only from this approach.

> Personal collection: Verify number, confirm name, then acknowledge receipt.
> No signing on another person’s behalf.
> Collection documents must match the claimant’s own settlement record.

The door mirror reflected all three of them. In it, Wang Hai’s badge still had a photograph, its name hidden by his cuff. The real badge remained blank.

Sun Ning raised her notebook to block his view. “Read what you’re holding.”

He checked the settlement sheet against his employee number, matching the barcode’s final digit to the one on the paper. He didn’t speak his name toward the mirror.

Lin Mo found two slots beside the reader: a narrow one for supporting documents, a wider one for signed collection materials. He pointed them out.

“Don’t test it for me,” Wang Hai said.

“I wasn’t going to.”

“Don’t put your ID in there.”

Lin Mo put his hand back in his jacket pocket and stepped aside.

Wang Hai held up his badge. The number that appeared matched his paper. The hand in the bucket reached up, hooked a slip, and dragged it toward the desk.

> Name: Wang Hai.

His breathing quickened. His left hand lifted without thinking; the bandage caught the rim, and he jerked back in pain. The paper floated where it was.

“Please confirm the name,” said the woman.

“After I sign, do I get the name and everything in my head back?”

“Registered ownership and recognition of the deposited name will be restored. Other lost memories are not guaranteed.”

He looked at Lin Mo. The first time Lin Mo had heard the name in the corridor, Wang Hai had still turned when called. Now he heard it like a number assigned to him.

“Take it out. Read everything.”

Five minutes counted down above the bucket. Beneath the exit button:

> Unclaimed items remain available until receipt is signed.

Wang Hai rested a fingernail beneath the line without pressing exit.

“Still here if I don’t sign,” he told himself, then took the paper with his right hand.

Water dripped from its corners. The writing held. He laid it on the dry side of the desk. Sun Ning brought out her blank verification form, but asked before using it to blot the water.

“Pending collection content must not be altered.”

“Only the back. No touching the words.”

“Assistance with preparation permitted. Confirmation may not be delegated.”

She folded her form into a strip and laid it against the edge. It absorbed enough water to free the collection page from the desk. She stepped back, leaving Wang Hai alone beneath the reader light.

Name and number matched. The acknowledgment added something absent from the metal plate:

> I confirm receipt of this name and its attached identity description.

The description was on the back. Below factory night-shift worker, smaller print stated that he voluntarily waived his companions’ assistance and would perform alone thereafter.

“I never said that.”

His reflection nodded and began moving its mouth.

Wang Hai turned his back on it at once. The hand in the bucket caught the other end of the paper, pulling half an inch from beneath his fingers.

Lin Mo stepped to the processing boundary. “Where did that attachment come from?”

The screen showed a process record: name deposited following mirror response; identity description from a prefilled personal-settlement application.

“Prefilled,” Sun Ning said. “Not confirmed.”

Lin Mo remembered the solo application waiving companions in the settlement area. He hadn’t signed. It could prepare one for Wang Hai too, even attach it to a separate collection.

Wang Hai pressed his finger to the word ‘voluntarily.’ “I recognize the name. I don’t recognize this.”

He took a pen to cross out the smaller print. Lin Mo stopped him just above the page.

“It said no altering the contents. Ask if they can be separated.”

Wang Hai stared at the sheet before putting the pen down.

“The sign says verify number, confirm name,” Lin Mo said. “Is the attachment compulsory?”

The page didn’t change. The countdown continued; most of four minutes slipped away. Wang Hai gripped the pen. Sun Ning pointed at a verification button and asked whether it was restricted to the current claimant.

“The claimant may request verification of material sources.”

Wang Hai pressed it with his right hand.

> Receipt countdown paused during verification.

His shoulders sagged. “Could’ve mentioned that.”

“You didn’t ask,” someone in the mirror said.

It sounded like Old Li from the workshop. Wang Hai half turned. Lin Mo caught his shoulder, holding the settlement sheet before him without calling his name.

He fixed on the number and faced the desk again.

“Whoever said that, I’m not answering. Keep checking.”

The prefilled application appeared. Signature and collection fields were blank. As Wang Hai reached toward it, the system offered two choices: accept attachment, or remove unestablished attachment.

“Remove.”

“Only the name will be collected. Subsequent performance eligibility will not be configured automatically under the appendix.”

He selected the second option. The hand released the sheet, splashing water against the desk. Both descriptions faded, leaving the name, number, and a personal receipt-signature field.

Lin Mo lowered the settlement record. The unsigned application remained onscreen. Wang Hai started to put the collection sheet by the bucket.

“Keep it dry,” Lin Mo said. “Finish signing.”

Wang Hai flattened a corner and confirmed the appendix had been removed before pulling his badge free from underneath. He kept the pen himself.

The charges section listed zero additional payment. Attachment status: removed. He traced the line without touching accept.

Lin Mo waited outside the boundary. Once verification ended, the timer resumed.

“Forty-nine seconds,” Sun Ning said quietly.

Wang Hai began his surname. The first horizontal stroke was steady; the second ran long. At the final vertical his fingers trembled.

“I used to sign fast.”

A curled corner covered the unfinished stroke. He tried to press it flat with his left hand, wetting a patch of bandage, then drew back. The paper curled again.

“Use the envelope,” Lin Mo said. “Leave the writing clear.”

Wang Hai weighted the blank margin with it and tested his hold. The paper stayed. His pen hesitated at a water-wrinkled crease.

“Will they recognize it if the line breaks?”

“It’s a signature,” Sun Ning said. “You’re not drawing a form. Write yours.”

He carried the nib across the crease. Ink held on the dry patch. Slowly he joined the vertical. The pen had whitened his fingertips; he loosened two fingers and shifted his grip.

“No need to rush now either,” Sun Ning said.

He added the last horizontal and began Hai. At the first dot of its water radical, he drew a sudden breath.

Lin Mo looked at the bandaged hand, expecting pain. Wang Hai laughed once.

“My mum used to complain I turned that dot into a slant.”

“Which one?”

“Here.”

He pointed with the nib without adding a stroke, finished his name, and fed the sheet into the wider slot.

The metal plate clanged. Paper sank in the bucket. The hand followed, finally disappearing.

His badge warmed. The photograph emerged first: Wang Hai in blue work clothes, hair shorter than now, mouth tight as though someone had just called him over for the picture. Two characters slowly filled the name field.

He lifted the badge to the light, then pressed it against his chest.

The woman confirmed restoration of name ownership. She did not declare every memory restored. In the mirror, the badge went blank. The bucket’s plate flipped to collected. No one else’s name appeared.

Wang Hai stood there for a long time.

“What is it?” Sun Ning asked.

“Want to hear someone call me.”

Lin Mo’s throat moved. “Wang Hai.”

His voice was still thin, unlike the one he’d had in the corridor. Wang Hai answered immediately.

“Yeah?”

Quicker than he’d managed his signature. He tucked the badge beneath his collar and almost hit the desk turning around.

“Before, I had to think. Work out whether you meant me.”

Sun Ning passed him water. He fumbled it, brushing the badge with the bottle. The plastic fastener clicked. He set the water down and found the chip in the clasp.

“Moving materials. That’s when I knocked this. Kept putting off asking the shift leader for a replacement.”

Lin Mo didn’t announce that his memory was back. He asked what else Wang Hai remembered from before today.

Wang Hai started describing his factory locker, then stopped at the door. His smile faded.

“Wrong place. That mirror was inside it.”

He brought the badge out, turned the real photograph toward himself, and eventually shook his head.

“Leave that one. I haven’t got everything back.”

“Then leave it blank,” Lin Mo said.

Wang Hai drank. He didn’t tighten the cap enough afterward, spilling a little on his hand. He wiped it on his clothes and folded the damp receipt, putting it in the same pocket as his badge.

Sun Ning asked about his left hand. He tried moving his fingers and immediately frowned.

“Got my name back. Could’ve thrown in a proper bandage.”

“You don’t ask for much.”

“Only asking. Didn’t get my hopes up.”

He lifted his shoulder and let the injured hand hang, no longer using it to search his pocket. Beside Lin Mo, he waited for him to take the first step before moving toward the door together.

“This time it’s me,” he said.

Sun Ning picked up her blotting paper, tore off the wet edge, and folded the rest into her notebook. She didn’t test her name on the bucket. Her reflection remained in the door; she avoided it on her way out.

One waistband clip caught the doorframe, stopping Lin Mo. Wang Hai reached to help, then withdrew his right hand.

“Still catching?”

“I can walk.”

“Seven wants those clips back. Her place first.”

At the collection exit, the broadcast still played beside the door. In a new program, Xiao Lu opened her box and lifted out a fastened nameplate. The audience demanded she read it. She did, put it back, and brushed her fingers across her collar.

Lin Mo stopped. Wang Hai let him, then held his restored badge before Lin Mo’s eyes, covering the unfamiliar name onscreen.

“One back today. You’ve still got your index?”

Lin Mo felt inside his pocket and nodded.

Seven took the two clips and had him stand beside the desk. From a hanger she fetched a broad cloth tie that could go over his clothes. Only after obtaining permission for temporary fastening did she wrap it around the gray jacket, leaving the blue top and skirt seams alone.

Wang Hai waited by the door. Every so often he reached into his gray shirt and pulled out the badge.

“Name still there?” Sun Ning asked.

“Yes.”

“Photo?”

“That too.”

He glanced at her and put it away. “Just checking.”

“I know. Try not to rub the clasp off.”

He lowered his hand. A little later, he pressed it through the cloth again.

When Lin Mo finished tying the band, Wang Hai straightened the badge.

“Come here. See for yourself.”

Lin Mo joined him at the door. Wang Hai rubbed the photograph’s corner with his thumb, opened the clasp, and closed it to hear the small click. He held the badge up until Lin Mo touched his name.

Then he clipped it back at his collar.
