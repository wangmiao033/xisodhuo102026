# Chapter 2: Names Awaiting Collection

The phone went dark, but the man in the mirror kept holding it up. After a few seconds, Lin Mo looked at the opposite door. 05.

04 was on his left, 06 farther right. The numbers increased to the right. Room 09 should be that way. He carried the broken chair leg down the corridor.

The thick green carpet swallowed his footsteps. His right wrist ached. He switched the wood to his left hand, pulled a splinter from his palm, and wiped the blood on his jacket.

“Mo.”

His sister's voice came from the mirror behind him. He didn't turn.

“You're going the wrong way.”

Two steps farther, he stopped beside 06 and studied its plate. Dust covered the brass. Old paint showed in the lower screw holes, as though someone had moved it here. The first rule said to reach 09. It hadn't said which direction to take.

His sister called again, farther away now. He pressed the ID card through his inside pocket and kept going right.

The door to 06 opened. Lin Mo stopped, lowering the chair leg.

A man half a head taller than him came out in blue work clothes, a plastic factory badge on his chest. Blood seeped around a towel wrapped over his left hand. He noticed the wood first.

“You just got here too?”

Lin Mo checked the opposite mirror. Two men stood in matching positions. Even the blood on the work clothes matched.

“Came out of one of these rooms?” the man asked. “Yours have a camera?”

Lin Mo nodded. The man wiped his forehead with the towel, leaving a red smear.

“Knew it. How the fuck are they doing this?”

His lips moved. The voice came from right in front of Lin Mo.

“Where are you headed?” Lin Mo asked.

“Nine.”

“Read the card on your door?”

“Room nine. Ignore the mirrors.” He pointed behind him. “And face them when the bell rings.”

Lin Mo glanced at the badge. A fold of blue cloth hid half the writing beneath the photo. Noticing his look, the man turned it over.

“Let's get out first. I was on the night shift. Didn't even shut the machine off.”

Water dripped somewhere ahead. The man looked right.

“Got a phone?”

“No.”

“Mine's gone too.”

He searched his empty pockets. Some of the anger holding his face together drained away.

The bell rang as they passed 07. Lin Mo faced the nearest mirror.

His reflection raised the chair leg in its left hand, aiming the screw at its eye. Lin Mo's own arm was still down. Beside him, the man's breathing grew loud. A phone had appeared in the mirror.

The other man’s reflection held it to one ear and waved with its right hand.

“Wang Hai!”

A shrill woman's voice. The man's shoulder twitched.

“Wang Hai, the machine's shorting! Xiao Wu's hand got caught!”

“Tell him not to—”

Lin Mo seized his arm. Too late. The factory badge fell onto the carpet.

The strap still hung around his neck. Its plastic clip hadn't broken; the badge had dropped straight through it. Lin Mo crouched to retrieve it.

White covered the face in the photograph. Only Hai remained beneath it, and then that faded too. He turned it over. The factory name, department, and phone number were untouched.

“Your badge.”

The man didn't take it. He stared into the mirror, his lips still shaped around his unfinished words. At last he closed his mouth.

“Who were they calling?”

Lin Mo stood. The reflection lowered its phone and wagged it at him. The missing badge photograph was stuck to the back.

“What's your name?” Lin Mo asked.

The man opened his mouth. Lin Mo held the badge closer.

“You said you were on the night shift. Which factory?”

He read the factory name, then rattled off his workshop and the machine's model.

“And your name?”

“I…”

He felt his chest, found the empty clip, and began searching his clothes.

The bell was still ringing. Cold sweat broke over Lin Mo's shoulders. He'd turned his back while retrieving the badge.

Something tapped the wall to his right. A dark red ring appeared around his reflection's right wrist. His own skin heated in the same place. He turned quickly, forcing the badge into the man's hand.

“Face the mirror.”

Still searching, the man didn't move. Lin Mo pushed his shoulder until he faced forward. He stumbled and stared at himself. Lin Mo straightened beside him.

The heat gradually stopped. His reflection tapped the glass.

“Lin Mo.”

He shut his eyes. The chair leg pressed against his left trouser seam. Keeping his nose pointed ahead, he counted six breaths. Still the bell.

He opened his eyes a crack. The face had stretched toward him until its lips touched the inside of the glass. His wrist hurt no worse. He closed his eyes again.

“Your ID's in that inside pocket, isn't it?”

The voice resembled his own, lower than his sister's, slightly nasal. His right hand remained over his pocket.

“Take it out. I'll change you back.”

Nails scraped glass. Beside him, the man's shoes scuffed backward. Lin Mo lowered the chair leg across his shins.

“Stay facing forward.”

The man caught his sleeve.

“He says I can get it back.”

“It says face the mirror. Doesn't say look.”

He wasn't certain. He only knew his wrist hadn't heated since he'd closed his eyes.

The grip tightened. The scraping stopped. Something slammed against the glass.

Lin Mo's eyelids jumped. He kept them shut, teeth clenched. The man tugged twice at his sleeve, then loosened his hold.

When the bell stopped, Lin Mo waited two more breaths.

His reflection stood in its original position, holding the chair leg, eyes closed. The other reflection's eyes were closed too. Its badge was intact: photograph, name, everything.

Wang Hai.

The real badge was still blank.

“Your name's Wang Hai.”

The man repeated it uncertainly.

“Wang Hai.”

“Remember it. You remember the machine and the factory. Your name's Wang Hai.”

He nodded, gripping the badge. His reflection smiled.

A few steps on, Lin Mo realized Wang Hai hadn't followed. He was holding his badge against the mirror, comparing the photographs. The man inside had raised a hand too.

“Don't touch it.”

Wang Hai pulled away and caught up.

“I can't remember.”

“You just said it.”

“I can say it.” He looked at Lin Mo. “I don't know if it's mine.”

Lin Mo followed a smear of dust across the carpet, touching the hard edge of his ID. Being able to say a name didn't mean you could get it back.

A bucket stood beside 08. Papers floated in it, sodden edges drooping over the rim. Names covered them: some only surnames, others crossed out and replaced with numbers. This was where the dripping came from.

The ceiling was dry. Water fell through the mirror above the bucket.

Wang Hai stopped. Lin Mo barred his way with the wood.

“Don't take anything.”

“Just looking.”

A paper turned over. Wang Hai was written on the back, a string of digits beneath it. Wang Hai compared them with his badge.

“Employee number. That's mine.”

Lin Mo pulled him back. The paper settled against the rim, facing them. Others turned too, crowding together as though waiting to be chosen. A metal plate was nailed beside the bucket.

> NAMES AWAITING COLLECTION
> Please acknowledge receipt.

“Can't I just sign and take it?” Wang Hai asked.

“With what?”

“My hand. A fingerprint.”

Dark shapes crowded beneath the paper. Lin Mo crouched lower and saw fingernails.

Hands lay in the water. One slowly rose, pressing its palm against the back of Wang Hai's paper.

Wang Hai recoiled into door 08, clutching his left hand. The towel left a bloody streak. Someone inside knocked three times. Lin Mo pulled him away.

“One more door.”

His injured wrist brushed the blue sleeve. Pain loosened his fingers. Wang Hai steadied himself and supported Lin Mo's hand.

“They do that to you inside too?”

Lin Mo pulled his sleeve down.

“Keep moving.”

09 was narrower than the other doors. No speaker, only a brass handle and a thin slot. A neatly trimmed notice above it looked newer than the corridor rules.

> Please present valid identification.
> Entry permitted after verification.

Lin Mo set down the chair leg and took out his ID. His photograph and name remained unchanged. The slot was wide enough for a card, its edges worn silver. Two short scratches marked the surface below.

“Lin Mo.”

In the mirror to his right, his reflection held the ID to its lips.

“Tell me your name.”

He looked away. The real handle stayed still; its reflection dipped, revealing bright lights beyond the door.

“You have to say your name to get in.”

He brought his card to the slot and hesitated. What if this took his name too? Wang Hai stood against the wall, squeezing his blank badge until it creaked.

Behind them, papers rose from the bucket and drifted onto the carpet.

Lin Mo picked up the chair leg. With a key, he scratched his name into the hard wood. The second character came out crooked. He went over it again, pocketed the key, and pressed the carving against his left palm.

Then he fed in his ID.

Something gripped it. He held on while the slot tugged twice, his nails whitening.

“Please complete document scanning.”

This voice came from inside the door. He pushed farther, letting both name and photograph pass through. A small light turned white.

> Identity valid.
> Original name retained.

The card came back. Name first, then photograph: both unchanged. The door opened a crack.

He wedged it with the chair leg and pocketed his ID. Wang Hai stared at his badge.

“Mine won't work now, will it?”

Lin Mo tried it. Red light.

> Identity missing.
> Please return to collect.

Wang Hai looked toward the bucket. Lin Mo caught his sleeve.

“Get inside first.”

He pushed the door wider. Bright lights, two rows of clothes racks, folded clothing on a chair. Nobody by the entrance.

Wang Hai lifted one foot to the threshold but couldn't put it down.

“Move.”

“Please return to collect.”

The red light was already off. Wang Hai had spoken.

Lin Mo pulled him halfway inside. His other foot stayed planted on the carpet, his bandaged hand clutching the frame.

“Please return to collect.”

Barely a whisper. Lin Mo shouldered the door open and tried prying his fingers loose. Blood spread over the frame. Wang Hai suddenly lowered his head, listening, and turned his badge faceup.

In the glass, his reflection answered another call. Lin Mo couldn't tell what it was saying. Wang Hai released the frame and broke his grip.

“I'll just go get it.”

He took two steps back. Lin Mo followed, but the closing door trapped the chair leg. Two hard pulls freed it, breaking off another chip. When he shoved again, he met only the cold door.

“Wang Hai!”

No answer. Water dripped into the bucket. He listened against the door until the footsteps stopped, then slowly released the handle.

Clothes stirred on the racks. A camera glowed in the corner; the viewer count climbed across a wall mirror.

> thought he could carry someone through

> lucky you've still got your own name

He raised the chair leg and headed for the camera. A notice lit the mirror.

> Reminder: Beginner protection applies only to the guaranteed individual.

He stopped. The glass showed his face and the wood at his side, his name cut into it.

“Who's my guarantor?”

No answer. A cupboard opened beside him. A pale jacket hung inside. Uneven stitches patched its cuff, the final stitch so tight it puckered the cloth.

His sister had sewn that on her hospital bed. She'd asked him for the scissors.

He approached, one hand half raised. A card lay beneath the jacket.

> Held in safekeeping for you.
> Please complete this round's costume change first.
