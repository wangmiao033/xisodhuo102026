# Chapter 18: Not That White Shirt

Sun Ning kept the blank form in her palm instead of feeding it into the reader. The edge still held the creases from soaking up water.

“Where's the original source?”

Lin Mo put away his response card and gave her the screen. His correction page had closed. Only when she pressed her own settlement receipt to the reader did the white-shirt entry brighten.

“Collection preview. Check the original?” the woman asked.

“Check. Not collect.”

A green door opened onscreen. Three garments hung inside, the white shirt in front, its hem neatly folded and cuffs intact. Sun Ning drew her injured right arm closer and pointed at the handwritten Sun beneath it.

“Who wrote that?”

“A temporary program label.”

“Since when is my surname a temporary label?”

The image shifted upward. Another character emerged: Min. Sun Min. A hanger covered most of the second character; the distant preview hadn't shown enough to read.

“That isn't Sun Ning,” Wang Hai said. He started to point out the covered stroke, then lowered his hand and let her enlarge it herself.

“Do you know her?” Lin Mo asked.

“No. Nobody at my old shop had that name.” Her left hand went to her collar. “We had to wear white. If you got the collar dirty, you still had to wash it after doing the accounts.”

Her fingers stopped on the coarse seam.

“But was the one I washed really white?”

“Do you remember the shop's name?”

After a moment, she shook her head. “I can say it. I wouldn't put it on a form. I could say it when I thought I knew the way, too.”

Wang Hai moved the water closer. “Then don't fill it in. They're not paying your wages. Why should you balance their books?”

She took the bottle and almost smiled, until the movement pulled at her shoulder. A collection number appeared beside the shirt. She opened her notebook to a handwritten entry. The first section matched; the rest was completely different.

“The part I remember is this first bit.”

“You wrote the one you're holding,” Lin Mo reminded her.

“I know.”

She closed the notebook. Her arm hurt and she wanted to sit, but there was no chair. She leaned against the wall with her water while Wang Hai stood where he could block the passage.

The shirt revolved. An unfamiliar woman reached for it. Her right arm was uninjured, her sleeve had two buttons. She said thank you and lifted it off the hanger.

Sun Ning reached after her. Her left fingers touched the screen's frame before she pulled back.

“Why do I feel like I have to chase her?”

Lin Mo didn't tell her it was only fake. He brought her right sleeve where they could both see it. Bruising spread beneath the torn fabric; loose threads fringed the exposed skin.

“This is what you're wearing.”

“I remember changing.”

“Where were you when she changed?”

“The green door.” Her gaze swung toward the passage. “I was standing there. She gave me the clothes.”

But the woman onscreen had put on the shirt and walked out alone. She hadn't exchanged anything. Sun Ning set down her water and traced the seam from her shoulder to the torn sleeve.

“Go back a little.”

Lin Mo rewound. Before leaving, the woman finished buttoning her shirt and turned her collar out. Sun Ning raised her own hand to her neck and found nothing.

“I remember doing that for her. She hadn't fastened it properly.”

Wang Hai watched the doorway without answering. She replayed it. The woman's hands never crossed the threshold. Sun Ning paused the image and stood with her hand suspended before resting it on her injured arm.

“Start when I entered the staff passage,” she told the voice.

“Only sources associated with this segment are available.”

“Then mark where they connect to me.”

Two short audio tracks appeared: white-shirt collection and staff-passage guidance. The same call had been laid beneath different pictures. A close-up of the red button ended the second track.

Before touching play, Sun Ning switched off autoplay beside the camera. Lin Mo checked that the pause symbol remained, then stepped aside.

“Can I hear just the second one?”

“Selected-segment playback is available.”

She pressed it and retrieved her water.

“Your things are here. Take the familiar route. One press stops it.”

It sounded like a coworker from the shop, someone who'd been there when she did the accounts. Her left hand drifted half an inch toward the passage. Wang Hai blocked its back with his right hand.

“Don't follow.”

“I wasn't going to.”

Then she saw her hand. Her grip tightened with a crackle of plastic. Wang Hai withdrew, and she brought the bottle back to her chest.

The audio label changed.

> Lost-property guidance sample. General use.

The shirt footage hadn't been made for her, but that coworker's voice seemed to circle close around her ear.

She capped the water, set it down, and covered the tear in her sleeve.

“Did you hear someone you knew?”

“Sounded like the fellow calling out at the supplies window,” Wang Hai said. “Can't remember his face.”

Lin Mo had heard a ward nurse by the green door, the endings of her words like a warning not to run in the corridor. He told them. Sun Ning lowered her head and stopped the track.

“Then I'm not collecting the shirt.”

She scrolled down without leaning toward the green door again. The voice didn't demand that she surrender all claims to clothing. The screen showed:

> No valid original record of personal collection in this segment.

She took out her pen, then saw that her blank form required a source for the supporting record.

“Can I have this page? Just the part saying there's no original.”

Paper popped from a slot. She took it herself, folded the old handwritten record inward, and tucked it behind the new sheet without copying the shirt number.

Lin Mo was ready to lead her out. She pointed to another entry beside the passage guidance.

> Linen-cart accident. Items temporarily stored with cart.

She tried to lift her right sleeve toward the camera and stopped halfway, in pain.

“The other half of this is still in there.”

The voice instructed her to check her own accident record. Wang Hai moved forward, then stopped. “Does this mean another performance?”

“Checking personal effects left behind opens the associated footage. No additional Physical modification.”

Sun Ning kept that line visible at the top before selecting Continue.

The cart's wheels were still turning onscreen. She lay trapped beneath it, arm caught against the axle. Nothing she wore, from collar to waist, was white. Lin Mo crouched beside the cart with a longer piece of wood.

“That one,” she said, looking at his pocket.

“It split later.” He pushed the shortened wood deeper, keeping the screw covered.

She nodded. Her recorded self shouted to stay back. Wang Hai was searching for a wheel pedal; Lin Mo was reaching toward the red button.

“Stop here.”

Asked to select a frame, she moved back to before his hand touched the button and enlarged her face and sleeve. The strained seam had twisted, but the screw hadn't yet torn it. She turned out the fabric she wore now. The same stitching lay inside.

“This is mine.”

“Don't just go by the color,” Wang Hai said.

She didn't snap at him. She pointed below the shoulder seam. It had broken once, and she'd repaired it with black thread: three stitches before she gave up, the last one long.

Onscreen, the axle pulled the sleeve apart. Two stitches stayed on her; the third rolled beneath the cart with the torn fabric.

She counted the stitches on her sleeve. Her finger stopped at the gap.

“I sewed the missing one myself.”

The screen directed her to check the uncollected item. A sealed bag beside the slot held a scrap of gray cloth, black thread hanging from its end, its edge smeared with oil from under the cart. No whole shirt. No white replacement.

Wang Hai guarded the passage instead of opening the bag. Sun Ning couldn't pull the seal apart one-handed, so Lin Mo pinned the bottom against the wall counter. She tore it herself.

The black thread lifted from the unfolded edge, half a stitch still attached. She held the scrap against her sleeve. Oil brushed the inside of the tear. She quickly turned the clean reverse toward herself.

“Wrong way round.”

Lin Mo reached to help. She shook her head. On her second try she opened the folded hem, lining up the two remaining stitches with the broken half on the scrap.

Wang Hai crouched beside her without touching it.

“That thread goes down. The higher you hold it, the more it curls.”

She slowly lowered her arm. The edges met under her thumb. It looked like a whole sleeve again, with the arm inside still swollen.

“Can't just collect it and wear it like this.”

“Taking it?” Lin Mo asked.

“Yes. This piece didn't sign anything saying I volunteered. Didn't ask either of you to get hurt for me.”

She folded it back into the bag, avoiding the oily side. A thread caught in the fold; she worked it free with her nail instead of cutting it.

The cloth could be joined, but her arm hurt too much to lift again. She stood holding both torn edges for a long time. Wang Hai offered to find a clip. She shook her head and put the scrap in her pocket rather than wrapping the injury.

“Dirty. I'll keep it for now.”

A note scrolled beneath the screen: rescuers, Lin Mo and Wang Hai. She enlarged their names, ignoring the female-streamer label added to the rescue footage.

“Can I keep this off the recommendations?”

“The original accident record remains. You may decline to submit it for recommended display.”

“Then don't submit it.”

She closed the section, collecting only her own scrap and verification sheet. Wang Hai loosened her bottle cap without taking the water away. She drank, then leaned on the wall to straighten up.

> all that for a bit of rag?

> if the shirt wasn't hers, why send her to collect it?

Sun Ning pointed to the first comment.

“You want a white shirt, go collect it. Leave my name off it.”

The woman didn't answer for the audience. Sun Ning put away the blank form, still without a new custody number but now accompanied by her accident verification. She rested the papers against the screen while settling the gray scrap in her pocket. Lin Mo blocked a draft with his hand until she'd folded them into the notebook.

A completion notice lit beside the door and opened program selection for performers who had shared the session.

“Do I have to choose one?” Sun Ning asked.

“Optional. You may leave after the current segment.”

She took two steps toward the exit, then pointed at Lin Mo's right wrist.

“That padding's getting old. If you perform again, don't start pulling your sleeve open the moment it hurts.”

“Where are you going?”

“Somewhere I can sit. My right arm needs a rest.”

Wang Hai offered to carry her water. She kept it wedged between her injured arm and her waist, freeing her left hand to swipe her card.

Beyond the open exit stood a chair. Once seated, she waved them away. Wang Hai held up his water to ask if she had enough. She shook her own bottle and laid the sealed bag on her lap, not under her as a cushion. Her torn sleeve remained exposed. At last she stopped covering it and settled her left hand on the chair back, leaving room for her right arm to rest.

Lin Mo scanned his card. The two-person performance was still gray: shared performance history and confirmation from both channels required. Below it, a stride demonstration listed the same hosting channel as Xiao Lu's index.

He opened it. Buckled shoes appeared in the preview. Keeping his card under his own hand, he turned to Wang Hai.

“This time, watch the floor for me.”
