# Chapter 15: She Didn’t Write the Note

Lin Mo held his waistband until he reached the verification door.

Its camera tilted down after his hand. Wang Hai raised the water bottle in front of it, and the lens swung toward the barcode.

“Film this. It’s got a date.”

Sun Ning pulled him back. “Don’t give it another activity.”

Lin Mo touched the form to the reader. A green light came on; cloth rollers rumbled inside. He peered through the gap before entering. Clothes moved slowly along an overhead rail. No retrieval button on the floor, only a square waiting area.

“No cart to crawl under,” Wang Hai said.

“I wasn’t planning on a second time.” Sun Ning held the doorframe and followed them sideways through.

Seven sat behind the verification desk, spectacles low on her nose, cleaning a stamp with a fine brush. A gray apron lay across her knees. She set the brush beside the ink pad.

“Clothes don’t fit again?”

“Just had measurements.” Lin Mo held the base of his zipper.

She stopped looking him over and held out her hand. He put down this session’s verification form separately from the jacket’s handover record.

“I’m checking who added the note.”

“Verify the materials first.”

He unfolded the handover record. Seven passed its number beneath a narrow lamp, bringing up the original deposit entry. At her indication, he held his ID near the reader, withdrawing it once the light turned green.

“Keep hold of original identification,” she said.

“Are you asking me to hand it over?”

“I haven’t.”

Wang Hai held a chair for Sun Ning inside the waiting square. It was too close to the desk for her injured arm. Using his right hand, he turned it so she could sit without squeezing between the armrests.

Seven set down her stamp and unfolded a small display.

“One verification per item. The note or the original owner?”

“I checked the owner. The note.”

“This establishes who entered it. It doesn’t prove everything in it is true.”

He nodded. She inserted the form into a slot that gripped one edge without swallowing it. A date flashed. The gray jacket appeared onscreen, its intake record beside a later addition.

Several transfers separated them. Lin Mo had thought of everything before this session as a single point in time. Now he saw the jacket had moved from its original storage to 09, then through collection preparation and an added note. Those things hadn’t happened together.

“Who?”

Seven expanded the final entry.

> Operator station: Clothing Recovery Seven.

Wang Hai’s hand stopped on the chair. “You?”

“Me.”

Lin Mo looked at her spectacles. Only the narrow lamp reflected there. No trace of his sister.

“Could you see me then?”

“Corridor injuries appear on this area’s clothing-processing page. I need to know whether a sleeve requires padding.”

“You watched the stream?”

“The page has selected footage, not the whole broadcast. You touched the mirror with your wrist. I saw part of what happened afterward.”

His right hand’s giving him trouble. He remembered the note. He hadn’t received the blue clothes yet, but the jacket was already waiting in the cabinet. He’d thought his sister might be behind it, or watching from the far end of the corridor. The words beneath Seven’s finger hadn’t changed. The person behind them had.

“Why didn’t you tell me?”

“You were checking the original account. Once the light came on, I couldn’t slip this operator page in with it.”

She put the pages side by side: the closed source account, her operator number. Different headings, not the same person’s name.

“The closed account can’t write anymore. You wrote it for her?”

“I added this entry. Don’t add ‘for her.’”

He turned his cuff out. The padding dragged across his pale scar. Trying to tuck it back, he caught both layers in his left hand and pulled them tighter.

“Want me to?” Wang Hai asked.

“Wait.”

He loosened his fingers. Seven slid a clip toward him without touching the jacket.

“Clip the sleeve seam. Then arrange the padding.”

He followed her instructions and returned the clip.

“I thought she’d left those words.”

Seven watched his hand. “I never saw her. There was already a recipient tag on the collar when the jacket arrived.”

“Then why did you save it for me?”

“The recipient was fixed at intake. I can’t change it.”

She opened the original instructions. The depositor’s name was still blacked out. Recipient: Lin Mo. One handling instruction read:

> Before delivery, inspect according to the recipient’s condition at that time. Do not allow cuffs to rub injured areas.

His left hand pressed the sleeve. “They knew I’d be injured?”

“If you’re injured, we adjust for it. We inspect either way. The original instruction didn’t specify your right hand.”

He pressed his fingernail beneath the words “condition at that time.” The jacket outside 09 had been looser than his old clothes. How could she have known how narrow his shoulders would become when she left it?

Seven enlarged the display. Another instruction had nothing to do with measurements: preserve unregistered personal items in the lining; do not recover the jacket as abandoned clothing.

He stopped asking. Through the pocket, he felt the hard blister containing half a tablet. His palm stayed against the jacket.

Sun Ning shifted in her chair. “Who wrote that?”

“Submitted with the original deposit. This page doesn’t establish whether it was written personally or relayed.”

“Can I take it?” Lin Mo asked.

“The verification result. No copy of the original instructions.”

“Can I copy that sentence?”

“Your recipient handling instructions, yes. Leave the concealed original name alone.”

He opened his notebook after the handover entries. The new scar pulled his pen at an angle. He switched hands, but after two characters the nib dragged a long line across the page.

Wang Hai didn’t take the pen. “Hold the paper?”

Lin Mo nodded. Wang Hai pinned the corner with his right hand and moved the lamp closer.

“Slowly. They’re not about to take the paper away again?”

Seven pointed beneath the form. “Verification time ends when results are delivered. They haven’t printed.”

Lin Mo copied only the handling instructions, leaving his guesses out. At personal items he paused and turned the blister pack over to compare the pen marks on its back.

Seven put the clip against the screen edge. “That’s a second item. Keep it out of the light.”

He drew it back into his palm. Wang Hai waited until he’d pocketed it, then reminded him there were four characters left. Lin Mo finished and circled the crooked stroke rather than turn it into a character he wouldn’t recognize.

“Will you know what that says later?” Wang Hai asked.

“Yes.”

“So will I. It didn’t print that for you.”

Sun Ning pointed at the last line. “Unregistered. You missed the word for ‘not.’”

He added it beside the line, with an arrow to its proper place. Only when he closed the notebook did Wang Hai lift his hand.

Lin Mo wanted longer with the screen. Seven locked it, but made no move to take the jacket. Wang Hai passed him water, the cap already loose. Even a small swallow stung his throat.

“Stay out of the draft,” Seven said.

She turned the overhead vent inward. The hanging clothes stirred; the loose thread at Lin Mo’s cuff stopped brushing his scar.

Two comments slid through the broadcast window above the verification lamp.

> waited all that time to find out it was a clerk?

> so is his sister still here or not?

Seven pushed her ledger beyond the camera’s view and tapped the printer with the clip. Lin Mo looked away from the comments without answering.

Paper jammed at the rollers. Wang Hai bent down; Seven barred his hand with the clip.

“Don’t reach in.”

“Only looking.”

She switched off the roller, opened the cover, and freed the sheet. Smoothing its crease, she handed it to Lin Mo.

> Operator identity verified.

The checked item number followed. He folded it with the handover record, then brought out his index slip.

“Do you know this name? Xiao Lu?”

“A performance name.”

“I know. I mean the person.”

“I’ve processed clothes from that channel. The person hasn’t collected anything here.”

She returned the slip without using his verification entitlement to open someone else’s records. He put it away, lifting his finger from the small image.

Sun Ning pushed up from the seat with her left hand. “I had a white shirt. There may be a storage number.”

“Receipt?”

She opened her notebook at the handwritten line. Seven turned it toward the light but didn’t scan it.

“You wrote this yourself.”

“I remember someone taking me to collect it.”

“Who?”

Sun Ning opened her mouth. Her right shoulder tightened. Wang Hai moved the chair behind her legs. She sat without inventing the rest.

“I can’t remember.”

“Then don’t fill in a number here. Check your own settlement sources.”

Seven gave her a blank collection-verification form. It asked where the supporting record came from, not what she remembered possessing. Sun Ning folded it into the front of her notebook without writing white shirt.

“Does this cost anything?”

“Blank forms are free.”

“If I get it wrong?”

“Get another, before submitting.”

Once Sun Ning was settled, Wang Hai held his factory badge between two fingers, away from the scanner.

“You gave him this before.”

Seven examined the barcode. “Cards aren’t clothing. You’ve got it back.”

“Not the name.”

“Where does it say?”

“Mirror Corridor 08.”

She turned a sign beside the desk. The collection-processing exit led to 08, but the claimant must carry their own pending-collection record. This session’s material verification did not include receipt of names. The clerk could not collect one for anyone else.

Wang Hai’s knuckles whitened around the badge, then slowly relaxed.

“Going?” Lin Mo asked.

Wang Hai took out his settlement envelope. Its seal was already torn; no need to work out how to open it again. He pointed to the collection number beside the badge’s barcode. Both still ended with the same number.

“Yes.”

He gathered the envelope’s opening around the badge, leaving its barcode exposed. Lin Mo offered to steady the paper.

“It isn’t sealed this time. I’ve got it.”

Lin Mo stepped aside so he could use his own document at the reader. As the light came on, Wang Hai slowed. He didn’t run back toward the voice inside as he had before.

Lin Mo drew his jacket around him and took a step. His skirt slipped again. He stopped to lift it.

Seven pointed beneath the chair. “Clips. No cutting or altering the costume. Temporary fastening, excluded from measurement confirmation.”

She took two broad fabric clips from the drawer, obtained the woman’s approval for temporary adjustments here, and handed them over. Through his jacket, Lin Mo gathered small folds at either side of the waistband, careful not to catch skin. Fastened, it finally let him walk without stopping.

“Return them to this drawer. Don’t deposit them.”

Wang Hai waited until he was ready. Sun Ning followed them. Seven tucked her crooked chair beneath the desk without asking how long she meant to keep going.

At the door, Lin Mo stopped. “Who else has seen those original instructions?”

Seven opened the transfer page and indicated two undisclosed stations.

“The people moving the clothes. This form won’t let you check them.”

He pressed his gray cuff. “You added the note. The jacket was still her deposit.”

Seven pushed the verification sheet toward him without reopening the screen.

He took it and turned away. A gray sleeve swung before his face on the overhead rail. He caught it, saw it belonged to an unrelated work uniform, and let go.

Wang Hai stood at the door toward 08, badge in his good hand. The bucket hung beside it. Its water was calmer than in the corridor. The paper inside hadn’t dissolved.

He bent down, fingertips hovering above the topmost strip.

“I know how to write that.”
