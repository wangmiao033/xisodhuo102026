# Chapter 14: The Clothes Hadn’t Shrunk

The measuring stand stopped at the stage. Its attendant released the handle and stepped on a wheel lock.

Lin Mo watched his foot. The motor under the last cart had kept pushing even after they dragged Sun Ning out. He waited until the wheels stopped.

“No motor,” the attendant said.

He wore a gray apron and a measurement number stuck to the back of his hand. No name. He knocked beneath the cart. It sounded hollow.

“Over there. Don’t bump the stand.”

Xiao Lu put her box away backstage and returned with three sheets. One went to the attendant, one to Lin Mo, and one before the camera.

“Measurements first, then a vote on the adjustment area. Removed outerwear remains in its owner’s custody. It will not be used as payment.”

Lin Mo held his gray jacket closed. “I haven’t agreed to adjustments.”

“This round chooses the area.”

“Can’t refuse this one either?”

Rather than answer for the woman’s voice, Xiao Lu pointed to the withdrawal clause. He could leave before measurements began. His response eligibility on this channel would end, but materials already collected remained valid. Further index applications would be his responsibility.

He looked toward the exit. This time he really could leave.

Wang Hai approached the red line. “See what the choices are first.”

The attendant turned the preview board around: waist and chest, or shoulders and back. Both used female performance templates. Existing modifications would not be reversed. Duration and pain intensity were excluded from voting.

“I have to stay to ask more questions?” Lin Mo asked Xiao Lu.

“Leave and you’ll have to apply again. I don’t decide whether you get approved.”

“Can you come out?”

She faced the camera. “Private arrangements with performers are not permitted.”

She spoke quickly, but held the paper closer, her finger beside the withdrawal clause. She didn’t hurry him.

The index slip was still in his inside pocket. He could search again outside, but he didn’t know which program Xiao Lu would move to, or whether another channel would let him speak.

He covered the participation box. “It says eligibility ends. Not that the index becomes invalid.”

“You keep the paper,” Wang Hai said. “You’re still here. You don’t have to agree to everything just to stay.”

After a moment, Lin Mo said, “You haven’t got your name back.”

Wang Hai tucked his factory badge inside his clothes. “Don’t use that to talk yourself into it. My bucket’s still hanging at 08. Three changes of clothes won’t make it walk over.”

Sun Ning leaned against a companion chair. “What do you get afterward?”

Xiao Lu unfolded the settlement terms: verification of one personal item carried into this session. No access to anyone else’s materials; no guarantee of returning an original that did not exist.

“My shirt isn’t with me,” Sun Ning said.

“A handover record will do. It has to be yours.”

“What if I only remember having it?”

Xiao Lu shook her head. Sun Ning put her notebook away without applying, biting her lower lip. Lin Mo waited until she released it, then brought his gray cuff to the stage edge.

“I’ll take part.”

“Confirmed?” asked the attendant.

“This activity only. The jacket isn’t payment.”

The man ticked the register. When Lin Mo refused to sign a blank space, he turned it over and indicated the space after the activity name, adding nothing else.

The scar tightened as Lin Mo gripped the pencil. He switched hands. Half the final character leaned sideways, but he finished the “black” component.

Wang Hai waited until the paper was clear. “Need a signature for every measurement?”

“To confirm personal participation.”

“Then don’t use it afterward to say he was delighted.”

The attendant tucked the pencil behind his ear.

Xiao Lu helped remove the jacket. Lin Mo supported his right cuff; she eased it down from his left shoulder. The blue top and skirt reappeared under the lights. Someone whistled behind a transparent panel.

Wang Hai reached for the jacket. The woman’s voice reminded them it must remain in its owner’s custody.

Lin Mo pointed to a chair. “Within reach counts?”

“You may not leave the measurement area.”

“I’m asking about the clothes.”

“They may be placed within your personal custody marking.”

The attendant nudged the chair inside a white rectangle. Lin Mo folded the jacket on its seat, cuffs uppermost. Wang Hai retreated.

“Call out if you need to. Don’t stop breathing every time it hurts.”

“You still can’t come in.”

“I can write it down. Next time it claims you weren’t hurting, who’s going to speak up?”

Lin Mo smoothed the skirt at his waist.

The tape came around his back and across his chest. Measuring over the blue cloth without touching skin, the attendant moved down to his waist. The figures were much smaller than those recorded when he enlisted.

Lin Mo drew breath, expanding his ribs.

“Breathe normally.”

“I am.”

“You’re holding it.”

Xiao Lu lowered her palm. He breathed out slowly. The tape tightened, and the attendant crossed out a figure.

For his shoulders, Lin Mo tried to draw them back as usual. Pain ran down his left arm to the elbow. He stopped. The tape slipped from his neck; the attendant caught it.

“This isn’t posture practice. Don’t change my measurements.”

“His shoulder’s injured,” Wang Hai said.

“I see.”

The man measured along the shoulder seam instead of making him raise his arms. Before Lin Mo could question its accuracy, he entered the figure separately: measured along seam, verification pending.

“That cloth never fitted,” Wang Hai called.

“Hence the verification.”

“Don’t measure fabric and call it bone.”

The attendant looked up. “Your line of work?”

“Get a measurement wrong in the workshop, you redo the job.”

“Here too. On the person.”

Wang Hai lowered his hand. His lips moved, but the joking stopped.

The choices appeared. One preview reshaped Lin Mo’s waist and chest beneath the blue top; the other drew his shoulders and back inward. Both retained his face. Both called him Lin Mo, written with a different Mo: jasmine.

He pointed. “My name isn’t being measured.”

“A preview designation. Not the registered name for this activity.”

He raised his signed sheet for the camera. “Use this.”

Xiao Lu held it flat, covering the blank fields. “Registered name: Lin Mo.”

The caption changed. Someone behind a panel clicked their tongue. Why fuss over one character? Wang Hai lifted his factory badge toward them without speaking the name he had yet to reclaim.

Voting began. No feedback window this time. Lin Mo didn’t want narrower shoulders. His left arm could no longer support him properly; if they took the strength from his back too, he might not even get close enough for another handshake.

Waist and chest passed 60%, stopping at 64%.

> Winning option: Execute.

The other outline went dark. Xiao Lu drew a fabric screen around the stand.

“Why are you blocking it?” a viewer demanded.

“The measurement form permits outlines of the relevant areas. This isn’t a stripping show.”

The woman confirmed that clothes would remain. The broadcast cut to the scale on the stand. Lin Mo could see Wang Hai above the screen, his good hand extended. Not a thumbs-up, just an open palm. Still here.

It began beneath Lin Mo’s ribs. His breath shortened. Both sides of his waist tightened as though bound by tape. He reached for the jacket but caught only its cuff. Xiao Lu nudged the chair half an inch closer, staying outside the white rectangle.

Clutching the old cloth, he leaned against the stand. A sliding block dug into his back. He moved forward and caught the upright instead.

Wang Hai crouched to check his feet beneath the screen. Xiao Lu made him stand. He pointed at the upright.

“There’s a catch. Don’t hold the moving part.”

Lin Mo slid his hand to a locked joint. His left arm still ached; he couldn’t put his full weight on it.

“Can you stand without holding on?” asked the attendant.

“No.”

“Then hold on until we finish.”

He bent his knees, lifting his back clear of the block. His toes crowded together inside his socks. He spread his feet and watched the white rectangle until the spots cleared from his eyes.

The blue hem crept upward. He tried to pull it down, but his knuckles caught against newly taut fabric across his chest. The stand rattled. He released the jacket. A seam had left a line across his palm.

“Keep your back clear.”

He moved, knees weakening. As his waist contracted, his balance shifted and his toes reached the white border.

“Feet,” Xiao Lu said. “Keep your socks off the line.”

He drew them back. His legs weren’t lengthening, but he couldn’t stand the same way. His upper body pulled forward. He staggered his feet to stay upright.

A family scene appeared onscreen. He sat in a hospital chair wearing the blue top. His sister held out the gray jacket.

“You forgot this, sis.”

Sis. His hand rose. The onscreen sleeve waited while a corner of his real jacket slid off the chair, its patch brushing his skirt.

“Where did you get the blue clothes?” Wang Hai asked.

Lin Mo opened his mouth. Nothing came.

“Not the image!” Sun Ning told Wang Hai. “Have him touch the receipt.”

Lin Mo reached into the jacket. The receipt was folded behind his ID. He couldn’t pull it out, only press a nail against its edge. On its back were the words he’d written in 09:

> Male on entry.
> Received blue clothes.

He dug his nail against the paper, repeating received blue clothes silently. He stopped reaching for the offered sleeve.

“Nine,” he managed.

Wang Hai repeated it aloud. “Room 09. Not the hospital.”

The process ended. Cognitive overwrite rose from 30% to 34%. Lin Mo pointed at the scene still playing, but the woman announced only synchronized performance recording, and Physical modification: 31%.

The attendant entered new figures and brought out the tape again.

“Stand straight.”

Lin Mo blocked him. “You’ve measured.”

“Verifying the result.”

He indicated the register. Lin Mo examined it: initial measurements and executed changes were separate. No satisfaction box, no recommendation for another round. He lowered his hand.

This time he didn’t draw breath. The attendant read a figure; Xiao Lu repeated it. A fine line separated the old numbers, still legible.

“Give me a copy.”

The man tore off the duplicate. Wang Hai moved it clear of the tape so it wouldn’t crumple. He didn’t ask how much had changed.

Lin Mo put his jacket on. The blue top caught beneath his new chest, its cuffs tight. He pulled the gray fabric together, but the zipper strained at his waist. What had been a quick movement now required one hand supporting the hem while the other eased the zip upward.

“The clothes haven’t shrunk,” Wang Hai said.

At the light permitting him to approach, he steadied the zipper’s base. It pressed into Lin Mo’s abdomen. He flinched and let go.

Wang Hai pushed the tape back onto the cart and held the chair while Lin Mo sat. Sun Ning kicked a lifted mat flat in the aisle and waited for him to stand again, bracing against the seat. His waistband slipped. He gripped it with his left hand.

“I want to verify the deposit note on this jacket.”

Xiao Lu handed him the activity sheet with a verification form underneath. He supported it on the back of his hand. She was already facing the camera, straightening the nameplate.

> Outerwear deposit note: One verification available.
