# Chapter 21: Don't Answer for Me

Xiao Lu pushed the pen back beside the paper and pointed to the script button. It was still gray. The shoe-return field beside it remained lit.

Lin Mo looked down. The buckled shoes no longer felt as loose as when he'd first put them on. His socks creased over their openings. He started to bend, struck his wrist against the jacket zipper, and straightened again.

“Sit down to take them off,” Xiao Lu said.

Wang Hai turned the stool outside the exit line and stood behind it. Lin Mo left the white lane before sitting. He opened the first buckle left-handed, but the strap caught on the metal tongue. Scraping it twice with his nail did nothing.

“Feed it back,” Wang Hai said. “Pulling makes it bite harder.”

He pushed. The tongue dropped and his foot came free. Cold flooded through the sock sole into the space the shoe had enclosed. The second came off faster. Wang Hai supported the box with his right hand.

“Planning to keep them?”

“No.”

“Your foot's out. You're still holding the shoe.”

Lin Mo let go and pushed the box into the return slot. A green light came on. The shoes slid inward; his feet stayed on the floor. Wang Hai crouched to smooth his sock soles, keeping the gauze on his left hand clear of the ground.

“They don't take back what they grew, though.”

Lin Mo tucked his feet beneath the stool and waited for the chill to pass. The script button lit. He stood, borrowing the stool back for support. Xiao Lu remained in her stage mark rather than crossing the exit line to join him.

He scanned the cooperation page beneath the button. The two signatures registered. Instead of opening the application, the screen played a preview.

Two long-haired women sat across a dining table. Xiao Lu pushed a bowl toward the other. The face that lifted to meet hers was Lin Mo's present face.

> Reunion.

“I'm glad you're home,” the recorded Xiao Lu said.

His left foot had already moved toward the stage. He caught the stool back, heel pressing his sock, and stopped short of completing the step. A paper corner jabbed his chest through the jacket. He reached inside and found the receipt's torn edge.

“I'm not doing this one.”

Wang Hai didn't press anything for him. Onscreen, Lin Mo sat and reached for the bowl.

“Sorry to keep you waiting,” his recorded voice said softly.

Xiao Lu covered her microphone.

“Did you choose that?”

“No.”

She paused the preview. The two hands beside the bowl froze onscreen. The livestream light stayed red. People called for them to play the whole thing.

> one show together and now it's a reunion?

> works for me. maybe she'll quit glaring

> drink the soup before it gets cold

Lin Mo withdrew his hand and wiped his palm on his cuff.

“The application isn't signed. Where did the lines come from?”

“A shared performance can generate recommended scripts,” the woman replied. “Unconfirmed content does not take effect.”

Xiao Lu turned to the project page and tapped recommended with the pen.

“Show him the others. I didn't choose this either.”

The table shrank to a thumbnail beside a list of alternatives. Lin Mo passed two response segments and stopped at Mutual Questions. The preview held two microphones. No dining table, no one agreeing on someone else's behalf to come home.

Asked to enter his questions, he left the pen and turned to Xiao Lu.

“I want to ask whether you've been to a particular hospital.”

“Asking everyone you meet?”

“Only you.”

She lifted the paper from the rail, out of the angle of the moving camera below.

“Why?”

His fingers caught the edge of the patch on his gray cuff. He didn't remove the jacket or offer her the sleeve.

“The person I'm looking for wore this.”

Her pen stopped. She glanced from the cuff to his face.

“Does that person look like me?”

“In places.”

“The footage can give you a resemblance.”

Wang Hai retrieved the shoe-return receipt and passed it into Lin Mo's free left hand. Lin Mo folded it away without using it to answer her.

“That's why I want to ask. Whatever you say is what stays.”

“Do you want to ask me something, or have me play her?”

Someone laughed. Lin Mo looked past Xiao Lu at the paused bowl. Her finger still held the pause button; the table light whitened the edge of her nail.

“Not play her. If you don't remember, say you don't remember.”

She didn't nod along. Holding the pen between her fingers, she moved half a step toward the stage edge.

“What's the hospital called?”

He reached into his pocket. The receipt's folded edge pressed into his fingertips. He unfolded the side bearing the payment-window stamp, leaving the handwritten lines on the back folded inward.

A camera tilted up. His thumb covered the name field; he raised his other hand to block the lens, but the paper slipped against his injured wrist. Wang Hai stood beside him, shoulder screening the camera. Someone knocked on a table.

“What's the point if we can't see the words?”

Lin Mo eased Wang Hai back, leaving the stamp facing Xiao Lu. Without saying his sister's name, he read the hospital's. His voice rose sharply through the speakers on the last syllable.

Xiao Lu didn't try to uncover the name.

“Which floor were you on?”

“I'm asking the questions.”

“Mutual Questions.” She tapped the option. “You can refuse. When I ask, tell me you won't answer.”

He folded the receipt. A cropped image of its stamp had appeared onscreen; viewers were already comparing it to old hospital footage. He tried to close it. The button shut only his own preview, leaving the audience's picture displayed.

Wang Hai called to him quietly. Lin Mo lowered his hand and pushed the receipt to the bottom of his inner pocket, thumb resting against the tear.

“I was accompanying someone. Don't use these clothes to put me back into that scene.”

Xiao Lu rested a hand on her microphone without responding to the old footage. Her pen marked the order of questions.

“You start. Then I get my turn before your next question.”

At the bottom, she struck out the recommended standard answer.

“Delete this field. Don't leave it blank.”

Lin Mo turned the request to the host. The field vanished. The woman announced that the performance would use their live answers; recommended footage could not answer for them. Xiao Lu still didn't sign. She continued reading.

Once the round began, the final clause said, performance would continue on both channels. One performer's departure would not stop the other's timer.

She pressed the paper corner beneath Lin Mo's finger.

“Read this too.”

“You leave and I keep performing?”

“Whichever of us leaves. I haven't agreed to stand there until you're done.”

He looked at the empty timer. Two settlement options appeared beside it. He opened Joint Ending. The host announced a six-minute limit for the next show, with audience interactions still permitted before it ended.

Wang Hai came forward from behind the stool.

“Six minutes, then. Her being onstage doesn't mean you have to accept everything.”

Xiao Lu capped the pen and held out her hand.

“Print it. Same relationship as before. Leave the names alone.”

Lin Mo started to lift his right hand, lowered it, and pushed the page toward the reader with his left. Xiao Lu stood opposite without pressing confirmation for him.

He entered collaborators for this performance and had the script reprinted. She checked it from top to bottom. When she looked up, he was still standing there.

“What else?”

“Before we go in, I need to see someone.”

Her mouth tightened. He took back the unsigned application instead of swiping halfway through an explanation, and pointed outside.

“She helped save me. Her arm got trapped beneath a cart. She's resting.”

Xiao Lu laid the pen on the microphone stand.

“I still have this round to close. Don't come back through the audience.”

He kept the application separate from the cooperation record. Wang Hai had picked up his water, but stopped in the doorway when Lin Mo reached toward the preview board again.

“Picture still there?”

“They've got the hospital name.”

“You showed her.”

Lin Mo nodded. His hand fell against his pocket. Someone wanted him to unfold the inpatient number too. He stayed away from the camera, flattened the paper, and followed the exit line out.

Wang Hai walked with him, carrying his own water. Lin Mo was slower than he'd been coming in, floor seams passing beneath his socked feet. Turning the corner, he leaned his left shoulder lightly against the wall instead of bracing with his injured wrist.

The rest-area door stood open. Sun Ning sat in the outer chair, sealed bag in her lap, water at her feet. At the sound of steps she reached for her notebook, relaxing only when she recognized them.

“Grown your hair out now?”

He pushed a lock from his mouth. It fell back as soon as his hand dropped. She pointed to the next chair, and he sat without arguing.

“One more show,” he said.

“I didn't say I was coming.”

“I'm not asking you to. I want to talk to her.”

Sun Ning looked toward the doorway. Half a stage screen was visible. Xiao Lu was retrieving her pen from the microphone stand, neither holding the gray jacket nor sitting at a dining table.

“She agreed?”

“Read the script. Hasn't confirmed her channel.”

“Then don't call it agreement.”

Lin Mo lowered his head. Beneath the chair his socked toes pointed differently now. He spread the application over his knees for her left hand to turn.

“Listen for me. I'm asking about the hospital. If I start talking about rehearsing with her before, call Wang Hai.”

“You still remember that bit?”

He nodded. “She tells me to try on the clothes. Her voice is right here.” He pointed beside his ear without touching his hair. “It didn't happen.”

She pushed the page back and pointed to live answers at the top.

“I'll listen to this time. I wasn't there before.”

Wang Hai set water between the chairs and tapped six minutes.

“I'll time you. If she doesn't want to answer, don't ask into the seventh minute.”

“I know.”

“You knew when you were walking, too. Your feet still rushed ahead.”

Sun Ning laughed, then tucked her right arm against her waist. Lin Mo saw the bruising at her sleeve and didn't ask her to lift it. She shifted the sealed bag to make room for him to fold the application.

He waited until his calves stopped shaking before standing. Wang Hai didn't hurry him, walking half a pace outside to leave him the wall. Sun Ning stayed in her chair, water at her feet, door open.

The reunion preview was gone. Xiao Lu waited at the entrance. Lin Mo set the paper between them. She picked it up and lowered the pen toward her name field.

“After both channels confirm, Joint Ending locks the duration at six minutes,” the woman announced. “The audience may submit new proposals within this performance's scope.”

“Including her name?” Lin Mo asked.

Xiao Lu lifted the pen before the ink touched paper.

“Identity display is eligible for proposals in this performance.”

At the back of the audience, someone raised a voting board. Lin Mo recognized the hand beneath the sleeve. The two fingers he'd forced back during their handshake still wouldn't close together.

“Xiao Lu's a lovely name,” the man said. “Let's try one she used before. See whether she agrees.”

She put down her pen and addressed the host.

“He hasn't submitted a proposal. Don't announce it passed.”

Lin Mo reached for the script. She pinned the corner before he could draw it away.

“You said you wouldn't make me play her.”

“Yes.”

“If they vote up a name, you don't get to start calling me that.”

He looked at her hand and left his credential alone.

“I don't identify people by vote count.”

She released the paper and wrote Xiao Lu in her own field. The channel-confirmation light came on. Two microphones turned toward the entrance. The six-minute timer waited for them to enter.

Outside the observer line, Wang Hai called his name. When Lin Mo turned, he pointed at the script's first item and tapped beside his own ear with his right hand.

Lin Mo tucked the paper inside his jacket and went to his microphone. His socked feet reached the mark. Hair covered half his view; he pushed it behind his ear.

Under the opposite light, Xiao Lu spoke.

“You're the one who wants to ask me about the hospital. Right?”

“Right.”

She lowered the microphone a little, looking toward the inside of his jacket.

“Then tell me. If you went there looking for someone, why do you have a payment receipt?”
