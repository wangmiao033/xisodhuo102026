# Chapter 19: Walk Across

The buckled shoes stood at the new program's entrance, toes pointing toward the stage. Lin Mo looked down at them without putting a foot inside.

“These are the ones they want you wearing?” Wang Hai asked.

“I never wore the earlier pair.”

The soles of his socks were dirty. Every seam in the cold floor pressed through them. Holding the doorframe with his left hand, he shifted the broad strip around his gray jacket so it wouldn't cut into his stomach when he bent.

The entrance notice lit up.

> Shoes are props for this performance and must be returned afterward.
> Demonstration restricted to the white lane. Unauthorized substitutions prohibited.

“The size is wrong,” Lin Mo said.

“Fitting follows selection of the demonstration template.”

“Changing the person again,” Wang Hai said.

Neither pair on the preview board would have fit Lin Mo before he entered. Low heels unlocked a female foot, ankle, and leg template. High heels added posture control. Both required the prescribed walk.

He curled his toes inside his socks, as if that could keep the preview lines from reaching him. Above them, the host's name matched the index. Xiao Lu stood across the stage, adjusting a low stool with her back turned.

Wang Hai pointed at the exit light. “This one hasn't started.”

“I see it.”

“You're afraid she'll be gone by the next show. You still need to hear all of this one.”

Lin Mo left the shoes alone and read on. Completing the demonstration earned eligibility to invite someone to a shared performance. It did not automatically grant two-person livestream access or waive the invitee's personal confirmation.

He kept the final line visible for Wang Hai.

“I can ask her to work with me.”

“Ask. Doesn't mean she has to come.”

“I know.”

Wang Hai reached to tighten the loose gauze around his left hand, then remembered he had only his right to work with. Lin Mo supported it with his left, letting Wang Hai pull the end tight himself.

“Not too tight.”

“You tied yours the same way.”

Lin Mo looked at his wrist. Padding covered the white scar. It hadn't swollen again but still hurt to touch. He released the bandage and scanned his response card. The welcome-round lettering dimmed; stride demonstration brightened. Behind him, the exit button stayed green.

He stood still. Wang Hai pulled his factory badge from his collar and flashed it at the camera.

“Leave this out of the shot. I just got it back. I'm not performing another collection for you today.”

Lin Mo finally looked away from the exit button.

“You're allowed to sit there without talking.”

“If I don't talk, who warns you when you step on a line?”

“Just talk about my feet.”

“Writing my lines now?”

Wang Hai tucked the badge into his gray shirt, fastening turned inward. At the reader he used his right hand, leaving his injured left alone, and waited for Lin Mo to decide.

They scanned their own credentials. Wang Hai selected accompanying observer again, without taking on Lin Mo's demonstration. This stage was longer than the welcome-round stage. Red lines flanked a central white lane. Entering either side area counted as leaving position; reaching the far end was the task.

Xiao Lu turned, saw the gray jacket, and glanced at his socks.

“Don't try walking in them yet. Wait until the template's settled.”

“Were they always that size?”

“They weren't kept for the feet you've got now.”

She crouched to loosen the low-heeled pair's straps, then put them back. She left the high heels alone. A narrow heel clicked against the stage; the audience in front had already begun hitting their buttons.

At the selection mark, Lin Mo checked the rule: one vote each, highest total executed. He held a shoe sole-first toward the camera without lifting his skirt.

“If it's a walking show, let me walk. Haven't you got a standing-still show next door?”

> new route, same cheap option

> high heels look better

> quit stalling. i want to see her walk over

“High-heel posture control restricts independent turning,” the woman added.

Lin Mo pointed at the stool near the far end. “If I can't turn, who's taking me around that?”

“Follow the demonstration route.”

“Then if I knock it over, you'll have to wait until I'm finished walking.”

Xiao Lu moved the stool aside. It hadn't been inside the white lane; he'd only used it to ask. But the audience could now see how long and narrow the route was.

“Low heels still mean modification,” she told them. “Both options show the scope. Shoes aren't a way to restore him.”

Lin Mo promised no one a good walk. He set the shoe down and waited inside the mark.

> Low heels: 63%.
> High heels: 37%.

Wang Hai kept a hand on his chair back. Cold spread into Lin Mo's toes as though the floor were seeping through the bones. He seized the entrance rail. His feet slowly narrowed, leaving space where his toes had filled the socks.

Pain climbed his insteps and tightened behind his knees. He tried to brace his feet as he used to; his knees turned inward first, tipping him against the rail. Wang Hai stood. His observer light flashed red, and he withdrew the half-step he'd taken.

“Hold on. Don't force yourself upright.”

Lin Mo turned sideways and got both feet back inside the mark. Fabric tightened along his outer thighs. Beneath the jacket, his skirt pulled downward and the broad strip bit into his waist. The knot hadn't loosened; his body had forced it elsewhere. His right hand tried to support it. Pain in the scar curled his fingers back, leaving his left hand to hold the rail.

Xiao Lu pointed at the strip. “You can loosen what holds your outer layer. Don't touch the costume seams.”

He waited for the voice to confirm, then undid the slipknot left-handed. The jacket fell open around his sides, gray cloth held out by unfamiliar contours. He looked down without trying to flatten them again.

He attempted a step and began tipping sideways. His left knee shook. Xiao Lu set the shoes beside his mark and retreated.

“Can you let go?” Wang Hai asked.

“Not yet.”

“Then hold on. It's not a pull-up bar.”

Lin Mo eased his fingers a fraction. Paint rubbed onto his palm. He left it. Wiping his hand and grabbing again could make it slip.

“Do you have to keep staring?”

“You told me to watch the floor.”

“My feet. Not how many seconds I can hold on.”

“Fine. Stop putting on a show for me, then.”

Xiao Lu lifted a shoe, pointing beneath its strap.

“When the light turns white, I'll put it here. Keep holding on while you put them on.”

He nodded. “Wait for white. Don't come forward on red.”

A sharp crack sounded beneath the rail. He looked down, expecting broken wood. His socked heel had crushed a piece of plastic packaging, leaving a white crease. His feet were still changing. He stayed put and waited for the pain to recede.

Wang Hai remained standing, occasionally hiding his left hand inside his clothes. Xiao Lu asked the host to keep the camera on the performer. Someone said the two of them looked more nervous than the person performing. She picked up the packaging without answering.

> Execution complete.
> Physical modification: 38%.
> Cognitive overwrite: 36%.

The light turned white. Lin Mo bent to reach a shoe. His waist tightened, and his hand arrived later than expected, missing the strap and pushing the shoe away.

“Hasn't grown legs,” Wang Hai said. “Don't chase it.”

Lin Mo steadied himself and slipped his left foot inside. His heel settled neatly, with a little room left at the toe.

“I'm finding my balance.”

“Put the other one on first. Try it when both sides are the same height.”

Wang Hai pointed without reaching across his line. Lin Mo looked at his right foot, still on the floor.

“I hadn't forgotten.”

“Sure. I'm waiting.”

Lin Mo put it on. The strap had to be worked free of his sock. One pull hurt his wrist, so he stopped and threaded the buckle slowly with his left hand.

“Second hole.”

“This one?”

“That's the third. It's not a rope. Tighter isn't better.”

He moved back one hole. Standing no longer pinched his instep.

His first step landed inside the white line. The sole held, but he'd lifted his leg too high and wavered. He retied the strip more loosely around his jacket, securing the skirt without trying to force his waist to its old size. Fabric checked his knees. His old stride wouldn't fit; his next step had to be shorter.

> definitely never worn those before

> all that mouth and now she's crawling

Wang Hai stood at his boundary. “Behind the shoe. Left strap's twisted.”

Lin Mo bent a knee but couldn't reach. Bracing himself on a short post, he lowered into a crouch. He flattened the strap where it had pressed into his sock. His fingertips touched the cold floor, making him realize how much height the soles added.

When he rose, his skirt caught against one knee. His old motion to shake it loose pulled his feet sideways. At the far end, Xiao Lu raised a palm to stop him.

“Just smooth it.”

He separated the hem from his socks with his left hand. The fabric lay against newly narrowed legs; what had been spare length now hung unevenly. He eased out the fold without pulling the waistband.

The audience called for a lower angle. The host kept the whole lane in view. Hearing a low camera turn, Lin Mo slowed. Xiao Lu pointed at the requirement for a complete stride demonstration.

“Keep his whole body in. We need to see whether he finishes.”

“Of course I know,” came the reply. She withdrew her microphone. Wang Hai stepped back half a pace and went quiet, leaving Lin Mo able to hear the shoes meet the tiles.

He stopped searching for cameras and watched two different-colored tiles. By the third, his breathing had found his steps. He tried letting go of the post.

Xiao Lu waited at the end beside another microphone, fingers resting on the stool's back edge. She didn't hurry him.

Five steps away, he could see the end of her eyebrow. He remembered a faint mark in the hospital footage but not exactly where it had been. His eyes fixed there, and his body hurried after them.

His left toe caught a seam.

“Bring it back!” Wang Hai shouted.

He pulled his foot back, but his weight had gone forward. The wood in his pocket struck his thigh. His fingers grazed the post and missed.

Xiao Lu turned the stool toward him, keeping its legs outside the endpoint. She brought the back half an inch closer without pushing it into the lane. He caught the crossbar, knees almost hitting the floor. The padding slipped from his right wrist, exposing the white scar.

“Ease your grip,” she said.

He couldn't. His hand shook while the other braced against the seat. She lifted her cuff to shield the injury from the camera, then checked his feet.

“The buckle's fastened. The shoe didn't slip.”

“I took too big a step.”

She withdrew her hand. He still had to finish. Holding the stool, he steadied himself, worked the sock bunched beneath his toes loose, and moved on.

His last two steps barely cleared the floor. Only when his heel entered the endpoint mark did the woman announce completion. Instead of dismissing him, the light revealed an invitation button beneath the microphone.

He didn't press it. Supporting his right wrist with his left, he released a breath he'd been holding. The buckle flashed. He checked it, then moved slightly to make room inside his mark. Xiao Lu remained in her own.

She fetched water from backstage, opened it, and held it beside his left hand.

“Slowly.”

The bottle dented in his palm. Swallowing hurt, stopping him for a moment. Her hand stayed beneath the bottle without urging him on.

Across the observer line, Wang Hai lowered the water he'd just opened.

Lin Mo offered the bottle back. As Xiao Lu reached for it, his left arm suddenly gave. Water spilled over her name badge.

She didn't reach for the badge first. Her hand passed under the bottle and closed around his forearm.
