# Chapter 3: The Clothes Stay. You Change.

Lin Mo didn't touch the jacket. He lifted its cuff with the chair leg and held it beneath the cupboard light.

Pale gray. A patch barely larger than a thumbnail on the outside of the sleeve. Cream thread changed to light blue near the end, with a tight little pucker where the colors met.

He remembered why she'd changed thread. His sister had cut it too short for the last few stitches. Only blue remained in her sewing kit, and she couldn't be bothered borrowing more from the nurses' station. She'd asked if anyone could tell.

He'd said no. She'd held the sleeve in front of his eyes.

“Look again.”

The jacket hung motionless. He set down the wood and pinched the patch. The surrounding cloth was soft, the repair stiffer. He lifted a corner with his nail and found the thread ends tucked beneath it.

Nothing bit his hand. No voice called to him. He lifted it from the hanger.

“The conditions for collecting stored clothing have not been met,” the woman said.

A speaker sat at the mirror's upper right corner, the same kind as in the dressing room.

“Picking it up counts as collecting it?”

“Please complete this round's costume change first.”

He spread the jacket on a chair. Both pockets were empty.

Since entering the hospital, his sister had stuffed them with tissues, hair ties, candy. He bought the candy. She complained of a bitter taste after meals, ate one, then hid the rest until the nurse left.

At the bottom of the left pocket, his fingers found a thin, hard shape inside the lining. He held it to the light. Someone had opened a narrow slit along the seam and secured it with two hasty stitches, worse than those on the cuff. He slipped two fingers through.

A torn strip of pills. Two blisters remained, one empty. On the foil were words he'd written in ballpoint.

> After food.
> Half a tablet.

He'd corrected the second line. When the ink failed, he'd scratched three test strokes beside it before finishing the last character.

He gripped the foil.

“Why'd you hide it here?”

Only after speaking did he realize he was addressing the jacket. He'd divided her doses, returned hours later, and found the pills gone from the table. She'd complained about her stomach. He'd assumed she'd taken them.

Half a tablet lay uneaten in her pocket.

He found a clean spot to put it down and wiped his fingers twice on his trousers. Anger came at an absurdly wrong moment. When he found her, this was the first thing he'd ask about.

> he can recognize that too?

He closed his hand around the pills and turned his back on the mirror. No bell. No one here had told him he had to face the glass.

He searched the lining. Below the back collar, black thread fastened a narrow paper tag behind the label. He folded the collar back.

> PERSONAL PROPERTY IN SAFEKEEPING
> Received: Before the start of this round
> Original holder: Restricted information
> Authorized collector: Lin Mo

A blue stamp marked his name. The back was blank.

He compared the blood on his hand with the jacket. No fresh blood inside the cuff, no smeared writing on the foil. This was something he could hold and examine. The patch and his handwriting both matched.

But how long before the round? Who had brought it here?

“I want to see the intake record.”

“Please complete this round's costume change first.”

“Then I can see it?”

Silence. He pointed to his name.

“It says me. I'm not collecting it for someone else?”

“Please complete this round's task to collect your item.”

“Can I leave her jacket by the door for now?”

“Stored clothing may not leave this round's premises.”

Without opening the door, he dragged the chair toward it. The camera turned at the scraping sound. He stopped, letting the gray cuff hang out of shot.

“Here, then.”

No further warning. He arranged the jacket so its pockets wouldn't touch the floor and stepped back into the light. His wrist brushed the chair; the red ring burned again. The wood in his other hand rose half an inch before he stopped himself.

After a moment, he draped the jacket over the chair back instead. He kept the pills out of the lining.

Three rows of pictures appeared on the mirror: a white outfit, dark blue, black.

> COSTUME CHANGE
> Task: Collect one costume and complete a fitting.
> 1. Upper and lower garments must belong to the same set.
> 2. Choices may be changed before collection is scanned. Collection cannot be reversed.
> 3. Return old clothing after completing the fitting. Personal effects may be retained.
> 4. The audience must approve the fitting.
> Complete collection before the countdown ends.

Ten minutes. He approached the racks.

The white set was a thin top with no buttons at the neck, and trousers. A short dark blue jacket hung above a skirt. The black set had a long coat and trousers. Matching numbered seals joined each outfit inside its plastic cover.

He felt the black coat first. Broader shoulders than his current jacket, long enough trouser legs. On the back of its size tag were measurements for shoulders, chest, waist, and leg length. No price.

A small machine stood against the wall, a glass plate at its center. Light leaked around a handleless door beside it; he could see a mirror inside.

> COLLECTION SCANNER

He carried the black outfit over, barcode downward, then stopped. Once scanned, he couldn't change his mind. Another button beside the machine sat beneath a clear plastic cover.

> PREVIEW OUTFIT

He put the clothes aside and pressed it. The voting display shrank into the mirror's lower left corner. A figure appeared at the center, wearing black.

It had his face, but its shoulders were wrong, far too broad. A thicker neck. Veins stood out on the hands below its sleeves. It turned, and his chest tightened.

In profile, it resembled his face before he'd arrived: broad jaw, pronounced brow. But two old scars marked its cheek, and its mouth twisted to one side.

He raised his right hand. The figure didn't follow. It finished its programmed turn and stood straight.

> Preview of your appearance after fitting.
> Actual fitting is subject to the approved result.

He clenched the plastic.

“You resize the clothes, or the person?”

“Costumes may not be altered.”

He left the scanner alone. After checking the timer, he returned the black outfit and took the white one.

The preview's shoulders narrowed. Its top clung at the waist; the trousers tapered below the knees. A face smaller than his current one, hair covering the forehead, chin lowered as though it never wanted to look up. This was nothing like his old face.

The counter beside the mirror jumped. Hearts appeared. He closed the preview.

Dark blue remained. The skirt reached roughly to the knees. Two rows of decorative buttons lined the collarless jacket. Comparing its measurements with his arms and shoulders, he thought the clothes themselves looked closest to his current size.

He set them beside the scanner and pressed preview.

The figure lifted its head and turned. Lin Mo watched its shoulders, hands, face. Changes, but smaller than either alternative. The jacket fitted close, calves bare beneath the skirt.

Then it did something extra. Its hands folded across its lower stomach, and the corners of its mouth rose.

He shut it off.

> don't close it

> blue blue blue

> how long would he last in black?

> he's shopping for the cheapest option

Just over six minutes left. His sister's jacket hung with its lining exposed, the tag showing his name. He went to straighten it.

He couldn't leave these things here. He put the pills in the inside pocket of his own jacket, separate from his ID so the foil wouldn't scratch it. A damp corner of the payment receipt had stuck around his wallet. He eased them apart, still losing a little paper. He touched his keys in his trouser pocket and left them there.

The chair leg went beside the scanner. Did it count as a personal effect? He hadn't brought it in, but it wasn't old clothing.

> keep that

> what's he gonna do with it?

He picked it up, keeping his carved name toward himself. Nothing in the rules required him to surrender it. He took the blue outfit again.

“After scanning, what do I have to do to finish?”

“Complete the fitting and obtain audience approval.”

“If they won't approve?”

“You may adjust according to audience feedback.”

“The clothes can't be changed.”

“Costumes may not be altered.”

“Undo a bit of the cuff seam?”

“Fittings must display the original garments.”

He drew his right wrist clear and held it up.

“What if it rubs the injury?”

The woman told him to complete the fitting. Someone in chat demanded black; another wondered whether he could even get his hand through a sleeve.

He opened the bag without answering, supporting the collar in his left hand so his injured one wouldn't need to pull the seal. Through the plastic, the skirt pressed against his knees.

All three options would cost him. The previews offered only a rough idea. He couldn't even find out what they would change. The black outfit looked like bait: perhaps it would restore his face, then add something else.

White was smaller still. The corridor's red ring remained around his wrist, painful whenever his sleeve touched it. He didn't have the strength to smash another mirror.

He tore open the blue outfit's cover, removed the clothes, and laid its numbered seal on the glass. Light swept beneath it. The door opened.

> Collection complete.
> Please enter the fitting room.

He draped both garments over his left arm, chair leg in his right, but stayed outside.

“When do I get her jacket back?”

He pointed at the chair.

> After fitting approval, you may collect the stored clothing and its handover record.

A handover record. His fingertip paused beneath the words.

A signature, number, or time. Even that would be better than restricted information.

He lifted the gray jacket. Something clicked overhead, like a breath drawn at a microphone. He only moved it to a chair by the fitting-room entrance. No warning appeared.

Sitting down, he took off his wet jacket and moved the keys and wallet into his trousers. ID, pills, receipt: he stacked them on the chair and weighted them with the wood, carving downward.

His black jacket already fitted loosely. He touched its shoulder seam, remembering the position, then picked up the blue top.

It smelled of laundry powder. An ordinary smell, the same as his sister's jacket. The little shop below the hospital sold that brand.

He turned the top inside out and found the label. Size on the front. A printed line on the back had been folded into the seam. He pried the edge loose with the screw.

> The wearer must maintain a smile until audience approval.

He stopped. That was how the preview had smiled.

The timer reached zero. A red light came on above the fitting room.

“Collection complete. Changes are not permitted.”

He looked up as the small print faded from the label.

He hadn't put the clothes on yet. Already his mouth was beginning to smile.
