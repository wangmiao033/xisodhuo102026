# Chapter 17: I Didn’t Say That

Wang Hai clipped his badge on, his hand lingering at his collar. Seven tapped dried ink from her brush against the ink pad.

“Why are you blocking my door?”

“Going,” he said, still smiling.

Lin Mo rounded her desk and followed the sound to the screen outside. The broad cloth tie was knotted around his gray jacket. Walking, he still reached for his waistband, remembering halfway that it was secured. Wang Hai let him lower his hand without a reminder.

The broadcast had changed. Xiao Lu’s stage was clear, her box gone, the nameplate still at her collar. Below her, a new edit played: Lin Mo in blue at the microphone.

“I’m excited.”

It froze as he exposed his right wrist.

> Newcomer chooses his own scar. A happy keepsake.

Lin Mo gripped the frame. His wrist, the correct bandage, even his frown.

“Where did happy come from?” Wang Hai asked.

Lin Mo didn’t answer. As his recorded mouth opened, the shot zoomed in on his wrist and the sound stopped. Wang Hai found the speaker beneath the camera, its small holes facing them.

Lin Mo selected full playback. The button was gray. Only the performer in the clip could apply for a correction.

“Doesn’t say you can’t,” Sun Ning said.

“Will applying mean another performance?”

“Provide a source for this session,” the speaker replied. “This activity concerns displayed footage only.”

Lin Mo brought his rigid response card to the reader. It almost slipped between his sweaty fingers. He wiped his palm on his jacket, exposing his pale scar to the lens.

Wang Hai blocked the lower camera with the bottle. “Waiting for you to lift that again.”

Lin Mo covered his wrist and pressed the card flat. This time the camera ignored the bottle, following him wherever Wang Hai moved it.

“You can’t block it forever,” Sun Ning said. “Your arm’ll get tired.”

“I know. Those few seconds still count.”

Wang Hai lowered the bottle and turned its cap between his fingers.

The card verified. The screen displayed the number for the still-unsettled welcome round. Wang Hai took out his notebook; Lin Mo raised a hand.

“It wants the session source. Not your notes.”

“I know. Looking for the time.”

Wang Hai found his mark for the start of feedback. Sun Ning leaned closer, caught her injured arm, then used her left hand to pull closer the support beside the screen.

“Include the time.”

Lin Mo entered neither satisfied nor dissatisfied. A statement beneath the sample text was already checked:

> I confirm that this clip accurately reflects my wishes at the time.

He unchecked it. The page stayed open.

In the reason field he began entering the two options’ vote totals. Pain stabbed at the scar beneath his sleeve before he finished. He stopped and shifted away from the camera.

“Just say you never said you were happy,” Sun Ning told him. “Don’t explain the whole round for it.”

He deleted the explanation, leaving eight characters:

> Never expressed satisfaction. Request verification.

Sun Ning pointed to the summary’s timestamp. He expanded the date, confirmed the session matched his card, and included it. Wang Hai moved the bottle clear so the verification lamp shone unobstructed.

After submission, the beginning played. He had said, “I’m excited.” The rest of the sentence was missing, the response cue light cropped out. It looked spontaneous.

He dragged the timeline to before the microphone turned green. The whole welcome round was selectable, but that stretch offered audio without the prompt. The woman’s countdown sounded over scattered applause.

At his own cough, Wang Hai frowned. “Later. I hadn’t stood up yet.”

Lin Mo moved the pointer. His full sentence played:

“I’m excited about the gift you’ve prepared for me.”

Then the demand that he repeat it, the woman confirming the text was complete and refusing additions.

He stopped there. “I didn’t choose those words.”

Two options appeared: remove this segment, or display the original response text. The removal button still had the injury image beside it. He moved away from delete and chose the original text.

The green light and countdown returned to the corner. A happy keepsake remained.

Wang Hai touched the caption with a knuckle. “That’s wrong too.”

It hadn’t changed with the audio. He tried again, but nothing responded. Sun Ning nudged his hand away before he could press the screen.

“He’s the applicant.”

Lin Mo opened the caption. No edit button, only a report that it did not match the performance text. Selecting it brought a request to confirm public correction.

“Who can change the original?”

“The performer may submit corrections. Audience voting determines the recommended version. Original sources will be retained.”

Keeping the display-only scope visible on the application, he selected public.

The live light turned red. Xiao Lu’s feed cut in. She was still onstage, but didn’t immediately smile. Seeing the correction window, she pointed at his speaker.

“You’re allowed to talk now?”

“For this activity.”

“Then don’t read its headline.”

His application appeared on the transparent panels. Wang Hai tried to move out of shot. Lin Mo had him stand behind him, keeping him out of the staff passage.

The audience’s original summary and his correction reason appeared side by side. Xiao Lu raised a hand, holding the host before the timer began.

She turned her microphone stand slightly. A small sign clipped to it read welcome round. Opening the edit timestamp, she showed its marker after the performance had ended.

“I hosted this round. I never announced a happy keepsake.”

Someone behind a panel said summaries didn’t need her announcements. She smiled and set the sign back, one finger on welcome.

“Then play my actual introduction. Don’t claim I announced it.”

Watching her, Lin Mo held the card against his inside pocket. He wanted to ask about the jacket. She turned and told the host to enlarge the correction page, leaving no opening for private questions.

“Anything else from the applicant?”

“Yes. Keep another second after the cough.”

“Why keep me?” Wang Hai asked.

“You were there. I didn’t dub this afterward.”

Xiao Lu had the host play it. Wang Hai coughed, Sun Ning laughed under her breath, then Lin Mo said he couldn’t even see their faces. The audio continued without a sudden break.

Sun Ning hid her mouth behind her notebook and glanced at the camera. “You really kept my laugh.”

“Want it cut?”

“No. Let them try saying you hired voices afterward.”

The vote opened:

> Keep original summary.
> Recommend corrected version.

The higher total would determine the recommendation. Both sources would remain; changes outside this round were excluded. Lin Mo indicated that scope notice. Wang Hai nodded and put his notebook away without copying it again.

> does he have to tell us he didn’t like it?

> she sure talked tough voting for the scar

> that part really was scripted

At the second comment, his throat shifted and his voice nearly rose again. He pressed the microphone’s edge until the pain passed.

“I chose the one that hurt more. Doesn’t mean I was happy.” He pointed. “Take out chooses his own too. Who supplied the choices?”

Xiao Lu lifted the summary card and turned it over. Wider than the screen frame, it neatly covered the final injury image.

“A recommendation doesn’t decide who someone is,” she told the audience. “The old version, or the one with the text?”

A viewer thumped the table, accusing her of speaking for the newcomer too much. She turned the card frontward and indicated both options without arguing.

The viewer put an original-summary voting marker at the front and raised their voice above the host.

“The camera worked. The words are right. What needs correcting? Picked the scar, and now you want to look forced?”

Lin Mo turned down the speaker. The voice came directly from another feed. He released the knob, restoring his own feedback volume.

“This is my face. Didn’t ask you to change it.”

“The tone was yours.”

“Keep cutting out one sentence and nobody’ll give you more next time.”

“Then shut up.”

“Who’ve you been watching for the last two minutes?”

Behind him, Wang Hai slapped his thigh, not his hands. Sun Ning’s mouth twitched; she pointed at the live light before he could lift them.

The viewer kept talking, but the panel beside them now lit with the text-included option. Someone told them not to decide for the whole audience. Another voice said that with two versions, the original should be available for checking too.

Xiao Lu let them argue. When heads turned behind the panels, she uncovered the caption and reset the version with the text to its beginning. No more injury replay. The picture held on the green cue light.

Support stalled at 47%. Wang Hai’s fingers tapped his collar; his badge clicked. He stopped, as if afraid that sound could be cut out too.

Lin Mo didn’t hurry him. The clip played again: female face, skirt, bandaged wrist, all clear. Avoiding the injury close-up, he moved his eight-character correction reason into the center of the picture.

The music beside Xiao Lu played twice. She didn’t raise her voice over it. At her ten-second call, Lin Mo moved his fingers from the card to the padding inside his cuff.

“Hand numb?” Wang Hai asked quietly.

Lin Mo shook his head, a thin sound escaping his throat. Wang Hai loosened the cap and put the bottle to his left, without touching his hand or setting it onstage.

He drank. Onscreen he stood straight, smiling steadily. He returned the bottle, caught the detergent smell at his gray collar, and pressed his feet against the floor again.

The corrected version won with 58%.

The summary disappeared. Newcomer chooses his own scar became welcome round result, followed by the original response text. His name still read Lin Mo. People in the comments still called him she.

“Clip correction complete.”

He braced against the wall and reached for his card. His fingers went to his mouth instead.

The slot dropped lower. He was back in the fitting-room chair, blue clothes across his knees, looking up at the door. Someone told him not to scowl. He took the clothes, smiled, and said yes.

Another word was coming when Wang Hai’s shoulder blocked his face. The wall screen jolted into view. The card remained in its slot. His knees pressed the wall. He wasn’t sitting.

Wang Hai withdrew the card and offered it to his left hand.

“You didn’t agree in the fourth performance. It pulled that smile out of you.”

Lin Mo seized the hard corner. His cheeks strained; one corner of his mouth pushed upward. He held it with his left hand, fingers sliding from jaw to lip.

“Did I just smile?”

“Your mouth moved. Didn’t manage a smile.”

“What about then?”

Wang Hai pointed to the screen before answering. The smiling face remained, its right wrist scar exposed. Lin Mo lowered his sleeve, fingers still against his mouth.

“You found my cough,” Wang Hai said. “Can’t remember what you just said?”

Lin Mo tried to answer. The yes from the chair came close again. He clenched the card, loosened his teeth, and stopped before following it.

The status display flashed.

> Cognitive overwrite: 36%.
> Physical modification: 31%.

He glanced at the corrected clip without playing it.

“After this, don’t let it loop.”

Wang Hai pressed pause. Lin Mo kept the card. Only playback stopped; nothing else was confirmed for him.

Sun Ning hadn’t moved toward the exit. Beside the correction page, a new line offered source verification for clothing-collection footage.

A white shirt hung inside a green doorway. Its right sleeve was rolled up, with the handwritten character Sun below it.

“I remember collecting that,” she said.

Wang Hai looked at her injured right sleeve without opening the entry for her. She took out the blank form with its damp edge torn away. Not one number had been entered.

“This time I’m asking who wrote that name.”
