# Chapter 27: What If You're Wrong?

“What if you've got the wrong person?” Xiao Lu asked.

Lin Mo had barely settled on the stool. His knees still shook. He tucked his feet underneath it, socks scraping the floor, and felt a sudden stab in his right sole.

Wang Hai bent, holding out his right hand.

“Lift your foot.”

“Let me finish.”

“You can talk with your foot up. I'm not checking your bed number.”

Xiao Lu waited against the wall. Supporting his knee, Lin Mo rested his heel on the stool's crossbar. Wang Hai peeled a stiff paper scrap from his sock. Its sharply folded edge had pierced the outer threads. No blood underneath.

“Look at the floor before you run next time.”

“The trolley was leaving.”

“So I follow behind and watch your feet for you?”

Lin Mo looked up. Wang Hai straightened and dropped the scrap into a wastebasket. His bottle stayed in his pocket.

“I didn't ask you to.”

“No. You leave, and I guess where you're going.”

“I waited just now.”

“You did. Then I asked trolley or person, and you nearly changed direction again.”

Lin Mo opened his mouth. His eyes went to the white mark on Wang Hai's badge. He lowered his foot and looked away.

“I wanted to follow the person from the window. Then the trolley came past and I changed my mind.”

“Tell me when you change it. I can't recognize your old face, and I don't have the map inside your head.”

Xiao Lu set her script on a low cabinet beside the door, pushing back a sheet that protruded from it. When Lin Mo followed the movement, she covered the script with her palm.

“My next performance. I haven't agreed yet.”

He looked back at Wang Hai.

“I'll tell you before I go anywhere. If you don't want to come, I won't say you do.”

After a moment, Wang Hai nodded. He turned his badge outward, his right thumb covering half his name beneath the photograph.

“Ask if this is me now, I'll say yes. Ask who came with me when they took the photo, I'd have to think. If I can't remember, that doesn't mean you get to fill someone in.”

Of course caught on Lin Mo's tongue. He'd seen the badge with its name taken away. He hadn't seen the person who took the photograph.

“I won't.”

Wang Hai lifted his thumb, revealing his name before turning the badge back. He set the bottle beside Lin Mo's feet.

“Drink. You two have plenty of questions left.”

Xiao Lu waited until he'd finished, then asked again.

“What if you're wrong?”

“I keep looking.”

“And me?”

Lin Mo held the loose cap. She wasn't watching his face. She was working the brown fiber off her clip. It clung stubbornly, taking two attempts to remove.

“Do I get to leave, or do I have to keep looking with you?”

“You can leave.”

“Whenever you look at me, it has nothing to do with what I've done.”

“I saw you help that man.”

“And afterward, you still wanted to know if that voice was mine.”

He started screwing the cap on crookedly, then backed it off. Wang Hai reached, but Lin Mo kept the bottle, aligned the threads himself and set it by his feet.

“She ended up like this because of me.”

“Who?”

“My sister. She paid all her existence value for my beginner protection. In the end, her name was gone. Her original account was closed too.”

Xiao Lu paused, the broken thread between her fingers.

“Did you see her sign?”

“I saw a recording. The account matched, and I was the one being protected. Later, the payment source led to your channel.”

She closed her hand around one end of the clip and glanced at someone changing scripts by the door.

“They've shown me lots of faces. Afterward, they make me copy them. If someone cries, I have to cry too. If you want me to look at that face before I answer, I'm afraid I won't know the difference.”

“I didn't bring that recording.”

She turned back.

“Am I in it?”

“Her old face is.”

Xiao Lu dropped the thread into the wastebasket and looked up.

“So do I owe you something now, or do you owe the person she was?”

His hand slid away from the bottle. He wanted to say it wasn't a debt. What came out was, “I have to get her back.”

“Then look for her. Don't make me give you an answer she owes you.”

She'd been standing a long time. She lifted one foot, tapping her heel against the wall. Lin Mo rose to offer the stool. She waved him off, went two steps deeper into the waiting area and sat on an empty wooden crate, clip across her knees, script against her chest.

Half a step farther between them. Lin Mo turned his stool, still outside the line.

“What happens if that performance never gets settled?”

“I did it for nothing. I still have to find another I can take.”

“I could do it for—”

She looked up. He stopped.

“Do what for me? Go back and do it again? You saw who that dish trapped.”

He nodded. She smoothed the script across her lap. Its outer corner stayed curled.

A voice inside called for someone to take a performance. Xiao Lu didn't move, fingers pressed against the script's back as though it might open by itself. Watching her knuckles, Lin Mo eased back from the stool's edge.

“I'm afraid you'll never recognize me.”

Her fingers stopped.

“As it is, I don't know what you used to be like.”

“I know.”

“And you're still going to ask?”

“Yes. You don't have to answer.”

She lifted the script upright against her body.

“One question, then. Don't give me the answer.”

He thought for a long time. The hospital corridor, the name card at the foot of the bed, the cabinet door striking an eyebrow all crowded in. His lips moved. First he swallowed the number he'd already said.

“Which bed were you in at the hospital?”

“You said bed six.”

“Not what I said. Do you remember?”

“If I say six, you'll count it as remembering. If I say no, you'll ask why. You already put the answer in my ear.”

Lin Mo closed his mouth.

Wang Hai stepped aside to clear the entrance, offering no rescue. A staff member approached carrying spare scripts. Xiao Lu shifted hers behind her. The person passed without stopping.

“Try another,” she said.

Lin Mo looked at the end of her eyebrow. His mind went blank. Almost everything he thought could prove something had been filmed or asked in front of her already. He searched for an uncontaminated detail from some forgotten day. But his left shoulder flinched first, remembering her hand there while she fitted him into the blue outfit.

“You gave me—”

He stopped.

Xiao Lu looked at the blue collar beneath his gray jacket.

“Gave you what?”

“You didn't. That part's wrong.”

He raised his left hand to brush his hair behind his ear, ran out of strength halfway and lowered it.

“I remember you telling me to wear this. Smoothing it at the back. You never did.”

“How do you know?”

Wang Hai pointed at Lin Mo's sleeve.

“I was there for the jacket display. She didn't try it on him.”

Xiao Lu nodded and asked Lin Mo, “Where did the blue clothes come from?”

“The costume-change performance. I chose them.”

Wang Hai's mouth moved. When Lin Mo looked over, he swallowed what he'd been about to say and started again.

“I didn't see the choice inside. I only heard what you said afterward.”

Lin Mo took responsibility for his own answer.

“They made me choose. Every outfit meant changing my body. I picked the one I thought I could survive.”

Her eyes moved down to his long socks and back.

“When you said you chose it, I thought you liked it.”

“I used to think that when other people said it too.”

She didn't tell him how pitiful he was. That made it easier to finish.

“The bed felt even more real. I know I never wore a hospital gown. My legs still wanted to sit on the edge. Waiting for Wang Hai just now, I nearly told the person pushing the trolley to stop.”

Xiao Lu drew a small breath, her shoulder easing away from the wall.

“Sometimes I wait for the next line too. Nobody's playing it, but it's already in my mouth.”

“Which line?”

She shook her head.

He stopped. The unsaid words stayed between them. A speaker sounded onstage. She didn't turn to follow it.

After a while, Lin Mo pressed his left pocket's lining toward himself.

“There are pills inside the clothes. The show didn't give them to me. I divided them for her. She didn't finish them.”

Xiao Lu looked at the pocket without reaching.

“A whole tablet?”

“Half. Only two pockets in the blister pack. One's empty. I wrote on the back.”

“You'd already divided it when you gave it to her?”

“I put it in first, then broke—”

He stopped, his forefinger pressing absently into his palm.

She frowned. “You just said you divided it before giving it to her.”

“Yes. I broke it first. I'm thinking of bringing the whole pack back from the pharmacy.”

She looked down, scraping a nail across her thumb, and said nothing. His hand had reached the narrow opening in the lining. Seeing her stay still, he drew the pack out only far enough to show its edge, the writing turned toward himself.

“Want to see?”

“And afterward you'll ask what it says?”

He held it at the pocket's opening.

“No.”

“It might be in the footage too.”

“It is. The writing. The crossed-out line. They filmed all of it.”

He put it back without asking her to guess. Her eyes followed the disappearing silver edge. Her hand loosened beside the script. He didn't know why she'd frowned, and he didn't make that moment answer for her.

“What have you got left to ask, then?”

Lin Mo looked at the crumpled corners of her script.

“Do you still want to finish what you were saying?”

She didn't nod immediately.

“Which part?”

“Telling him not to reach in. And the things they added for you.”

The spare screen beside the door lit up. Expecting another demand for Xiao Lu to return, Lin Mo looked up and saw his own face. The shot showed him on the stool, bottle cap in hand, paired with an image of her looking up as she helped the man.

> Reunited at last. Both parties have implicitly agreed to continue.

In the recording, Xiao Lu said, “You can ask.”

But let me ask you things too was gone.

Lin Mo stood. His sole stung, but this time he didn't rush the screen. He looked at Xiao Lu. She was turning her script's pages toward the blank section, faster and faster, the corners scraping her hand.

“This isn't in mine.”

“I didn't agree either.”

She stopped turning pages and looked up.

“Then let them hear two people. Don't record you first and make me fill in the rest.”

Lin Mo pointed to two microphones put away outside.

“We ask together. Tell me if you want to stop.”

“You can refuse to answer too?”

“Yes.”

“When I ask what happens if you're wrong, you can't keep saying you'll keep looking.”

He nodded.

“Here's my answer again. If I'm wrong, I'll take it back. You don't have to become her.”

Xiao Lu closed her script, rose from the crate and approached the entrance, toes stopping inside the white line.

The host asked whether they confirmed the recommended reunion. Lin Mo shook his head. Xiao Lu said outright that they didn't need a recommendation. Their cooperation record still hung in the screen's lower corner. She pointed to it and asked the host to open a live two-person performance.

“This session continues until both parties end it,” said the female voice. “Identity proposals are open. Approved proposals will not be revoked if one party leaves.”

Lin Mo looked at Xiao Lu instead of answering for her. She stood by the line, watching the word “Reunion.” After a while, she said, “Remove the recommended answer. We'll speak for ourselves.”

Lin Mo addressed the host.

“Her last performance doesn't count as agreeing to this one.”

The voice told them to confirm separately in the new performance. Xiao Lu listened, then looked ahead. Neither microphone was lit yet.

“Who asks first?”

“You.”

“I might ask something you don't want to talk about.”

His fingers curled for the chair leg. It was still against the wall. All he caught was his pocket's edge. He released it.

“Then I'll say I don't want to. Same for you.”

She fastened her clip to the script's edge. Her sliver stayed inside the book.

He nodded. The old cooperation page remained in his pocket; he didn't produce another handful of papers. The person by the door stepped behind the equipment. Both microphones began moving toward the stage entrance.

Wang Hai picked up the bottle, then pointed right-handed toward the wall.

“The wood.”

Lin Mo retrieved the chair leg and looked at him.

“I'm going to the new performance with her. Will you come?”

Wang Hai put the bottle in his right pocket and patted the broken casing in the other.

“Yes. I'll watch the lights and make sure you're steady. Don't ask me to recognize your past for you.”

“All right.”

Xiao Lu had taken two steps toward the stage. Hearing them, she turned and waited. Lin Mo moved the chair leg into his left hand and followed outside the white line, without going ahead of her.
