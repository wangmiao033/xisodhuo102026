# Chapter 25: The Voice They Sent Away

Before the door shut, Lin Mo wedged the chair leg into the gap.

Through the gap, he saw the trolley's undercarriage turn a corner. A paper scrap dragged beneath it, then vanished too.

His sock touched the threshold. He drew back.

Wang Hai wasn't here yet.

The door spring pressed against the wood. Its four fresh gouges faced him. He changed his grip to keep the screw in the broken end away from his wrist. Something metal fell inside. He looked up, almost calling out for them to stop moving the bed.

The words caught in his throat. He looked down. No bed. Only the gray soles of his socks and cold air slipping through the door.

Someone spoke behind him.

“Who did you promise to wait for?”

Wang Hai approached, bottle tucked fully into his right pocket, factory badge against his chest. He inspected the wedged wood.

“I am waiting,” Lin Mo said.

“Is the door doing it for you?”

Lin Mo let go with his left hand and moved back from the gap.

“Sun Ning?”

“Door's shut. Says her arm's finally getting a rest, and she's not coming to find out which of us runs slowest. Say our names when we get back. Don't just knock.”

“You told her which way?”

“Yes. She wants to know what you plan to do when you catch him.”

Lin Mo pushed inward and freed the chair leg.

“Find the box. The rest of that recording's still in it.”

“How do you know?”

“I don't. We have to catch up and see.”

Wang Hai stepped through sideways, staying beside him. Lin Mo held the door, then they followed the wall. Two black tracks marked the floor; one broke at intervals where a wheel scraped with every half turn.

Another clatter ahead. Lin Mo quickened his steps.

“Tell me what you see first,” Wang Hai called.

“Trolley on the left. It hasn't gone out yet.”

“Then say trolley. Don't just disappear.”

Dismantled scenery filled the corner, paper snagged on its lowest iron brace. Lin Mo peered through. The trolley stood before a rolling shutter. The person from the window had their back to them, mask under their chin, and was tugging the black box free of its tangled wire.

Lin Mo started over the brace. Wang Hai put his right foot on its raised end.

“Here.”

Thinking he meant hurry, Lin Mo stepped across. A protruding nut caught his skirt, pulling it tight across his knee. He stopped before it tore and turned to free it, but his left shoulder wouldn't take the strain. Wang Hai couldn't get around him without taking his foot off the brace.

“Hold it down. I'll unhook it.”

“Finally worked out we can do different jobs.”

Lin Mo lifted the fabric and backed it off the corner of the nut. It came free, untorn, streaked gray beside his knee. Beyond the panels, the person raised the box. Lin Mo could see the broken lever. The same one.

He held the chair leg crosswise and signaled Wang Hai to retreat first. Wang Hai lifted his foot. The brace sprang up and struck the scenery. Both stayed still until the person turned away again, then stepped out.

The shutter was open to waist height. From the other side, a long hook reached out and hauled a paper sack off the trolley. Its bottom split, spilling thin strips across the floor. Lin Mo crouched. His knees dipped inward; he steadied himself against a panel with his left hand.

Wang Hai touched his elbow, then withdrew.

“He's still there. Don't try walking crouched down.”

Lin Mo came around the side. Hearing his socks scrape, the person turned, saw the chair leg and shoved the box under the shutter.

“Leave that recording,” Lin Mo said.

“Go listen onstage.”

“Which recording's onstage?”

The mask went back up. The person kicked at a wheel to turn the trolley. Lin Mo hurried forward, slipped on the paper strips and struck the rim with his right hand. His white scar felt as though it had cinched tight. He couldn't hold on. The chair leg fell between their feet.

Wang Hai didn't stoop for it. He caught the trolley's corner right-handed and braced his shoulder against the turning frame.

“The wood. On the floor.”

Lin Mo nudged it toward himself with his foot and crouched to retrieve it. Wang Hai drew a sharp breath. Only then did Lin Mo notice the rim pressing his factory badge into his chest.

“Move. Let me.”

“Get the thing. We're not changing shifts.”

Under the shutter, the box slid onto a sloping iron plate. Open casings littered it. Their thin slivers were clamped together and fed between two rotating rubber rollers. The box with the broken lever still trailed its wire at the outer edge.

Lin Mo slid the chair leg beneath the trolley and braced it against a crossbar. Wang Hai released his right hand. The frame settled onto the wood and stopped turning into him.

“Keep it out of the wheels,” Wang Hai said.

Lin Mo nodded. The person tried to kick the wood away. Wang Hai blocked it with the side of his shoe, leaving their leg alone.

“Keep pushing if you want your badge crushed.”

The person let go. A thin badge on their own chest was caught between the trolley's rim and the iron plate. Two tugs failed to free it. They untied the cord and squeezed around the far side.

Lin Mo couldn't follow. The box's wire pulled taut. The casing split open, exposing the empty middle slot. Beside it, another sliver was sliding toward the rollers.

A red handle stood alongside the plate. He reached for it.

“That lifts the plate!” Wang Hai shouted from behind the trolley.

Lin Mo's fingers were already on it. Looking sideways, he saw a thick cord running from the handle to the plate's outer edge. Pulling down would tip the whole thing upright and send the box into the rollers.

He released it.

“Stop!” he called through the shutter.

The person inside kept working, lifting the wire off the hook. The sliver slackened half an inch. Lin Mo reached with the chair leg, but the trolley tilted toward Wang Hai, who caught its corner again.

“That has to stay.”

Lin Mo replaced the wood. He pulled his right arm out of his gray jacket, wrapped the empty sleeve around the broken wire's outer end and dragged. He couldn't put pressure on his right wrist. Even through several layers of cloth, the wire bit into his left knuckles.

The box struck the plate's edge. Its casing cracked, dropping half a piece of black material.

The rollers squealed. The rest was already inside.

He didn't reach farther. He pulled the sleeve back with the broken wire, shattered casing and a sliver whose edge had turned white. It fell to the floor. He crouched, covered it with his sleeve and rested his palm on the cloth, waiting for the urge to thrust his whole arm inside to pass.

Wang Hai had to call twice before he looked up.

“Got it?”

“It's broken.”

“Come out first.”

The long hook swept outward, pushing loose paper onto the plate. Lin Mo backed away. Wang Hai lifted the trolley's corner enough for him to withdraw the wood. Released, the whole frame slid inside. They moved against the wall as the wheels crossed the spot where Wang Hai had stood.

The shutter lowered to a bright slit.

Lin Mo's right sleeve trailed on the floor. He retrieved the sliver and put his arm through. His hair caught in the collar, taking two tugs to free. Sweat covered his temples.

“Tell me before you wrap your sleeve around a wire,” Wang Hai said.

“I thought we'd run out of time.”

“I thought you'd go in with it. We're both scared. Can one of us say something first?”

Lin Mo lowered his head. A black smear ran down the sleeve's inner side. The patch hadn't touched the rollers. His left pocket's lining still lay against his waist. He spread the little fragment across his cuff.

“I will next time. Did that hurt your chest?”

Wang Hai freed his badge from beneath a button. A white mark crossed the plastic edge. He couldn't rub it off.

“Not as tough as your wood.”

He picked up the broken casing and glanced at Lin Mo, who was still breathing hard.

“Will it play?”

Lin Mo didn't take it yet. His left hand shook too much; the sliver kept tapping his cuff button. Wang Hai took out the bottle, tucked it beneath his arm and unscrewed the cap right-handed before holding it out.

“Take it yourself. I'm not feeding you.”

Lin Mo laid the sliver on his turned-back cuff and took the bottle in his left hand. After one mouthful, he tried to return it before swallowing. Wang Hai wouldn't take it.

“That was you shouting stop? You sounded like you had no breath left.”

The second swallow brought pain to Lin Mo's throat. Knocking came from beyond the shutter. He turned toward it without moving the bottle, spilling a few drops into his collar. Wang Hai caught it and replaced the cap.

“This time, let me finish.” He put the bottle away. “If I'm letting go of the trolley, I'll tell you. If you're reaching inside, I need to know. I've only got one hand to pull you back. One wrong guess could be all it takes.”

Lin Mo watched the white mark on the badge. It still turned over. The name was still there. But the plastic wouldn't be the same again.

“I wasn't going to reach in. I wanted to pull the wire out.”

“Say that, and I'll know.”

“All right.”

He flattened the sliver on his sleeve and brought it toward the broken box.

Only one end of the slot still had its tiny copper-colored contacts. Remembering Xiao Lu pressing the broken lever with her clip, he set the intact edge against them while Wang Hai held the casing steady.

They waited. Nothing.

Lin Mo moved his fingers. The woman's voice burst out.

“…give him the clothes…”

The words stretched, their ending dissolving into static. He released it and tried again. This time there was only a shrill “him.”

“Stop wearing it down,” Wang Hai said.

Lin Mo withdrew the fragment. Tiny splits covered the edge the rollers had swallowed. No adjustment would join it back together.

Wang Hai turned the casing over. A narrow yellow label, peeling at one corner, bore Xiao Lu's channel number.

Lin Mo recognized the digits. He'd checked them one by one when he first got the index. He kept the last half sentence behind his teeth and pointed at the number.

“Her next performance.”

“So that was her speaking?”

“I don't know. The trolley was taking it to her.”

Voices beyond the shutter silenced them both.

“Take the yellow connector. They've started onstage.”

“This one's broken.”

“Don't wait for it. There's another.”

Lin Mo bent down. Through the slit, he saw an intact black box placed on a narrow trolley, yellow tape wound around its plug. A little white box glowed beside it. A thin wire ran along the wall. Its light flashed twice as the person inside spoke.

Watching the white light, Lin Mo coughed. No response. The person inside cursed the trolley for blocking the way, and the light jumped at once. At the yellow connector, the glow stayed constant through the pauses in speech.

He touched Wang Hai's cuff and pointed. Wang Hai didn't reach for the wire. Instead, he moved Lin Mo a step closer to the wall, clearing the trolley's path.

“Just remember where it plugs in.”

A small door beside the shutter opened. The trolley emerged, its yellow wire gathered into a coil. Lin Mo stayed against the wall and let it pass.

“Don't want it now?” Wang Hai murmured.

“Let's see where it goes. Taking it won't tell us what's on it.”

The person from the window followed, no longer carrying the photograph. They hesitated at the sight of them, then hurried through another door. Lin Mo looked that way. Wang Hai raised his right hand before him.

“Which way?”

The wheels kept moving.

“The trolley,” Lin Mo said.

They followed it to the passage's end, where warm light shone beneath a thick curtain. The person pushing stopped and passed the yellow-taped plug inside. Lin Mo heard Xiao Lu telling someone outside to put their money away.

He knew the line. She had tested half of it after retrieving her sliver.

Wang Hai put the broken casing in his empty pocket, separate from the bottle. Lin Mo took out the booklet, slipped the fragment between blank pages and put it away again, leaving the original receipt beside it untouched. Outside the curtain, they watched the yellow wire pulled through and plugged in beside the doorway.

Lin Mo held out an empty hand to Wang Hai. Wait.

A ticket clip struck a table inside, loudly.

“Wait for them to let go inside. Don't grab,” Xiao Lu said.

The woman's voice continued after hers, coming from a little speaker by the door.

“Don't take it out of the jacket. Just give him the clothes.”

Lin Mo pressed his left pocket, his fingertips cold. The crowd laughed. Someone shouted for the person asking questions to take off their jacket so everyone could see.

Xiao Lu's voice came from closer than the laughter.

“I never said that.”
