# Chapter 175: The Person Behind the Name

Someone asked whether Lin Mo was getting sleepy.

He raised his head and saw Yu Mei. He could connect that name with where she sat. He remembered how her sleeve had torn and how she had let him choose whether to touch her hand. Why she was here in the first place was something he would have to ask.

“I wasn't asleep.”

She watched him. Something was missing from his answer; he didn't know what to add. Looking for his recent self in another person's face was harder than looking at paper.

“My name is Lin Mo.”

“I know.”

“Twenty-five. Special Forces.”

“You don't have to keep telling me.”

He stopped. The three facts were firm, available whenever he wanted them. The training ground was gone, along with the hand that had rested on his shoulder and whoever used to laugh first when he made a mistake.

Special Forces remained an identity he understood. It couldn't establish that he was capable of standing from this wheelchair.

His wrist hurt, with embarrassing promptness.

He left it in the splint instead of demonstrating movement. He knew the small front wheel mustn't touch the raised metal. Yu Mei couldn't haul him from behind with her injured shoulder. He mustn't reach into the gap after the bottle.

Each fact had a reason. Some reasons belonged to the last few minutes; others were tools already in his hand, with no memory of who had given them to him.

Two lines appeared at the screen's lower edge.

External Receiving Structure Open.

Source Not Released.

“When you lift your head, I can see a little,” Xiao Lu said from beside the door.

He looked toward the port. A faint outline occupied part of the narrow screen, where words had been. It wasn't standing straight or holding an umbrella.

Half a face seemed pressed behind dusty glass. Lines intermittently cut its edges, but it remained connected to the depth behind it. No piece had come away separately.

The old conversation-camera marker still read Off. The outline lay between the transfer's continuity lines. He couldn't tell how far this new sensory connection reached; this interface showed no reopened camera or new recommendation entry.

He heard an intake of breath. The outline moved with it, lips caught in an awkward shape.

“Don't lean closer just to look at me.”

He had begun to lean. He stopped. Yu Mei checked his feet without pushing him nearer.

The gray door remained a door. No new weight rested on the concrete outside it.

“What can you see?”

“Your hand. On your leg.”

He moved his left hand. She didn't respond at once. When the outline cleared, she asked if his finger hurt. He said it did, then asked whether she needed them to slow the conversation.

“Not slow down. Let me look.”

He fell quiet. The face was unfamiliar.

The discovery didn't crush him as he might have expected. He moved from eyebrow to eye to mouth, recognizing human features without finding an afternoon behind any of them.

He knew she was his sister. That fact remained, and she was speaking through the same unbroken continuity. Knowledge didn't bring familiarity with it.

“What are you looking for?”

He wanted to say nothing. She sounded exhausted. Perhaps if he managed a little joy, it would spare her some pain.

“The feeling of recognizing you from before.”

The outline stopped moving.

“Did you find it?”

“No.”

The bottle rocked below the door. She turned away, or the image lost another section; he couldn't tell. He didn't ask her to smile again, or repeat brother until he responded properly.

“Look at this one for now, then,” she said at last.

“All right.”

This time he asked for water himself.

He Cen passed the fifth bottle over. Lin Mo watched the remaining water shift along its bottom. His left fingers hurt when he gripped; his right wrist was splinted. He couldn't hold the bottle at his mouth.

Yu Mei asked whether she should help. Yes, he said. He would tell her when to stop.

When the rim touched his lip, he didn't immediately drink.

The cool plastic reminded him of refusing earlier. The reason remained: he had feared someone taking over his choices. Nobody took the bottle from him now, and nobody looked vindicated because he had finally agreed.

He swallowed a little and winced. Yu Mei immediately drew the opening away rather than letting water enter his still-open mouth. Once he could breathe, he shook his head.

Enough. He was still thirsty.

“There's a little more.”

“Keep it.”

She didn't argue about who needed it most. She replaced the cap and returned it within He Cen's reach. Lin Mo followed its passage, remembering each movement.

No childhood came back with the water. His body had swallowed, though, and his mouth was less dry.

“Was that drinking?” Xiao Lu asked.

“Yes.”

“I can't see all of it.”

He opened his left hand so she could see it held nothing. Her limited view reached that far. He nearly promised her a bottle later, then stopped.

The fifth wasn't empty, but it was close. He couldn't celebrate her arrival with water that didn't exist.

“When you can touch things, you can decide whether you want them.”

She didn't ask him to explain which things. The half-visible face edged closer, its gaze still on his hand.

The seatkeeper shifted against the wall and grunted. His injured leg hadn't moved; the stretch supporting his back was uneven. Lin Mo knew where the man was sitting. Even the odd title now required him to search what others had recently said.

Seeing him look, the man said he didn't need help.

Lin Mo nodded without deciding that he never needed any. Fang Ji sat at the corridor's edge. The light bag beside his feet sagged where a large empty bottle was missing. The notebook was back in the room; the retrieval indexes remained on paper, not suddenly supplied to Lin Mo's mind by the clearing.

“Where is it worst?” Yu Mei asked.

“Behind me.”

She glanced at the backrest.

“Not where you can touch.”

He tried several descriptions. It was like leaving a door at his back with something leaning against the other side. He couldn't turn to look or move away.

He didn't know how much of that comparison was true. Ordinary shoulder pain could be pointed out to her. This couldn't.

She didn't search for the nonexistent door.

“Do you want further clearing paused?”

He considered before answering. An immediate refusal merely to prove courage would be useless. He understood that pausing wouldn't return what was gone.

Continuing would cut more of the remaining associations. The pressure promised him no relief and couldn't speak for whatever might remain inside.

“The original range for now. If I can't make myself clear, wait. Don't supply agreement for me.”

“I'll wait until you can say it clearly.”

She didn't promise that moment would necessarily come. He shut his eyes briefly. When he opened them, the outline was still there. Xiao Lu seemed to be looking down, trying to identify something that had never properly supported her.

“Still nothing under your feet?”

“Not nothing. There's something. I can't step across it. It's still attached to me.”

Source Not Released remained lit. The bright lines spread outward and stopped near the original arm. Nobody walked through the door. The gray outline approached, still tethered, as though a whole person were drawing nearer while something behind her couldn't let go.

At the same time, a broad expanse of places Lin Mo had been able to return to went dark.

He thought of the notebook containing his name but couldn't remember the first urgency to write. He knew words were hidden in residual circuits and that their indexes were on paper. The three stories had shrunk to subjects: umbrella, boot, lamp.

He could work out how to remove a boot without tearing injured skin. The self who had crouched there, listening to someone hurry him and choosing to wait, was absent from the action.

“The page about the boot,” he said.

Fang Ji looked up.

“The person in it. Now I…”

“You don't have to report every missing piece.”

His throat closed. He had meant to describe each loss, as though someone recording the severed connections might prevent their passing in silence. But Fang Ji was listening to a man in front of him cease to know him. He wasn't obliged to carry an expanding inventory.

“I'm not telling you not to talk,” Fang Ji added. “Say it if you want. You don't have to finish explaining before you're allowed to rest.”

Lin Mo lowered his head. His left hand opened and closed. He could perform the movement; the hand was his. The sense of everything it had once grasped was unimaginably distant.

He tried to recall his former face.

The figure that had reached from the door appeared instead. That wouldn't do. He tried to remember the height of a mirror before the forced changes. He knew it had been low, but not where he had looked from when he bent.

His present weight remained in the chair. Resistance and shouting had become things he understood had happened, not somewhere he could return on a breath.

Something small persisted.

No face, no complete sentence. A smaller hand held two of his fingers, reluctant to step down. He remembered being taller, wanting to hurry the person, then not doing it.

There seemed to be an edge ahead. He couldn't see its height or place it in a day.

Only warmth remained in his palm.

He didn't dare name the hand. He couldn't even establish whether this was one genuine memory's last trace or several similar gestures overlapping in the clearing. The warmth brought a faint sound from his throat.

“What remains?” Yu Mei asked.

“A hand.”

She looked at his left hand, stopped, and raised her eyes to his face without touching him.

A low vibration came from the door. Xiao Lu's outline advanced and stopped. The remembered warmth became heavy, as if the smaller hand were no longer borrowing his balance but falling behind him.

His real fingers curled. His shoulder tightened.

Fang Ji offered support.

“Don't pull my hand. Keep the chair still.”

Yu Mei watched the brake lever without moving him closer. Lin Mo found the original continuity lines. A dark stretch remained between them. It offered no easy release and no way to preserve one final story without cost.

He couldn't hide that warmth behind a page and pretend it was outside the clearing range.

A muffled impact came from Xiao Lu's direction. She didn't cry out, but the outline dipped sharply. He nearly said don't be afraid, then realized he didn't know whether she was afraid or what the words could shield her from.

“I'm here.”

“I can see.”

Her voice came from the door's edge, almost as clearly as those outside. Yet Source Not Released remained. The bottle still marked the gap where they mustn't reach. Nearness hadn't turned either fact into completion.

“Keep your head up,” she said.

He realized it had been sinking. Exhaustion felt unthreatening; it only wanted him to close his eyes. Yu Mei called his name without slapping his face. He lifted his head and struggled to focus on the gray outline.

“Here,” Xiao Lu said.

Her face didn't give him his past. Her name brought no lost days home. The last remembered warmth held tight, the structure behind the door continued bearing its load, and Xiao Lu had not reached their side.

Lin Mo opened his real left hand and waited for the next surge to pass.
