# Chapter 173: Don't Touch Me Yet

When Fang Ji reached out, Lin Mo recoiled.

He could see that the hand had only been going to steady the wheelchair's frame. He remembered Fang Ji freeing Yu Mei's cuff, too. But he couldn't find the reason he'd let this person stand so close. The other hand hung bandaged at Fang Ji's side; its exposed knuckles suggested someone he ought to guard against.

Against what, he couldn't say.

“Don't touch me yet.”

Fang Ji withdrew immediately. Yu Mei turned, and Lin Mo feared whatever she might do next. They seemed to have crowded him into a narrow enclosure, gray door in front, chair back behind. His legs, unable to bear proper weight, felt as though they'd been placed on the wrong body.

“Stop.”

Yu Mei's hand never reached his shoulder. She pressed the agreed control at the outer edge instead. The narrow screen lit.

Further Clearing Paused.

Lin Mo still shook. The bottle kept scraping beneath the door, and the low sound inside the arm continued.

“I said stop!”

“The clearing after this point has stopped.” She stepped back so he could see the words. “The connection already left in place is still running; unplugging it won't make this never have happened. I haven't pressed anything else.”

He watched her hand. Dust lodged beneath her nails; a corner of her cuff was newly torn. That same hand had rested beside his earlier, letting him choose to touch it. Now it made no hurried attempt to prove itself trustworthy.

He slowly settled against the chair's back.

Fang Ji cleared the space beside him and stood where Lin Mo could see his whole body. “I'm here. Tell me if you want me to leave.”

Lin Mo wanted him gone. Then he feared the chair might slide toward the gap if nobody held it. He could see the brake lever and understood how the mechanism worked. His fear refused to listen to that knowledge.

“Don't go far yet.”

“All right.”

That was all that remained between them. He remembered Fang Ji sometimes speaking harshly, remembered calling him by name earlier. Looking farther back, he found no reliable map behind that name. He couldn't say where Fang Ji had helped him, much less what had preceded the help.

Yu Mei asked whether he wanted water.

“No…”

Too fast. For an instant he'd been afraid that opening his mouth would let someone pour something into it. Yu Mei let the question go without pointing to his cracked lips as an argument. She sat against the wall, slightly turned, within reach of the control but clear of his view.

Heat rose in his face. “I didn't mean you were…”

“You don't have to make it sound better for us right now. You were frightened. You made that clear.”

He thought of the notebook. That thought survived, like something just lost whose last location he could still remember. Fang Ji asked whether he wanted it brought over. Yes, he said, but leave it where he could see it, without pushing it straight into his hands.

Fang Ji retrieved the original from the little room. Old Chen's pages and the photograph remained tucked inside. Before setting it on Lin Mo's lap, he waited for a nod. Its spine settled against the cloth without shifting his right knee.

Lin Mo asked him to turn to the page where he'd written his name.

Lin Mo appeared twice, one line crooked, the other straight. He could identify the badly formed characters, but their ugliness didn't restore the effort his fingers had made. Beside them was a note identifying who'd dictated and who'd copied. The writing offered an explanation, but it brought nobody back.

Fang Ji didn't point to it and call it proof.

Lin Mo read farther: twenty-five, Special Forces. He knew those things as he knew his name. Below them lay the sentence saying a future reader needn't believe immediately. He read it three times before realizing the man holding the paper wasn't requiring him to use that sentence as a reason to trust.

“I told you to write this?”

“I heard you say it. I didn't witness those old events. I can't tell you whether they're true.”

Lin Mo looked at the injured hand. “Were you trying to pull her just now?”

The air went still. Fang Ji didn't answer immediately. His jaw tightened, but his feet stayed where they were. The pause frightened Lin Mo more than an easy assurance would have.

“Yes. When her cuff caught, I reached for her before I'd heard properly.”

“Then you stopped?”

“You hit the armrest and called again. I took my hand away.”

Lin Mo remembered those two strikes. He could connect them to the hand before him. He'd been pushing his fear into some older darkness, as though someone bad enough to explain all his unease must be hidden there. What he could actually verify was a reckless reach followed by a genuine stop.

“You can keep telling me not to touch you,” Fang Ji said.

Lin Mo wasn't wholly reassured. At least Fang Ji wasn't using one instance of stopping to demand access to his body. He left the notebook on his knees and asked him to stay where he was.

Someone called through the voice port.

“Xiao Lu,” Lin Mo said first.

“Yes.”

“You're my sister.”

She didn't answer at once. The fact was perfectly clear in his mouth, like a signpost he couldn't misread. What lay beyond it no longer gave him enough to find his own way there.

“Can you recognize my voice now?” she asked.

“I recognize it as the voice that was speaking to me earlier.”

He waited, unable to add anything. Small knocks continued inside the door, and the silence on the other end of the port remained. He couldn't see how she received his answer. He could only stay through the time neither of them filled with reassurance.

“Let's leave it there for now,” she finally said.

He Cen suddenly slipped forward. A corner of gray clothing fell from behind his shoulder, and his head struck the wall. The impact wasn't loud, but Lin Mo almost pushed himself upright. His right wrist had barely approached the armrest before pain made him withdraw.

Fang Ji moved too, then looked at him, apparently unsure whether don't touch had included everyone nearby.

“Help him!”

Fang Ji crouched beside He Cen and called his name without trying to haul him up by the arms. He Cen opened his eyes, swore, and mumbled that he could hear. Yu Mei moved farther from the control. Unable to kneel, she sat low against the wall and gathered the slipping gray cloth.

The seatkeeper made room against the wall to support He Cen's upper body. All three moved slowly; nobody could simply lift the feverish man whole. Lin Mo noticed his fingers still reaching after the cloth and told Fang Ji not to push it beneath his neck yet. He was trying to find the collar.

At last He Cen pulled the neckline open a little.

“Hot.”

Yu Mei loosened her hold instead of wrapping him tightly again. The fifth bottle came from beside the wall into his sight, touching his mouth only after he'd been asked. He drank very little, swallowed, and turned away. Nobody required another sip to reassure them.

Watching his flushed face, Lin Mo remembered a movement without an image: the damp back of a hand touching a forehead and immediately withdrawing. Who'd checked whose temperature, he no longer knew.

His left hand lifted slightly. From this distance, it could reach nothing.

He Cen saw. “Don't get out of that chair.”

“I wasn't going to…”

“Then don't start.”

Lin Mo leaned back. He didn't know whether he'd liked this person before. He still didn't want to see him fall away from the wall. The small present concern couldn't fill an entire stretch of missing past, but its inability to do so didn't make it useless.

The screen dimmed a shade, reflecting half his face.

He saw reddened eyes and a mouth held tight against pain. His face now. He needed nobody to identify it: the pull beside his neck when he breathed, the sore inside his cheek, already joined that reflection to the body in the chair.

Suddenly he couldn't locate what had once seemed unfamiliar about it.

The shock of looking into a mirror after the forced changes had already been damaged before the pause. Only seeing his face now revealed that he remembered the shock occurring without recalling precisely which contour he'd wanted to escape. Trying to picture his former jaw, he first pictured the man who'd just offered a hand from the door.

“That image…”

Yu Mei looked over.

“What I can bring to mind seems to be all the things it just showed me.”

She was quiet, neither confirming that the image was him nor declaring it unlike him. Lin Mo slowly looked away. He didn't want an inducement occupying his lost past, its borrowed picture directing how he should hate or accept his body now.

“You don't have to decide you like this face today,” Yu Mei said.

“Or hate it.”

“Right.”

That simple answer tightened his throat. He could still say how he'd understood himself before, but a sheet of paper couldn't order his body to produce exactly the same feelings immediately. Nobody used that uncertainty to rename him. Xiao Lu, too, waited for him to finish.

He lifted his left hand and stopped near his face. His index finger was hurt; there was no need to touch the sore inside his cheek again. He lowered it. The unfinished gesture suddenly mattered: stopping now didn't require knowing everything he'd ever been.

Fang Ji held the open page's corner, keeping the notebook from sliding onto a more painful part of his leg. Lin Mo watched the hand without quite trusting it. This time he'd seen its entire approach and withdrawal. No paper was missing, and nobody had slipped out the photograph while he studied his face.

The bottle in the gap stopped scraping.

Silence lasted two breaths. On the third came a low friction against the door's back, like something slowly seeking an edge to hold. The pressure behind Lin Mo, nowhere on the chair itself, deepened.

He wanted to call stop. His tongue touched his teeth before he remembered they'd already stopped.

“I'm still inside the same line,” Xiao Lu said. “The place that came closer hasn't moved back.”

He watched the word Paused. It hadn't lied; it hadn't ended everything either. What he'd already given still bore its weight. Outside, he sat reading paper and waiting for terror to pass, as though making no more decisions could hold everything somewhere between better and worse.

“Can that part feel pain?”

Nobody could answer. He heard Xiao Lu breathe out slowly before saying she didn't know; Yu Mei shook her head. He wanted a gentler answer, at least an assurance that the feeling behind his back came only from him. He didn't ask them for one.

Two knocks sounded against the door, and Fang Ji looked without approaching. Lin Mo asked Xiao Lu whether she'd made them; she hadn't. The sound didn't recur. Nobody outside could turn those knocks into consent or refusal on behalf of something they couldn't see.

Lin Mo closed the notebook. “Don't use my earlier yes to hurry me.”

“Nobody's hurrying you,” Yu Mei said.

“I know. I'm afraid I'll use it to hurry myself.”

Saying it brought relief, and the relief made him sad. He still had a present in which he could ask other people's hands to withdraw. The parts he'd given and could no longer call back paid no less because he'd understood something a little more clearly.

Xiao Lu asked what he wanted to know.

He looked at the arm. He didn't ask her to prove again that they were siblings, or demand a secret only they shared. He'd seen the Theater use lost outlines to tempt him. A correct answer about the past might tell him little more about the present.

“Why do you want to come out?”

A brief sound came through the port, almost a laugh that hadn't quite happened.

“Because I want to know what people's faces look like when you're talking.”

He looked toward the wall. Yu Mei frowned. He Cen's eyes were closed, Fang Ji's lips pressed tight, the seatkeeper's gaze averted. No face belonged in a lovely reunion.

“They don't look very good right now.”

“I want to see for myself.”

Lin Mo returned the notebook to Fang Ji, asking only that he put it away. Passing it over offered no invitation to touch his arm. He turned to Yu Mei and waited for her shoulder to ease.

“I want to continue within the scope we agreed. Don't press it yet. I want to hear myself say that.”

Her hands remained on her knees. Lin Mo said it again, this time without looking at the paper.
