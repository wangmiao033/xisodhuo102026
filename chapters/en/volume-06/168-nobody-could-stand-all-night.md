# Chapter 168: Nobody Could Stand All Night

Yu Mei withdrew the hand she used at the panel.

Standing too long made her shoulder creep upward, as though it could still hold something. She said her fingers were numb. When Lin Mo asked her to sit, she didn't argue. Lowering herself against the wall, she turned away. He heard a brief, suppressed breath and didn't ask whether it hurt.

That was a shoulder they would need.

He asked Xiao Lu to end the conversation for now. Would he return? Yes, he said. First they needed to arrange care for his body. He didn't say soon. She didn't question its absence.

With the channel closed, the room's other noises sounded farther away. The seatkeeper offered to brace behind the wheelchair and catch Lin Mo if he slumped.

“For how long?” Fang Ji asked.

“Until it's over.”

Fang Ji raised his left hand from his knee and couldn't keep it there. He needed no weight to demonstrate what the injured shoulder could manage.

“Which part being over?”

The seatkeeper looked toward Lin Mo.

Clearing would not mean transfer was complete. Completed transfer wouldn't release the retained structure. Nothing stated when the person in this chair would be able to sit steadily again.

“We can arrange the first part now,” Lin Mo said.

“And afterward?”

“Take turns. Move back where people can rest. If we can't, stop again.”

“Don't say take turns yet,” Fang Ji said. “With whom?”

He Cen opened his eyes against the wall. Nobody needed to name him. Lin Mo couldn't pretend a healthy person remained available to rise at any moment. Yu Mei looked from the seatkeeper's leg to Fang Ji's right palm, then spread her own hands on her thighs.

They had already used everyone.

Forget planning a whole night, Lin Mo said. Start with one person operating the panel while the others dealt with the body. If anyone couldn't continue, they needed a way not to hand that body into empty space. Fang Ji should look at where the braked chair actually stood, not draw a plan wider than the corridor.

For the first attempt, Lin Mo only closed his eyes. He didn't pretend to be unconscious.

Yu Mei stood diagonally beside the panel. Fang Ji was at the chair's side. The seatkeeper waited near the rear wheel without blocking the brake. Lin Mo relaxed slightly. His head tipped left; his shoulder hurt at once.

He opened his eyes. “No.”

Fang Ji's hand stopped halfway up.

“Did I catch you?”

“You didn't touch me. I pressed on it myself.”

He settled his head against the backrest again. Even testing a position while awake hadn't gone as expected. That couldn't establish what would happen if he truly lost consciousness. Yu Mei returned to his side and asked whether resting more fully against the back might help.

He wouldn't promise it. They waited for his right leg to relax. Yu Mei released the brake, eased the chair back a little, and turned it through a shallow angle, away from the doorframe. The floor remained level. No wheel crossed the threshold or entered the gray door.

She braked again. Reading the screen now required a slight turn of his head instead of leaning forward. Fang Ji had room to reach low toward him without lifting him by an injured wrist.

The seatkeeper sat back against the wall, feet clear of the wheel rim. He could block a slump toward him. He couldn't lift Lin Mo's whole weight. This time he stated the limit himself instead of repairing it with a promise to try.

“I'll call someone first.”

“Don't grab my wrist.”

“I remember.”

Fang Ji indicated where his uninjured hand could reach without pressing Lin Mo into position. His left shoulder was hurt too. He could offer brief support, then would need to settle the weight against the chair and wall. His body couldn't replace everything the wheelchair lacked.

Lin Mo noticed his palm trembling.

“Don't hold me yet.”

“I'm not.”

“I mean don't start getting ready to hold me indefinitely.”

Fang Ji hesitated, then returned his hand to his knee. Usually, when somebody stopped, Lin Mo felt obliged to find the next thing to get them moving. Now he needed to retrieve effort before they spent it. Otherwise they would use themselves up preparing to begin.

He Cen's head tilted. The seatkeeper called to him. He opened his eyes and explained that he only wanted something behind his neck. Could they move the bag? It blocked his leg.

Empty bottles made the bag light, but their necks would jab anyone leaning on it. Fang Ji pushed it a little with his shoe. The seatkeeper gathered its mouth. Lin Mo had once thought preserving their possessions meant they were prepared. Now even a badly placed empty bottle could cost an injured person another movement.

Some water remained in the fifth bottle. He Cen asked whether they should save it for Lin Mo, who might not wake.

“Don't ration it that way,” Lin Mo said. “Say if you need it now.”

He Cen watched him, then took a small drink. He hadn't stolen it from someone's future. He offered no apology for remaining ill.

Light spread across the wall.

A bed appeared, too clean to belong here. Lin Mo saw his name beside a pillow. Care Ready was written along its edge. Then the person in the bed sat up with a sound shoulder and a knee that bent easily.

Lin Mo's foot moved first.

A small response: seeing someone rise, his body began to find the balance for the next action. His right knee tightened. He stopped. Fang Ji didn't seize him. He said his name.

Lin Mo looked down. His legs remained on the footrest; the brake hadn't moved.

“I wasn't trying to stand.”

“I saw. Your foot moved.”

He couldn't deny that. Retained motor programs might do the same, beginning half a movement before present judgment caught up. The words Special Forces wouldn't make an injured knee weight-bearing. Familiarity might make the dangerous impulse faster.

Yu Mei didn't try to cover the wall. She stepped into his view and identified the real chair, herself on his left, the right knee that mustn't follow an image. He waited for the urge to rise to subside. On the wall, the person lay back as though obedience guaranteed care.

“We don't have that bed,” he said.

“No. We have this. A chair isn't a bed.”

Her plainness loosened something in his chest. She wasn't pretending their small space could accommodate every outcome. She wasn't asking him to be satisfied with its poverty. He could choose to continue while knowing preparation didn't mean possessing everything they needed.

They placed the original notebook on his lap. The photograph and Old Chen's folded pages stayed untouched. Fang Ji found a blank space for a present-body note. Not a list of people his future self must obey. It said his wrist, shoulder, and right knee were injured; he currently used a wheelchair; he should ask those present before standing.

Lin Mo changed the last instruction.

“Asking doesn't mean I can do it. Say a label doesn't establish that I can stand.”

Fang Ji amended it and read the sentence. The note taught no mirror exercise or test of selfhood. It didn't make failing to recognize a person an offense. It went at the front, the diary behind. They served different purposes.

Should the limits of care be recorded too? Yu Mei asked.

Lin Mo considered it. Anyone could say their hand hurt or they couldn't keep holding him. Silence wasn't agreement. If he couldn't speak, they should describe a touch first. If he was actually slipping, they could stop a fall without waiting for an answer that came too late.

“What if you're awake and say don't touch?” Fang Ji asked.

“Stop first. Tell me the danger you can see. Don't use this page against me.”

“And if you fall?”

He wouldn't force both situations into one absolute prohibition. Catch him if he was falling, then explain. Don't use a hypothetical fall to justify every restriction. Fang Ji checked the gap beside the wheelchair. The words hadn't created hands that could always catch him.

Yu Mei stopped again while arranging his clothing. A fold near his left side pressed against his skin. She had been about to straighten it, but his rising hand made her ask whether he wanted her to leave it.

“No. Just slowly. It presses there.”

He indicated the spot. She freed only the fold, without lifting the garment. Lin Mo looked down at his present body. The Theater liked to provide it with a more compliant posture, as though a changed shape made refusing touch unnecessary. Yu Mei was simply waiting for him to locate a painful inch of fabric. It still took him time to unclench his hand.

“Later, if you check injuries, only what's needed.”

She agreed. Nobody turned that into evidence that he feared his body, or added a wish to restore his former appearance. They were caring for the person hurting in the chair, not the intact old face behind glass.

He thought of the retained label Special Forces, then the fact that Xiao Lu was his sister. Brief labels invited others to assume they knew how the labeled person ought to live. He asked Fang Ji to add: The past can wait. Start with what is happening here.

Not permanent secrecy or an invented, simpler life. Just time for someone awake in an unknown place, before every former obligation called his name at once.

They tried something smaller next.

Yu Mei returned to the panel. Fang Ji sat; the seatkeeper stayed where he could provide brief support. Lin Mo said he wanted to stop. Yu Mei stepped away without touching an execution control. Fang Ji didn't demand a reason.

His heel was pressing, Lin Mo explained. They dealt with that actual need instead of turning stop into a refusal to save his sister.

Afterward Yu Mei's shoulder had tired again.

“I need to stop now.”

This wasn't rehearsal. Lin Mo almost said only one more thing. He swallowed it and had the notebook closed on his lap. Fang Ji didn't replace her at the panel. The seatkeeper didn't volunteer to supply her shoulder.

They rested where they were.

The Theater found an opening. How could a brother sit comfortably with his sister still inside? Lin Mo looked toward the closed voice port. She was indeed inside. He couldn't call the entire sentence false. Its lie was removing Yu Mei's injury from that fact, as though only his willingness to sit mattered.

He Cen slowly set the water within her reach without standing.

Lin Mo wanted to pour her some. His left hand was halfway out before he remembered that they had no clean cup and his right wrist couldn't help. He stopped and asked whether she wanted water.

“Let me catch my breath.”

He waited.

She took the bottle herself afterward, drank, and offered it to him. He neither refused nor hurried to prove he had recovered. He kept the rim away from his wounded cheek and actually swallowed before saying he wanted to leave a short addendum.

“More of the past?” Fang Ji asked.

“Just now. My part of it.”

He didn't search for a moving sentence. His palm remembered loosened fabric; his heel no longer pressed so hard. Small changes let him remain here. Their worth didn't depend on applause.

Fang Ji looked at the pen without picking it up.

“After my hand rests.”

Lin Mo nodded. The panel remained where they had left it. Nothing advanced automatically. The bed on the wall faded. Five people stayed outside the door, and nobody promised to stand all night.
