# Chapter 160: The Part That Stays Inside

While Yu Mei slept, Lin Mo closed his eyes too.

He slept badly. In a dream, somebody sat on the concrete ledge waiting for him to finish speaking. His own shoulder always blocked the person's face. He tried to move around it, but beneath his feet there was only the wheelchair's footrest.

When he woke, He Cen was watching.

“Your knee?”

“A little.”

“You said stop.”

Lin Mo checked his leg. Nothing had shifted; his right hand remained supported. He couldn't remember what he had wanted stopped and said so. He Cen offered no interpretation, only settled more securely against the wall and told him Yu Mei was still asleep.

He rested his head against the chair again.

When she woke, everyone first had to move themselves. She pushed herself upright slowly. The injured shoulder hadn't been underneath her, but the hard, cold floor had stiffened her. Fang Ji offered help. She refused to trade his shoulder for hers.

The seatkeeper moved the bundle. Empty bottles rolled toward its opening; he caught and returned them one by one. Four large ones, two old small ones. None missing.

The fifth bottle still had water. They shared a little. Lin Mo didn't refuse. It was close to room temperature, and swallowing still pulled at his sore cheek. They had no new medicine, no clean roll of bandage to replace the old dressings. Sleep had not ended He Cen's fever.

“What now?” Yu Mei asked.

“Bring the retention conditions onto one page. Make sure we haven't joined different stages incorrectly.”

“Don't read from the first line to the last all over again,” Fang Ji said sharply.

Lin Mo heard the fatigue beneath it. They had spent too long here. Even an unfilled field could drain them.

“Only the route available now. If the result hasn't changed, we stop trying static copies again.”

Fang Ji nodded. He didn't have to prove indefinitely how long he would help. Lin Mo would not treat this agreement as permission for every subsequent page.

Their query sheet was crowded. Yu Mei tore a narrow half-page from unused notebook paper, leaving enough beside the binding and avoiding the photograph and Old Chen's folds. Using the cover as backing, she wrote three points on the new sheet: Complete Export, Continuity Within the Layer, Succession After Completion.

Lin Mo gave her the location of each condition. No candidate's name, no commitment to care for a body. The new sheet went behind the original transcription. Only the original would enter the reader; the new notes would remain on his lap.

At the doorway, the source matched again. The arm hadn't sagged during their rest. Yu Mei checked the aligned white marks and catch without shaking it, then placed the source view beside the current assessment.

Xiao Lu didn't answer immediately.

Lin Mo waited.

His old face remained in the displaced glass at the end of its channel. Closing other things hadn't erased it. Silent now, it looked more like him than before. Short hair, the familiar jaw, a tiredness he once wore without thinking about it.

He lowered his eyes to the new sheet.

He remembered standing before mirrors, affirming who he was, his voice growing quieter. Sometimes he had feared other people seeing his changed body. Sometimes he feared forgetting why the words mattered. His body now sat in the chair, each injury real. He knew he was Lin Mo. That old face could still drag him toward the need to prove it repeatedly.

Yu Mei didn't call him. He looked away on his own.

“She hasn't connected. We wait. Don't call into it.”

Yu Mei asked whether she should turn the notes over so distant windows couldn't read them. He said to cover only the written side, without draping clothing over the port. They had shut the displays they knew about. No guarantee covered the whole circular room.

The turned paper exposed its torn fibers. He suddenly thought how much easier it would be to hand the notebook to the tray. Perhaps Old Chen's technical sheets contained something useful. Perhaps the private fold held something he had never been allowed to read. Perhaps could make a rescue seem sufficient reason for any intrusion.

He left the thought alone. They could recheck the technical material already available. His sister's confinement did not turn the unopened fold into a possession he was free to inspect. The protection Old Chen had given him couldn't become his entitlement to take more when he most needed help.

Yu Mei showed him that both viewing controls remained closed, then stepped back against the wall.

At last a reply appeared.

[First tell me who's still outside.]

The same companions, Lin Mo had Yu Mei write. Nobody had been entered for retention. Xiao Lu asked after the feverish man. He was still in the shelter, able to speak for himself, with somebody nearby.

Only then did she agree to sound.

“I didn't want to listen just now.”

“All right.”

“I thought about telling you to stop looking.”

His chest tightened. He didn't say no, but his hand caught the paper's edge before he could prevent it. Pain stung his injured finger. He released it and waited.

“But I still want to get out.”

Quieter than a cry for help, it struck him harder than being called her brother. He didn't have to guess whether she was afraid of troubling them or felt obliged to be considerate. She wanted to leave. She wouldn't commit somebody else to remaining. Both could be true.

“I know.”

“Don't leave out the rest because I said that.”

“We're checking the conditions together. I'll read the result.”

Yu Mei opened the combined conditions for the same complete scope. Only capacity relationships were displayed, not its contents. The source was the current item they had verified; the destination followed the original interface. The bypass had not been substituted with its static output.

The narrow opening, unspecified retention end, and outside arrival end appeared together.

Lin Mo checked that partial copies weren't being counted as complete export. Then that near-end conversation hadn't replaced retention. Finally, after arrival, the retention line still extended inward through the data layer.

Yu Mei read the corresponding explanation for each. Fang Ji heard the last part from the doorway.

“So somebody has to stay awake inside forever?”

Lin Mo didn't translate continuing into forbidden to sleep. Nothing here explained how human sleep related to continuity. He could say only that a source had to keep operating in the layer. Leaving their conversation connected wouldn't qualify.

“The name's still blank?”

“Yes.”

Neither person's confirmation was available. Looking at the empty fields, Lin Mo suddenly felt angry. They had done so many small things correctly: stopped, asked, returned, drunk water, repaired the connection. The same hard condition remained. Caution had prevented them from accidentally transferring a person. It had not rewarded his sister with an easier way out.

He clenched his teeth and caught the inside of his cheek.

Yu Mei asked what was wrong. He told her not to move and slowly released his jaw. Xiao Lu waited, then asked whether he was upset because all that checking had left the answer unchanged.

“Yes.”

“I'm angry too.”

He had been about to comfort her. Instead he simply acknowledged it. On either side of the arm, they could each be angry without one first mastering it to give the other permission.

Yu Mei waited until they finished, then enlarged the combined result.

[Complete export on this route requires a continuing retained source]

Below, Individual Confirmation Not Obtained and Exchange Not Executed remained. Reading the result had not consented for anyone. Refusing execution had not canceled the present person able to answer from inside.

Lin Mo read it aloud.

Did that prove every route in the world required an exchange?

No. They had checked the route corresponding to her complete source. The bypass carried copies. Unfound routes couldn't be declared nonexistent, but he couldn't promise one would bring her out today.

“Then what do you know now?”

He looked at the fixed metal arm.

“To use this complete route, something continuing has to remain in the data layer. After delivery, it still has to continue.”

She was quiet for a long time.

He expected her to ask whether he would do it. His chest had already saved breath for the answer. Instead, she asked whether, once everyone outside walked away, nobody would ever ask the retained source anything again.

He didn't know. He hadn't even established how a retained person could express a need or tell those outside they had changed their mind. Repairing their conversation couldn't guarantee that the new ends would hear each other.

“We still need to check communication.”

“Write that down. Don't just write how much has to stay.”

Yu Mei added Communication Unverified to the new sheet, not Xiao Lu's complete words. After writing, the pen almost slipped between her fingers. Lin Mo told her to put it away; her shoulder had carried enough.

Fang Ji called them back. He Cen wanted to change which way he leaned. It wasn't urgent; his voice held none of the earlier alarm. Yu Mei could push Lin Mo along the flat passage once more, she said, then she needed another rest.

He told Xiao Lu they would end this exchange. She agreed, but stopped him before disconnection.

“Have you entered yourself?”

“No.”

“Does thinking about it count?”

He paused. “Thinking isn't execution. But I'll tell you.”

She didn't say she felt reassured. If the next query concerned a particular person, she wanted to know before they finished checking, not afterward. He agreed and waited for her words to end. He did not record the request to defend himself with later.

As Yu Mei closed the combined conditions, a previously folded line appeared beneath Retained Source.

No name. Only the small marker for a type description. He almost told her to close it immediately, but had already read the words.

[Blank continuity structure]

[Requires clearing autobiographical links]

Beneath them was Assessment Only. The entry remained unopened.

He didn't ask her to press it.

Autobiographical made him think first of something ordinary, not an ordeal. His sister had once stolen a piece of food he had already bitten, complaining that he was too slow. He couldn't place the year. He remembered raising the bowl deliberately beyond her reach, and her kicking his chair leg.

Such a small thing, attached to him too.

Should she note the location? Yu Mei asked.

Yes, but they should return first. She marked only where the entry lay, not that they had found a replacement. They hadn't asked whether such blankness could continue, or what would have to be emptied.

In the shelter, He Cen specified that he wanted to lean right. They waited until his feet were secure. Yu Mei helped him turn slightly; the seatkeeper only made room, without bracing him with his injured leg. Fang Ji returned the bundle to the wall. All the bottles remained. The water stood farther in on the ledge.

Lin Mo's wheelchair stopped beside them, brakes set. Yu Mei sat before placing the new notes within his view. The original query sheet stayed separately against the notebook.

The last entry was faint.

He studied it without asking anyone to add his name. Then he requested the notebook on his lap. She passed it cover upward. Photograph, three technical sheets, private fold: none had moved.

His left hand rested on the cover.

There was still a place for him outside. Nobody had yet agreed to hand over what must remain within.
