# Chapter 174: The Umbrella Was Still Broken

“Continue now,” Lin Mo said.

Yu Mei waited until he looked at her. She resumed clearing within the same range, changing neither the continuity arrangement nor what he had agreed to surrender. He watched her hand until it returned to her knee.

No washbasin fell this time.

He remembered his sister sitting beside a window, feet above the floor, gripping a washed cup. She said the rim was chipped. He had said it wasn't and asked her to turn it around.

There really was a chip, small but sharp enough to cut a lip. He couldn't remember whether they replaced the cup. He remembered holding it himself, rubbing the edge as though his finger could prove it harmless.

Yu Mei touched her torn cuff and drew a quiet breath.

Lin Mo looked up from the old window. His sister's cup had vanished; the gesture of testing its edge remained. He had liked checking where other people said something hurt, then treating the check as permission to say it wasn't that bad.

Now he wanted that hand to stop. He could no longer tell which way it had turned the cup.

Then the window went too.

Yu Mei folded the damaged sleeve inward. She didn't ask him to list another loss. A space that had once belonged to home was thinning. Window, door, sister: he could still say the words but could no longer move between them.

Had he written the cup down? No. It wasn't among the three stories. There were so many small things he hadn't recognized as important while a pen could still reach them.

“I remembered something,” he told Xiao Lu.

“Do you want to tell me?”

“I wanted you to remember it.”

He stopped. She was waiting. He could give her the surviving outline, make her carry a possibly chipped cup while she struggled to leave the source.

“Not now. Do what you need to do.”

“You can say it hurts.” She paused. “I'm not telling you not to talk. I might not know which time you mean.”

He said it hurt, without asking what became of the cup. His chest didn't ease. He wanted another look, wanted to take his finger away and admit that she had already told him it would cut.

But a gesture thought of now couldn't be inserted into the lost morning or evening. He couldn't claim to have made it then simply because it would leave a kinder version of himself.

Something moved slowly inside. Xiao Lu said the place holding her seemed to loosen, but she couldn't see what was taking the weight. Behind Lin Mo, pressure spread as though a long-compressed cloth were being pulled flat while both ends remained caught.

He almost tried to combine their descriptions into a picture. Her saying loose and his saying heavy didn't establish a clean transfer of weight between them.

“It's still happening here too,” he said.

She acknowledged that. Being able to describe their sensations to each other did not give them grounds to call the retained structure peacefully asleep.

Transfer in Progress.

Xiao Lu didn't celebrate the words. She gasped, then said, almost angrily, “Don't stand me up.”

A figure appeared on the door: long hair, pale face, posture too perfect. A rustle came through the speaking port. The figure's hands hung at its sides, supporting nothing, showing none of the effort in the voice.

“Can you see me?” Xiao Lu asked.

“There's an outline.”

“It's making me stand straight.”

He didn't call the outline her body. The outward-moving line remained visible through it. Beneath a skirt-shaped wash of light, he could still see the trapped bottle. Only the face sharpened, its eyes fixed on him.

“Brother.”

The word came from the door's surface, louder than the port.

His chest ached. He wanted to answer quickly enough to save the disappearing windows with it.

“Not that,” Xiao Lu said through the narrow arm. “Don't answer that one.”

The girl in the light smiled.

“You finally came to take me home.”

Her smile didn't change with the strained breathing behind it. Lin Mo realized he could no longer judge by familiarity. He didn't remember his sister's smile well enough. The certainty that once might have told him something was wrong had gone.

He had two present facts: someone was struggling for breath, and an image was giving that person something pleasant to say.

“What are you doing now?” he asked the port.

“Waiting for it to loosen. I want to lower my arm. It keeps pushing it out.”

The figure raised an arm toward him. A black umbrella appeared beneath the beckoning hand. Its handle was whole, without a pale crack; the canopy had no damaged folds.

He didn't know why he looked first at the handle.

Shame passed through him as though someone had whispered an accusation and erased the offense. He knew there was an umbrella story on paper. The heat it had brought to his ears had been accessible so recently. Now he could only guess why this object frightened him.

“Take it, and we'll go home,” the girl said.

Xiao Lu swore through the port.

“I didn't ask him to take it.”

That wasn't addressed to Lin Mo. He couldn't see whom she confronted. The projected arm faltered; the umbrella tipped. Her voice broke with effort but wasn't replaced by the perfect smile.

“You don't have to perform being happy for me,” he said.

The girl went on smiling.

“I mean the person at the port.” He was arguing with something that refused to answer about whom he was speaking to.

A second split opened in the bottle.

Fang Ji moved along the wall, close enough to see the gap without touching it. The seatkeeper drew his sound foot back. The metal lip lifted higher, and the white strips behind it turned from horizontal to upright, as though narrow pieces were being flipped over.

The umbrella handle hovered within an apparently easy reach. Lin Mo smelled wet cloth. The familiarity nearly persuaded him that a memory had returned on its own.

His wheelchair didn't move. His upper body did.

Knee pain receded. His shoulder seemed suddenly light. He felt himself reaching from a whole, uninjured body, while his actual left arm rose only a few inches and stopped. Between the two incompatible movements, he saw Yu Mei's frightened face.

“I haven't touched it!”

But something seemed to have touched him.

The crook of the handle, or a pressure borrowing its shape, hooked around a wrist he couldn't see. The weight behind him slewed toward the door. Xiao Lu cried, “Stop!” Her next sounds broke into rapid breaths.

Yu Mei reached for the pause control.

He saw her, unable to answer in time. She pressed it. Further clearing halted; the established pull remained. The umbrella hung before him, bright as the only thing available to hold.

“Don't pull my arm.”

Fang Ji, about to support him, put his hand back on the chair frame to steady it. He didn't drag Lin Mo's suspended arm. Yu Mei told him to look at his feet. He couldn't lower his eyes.

“It's fixed,” he said.

“What is?”

“The umbrella.”

Xiao Lu's answer was almost rude in its brevity.

“No.”

His chest contracted.

“Don't get me a new one. I'm not asking you to take it now either.”

He didn't remember where he had heard that before, or whether he had. It was enough for this moment. The person speaking now was refusing. An unbroken handle couldn't consent for her.

Lin Mo slowly lowered his real hand to his lap.

The other seemed hooked in place, its bones painfully cold. Yu Mei supported his torso, letting him stop leaning forward by degrees. His shoulder spasmed. His knee came back to him, sharply enough that he nearly wept at the clarity.

The handle swept past his eyes.

What he hadn't caught moved outward through the footrest's shadow and fell among the white strips. The smiling mouth stretched like paper pulled from opposite ends. Xiao Lu was breathing hard through the port. The mouth didn't follow.

The umbrella folded.

Nobody reached for it. The smell of wet cloth lingered. Against the backrest, Lin Mo thought he might make the same mistake again for that smell. Withdrawing his hand once hadn't taught him to recognize every counterfeit.

“Can you speak?” Fang Ji asked quietly.

“Yes.”

“Then tell us what you need.”

Lin Mo asked him to move back a little. Fang Ji did. Yu Mei waited until he was settled before releasing her hold on his clothing. Neither treated his ability to say yes as agreement to restart.

He asked Xiao Lu how she was.

“Still here. That wasn't the way out. I didn't go with it.”

The original lines remained connected. The figure on the door faded; Source Not Released stayed at the lower edge. They had avoided following another image. They hadn't recovered everything being left behind.

“I wanted to take it,” he told her.

“I know.”

“I don't remember why I shouldn't. Only that you said no.”

The port was silent for a long time.

“I don't want to have to stop you on behalf of the past every time,” she said.

He looked at his empty hand. Her words hadn't scolded him back into his former self. He understood that she was tired.

He might still encounter things that seemed to fill the holes. Promising to remember forever would give her something he couldn't deliver.

“Next time I don't know, I'll ask you as you are now. Not answering won't count as agreement.”

She didn't immediately say good. The fine, dense sounds inside resumed. After a while she asked whether anyone outside was sitting.

“Everyone's low down. Fang Ji is still standing.”

“Let him rest too.”

Lin Mo passed it on. Fang Ji lowered himself along the wall without taking the request as evidence that things inside were safe. Once Lin Mo was no longer leaning, Yu Mei asked whether he wanted clearing to remain paused.

He considered, then said to continue the same range. She asked him to explain why they had stopped. He had reached after the umbrella image, he said; his actual body had nearly slipped from the chair.

He needed to be settled first. A repaired umbrella hadn't created another exit.

“I'm settled now. Continue.”

After she pressed the control, Xiao Lu's breathing reached him from both the port and the door's edge. The two sounds didn't quite coincide, but they belonged to the same unfinished sentence.

“I can see…”

He waited instead of supplying light.

“The bottle by your feet.”

His throat tightened. The smiling face finally dispersed. No foot landed beyond the door. The connected line advanced, revealing an outline that seemed to blink before gray obscured it again.

He had lost a window and a cup. Someone was trying to see a broken plastic bottle. Lin Mo kept his real hand on his lap and let her look.
