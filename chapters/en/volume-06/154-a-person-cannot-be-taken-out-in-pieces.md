# Chapter 154: A Person Cannot Be Taken Out in Pieces

Xiao Lu asked whether the assessment would put her to sleep.

Yu Mei read to the bottom of the scope page. It mentioned neither sleep nor a body entering. A fine line stated that the query would read the current complete scope without writing or transferring anything.

She withdrew her hand and waited.

“You can look,” Xiao Lu said. “If you see something I haven't told you, don't start reading it aloud for me.”

Lin Mo agreed. He had been about to suggest checking memories together. A complete scope did not entitle him to examine every private thing his sister could not remember.

Yu Mei started the assessment.

The exported sentence shrank into a small square. A pale band opened beside it. Lin Mo expected it to fill across the screen and stop. Instead, it reached the edge, folded onto another row, and continued below that.

Yu Mei waited until a little arrow appeared at the bottom.

“Still going?”

“Look farther down. Don't choose the contents.”

Bands slid out at the top while new ones entered below. They contained no readable words or recognizable scenes. Fine lines stretched between them, crossing beyond the visible page into areas not yet shown.

Xiao Lu called to him.

He remembered that she could not see it. He explained that the interface was unfolding the complete scope, but they had not examined its contents.

She asked where the bottle sentence was. Yu Mei found the present exchange and returned to its first entry.

The short sentence now had more connections.

Within the complete scope, it linked to their discussion of flattened bottles, possible leaks, and not wasting water. It also led back toward the earlier choice to let other people hear. The exported copy remained one sentence. It had not grown with the conversation.

“Does it include what she thought afterward?” Yu Mei asked.

“We can't say that yet. It only calls these links.”

She opened the field description. The outside view showed relationships without naming the exact memory represented by each line. Its categories were Expressed, Unexpanded, and Continuing. The growing band contained more than the sounds they had heard.

Lin Mo looked toward the phone in Yu Mei's pocket. It could hold a long audio recording. He had imagined that more time, more blank space, might let him bring more of her out. But the lines before him joined beyond sight. Receiving a sentence had not given him the process that chose to say it.

“I don't want to tell you everything,” Xiao Lu said.

“You don't have to.”

“Then how would I know which bit you hadn't taken?”

He had no answer. The integrity field was still assessing. He asked whether the results could be displayed without opening private contents. Yu Mei found that choice. The bands collapsed into one complete-scope frame.

The result was no easier to face.

At one end was a narrow export opening. At the other was a region too large to fit on the same page. There was no progress bar that might finish with a few more minutes of patience.

[Complete scope exceeds current capacity]

[Preserving the same continuity: separate transfers unavailable]

Lin Mo asked Yu Mei to leave the result open while he read both lines to Xiao Lu. Only a faint background hiss came through the channel.

“Separate transfers means what? A sentence today and another tomorrow?”

“I want to ask that too.”

The underlined restriction led to another explanation. Three empty frames appeared: a current segment, a break, and a later segment. Yu Mei chose only the demonstration, without supplying any of Xiao Lu's contents.

The first frame moved through the narrow opening and stopped outside. The other two stayed behind. When the second moved, the first frame's boundaries were already fixed. A connecting line fell into the gap without joining them.

[Separate transfers yield independent segments]

[Complete continuity is not established]

Nobody in the demonstration suffered. Still, Lin Mo's stomach tightened. He could collect sentences, order them, fill the spaces, and construct a sister who never interrupted or refused him. The person who changed her mind and asked who would hear her would remain where she was.

He closed the example.

“Don't try it with her.”

Yu Mei rubbed the base of her palm. She did not repeat the query. Lin Mo kept looking at the opening, wondering what might be unnecessary. A face to display? The texture of a voice? Neither was required simply to exist here.

He asked Xiao Lu rather than selecting for her.

She agreed to assess an output without a display, but warned them not to treat unopened contents as unwanted. Yu Mei changed the proposed presentation from picture and sound to no display, keeping the action assessment-only.

The empty human outline became a square. The complete scope still did not fit.

A new mark appeared at the assessment's edge.

Yu Mei pulled her hand away, afraid she had touched another setting. Beneath the mark, Lin Mo read Current Inquiry. Their discussion about closing the display had joined the scope. Its continuing edge shifted a little farther out.

“It's growing,” Yu Mei said.

For an instant, he thought of telling Xiao Lu to stop speaking. If nothing new were added, perhaps the opening could catch up. He had already glanced toward the audio switch before remembering that she was waiting for an explanation.

“The questions we just asked have been added.”

“Did I say too much?”

“No. We're still asking things. It's assessing you as you are now.”

“Would I be smaller if I stopped talking?”

His back tightened. Hearing his own unspoken idea from her made it feel as though he had already pressed the words against her mouth.

He had Yu Mei compare the previous assessment. Both results said the scope exceeded capacity. The added connections were a little at the edge; they had not crowded out something that would otherwise have fitted.

He explained the visible difference.

“There wasn't enough room before. You can still ask questions.”

“Can that wheel go to another door?”

He looked toward the circular passage. They would examine usable routes, he said. This interface matched her source, but they had not found another complete receiving endpoint. He would not turn one unavailable option into a declaration that nowhere else existed. Nor could he promise a door he had not seen.

She said she understood. Another small mark appeared at the assessment's edge. This time he did not watch it, willing it to stop.

They assessed the viewing and audio overhead separately. Some visible borders disappeared, but the integrity field still pointed to the same complete region. They could reduce what was shown to the person outside. Inside, someone still had to continue knowing what she had just asked.

“Turning off the face doesn't do it.”

“I'm more than a face.”

Her voice was sharp. He wanted to say he knew. Yet he had stared at his own former face and almost mistaken it for everything he needed to carry back.

“Yes. That setting hasn't solved it.”

Fang Ji shifted in the corridor, making room for the seatkeeper to approach the turn. The man dragged his injured left leg. He stayed away from the panel and asked whether they needed a larger container, then glanced toward the notebook in the small room.

Lin Mo felt a familiar pressure in his chest. The three maintenance sheets believed to be Old Chen's remained there, along with the unopened private fold. A field labeled complete did not entitle him to have those pages brought over.

“Capacity here means more than a box.”

The man lowered his eyes. “I used to get one piece at a time. Ask for the next when I wanted it. I never thought about where it broke.”

“That was what you wanted to watch.”

He neither argued nor offered compensation. Fang Ji kept the path back to the small room clear. Lin Mo watched that space, remembering how Old Chen had still been impossible to bring out after the barrier was opened.

This was another source, another present inquiry. An old ending could not settle it. Refusing to endure the same thing again did not prove a solution existed either.

He Cen called that he was returning the water to the ledge. The bottle's uneven base needed care, but Yu Mei did not have to come. He could set it down slowly.

She acknowledged him. Lin Mo realized how long they had stayed by the door. The others were reporting even a drink of water down the corridor, as though every ordinary movement risked interrupting him.

“We need a break.”

He explained to Xiao Lu and asked whether the scope page could wait.

“If the page stays still, do I have to stay still?”

They checked. Pausing the outside inquiry was separate from ending their exchange. Only after finding that distinction did he answer.

“Then stop a while,” she said. “You don't have to stand guard over every line.”

Once audio closed, Yu Mei went back for water. Fang Ji stayed where he could call to both ends. He did not try to push the chair. Lin Mo remained outside the doorway, brake on, facing the result and its unchanged Not Executed notice.

The dark surface in the circular room rose again.

A voice murmured that he could bring her out slowly. Piece by piece was better than nothing. It did not bother imitating Xiao Lu. It spoke his own persistent thought with perfect sympathy.

He studied the little square beyond the opening. There were things he could take: a reminder, a breath, perhaps many fragments he treasured. They could matter, and his sister could still remain inside.

“She's in there.”

Fang Ji looked over. “Who are you talking to?”

“Reminding myself. So I don't report it wrong when we go back.”

“Then I heard it too. I won't turn it into good news.”

After resting, Yu Mei returned with the water. She gave him a small drink, keeping the bottle rim away from his sore cheek. Only then did he realize how raw his throat felt. She made room for herself to sit and set the bottle clear of the controls. Once he had recovered, she took it back to the small room. It did not become another proposed input.

They reconnected with Xiao Lu.

First she asked whether they had rested. Then she asked whether the assessment had started moving anything while they were gone.

Yu Mei read the unchanged Not Executed notice. Lin Mo said they had not selected another scope during the break.

Xiao Lu told him to continue.

This time he did not seek a smaller display. He asked whether capacity could be expanded, and under what conditions, without opening execution.

Yu Mei expanded the notes beneath the failed assessment. Most concerned the partial export they already knew. The final entry was still folded.

When she opened it, a line emerged from beneath the complete scope, bypassed the narrow opening, and ended at another blank region.

It was just as large. Nobody's name appeared beside it.

[Complete export: separate retention item required]

Lin Mo stopped Yu Mei before she pressed anything else.

Xiao Lu heard the silence and asked whether something new had appeared.

“Yes,” he said. “I'll read the exact words first.”
