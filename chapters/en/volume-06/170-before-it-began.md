# Chapter 170: Before It Began

Lin Mo didn't immediately ask Yu Mei to return to the panel.

He watched her finish drinking and set down the bottle, then waited for Fang Ji to secure the notebook. He Cen woke long enough to listen before settling against the gray clothing again. The seatkeeper left the light bag against the wall inside the small room, its empty bottles clear of their feet. Nobody arranged themselves in a solemn row.

Lin Mo said he had decided.

His voice was lower than expected. He had thought saying it might loosen his chest. Instead, it only placed the sentence where other people could hear. His knee still hurt. So did his inner cheek. Making a decision hadn't healed anything in advance.

“Decided which step?” Yu Mei asked.

“To use this part of myself to establish the blank continuity structure. To bring Xiao Lu out along the complete route we've checked.”

He didn't look to Fang Ji for a nod. He explained the scope: lived associations connected with his autobiographical male self. Growing up. Becoming the person he was through training. Living with his sister as her brother. The days spent resisting forced changes. The boundary followed the assessment already made. It couldn't be reduced to a few episodes he was willing to call unimportant.

“You'll forget you used to be a man,” Fang Ji said.

Lin Mo's left hand slowly tightened.

“I'll lose the experiences that let me remember that. It isn't changing a gender setting on a screen.”

He wouldn't make the choice cleaner by claiming he no longer cared. His former body. The ways people had called his name. His insistence that he remained present while they changed him against his will. Those things mattered. That was what made this a cost rather than rubbish he could finally discard.

Yu Mei watched him.

“You don't have to say you like the method.”

“I don't.”

The quick answer let him breathe. He didn't want to lose these things, call the retained side's unknown experience easy, or prove the Theater right that altering a person solved everything. He chose this route because Xiao Lu was still inside and its conditions now lay between them.

Other routes might exist. They hadn't found one. Continued searching couldn't honestly be described as a guarantee of a timely answer. Continuing now and stopping now both had weight. He wouldn't hurry himself with an invented deadline, but neither would he use unexhausted possibilities to pretend he could defer choosing forever.

Was he doing it because everyone had already helped this much? Fang Ji asked.

No. Their effort didn't purchase an obligation to finish. If he stopped, preparation would remain preparation, not a loss he must repay with himself.

“You helped hold me. That doesn't mean you've agreed to everything afterward.”

“And regret?”

“I might regret it. Saying this can't delete that possibility.”

He looked at the notebook and resisted adding another page. Diary One and Current Addendum held stored words under separate indexes. The paper and photograph remained. Nothing made clearing a temporary loan. He might read those words later and find a stranger who had made a decision using his name.

Only after resting did Yu Mei return to the panel. Fang Ji stayed beside the chair. The seatkeeper could provide brief support, not lift him. He Cen wasn't assigned the job of remaining awake as a second observer. Lin Mo could see Yu Mei's face and when her hand left a control.

They reconnected with Xiao Lu.

He told her he had chosen, but nothing had begun. Hearing his decision didn't amount to her consent to transfer. He began with the absence of a usable channel from the retained side, its unknown subjective experience, and the continuing requirement after delivery. He didn't begin with the prospect of seeing each other soon.

“What happens to the Lin Mo outside?” she asked.

“My name, twenty-five, and Special Forces remain as facts. So does the fact that you're my sister. Motor programs are retained within the assessed scope. Injuries don't heal. The feeling of having lived those days together doesn't stay just because the words do.”

“If I tell you I'm your sister, you might just say you know.”

He studied a worn spot on the screen's edge.

“I might. I might ask again why you're saying it.”

“Can I choose not to explain?”

“Yes.”

Only then did he recognize the expectation he had quietly assigned her. Even without asking aloud, he had given her a future task: fill every absence, teach him who he had been. She could tell him. She could also be tired or unwilling to undergo her own losses again.

“You aren't responsible for talking me back into who I was.”

She was quiet. No applause arrived through her channel. Nobody told her to be grateful. Lin Mo waited for her, not for a sentence that would make continuing easier.

“I want out,” she finally said. “By the complete route you've checked. Not a few sentences. Not a picture that you call me.”

Yu Mei opened Xiao Lu's transfer conditions separately, reading the current source and original destination. The retained candidate hadn't been changed to her. After listening, Xiao Lu made her own transfer choice. Her consent appeared in that field. The source remained where it was. Transfer had not begun.

“That doesn't answer for you,” she added.

“I know.”

“You do your own part.”

He nodded, then answered aloud. His sister's willingness wasn't a hand reaching out to press the control for him.

The panel returned to his assessed scope. Yu Mei checked retained semantic facts and motor programs, then placed the clearing summary beside them. No childhood images were opened. No old face authenticated him. The candidate connection still identified the Lin Mo currently speaking from outside.

The lowest controls offered neither a restored appearance nor an end to pain.

Submit This Choice.

Clearing Must Be Started Separately.

He had Yu Mei read both lines for everyone awake. Submission would prepare his chosen scope and retained items. Reading a diary wouldn't start it. Neither would his sister's consent. Actual execution required a separate operation on the next page.

Then he couldn't see the lowest words clearly.

He first thought the panel was changing again. Turning his head, he realized Yu Mei could still read. The blur was in his eyes. He lifted his right hand. The support pulled at his wrist, and pain halted it halfway.

“My sleeve,” he said.

She didn't understand. With his left hand, he indicated the cuff and explained that he wanted to wipe his eyes. She brought the fabric within reach without lifting his face for him. He wiped slowly, then lowered his hand. The words came together again.

Nobody told him not to cry.

Fang Ji moved the paper on his lap away from a possible drop. It wasn't a tender movement. The corner made a short, dry sound against his trousers. Lin Mo was grateful he hadn't closed the preparation page, as though tears revoked a person's right to choose.

“I haven't changed my mind.”

“You can say if you do,” Fang Ji replied.

Lin Mo nodded. After a while, he repeated that he hadn't. Thinking of comrades whose faces were difficult to describe without names, or of his sister not always being pleased when he came home, still hurt. That grief didn't need shaping into a better sacrifice story. It didn't authorize the interface to hurry him either.

Yu Mei waited until he looked toward the panel himself, then finished the interrupted line. She didn't soften her voice. She read clearly enough for him to hear every limitation.

He wouldn't turn the separation between preparation and execution into another indefinite delay.

“Submit this scope. I choose the clearing myself. I've heard the cost and what cannot be restored.”

“Do you want to touch it yourself?”

He looked at his left hand. Reaching would require leaning, straining his shoulder and knee, and having her move him into position first. He nearly made everyone undertake that risk just to make the gesture look more like his own.

“Don't move the chair. I tell you, you press. I'll watch.”

Yu Mei selected the submission control he had identified. She didn't touch a Start control that hadn't appeared yet. The chosen scope settled into place. Each retained item occupied a line. Not Executed remained lit.

His heart kicked at Not.

Not because he wanted to retract his words. His body had finally understood that next time they would change more than fields on a screen. Sweat filled his palm; his fingers cooled. A doorway, a call to formation, a meal he hadn't properly finished all crowded forward. None volunteered to be less important than the others.

He didn't choose one to tell the group.

From farther away, his former voice said, perfectly clearly, “Begin.”

Yu Mei's hand stayed still.

Fang Ji looked up, checking Lin Mo's mouth first. Lin Mo watched them and waited for the sound to pass. They knew a similar voice couldn't replace the person here. This time, knowing became an action that didn't happen.

“I haven't told you to execute.”

“I heard,” Yu Mei said.

An intact hand appeared on the wall, reaching toward him. Its joints and palm lines resembled his former hand. It carried a button labeled Painless. Recognizing the lure didn't erase his longing. He placed his actual hand on his thigh, feeling fabric and the finger that still hurt.

“Open the real next step.”

Yu Mei opened the execution page and stopped at its explanation. Pause Further Clearing remained separate from Terminate Continuous Carrying. The former wouldn't restore lost associations; the latter couldn't casually replace it during transfer. If someone's body failed them, they would deal with the immediate situation, not use earlier consent to suppress a present refusal.

Lin Mo had her show Fang Ji where the pause was. Not unlimited permission to act for him. He didn't want them searching through similar labels when stopping mattered most. If he no longer recognized them during the clearing, they should start with what was happening around him, not demand that he recite his diary as proof of willingness.

Yu Mei lowered her hand.

The actual execution control waited at the bottom of the narrow display, untouched. Preparation was marked ready above it. Xiao Lu remained at the source. No continuous structure had been established. No interface had pulled him anywhere. The wheelchair brake remained engaged, his right wrist in its existing support.

“I'm here,” Xiao Lu said.

He heard his sister and could still remember many earlier times. Her voice carrying across a street. His refusal to admit taking the wrong thing. Later, the struggle required to hear just one more sentence. Contradictory memories crowded together. Preparing to lose them hadn't made them perfect.

“So am I.”

He asked Yu Mei to let him settle his breathing first. She agreed. Fang Ji secured the notebook. He Cen shifted the gray clothing without fully waking. The seatkeeper kept his hands on his own knees instead of treating Lin Mo already as a falling weight.

Lin Mo looked at the real beginning.

The scrape on the doorframe remained at the edge of his vision. He remembered watching it with Xiao Lu, remembered that not wanting to talk hadn't meant she was angry. That small present still belonged to him. Preparation hadn't taken it out of his body. He opened his hand and waited for his breathing to slow.

The next step hadn't happened.

Nobody spoke the command for him.
