# Chapter 172: The Bottle in the Gap

Xiao Lu's voice was closer. Lin Mo still couldn't count another person outside the door.

Darkness moved beneath the raised metal edge, advancing with the line of light inside like a negative slowly being drawn out. His right footrest wasn't far away. They'd already moved the wheelchair back once; another retreat would catch Fang Ji's knees.

“Don't stand behind the chair.”

Fang Ji stepped aside without asking why. Yu Mei kept her hands still on the push handles. Only then did Lin Mo realize he'd made two different actions sound like one. Asking someone to move didn't mean moving the chair again.

“Leave the chair where it is. I just don't want you caught behind it.”

“I know.” Fang Ji came around the side, where he could finally see beneath the metal. “What's that?”

Lin Mo couldn't make it out. White strips flickered through the gap, crowded together and dotted with black. The parts resembling teeth were too regular; the parts resembling writing moved too fast. He didn't lean closer; his left hand had already moved to shield his splinted right wrist.

A scream burst from the voice port.

Yu Mei almost lunged toward the arm. Lin Mo pitched forward before Xiao Lu called, “That isn't me!”

The screaming continued, climbing the door's edge and passing overhead, its ending splitting into many separate laughs. He'd heard that laughter before. While his bones strained in front of a mirror, the audience had waited like this for his voice to change. A few complained about the noise; more wanted him to hold out a little longer.

He remembered bracing a hand against the wall, still believing that standing straight through sheer effort could slow the changes. A patch beneath his palm was cold. Flaking plaster had exposed the back of the mirror.

Then the hand against the wall lost its history.

He knew the Theater had altered his body, knew he'd said something in front of that mirror. But words he'd repeated so often suddenly resembled an answer someone had taught him. He still recognized them and could say them aloud. Behind their sound, the path back to every time he'd clenched his teeth was breaking.

“Lin Mo. Head up,” Yu Mei said.

His chin was nearly against his chest. Leaning forward had let him slide lower; his right knee pressed against the footrest, his left shoulder unsupported. Yu Mei wouldn't haul him up beneath the arms. She held the handles steady and asked him to ease his weight backward first.

He tried. He couldn't do it.

The pressure of the unseen hand returned, this time squeezing behind his shoulder blade. He seemed to be leaning in two directions at once: toward the chair's back and toward a narrow opening without an edge. Neither direction yielded. He could only gasp with his mouth open.

“Don't hold your breath,” He Cen said from the wall.

“I'm not.”

“You didn't make a sound for ages.”

Lin Mo tried to argue and released a breath he'd trapped instead. He asked Yu Mei to support him where the cloth lay against his left side, without pulling his arm. Fang Ji braced the frame, giving him time to inch back. When the chair finally supported him, black specks still circled his vision.

Xiao Lu asked where it hurt.

“My back. And everywhere that was already hurt.”

“Things are changing position here.” She paused. “I don't know where the scream you heard came from.”

He wanted to tell her not to be afraid. What rose first was a plea for her to stop moving. He couldn't say that. She wasn't wandering around for amusement; none of this had happened because she'd disobeyed him.

“Just tell us what's happening on your side.”

A small click came from beneath the door. The seatkeeper extended his good foot and checked it just short of the metal, apparently intending to press the edge down. Lin Mo told him not to. The man looked up, moved his lips, then lowered his heel where it had been.

“It used to move downward,” the seatkeeper said.

“You've seen this?”

“I've seen strips fed through under the door. Never seen a person come out here.”

Lin Mo registered the distinction. The man's experience hadn't supplied them with a ready-made exit. Those white strips might merely be carrying images, changing the material for some viewing still in progress. Pinning them down might only trap someone in the gap.

“Get an empty bottle.”

Fang Ji returned to the little room's doorway, brought the light bag beside his feet, and took out one of the large empties. Old creases marked its sides. He didn't hand it to Lin Mo. Crouching at an angle, he laid it where Lin Mo indicated and nudged it with his shoe.

The bottle rolled once. Just before it reached the white strips, its mouth swung toward them. Nobody touched it again, but it slid several inches into the gap. The plastic collapsed with a sharp pop.

He Cen flinched. Fang Ji had already withdrawn his foot.

The metal held the bottle fast, leaving only its base exposed. Tiny faces reflected in the transparent cracks; before Lin Mo could distinguish one, they crowded together into white. The base quivered against the floor with an intermittent rasp.

“Leave it,” he said.

Fang Ji couldn't stand immediately. He waited for the pain to ease, then rose slowly without pressing his injured hand against the floor. Looking at his bandaged right palm, Lin Mo encountered a small emptiness. He'd known why that hand was wrapped; now he had to follow a distant thought to find the reason.

This time he found it. The connection hadn't broken completely. Finding it brought no relief: the experience had become something he could lose at any moment, while Fang Ji stood alive before him, lowering his arm carefully to spare his left shoulder.

“Stay away from the gap now.”

Fang Ji glanced at him. “I wasn't going to reach in.”

Lin Mo nodded. He'd spoken too sharply. Old habits still found someone to order around, without ensuring he remembered why that person might dislike being ordered.

Another section of bottle crumpled, lifting the metal edge farther outward. Yu Mei was stepping aside when its edge caught her cuff. She stopped at once, but her shoulder had already been pulled back. Color drained from her face.

Fang Ji reached to grab her.

“Don't pull!” Lin Mo shouted.

Laughter from the door drowned him out. Fang Ji hadn't heard; his left hand was already against her clothing. Ignoring his injured finger, Lin Mo struck the armrest twice with his left palm. Fang Ji turned, and he shouted the words again with all his strength.

Yu Mei held the painful angle. “It's hooked. Not caught inside the gap.”

She made each word clear. Lin Mo couldn't see the snag, only a taut line of cloth. An efficient solution came to mind: press the edge flat with his fingers, lift the fabric back the other way. He'd probably done such things many times; now he couldn't lean another half inch.

“Can you see it?” he asked Fang Ji.

“Yes. A little burr.”

“Listen to her. Don't follow my guesses.”

Fang Ji's hand stopped. Yu Mei lowered her forearm slightly, her breathing trembling with pain, then eased it a fraction toward the door. As the fabric slackened, Fang Ji used only his left fingers to release it from the burr and guide it through the opening. He neither dragged her whole garment off her shoulder nor pressed the moving white strips below.

A fresh tear remained in her cuff.

Yu Mei withdrew beside the chair and sat low, her other hand against her shoulder. She closed her eyes. Lin Mo's heart raced, but all he could say was, “If he'd kept pulling…”

He stopped. She was already sitting down. She didn't need him enlarging her pain with a worse outcome that hadn't happened.

“Sorry,” Fang Ji said.

“You stopped. Next time, hear me before you move.”

Someone in the door's laughter asked why he was complaining about pain when he'd agreed to this himself.

Lin Mo raised his head. The sentence rolled across the gray surface several times, lighter and more cheerful each time. He didn't deny his pain or presume to answer whether Yu Mei had wanted what had happened to her.

“Her clothes weren't part of what I agreed to give you.”

His voice couldn't rise above the laughter. He left it at that. Yu Mei opened her eyes, checked whether the tear had spread, and tucked the loose corner inward. Only after she'd finished did she return her hand to her knee.

A small luminous frame opened on the door.

The washroom appeared again: basin, mirror, rolled trousers. The younger face turned toward the doorway. Lin Mo almost held his breath, waiting for the forgotten person to enter. Instead, the image cropped around his chin, enlarging the shaving nick, then showed his shoulder, collar, and once-familiar stance.

Nobody came through the doorway.

Xiao Lu remained silent for a long time.

Lin Mo heard intermittent knocking. By the third knock, he realized it didn't match his heartbeat. He asked whether she'd struck something, but she hadn't. She could now hear someone outside breathing hard, very far away, without the sound passing through the voice port.

He Cen coughed twice against the wall. “Like that?”

After a pause, Xiao Lu said she'd heard. The surprise in her voice tightened Lin Mo's chest. Instead of announcing that she was out, he told He Cen not to cough for them again. The feverish man closed his mouth and leaned back without smiling.

“Anything else?” Lin Mo asked.

“An empty bottle, I think. It keeps scraping.”

He watched the crushed base beneath the door. Each tremor sent a rasp from the real floor into his ears. That sound now reached her too, although she hadn't crossed over.

He wanted to tell her she could complain about noisy bottles once she was out. Instead he said, “There really is a bottle out here.”

“I know.”

For the first time, she knew a small object outside was still moving without relying on his description. Listening to the plastic scrape, Lin Mo suddenly couldn't remember the first time his sister had handed him water. There wasn't even an image worth trying to pursue.

He didn't look up at the luminous frame again.
