# Chapter 167: Silence Was Not an Answer

The empty space didn't open into a voice port.

Yu Mei enlarged the text. Retention Monitoring appeared, followed by Operation, Continuity, and Load. Lin Mo found nothing that would let what remained inside call out. Xiao Lu could say she didn't want to listen. She could end a conversation. This panel didn't even identify a place where an answer might appear.

“Anything below?”

Yu Mei scrolled to the last line, elbow tucked near her waist instead of holding her shoulder up. The explanation was brief.

Operational records do not establish subjective state.

She paused and repeated the second half.

Lin Mo thought it worse than an empty space. The interface apparently anticipated people mistaking still running for all right. It warned them against that inference without saying how anything inside could ask for help.

“Would it call you?” Xiao Lu asked.

“There's no available channel for it to initiate contact here.”

“No channel, or one that isn't open?”

He asked Yu Mei to examine the connections for this type, without trying another person's source. Their existing channel led to Xiao Lu's current source segment. Once the blank structure existed, they couldn't simply redirect that channel toward it. No immediately available alternative address was listed.

Lin Mo looked toward the voice port. They had only just learned not to take silence for agreement. If the thing that remained couldn't produce anything except silence, what would all their careful waiting achieve?

“When you can't make it work, are you going to say it doesn't count as a person?” Xiao Lu asked.

He wanted to answer no. Then he remembered his relief at finding a method that didn't require putting one of his companions inside. He had made blankness lighter in his mind: enough of something left behind, without having to ask who would be there.

“I hoped it might not suffer,” he said. “I can't make that a condition we've established.”

She didn't answer. He didn't use her silence as forgiveness.

A voice farther away softened. Empty meant nothing was there, it said. Forget, and you could sleep. Its tenderness moved along the wall like someone afraid to wake a child.

Lin Mo felt cold across his shoulders. If it really was sleep, the important questions would be whether it could wake, and whom it could ask when it wanted to. Gentleness didn't answer either one.

Yu Mei didn't try to silence a speaker outside their control. She left Xiao Lu's connection and Retention Monitoring visible together so he could see their different labels.

“Want to run an empty model?”

He nodded. The model held no name or experience of his, no consciousness inserted to follow instructions. A short line lit on the retained side. An operation marker moved with it. Selecting the monitor returned Connection Established. No voice. No image of a silent sleeper.

He asked whether they could attach a sentence and make it answer. Then he stopped himself.

The diary stored in the residual circuit consisted of words. Adding a preset I'm all right and having it appear periodically would only make the people outside feel better.

“Don't try that.”

Yu Mei hadn't touched anything. She looked up and asked why.

He explained his impulse. He wanted something that could return and report safety, even if he had composed its report in advance. He hated admitting that in front of his sister. But without the admission, the soothing instruction to sleep would merely emerge in his own voice.

“What do we look at later, then?” Fang Ji asked from the corridor.

“What it can prove. Continuity. Not a statement about how anything inside feels.”

“What if you still want to stop?”

Lin Mo studied the model. This part remained unresolved despite their earlier examination of withdrawal. Delivery wouldn't release what stayed behind. But if he asked them to stop while clearing was still under way, did that necessarily require cutting off the continuous source as well?

Yu Mei's fingers trembled. He had her lower her hand while he searched the current page. The process explanation was at the bottom right, between establishment and carrying. It required no further identity check. After a rest, she enlarged it.

Two kinds of stopping appeared.

Pause Further Clearing.

Terminate Continuous Carrying.

The first would not restore associations already cleared or shut down basic continuity already established. The second affected a requirement of outward transfer. Lin Mo asked her to finish reading the first control's limits. It paused clearing that hadn't yet proceeded. It couldn't arbitrarily freeze a transfer already in progress or promise recovery of damaged associations.

“So if I say stop,” he said slowly, “whatever I've forgotten stays forgotten.”

“Yes.”

“But stopping the next deletion doesn't require unplugging what's inside.”

“That's what this says.”

They demonstrated only on the empty model. Several groups of contentless lines faded in sequence. Yu Mei selected Pause Further Clearing. The remaining lines stayed lit and still. The retained-side marker continued moving. None of the faded connections grew back.

Lin Mo watched, searching for some small opening through which he might reclaim himself. There wasn't one. He could prevent the next step. Stopping couldn't turn the previous one into something that hadn't happened.

His former voice suddenly broke in from farther away.

“Continue.”

Yu Mei withdrew her hand. The model remained paused. Only then did Lin Mo exhale. Speaking could tell a companion what he wanted; it hadn't activated this control by itself. In the real procedure, anyone touching it on his behalf would still need to know whom they had heard.

“Who do they listen to when you don't recognize them?” Xiao Lu asked.

Lin Mo looked at Fang Ji. The man sat where he could see him, pale and tired. He wasn't offering a noble expression that promised to endure any future misunderstanding.

“Me, then,” Lin Mo said.

“What if that you says he doesn't want me?”

His mouth remained open.

He could promise it wouldn't happen. That would ease this moment. But he was preparing to clear the experiences that made such a promise instinctive, retaining only a relationship as a fact. Knowing the word sister didn't necessarily mean agreeing to further damage for a relationship described on paper.

“They still can't use what I'm saying now to shut him up.”

A faint noise came from her end. Breath or interference; he couldn't tell. He refused to name it for her. Pausing further clearing would still be different from ending something already established, he added. They would have to examine actual progress then, not press any control that happened to resemble Stop.

It wasn't a promise that everybody would come back safe.

Yu Mei noted the control's location on the narrow unused edge of their query sheet. Only the distinction between further clearing and carrying, not Xiao Lu's words. Before she finished, He Cen's head dropped forward.

The seatkeeper called his name.

He didn't immediately lift it. Fang Ji planted his right foot to rise. Lin Mo saw He Cen's clothing still moving with his breathing and asked Fang Ji not to rush. Breathing alone didn't prove he had merely fallen asleep.

Yu Mei left the panel, clearing the narrow approach. The seatkeeper announced that he was moving closer. His hand stopped beside He Cen's shoulder without pulling it. At the second call, He Cen opened his eyes and swore through a cracked, dry voice.

“I heard you. Waking up takes a moment.”

His head tilted toward the wall again. Yu Mei asked whether leaning there hurt. No, he said. His neck was tired; he wanted to turn. They waited for his feet to settle before helping him shift. Nobody translated the fever into permission to sleep without concern.

Lin Mo considered those few moments without an answer.

Here there was a body, breathing, a name to call before touching. A slow waking could become irritation at being watched too closely. An operation marker offered none of that. The two silences resembled each other only because someone outside was waiting for a response.

He asked Xiao Lu to wait. Her channel remained open. She could hear the activity without being assigned responsibility for a sick man she couldn't see.

He Cen drank a little and returned the bottle to the inner ledge. He wanted to sleep. They shouldn't keep him awake merely to preserve someone capable of answering.

“How often should I call you?” the seatkeeper asked.

“Don't count it like that. I'll say if I want to move. Call if I'm sliding down.”

Lin Mo didn't apply this workable, present arrangement to the retained structure.

The empty model remained paused when they returned. No further lines had vanished while they tended He Cen. Yu Mei dismissed it. The real status still read Not Established and Not Executed. She checked that distinction once, without rereading every condition.

“What we know got worse,” she said.

Yes. Knowing they could pause hadn't reduced the empty space where a channel ought to be. They couldn't answer for the retained side through the body outside. A lack of tears here wouldn't prove an absence of pain there.

Lin Mo remembered repeating his name before mirrors. Once, he had thought saying the same thing again and again proved that the essential part remained intact. Now he saw that a sentence written beforehand could also keep returning, while whatever was actually enduring something might be unable to speak.

He pressed his palm against the rough fabric on his thigh. That sensation belonged to him here, in the chair. He couldn't extrapolate from it to the retained side, claiming either that he would feel the same there or that no self would exist there. Two boxes on a screen couldn't settle that.

“There's something I don't want us to do,” he said.

Yu Mei looked up.

“Find a light that's still on and call that part of me cared for.”

She studied the operation field before answering. She could report honestly what a check showed. She couldn't promise always to have the strength to search deeper.

“I know.” He stopped. “I've heard you.”

The difference seemed small and tasted bitter. Knowing sounded like an answer he already owned. Hearing meant someone had just placed a limitation before him. If he continued, he had to carry that limitation too, without secretly replacing it with she would find a way.

“Still preparing?” Yu Mei asked.

She wanted an answer only to that question. Not one that justified everything they had spent getting here.

“The care arrangements. The final scope. Still no execution.” He paused. “We leave the unknown in.”

Xiao Lu finally spoke, quietly. Would he describe the part left behind as something they had finished using?

Lin Mo considered that for a long time. He couldn't promise to hear it later or find an immediate way to bring it back. But he could choose how they spoke about it now.

“Not used up. Your arrival wouldn't mean its side was over.”

Yu Mei folded the paper, leaving the unresolved space uncrossed. He didn't ask her to mark it solved. Far away, someone imitated sleeping breaths, perfectly regular. He listened instead to He Cen's tired breathing, interrupted by a small cough before resuming unevenly.

For now, he preferred that sound.

Yu Mei returned to his side and waited for him to relax his shoulder. He didn't consume her rest with another string of questions. The paper on his lap moved slightly with his breathing. Its unfinished space remained empty.
