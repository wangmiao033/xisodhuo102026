# Chapter 152: Show Her the Chair First

Xiao Lu chose the wheel.

She asked whether the chair could move by itself and whether anyone was standing beside it. Yu Mei was still sitting against the wall when Fang Ji read the questions aloud.

“Me,” she said. “Don't put me in the picture yet.”

Lin Mo agreed. Fang Ji explained, and the person inside accepted the restriction. Then she asked to see only the floor beneath the wheel.

Two small frames appeared at the bottom of the narrow interface. One was marked Outside View. The other remained blank.

Yu Mei stood too quickly and caught the wall.

“Wait,” Lin Mo said.

It was her shoulder, she told him, not dizziness. She loosened her fingers without raising her arm, waiting until the pull eased before inspecting the frame.

The chair was already visible in it: the lower half of a large wheel, a chip in the painted rim, gray concrete beneath.

The image came through a narrow opening under the metal arm. Yu Mei found a sliding cover along its edge. The earlier live display remained off. This separate control was marked Current Exchange View, limited to the area selected for the recipient. Nothing said it stopped observation elsewhere.

She moved the cover slightly.

More of the frame appeared. The edge of Lin Mo's left shoe entered the image, and she immediately slid it back. Only the outside preview changed; the receiving frame remained dark.

The glimpse had not hurt his foot. It had made him more uneasy than the heat where the shoe rubbed his heel.

“Show her this part.”

Yu Mei checked whether sound would be included. The audio control was still closed. Fang Ji described the selected image: wheel and floor, no foot, nobody nearby.

[All right. Don't move when you open it.]

Yu Mei enabled the view. The receiving frame lit with the same narrow image. Lin Mo almost reached to cover a patch of dirt beside the wheel, stopping at the armrest.

Xiao Lu did not ask him to clean it.

[Is that black line on the right a crack?]

“No. The wheel's shadow.”

Yu Mei looked down. “She might mean farther right.”

There was another fine line beside the shadow, extending beyond the frame. He could not judge its depth. Yu Mei enlarged the outside preview while keeping the recipient's view unchanged. White dust lay inside the line; the wheel's shadow had almost concealed it.

He asked Fang Ji to write that he had been mistaken and needed to look.

“Easy enough to type,” Fang Ji said with a short, relieved laugh.

Yu Mei crouched and touched the line's edge with her shoe. It was an old concrete joint, too narrow to admit the toe. She did not run the wheelchair over it as a demonstration. Standing again, she reported what she could see.

“Write: there is a joint. We can see the bottom here. The wheel isn't on it.”

After receiving that answer, Xiao Lu asked whether the brake was on.

Yu Mei held the handles and tested them gently. The large wheel stayed still. This time Lin Mo reported the check instead of answering from the familiar outline of the chair.

[Then stay there. You said you were sitting. I was worried the chair might roll backward.]

He stared at you said. She had connected his recent words to the wheel and noticed something he had answered incorrectly. The chair remained motionless, but the exchange had taken a direction he had not supplied.

“She remembers,” Yu Mei said quietly.

He nodded. He wanted to show his face, ask whether she recognized it, make this thin connection carry everything at once.

Instead, he asked about a brief two-way audio channel. It would spare the others some typing.

[Will the people nearby hear me?]

“Yes.”

[Then tell them first. I don't know what to call them.]

Fang Ji went back down the corridor. He did not shout that they had found her. He explained that someone was answering in the present and wanted the others to know they might hear her voice.

He Cen asked whether this was the repeated instruction to reach out.

“This time she hears questions and changes her mind,” Lin Mo called. “We aren't asking you to hear that old piece again.”

After a pause, He Cen said he could hear well enough from where he sat. He would not get up. The seatkeeper remained silent. Fang Ji reported only what He Cen had actually said.

Yu Mei agreed to be heard, but still did not want to be shown. Fang Ji agreed to audio too. Lin Mo had the view closed before they sent the new request.

A point of light appeared on the other side.

Yu Mei opened the outside audio control and stepped back against the wall. A fine breath of sound emerged, as though someone were approaching a distant microphone.

“Can you hear me now?”

“Yes.” His lips trembled.

“Don't ask anything yet. Let me finish.”

“Go ahead.”

“When I could see the wheel, there was something white beside it. I don't know whether it was light or whether nothing was there. Don't tell me to walk toward it.”

Her voice broke slightly on white. He wanted to ask where she was standing, but waited.

“I don't know where my feet are either. When I think about feet, there are other people's shoes.”

He looked toward the closed seam beneath the gray door. He had still been searching for somewhere his sister might stand. Now she was telling him there were no feet he could locate by following her voice.

“I won't tell you to walk.”

“What were you going to ask?”

Whether she could get comfortable, he said. She did not answer that. Instead, she asked whether the person beside him often had to help him.

Yu Mei murmured yes, then introduced herself.

“Thank you,” Xiao Lu said.

Yu Mei rubbed her hands on her clothes before replying. “You don't have to thank me for him. You can tell him what you want to say.”

The channel fell quiet.

Lin Mo felt rigid against the backrest. Perhaps his sister did not know why he was hurt. Perhaps the thanks had only been courtesy. He could not make it an acknowledgment of everything he had suffered.

“I wanted to find you. These people came part of the way with me. They each have their own concerns.”

“Then ask whether they're tired.”

“I did. They still are.”

A little laugh broke off before its final breath. He went still until she continued. If they were tired, they should sit; she had not asked them to queue.

Yu Mei smiled a little and lowered herself against the wall.

“Already sitting.”

That laugh could not tell him how much of Xiao Lu's past survived. But she had made a fresh, slightly impatient judgment, and Yu Mei had acted on it. Her shoulder would not have to keep holding her upright out of politeness.

He Cen coughed down the corridor, muffling the first cough before letting the next one out.

Yu Mei turned but did not try to be in two places at once. She asked whether the water was within reach. He said it was. The seatkeeper added that the cap had been loosened.

Xiao Lu asked who it was.

Lin Mo said someone traveling with them had a fever. He did not supply a name or the man's earlier history. She asked whether they should close the sound so he would not have to be overheard.

Yu Mei relayed the question.

He Cen caught his breath. “Don't close it over a couple of coughs. Just don't call me over to talk.”

“He doesn't want to come here,” Lin Mo explained.

“I heard.”

“Good,” He Cen answered from farther away.

The exchange contained nothing that could prove a family connection. Yet Lin Mo lowered his head. So many voices had demanded they approach, come closer, expose the hurt place. She had heard someone refuse and asked nothing more of him.

After a moment, he asked whether she wanted to be called Xiao Lu.

“That's what you've been writing.”

“We used to call you that.”

“Then keep it for now. I don't want to look for another name.”

He agreed. The photograph remained in the notebook in the small room. He could have brought it over and described details, including some he was no longer certain of. Instead, he let for now remain where she had put it.

His lower back tightened. A fold of cloth was pressing into him. He had been sitting too straight toward the sound, even pushing down through his knee. Before he could wait for a convenient pause, a breath escaped.

“What happened?” Xiao Lu asked.

“The cloth behind me isn't flat.”

Yu Mei asked which side before getting up. He located the pressure lower down and asked her not to pull the whole pad away. Holding the wall, she rose, loosened the exposed edge, and waited while he leaned slightly away from his injured shoulder.

She flattened the fold. He stayed seated, feet still. The sharp pressure eased; the pain in his joints remained.

“That's enough.”

The channel had been quiet throughout. When he asked whether Xiao Lu had heard, she said she had been waiting because Yu Mei was asking him which side.

The embarrassment of being heard needing help suddenly had nowhere to settle.

“Better,” he said.

“Just the cloth, though?”

His mouth moved. “Yes. The rest will take longer.”

He did not straighten himself into the painful position again.

Yu Mei warned that they had been connected a while. The outside notice indicated a coming pause, though the voluntary end control was still available. Nothing promised an indefinite connection.

“We might lose sound for a bit,” he said. “I'll use the same reference to ask again.”

She did not say all right.

“Do you know it's only a bit? Or could it be gone?”

His throat hurt. “I don't know. There is an end control, and there's still a way to query again.”

“Don't close it yet.”

He waited. She asked him to say once more that the brake was on. He looked at the actual lever. Yu Mei braced where she would not strike his knee, tested the handle gently, and nodded.

“The brake is on.”

“All right. Now you can close it.”

Yu Mei ended the exchange. A faint inhalation stopped. From the small room came the sound of He Cen turning the bottle cap.

When the request control became available again, they queried the same reference. Historical preservation and current source aligned. No new face appeared.

For a long time the reply field remained empty.

He wondered whether letting her choose the ending had respected her wishes, or merely proved he could let go. His shoulder hurt more distinctly. He wanted to use that pain as a reason to make Yu Mei press again.

She waited.

At last, words appeared.

[Don't open sound yet. You said the chair's brake was on. Are you still in the same place?]

“Still here. Write that.”

Yu Mei sent it. After another pause, Xiao Lu asked: since this connection could carry words outside, could she go with them?

Lin Mo looked toward the seam beneath the gray door. His hand slowly opened on his lap.

“That's what I'm going to find out.”
