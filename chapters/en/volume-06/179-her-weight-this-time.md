# Chapter 179: Her Weight This Time

The place where Xiao Lu sat had lost a patch of dust.

Lin Mo had been watching the panel until she complained of cold legs. Only then did he notice her shadow obscuring the actual floor. Not a flat image thrown from the door: when she leaned left, fabric at her hip carried dust aside, uncovering cleaner concrete.

Source Not Released.

He'd promised to report only changes, so he didn't read it aloud.

Her hand rested on her knee, as if guarding against another attempt to straighten it. Color slowly returned to her knuckles, though a gray line still showed through her sleeve's end. Remembering the white mark on Fang Ji's shoe, Lin Mo couldn't decide whether the whiteness before him belonged to light or her clothing.

“Can you see your hand clearly?”

“Yes. Don't make me count fingers.”

“I won't.”

She looked up, expecting another requirement. He asked nothing else. Her thumb rubbed dirt from her knee. That tiny movement meant more than the panel's complete figure, without becoming a new test of who she was.

The gray connections contracted.

Her torso pulled away from the wall. The hand slid from her knee, fingers clutching empty air. Yu Mei moved forward. Lin Mo called stop, regretting it immediately. She was still some distance away, only trying to see which way Xiao Lu might fall.

“I'm not pulling her.” Her voice was hard.

“I know. Sorry.”

He gripped the part of his chair he could reach and waited for the tug to ease. Fabric across Xiao Lu's solidifying back stretched over something unseen. Rather than reaching toward them, she tucked in her elbow, keeping her palm away from the slot beneath the door.

“Still dragging from that side?”

“I don't know. Just then.”

The fine sound inside the arm broke off, then resumed. Fang Ji moved close enough to read and reported continuity still active. He didn't invent an unseen malfunction. The seatkeeper watched the empty long frame, palm against his injured leg, no longer offering his body as padding.

Lin Mo touched his sore cheek with his tongue. He knew a movement that should help: support her shoulder as her balance shifted, stop her falling sideways. But half that shoulder remained gray. He didn't know what his hand would meet.

“Don't catch her shoulder yet. Is that side on the floor all solid?”

Yu Mei looked along Xiao Lu's body.

“Weight on the wall side. Can't tell about the other.”

“That's how it feels,” Xiao Lu said.

They paused. She could finally say more than that she couldn't locate herself. Which part felt cold, which ached, which touched nothing: she could identify them. What they saw wasn't necessarily more useful than what she knew.

Lin Mo let her decide whether to lean farther toward the wall. She refused. Her waist hurt; she wanted to wait. He nodded, and darkness briefly crossed his vision. Without calling to her, he watched a bright speck on his wheel until it stopped being two specks.

Yu Mei noticed. “Are you still clearheaded?”

“Yes. Don't make me lift my head quickly.”

“Nobody is.”

He almost laughed, but couldn't. Everybody kept explaining what they hadn't asked others to do. Their bodies had learned to brace before a request, discovering afterward how little strength remained.

A clean female voice sounded inside the door.

“Reception complete. Remove the received subject.”

It was clearer than anything the people around him could manage. Xiao Lu raised her head, withdrawing her hand from her knee. Fang Ji shifted a foot.

Lin Mo checked the panel. Source Not Released remained. The gray lines still connected her.

“Don't move yet.”

Quietly this time, to everyone, himself included. He didn't know which sound seam carried that announcement. Clarity wasn't verification. The arm still pulled; her clothes were still taut. Dragging her away would only make them pull against the other end.

The voice repeated itself, adding a brief chime.

Xiao Lu pressed her hand against the floor, deathly pale. “I didn't say I could move.”

“We heard,” Yu Mei said.

Fang Ji returned his foot to its place, cursing his own near mistake. The seatkeeper turned his back to block a slanting strip of light from the door, touching neither metal nor panel. The announcement persisted, slightly muffled.

“Better?”

“Still there. Not as sharp.”

He held the position until his leg made him shift. Light slipped through again. Nobody demanded he remain rigid until this ended.

Xiao Lu pressed her fingers to the ground and asked whether they still had the notebook.

Lin Mo knew which notebook. He looked at his lap, then beside the chair. Nothing. Cold panic arrived first, as though he'd lost the most important thing. Before he could ask to roll backward and look, Yu Mei said it was in the small room, somewhere dry, not beside the door.

“You said not to leave it here for the light,” she reminded him.

He nodded, trying and failing to catch the moment he'd decided that. Instead he asked exactly where they'd put it.

“Inward on the concrete ledge. Closed. Photograph inside, copied pages at the back.”

Fang Ji added that reaching it required the short corridor. Not underfoot; no need to search. He spoke ordinarily, without treating Lin Mo's panic as something everyone must cautiously avoid. Lin Mo tried to receive it in that ordinary spirit.

Xiao Lu's fingers relaxed. “Don't fetch it now.”

“We won't.”

“I don't want to read it. I just—”

A tug moved her shoulder. She swallowed the unfinished words. He waited without supplying them himself. Once they could leave this place, he told her, they would return to the small room. They hadn't thrown anything away.

The voice demanded removal a third time. He Cen coughed with the chime, then covered his mouth. Xiao Lu told him not to suppress it. A cough from someone she could see was easier to place than those repeated commands.

He lowered his hand and finished coughing. He hadn't done it to help her find her bearings, he said. She knew; nobody needed to cough louder on purpose for her.

Listening, Lin Mo slowly unclenched his teeth. They waited through a process they couldn't hurry, but at least they weren't turning every breath and pain into its required performance. The seatkeeper shifted a little, finding a less painful angle that blocked the glare without obstructing Fang Ji's view.

The real change made no sound.

A gray connection beside Xiao Lu's waist slackened first, sinking along her clothes. A fold collapsed against the floor, not an image disappearing but fabric finally following her body. Then the gray surface between her feet narrowed, exposing concrete. She leaned backward. This time her back struck the wall.

The impact was slight but made her hunch in pain.

Yu Mei lifted a hand instinctively, stopped, and asked to touch her upper arm. Xiao Lu took two breaths and nodded. Which side? She indicated the outer one with her chin.

No fine lines crossed its fabric now. Yu Mei rested her palm there, steadying without dragging or lifting. Lin Mo saw the fabric settle against Xiao Lu's arm beneath Yu Mei's palm. An ordinary contact, and here he hardly dared blink.

“Solid,” Yu Mei said.

“Obviously,” Xiao Lu whispered, immediately followed by, “Don't let go.”

Yu Mei didn't.

The panel's words finally changed.

Lin Mo read them once without speaking. Fang Ji leaned closer to check and read aloud: Source Released.

Reception Complete illuminated below. Sustaining Continuity Active remained elsewhere, not extinguished by their change.

Lin Mo told Xiao Lu the first two results, then the third. One thing ending didn't mean its entire cost had ended. Supported by Yu Mei's hand, she looked at the arm, lips moving.

“Don't go over there.”

“I won't.”

This time her voice came from the body on the floor, close to the wall, with the broken breath in her chest. The arm didn't send out a second ending to the same sentence. The clean announcement continued farther inside, apparently unconcerned which end she had left.

Lin Mo could finally hear the two sounds separately.

Xiao Lu tried drawing her knee closer. Her heel scraped concrete. She frowned in pain, moving slowly, but no line pulled that little distance back. Yu Mei adjusted her palm without using the opportunity to haul her upright.

He Cen asked whether they could check for injuries. Xiao Lu begged them not to crowd her; she couldn't breathe. He stayed where he was and turned his watching face slightly away.

Lin Mo withdrew his gaze too.

He couldn't leave his wheelchair. Withdrawing meant only looking from her face to the floor beside her feet. Two new, uneven drag marks joined the earlier dust prints. He wanted to remember them firmly, uncertain how much that word still meant for him.

“Lin Mo.”

She used his name, not brother. He answered.

“I'm not missing a hand. I haven't become two people.”

Looking down at herself, she spoke slowly. “But I can't tell you everything yet. Don't immediately ask whether I'm all right.”

“I won't.” His throat tightened.

“And don't let that thing answer for me.”

She still meant the door. He agreed, unable to keep his voice steady. Hearing it, she glanced up and seemed about to move closer. Yu Mei reminded her not to stand yet. She stopped, fingers scraping the concrete, without insisting the chair come to her instead.

That suddenly made him terribly sad.

He knew she was his sister, why he'd come, what he'd paid. The knowledge resembled neatly arranged possessions, each present, none leading to a particular afternoon in the past. He'd expected rescue to supply a natural movement: her familiar form of address, the hand she recognized, offered without thought.

Instead he sat with a splinted wrist and a painfully dry throat, able only to ask whether she wanted water.

She did.

At last, an answer gave him something practical. He asked the seatkeeper to pass Yu Mei the bottle, then noticed her hand supporting Xiao Lu and corrected himself: Fang Ji should take its base first. One person couldn't perform two tasks requiring good shoulders.

Fang Ji sat between them and accepted the bottle in his left hand. He waited to open it until Xiao Lu could lean slightly against the wall and Yu Mei moved her support behind her back.

The handover proceeded slowly as a ceremony. Really, nobody could afford another fall.

Source Released remained in the gray light. Lin Mo looked once more without touching it. He didn't know what the sustaining blank experienced now. One living person outside didn't authorize him to invent a peaceful ending for what remained within.

Beside the wall, Xiao Lu nudged Fang Ji's shoe with her own grounded foot, asking for the bottle to come closer.

This time, no image had chosen her position.

He moved his shoe out of her way and held the bottle within reach. No at last, no hurry. Lin Mo watched that small space open between them, for once without fearing a gray connection would fill it again.
