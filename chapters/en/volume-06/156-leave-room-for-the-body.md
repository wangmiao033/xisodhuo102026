# Chapter 156: Leave Room for the Body

“The body first,” Lin Mo said.

Yu Mei followed the retention details downward. A little diagram showed someone lying flat, with a line entering the gray door. No face, nothing beneath the body. After a moment Lin Mo realized he had already imagined his wheelchair in that empty space.

He asked her to open the text instead.

[The body does not enter with the continuity]

[External maintenance remains an external responsibility]

“Maintain what?” Xiao Lu asked.

The notes mentioned breathing, posture, and external injuries. There was no monitoring connection, no place where an injured body would be safely kept. Yu Mei turned the page, found an empty field for arrangements outside, and went back.

“They don't even provide a bed.”

Lin Mo looked down. The armrests were narrow. His wrist support occupied part of one; the cloth behind his back had only recently been flattened. Sitting here awake required adjustments. If someone could no longer say where pressure hurt, another person would have to remember.

“Would they know if someone outside moved them?” Xiao Lu asked.

“It doesn't say.”

“Then don't just ask whether they'd stay alive.”

A faint sound followed. He waited, but she said nothing more. Alive had been precisely what he meant to ask. It might have seemed enough to proceed. He hadn't considered how someone would communicate thirst, cold, or a fold of clothing trapped behind their back.

Yu Mei left the page at Not Executed. She looked at his left foot.

“Is the shoe still rubbing?”

“Feels hotter.”

“Why didn't you say?”

He almost said he could bear it. His sore cheek pulled. “I was waiting for you to finish reading.”

She couldn't crouch comfortably. She bent just enough to ease the shoe's back edge away. The red skin was unbroken, though the pressure mark had deepened. Without removing the shoe, she flattened the existing cloth edge clear of his skin.

“That's enough.”

Xiao Lu asked whether they were already taking care of the person who would stay outside.

“Only my foot. Nobody has agreed to retention.”

“I know. I heard her ask. She waited for you to answer before she moved anything.”

He felt uncomfortable. He had learned to report pain briefly, like a minor fault that shouldn't hold anyone up. What Xiao Lu heard was someone waiting for permission to touch him.

A bottle cap struck the floor down the passage.

Fang Ji called He Cen's name, then Yu Mei's. Turning, Lin Mo could see only his raised left arm at the corner. The hand had found nowhere safe to support the weight.

“Close this. Back.” He addressed the port immediately. “Someone outside can't sit up. We need to stop.”

“Go,” Xiao Lu said.

Yu Mei ended the exchange, checked the darkened line, and reached the handles. Her first push met the brakes. She stopped, released them, then backed the chair away. The abrupt movement pulled through Lin Mo's knee. He kept his feet still.

He Cen's head had slipped toward the corner. His glasses hung crookedly. He opened his eyes at their approach.

“Don't pull my shoulder.”

Fang Ji released his collar. He had tried to stop the slide without using his wounded right palm; now his left shoulder had drained the color from his face. Beside him, the seatkeeper held clothing against He Cen's side. His injured leg prevented him from bracing the man's whole weight.

The fifth bottle remained upright on the ledge. Only the cap had fallen.

Yu Mei secured Lin Mo's brakes before going over. He warned her against lifting under the arms. He Cen said he could move inward himself, if they waited.

They waited. He set his feet little by little. With Yu Mei supporting his back at the side, he edged along the ledge until he could lean securely.

Such a small movement. Everyone was sweating.

He had leaned down for the cap, He Cen explained. Suddenly his strength went. Hearing Fang Ji had made him realize he was slipping. He did not call it sleep or announce that he was better. Asked whether his vision was still dark, he shook his head, then requested that they not make him drink immediately.

“Sit steady first.”

Lin Mo gave the episode no diagnosis. They had no thermometer, no medicine. He Cen's forehead was wet and still hot to the touch. That was enough to make them stop something else.

Fang Ji retrieved the cap. Dust clung inside. He swore quietly and set it down instead of replacing it. Yu Mei considered rinsing it. Lin Mo stopped her, then hesitated himself.

There was no perfectly clean answer. She wiped away visible grit with a cleaner inner edge of her clothes, keeping it off the bottle's mouth, and laid the cap loosely over it. When He Cen had recovered, he took a small drink without touching the cap. Nobody called the arrangement sanitary.

“I wasn't trying to pick up the water,” he said.

“We know. The cap rolled.”

“I thought you might think I'd done it to bring you back.”

His mouth was tight, his glasses still crooked. The room beyond could stretch a filmed hand into endless appeals for help. Here, a man who actually needed assistance was explaining himself before anyone accused him of being a burden.

“If you call, we come back,” Lin Mo said. “You don't have to prove it's worth it first.”

He Cen adjusted his glasses without answering. Yu Mei sat beside him instead of looking toward Lin Mo for the next departure.

“I couldn't hold him,” the seatkeeper said.

“Neither could I.” Fang Ji's tone was hard, but no accusation followed.

For the first time Lin Mo seriously considered what happened if the person in his wheelchair wasn't awake when Yu Mei had to help He Cen.

He had imagined care as a name. Yu Mei, or somebody else remaining outside. In this room it became two bodies slipping at once, an arm that couldn't lift, a doorway that still had to stay clear. Writing responsible beside a name would not strengthen a shoulder.

“The body stays outside,” he told them. “The interface doesn't care for it. It hasn't promised the retained person can still speak through it.”

“Now do you understand her question?” Yu Mei asked.

He nodded.

Fang Ji moved the bottle bundle within He Cen's reach so he wouldn't bend again. Four large empties, two old small ones. Lin Mo considered asking them to open one out as a cushion, then pictured a hard crease pressing into a back. Fang Ji left the bundle against the wall.

“Could we secure somebody to the chair with a rope?” Fang Ji asked.

Lin Mo's chest tightened. Straps had closed while he was still capable of answering, buckle after buckle. Knowing Fang Ji meant preventing a fall did not stop him drawing his hand toward his thigh.

Yu Mei noticed.

“If you don't want that, I won't look for anything.”

“We don't have anything suitable anyway,” he said when he could breathe. “And someone would still have to check the pressure. Keeping a body still doesn't make it safe.”

Fang Ji said he wouldn't write restraint into their notes. He hadn't expected the question to frighten him. He stopped there, without demanding the history behind it.

He Cen asked them to call his name before moving him, even if he looked asleep. Being pulled up suddenly made everything darker. Yu Mei agreed. She called him now, waited for him to look at her, then eased a rolled edge of his coat from beneath his elbow.

It didn't hurt. He watched her hand until it withdrew.

The diagram had shown only lying down and entering a line. Everything between had been omitted. Here, moving an inch of cloth involved whose name to call, which reply to wait for, where to reach. Lin Mo remembered without having anyone put their personal requests on the sheet that went into the reader.

Eventually He Cen asked for room to lean his own way. Yu Mei moved aside while staying where she could see him. Returning to his usual place, the seatkeeper stopped twice because of his leg. No one asked him to turn one useful act into another effort.

Lin Mo waited until He Cen could finish a sentence, then asked whether he wanted them to remain for now.

“Yes. Don't go yet.”

“All right.”

He added no promise to be only a moment. He gave Fang Ji the query sheet to place outside the notebook and closed his eyes. The cloth pressed against his back, smooth now but still distinct. He shifted, stopped at the pain in his knee, and asked Yu Mei to check the footrest.

He could describe every movement. Someone could answer.

What if he couldn't?

He opened his eyes. Yu Mei was slowly rubbing her injured shoulder with her other hand. Instead of asking her to plan how she might watch over him, he let her have a silence that required no reply.

Later He Cen asked whether they had kept his sister waiting.

“I told her we were stopping.”

“Can she wait?”

“I don't know how waiting feels to her. I'll ask when we return.”

He Cen nodded. Lin Mo studied the old cement marks on the wall. The person who couldn't locate her own feet had asked about the body outside before he had thought of the red patch on his heel.

They left only when He Cen said a short visit would be all right and Yu Mei's shoulder had rested. Fang Ji sat at the shelter entrance, able to watch He Cen without standing between both rooms. The water stayed behind. The phone remained put away, its last known nine percent no invitation to spend it.

Reconnected, Lin Mo asked about the bottles. Xiao Lu answered, then asked after the person outside.

“Talking for himself. Sitting securely. Still feverish.”

“Then don't all stand here.”

“Someone's with him.”

He explained that the interface offered no guarantee of care. Its empty arrangements field wouldn't bring water or notice a rolled collar. He did not relay He Cen's embarrassment or every detail of the slump. They had returned to meet an actual need.

“Whose name is in that field now?” she asked.

“Still empty.”

“I meant outside.”

He looked at Yu Mei. She met his eyes, plainly exhausted.

“We haven't committed anyone outside either.”

He had her return to the view where neither end had been established. The diagram's body still lay above blank space. This time he didn't immediately put his wheelchair there. He asked her to close the picture and retain only the conditions they had read.

Something clicked softly inside the metal arm.

“Are you still there?” Xiao Lu asked.

“Yes.”

She did not answer. The connection line flickered once, then again, as though something were working loose in a very narrow place.
