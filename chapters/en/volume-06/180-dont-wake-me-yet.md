# Chapter 180: Don't Wake Me Yet

Xiao Lu hadn't been given a full bottle of water.

As Fang Ji removed the cap, the transparent remnant slid to one side, so thin it seemed a tighter grip might make it vanish. He didn't put the bottle in her shaking hands. He held its opening within reach and waited for her permission.

She took a small mouthful and immediately coughed.

Yu Mei supported her back without pounding it. Xiao Lu bent her head, eyes reddening. Water escaped her lips and dampened her collar. Watching the little wet patch, Lin Mo found the word waste suddenly cruel. They were all thirsty. Scarcity couldn't require someone newly arrived to swallow without losing a drop.

Fang Ji withdrew the bottle and waited.

“Slowly.” Then, “Not hurrying you.”

She barely had the strength to glare, only nodded. The second time she wet her lips before swallowing. Lin Mo didn't unclench his teeth until he heard it.

She stopped before finishing, wanting to leave some for everyone else.

The seatkeeper quietly said there wasn't enough to divide. He sounded afraid admitting it constituted another wrongdoing. Xiao Lu studied the bottom for a long time. Yu Mei finally asked Fang Ji to pass it to He Cen. The feverish man moistened his mouth and inspected the remainder as he drew away.

“Can't even tell how much.”

Lin Mo declined for now. His tongue tightened as if disagreeing; the sore inside his cheek scraped a tooth. He didn't pretend he wasn't thirsty. He hadn't been choking, he said. He could wait a little longer.

Xiao Lu looked at him without demanding he drink to prove something.

The last water reached Yu Mei. She tilted the bottle slowly, checking its bottom before swallowing the final mouthful. When the cap returned, nothing ran down the plastic.

The fifth bottle was empty.

Fang Ji crushed it. Xiao Lu flinched. He stopped, asking whether to continue. Yes. Sudden nearby noises were unfamiliar; she wasn't banning all sound.

The bottle went into the light bag: three large empties became four, two old small bottles beside them. Another large one remained trapped below the door, a corner of its base showing. Nobody retrieved it to complete the count.

Lin Mo counted silently, forgetting by the fourth why he'd begun.

“Don't fetch the one under the door.” That much he said.

Fang Ji agreed and closed the bag.

Xiao Lu's feet remained by the wall. She tried tiny ankle movements, pausing between them. Could she sit unsupported? She nodded, but when Yu Mei withdrew her palm, she listed sideways.

Yu Mei supported her again without rebuking the answer.

“I thought I could.”

“Then sit a little longer.”

“Your shoulder hurts.”

“Yes. So don't suddenly stand and make me catch you.”

Xiao Lu stayed still. Gradually she arranged her legs to balance herself, palm on the ground, and asked Yu Mei to try again. This time she remained upright. Yu Mei put her hand on her knee and slowly flexed it, as though reclaiming something lent out too long.

Fang Ji asked to see Xiao Lu's palms for scrapes from the wall. Dust covered both; shallow marks crossed her fingertips, but no blood emerged. He didn't wipe her with his own dressing. His injured palm was already dirty. Someone else's smaller hand didn't make that dressing clean.

Xiao Lu told him to stop clustering around her and check Lin Mo.

He turned. His expression was less certain than when reading the panel, as though no label could establish whether this person had completed anything.

“I haven't fallen,” Lin Mo said.

“I know. Checking you're not sliding.”

He pointed at the seat edge. Half Lin Mo's left palm hung unsupported, fingers barely touching. Unable to remember releasing his grip, he repositioned it without pulling with his injured index finger. The small movement brought cold sweat.

Yu Mei told him to lean fully back. She didn't take his shoulder, only checked his clothes from behind for anything caught and pulling him forward. Watching from the wall, Xiao Lu suddenly asked whether she'd taken too long to come out.

“No.”

He'd answered so quickly it almost dictated what she should think. After a pause, he explained differently. His injuries, thirst, and what he'd just undergone were already there. Arriving faster couldn't erase them. He spoke briefly, pausing for breath, without turning those things into debts she must repay.

She looked aside, blinking tears away, and didn't thank him. He didn't wait for thanks. When she looked back, his hand rested securely; she could check he wasn't sliding again.

The seatkeeper touched the bag, then asked whether to bind the empties tighter. Fang Ji said not yet; how they carried it might change. They discussed an ordinary bag. The practical exchange helped Lin Mo slow his breathing.

A louder scrape came beneath the door. Xiao Lu's toes withdrew until they touched the wall. She didn't jump up. Yu Mei checked the bottle remained trapped and told her nothing new had fallen out. She nodded, hand on her knee, without asking anyone to look inside for her.

Watching those small attempts at balance, Lin Mo realized he remembered the first and second differently. That recent interval hadn't vanished. He wasn't losing each sentence as the next arrived. The past connections between people had become unreachable, and sometimes even recent arrangements needed prompting.

He didn't rush to diagnose himself. When Yu Mei asked about dizziness, he admitted it and said he was waiting for it to ease.

“Not everything eases because you wait.”

“I know.”

Her expression doubted him. He almost explained that his name and age remained clear. But being able to say twenty-five didn't mean his body could manage another twenty-five minutes. Instead he warned her not to pull his right hand if his head dropped.

“Never intended to.”

Hoarse, not angry. That reassured him more.

The female voice inside the door finally changed its announcement. He couldn't make out the words, only a long trailing sound toward the chamber. Fang Ji asked whether to retreat. Looking at Xiao Lu's unsteady body, Lin Mo said not to move everybody at once. Wait until she could shift, then handle one thing at a time.

She said it wasn't that she didn't want to leave.

“I know. Nobody's hurrying you.”

“You sound like you are.”

After a pause, he offered to talk less.

Her mouth moved faintly, then settled. Not a familiar expression returning from their past. A tired person accepting this particular remark. He feared he might search every movement of hers for memory until she became another image required to adopt the correct pose.

He looked at the ground instead, giving her somewhere not to answer.

The seatkeeper suggested moving the bag toward the short corridor before the wheels turned onto it. Fang Ji would carry it himself; no need to pass it among everyone. Irritation remained in both voices. Xiao Lu reaching the floor hadn't made them selfless, tireless people.

He Cen asked whether he could sleep in the small room.

“Get back first,” Yu Mei said. “Not here.”

He adjusted his glasses and straightened, then soon folded under his own weight. His gray collar sagged. He hadn't become ill for Lin Mo's sake, and shouldn't continually be kept waiting for him. Before Lin Mo could suggest arranging He Cen's departure first, Fang Ji volunteered to watch his footing, without pulling his shoulder or letting him use the projected rail.

The projected rail.

Lin Mo knew it was false, unsafe to hold, but couldn't find the moment he'd seen it. Where was it? Fang Ji glanced at Yu Mei before explaining: on the curtain route back, not beside this door. Nothing to worry about yet.

“Tell me when we get there.”

Fang Ji nodded. Nobody required him to remember the whole journey now.

Xiao Lu picked at dried dust on her palm, then abandoned it. Was there a mirror in the small room? Yu Mei hadn't seen a usable one. Xiao Lu didn't pursue it.

Lin Mo noticed the metal arm behind her.

Its darkened surface reflected part of his face: delicate features, lips drained pale by thirst. It didn't resemble the adult man he knew himself to be. It was his because when he moved, it moved. More direct than any reassurance, but directness didn't make unfamiliarity easier.

He raised his left hand slightly and lowered it. The reflected hand followed.

“Don't reach over,” Xiao Lu said.

“I'm not.”

Now he was using the same explanation everybody had repeated. Xiao Lu looked at him and swallowed something unsaid. Did she recognize this face? After a silence, she said she recognized him.

“I meant the face.”

Her eyes reddened. “I know.”

He didn't press. She had only just escaped. Filling every unfamiliar space in him couldn't immediately become her responsibility. Looking away from the metal, he faced his actual sister on the ground and spoke her name.

She answered.

“I know who you are.”

“Mm.”

“But I—”

He stopped. No adequate words described the space between knowing and remembering. He wouldn't make it sound as if she'd been insufficiently rescued, or pretend everything would return in another second.

Palm on her knee, she slowly nodded. “You don't have to finish that yet.”

He lowered his eyes. His left hand remained on the armrest, fingers numb, his entire arm seeming farther from the chair. Yu Mei moved behind him and proposed turning him slightly away from the door. Before the wheels moved, he shook his head.

“Wait.”

Not reluctance to leave. He suddenly couldn't distinguish the little lurch between left and right and feared sliding out when the chair moved.

Yu Mei checked the brakes and bent to inspect his face. He tried lifting his head to reassure her. His neck was heavier than expected; only his lips moved. She called his name. He heard, knew he should respond.

Xiao Lu called him brother too.

He wanted to tell her not to stand. She could call from against the wall; he could hear. But something thick seemed to separate his lips, sending even those simple words far away. He blinked once, unsure whether she could take it as an answer.

Brother restored none of the lost afternoons. It did make him want to look at her again. She divided into two images, slowly rejoined, and then the wall's edge softened too.

“Don't—”

He didn't know whether he'd made a sound. He wanted to say don't wake me yet, then thought no, he couldn't sleep here. Hadn't He Cen just been told that? The two sentences crowded together. Neither reached its end.

Yu Mei's sleeve brushed his cheek, its rough tear flashing past. The chair back remained. Nobody pulled his right hand. Support hadn't suddenly disappeared beneath his feet.

Those small facts scattered.

The last sound wasn't the door declaring completion. Xiao Lu called once more.

He couldn't answer in time.
