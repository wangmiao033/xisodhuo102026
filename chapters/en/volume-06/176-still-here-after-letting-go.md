# Chapter 176: Still Here After Letting Go

The surge passed. The warmth in Lin Mo's palm remained.

He felt as though he had tightened his hold. His real left hand lay open on his leg, the injured finger intermittently numb, but a smaller hand still clung to the other one. No face, no place. Only that hesitant weight. He wanted to let its owner find their balance before they moved.

The outline beside the door stopped advancing.

Xiao Lu didn't hurry him. Her head was lowered, gray lines dividing her face as though she were examining something too close to understand. She said something was wrong underfoot. Yu Mei immediately looked down, stopping her shoe short of the raised metal.

“Under my feet. In here.”

Yu Mei looked up again. Sharing a word hadn't placed them on the same floor.

“Is it the hand I'm holding on to?” Lin Mo asked.

“I don't know what hand you mean.”

He shut his eyes briefly. He had nearly assigned an unverified memory to her. Then he could ask her to let go, as though her reluctance were keeping her inside. She hadn't said she was holding anyone. He was the one holding that warmth, as far as he could feel.

“It's on my side. Don't look for it.”

Clearing continued below the narrow panel. The last association had no name beside it, no separate box offering to save it. The chosen range hadn't changed. Keeping this part would require pausing further clearing; he couldn't ask it to continue while declaring the final piece exempt.

He looked at Yu Mei and asked her to pause.

She pressed the control and withdrew. Further Clearing Paused lit up. Source Not Released remained below it. Nobody relaxed. He didn't expect this pause to have more power than the previous one. He needed a moment in which the speed of loss didn't dictate his answers.

“I don't quite want to let go.”

Fang Ji sat beside the chair, his injured palm resting on his clothes. He turned his head without asking whether Lin Mo regretted it.

“Then don't give it a name yet.”

Lin Mo looked at him.

“Call it her as a child, and suddenly it sounds as if she has to come back and pull you out.”

It wasn't gentle. He couldn't find a denial. He wanted Xiao Lu to say it was her, that she remembered. He wanted her recollection to give the vanishing hand somewhere outside him to stay.

A faint scrape came through the port.

“My hand isn't in yours now,” she said.

“I know.”

“You looked at me again.”

Her view was limited, but she had seen that glance. He lowered his eyes, wanting to explain that he was afraid, not blaming her. She drew breath.

“You can be afraid. Don't give me that hand to carry as well.”

He agreed without asking her to believe him immediately.

His real hand was empty. Fine dust lay in its creases, and a red band marked the injured finger near the joint. He moved his thumb until the cloth stopped pulling there. Doing something small for this hand was easier than deciding what the other must lose. It still took effort.

The bottle beneath the door screamed.

Plastic stretched against metal; the sound sprang out of the narrow gap. The seatkeeper recoiled, knocked his knee against the wall, and began breathing harder. When Lin Mo looked up, the man was staring at a small patch of light on the door.

Someone sat high inside it.

Lin Mo could distinguish a chair back and some red. He didn't recognize the occupant. The seatkeeper's face flushed, then drained. He turned away, but his right hand crept forward along the ground.

“What are you reaching for?”

“Nothing.”

“Your hand's past your shoe.”

The man looked down as though discovering it there. He returned it to his knee without brushing away the dust. Faint writing rose beside the high chair. Lin Mo could make out Return to Your Seat, nothing else.

“It says I can sit again.”

Nobody asked where. The words had brought something old among them. Although Lin Mo had lost most of his lived acquaintance with this man, Yu Mei's tight mouth told him this wasn't an ordinary chair.

“Do you want to go?” Fang Ji asked.

After a long time, the seatkeeper shook his head.

“I want my leg to stop hurting.”

Lin Mo studied the leg that couldn't fold any closer. Wanting relief hadn't automatically become an intention to go. The hand had nevertheless moved. He wouldn't merge those facts into innocence, or demand continued pain as proof of change.

“What's it actually given you?”

“An image.”

“And here?”

The man touched the dust on his knee. “The ground's too hard.”

Fang Ji moved the light bag away from his feet, clearing a flatter stretch against the wall. He didn't wedge bottle necks beneath an injured leg. The seatkeeper shifted himself over, stopping twice. When his back reached the wall, some of the strain left his breathing. Nobody announced that his old debts had thereby been paid.

The high chair vanished.

Lin Mo felt the remembered warmth again. All the intervening noise seemed to have happened outside it. Exhaustion came suddenly, beyond the kind another careful thought could settle. He wanted someone to choose for him and take responsibility for every night that followed a wrong choice.

Yu Mei asked whether he wanted to return to the room.

He shook his head, then specified that the chair should stay where it was. That decided the wheels, not further clearing. She left the control untouched.

“The voices outside are getting farther away,” Xiao Lu said.

Her half-visible lips still moved. Yu Mei said she heard no change.

“Not yours. The others. Farther off. It was like several people talking for me through a wall. Now I only hear the ends.”

“What do they say?”

“That I've already forgiven you.”

His chest tightened. The panel hadn't declared delivery complete, yet those voices had arranged her first words after arrival. He couldn't tell whether this belonged to the smiling image or map the Theater from the distances she described.

“You didn't say that.”

“I know I didn't.”

He fell silent. She wasn't asking permission to know her own speech. She was telling him someone was still using her voice. Yu Mei shook her head and drew her hand away from the controls. They had the original channel and the clearing controls, not a button that could silence every stolen voice.

He remembered the closed camera marker and refused to offer it as comfort. It established only that camera's state.

“I can't turn them off right now.”

“I didn't ask you to look for them.” Her breathing thinned. “I wanted to tell somebody I hadn't said it.”

He had been about to ask whether she could endure a little longer. Instead he said he'd heard her. He didn't turn her sentence into another fault awaiting repair. Fang Ji didn't record the whole conversation. Plastic continued scraping beneath the door.

The half-visible face angled away. Lin Mo couldn't see where she looked. For once he didn't immediately want her to turn back and reassure him. She remained within that continuity; she could also spend a moment not watching her brother lose his past.

“Could you stop looking at me?” he asked the others.

Fang Ji turned toward the corridor. Yu Mei stayed where she could notice him slipping, but stopped studying his mouth. He Cen seemed asleep, a muffled cough breaking his breathing. Lin Mo didn't require everyone to close their eyes. He certainly couldn't drive the watchers out of the gray door.

He only wanted to hesitate without finding several faces waiting for an answer.

“I can't see all of you,” Xiao Lu said.

“I'm not asking you to close anything. Look where you want.”

The outline moved slightly. He watched the unlit ground below the door, trying not to chase the small hand's origin. No final childhood scene appeared. No perfect farewell arrived in time.

The less he searched, the smaller it seemed. Too small to uphold a correct decision, still too precious to relinquish easily.

He remembered his question to the seatkeeper. What was here?

An injured wrist. A wheelchair. Someone who said her present hand wasn't in his. She hadn't promised to like him afterward, called waiting forgiveness, or accepted the last memory he wanted to place in her keeping.

He still wanted her out.

Without years of familiarity supporting it, the wish felt thin. He tried to let it remain thin. He wouldn't strengthen it with parental instructions he could no longer recall or an invented brother who had always known what was right.

Yu Mei turned when he raised his eyes.

“Continue the original range.”

“You wanted to keep that piece.”

“I still do. I don't have another way to keep it and bring her here. Wanting one doesn't mean I have one.”

She waited for him to finish. He didn't hurry her. Her fingers needed stretching; her shoulder needed the wall. Finally she asked whether he meant resuming only the already selected associations. He said yes.

The pause indicator went dark.

The warmth gripped him sharply.

He opened his mouth, but no name came. He no longer knew whose name to call. Only a barely audible wait escaped. Yu Mei heard it and reached to pause again.

“Not the control,” he gasped. “It hurts. Don't change it yet.”

Her hand stopped, then returned to her leg. He watched the whole movement. Someone was still listening to him now, rather than only to his earlier decision.

He didn't force the remembered hand to open. His real hand didn't reach to seize it.

Between those two things not done, the warmth faded. Size went first. He couldn't tell how much smaller it had been. Then he lost which two fingers it had held. Finally there remained only the knowledge that a moment ago he had been grieving.

He lowered his head and saw his left hand.

Yu Mei asked if it still hurt. He said his finger did, then realized that might not have been her question. He looked toward the port, searching for a word to call the lost thing back.

“That hand just now…”

Xiao Lu didn't finish for him.

A long, low sound came from within. Gray withdrew from the outline's edges into a single line, as though loosened behind it. No light shot upward. Nobody applauded. He heard He Cen wake and ask who had touched a bottle cap. Fang Ji said nobody had; the door was moving.

Selected Associations Cleared.

Sustaining Continuity Active.

Source Not Released remained beneath the new text.

Lin Mo recognized every word. Recognition couldn't show him what they had taken. Xiao Lu called him by name. He looked at the half-gray face lifting toward him.

“I'm still on this side.”

“I see you.”

His answer came slowly. His voice sounded unfamiliar, but he couldn't say whose it ought to resemble. She wasn't crying for him. He didn't ask her to. Yu Mei rested her hand near the chair back and said she was checking whether he had slipped.

He told her he hadn't.

This time, he was indeed still where he had been.
