# Chapter 159: Who Stays After Delivery?

Yu Mei offered the water. Lin Mo did not drink at once.

The remaining water in the fifth bottle rocked against faint marks on the plastic. He meant to save it for He Cen, but He Cen was already watching him.

“I just had some.”

Lin Mo accepted a little, avoiding the sore place inside his cheek.

“Stop handing every mouthful back to somebody else.”

After so many corrections, his first feeling was irritation. Embarrassment followed. Refusing water was easier than admitting thirst, as though each refusal reduced what he owed. Yu Mei still had to hold the bottle while he decided.

He drank and asked her to put it back.

The seatkeeper studied a line on their sheet. Could someone enter, wait for the sister to finish leaving, then come out?

“That's next,” Lin Mo said.

“Some old scenes released your seat when the show ended.”

“Your old seat,” Fang Ji said.

The man acknowledged the distinction. Lin Mo didn't let them argue over whose experience mattered more. When Xiao Lu had asked whether she would remember a sentence after they took it, they could copy one and ask her afterward. Retention offered no equally light experiment. Testing could not quietly come to mean putting a person through it first.

Yu Mei opened her stiff fingers. She could go once more, she said. After checking withdrawal, she needed to lie down. She indicated a dry stretch of floor where she could rest her shoulder instead of holding herself upright.

Lin Mo agreed. Fang Ji moved sharp debris with his shoe rather than his injured hand. The seatkeeper shifted the bundle, clearing room for her legs. They had not turned the shelter into a clean ward, only made somewhere she could rest.

At the gray door, Xiao Lu asked whether they had found a volunteer.

“No. We're checking what happens after delivery.”

She wanted to know too. If the answer said the person couldn't stop, would he omit it?

“I'll read it.”

“Even if it's ugly.”

“Yes.”

The first item under Exit Conditions was not a prohibition. A bright Cancel arrow appeared beside the export frame. Relief stirred in him before he asked which stage it applied to.

[May be canceled before establishment]

The arrow pointed toward the two unestablished ends. No retention, no export begun. Farther down, the same arrow became a little bent line marked Separate Verification After Handover.

Yu Mei swore under her breath.

Her hand had moved toward Cancel, as though finding the word meant finding a door handle for whoever might enter. It now hung between two entirely different moments. He had her stop at the boundary and read both conditions to Xiao Lu.

Could the arrow be moved to the later stage?

She meant it seriously. He checked seriously. It was a stage marker, with no draggable edge. Opening its note left the view read-only. There was no control for rearranging the sequence.

“We can't change it here.”

“Keep looking.”

They selected an empty model, entering no source. Its retention frame lit first. Only then did the complete-export frame begin moving outward. Halfway through, Yu Mei selected withdrawal in the demonstration.

The retention line went dark. Export stopped halfway too.

[Continuous capacity interrupted]

No actual person had been cut apart. A simple empty frame sat across the division. Lin Mo asked Yu Mei to pause until he could stop imagining his sister's face in the gap, then read the result aloud.

“Would she be caught halfway?” Yu Mei asked.

“It says interrupted. It doesn't show what recovery would look like.”

He could not invent the degree of harm the display had left unspecified. After a long silence, Xiao Lu asked whether they could withdraw after she arrived completely.

They reset the model without loading her scope. Retention lit, export traveled along the line, and Arrived appeared outside.

The retention line stayed bright.

Yu Mei waited. It did not turn off.

“Open what follows.”

Two lines appeared beneath completion.

[Completed export does not release retention]

[Stopping requires continuous succession]

His voice roughened on the second sentence. The person inside would not automatically be released once his sister left. Delivery was not the last step before everyone could go together.

“Read the first one again,” Xiao Lu said.

He did.

“So even if I get out, I can't just call them back.”

“This doesn't offer that. Stopping requires something to continue in their place.”

“Find someone else?”

He watched the panel without saying yes. It gave a condition, not a new source or a name. He asked Yu Mei to leave candidate selection closed and check what counted as completed export. Complete arrival and continuity confirmation were required. A picture appearing or a voice answering could not substitute.

The words exhausted him. He had feared his sister wouldn't come out. Now he also had to fear declaring her out too soon. Every good result needed an ending he wouldn't hastily omit.

Fang Ji stood in the passage and asked whether they were coming back. He couldn't see all the writing; he could hear how long Lin Mo had gone quiet.

“One more question. Hers.”

He looked at Yu Mei. Resting her shoulder against the wall, she said she could manage one question, not pages and pages. He told Xiao Lu the person operating the interface needed rest. What mattered most right now?

“Could I go back myself and let them leave?”

His stomach dropped.

This didn't mean she wanted to remain. She was trying to leave a way out for somebody she hadn't met. Yet his first impulse was refusal, before he had even looked for an explanation.

“We can check. We aren't agreeing to do it.”

Yu Mei opened the description for returning the same source. It required a new assessment of complete continuity. Neither an outside fragment nor a speaker's willingness could cancel established retention. It offered no automatic reversal available now.

Xiao Lu said she understood, then quickly added that she hadn't agreed to export. Her question wasn't consent to come back.

“No,” Lin Mo said.

Before leaving, he asked Yu Mei to return to the first bright Cancel arrow and close the empty model. The actual main view showed none of the demonstration's Arrived labels. Only Unspecified and Unestablished remained.

They had to keep those pages distinct. Fang Ji had heard delivered from the doorway. Unless they explained, he might think Xiao Lu had moved during the demonstration.

“The empty diagram completed the route,” Lin Mo called. “She hasn't moved.”

Fang Ji asked them to remove the model's line too, so nobody mistook it on the next visit. Yu Mei collapsed the demonstration. Temporary storage still held only the authorized sentence, with no new recording.

Xiao Lu asked him to repeat that he had chosen no source. He did, then let the silence remain rather than filling the opening with repeated assurances.

Yu Mei read out that both real ends were unestablished. Xiao Lu wanted to stop here until she felt ready to reconnect. Lin Mo said they were returning to the shelter. This time the arm stayed steady when sound ended.

Back inside, Yu Mei lay on her side first.

She kept her injured shoulder uppermost and her loosened sleeve knot clear. Fang Ji considered offering the bundle, saw how its bottle necks would press into her, and put it down. Would she like the hem of her outer clothing under her face?

No, she said. Let her find a position that didn't hurt.

Only once she stopped moving did Lin Mo explain the result to the others.

“Was sending her out a lie?” He Cen asked.

The complete-export conditions existed, he said. The cost hadn't all appeared in the first line. He would not defend the arrangement, nor speak of the retention they had just read about as though it had already happened.

“It's we'll deal with that later that frightens me,” Yu Mei said into her clothing.

He couldn't see her expression, only the slow movement of her shoulder and back. Earlier, he had treated care as a name. He could so easily have said they would arrange it later. Applied to her, that might mean even this rest had to await his permission.

“If we don't know later, we can't write that someone will come back today.”

Fang Ji picked up the pen, then lowered it. Pain had made his fingers shake halfway through lifting his arm. The cap tapped against the sheet. Lin Mo knew saying it would only take a moment meant asking another shoulder to bear that moment for him.

The sheet was almost full. Should they use another page?

Lin Mo opened the notebook toward blank paper, away from Old Chen's fold. His injured forefinger couldn't hold the page; its corner sprang back.

“You can tell us aloud first,” He Cen said. “The paper won't run away.”

He steadied the book and stopped insisting everything be recorded immediately.

If someone knew all this and still wanted to enter, the seatkeeper asked, would the others prevent it?

Fang Ji looked at him at once. The man admitted he was thinking about it. He hadn't thought it through.

A convenient ending appeared in Lin Mo's mind: let him think, let him repay, let everything continue. He held it back instead of calling the man's uncertainty volunteering.

“Thinking it through only lets you say what you want. We'd still have to establish whether it works and who cares for your body. Right now you're saying you don't know. That's what we keep.”

“What if you wanted to?” the man asked.

The room went quiet.

Fang Ji frowned. He Cen looked up. Yu Mei remained lying still. The question had been among them all along, protected by an empty field. Nobody had pointed it directly at Lin Mo.

He thought of Xiao Lu waiting, the wheelchair, his old face still displayed in the glass. Before coming here, he had planned nothing so precisely. He only knew he had to find her. Having found her, he discovered that every I dragged other people's responsibilities behind it.

“I haven't chosen.”

“Will you tell us?” Yu Mei asked.

“Yes.”

“Before doing it? Not afterward?”

His throat tightened. He couldn't resent the suggestion that he might act behind their backs. That was exactly the easiest solution his mind offered: give nobody the chance to object.

“Before.”

She didn't immediately declare she trusted him. She shifted into a more comfortable position and asked to sleep for a while. He closed the notebook. Photograph, technical sheets, and private fold stayed where they belonged.

He Cen glanced at her before handling the water. He opened and closed the bottle himself without waking her. Lin Mo waited. His sister's continued presence inside couldn't require every injured body in this room to recover now.

A thin voice in the passage said that with enough determination, anything could be exchanged back.

Fang Ji heard it too. His left hand stayed on his knee. The voice made their remaining strength sound like a matter of willingness.

Lin Mo looked instead at Yu Mei's shoulder. At last she had stopped holding it with her hand.

He gave the voice no answer.
