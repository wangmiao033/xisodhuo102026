# Chapter 164: Between the Maintenance Lines

Once Yu Mei sat down, Lin Mo did not resume reading the diary.

Her sleeve had brushed a cover beneath the narrow arm, exposing some gray lettering. It belonged to the arm's base, separate from Xiao Lu's reply field. Beside it was a symbol resembling a stitch.

[Maintenance Notes]

He told Yu Mei not to get up yet. From the chair, he could make out only the beginning of the next line: Store. Months ago, he would have leaned closer. Now moving his shoulder forward tightened his right knee. He settled back and waited.

“Found something else?”

“Possibly somewhere to put words. Don't touch it yet.”

She thought the second line said Read Back After Storage. The metal edge obscured its end.

Old Chen's three sheets had distinguished missing from empty, and explained that return meant returning to position. They had taught Lin Mo nothing about storing a diary here. He simply didn't want one word, store, to become reassurance by itself.

Back in the small room, Fang Ji opened the exposed equipment diagrams. The private corner remained folded. Lin Mo examined Lower Maintenance Port, then the note about return, and had the pages put away.

“Does it tell us how?”

“No. That transfer belonged somewhere else.”

“You looked ready to recognize a relative.”

Lin Mo couldn't object. He wanted someone who had arrived earlier to have solved this, someone trustworthy even in the spaces left unwritten. Old Chen had promised nothing there. Lin Mo separated his diary from the maintenance sheets and asked whether Yu Mei would examine the actual fitting after resting.

They brought only the query sheet back. The diary stayed behind, along with the fifth bottle, within He Cen's reach. Nobody switched on the phone to read small print. The panel's own dim light would suffice; anything it failed to reveal would remain uncertain.

Yu Mei checked that audio was closed. Without opening the casing, she moved the existing cover slightly along its groove. Two full lines appeared.

[Maintenance Notes: Text Storage]

[Read back after storage. No source segment attached.]

A narrow control lay beneath them.

From the corridor, Fang Ji warned her not to pry with her fingernail.

“I'm not prying.” Her voice hardened.

Lin Mo nearly settled the exchange for them. Instead, he pointed out that Fang Ji could see her turned shoulder, not her hand.

“I thought she was holding it up too long again,” Fang Ji said.

“Then ask,” Yu Mei answered.

Lin Mo waited for her to choose to continue. She opened the detail marker beside the lettering.

A narrow input field appeared, smaller than the one for conversation. Below it were Draft, Store, and Read Back. Linked Display stood separately with an unselected box.

The explanation allowed outside submission of maintenance text to this residual circuit. It stored written characters only: no voice, images, source consciousness, or learned procedures. It didn't require Xiao Lu's reference. It would produce an index for retrieving the entry.

Could they inspect without writing? Yu Mei backed out. The field remained empty, marked Not Stored. Reopening showed the same blank. Lin Mo noted the distinction between drafting and committing along the query sheet's edge.

Part of an old note remained visible nearby, beginning with Contact. Before opening it, they checked its scope. It was listed under Equipment Faults, with no personal-source field.

Inside was a short maintenance entry: Unstable contact. Read again after resetting. A local draft display is not a completed write.

No signature, no face to recognize. They had just repaired the arm's contact. The wording matched a real fault, but established neither author nor date.

“Somebody else left words,” Yu Mei said.

“These are equipment notes. Don't go looking for personal contents.”

She returned to the empty field. Following the warning about local drafts, Lin Mo asked whether retrieval required staying on this page. The explanation showed two small frames: the write point at the arm's base and Maintenance Query on the narrow upper interface. One circuit connected them; no limitless line stretched beyond it.

They could test something real. Store at the base, close the writing interface, retrieve from the query above. They didn't need to submit his memories first to discover how to check them.

“What if the upper one only mirrors this one?”

They could leave the draft and close its session before using the index, he said. That would at least test whether the words existed only as unsubmitted text on this screen. It wouldn't establish that the circuit would last forever.

“Did repairers have to keep trying like this?” Yu Mei asked, touching an old worn mark.

“Old Chen recorded failures.”

“I didn't mean him.”

He had put a familiar name onto every unknown pair of hands. Perhaps, she said, somebody they would never know had tried several times here and worried that the next person would leave as soon as words lit up.

Lin Mo studied the anonymous note again. This time he didn't suggest recording Old Chen as its possible author.

“How long would it last?” Yu Mei asked.

There was no expiry date. Storage depended on this residual circuit; loss of the circuit could prevent retrieval. A matching active access point could use the index, but a continuing connection had to be established by actual readback.

His pulse quickened at matching access point. Yu Mei pointed to the warning.

“Copy that too?”

“Yes. And the part about anyone holding the index being able to read.”

There was no password, no lock allowing only its owner. At best, this place could avoid automatic public presentation. It wasn't a permanently secure vault. If he lost the index, the words might remain somewhere like Old Chen's pages, waiting for someone who might never arrive.

Instead of a name, he had Yu Mei draft: Dust beside the wall.

She typed slowly. Her shoulder dropped at the final character. Nothing completed the sentence for her.

“Store this?”

“Check the display choice first.”

Two routes appeared. Readback returned text. Linked Display would incorporate the contents into outside recommendations. Neither had been confirmed. They chose text storage and left Linked Display unchecked. Writing had not begun.

Soft applause came from farther away.

Light gathered on the wall into a black umbrella. Across its canopy were the words For the Self Who Forgot. Lin Mo's stomach tightened. A small invitation below offered to turn his story into pictures.

He didn't identify that handle as the one he remembered. Its appearance didn't prove the diary had been stored.

“We didn't bring the pages,” Yu Mei said.

“I told the story here.”

Her expression changed. The nearby live display was off, but they had spoken beside the door. He couldn't know what every distant seam might have taken. Telling a story to Xiao Lu did not establish that only she could hear.

He asked Yu Mei to leave the umbrella alone and inspect the actual input field. Dust beside the wall remained unchanged.

The light moved across the floor. The umbrella opened. A hand appeared beneath it, as though offering to shelter the wheelchair.

Lin Mo leaned right to escape its shadow. The chair stayed still; his knee pressed against the footrest edge. He gasped and instinctively braced his left hand on the armrest. Pain stabbed through his injured finger.

“Don't pull me.”

Yu Mei stopped halfway through her step. She steadied the handles and asked him to lower his arm. He couldn't immediately obey. His shoulder was rigid, as though relaxing would drop him beneath the umbrella.

“The wheel hasn't moved!” Fang Ji called. “Look at the wheel!”

The chipped rim still aligned with the same floor joint. The brake remained engaged. The shadow advanced. His body had not.

Yu Mei told him to breathe out instead of putting his weight through his fingers.

At last he settled against the backrest. Recognizing the lure didn't stop his knee throbbing. Yu Mei couldn't crouch to adjust him. From the side, she checked that his leg hadn't slipped from the support. She released the handles only after he said the pain was no longer increasing.

“We're stopping.”

“All right.”

The test sentence remained uncommitted. They didn't need a successful write to prove they hadn't been frightened.

The umbrella folded. Applause became an impatient cough. Yu Mei sat against the wall; Fang Ji didn't crowd in to take over. Once Lin Mo recovered his breath, he described what had happened. Avoiding the picture had strained his hand and knee. The chair hadn't been pulled. They had no evidence that storing text moved a body.

“But it knew to use an umbrella,” Yu Mei said.

“Yes. Hiding something here can't mean nobody could learn about it.”

He no longer asked her to secure a perfect refuge.

Would he still store it? Paper could be soaked or lost too. That didn't make another copy worthless.

“Only the test sentence. If we can't manage today, we stop.”

When Yu Mei was ready, they checked the text and the unselected display option again. She pressed Store. The sentence contracted into a thin line. A short index appeared beside Stored. No count of saved memories, no figure leaving the outside.

The index's last two characters looked similar. Yu Mei initially read one incorrectly. Enlarging it revealed an extra stroke. She crossed out the mistake, copied it correctly, then retrieved the entry.

The same sentence returned, labeled Stored Text.

Lin Mo had her leave and reopen the interface. The draft field was now empty; the sentence hadn't survived merely as an image of unsubmitted work. She entered the index. Dust beside the wall returned again.

“That sentence, at least, was stored.”

Below it, Linked Display Not Established accompanied the absence of a recommendation entry. Yu Mei inspected the visible outside frame. The black umbrella had become something else. Their test sentence wasn't there. She didn't claim to have searched the entire Theater.

Lin Mo asked her to record the actual result: readback after exiting, from this port only; no public display created; observation elsewhere not excluded. The paper's margin was crowded. She wrote the index and essential points without producing a full maintenance report.

His knee still hurt on the way back. He asked her to take the turn slowly. The fifth bottle was a little lower. He Cen tightened its cap and asked whether they had brought back another number that couldn't find a person.

“This one finds words.”

Fang Ji opened the diary. The crooked handwriting remained. Old Chen's private fold was still closed. They hadn't borrowed a promise from it.

“Write more tomorrow?” He Cen asked.

Lin Mo didn't know how much of this day remained. First they would rest. Then check the words already written, rather than force out every remaining memory. Fang Ji placed the pen beside the notebook instead of putting it into his hand.

Lin Mo read the line about the garment on the chair. That evening he had only moved a lamp. The page claimed no more.

For now, he wanted those few words to exist on more than one piece of paper.
