# Chapter 155: Leave the Space Empty

When Lin Mo finished reading separate retention item, Xiao Lu asked, “Retained where?”

Yu Mei did not touch the blank frame. She expanded only the explanation beneath it. Assessment Only, No Execution remained visible. A line of equal length joined the two regions, with Not Established at either end.

[Equivalent retention]

The next line required continuous maintenance within the layer.

Lin Mo looked at the wall behind the metal arm. The layer might mean beyond the gray door, the circular room, or somewhere no body could enter. There was no diagram. He explained that uncertainty. Yu Mei did not point to a patch of floor and declare it a seat someone must occupy.

“Leaving the same amount of something?” she asked.

“Check that.”

The detail did not mention weight or container size. It offered two general queries, Static Copy and Continuing Source, each followed by a question mark. He asked her to examine Static Copy without entering anyone's reference.

The familiar diagram of an independent segment appeared. It could be enlarged or placed alongside many others, but the retention field remained unlit.

[Static copies cannot provide continuous maintenance]

Lin Mo thought of the bottle sentence in the phone. He did not select it for another test. The explanation already excluded that type. Something sufficient to identify a voice could not hold the place of someone still able to answer.

“What does it say?” Xiao Lu asked.

“Old recordings alone won't do.”

“They want something new?”

He did not answer yes. Yu Mei opened Continuing Source. The first field read Current Consciousness Reference, leading through Source Verification to Individual Confirmation. Confirmation was unavailable. The empty field had not filled itself with Lin Mo's identity or portraits of his companions.

Yu Mei slowly withdrew her hand.

The laughter in the circular room had quieted. Its dark surface pressed toward the stone railing, as though many people were leaning forward to see whose name he would enter.

“Nobody has been selected,” he said, for Xiao Lu and for Fang Ji in the corridor.

He read the new wording exactly. He did not turn current consciousness into a piece of memory, or claim that confirmation meant one press would explain everything.

“I didn't ask you to find someone else,” Xiao Lu said.

“I know.”

“You're looking at their method. Don't tell anyone I asked you to do this.”

His throat tightened. He had expected to reassure her about still being inside. Instead, she feared becoming the reason someone else remained.

“I won't.”

Yu Mei asked quietly whether staying in the layer meant never returning. She did not direct the question into the port, but neither did she conceal it.

The next expandable restriction said Exit Conditions Require Separate Verification. Nothing more was available there. Lin Mo read that too.

“It doesn't say they can come back,” Yu Mei said.

“We can't treat silence as permission to assume they can.”

They stopped without offering a name for the next page. If selecting a person was the price of learning how that person could leave, that would be another condition to investigate. A choice could not be disguised as a look.

Fang Ji approached, his left shoulder drooping. After studying the blank, he told Lin Mo that He Cen was awake and asking how much longer they would be.

Lin Mo remembered his promise: establish whether it was her, then return and tell the others. He had substituted several relayed messages for actually coming back. Yu Mei had rested on the floor; He Cen had remained down the corridor, waiting for a complete account.

“We're going back now.”

He told Xiao Lu they would leave the sound port and explain what they had found. They still had no way to bring her out whole.

“Will you play them that sentence?”

“They need the result. You don't have to say it again.”

She asked him to return to the subject of bottles when he reconnected.

Yu Mei ended audio and exited the assessment, leaving the retention field blank. The query sheet came back from the tray without a new name or confirmation mark. She checked that sound was closed and left the metal arm in its existing position.

Lin Mo's right knee pulled as the wheelchair reversed. He did not push with his feet. He waited while Yu Mei set the direction and took him around the corridor turn. The small room was only a few meters away. Returning still took time.

He Cen sat on the concrete ledge, glasses slipping down his nose. He pushed them up with the back of his hand. The fifth bottle stood toward the inside, lighter than when opened but not empty. Four large empty bottles and two old small ones remained in the bundle. Fang Ji moved it toward the wall to clear the wheel.

Yu Mei parked the chair and immediately sat down.

Lin Mo asked whether He Cen still felt cold.

“Less. My head's still hot. Being awake doesn't mean I'm better.” He looked up. “Is it her?”

Lin Mo nodded slowly.

“There's a present consciousness answering from the backup linked to her original reference. She can listen, remember what we just said, and refuse.”

“Where is she?”

“Still inside.”

He Cen lowered his gaze. He did not call a voice enough, or demand the bottle recording as proof. After a while he asked whether they needed to move something.

Lin Mo described the partial delivery, the insufficient capacity for the whole, and the retention conditions. Fang Ji spread the query sheet on an empty stretch of ledge and weighted its curling corner with a stone already lying there. When something needed checking, he looked at the paper instead of inventing a complete set of rules for the others.

The seatkeeper had stayed against the wall.

At current consciousness, he glanced at the sheet, then at his injured left leg. The dressing's edge remained dirty. Ending his local viewing had not healed him.

Could something else count, he asked. The viewing records from his old account, for instance.

“We haven't found that option.”

“I've left more than you have.”

Fang Ji's expression hardened. Lin Mo heard the possibility in those words: let the things the man had left behind occupy enough space to bring someone else out. Once, he could select an ending in a little frame. Now he was searching for a frame that might make the accounts balance.

“Do you mean the records, or yourself?”

The man's lips moved without an answer. He drew his leg closer, pain bringing sweat to his forehead. Finally, he said he did not know. He only wanted to ask whether it counted.

Lin Mo did not force I don't know into I volunteer.

“He does owe for some things,” Fang Ji said.

The room went still. Yu Mei's hand stopped at her knotted sleeve. He Cen looked at Fang Ji without interrupting for either of them.

Lin Mo remembered Old Chen on the other side of the barrier. He had wondered many times what he might do if their positions were reversed and he reached the control instead of the seatkeeper. Now the empty field was close enough. Nobody had needed to urge him. He had already imagined someone who could fill it.

“Yes,” he said. “But this page isn't his account.”

“I didn't tell you to enter him.”

“I know. I'm talking about what I thought.”

He demanded no gratitude. The man had not been excused. His earlier application remained, and Old Chen had not returned. Lin Mo only moved his left hand away from the paper, refusing to let an old act press a new control for him.

“Even if someone wants to choose themselves,” Fang Ji said eventually, “they should know what staying means first.”

He sat at the other end of the ledge, injured palm resting on his lap.

He Cen pulled his gray coat higher.

“Don't do the calculation for me because I'm the slowest.”

“Nobody said that,” Yu Mei answered at once.

“I'm saying it first. Not being able to walk isn't agreement to stay.”

Lin Mo told him he had heard. There was no need to establish who might have entertained the thought. Everyone sitting here should be able to say it before someone else decided.

Yu Mei loosened her sleeve knot enough to expose the pressure-red skin. Her shoulder needed rest, she said. If the query still required standing at the port, it would have to happen in stages. Lin Mo could not decide that the only proper breaks were when the person inside fell silent.

“When you say stop, we stop.”

“What if she needs to talk?”

He looked down the corridor. “I tell her you've stopped. I can't treat you like a hand with no life of its own.”

Yu Mei did not rise because he had said the right thing. She settled against the wall and closed her eyes. The wheelchair stayed where it was.

He folded the query sheet and placed it outside the notebook, away from Old Chen's private pages. Fang Ji added Source Unselected, Not Executed to their record. The photograph remained between its old pages. Nobody held the sunlit face up to make the others answer whether they would stay for her.

Yu Mei asked what use careful reading would be if the instructions were lying too.

Lin Mo did not promise that written words could not lie. He separated what they had tested: the audio had stopped when requested; one authorized sentence had been copied outside; the complete-transfer obstacle was an assessment result, not something verified by risking a person. Their direct evidence grew thinner the farther they went.

“So we ask whether we can examine the conditions without committing. Asking a question can't become agreeing to the answer.”

“And if they require agreement first?”

“We stop there and come back.”

That answer added no water. He Cen drank, tightened the cap, and returned the bottle to the inner ledge. Lin Mo watched the lower waterline without pretending they had unlimited time.

He asked Fang Ji to note that they must check the empty field first on returning. If it had been filled, they would investigate that, not simply proceed. Fang Ji wrote a few short words and rested his shoulder.

Only after Yu Mei had truly rested did they go back. Fang Ji stayed within calling distance of both rooms. Lin Mo took the same query sheet. Water and notebook remained behind, and the phone stayed in Yu Mei's pocket.

Xiao Lu first asked whether they still had the empty bottles.

“Yes. We went back too.”

“Did you explain?”

“Yes. Nobody has been entered.”

A faint exhalation came through the port. She asked what they would examine next.

What retention meant, he said. How equivalence was measured. How anyone could stop. They would not put a person in first and use that person as the test.

She did not thank him. She asked whether he would read every condition aloud.

“Yes. Tell me when you need to stop too.”

Yu Mei reopened the unestablished retention explanation. The complete-export region and empty space appeared again. Their connecting line remained broken. At the bottom, previously hidden beneath the folded exit conditions, was another readable entry.

[Retained person: continuity must be sustained within the data layer]

Lin Mo read it, his voice stopping on the final word.

“Then who takes care of the person outside?” Xiao Lu asked.

He looked down at the wheelchair beneath him, outside the circular room's threshold, then at his immobilized right hand. He did not reduce her question to a name. He did not reach for the blank.

“We haven't checked that yet.”
