# Chapter 169: The Drink Was Not for the Audience

When Fang Ji picked up the pen, Lin Mo told him not to open Diary One.

He wanted a separate, present-tense addendum. The original three incidents would remain what they were, not quietly become a farewell that had known the outcome all along. Fang Ji found half a blank page near the back and waited.

“I wanted water just now.”

He wrote that and looked up.

“And?”

“I didn't ask at first.”

Lin Mo looked at the bottle. They could see how much remained, but couldn't divide it into everything each person might need. He had reasons for not asking. Conserving water. He Cen needing it more. His own injured cheek. All true. Another reason hid among them, less comfortable to record.

“What I'm preparing to do seemed so big. Asking for water seemed too small.”

“Seemed that way to whom?” Fang Ji asked.

Lin Mo closed his eyes briefly.

“To me.”

Only after saying it did the dryness in his throat stop needing a respectable name. Someone preparing to lose a great deal could still be thirsty. His body didn't have to stop needing things in advance.

Fang Ji wrote slowly. His right palm couldn't hold the paper, and writing with his left hand pulled at his shoulder. He stopped after short passages. Lin Mo waited without adding more memories to fill the pauses. Yu Mei weighted the curling edge with their small stone rather than keeping a hand there.

The addendum was brief.

Lin Mo could make a decision and still need help settling his legs, reading text, or drinking water. He had tried to prove he could bear everything alone by refusing those things. In practice, he had only made it harder for people to know how to help. He had waited, then stated his need. The water hadn't become a reward for him.

He stopped Fang Ji there.

“Don't say everyone wanted to help.”

“I haven't.”

“I'm reminding myself.”

He had nearly supplied another ending where everyone finally understood. Yu Mei still hurt. Fang Ji could still be impatient. He Cen's fever hadn't receded because Lin Mo abandoned a show of endurance. Accepting care let an actual situation be addressed. It didn't suddenly make him someone everybody had to like.

He shortened that thought to two plain sentences. Helping him once didn't oblige anyone to continue. Finding the addendum later wouldn't authorize someone to make a future Lin Mo endure more.

“This isn't asking them to forgive me immediately.”

Fang Ji didn't ask whom them included. Space remained at the bottom. He didn't fill it with every debt from Lin Mo's past.

The last note would say that Lin Mo still had his memories and clearing hadn't started. The addendum couldn't establish which steps were ultimately performed. Actual events would require a separate record. Today's intent couldn't pass for tomorrow's outcome.

“Store it in the circuit?” Yu Mei asked.

“Only this part about me. Not the care arrangements or what any of you said.”

He didn't add another family story. The photograph's edge showed between the notebook's pages. He reached toward it, then stopped. Not because it might change his mind. He feared wanting it to authenticate every fragment he was preparing to surrender.

“Look if you want,” Fang Ji said. “Looking doesn't mean you have to write more.”

He sounded too tired for an essay about picking up a photograph. Lin Mo asked him to pass it over without disturbing Old Chen's folded pages.

It rested against the side of his left palm.

His sister's face was there, her unsteady hand still slightly blurred. The person beside her had long since been rewritten as a woman. Sister on the back hadn't corrected itself because he had reached this place. Familiarity and visible evidence remained out of alignment. He thought he might have told her to keep still when the picture was taken. That sentence could belong to a different photograph. He didn't promote it into a new memory.

He looked for a while.

The photograph couldn't supply his remembered face or prove the day had originally looked like this. But failing to do those things didn't make it worthless paper. Later, someone who didn't recognize it could still look, without being required to succeed immediately.

He asked Fang Ji to put it back facing inward, corners uncreased. Fang Ji waited for him to release the edge. That waiting made Lin Mo want to hold on to the familiarity a little longer.

Eventually he let go.

The wall brightened.

An open book appeared. Its pages held none of their crooked handwriting. In large, orderly characters, it announced that Lin Mo had never asked anything for himself. A small response field invited him to complete his final account for every witness.

His face warmed at never.

He had just written down needing a drink. Here was a man who needed nothing. He wanted to shout the truth: he was frightened and thirsty; he had often been unreasonable. He wasn't their brother on the page.

Yu Mei saw his mouth open.

“Coughing again?”

He shook his head.

Correcting the lie would also give them a new account. Make him angry enough, and he would deliver each private detail into their response field himself.

He looked down at the real paper.

“Don't answer it.”

“It's speaking for you.”

“I know. That isn't my book.”

Silence wouldn't erase the words. They remained while someone read them as praise. He left the actual addendum beside Fang Ji rather than entering it into a contest over who understood him best.

The luminous book turned another page. A faint reflection appeared near its spine. Lin Mo could make out the notebook on his lap, only its outline. He couldn't tell whether it was a live reflection or an assembled image. He still asked Fang Ji to turn the writing inward.

“Already seen?” Fang Ji asked after doing it.

“We can't prove it hasn't. We don't have to open the rest for it.”

A corner caught near the binding as the notebook closed. Lin Mo asked him not to pull, but ease it free. This was the page with the first crooked attempt at his name, not the clean copy. A narrow pale crease appeared. The paper didn't tear.

“Easy to mix all these sheets up,” Fang Ji said.

Lin Mo agreed. The clear copy was for legibility, the original for preserving how the account had formed. Neither automatically carried more truth. He asked Fang Ji to label their titles and sequence instead of writing Final Version over the earlier words, burying every hesitation.

Afterward Fang Ji rested his hand on the paper. He didn't ask whether this justified the effort. Lin Mo offered no guarantee of future retrieval. For now, they could distinguish the pieces rather than leaving someone else trapped by their own disorder.

The book on the wall turned faster than Fang Ji's hands. Lin Mo waited for the real pages.

Once Yu Mei's shoulder eased, they handled storage. She opened a fresh maintenance note through the known access point. Diary One remained unchanged; Linked Display remained unselected. The short addendum fit on one page. She entered it from the paper. Lin Mo checked only his own words, without copying the wall's claims in order to refute them.

They named it Current Addendum.

Storage returned a new index. Yu Mei copied it, closed the entry, and retrieved it through the existing readback access. She read the opening wish for water and the final statement that clearing hadn't begun. She didn't skip the unflattering part between them. The words were there. Linked Display remained Not Established. None of that guaranteed nobody elsewhere would ever read them.

They didn't make another farewell out of a successful retrieval.

The new index went at the bottom of the original addendum. A second copy went on the clean diary copy's reverse, on a separate line from Diary One's index. The old index remained. The two texts weren't merged. The original stayed in the notebook; the clear copy was secured separately. Finding one address shouldn't imply that everything had been found.

“More?” Fang Ji asked.

Many things came to mind. A night road. Sweat inside military boots. The drawn-out ending when his sister called him. Each pressed forward as though another unfinished page could keep the decision inside preparation indefinitely.

“This entry ends here.”

“And what you've left out?”

“It's left out.”

He wouldn't call everything omitted unimportant. Important things could run out of time too. The diary was allowed to be limited before he recorded absolutely everything. Fang Ji capped the pen and exhaled, no longer waiting to discover whether another memory would postpone his rest.

Once the notebook was secure, Lin Mo asked Yu Mei to find out whether Xiao Lu wanted to talk. She agreed without asking how much he had written. He told her the new text concerned his own present experience. Her voice wasn't included. This conversation wouldn't become material either.

“Can you stop telling me what you want to leave behind now?” she asked.

“Yes.”

“Tell me what you see. Don't choose something important.”

He looked up. Worn paint at the arm's root. A diagonal scrape low on the doorframe. A loose thread at Yu Mei's cuff. Without turning, those were the available things. He nearly described the light farther away, then stopped and told her about the scrape. He didn't know who had made it.

“What does it look like?”

He considered it. Like someone had moved an object too wide for the door, turned it badly, and scraped the frame. She asked whether it eventually got through.

“You can't tell. The mark only goes this far.”

She laughed softly.

“You always want to finish the story.”

He laughed too, careful of his injured cheek. Yes, he said. Then he stopped supplying the unknown scrape with an eventual success. They were quiet. The mark didn't become more like an answer.

“Still the same mark?” she asked.

“Still there. Nobody's wiped it.”

“Keep looking, then.”

He did. Fang Ji's sole scraped as he changed position. He Cen coughed through drowsiness. The seatkeeper slowly resettled his leg. Lin Mo didn't identify each sound for the voice port or arrange them into a warm scene worth preserving.

After a while, Xiao Lu called him.

He answered with his eyes still on the doorframe.

“If you don't understand what I mean later, ask.”

He wanted to promise he would. They had spent a long time admitting that a future Lin Mo might not understand why this one agreed. He said he had heard her now and would carry that into his final choice. He wouldn't give her an empty guarantee on his future self's behalf.

She didn't insist that he make it sound better.

“Then ask something now.”

He thought, then asked whether she wanted to keep talking.

“No. I'm not angry.”

“All right.”

He waited for her to finish without reshaping the last pause into a farewell. As Yu Mei closed the channel, the book on the wall kept turning. Its occupant always seemed to have more to leave the audience.

Lin Mo looked at his hand.

The water's coolness had gone. Thirst wouldn't be gone forever. He asked Yu Mei to leave the fifth bottle on the ledge within everyone's reach, without reserving a share for him. He didn't know which day he might forget first. But he had taken that drink. Nobody needed to assign it a meaning.
