# Chapter 161: The Blank That Stayed

The notebook remained on his lap for a while before Lin Mo asked Yu Mei to put it away and leave it in the small room. After resting, she agreed to push him to the door again, only to read the newly found explanation. He Cen was leaning securely. Fang Ji stayed beside him.

The arm’s marks remained aligned. They checked the original reference and asked Xiao Lu whether she wanted to reconnect. She first asked if anyone had been entered this time. Only after hearing no did she agree to audio. Yu Mei returned to the assessment-only entry and read its name slowly.

Lin Mo heard his own breathing first.

The words remained at the bottom of the interface: Blank Continuity Structure. Autobiographical Links Must Be Cleared. Yu Mei rested the back of her hand against the doorframe, waiting for her shoulder to ease. The repaired contact on the metal arm no longer crackled. A clear signal did not make what followed clear.

“Clear what?” Xiao Lu asked.

She had heard the wording. He could not choose a kinder version now. He asked Yu Mei to expand the explanation, first checking that they were still inside Assessment Only.

That notice remained above two distant frames. One represented the continuing structure retained inside. The other was labeled Outside Self-Continuity. Fine connections fell between them like rain into something bottomless.

“Don't enter a reference yet.”

Yu Mei selected an empty example. The connections became dotted. There were no names or scenes from anybody's past. A small section on the retention side continued advancing, leaving a faint trace. Something diminished in the outside frame before the explanation appeared.

[Clear associations without stopping basic continuity]

The word without did not reassure him. If something simply stopped, he could imagine calling along the break until it woke. This would continue. Which part of him would be continuing?

“Leave something empty to hold her place?” Yu Mei asked.

He waited. Another line explained that the retained structure must continue operating. Blank paper or a recording could not replace it. Cleared autobiographical associations would not automatically be saved outside.

Xiao Lu called to him. He read the wording aloud.

“Who would the person outside still recognize?”

Yu Mei stopped searching for the next entry and looked at him. Nobody had entered his name. The example had no face. Yet all three of them were looking at the same place.

“That's what we need to check.”

“Don't agree while you're checking.”

His right knee twitched on the footrest. Pain escaped before he could contain it, and Xiao Lu immediately went quiet. Yu Mei bent to look without moving the support. Had the footrest changed angle?

“No. I've been holding too tight.”

“Then let go first.”

She sounded as though he were crumpling a sheet of paper. He released the tension gradually, only to find his shoulder rising. He stopped again. Enduring longer would not remove any of the dotted connections.

Fang Ji approached from the corridor. Lin Mo asked him to remain where he could see the small room. He Cen's recent difficulty could not be settled by another claim to be fine. Fang Ji agreed without reaching for the wheelchair handles.

“I'm still here,” Xiao Lu said.

“I know.”

“I'm not saying it to hurry you.”

He looked at the dust between his fingers. He had thought not finding her was the worst part. Now she wanted to know who he would become, and he could not promise one thing that would remain unchanged.

Yu Mei expanded Outside Self-Continuity. The explanation listed types without reading a person: factual knowledge, learned procedures, autobiographical associations. Their edges overlapped. Some connections fell between two labels.

“Autobiographical means stories you've told?” Xiao Lu asked.

“Some of them, perhaps. Things never told too. This isn't a complete list.”

At the overlapping area, smaller text said a single experience could contain different associations. The individual must verify the scope. Retaining one item did not guarantee that related feelings would survive intact.

“Knowing a person's name,” Yu Mei said slowly, “and remembering why you want to see them. Those can be different?”

The example suggested a distinction, Lin Mo answered. It did not promise they could preserve every connection they wanted. His voice sounded steady. He almost hated that steadiness. Xiao Lu wanted to know whether he would forget her, while he kept measuring the limits of small print.

“I might still know you're my sister,” he said at last, “and not remember being with you. We don't know how much could go.”

No answer came.

A low whistle sounded across the circular room. Someone laughed. Hadn't he already changed his face? Why cling to the little that remained?

His back went rigid. He did not look toward the voice, but felt his former appearance being lifted for inspection on some unseen screen.

Yu Mei reached for the nearby cover. Her shoulder stopped her halfway. The whistle came from a more distant seam; closing this one would not help.

“Leave it,” he said. “Our exchange view is already off. Don't hurt your shoulder for a port we can't see.”

She withdrew, paler than before.

“They want to watch this too.”

The example had no surname, but the audience already had an ending worth applauding. He almost demanded what they knew about giving anything up. Instead, he swallowed the words. His sister should not have to squeeze her next question through an argument with spectators.

“Xiao Lu. What were you going to say?”

“If the empty thing can still think, aren't you leaving someone there anyway?”

They both looked at the display. Blank had almost let him imagine something that needed no care. As though forgetting pain meant nothing could suffer.

He asked Yu Mei to examine sensation on the retention side without using Xiao Lu as the example. The explanation promised neither unconsciousness nor freedom from pain. Clearing affected self-association; the retained structure continued operating. Subjective experience was outside the verifiable scope of this assessment.

He read that aloud.

“Does it not know, or won't it tell you?”

“I can't distinguish those.”

“Then don't call it just empty.”

“All right. I won't.”

Yu Mei turned over the query sheet. Most of its back was full. In the small space remaining, she wrote Sensation Uncertain. Lin Mo asked her to leave room beneath it. The page must not look as though they had gathered every answer.

A bottle cap rolled in the small room.

Fang Ji turned immediately. He Cen called that he had missed the thread, but spilled no water. The seatkeeper said the cap had stopped beside his foot. Give him time to bend. Nobody pretended these little events had ceased to matter so Lin Mo could continue reading.

He could hear where each person was without seeing them all. If his body stayed here, who would smooth a painful fold beneath it? Who would know his throat was dry? Who would wait when he could not answer?

“We should come back out.”

Yu Mei thought he meant the menu until he placed his left hand beside the chair.

“I need to go to the room. Closing this doesn't make it nothing.”

He explained to Xiao Lu that he wanted to discuss a possibility. No source had been selected. When she asked which possibility, he did not save it for the people outside.

“If this could bring you out whole, I want to find out whether I could do it.”

The whistle had stopped too. He almost suspected the silence itself was waiting to be watched.

“I haven't agreed to press anything,” he added. “I want to examine my own case. Not have someone chosen for me.”

“You can ask about yourself,” she said. “But don't ask me to tell you it won't matter if you forget.”

Something pushed gently against his chest, leaving him nothing to grip.

“I won't ask you that.”

She wanted the bad parts first when he returned, not a speech beginning with her escape. He agreed. Before disconnecting, she asked Yu Mei to check that the chair did not block the way back.

“It doesn't. I'll turn it slowly.”

After audio closed, Yu Mei folded the example away. The retention field remained blank. She did not type his name. As the chair reversed into the corridor, the metal arm clicked behind him. He resisted immediately calling for another stop.

In the room, He Cen studied his face and said he would try not to fall over again and distract him.

“You didn't distract me.”

“Then stop looking at me like that.”

Yu Mei parked the chair and sat down first. Fang Ji cleared the flattest section of the ledge. Lin Mo explained the new possibility, including the uncertain sensation, the absence of automatic restoration, and his wish to be assessed as a candidate.

At the end, Fang Ji glanced at his splinted wrist.

“You can't even write properly.”

“I know.”

“I'm not saying you don't qualify.” Fang Ji met his eyes. “I'm saying everything you decide leaves somebody else's hands doing what comes next. You need to say that part too.”

Heat spread into Lin Mo's face. He had considered what he could give up, while treating the people keeping him seated, typing for him, and taking his body away as a natural continuation of the plan.

“That's why I'm back asking. You can refuse.”

The seatkeeper watched the doorway. He Cen said he could listen, but could not promise to keep walking later. Yu Mei could read and push along this stretch. She could not promise on behalf of everyone outside to look after him forever.

He accepted each limited answer without adding them into an unlimited pledge.

“What if the assessment says it can't be done?” Yu Mei asked. “How will you come back?”

He said along the same corridor. She shook her head and tapped her knee. Only then did he understand she was not asking which way the wheels would turn.

“I might want to search for another entrance.”

“Or decide you weren't brave enough?”

He did not immediately deny it. That accusation had been waiting before she asked. Others had already lost things for him. Now a cost seemed available in his own name, and part of him had thought: finally, no more debts.

Fang Ji's hard expression changed.

“You think paying once will make you owe nothing?”

Lin Mo looked at the injured right palm. Fang Ji neither hid it nor held it up for inspection.

“I didn't ask you to pay for this hand. Investigate because you want to. Don't put me in your reasons.”

He Cen said that, counted like that, all the water he had borrowed must already amount to a person. It was not a funny remark. He did not laugh. He settled the bottle where the others could reach it.

Lin Mo lost the reassurances he had prepared. He could not promise a better method or promise never to think about debts again. Instead, he said he would return with the full reason if this could not be done. He would not substitute cowardice for the reason.

Yu Mei watched him before nodding.

“I can listen to that.”

Fang Ji placed the pen on the ledge, point inward.

“If you're going to check, record how far you've checked. Don't leave us guessing which things you're still considering and which you've done.”

The pen did not choose for him. It would not automatically write him whole again either.

“Candidate first,” he said. “I'm willing to investigate. I haven't confirmed any clearing.”

Fang Ji asked him to shorten that before writing. Lin Mo spoke slowly; the pen moved slowly. When they finished, the paper remained between them. Nobody sent it through the gray door.
