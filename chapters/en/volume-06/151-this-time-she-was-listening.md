# Chapter 151: This Time She Was Listening

“I want to read first,” the voice said. “Sometimes you say one thing and I hear it twice.”

Lin Mo did not ask which two things. Beneath the narrow glass beside the sound port, Yu Mei found several folding keys marked Text Exchange. She explained the change, waited for agreement, then closed both audio channels. The source reference remained lit. His wheelchair stayed outside the circular room.

A question mark appeared in the empty reply field.

He did not ask her to call him her brother. He did not have Yu Mei bring the photograph. Another line followed the small curved mark.

[Are you sending me words now, or words from before?]

The gray door offered no passage for a body. A narrow metal arm brought the interface within reach of the doorway; its thumb-sized sound hole was silent. After everything he had heard through other windows, Lin Mo could not decide what to say first.

“Write: words from now.”

Yu Mei typed. The words waited on their side until she sent them, then slipped beyond the dividing line.

[Then why weren't you saying anything?]

He looked at his hands. His injured left forefinger rested against the armrest, his splinted right wrist beside his thigh. He had fallen silent after the change, but the interface might simply have registered the delay. A relevant reply was not enough by itself.

“Because I was afraid you'd tell me to reach out again.”

Yu Mei sent the sentence without softening it.

The answer took longer. Beyond the corridor, low laughter moved around the stone railing. Someone asked what the person inside had said. Lin Mo kept watching the line aligned with the original source.

[I didn't tell you to reach out.]

[Not just now, anyway. I can't see your hands.]

His throat tightened. The woman in the preview had looked at him over and over, her gaze already arranged. This answer made him aware of what was missing between them.

“I know. That was an earlier preview.”

He added that his name was still the one he had given. Yu Mei lowered her hand to rest her shoulder. The interface did not finish their greeting for them.

The person inside asked whether he had come alone.

Before he answered, Fang Ji called from the corridor's far end. He Cen was awake. Did they need someone to read the screen?

“Not yet.”

Lin Mo asked Yu Mei to explain that people were nearby, but he would ask the questions for now. There was no need to send their names.

“Can she refuse?” Yu Mei asked, pausing over the keys.

He looked at the unsent sentence. He had guarded against the Theater answering for people, yet was already keeping possession of every next question. He had Yu Mei revise the ending: she could write stop whenever she wished.

[Can you write stop too?]

“Yes.”

[Then stop for a moment. I don't want you to keep telling me your name.]

Yu Mei withdrew her hand.

His breathing grew heavier. Everyone wanted something definite; he wanted it most. But no face appeared, and no demand for payment followed the pause. He listened to Yu Mei drying her fingers on her clothes.

[Still there?]

“Write: I'm here. Don't say we're all waiting for her.”

The line flickered.

[I'm here too. We weren't disconnected.]

Something holding his chest rigid suddenly loosened. Yu Mei stepped forward, but he asked only for the query sheet to be flattened on his lap. The photograph remained in the small room.

The reference had followed him from the basement. Historical delivery, present access, and these newly exchanged words each proved something different. At least he no longer had only an old voice calling him brother.

He could keep the arrival record separate from what he had just heard. He could keep this exchange separate from every question he still needed to ask. Wanting the answers to agree did not make them interchangeable. The copied reference lay exposed on his lap, ordinary paper with a newly bent edge. He watched that edge until his breathing settled enough to speak.

“One more thing to try.”

Yu Mei looked tired. “What?”

He had almost asked about the hospital curtains. Such details had already been filmed, and had entered his own false memories. He changed the question.

“Ask her to choose a short sentence now. I'll answer. She can change her mind.”

[Don't ask about curtains.]

His heart lurched. Yu Mei's hand froze. He had thought about curtains without mentioning them.

Another line arrived.

[Someone kept asking about colors before. I don't know who. I don't want those questions now.]

Yu Mei asked quietly whether to continue.

Sweat gathered on his nose. He did not know what before meant to her. The coincidence did not authenticate her account, but the refusal belonged to her.

“Write: all right. We won't.”

He kept his unspoken thought to himself.

[Say there's no rain outside the door.]

The corridor was dry. Cloth moved farther away, but he heard no water here. He asked Yu Mei to say that there was no rain where they could see, then ask what his reply should be.

[Anything. Don't write my next part for me.]

“Then write: I heard you.”

Before Yu Mei sent it, another message appeared.

[I've changed it. Not rain. Ask whether I want to hear your voice.]

Yu Mei deleted the unsent response, tapping slowly until the field was empty. Lin Mo realized he had been holding his breath.

He asked the new question.

[Yes. Just one sentence.]

An audio marker lit below the panel. Its switch remained closed. He leaned forward to read, but his left shoulder stopped him halfway.

Yu Mei read the small print. “Single utterance. Outside transmits, inside receives. No video.”

At his request she moved the agreement beside the audio marker. The recipient's acceptance appeared. Only then did she open the channel.

He coughed. The sore patch inside his cheek stung. When it passed, he said, “I'm sitting outside the door. My hands are on my lap.”

The light went out.

He had not squeezed the hospital or his name into the opening. He had not demanded recognition of his altered voice.

[You coughed first. Then you said you were sitting.]

He closed his eyes briefly.

[Do you want to say more?]

“Yes,” he said aloud, forgetting the channel had closed. Yu Mei's mouth moved slightly, but she neither laughed nor immediately typed.

“Write: I'd like to say a few more things.”

The person inside asked why he was sitting. Was he hurt? He mentioned his wrist, shoulder, and difficulty using his legs, without dictating a catalog of injuries.

[Then don't stand up to find me.]

There was no bed beyond the interface, no pair of his sister's shoes, nobody he could lift into the space beside his chair. The original reference still glowed beneath the receiving symbol.

“She heard me this time.”

Yu Mei nodded, eyes red. She had been twisting sideways to avoid pressing the wrong keys, leaving her sore shoulder unsupported.

“Rest.”

She sat against the nearby wall. He could see the interface but could not reach it. He had expected to panic over that small distance. Instead, another question appeared.

[Is the person outside tired?]

He asked Fang Ji to explain that Yu Mei needed a break. Fang Ji came within reading distance and typed with his left hand held low. The few words took time. He lowered his arm immediately afterward.

[Then I'll wait.]

“We don't have to rush an answer to that,” Yu Mei said, wiping her face.

Fang Ji moved a fold of her clothing clear of her heel. Her torn sleeve was still knotted, her palm still marked from the wheelchair handles. Everything that required them to move slowly remained. For the first time, his sister was slowing down with them.

Laughter sounding like Xiao Lu came from the circular room.

Lin Mo checked the audio marker. Closed. The reply field had not changed. The laugh moved away beneath the stone rail, trailed by other voices urging something on. He did not reopen the channel to collect a comparison.

He had Fang Ji explain that other sounds were coming from outside. They would warn her before opening audio again.

[All right. Don't go chasing voices either.]

He thought of the unfinished message at the hospital: Have you eaten? It seemed impossibly far away, beyond faces he could no longer fully recognize. He did not insert it into this conversation.

“What else?” Fang Ji asked.

Lin Mo looked at Yu Mei's phone pocket. He wanted photographs of every reply, beginning with the question mark. If another counter denied this person existed, he could put the images in front of it.

Instead, he asked whether he could keep a copy of their conversation.

[Where? Who else will see it?]

He had thought about proof, but not about whom he needed to convince. Too many people here treated keeping something as a kindness requiring no further question. First a voice, then fear, then even refusal became a little square available for replay.

“On my phone. I'm afraid of losing this connection. I wasn't planning to show it to the windows outside.”

Fang Ji typed in two stretches, resting his shoulder between them. Lin Mo waited rather than passing the remaining words to Yu Mei.

The person inside asked whether the phone could send the images away by itself.

“No signal now. I can't use that as a guarantee once we're out.”

He felt as though he were pushing away the only reassurance within reach. He almost suggested photographing just the text and keeping the phone offline. But he did not know what this place could add to an image, and he could not decide that written words mattered less.

[Don't photograph my words yet. You can note what you asked and how far you got.]

His jaw tightened, then relaxed.

“All right.”

Fang Ji did not question the difficulty. Yu Mei left the phone in her pocket. After resting his arm, Fang Ji wrote on the back of the query sheet: the original reference's page, questions exchanged, refusal given, one utterance received. He did not copy I'm here too.

Lin Mo asked him to add: Not brought out.

The paper had room for that fact. It hurt to read, but it was closer to what he had accomplished than a photograph hurriedly presented as a reunion.

Only then did he ask whether she would like to see a small part of the doorway. There was no hurry to open the picture.

Fang Ji sent the question and returned to the corridor wall.

The reply took a long time.

[Can you tell me what I'll see first?]

Lin Mo looked at the footrest. A little skin above the back of his left shoe was still red. Against the wall, Yu Mei slowly uncurled her fingers.

“Write: one corner of the chair. She can choose which part.”
