# Chapter 158: That Route Could Not Carry Her

When Yu Mei woke, she touched her shoulder first.

She had slept against the wall only briefly. Neither Lin Mo nor Fang Ji had called her. He Cen sat with his eyes closed. Since slipping sideways, he hadn't tried to bend down again; the capped water stood within easy reach. Occasionally a shoe touched the bundle, making an empty bottle crackle.

“Where's the bypass?” Yu Mei asked.

“So far, we've only seen a symbol.”

He turned the query sheet for her. Their notes had used most of its blank back. Beside the symbol was a question mark, not an arrow pointing to an exit. She pressed the curling edge flat. Beyond the doorway, she would inspect the bypass herself. The chair should not follow her farther in.

He agreed. Once her shoulder had eased and Fang Ji was settled by the shelter entrance, they returned to the gray door. They left audio closed. In the original read-only query, a line extended from the symbol toward the back of the reference frame.

It was thicker than the export line, with an outward arrow at its end. Lin Mo's pulse quickened.

“What does it say?”

“Diversion.”

Below was an old interface directory, listing positions and uses without identifying any people within. The bypass wasn't gray. It looked easier than complete export. A light marked its outer destination.

He told her not to connect anything. First, establish what it did.

Fang Ji could see one corner behind the frame from the passage. Yu Mei moved two steps sideways without crossing the stone rail. Lin Mo remained outside, his view half blocked, listening to her description.

Two metal labels stood above the low channel. One read Diversion. Only part of the other survived, suggesting storage. Each had a small port beneath it, no platform for a phone or cup, no doorway for a body. The right-hand line entered the wall beside the column used by their temporary store.

“And the left?”

She didn't answer immediately. Changing her angle, she caught a fragment beneath her shoe, stopped, and stepped back. Hearing the hesitation, he told her not to lean closer.

The old display frame obscured the writing. Moving it would require shifting the whole sheet of glass. His former face remained lit within it.

His left fingers curled.

He had once been reluctant to slide that face aside. Now he had a perfectly legitimate reason to touch it again: checking for another route. The central darkness rose slightly, as though it understood.

“Come back. Rest your hand.”

Could the frame move farther? Yu Mei said the side channel ended there. They would have to lift it out. Her shoulder couldn't manage it. Neither could Fang Ji. The seatkeeper moved to the passage turn and offered his hands, while admitting his leg wouldn't support him securely.

“No lifting,” Lin Mo said.

He had spoken too quickly. He took a breath. An unidentified port wasn't reason enough to put heavy glass against someone's injuries. They should first look for its location in the directory.

Yu Mei expanded the side of the diagram. The left port had a number. She matched the right-hand temporary-storage port first, then found the left-hand entry.

[Viewing-copy distribution]

The thick line ended beneath those words.

He still asked her to check the position again. Fear of touching his old face couldn't justify dismissing a real exit. The visible edge of the label, the gap between the ports, and the right-hand channel matched. The left port distributed display copies.

His old face turned toward him.

“Come over. You can take it.”

He looked at the directory, then at the actual wheel. The face offered no different receiving endpoint, no explanation of what he could take. It waited before the place he had been unable to inspect with his own hands.

He asked Yu Mei to return without lifting the frame.

During the inspection he had wanted the wheelchair moved slightly left so he could see the markings beneath the port himself. Half a turn had altered the pull from his footrest. His right knee hurt sharply, forcing them to stop.

The new angle showed him only more of the glass's back edge. The marks remained hidden. His body had paid before he gained anything. Yu Mei asked whether to continue. He shook his head, had her set the brake, and waited for the pain to ease.

She neither said she had known it was pointless nor moved farther to satisfy his urgency.

Sweat ran inside his wrist support. Unable to scratch, he asked her to touch only the outer edge with dry cloth, inserting nothing beneath it.

“I'll tell you what you can't see,” she said afterward.

She wasn't promising never to miss anything. Her shoulder hurt; dust could obscure letters. He would have to compare her observations with identifiable markings instead of treating his own physical proximity as the only reliable inspection.

“Tell her?” Yu Mei asked.

“Yes. She asked us to look for this first.”

Once they had reconnected, he described the two ports. The frame partly obscured the left one, but its visible position matched the directory. Its stated purpose was distributing viewing copies. He did not declare that no other route existed anywhere, or claim they had removed the glass.

He hesitated at the part where his old face blocked the view.

“Was somebody there talking to you?” Xiao Lu asked.

Only then did he realize he had omitted the voice.

“Something outside. Using my old face.”

“Did you go?”

“The chair's still outside the doorway.”

“Did you want to?”

He looked at the displaced frame. This was harder than reading any condition. Yes, he said. He had genuinely thought lifting it might reveal a different answer.

He waited for a warning or reassurance.

“Then don't leave that part out.”

“All right.”

She was no longer simply waiting for his results. She wanted to know how he reached them. If he protected her from every disappointment, he would still be giving her a page selected for her in advance.

Yu Mei proposed checking the diversion with an empty model. Xiao Lu agreed to inspect only the interface, without supplying her contents. Yu Mei joined the bypass endpoint to the outside of the complete-export frame.

The line lit briefly and stopped.

[Static output only]

She enlarged the capacity field. The model's square grew, but no continuing line extended from its end. Complete continuity remained unestablished. Even if the port could receive more pictures or words, the problem they had identified involved more than a small container.

Could the source keep sending, Xiao Lu asked? Another sentence after the first?

Lin Mo didn't tell her they had already discussed this. She hadn't seen their earlier diagrams. He, too, had confused separate transfers with a continuing route. Yu Mei opened the description of continuous output, still using an empty model.

A second square followed the first. Then a third. Outside, the row lengthened. The source frame stayed inside, attached to the output by a gray line. The description called this continuous generation of copies. It didn't provide an exit for the source.

He explained the difference.

“Could it make somebody outside who answers, while I stay here?”

He would not declare unseen things impossible. This interface's output type offered no current receiving endpoint. Their one copied sentence had, in fact, failed to receive what they said afterward.

“Then don't call it me coming out.”

“I won't.”

He felt suddenly tired. Each proposal could give him something precious, except the person who asked him not to omit anything. He already had a voice. He might obtain much more. Those were not additional steps toward the same result.

From the passage, the seatkeeper said old scenes had allowed accumulated viewing to be exchanged for longer clips.

Fang Ji looked at him. The man clarified that this was only his memory. He didn't know whether it applied here. But if the condition said equivalent, wouldn't everything in his old account be more than one person's spoken words?

Lin Mo did not ask for his account number. Xiao Lu fell quiet too, waiting to hear whether he thought it worth testing.

“First we check what it measures.”

Yu Mei expanded equivalence. There were no pictures of wounds, no ranking by number of scenes watched. The two frames measured continuity capacity. One was insufficient; the other unspecified. Static records remained among the excluded types.

At his request she brought both frames onto the same page without opening anyone's experiences. Their shapes differed. Only the line across their capacity requirements was equal. The idea that one person's suffering must match another's had partly come from that line, and partly from what they had brought to it.

The seatkeeper said quietly that he understood.

“You don't have to list everything you watched,” Fang Ji told him.

The man lowered his eyes. His hand rested beside his injured leg without offering its pain as another number. Lin Mo heard the faint channel noise. He would not ask his sister how much she had suffered inside so they could decide whether she merited somebody outside staying behind.

“It's measuring whether the source can support it,” he told her. “Not who deserves to leave.”

“How much is enough?”

They checked the lower limit. The empty retention model lit only when it matched the complete export's capacity requirement. Below the marked level, transfer remained unavailable. No unit converted memories into years, ages, or names. Lin Mo supplied none.

Could several people each leave a little, Fang Ji asked? It was a question, he added, not his name for the field.

Yu Mei examined the type description for distributed static items. The result still required an established continuing source. A collection of old fragments could not be assembled into somebody who had consented.

They lacked the means to prove anything about complex combinations the interface hadn't offered. Lin Mo would not invent further options simply to make his conclusion exhaustive. They had reached the end of the visible bypass.

Xiao Lu wanted a break.

He said they would return to the shelter and would not quietly open somebody else's candidacy during the interval. She waited for him to finish before ending. On the query sheet, Yu Mei had room only for the bypass's purpose and Not Executed. The margin no longer allowed expansive notes.

A small flake of dust shifted beside the wheel as they left. Lin Mo checked the floor joint; the chair went around it. He wanted to say at least they hadn't damaged her today, then disliked how that made his sister an object whose intactness ought to be enough.

“Find a new route?” He Cen asked.

“An old bypass. Viewing copies only.”

“So a wasted trip?”

Lin Mo looked at the nearly full sheet.

“We checked it. Now we don't need to use a person to test that route.”

He Cen nodded and made room for Yu Mei. She lowered herself slowly, arm against her abdomen, unable yet to reach for the water.

Lin Mo waited for her to recover. He didn't ask her to turn the next page.
