# Chapter 178: Don't Straighten Her

After Xiao Lu found her knee, the handprint on the wall moved upward.

Lin Mo looked from the disturbed dust to her shoulder. The shoulder hadn't risen. A fine line tightened beside her neck as though someone behind her were lifting her collar. She tried to inhale and couldn't finish. Her right outline straightened while the side nearest the wall remained crooked.

“I'm not standing up.”

He swallowed the command to crouch.

No new fragments lay on the floor. Fang Ji's shoe remained clear, Yu Mei sat behind the chair, He Cen had withdrawn his legs. Nobody touched Xiao Lu. The straightening lines extended from the gray surface behind her, connected to the metal arm. Her fingertips dragged across the real wall at an awkward angle, lengthening the dusty marks.

“Can you take your hand away?”

“Yes. But then there's nowhere—”

She stopped. Her face tipped forward, lips blanching. She was trying to hold something that didn't move with the image, but loose dust wasn't a handrail. It couldn't support weight that hadn't yet arrived completely.

“Let go first. The wall won't run away.”

She released one finger. The others shook harder. Swearing at something, she finally withdrew her hand to her chest. Her upper body straightened another fraction.

Two sounds came from the arm: the persistent fine scraping and a shorter, lighter noise every few breaths, like stiff paper being flattened. Lin Mo leaned toward the port, trying to locate that rhythm. The words blurred into overlapping lines.

Yu Mei told him not to fall forward.

His forehead was almost against the armrest. Neither injured wrist nor shoulder could jerk him upright. She steadied the chair while he eased himself back, without pulling his arms. With his head against the backrest, the words separated again.

“No. Don't keep your hand up too long.”

“Already aching.”

Fang Ji lowered it and blocked the glare with his body instead, visibly irritated but remaining in place. Lin Mo meant to thank him. Different words emerged.

“You don't have to listen to me.”

“Why say that now? I'll tell you when you're wrong. Didn't I just tell you I couldn't reach?”

Lin Mo couldn't locate that particular remark among all the similar scraping sounds. He found the armrest with his fingertips, hoping touch would hold the present voices steady. An old notch restored nothing, only confirmed where he sat. He asked Fang Ji to repeat the last part.

“You'll tell me when I'm wrong.” He repeated it before returning to the receiving lines.

Body Position wasn't a new switch. Its outline filled upward as reception continued. Each time Xiao Lu bent her knee, the upright figure lost a section, then promptly repaired itself.

Lin Mo didn't treat the label as a button that solved everything. He asked whether its border was within the port or moving with the outer light. Fang Ji turned his head and winced at his shoulder, then used only his eyes.

“In the light. Gray underneath. No words.”

The seatkeeper offered his leg for her to lean against.

“You can barely stand,” Yu Mei said.

“Not stand on me. Lean.”

Annoyed, he looked to Xiao Lu for permission. His injured leg shook beneath his trousers. Good intentions didn't make every offered support usable. She refused. He withdrew his knee, mouth tight, and didn't offer twice.

Her back was near the real wall, but whenever she tried to sit, the upright image shifted her shoulders forward, choosing the best position to display her face. Sweat gathered on her nose. Her actual eyes remained fixed on the ground below the wall.

Lin Mo remembered the direction of the dusty handprint.

“When you touched the wall, did the line behind you move?”

“Which line? I don't know.”

He corrected himself. “Don't answer. I'll watch.”

He didn't make her test it. When she reached for the wall again, the gray connection behind her arm curved aside while the upright figure held forward. A thin brightness stretched between them, revealing concrete behind it.

There was no real chair there, no solid supporting hand. The brightness only covered her pose as she tried to settle.

“Yu Mei, can you see the actual wall behind her?”

“A little.”

“Don't hold her shoulder. Let her lean herself. Tell her how far.”

Yu Mei stood, paused until her knees straightened, then moved sideways. Without entering the gray boundary, she crouched where she could see wall and back. She had to release the chair; Fang Ji braced his shoe outside its locked rear wheel, in case she borrowed its support without thinking.

“A little farther,” she said.

“How much is a little?”

“About the length of your finger. I'm looking at the wall, not that shining skin.”

Xiao Lu turned toward her. The smiling image followed a beat later, still tidy. The real girl wrinkled her nose and drew a broken breath. Her back had no strength left.

The seatkeeper indicated the water. What little remained barely covered the bottle's bottom. Lin Mo's throat hurt too. Swallowing scraped his inner cheek against a tooth, bringing a trace of blood. But offering a bottle to a mouth still shifting between two positions wasn't giving her a drink.

“After she's sitting.”

The cap turned. Xiao Lu looked toward the sound.

“There's water?”

“A little. Not a bottleful.”

Better to disappoint her now. Her reaching hand stopped and returned beside the wall. She swore quietly. Nobody told her to save her strength, as if even that required permission.

Yu Mei's mouth moved, not quite smiling, more like unclenching her teeth. She told Xiao Lu where the bottle was and promised to pass it after she was steady. Xiao Lu understood. Then she warned nobody to spit out their own last mouthful for her.

“Couldn't drink it if we did,” Fang Ji said.

Her glare was too brief to become a complete picture. She still had energy to dislike an ill-timed remark.

“Don't push backward, then,” Yu Mei said. “Put your hand lower. Touch what you can.”

It wasn't a whole solution. Nevertheless, Xiao Lu stopped trying to fit her shoulders into the upright image. Her palm traveled down the real wall. Dust fell between her fingers. The continuous gray lines bent without breaking.

A foot appeared below.

Its toes turned inward, landing awkwardly. The displayed foot pointed neatly ahead. Xiao Lu couldn't see underneath herself; hearing Lin Mo inhale sharply, she demanded to know what happened.

“Your foot touched the ground. Crooked. Don't straighten it.”

She paused, as though disbelieving. Her toes curled against the concrete, gathering dust into an arc. The upright figure split at the knee. The paper-flattening sound suddenly accelerated.

Yu Mei drew back. Xiao Lu cried out as something pulled her, palm pressed to the wall, shoulder tilting. Darkness washed across Lin Mo's sight. He tried reaching with his left hand and only hurt his shoulder.

“Don't pull me!” Xiao Lu shouted.

Nobody had touched her. He withdrew immediately anyway.

Thin light rose beside her foot, covering the crooked knee. The closer it came, the tighter her real toes curled. He didn't know what she endured at the source. A foot touching ground wasn't the loose end of a rope they could drag out.

“We aren't pulling. I moved, but didn't touch. What about your side?”

“Like clothes caught on something. Not clothes.” Her breath shook. “I can't explain.”

“Then don't.”

He asked Fang Ji to check the source wording. Still not released. The receiving shape hadn't reset; continuity remained active. They didn't know what withdrawing the connection would do. He didn't tell anyone to wrench the metal arm away simply because he was afraid.

Yu Mei watched the actual wall, quietly telling Xiao Lu her back had reached it.

She closed her eyes. One shoulder touched concrete, the other still suspended. Instead of making herself symmetrical, she used that contact to sink sideways. The grounded foot turned outward. Gray definition filled down her calf. Her mouth could no longer form whole words, only thin breaths.

The light kept trying to present her neatly.

Lin Mo looked away from her exposed face to the distance between her curled foot and the wall. Yu Mei watched her back; Fang Ji watched the dangerous ground. Nobody declared this ended the watching. Voices persisted inside the door, and other places still watched. They simply stopped demanding she look good for that figure.

At last, the side of her hip met the floor.

The sound was soft as clothing dropped in a heap. Lin Mo heard it distinctly. She leaned against the wall, right shoulder lower than left, legs not yet fully solid, gray connections taut behind her. The upright image disappeared outward from the middle of its face, leaving an empty long frame.

She didn't smile.

“Hurts.”

Yu Mei asked where. Xiao Lu begged her not to ask yet, voice almost gone. Lin Mo waited for her breathing and discovered his own came faster. Things he'd thought he could understand depended entirely on her telling him. Knowing he was her brother supplied none of the hands she needed now.

“I'm here.”

She opened her eyes slightly, perhaps about to answer. In the end she only murmured agreement.

He didn't repeat himself. Yu Mei's legs had gone numb; she stood slowly against the real wall, avoiding Xiao Lu's unfinished outline. Fang Ji moved his shoe away from the rear wheel. The seatkeeper studied the empty frame and gradually relaxed his own rigid back.
