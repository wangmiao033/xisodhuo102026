# Chapter 162: Knowing Was Not Remembering

Lin Mo wanted to write his own name.

Fang Ji placed the pen in his left hand without arranging every finger. It touched the old injury on his forefinger. He tried another grip and immediately slipped. The paper shifted on his lap, leaving the first character as a long, wandering stroke.

“Something firm underneath,” Yu Mei said.

She closed his notebook and used it as backing. Old Chen's pages stayed tucked at the rear, unopened. Lin Mo held the edge of the separate sheet and slowly completed his surname. The next character crowded beneath it like a component that belonged to something else.

He almost laughed.

“Nobody could identify a signature from that.”

“As long as you recognize it,” Fang Ji said.

That was precisely what might not be possible later. Lin Mo asked him to write the same name clearly beside it, noting which was his own left-handed attempt and which Fang Ji had copied at his request. Neither promised recognition later.

On the blank reverse, he wrote Twenty-five. The pen hesitated. This was the age he could presently give, not a complete series of birthdays supported by photographs and dates. Special Forces crowded beneath it.

Yu Mei heard his breathing and told him to rest the pen.

He did not rush to add Man. He had said it so often: before mirrors, onstage, at a gate that sorted him by shape. He knew who he was. Now he needed to learn whether written facts could be separated from the experiences within him.

After resting, they returned to the doorway. Xiao Lu asked about bottles, then heard the bad parts first. Sensation within the retained structure remained uncertain. Outside, autobiographical associations might be lost. Keeping a name would not guarantee recognizing people from before.

“You're going to check yourself?”

“Types and scope only. No display of the contents. No clearing.”

“Then ask. I'll listen. Don't read my things out to everybody.”

He would not use her scope instead of his own. Yu Mei read the individual-verification notice. It authorized reading the present scope; clearing was listed separately and remained locked. He had her read the entire explanation before speaking his agreement into the port.

Nothing pierced his forehead. No unfamiliar memory flooded in. Yet his chest tightened as though the absence of sensation meant they had missed a step.

Current Candidate Verified appeared above Not Executed.

He checked that the candidate was tied to the outside participant who had just agreed, not his former face in a distant window. No recommended stage identity appeared beside it. Yu Mei stayed in the scope summary without opening particular scenes.

Overlapping clusters appeared. The larger ones were labeled Autobiographical Self, Bodily Experience, and Relational Associations. An existing cognitive overwrite pulled one region into a doubled outline. The continued presence of a connection did not prove it true.

“Can it separate true from false?” Yu Mei asked.

“It doesn't say it can.”

A faint salty taste rose from his sore cheek. The implanted bed, rehearsal, and stranger's collar had not become a clean, authentic past merely because he had reached the core. If he discarded everything he disliked as false, he might discard things he had actually done wrong.

Xiao Lu asked whether it was large.

More complicated than the example, he said. He could not read the separate experiences. He did not compare the length with hers or claim his past was exactly enough to exchange for one person.

The retention categories distinguished factual information from learned procedures. Keeping a name, age, or professional knowledge did not preserve the feeling of having lived the associated events.

He asked Yu Mei to enter the three lines from his paper as proposed retained facts, assessment only.

[Lin Mo]
[Twenty-five]
[Special Forces]

The entries glowed without the hands he had held, the roads he had run, or the people who had called his full name.

He tried to remember the first time his sister had shouted it. She stood near a door, hair untidy. When he tried to look farther, the door changed color. He stopped without offering the scene for everyone to certify.

“Would I still know how to avoid danger?”

The explanation said retained procedures did not ensure the current body's ability to perform them. Injuries and existing overwrite remained separate factors. He looked at the splint. Remembering how to brace himself would not let that wrist support him. A learned movement might also remain when he could no longer remember who had taught it.

“Don't write that I'd still be able to fight.”

Yu Mei did not smile. She recorded the distinction.

The next entry was not a switch for sex. Under Autobiographical Self, an Existing Male-Self Association cluster reached into many regions. Lin Mo requested only its boundary summary. Connections crossed childhood, training, and later forced changes. Other people's relationship markers hung from some of them.

“It connects to her too,” Yu Mei said quietly.

“I heard,” Xiao Lu answered.

He did not signal Yu Mei to be quiet. If the price had to be concealed from his sister to seem courageous, he would rather make it ugly now.

“The experiences in which I've been your brother might break away too.”

“Would you still know I'm your sister?”

“I can ask to keep that fact. Knowing it won't bring every day back.”

She did not offer to tell him the stories afterward. The silence lasted. His left hand tightened, then opened. He almost wished she would shout at him. An argument might still sound like something they could change.

“If somebody tells you afterward, might you think they're lying?”

He studied the crooked characters. They had no power to make a person without a past trust the person who wrote them.

“I might.”

“Then write that you don't have to believe.”

Yu Mei looked up. Lin Mo waited until she saw him nod. A future reader need not accept the page at once, he said. They could check where things happened and ask whoever remained. His name on the paper should not make every claim true.

Something scraped against the wall. Fang Ji called from the corridor for them to check the floor.

A thin light swept along the seam toward the threshold without touching the wheel. Yu Mei withdrew from the controls and moved beside him. Inside the light, a man held a pen. His posture was straight; his wrist unbroken.

Lin Mo recognized the outline of his former body. He did not recognize the event.

The man was signing.

“I haven't signed,” he said, for Xiao Lu and Fang Ji.

Not Executed remained on the interface. On the floor, his image pressed a glowing confirmation. Yu Mei did not follow it with the chair. She eased him back far enough to keep the advancing light from his left foot.

The man bowed his head in apparent relief.

Nausea rose in Lin Mo. He did not demand that everybody declare the scene false, as though agreement could erase it from him. Fang Ji said only that he had seen Lin Mo reading explanations, not performing a clearing.

The light moved on.

Yu Mei checked the scope page again. The three proposed facts remained. Assessment Only was unchanged. At Lin Mo's request, she added Xiao Lu Is My Sister. A notice warned that a relationship fact could be proposed for retention while lived associations remained subject to the actual clearing scope.

Brother would not save their whole past by itself.

He asked whether he could clear only the experiences he was willing to lose and leave the rest untouched. Without supplying particular memories, they checked what such exclusions would do to the continuing structure.

Yu Mei applied a boundary exclusion to the empty example. Connections separated. The retention side did not show integrity; it showed Insufficient Scope.

He had almost imagined selecting the worst days, paying once, and keeping the better ones. This result said only that his preferred selection might not work.

“You can't cut it where you like,” Yu Mei said.

“We can't guarantee it. We've checked this example.”

He knew he wanted to keep trying. Every new arrangement would postpone admitting the cost. Xiao Lu asked whether they needed to examine all of them immediately. Her voice sounded rough, perhaps because of the channel, perhaps because she was tired. He did not require her to establish which.

“Not now.”

“Are you afraid you'll regret it?”

“Yes.”

“Then write that you're afraid. Don't turn yourself into someone who never was by the time you've finished.”

Yu Mei left that sentence to him rather than copying it as another rule. The figure signing on the floor had never hesitated. It had looked like an ending somebody else had completed. He did not want the last version of himself in the notebook to look like that too.

He asked Yu Mei to exit the assessment without retaining an automatic pending action. Candidate history remained available, but execution was empty. Agreement to examine had not unlocked agreement to clear.

“Now I understand why you wouldn't tell me it didn't matter,” he told Xiao Lu.

“You shouldn't only understand now.”

“No. I was still looking for words that would make it easier to say.”

She did not reassure him. Cloth moved against his right knee. A precise fear arrived: someone might call him Lin Mo and he would turn, without remembering his sister shouting the name across a street.

“I want to write the past down.”

“For whom?”

“For the version of me who might not remember. A copy for you too, if you want it. I'll ask before using your things.”

She said he could start with himself. He must not explain every time she had been angry as reluctance to part with him. Sometimes she had simply been angry.

He laughed, hurting the inside of his cheek.

“I remember that.”

“Write it.”

He agreed without having Yu Mei immediately produce a beautiful farewell. First he stated his decision: he was willing to prepare this approach and investigate the male-self autobiographical associations it required. He would keep the opportunity to stop. Today's assessment was not final consent to execution.

Xiao Lu said she understood. Could he finish a page before telling her, rather than making her lose him again with every line?

“Yes.”

After audio closed, Yu Mei remained standing. He asked whether her shoulder hurt.

“Yes. And I'm angry. I don't know with whom.”

He did not select a target for her. After a rest, she pushed him back. His badly written name stayed separate from the query and the record of non-execution. A drop of sweat fell beside the second character. The ink did not dissolve.

He asked Fang Ji to open the notebook at a genuinely blank page.

It would not lie flat. He held one corner; the other rose. Fang Ji weighted it with the same small stone they had used before. The pen slipped from Lin Mo's fingers. He asked for it back, then waited before trying to grip it again. His palm was wet before he had managed much.

He did not want someone inventing his past. He did need someone helping him put it on paper. Both facts would have to stand.

This time he did not begin with I am a man.

After a long hesitation, he wrote that his sister had once refused to carry an umbrella for him.
