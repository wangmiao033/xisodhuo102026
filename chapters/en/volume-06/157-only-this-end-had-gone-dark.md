# Chapter 157: Only This End Had Gone Dark

On the third flicker, only half a word came through.

Yu Mei's hand rose. Lin Mo stopped her from requesting another connection. The source reference remained, verification had not turned red, but a little light beneath the arm was out. She leaned to read, cast her own shadow across the text, and stepped back.

[Near-end connection unstable]

“Our side,” she said.

He heard her. He called Xiao Lu into the opening anyway. No answer. Before he could call again, his teeth caught his sore cheek. He looked down at his left hand gripping the armrest, the injured finger trembling.

“End this exchange first.”

Disconnect responded: Connection Ended. Yu Mei left the complete-export page untouched. Neither end had been established; losing sound had not joined the broken line.

“Brother.”

Lin Mo looked up sharply. The voice had come from the center. He knew its direction, yet it felt closer than the little opening beside the door. It asked why he wouldn't answer, as though its first word had been waiting a very long time.

Yu Mei stood before him. She said his name, then asked whether they should retreat.

He saw her knotted sleeve, the old burn beneath it. Familiar objects slowly brought his attention down. He asked to stay here without moving the arm. She leaned against the wall while he recovered enough breath to speak.

Fang Ji called from the corner. Lin Mo explained that their end had lost sound. They did not yet know what had happened at Xiao Lu's end. Fang Ji relayed this to He Cen and stayed where he was.

Keeping the original source, Yu Mei checked the near-end status. Text input still lit, but Send also reported no connection. She lowered her hand instead of repeatedly pushing a word against it.

“More than the audio.”

Lin Mo nodded. If both used the same connection, typing wouldn't create another route. He had already thought of asking Fang Ji to type, as though more hands could bridge the break.

The arm's base was low. Yu Mei bent and immediately felt her shoulder pull. He asked her to straighten. From the chair, changing his angle slightly, he could see a white alignment mark. A small part protruded beyond the catch where it should have been enclosed. The arm had not fallen, but its end sagged.

“The catch is down,” she said.

“That doesn't mean the connection is seated. Don't force it back.”

An old arrow pointed toward a disconnected position. Below was an inspection cover with two screws, both secure. He told her to leave those alone and asked Fang Ji to check the alignment from the doorway.

Fang Ji bent only far enough to look, without supporting the metal. The marks were off by half a fingernail. He straightened immediately, sweat sliding beside his nose.

“You didn't hit it with the chair,” he told Yu Mei. “The base is out of reach.”

She was silent. Lin Mo could see how her hand had withdrawn. If he kept asking who had touched it, she might begin accusing herself of every press on every previous visit. The arm was old and had clicked during their exchanges. That justified inspection, not appointing somebody to confess.

The outside panel provided the instructions for the disconnected position: Disable Near End, Release Locator, Return Along Marked Arc. No hand had to enter the gray door. No consciousness appeared as repair material.

Still, he asked Yu Mei to check the main view first. Their read-only exchange had ended. Both viewing controls were off. Neither export endpoint had a candidate. Their only task now was the metal arm.

“I can turn it slowly,” she said. “I can't hold its weight for long.”

Beneath it was a short metal ledge, previously hidden by the extended arm. It met the end of the marked return arc and bore deep wear. Before releasing the catch, Yu Mei checked with an empty hand that it could support the end. Then she sat down to rest.

Waiting hurt. Had Xiao Lu heard his yes? Did she think he had simply stopped answering? The voice in the center supplied the rest, asking how long she had been abandoned again.

He watched the wheel's chipped paint. He wanted to tell Yu Mei it would take only a moment. The thumb of the hand that had supported He Cen was pressing into her trembling palm.

“Rest first.”

“I know. You can breathe too.”

He exhaled and admitted he was afraid. So was she, she said, then closed her eyes and actually rested. Understanding each other did not return the arm to its socket.

During the repair Fang Ji only read out positions. His injured palm stayed against his abdomen; his other arm did no lifting. Yu Mei disabled the near end, waited for the status to settle, then raised the locator catch and slowly turned the arm along the marks.

It moved easily at first. Halfway, it caught.

“Don't force it.”

She stopped. Her shoulder slowly dropped. Holding it longer would hurt, she said. Fang Ji could see the end above the support ledge. At his direction she reversed it slightly and let it settle. Metal clicked. She pulled her arm away as soon as the weight left her hand.

Lin Mo's heart raced. They couldn't know whether forcing it would have broken something. What they could see was the white mark aligned after the arm settled, with a locator tooth still partly engaged.

Once the pain eased, Yu Mei raised the catch the rest of the indicated distance, keeping her fingers out from under the tooth. The arm returned smoothly. She rested it on the ledge and secured the catch without immediately extending it again.

The outside light flashed.

[Near end ready]

Lin Mo checked the source reference first. Its final groups were unchanged. Ready meant their side could operate; it did not tell them whether anybody would answer. He said this aloud.

The seatkeeper called Fang Ji from farther down the passage. A light in the low wall channel looked like an old standby port.

Lin Mo told him not to touch it. The two men examined it from different angles. It was a reflection of the arm's indicator. When the seatkeeper held his hand outside the channel, the light vanished. Removing his hand restored it. His fingers never entered.

“I thought I'd seen it before.”

Fang Ji told him to separate what he saw from what he thought. The man sat again with his injured leg extended. His help hadn't produced a new passage; it hadn't made him owe them for the time, either.

Lin Mo remembered the location without asking anyone to dismantle the wall. He used to follow a cable toward its other end. Here, a reflection could become that cable. Seeing something go inward didn't establish whom it reached.

After another rest they returned the arm to its position a little over a foot inside the threshold. Catch fully engaged, white marks aligned. Yu Mei released it and waited through a dozen breaths. The light remained steady. The chair stayed outside.

They checked the transcription and sent the original connection request.

Lin Mo did not call immediately. When Existing Item Responding appeared, he had Yu Mei type that their outside connection had failed and they had stopped to inspect it.

[You only said yes.]

He closed his eyes briefly.

[Then I asked and you didn't answer. I thought you'd gone.]

Yu Mei's voice roughened as she read. He explained that he had remained there. They had ended the exchange to repair the outside arm; only now were they asking again. She typed without adding don't be afraid.

Had they begun moving Xiao Lu to repair the connection?

“No. Your source wasn't entered for export. We repaired the metal arm beside the door.”

She asked him to wait again.

The central voice still spoke, always faster than her replies. It never needed to wait for pain or permission. It could endlessly supply the next words he feared would not come.

At last Xiao Lu agreed to sound.

“I don't like it when you disappear.”

“Neither do I.”

“Will it happen again?”

“It might. We found a loose connection. It's steady since we reset it. I can't promise it won't loosen again.”

After a pause she asked whether a retained person could be lost inside if they disconnected like that.

Lin Mo asked Yu Mei to compare retention continuity with their near-end exchange, without choosing a person. In the diagram, the arm's connection opened and closed at the outer edge. The retained source entered the data layer elsewhere.

[Conversation may be interrupted]

[Near-end reconnection cannot replace retention continuity]

He read both lines. Xiao Lu said she had continued thinking, waiting, and becoming angry while the sound was gone. Losing the conversation hadn't carried her outside. It was her account of this interruption, not proof that every interruption was harmless.

“I got to seven,” she said.

“Seven what?”

“Times I wanted to ask if you were there. I didn't ask the first six. I was afraid you'd give me half an answer again.”

He looked at the thumb-sized opening. He wanted to promise he would always wait until she finished. Screws, catches, and tired people would not stop failing because he promised.

“If it breaks again, I'll tell you what we did outside when I return.”

“Tell me whether you left. Not just what the connection did.”

“All right. I didn't leave this time. I stayed outside the threshold.”

She said he could also tell her when he wanted to go, and for how long. He agreed without treating the request as proof she was no longer afraid.

They could now see that speaking into the port all day would not provide the required source within the layer. Keeping a wheelchair by the door, repeatedly opening audio, did not constitute retention.

“Would keeping the power on do it?” Yu Mei asked. She glanced toward her phone pocket and gave a bitter little laugh, without taking the device out.

The requirement still named current consciousness, not a power supply or a machine online.

“So fixing this arm hasn't given us a way back.”

“No.”

Xiao Lu asked whether the person outside had slipped again. Fang Ji heard from the passage and said he hadn't. Lin Mo relayed the answer as a statement about the present. Then, she said, the person who repaired the arm should sit down.

Yu Mei murmured agreement. Her hands rested on her lap, fingers closed for a long time. One local fault had frightened them this much. Whether someone actually transferred inside could stop at will had to be checked before anyone went.

“Next time we'll examine withdrawal,” Lin Mo said.

“First see whether there's a way without using anyone.”

He looked up. Beside the complete-scope opening, a small bypass symbol remained half hidden by expanded conditions. He didn't press it. Yu Mei marked its location on the query sheet's remaining margin and put the pen away.

Before they disconnected, Xiao Lu asked him to warn her first.

He said they were returning to the small room so the injured people could rest. She acknowledged him, then gave permission to end. Yu Mei waited for the words to finish before touching the checked button.

This time he had told her.
