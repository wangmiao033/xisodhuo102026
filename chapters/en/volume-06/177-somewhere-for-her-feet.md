# Chapter 177: Somewhere for Her Feet

The bottle's exposed end turned halfway over. The scraping stopped.

Lin Mo thought the gap had finally swallowed it. Looking down, he saw the base still protruding, lifted slightly by the bent metal. Upright white strips continued feeding downward behind it, buckling against the plastic. The outermost split resembled a mouth waiting for someone to mistake silence for safety and reach inside.

“It hasn't stopped.”

Fang Ji hadn't approached. Rubbing his left shoulder, he repeated the warning to He Cen against the wall. The feverish man opened his eyes twice before returning his heel to its previous position. Nobody had the strength to sacrifice another bottle testing the gap.

“Is the way blocked?” Xiao Lu asked.

“There's a trap below the door. Don't follow the sound down.”

“I can't find a way down.”

Her voice was strained. Half a face had become a whole one, but only her face and shoulders were clear. Below them, gray lines overlapped, exposing the door through their gaps. She felt something beneath her feet, she said. Pressing down was like stepping on another foot.

He didn't ask her to try harder.

He had assumed completed clearing meant only waiting remained. But waiting occupied space. His chair took one patch, Fang Ji's shoes another. Before Xiao Lu arrived, they had filled the ground she needed.

He asked what else the panel showed. Yu Mei moved sideways, revealing the corner her shoulder had hidden.

External Landing Area Occupied.

No name accompanied it. A small gray plane extended along the door's edge. He looked for a wheel in it and found a foot's outline instead, beside a line emerging from the arm's base. On the real floor, the seatkeeper's good right shoe rested over the faint gray glow.

“Me?” the man asked.

He withdrew his foot. His injured leg shifted with it, twisting pain across his face. The occupation warning remained.

Lin Mo didn't hurry him. A line still touched his shoe. He remembered the bag Fang Ji had moved when the seatkeeper changed places. Its empty strap lay flat in shadow, crossing the glow.

“The strap.”

Fang Ji found the end beside his own foot and drew it back slowly. He didn't reach beneath the door or jerk it free. Dust trailed behind it. Only when the last section cleared did the displayed footprint fade.

Xiao Lu cried out briefly.

Lin Mo tensed, but she said the surface beneath her had flattened.

No new floor appeared. The concrete remained. A boundary previously hidden within her outline opened outward and stopped along the wall. A narrow strip of bare ground separated it from the feed slot; the jagged metal still projected between them.

“The door isn't opening,” Yu Mei said.

“I see.” His answer sounded sharp. After a breath, he added, “I was waiting for it to.”

An opening door would have made sense: someone walked out, someone caught her. The difficult route they had taken hadn't changed into that easier one. The port, the receiving structure, the ground still supporting no new weight were what they actually had.

“Is the floor dry?” Xiao Lu asked.

“Dry. Dusty.”

“Don't put anything down for me.”

He had started suggesting the bag as padding. She heard its name.

“I haven't found the floor properly. Another layer might slide.”

He nodded, remembered she might not see, and answered aloud. Fang Ji moved the bag fully against the wall and gathered its strap. The empty bottles rattled. Xiao Lu said that helped; she could hear where they were moving.

“Where do you want to come down first?”

The outline sank a little, its shoulders crooked, like someone struggling into ill-fitting clothes. He couldn't know the sensation. Her face approached, her eyes repeatedly turning the same way.

“By the wall. Don't make me stand.”

Yu Mei looked at her injured shoulder, then at Fang Ji. Neither promised to catch her. Lin Mo could imagine supporting an elbow, a back, knees. The movements joined smoothly in his mind. No body here could perform them all.

“Sit first. There's room against the wall.”

“I have to find my legs first.”

He heard something close to furious embarrassment. He stopped directing her and asked Fang Ji to describe the actual ground. Fang Ji touched a raised patch away from the slot with his shoe. Rough beside the wall, smoother farther out; beyond that lay the dangerous metal.

Xiao Lu asked him to stop tapping. Too many sounds were obscuring the first one.

He stopped. Lin Mo watched the gray lines move over the empty patch.

A black speck sprang from the feed slot.

It landed inside the boundary with a tiny sound. He took it for grit until it flipped, revealing a fragment of a smiling mouth. The miniature lips opened repeatedly against the wall, beckoning to someone who hadn't yet arrived.

Xiao Lu's shoulder tipped toward it.

“Something fell out,” he said. “Below you.”

“Someone's telling me to step there.”

A gray line enclosed one corner of the fragment. He knew he had misidentified a hand earlier, but could no longer find its sensation. Now his eyes were misidentifying something else. One leg in the image stood perfectly straight. Xiao Lu was still saying she couldn't find her knee.

“Don't follow the straight one.”

“I'm not!”

He closed his mouth. Urgency had turned an observation into an order, as though she refused to distinguish the danger. The image's straight leg advanced; its toes passed silently through the metal.

“Fang Ji, don't tread on it. Does it move when the bottle does?”

The trapped bottle trembled. The black fragment slid back a little in the current of air, uncovering dry concrete beneath the glow. Xiao Lu heard the plastic and said something below her had loosened.

Lin Mo asked Yu Mei to turn the chair slightly, making space for the right footrest. She released the brake, waited for Fang Ji to clear the side wheel, and eased it around. Each movement jarred his knee. He waited through it without grabbing the arm, until she secured the brakes again.

From the new angle, Fang Ji could see the floor. Keeping his toe outside the bright boundary and clear of the jagged edge, he pushed the black fragment toward its original gap. His shoe interrupted the little mouth. Moving white strips caught the fragment and took it away.

The bottle shrieked again.

Fang Ji recoiled. White dust marked his shoe like an unerased bite. Only after sitting did he say it hadn't touched his foot. His voice was unusually high. Lin Mo didn't ask him to wipe the mark and check again.

“That wasn't me standing properly,” Xiao Lu said.

“I know.”

“You shouted as if I'd walked into it myself.”

He admitted the mistake. Retained training didn't make him understand every struggling person. He could order four people apart and still blame the one whose movements the image had stolen.

Yu Mei released the chair back and sat. Something clicked softly in her shoulder. He asked if she needed a rest. She glared and said she was already sitting.

He looked and couldn't answer.

Xiao Lu released a short laugh. Pain cut it off. Her shoulder withdrew, her mouth open. A hand emerged from the gray lines toward the wall.

Then Lin Mo remembered He Cen's foot.

The man had pulled it back earlier, but his knee had relaxed again, pointing his shoe toward the cleared ground. Lin Mo called his name. No response. At the sharper second call, He Cen startled awake and tried to push himself upright. His body suddenly sank, his head dropping with it.

Fang Ji caught his shoulder with his left forearm, unable to lift him. Both men lurched into the wall. Their cries tangled. Lin Mo could only watch them stop. The chair he'd moved to help Fang Ji see now left him too far away to reach either man.

“Stay down,” Yu Mei said. “Both of you.”

She checked their heads were clear of the door before telling the seatkeeper to move the bag and give Fang Ji room to extend his leg. He Cen slowly settled his back against the wall. His lips moved several times before he explained he'd only wanted to hear.

“I wasn't asking you to work,” Lin Mo said.

“Then call more quietly. I thought something had happened.”

Something almost had. Lin Mo understood the rebuke. Everybody awaited danger; a sharp call could make a body move before judgment. He described the shoe, asking for one small shift, no standing. He Cen slid his heel across the concrete and cleared the space.

Xiao Lu hadn't hurried them. Her hand waited at the gray boundary. When their breathing eased, she asked whether someone had fallen.

“Not down. Fang Ji caught him. They're both hurting.”

“I'll wait until they can breathe.”

Nobody promised it would take only seconds. Fang Ji withdrew his forearm, flexing his fingers. He waited until pain no longer shook his voice before saying he was ready. This time he checked his own footing first.

Xiao Lu's fingertips didn't pass through the wall.

They pressed a shallow mark into its dust. A few grains fell onto the real floor. She inhaled and said it was cold.

Lin Mo didn't reach to seize her. Source Not Released remained on the panel. Lines still connected the arm behind those fingers. Contact was not a whole person's arrival.

She moved her fingertips away, then touched the wall again.

“Still here.”

He couldn't tell whether she meant the wall or the part of herself finally able to approach it. He didn't ask. He told her no new fragments had appeared at its base.

Fang Ji studied his shoe. The seatkeeper coiled the bag strap on his lap again. He Cen stayed awake. They waited for the hand to stop trembling.

A bent line appeared below her outline.

This time it didn't straighten her for display. She said she'd found it, so softly that the door's friction nearly covered the words. Lin Mo didn't demand to know how much. A number meant to be encouraging could become another reason to push her onward.

He watched the bent line descend until she asked for a moment.

Yu Mei neither touched the clearing control nor closed the continuity. Clearing was finished. The old pause button wasn't a remedy for every need to rest. Xiao Lu simply stopped trying to press downward. The live lines continued holding what hadn't arrived.

“It's too cold here,” she said again.

He nearly promised it would be better outside. The wall was cold outside too. They had no blanket, no extra water; he couldn't even stand. Instead he asked if she wanted to withdraw her hand a little.

“No.”

Her fingertips moved slowly along the wall, leaving a short, crooked mark in the dust.

“At least this cold doesn't speak for me.”

Lin Mo looked at the mark she had made and left it untouched.
