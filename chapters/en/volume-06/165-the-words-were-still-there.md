# Chapter 165: The Words Were Still There

Fang Ji stopped at the boot story.

“Someone in your group here, a comrade later. Same person?”

“The same person as I remember him now.”

“Then no extra name.”

The notebook lay on the concrete ledge. Lin Mo sat close enough to read larger characters, but couldn't keep reaching to pull it closer. Fang Ji read each passage; he checked it. Repetition didn't resolve uncertainties. Some of his own freshly written characters were already difficult to recognize.

The clear copy went in front, the crooked original behind. Three incidents: the umbrella, the boot after training, the garment he had moved at home. None included Xiao Lu's recent replies. They hadn't brought out the photograph to authenticate them.

Should the introduction describe his present body? Yu Mei asked.

He looked at his splinted wrist. The forced changes belonged to his history. His former face mattered too. But an inventory of damage might teach his future self only how much the previous occupant resented every unfamiliar part.

“Say I was forcibly changed. Don't decide which parts someone should hate later.”

He asked Fang Ji to add that he still understood himself as a man and his sister's brother. If a future reader didn't feel the same, that reader could stop. Nobody needed to hurt their body to prove the paper true. Name, age, and occupation were proposed retained facts. Final execution remained undecided.

“So this diary could still change?”

“Yes.” Today's version couldn't sign for things that hadn't happened. Leaving that possibility open made it easier to continue.

He Cen called for a pause. Everyone turned. His fevered face rested against the gray clothing.

“If he wakes up with just Special Forces left, won't he think he has to stand immediately?”

Lin Mo remembered bracing against the armrest beside the door.

“He might.”

“Write that he can't. Don't just give him a name.”

Fang Ji asked how much to include before making a list. They settled on his right wrist, shoulder, and right knee being unable to bear normal weight when the record was made. Receiving help didn't create a debt payable with himself. They omitted individual causes and avoided making his companions' names into a list of people he must trust.

“You can write that I pushed you to the doorway,” Yu Mei said.

He would keep it in a paper note, he answered. Today's storage would contain his own three memories and the explanation, rather than her experiences too. She nodded without seeming excluded.

The introduction identified the text as Lin Mo's current account: some details unverified, not equivalent to complete memory.

“Who can guarantee you wrote it?” Fang Ji asked.

“You can say what you heard.”

“I heard you dictate it and saw you write some of it. I can't guarantee your past.”

“Then say that.”

The limited witness statement went on the original's reverse. It became no grander certification. A future Lin Mo might resent this page, resent its writer, even regard the familiar name as a deception. He left You don't have to believe this immediately instead of replacing it with Please remember.

From the wall, the seatkeeper asked whether possessing the index would help a stranger claim to be someone from his past.

Lin Mo almost answered no at once. The paper couldn't endorse anyone. But someone terrified of having no past might confuse access with trustworthiness.

“Add that finding this text doesn't give anyone the right to decide for me.”

Fang Ji looked at the seatkeeper. The man settled his injured leg and didn't ask for his contribution to appear in the diary.

Lin Mo extended the warning to people claiming to help recover his memories. The writer couldn't leave a command requiring obedience to himself, or use an earlier self to choose every future companion.

Yu Mei read the small addition, then flattened the curling page with the side of her hand.

After resting, they brought the clear copy to the doorway. The originals, photograph, and Old Chen's sheets remained in the room. Fang Ji sat where he could call to either end instead of standing throughout the transcription.

Yu Mei checked the test index first. Dust beside the wall remained retrievable, with no linked display. She opened a fresh field and typed as Lin Mo read in sections.

At the cracked handle, the field's edge lit up: Full.

She stopped. His chest went cold. He asked her to read the notice before cutting anything to fit. One draft page had filled; text continuation pages were available. The explanation linked written pages under one note. It did not attach a source or establish consciousness continuity.

“Different from not being able to transfer her in pieces,” Yu Mei said.

“Yes. These are words. Check that continuation doesn't lose any.”

The page preview preserved the final characters and continued with the next sentence. Full didn't require discarding an incident. Yu Mei kept her place with the pen tip rather than striking through everything already entered.

Her shoulder began hurting at the boot's heel. They stopped again.

Fang Ji offered to take over. She wanted to sit first and decide afterward. The draft remained uncommitted. For a moment, Lin Mo wished it would save itself and spare her the work. Yet that would take away his remaining choice about when to send it.

After the break, Fang Ji entered only a short section, left hand held low. He lowered his arm immediately afterward. One wrong character turned the sentence about not apologizing into something that made no sense.

“That needs changing.”

He corrected it, then read the whole sentence rather than announcing that the job was done. Lin Mo listened; Yu Mei checked the paper. The small error wasn't a dramatic trap. It was enough to establish that every page needed reading before submission.

Yu Mei finished the remaining text. The memories, introduction, and current bodily limitations were present. Xiao Lu's ongoing conversation had not slipped into them.

Lin Mo had considered a weighty title. At the name field, he chose Diary One.

“Will there be more?”

“If there's more to write.”

He couldn't promise time for every experience. These few short pages had left Yu Mei's shoulder drooping and Fang Ji unwilling to touch another key. Recording the past consumed the present time and strength of other people.

After checking every preview page and the still-unselected Linked Display box, Lin Mo asked her to store this text. Yu Mei pressed the control. A line advanced through the pages, pausing a long time before Stored appeared with a new index.

He didn't ask her to leave immediately.

Beside the result was No Receiving Endpoint. Like the bottle sentence, these words had found a place without creating someone to answer questions. He looked at the back of his hand. Pain remained. Thinking of the umbrella still warmed his ears. Storage hadn't removed those things from him.

“It only took the words.”

Yu Mei copied the index onto the clean sheet, carefully checking two easily confused characters. She read the final sentence, then returned to the opening. Both remained. But this was still the same interface immediately after writing.

“Close it.”

She left the writing point and returned to the arm's initial page. The draft area was empty. The diary preview vanished. Only after she withdrew her hand did Lin Mo ask her to open Read Back through the upper maintenance query, bypassing the writing field.

She entered the new index from the paper.

The narrow screen checked the circuit, then returned an error: Final Characters Do Not Match.

His heart dropped. He nearly asked her to return to the old port and save another copy. She was already shaking her head.

“Wait. I mistyped the end.”

A fine stroke was difficult to distinguish in this interface's font. She compared each character, reentered the complete index, and checked it before submitting.

Diary One appeared.

She read the opening while Fang Ji checked the paper from the corridor. She turned to the middle. The unnamed comrade remained unnamed. At the end came the garment, the lamp, and the absence of an explicit forgiveness.

Lin Mo asked her to check more than the title or length. They also verified the corrected sentence about apologizing.

Two access points had returned the same words after the writing session ended. That was what they had actually done. They hadn't left this circuit or returned to reality to establish retrieval elsewhere. He recorded the limit on paper instead of calling a second access point a backup outside the Theater.

Linked Display still read Not Established. The current recommendation list contained no Diary One. Yu Mei inspected the visible presentation frame and found none of their sentences. Farther away, images continued changing.

They could establish that this submission hadn't created a display, and report what the nearby frames showed. They couldn't prove all observation had been shut out.

“Does that count as hiding it?” Fang Ji asked.

“At least we didn't hand it to recommendations. We keep the index ourselves. I can't promise nobody will ever find it.”

Yu Mei left the readback and folded the clean copy without creasing the index. It could lead someone to the words. Losing it might lose the way altogether. Lin Mo asked her to copy it onto the original's reverse when they returned, with the two copies kept on separate pages. Nobody photographed the whole notebook or spent the phone's remaining charge on a check paper could support.

Before leaving, he reconnected with Xiao Lu. He told her only that the permitted stories were stored. Her voice, photograph, and current conversation hadn't gone with them.

“Does finishing mean you can come back?”

It took him a moment to understand. She meant the person who might no longer remember.

“I can't promise that. Paper or stored words can only give him something to read.”

“And you now?”

“I still remember. Nothing's been cleared.”

She didn't ask for the diary to be read aloud. Was he hoping to say that writing it down meant there was nothing left to fear?

He admitted he was. He had expected another copy to lighten fear like setting down a bundle. Instead, he had acquired an address that might remain when he wasn't there.

“I'm still afraid.”

She acknowledged that quietly. After a while she said she didn't want to hear about the umbrella again today.

“All right. Do you want to stop?”

“Yes. Ask about something else later.”

Yu Mei ended audio without entering the clearing page. The journey back was still a few meters, still dependent on her pushing slowly. Fang Ji took the clean copy and let her sit before adding the new index to the original.

Lin Mo looked again at his crooked first line. Another copy hadn't made this paper disposable. He had the notebook closed securely, with the original kept inside.

“Stored?” He Cen asked.

“This text. Stored and read back.”

“And the person?”

No foot had appeared beyond the doorway. Nobody was walking toward them.

“Xiao Lu is still inside.”

He Cen nodded and left the bottle toward the inside of the ledge. He offered no congratulations to a newly hidden diary.
