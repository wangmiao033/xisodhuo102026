# Chapter 166: Put Your Hand Down First

Someone called from outside as Lin Mo swallowed.

The voice sounded like his own, before. Lower, with the hard edge he used when he wanted an argument finished. He turned too quickly. Water caught against his injured cheek, and a cough closed his throat. Yu Mei still held the bottle. He couldn't bend forward properly or get out the words telling her not to pat his back.

Her hand stopped behind his shoulder.

“Can you breathe?”

He nodded once and coughed again. His chest contracted. His right knee lifted slightly as his whole body tightened, and pain drove his left hand toward the armrest. The injured forefinger recoiled from contact. Only his middle finger and the side of his palm remained against it.

He heard the bottle touch the ledge. He wanted its cap closed before anyone spilled it. All he managed was a short breath.

“Leave the bottle,” Yu Mei said.

She followed his glance and set it farther in. He Cen moved his feet out of her way.

The voice outside spoke again.

“Push me over. Don't wait.”

Clearer this time. Lin Mo recognized his younger cadence, the way he had hurried people through training. He tried to inhale. His chest seized again, leaving a faint metallic taste.

Yu Mei stood behind him, one hand already on the chair's handle.

She didn't push. But she couldn't see his mouth. She had heard Lin Mo speaking and seen his earlier nod. Suddenly he understood how easily she could join two things that didn't belong together.

He opened his left hand toward her.

“Wait,” Fang Ji said from the ledge.

Yu Mei stopped. The brake remained engaged. The chair hadn't moved. Fang Ji pointed at Lin Mo's hand without taking his injured finger.

“He didn't say that.”

Lin Mo coughed twice more, then rested against the backrest. Only when air came properly did he say the voice hadn't been his. The few words hurt his mouth enough to stop him again.

Yu Mei came around. She checked his face and the footrest before looking toward the corridor. Nobody stood there. The arm remained at its established position inside the doorway; its catch and contact marks were unchanged.

“You want me to take you over?”

“No. I nodded because I could breathe.”

Her fingers loosened from the handle one at a time. He wanted to say everything was fine. The chair hadn't moved an inch. But if she had released the brake a moment earlier, fine would simply have meant avoiding the cause because the outcome hadn't yet turned bad.

“I was behind you,” she said. “I couldn't see.”

“And hearing my own voice made me turn too fast.”

He couldn't tell her always to recognize his voice. The Theater had already borrowed his former face. This imitation chilled even him. A password chosen here would be spoken somewhere others might hear it.

Laughter came from outside. Apparently even a big brother needed supervision to get anywhere.

Lin Mo wanted to swear. His throat hurt first. He asked Yu Mei to wait before offering more water, until his knee stopped tightening with each breath.

Fang Ji half rose, then sat. The hurried movement had pulled at his left shoulder. Lin Mo saw his expression change and didn't give him another job guarding the doorway. The seatkeeper moved the light bag toward the wall. Empty bottles knocked together. Nobody promised never to mishear anything again.

“Next time you want to move, say it where she can see you,” He Cen rasped. “Don't issue orders from the back of your head.”

Lin Mo almost laughed.

“I should see whether she's free, too.”

“That sounds newly learned.”

“It is.”

The humor went no further. He Cen still had a fever; Fang Ji still held his shoulder. The accident that hadn't happened had nevertheless used something from each of them.

Once the urge to cough passed, Lin Mo said he wanted to go to the doorway. Yu Mei faced him and asked why. He wanted to finish checking how the retained structure could communicate, then consider the next step. Storing the diary hadn't supplied permission for everything afterward.

“Let me settle my legs first.”

His left heel pressed against the rear of the footrest, the existing red patch sore under his shoe. Trying to lift it made his right leg tighten. He stopped and described what he needed: check whether the shoe's edge had folded inward. Don't pull the foot or disturb his right knee's support.

Crouching cost Yu Mei too much. She bent from the side; Fang Ji supplied the other view without bracing on his injured palm. Lin Mo relaxed his toes until the heel no longer pressed so hard. Fang Ji confirmed the shoe wasn't folded. He stopped there.

It took longer than he had expected.

Outside, voices called as though an actor was late onstage. Nothing had established how much time remained, but his old voice had already promised “coming” twice. Lin Mo ignored it and waited for Yu Mei to straighten, steady herself against the wall, and nod.

“I can push this distance now. Then I sit.”

“All right.”

Before releasing the brake, she stood where he could see her and waited for his hand to return to his lap. Only then did she move behind him. It wasn't a procedure that would defeat every imitation. This time, at least, they knew who had spoken to whom.

They crossed the short, level corridor. The arm hadn't sagged. Lin Mo watched the chair's rim against a familiar floor seam and asked her to stop at their established distance, leaving room to see his footrest.

After braking, she started toward the panel.

“Can you still see me from there?”

She tried. She could see his shoulder, not his mouth. Reading the lowest text required turning farther away. If he coughed again, she would have to step back to see him properly.

She didn't immediately promise to keep looking around.

“Where do you want me?”

Moving the chair closer would have been simplest. It would also trap anyone tending his legs between the arm and the chair. He explained both things.

Yu Mei tried standing diagonally against the wall. With her elbow lower, she could reach the panel and see his mouth. But she blocked Fang Ji's route. Another person could approach the chair only after she moved aside.

“Then one thing at a time,” Fang Ji said.

He sat near the inner corridor mouth. While Yu Mei operated the panel, he could watch the chair and passage. If Lin Mo needed touching, she would leave the panel and make room. Nobody would use a pair of injured hands to guarantee two places at once.

The seatkeeper drew his injured leg toward the wall. He Cen wanted to see instead of guessing from distant voices, so he eased himself to the small room's doorway and sat. The bottle remained within reach behind him. The bag stayed out of their retreat path.

Lin Mo disliked how close everyone was. Any tremor would force the others to stop. But the previous arrangement had merely hidden those tremors. Out of sight wasn't spare capacity.

The seatkeeper offered to watch He Cen, freeing Fang Ji from watching both directions.

“What does watching mean?” Lin Mo asked.

“Catch him if he falls.”

“You can't reach him from there.”

The man extended a hand. Empty space remained between his fingers and He Cen. Lunging would bring his injured leg behind the wheelchair. He stopped before finishing the movement.

“I'll call you first.”

“Call me, too,” He Cen said. “I might just be changing position.”

The seatkeeper withdrew his hand, his face stiff. Lin Mo recognized the urge to make a promise large enough to be useful. He had nearly done the same. He neither excused the man nor treated helping now as erasure of earlier responsibility. He only had him sit within hearing distance.

Yu Mei freed a hanging fold of clothing beside the handle so it couldn't catch in a wheel. She didn't tear it into a restraint. If Lin Mo couldn't speak later, could she steady him first?

He nearly authorized all care at once, then remembered being unable even to refuse a pat on the back.

“If I'm falling, stop me. Otherwise, let me see you first.”

“Where should we hold?”

He indicated the space beside his left side, then shook his head. He didn't know how he might slump or which position would hurt. Yu Mei waited rather than making his temporary indication a permanent instruction.

“Tell me where you're going to touch. If you can wait for an answer, wait.”

That much she could do. Arranging him if he lost consciousness needed further thought. The armrests couldn't stop a fall in every direction. She wouldn't promise that her injured shoulder could serve as a bed.

Only then did he ask her to connect the channel. His open-hand signal rested in his lap. It hadn't solved every silence to come.

When Xiao Lu agreed to speak, he told her about coughing and said he could talk now. She asked who had been shouting. He realized she hadn't heard everything in the room and described the borrowed voice without making her a witness to something she hadn't seen.

“What did it say?”

He repeated the instruction. His throat tightened again. He had used those words himself, assuming he had considered every consequence and only needed someone else to perform the movement.

“Are you still asking them to push?”

“We're at the original doorway. The chair's stopped.”

“Then don't let it do your hurrying.”

Yu Mei stood where she could see him, her hand away from the handles. Fang Ji sat with his arm low. He Cen's rough cough broke the corridor's overly careful quiet.

“Is that the man with the fever?” Xiao Lu asked.

“Yes. He's where he can see us now.”

“Does he have to watch the screen, too?”

“No. He can rest.”

He Cen announced that he had no intention of supervising anyone's reading. Lin Mo let him lean back. There was no reassuring quota of people who must stay awake.

The unresolved communication conditions finally opened on the panel. Beside the retained-side marker, there was no counterpart to Xiao Lu's voice port.

Lin Mo stared at the space.

He had nearly failed to get this body's wishes to someone a few steps behind him. If a part of him remained somewhere they couldn't see, without even a hand to open, how would it ask them to stop?

“Keep reading?” Yu Mei asked.

“This part. Don't start with whether I could say thank you afterward. Find out whether it could tell someone what it needs.”

Water splashed onto his hand during the coughing fit still felt cool. Yu Mei had closed the bottle, but hadn't reached to wipe him. Even that small contact could wait for him to ask.

Outside, his former voice said “coming” again.

This time Yu Mei neither turned nor reached for anything. She was waiting for the person in front of her to finish speaking.
