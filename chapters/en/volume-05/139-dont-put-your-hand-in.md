# Chapter 139: Don't Put Your Hand In

Two finger grooves had been worn into the black handle.

Yu Mei lit the hollow. A hardened scrap of cloth clung inside, stained faintly brown. She withdrew her hand completely and used the short stick to clear dust beneath it.

The wire door rattled.

Fine wires ran from a seam beside the handle into the wall. Not an ordinary latch cable. They connected to a little glass plate that began glowing when the dust shifted.

[HAND SUPPORT]

MALE POSITION remained lit beneath it.

“Put a hand in and the door opens?” Yu Mei asked.

Lin Mo compared the operating box on the paper with hold. The handwritten Chinese note beside it was brief: Grip and wait for release. No duration. No explanation of how to remove the hand.

Below it, a cross led to Don't use a person as a cart.

“Someone's suffered here, at least.”

He didn't ask her to substitute the stick. The theater had accepted objects before, and people too. Testing a similarly shaped item might only wake whatever waited behind the door. He asked her to inspect the round cover. The bypass circle sat in the same position on the diagram.

One screw was loose; the cover's other side hooked over a short spindle. Yu Mei couldn't turn the screw, but she could swivel the cover aside a little. A flat-handled bolt lay within, separate from the black handle's wires. The lane number printed beside it matched the copper plan.

She didn't pull it immediately.

A pressure rod ran beneath the wire door, its end entering the frame. Following the slot downward, she found that it connected to the flat handle. The fine wires led instead to a higher black box.

Lin Mo asked her to swing the cover far enough to read the whole instruction. Half a line appeared on its back.

[MAINTENANCE BYPASS: ACTIVATE WITH LANE CLEAR]

Through a narrow window, they could see concrete sloping gently away. A rail followed the right wall. Nothing blocked the nearest bend; anything farther was out of sight.

“Clear of people?” Yu Mei asked.

“Could mean carts. Don't step down yet.”

The words gave him little to go on. He asked her to light the frame. An arrow beneath the bolt showed press, then turn. Beside it, a small window revealed a metal tongue against a black mark.

Yu Mei pressed the flat handle lightly with the stick. The tongue shifted halfway. The door stayed closed. She released pressure, and the handle returned. MALE POSITION didn't change. The handgrip didn't close.

All they'd learned was that the rod could move.

“Dust jamming it?”

She crouched and cleared a wad of grimy cloth from below the handle. It wasn't padding a load-bearing point; it had wound around a jutting metal edge. When she pulled it a little, the wire door sprang outward and caught her sleeve.

She stepped back. The fabric went taut.

Lin Mo told her to stop pulling. Looking down, she saw the hook had caught only the outer layer, not her arm, but her hand had been drawn much closer to the black hollow. The door was slowly returning, carrying her sleeve toward the frame.

“Get your arm out. Stay away from the door.”

She gripped her collar with her free hand and tried drawing the trapped arm inward. Her elbow caught. She sucked in a breath, unable to free it at once. Lin Mo's left hand reached instinctively, then stopped short of her. Something seemed to snap taut inside his shoulder.

He returned his hand to the armrest and fixed his eyes on the hook.

“The stick's beside your left foot.”

She bent, retrieved it, and braced the closing edge. The door didn't stop, only slowed. In that moment she pulled her cuff down. Fabric ripped. The hook took a narrow strip, and the door slammed into its frame.

Yu Mei retreated beside the wheelchair and crouched, breathing hard.

A red mark crossed the outside of her arm. No broken skin. A piece of sleeve was missing, exposing the inner layer. Lin Mo didn't reach to inspect her. He asked whether her fingers moved. She spread them and closed them for him, then swore.

“It pulls itself shut.”

“The return force never went away.”

They withdrew to the level ground beyond the original mesh gate. Fang Ji called from outside, asking about the noise. Yu Mei told him she'd torn her sleeve free; she hadn't been caught herself. He should stay there.

Lin Mo repeated the details instead of reducing everything to we're fine. Fang Ji said they could come back now if they wanted. The water was still shallow. Yu Mei didn't stand when his voice faded. Lin Mo didn't hurry her.

She rubbed the red mark beneath her sleeve. “Anything on that paper about it closing again?”

He asked her to turn to the already exposed side. A tiny line beside the arrow had been partly covered by his thumb.

[LEAVE BYPASS ENGAGED UNTIL EVERYONE HAS PASSED.]

He moved his hand and read it aloud.

She was silent a moment. “You didn't read that before.”

“I missed it.”

His face burned. He'd been afraid of attributing the paper wrongly, afraid of trespassing into the private fold, yet hadn't noticed half a sentence beneath his own finger. Yu Mei neither comforted him nor swore again. She reread the whole page, then laid the notebook flat on his knees, fingers clear of the writing.

He asked whether the mark hurt.

“Yes. Stop asking.”

She lowered her sleeve. It couldn't cover the gap, so she broke off the loose threads before they could snag again.

Lin Mo looked at his supported wrist. The cloth around the splint had been clean once. Dust now coated it. Fang Ji's palm, Yu Mei's sleeve: each grew a little less usable. Instructions left on a page didn't pay today's costs for them.

He pointed out the missed line and asked her to turn the sheet halfway around, bringing light from the opposite side. Writing lay in creases and along an oily margin. Printed boxes crowded handwritten warnings. They identified corresponding symbols first and read the adjacent notes, rather than leaping from a promising sentence to a physical action.

Beyond the bypass arrow, a correction had been made. Pull was crossed out and replaced with press, then turn. Whoever had struck it out had pressed so hard the rule beneath was broken too. Yu Mei mimed the direction without touching the door.

“I only pressed it partway. I never found where it could turn.”

“I was watching whether the tongue moved. Nothing else.”

Lin Mo compared the arrow with their photograph. The handle's lowest end wasn't clear at the image edge. He asked her to light that first when they went closer, without rushing to press it.

She set the stick across her feet, within reach. Fang Ji offered water from outside. She said she'd drink on returning, her voice steadier. Lin Mo knew she wanted to understand the gate. That didn't mean she wasn't hurting.

He looked at the old cloth inside the black grip. Whoever left it might have tried to tear free of a sleeve too. Or not. There was no name in the hollow, only grooves worn by fingers. He suddenly wanted to cover it, so the others wouldn't see. But that would make the next person more likely to put a hand inside.

“When we can pass, show everyone the grip. Don't just tell them the door's open.”

She nodded.

He flattened the notebook's curling edge. His left index finger hurt, so he used the next two fingers on a blank corner, avoiding the private fold. This time he let Yu Mei read instead of rushing to pronounce the words he recognized.

She stood, then sat again on a low concrete ledge. The shoulder she'd struck pulled painfully; the red mark rubbed beneath her clothes. No position was comfortable. Finally she simply kept that side away from the wall. Lin Mo settled his own back instead of trying to hurry everything he owed her into a thank-you.

Fang Ji reported the water was still near the stool legs, without a sudden rise. Yu Mei tightened both shoelaces. At her breakfast stall, she used to keep walking with a loose lace because a hot pan wouldn't wait. Here, there was only a door that caught clothes. She tucked the ends into her shoes.

Lin Mo waited. “Which side will you stand on?”

She indicated the corner beyond the door's swing, then the flat handle. He watched the step she would have to take and held the notebook toward the light. Only after reading every visible operating instruction did they approach again.

A second horizontal notch lay inside the cover, reachable only after the handle was fully depressed. The wad had blocked part of its travel. Cleared, it could move another half inch. Yu Mei tucked her sleeve away and stood beyond the outward swing. She pressed the handle down and turned it into the notch.

The metal tongue moved from black to white.

The door cracked open and stayed. HAND SUPPORT dimmed beside the black grip. Another short line appeared.

[MAINTENANCE PASSAGE]

Keeping hold of the lever, she pushed the door with the stick. It opened wide enough for the chair without drawing shut. She gradually eased her pressure. The flat handle stayed seated in the horizontal notch.

Lin Mo asked her to wait a little longer.

Beyond lay no shoulder supports or handholds meant to trap someone. A railing followed the gentle descent. Rough ridges crossed the ground, worn shallow by two wheel tracks. It was a road for wheeled things, at least for the first dozen steps, where there were no stairs.

He noticed a matching lever inside. They couldn't rely on someone waiting outside for their return. Yu Mei entered empty-handed, remaining on the near level patch, checked the inner control and its bypass position, then stepped back out.

“Works from inside too. This side's seated now.”

Two white marks on the frame aligned when the handle was secure. She photographed them and shifted the round cover to an angle that wouldn't press the lever, protecting it from a passing bump.

“First bend only,” Lin Mo said. “Before bringing anyone else.”

The gentler lane was much wider than the steep route. Yu Mei kept both hands on the chair, slowing to check every short stretch. Lin Mo felt its weight shift forward onto her. He asked her to stop on the first level patch, before her hands began shaking.

There was indeed a landing at the bend, with railing all the way. She braked and followed the wall to inspect the next turn. Half of her remained visible. Beyond that she would disappear, and he called her back.

“Let's report the road.”

She turned the chair and pushed uphill slowly. It cost more than descending; she rested again at the gate. Watching that short slope, Lin Mo understood that moving all five people down wouldn't happen as quickly as an arrow crossed a diagram.

The wire door remained open. White marks aligned. The black grip stayed dark.

Yu Mei didn't reset the bypass after they came out. The paper said until everyone has passed. They hadn't promised anyone safe passage yet, but they could preserve the state they'd checked.

Back in the repair room, she sat and tied the torn sleeve edge into a small knot to stop it unraveling.

He Cen watched the knot without asking whether the route led outside.

“I can walk one bend. Then I need to rest.”

Lin Mo nodded and described the bend, asking He Cen to mark it on the paper. He Cen set the notebook beside him on the bench, picked up the pen, and drew a lopsided square. Then its tip moved downward.

“We haven't seen beyond it,” Lin Mo said.

He Cen put the pen down. Sitting beside them, Yu Mei looked at the blank space below the square and nudged the wet cloth away with her foot. Dust on her shoes had turned to mud. Fang Ji offered the stick, and she slowly scraped off the layer that might make her slip.
