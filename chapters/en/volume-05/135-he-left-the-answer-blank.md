# Chapter 135: He Left the Answer Blank

Lin Mo first asked Yu Mei to check the photographs.

The boxes, the narrow transcriber's label, Old Chen's name, even the folded edge were captured. The damaged screen corner hid a small part of the image. She rotated the display to see it. Nothing was missing from the photograph itself.

“We can keep this.”

He nodded without asking her to pull the paper free. The cover remained on its original hinges. Its narrow paper clip was separate from the frame's locking pin. He asked her to check that removing the sheets wouldn't disturb the lock before releasing it.

She examined the edges and lifted the clip with two fingers. A small sound, then the paper loosened. The pin stayed in its second groove. The pads remained raised.

Three sheets.

Two were largely intact; the third held half an equipment diagram. Oil stained the first sheet's edges. The second had a tightly folded inner corner. Scorch marks yellowed the back of the third. No complete cover, no stitching that would join them to the booklet Sun Ning had carried away.

Keeping the inner fold closed, Yu Mei stacked them in their original order and held them before him.

“Take them back?”

He hesitated over the unopened corner, then nodded.

“Preserve what's exposed. We won't unfold the inside for him.”

His notebook had enough unused space to hold them flat. She left the photograph on its old page and placed the maintenance sheets farther back, using one of his blank pages to separate the oily edge. She flattened the notebook carefully, keeping the stained corners away from the photograph.

Before she closed it, handwriting showed on the half-diagram's reverse.

Here, return means back to the original position. It doesn't mean out.

“Wait.”

Below it, a printed box connected to the right lane's marking. The English was return again. Its arrow looped to the preceding box rather than leaving the paper. Lin Mo knew return belonged to this machine's terminology. He'd still quietly left room for it to mean returning to reality. If Old Chen had written these pages, he'd paused over that word too.

“So nothing to do with the wall outside?” Yu Mei asked.

“We can't take it as an exit.”

Lin Mo touched a blank notebook page with his left finger, wanting to record the distinction. Pain from the old injury made him recoil. Instead of taking the pen, he asked her to copy the line exactly and identify the source page.

She didn't name Old Chen as its confirmed author. Lin Mo had her add that the label read Clothes Mending Four, that Old Chen also appeared, and that they'd found the sheets behind the cover on the male load-bearing frame's right side.

The details occupied more space than he'd expected. He found the lines of precise locations irritating to look at. If he forgot how they'd found these pages, they would serve him better than Finally found Old Chen's notes.

Another printed line pointed toward a missing part of the diagram.

Residual fragment transfer: Lower maintenance access

Beside it, Original segment not seen had been heavily crossed out in full and replaced with Destination not seen. The writer had returned to correct which part they hadn't seen, distinguishing the results instead of leaving both phrases to mean the same thing.

Lin Mo remembered the hospital's receiving acknowledgment. It traced Xiao Lu's old transfer to the entrance. Inside, he still hadn't found where that same source account had gone next. This wasn't its record. Lower didn't put his sister behind the next door.

It did give them somewhere they could actually inspect.

He looked toward the descent.

A damaged sign leaned beside it, knocked crooked at one corner. Part of its reverse showed words similar to the diagram's. Yu Mei left the chair on level ground and approached the slope, using firm footing beside the rail to straighten the sign.

Lower maintenance

Beyond it, the passage vanished around a sharp bend. Part of its railing was broken. This slope was steeper than the earlier ramp. A thin gap crossed the floor; from above, they couldn't tell whether the chair's small wheels would cross safely.

Yu Mei didn't bring him closer for another angle.

“This is far enough.”

Lin Mo wanted to ask for a little farther. The words on the sign had already laid a route through the most urgent part of his mind, past Xiao Lu's transfer marker, past the answer Old Chen hadn't heard.

He saw Yu Mei's hand on the rail. The old burn, the newly reddened skin beneath her fingers. Fang Ji couldn't use both arms to lift him. He Cen still had a fever. Lin Mo had no leg that would let him crawl back from a mistake here.

“Back we go.”

She didn't praise him. She returned, put the phone away, and checked that no paper protruded from his bag before turning the chair toward the mesh gate.

White light crossed him in the right lane. Female lane lit. The pads remained overhead. He checked the maintenance notice and the pin head against its socket rather than trusting a route merely because they'd used it once.

Outside, Fang Ji stayed beyond the safe boundary without reaching for the handles. He watched the door's corner while Yu Mei straightened the chair, then followed them back.

He Cen leaned on the joined benches, jacket behind his back. Still hot, he opened his eyes as soon as they returned.

“Food?”

“No,” Yu Mei said.

“What took so long, then?”

Fang Ji pointed toward Lin Mo's bag. They'd found papers. He Cen's mouth moved, but he spared Lin Mo the observation that paper wasn't edible and asked for water.

The first new bottle was nearly empty. Five people had drunk from it over several hours; the remainder wouldn't allow another generous tilt. Yu Mei gave Lin Mo a small mouthful and passed the rest to He Cen. He held it level, waiting for the final drops to reach his mouth.

She kept the empty bottle, capped it, and opened the second. Three sealed new bottles remained by the wall. The two old small bottles still held their remnants in the same place.

Watching the empty one set aside, Lin Mo stopped repeating five bottles as though it described what they still had. Water found and water left were different quantities now.

“There's a maintenance passage below,” he told He Cen. “I didn't descend. Too steep. Need another way.”

He Cen asked about crossing the frame. Lin Mo explained the pin's position and the maintenance lock's limit to this frame. They hadn't inspected the equipment below. On returning, they would have to check the pin again.

The stranger had listened throughout, eyes on the notebook's edge inside Lin Mo's bag.

“You found his things?”

“Some copied maintenance pages he may have written.”

“Recognized him from that designation?”

“No.”

Lin Mo didn't spread every personal detail before him to win the argument. The man could question whether a designation identified a person. That didn't entitle him to all the pages.

“I recorded what looks like his work. What isn't verified too.”

After moving his lips for a moment, the man said he'd seen Clothes Mending Four with a pen before. Lin Mo waited. He couldn't name a show or a place.

“For now, that's your account.”

He didn't argue, lowering his eyes to touch the broken letters beside his shoulder. Lin Mo didn't ask him to remember the child's name. Recognizing old dialogue didn't qualify him to fill Old Chen's blanks.

Yu Mei asked whether Lin Mo wanted to lie down. He Cen began rising from the joined benches. Lin Mo stopped him.

“I can sit a while. We'll change when you're feeling better.”

Fang Ji suggested padding the clear ground along the wall instead of competing for the benches every time. He and Yu Mei moved only light clothes and dry cloth, leaving heavy cupboards alone, to make supported resting places for himself and the stranger.

Lin Mo leaned back and kept his left foot completely off the floor. Released from bracing the wheels, his heel slowly cooled. His right wrist remained splinted. His shoulder hurt. No voice belonging to his sister had emerged from the pages.

He Cen heard a small sound beside the benches and opened his eyes again.

“Who's leaving?”

Only Fang Ji moving an empty bottle away from his feet. Yu Mei crouched within He Cen's sight and told him everyone was back. Nobody was going.

“I know.”

He put his glasses on anyway. The arm slipped along his sweaty ear. She adjusted them without taking them away. Lin Mo watched his eyes check the entrance, then the people against the wall, finally the bag.

“You're going to find your sister.”

“Yes.”

“What if only you can use that route?”

Lin Mo nearly said that wouldn't happen. He'd already seen a route divide people between two sides. Securing one pin didn't justify promising there would never be another.

“I won't take you somewhere we can't retreat from.”

He Cen studied him.

“I'm not asking you to promise to save us all.”

“I know.”

“Tell me before you go. Don't let me wake up with nothing but the water bottles.”

Lin Mo looked at the newly capped empty bottle. When he entered, he'd feared people stopping him from finding Xiao Lu. Someone here feared waking without even knowing whom to stop.

“I'll tell you. If I'm not back and you find a way out, don't stay for me. Leave word of your route.”

Fang Ji didn't agree for anyone else. Yu Mei put the second bottle within He Cen's reach and told him to see where everyone was sitting before removing his glasses.

This time he removed them himself, wrapped the lenses in their cloth, and tucked them beneath a corner of the gray jacket.

Lin Mo left his notebook in the bag until Yu Mei had sat down and finished rubbing her hands. Then he asked her to open it only to the new notes.

“One last sentence.”

She waited with the pen.

“We haven't found Old Chen's original name, his son's name, or the original answer today.”

Her pen paused. They had the pages, the locked frame, the landing three people could reach and leave. He wanted to record what they hadn't found.

He didn't ask her to soften it.

“If I only remember recognizing his designation today, I might think the rest came with it.”

She finished the entire sentence and showed him, her last stroke steady.

Lin Mo watched original answer. If he reached that place in the hospital again, he wouldn't tell the broken voice his child had forgiven him, or that he'd only wanted the best for the child. He would say they'd found pages behind a damaged machine that were probably his. They still existed. Lin Mo hadn't completed the unheard part on his behalf.

Before closing the notebook, Yu Mei asked where they'd look next.

Beyond the room lay the iron door they'd crossed. The deeper maintenance passage remained unexplored. The original entrance still needed checking. They had no food, and the injured needed somewhere they could be taken out.

“Establish a route we can actually use tomorrow. Then follow the lower transfer.”

Fang Ji acknowledged him without promising a healed shoulder by morning. He Cen pulled his gray jacket closer and asked to sleep without being woken for every new word they discovered.

Yu Mei turned off the phone's screen and returned it to Lin Mo's bag. The slope outside still led downward. The half-drawn arrow on the page had grown no longer.

Lin Mo closed his eyes as someone screwed the cap onto the second new bottle.
