# Chapter 121: After They Let Go

The hands left the push handles.

Lin Mo planted his left foot and drew it back a little. A caster struck the metal threshold, twisted, and caught in the dark gap behind it. The chair stopped.

Yuan caught the handles again, his feet still on the basement floor.

“Come back. You can't cross this.”

Lin Mo stopped pushing. His right leg remained supported, but leaning forward tightened the knee. The clip-on lamp showed the trapped wheel. Forcing it would only throw him out.

“Don't turn the wheel.”

A man lifted the black cloth at the passage's far end and approached. His sleeves had been cut short. He examined the caster, then looked behind Lin Mo and touched the wall.

“How did you squeeze through there?”

“From outside. Someone's holding the chair.”

The man looked again. He could see gray wall, he said, with part of a wheelchair protruding from it.

Lin Mo turned. Yuan was still there, telling the person on the phone that the wheel was stuck.

“Someone inside is helping me,” Lin Mo told him.

Yuan kept telling him to come back. He didn't push.

The stranger brushed dust from the first intact tile and tested it underfoot.

“I can lift the front frame onto this. Stay off the chipped tile farther in. If you want to retreat, let them pull. I won't pull you.”

Lin Mo looked where the man stood.

“I want to go forward.”

After a pause, the man gripped the metal frame behind the casters. Lin Mo explained about his shoulder and leg. The man nodded without touching his arms.

He lifted gently. The casters cleared the gap and settled on the sound tile. Yuan only steadied the back from the real side. Lin Mo placed his left foot where the stranger had stood and edged forward. The man guided the frame while the large wheels rolled over the strip.

The telephone voice receded.

Looking back, Lin Mo could see only half of Yuan's face. He was still urging him to retreat. Lin Mo answered, unsure whether the words reached him.

The stranger tested the intact row on the left and guided the chair along it. Both wheels stayed clear of the damaged tile against the right-hand wall, where the wooden strip had sunk two days earlier.

Once the rear wheels were through, Lin Mo stopped with his heel against the floor.

A little ordinary concrete remained visible behind him. No faces.

The man had come to fetch waterproof canvas from beside the hall, he explained. The metal striking had brought him over.

“I'll get it first. Wait at the corner. Don't enter the hall.”

He walked away and disappeared behind the black cloth. Lin Mo studied the narrow passage. With the leg support projecting ahead, there was no room to turn. He needed the wider space beyond, and the man's help.

Keeping the clip-on lamp aimed at his feet, he edged forward. Half-finished plaster exposed yellowing wooden strips beside his elbow. Grit dragged beneath his left shoe.

He rested before the fifth tile. His back was tiring before his shoulder. He remembered the doctor watching him sit forward and settle against a chair again. Nobody was timing him here. He checked his phone and noted when he'd entered.

Beyond the corner, the ceiling rose abruptly.

Part of a sign hung beneath a crooked beam. He could make out RECEIVING. Strips of rubber flooring had peeled back, exposing parallel grooves. Upholstered chairs crowded the opposite wall. One hung unsupported, all four legs clear of the ground.

Its cushion sank farther, as though someone had settled into it. Nothing moved nearby. Far off, someone cleared their throat. Two scattered claps followed.

Lin Mo returned his phone to his pocket.

A clear strip ran along the right-hand side toward a fallen sign frame and a narrow door. To the left, broken railings bordered a steep drop. He couldn't see the bottom.

Thinking he was still inside the corner, he drew his foot back half a step to get a better view of the door.

The casters passed the hanging cloth. A light came on overhead.

A white beam grazed the chair's left side. His foot remained planted, but the seat began moving beneath him.

He braced his sole harder. The chair stopped briefly, then pressed against his back. Its casters turned toward the suspended chair. Rubber scraped beneath the tires.

His injured knee struck the support. He gasped, his shoe sliding through the dust.

“Where are you?”

The light climbed to his chest. His shoulder support tightened as if someone were pulling him upward through his clothes. He reached left for the brake. Pain stopped his hand at the armrest.

“Don't lift your arm!”

The man who'd helped him across the threshold emerged from the narrow door with a roll of canvas. He dropped it, caught the handles, and pulled.

The tires scraped a line across the floor. Light still covered Lin Mo's right leg. The man changed direction, turning the chair sideways out of the beam. Lin Mo snatched his left foot clear; the leg support struck the man's shin.

He swore but held on.

As the light left Lin Mo's knee, the force stopped. The man parked beneath the broken beam and bent over, breathing hard. His stubble was streaked with gray. A healed split ran behind one ear.

“Keep that foot out of the way. Run over mine, I can still walk. Yours—who's going to carry you?”

“Thank you. That light—”

“I saw. Don't sit under it.”

He retrieved the canvas. A red scrape marked his shin, but he said the skin wasn't broken.

Applause sounded overhead. The beam reached the chairs, and an empty cushion slowly rose.

“Can we leave the way I came?” Lin Mo asked.

“Someone really is waiting outside?”

“I could still see them a moment ago.”

The man went to look. Lin Mo watched him reach the passage entrance and touch the wall. He didn't call out.

“Gray wall at the end,” he reported. “I can't see your opening.”

Lin Mo's foot moved. He wanted to inspect it himself, but the light lay between them.

“Don't try charging through,” the man said. “You're sweating just sitting there.”

“Will you push me back to look?”

“Will you let me bring the canvas first?”

“Yes.”

He moved the roll beside the chair and cleared stones from the passage mouth. In the hall's wider space, he turned Lin Mo to face the passage, then pushed him forward along the intact tiles.

At the end, Lin Mo found the floor seam he'd just crossed.

Gray wall stood beyond it. Plaster ran uninterrupted down to an old baseboard. He put his left toe against it and called Yuan's name.

This time his voice echoed back.

“Did they know you might not return?” the man asked.

“Yes. I told them to stay outside.”

“Then don't call them in.”

Lin Mo kept his toe against the wall. The stairs and street sounds were somewhere he couldn't see. His phone had no signal. Those outside could still wait and seek help as agreed. Neither would shift this wall.

He asked to go back. The man drew the chair backward, pausing at the boundary of light and shadow until the beam moved away. Lin Mo saw the crooked lamp turning on the overhead structure. Its cable vanished into fallen masonry. Whenever it crossed the seats, applause sounded below them.

Safe in shadow, the man said his name was Fang Ji.

“Lin Mo.”

“What did you come for? Surely not to rent a chair.”

“My sister. She didn't get out with me. They transferred her farther in.”

Fang Ji crouched to pick a sharp stone from beneath Lin Mo's shoe, then sat on the canvas.

“You got out?”

“Yes. I've come back.”

Fang Ji looked up sharply. Lin Mo expected a question about the route. Instead, he asked whether the streets still had lights.

“It's daytime. The hospital had lights.”

“Anyone selling food?”

“The car stopped by a breakfast shop today.”

“What were they selling?”

Lin Mo remembered someone tying a plastic bag while the transport workers adjusted his leg support. White and brown shapes had shown through it. He'd only wanted the car moving again.

“Steamed buns, I think. I didn't look properly.”

Fang Ji rubbed his hands on his trousers.

Only then did Lin Mo feel how recently he'd been somewhere breakfast was still for sale. He still had money, unread messages. Had he turned back, he could have eaten something hot on the journey home. This man needed to ask whether shops were open.

“Didn't you reach the outer door?”

“I was in the fabric store underneath when the beam fell. By the time I crawled out, everything above was blocked.”

Fang Ji showed him a rough scar across his shin, then lowered the trouser leg.

“How long it took to reach here, I couldn't tell you. Don't ask me to give directions by that.”

“Only places you've been.”

“Then let me catch my breath.”

Lin Mo leaned back. A strap protruded from the canvas, tied into uneven knots. Fang Ji kept one hand on the roll even while rubbing his injured leg.

After a while, he noticed the two bottle bottoms bulging through Lin Mo's side pouch.

“Water?”

“Yes.”

“Keep it close. Others ahead aren't all as polite as me.”

He laughed dryly. Lin Mo saw the splits in his lips and offered him some.

“Let's get out of here first. That light won't wait while I drink.”

The narrow door led into a scenery passage. Fang Ji had carried sets here before, but knew only the nearby rooms. He hadn't explored the collapsed areas. The canvas was for a leaking laundry room; without it, they'd have no dry floor by night.

“Can I come with you?”

Fang Ji measured the width between the chair's wheels with his spread hands.

“There's broken floor ahead. I'll push you there. If we can't cross, you'll have to wait.”

“You don't have to promise to carry me.”

“I couldn't.”

“I noticed.”

Fang Ji glared at him, then shouldered the canvas. Lin Mo waited for him to take the handles before lifting his foot. He explained again: right wrist braced, left shoulder not to be pulled, right leg kept supported.

“That thing sticks a long way out,” Fang Ji said, studying the leg rest.

“The leg's more trouble.”

A fallen lighting frame blocked the narrow door. Fang Ji placed the canvas inside, then returned to shift it. Applause suddenly grew louder. He dropped the frame and retreated.

Lin Mo stayed still.

A brighter beam swept across their route. Two narrow footprints appeared in the illuminated dust and took three steps toward the seats. They stopped. So did the clapping.

“More of them,” Fang Ji muttered.

Lin Mo stared. No legs rose from those prints; nobody stood where they ended. He'd been about to ask where the nearest storage facility might be.

“Wait until the light moves,” he said instead.

“I know.”

Fang Ji stood behind him, breathing heavily. The canvas was through the door. Half a chair's length remained for Lin Mo to cross.

They waited together. The footprints didn't fade.
