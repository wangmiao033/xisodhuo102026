# Chapter 128: The Water Under the Beds

“How far?” Fang Ji asked.

The man drew a line in the dust, discovered water in it, and withdrew his finger. Follow the pipe the other way from the blue door, he said. Past two rows of overturned seats, an unpainted door stood against the wall. The room had held equipment for repairing lights.

“Beds?”

“Two long benches.”

“Why did you leave?”

He looked at the wet crack. “The water got louder. Thought there was a way out below.”

Fang Ji grunted. He'd trusted an old route here until the floor collapsed; he didn't mock this answer. He asked which way the door opened and whether the room had another exit, leaving unanswered things unanswered.

“Want me to walk over and show you?” the man snapped.

“I want to know where to stop.”

Lin Mo listened from his bed, cool cloth against his cheek and pain in his back. He didn't insist on going. Fang Ji showed him the phone's remaining battery. Lin Mo told him to keep the screen off and use the flashlight only in darkness.

“I'll come back when I've seen the door. Looking first. Haven't promised to move you.”

“Wet prints, you come back too,” Yu Mei added.

Fang Ji emptied the carrying bag and left through the cleared maintenance passage with the phone and one sealed bottle. Through the open door, they heard a dozen or so steps on dry tiles before silence.

He Cen rested a hand against his hot forehead. He no longer offered to help. Moving the buckets had used what strength he had, and his eyelids drooped. Yu Mei asked him to pull his wet shirt from his back. He agreed but only caught its hem.

“Shall I?”

He nodded. She slipped folded dry cloth behind him, stopping when her hand touched his feverish neck. Lin Mo watched it linger and suggested recording when he drank and when they checked his temperature. Otherwise each of them might assume someone had just done it.

“Can you write?”

“A few words with my left hand. If I can't, I'll tell you.”

She opened his notebook on dry bedding beside his waist. Keeping his arm flat, he held the short pen between his left fingertips and recorded the time. Nobody knew precisely when He Cen's fever began. He left that blank.

The writing slanted; his last stroke trailed. He stopped and asked Yu Mei to put it away.

“Still keeping notes here?” the stranger asked.

“I forget things.”

“What you write changes too.”

He was staring at the closed notebook. He didn't look threatening, more like someone who had hoped words would preserve something and discovered they hadn't.

“Tell me what happened to you,” Lin Mo said.

After a pause, the man said he'd remembered his home address when his seat disconnected. When he'd tried to write it just now, the final digit was gone.

“When did it change?”

“I don't know.”

“Then don't tell me mine already has.”

He leaned back against the wall. Lin Mo couldn't verify the address story. The man's fear didn't prove the theater had become fair. His red words had still hung secure when Old Chen struggled for his son's name. One didn't cancel the other.

He Cen turned from his pillow.

“Before, could you tap something from your chair and make a person keep standing?”

The man glanced at Yu Mei, as though hoping she hadn't heard.

“I'm asking you.”

“Depended what was available. You couldn't just have anything you wanted.”

“I didn't ask whether you could make the whole world stand. I asked about my night.”

“I don't know which show you were in.”

He Cen's fingers trembled around his glasses. Yu Mei was about to urge him to rest; he raised his other hand for her to wait. Lin Mo let him ask. Not knowing who had selected him didn't mean he couldn't question someone who'd occupied those seats.

“Could you choose nothing?”

“Sometimes there was a countdown.”

“And when it ran out, what happened to you?”

“You missed that round.”

He Cen laid the glasses on his knees and tried to sit straighter. He couldn't, and sank against the pillow. Cloth scraped his feverish back, clearer to Lin Mo than the explanation that followed.

“Later it changed. The seats could—”

“Then wait till I ask about later.”

Yu Mei caught the glasses sliding from his knees and put them in his jacket's breast pocket. He studied his empty hand. He couldn't remember the latter part of that night, he said. Only wanting desperately to sit. Anyone bending a knee seemed to be doing it deliberately for him to see.

“I hated people sitting down.”

Lin Mo looked toward his wheelchair. He Cen saw it too. His lips moved, but he didn't take everything back.

“I know you aren't one of them.”

“I heard you.”

Lin Mo didn't ask for an apology. People pushed his chair; He Cen waited feverish for water. Needing to sit didn't require every hatred of seated people to make room around him.

Yu Mei settled the gray jacket on He Cen's shoulders and urged him to close his eyes. The stranger closed his too, then opened them to inspect the palm that could summon nothing.

Water spilled from the basin.

Yu Mei heard it trickling over the edge. A wet ring already spread beside a bed leg. She changed the basin and carried the full one toward the door, intending to empty it onto broken ground outside.

“Put it down first.”

Water rocked against its rim. Lin Mo pointed beneath the sinks. A faint light emerged along a floor joint, traveled an inch over the water, and vanished.

No lamp near the door had that color.

Yu Mei set the basin on dry floor. Beside her knees, the catch bucket rattled. The drops were coming faster, almost joining into streams. The canvas pocket sagged lower.

“Damn pipe.” She reached to loosen its strap.

Lin Mo warned her to stay out of the water. As he spoke, a corner gave way. She snatched her hand back. Water poured between the beds, soaking an old sheet and flowing beneath their legs.

Applause burst from the drain.

He Cen sat upright. The stranger reached for his palm again. Lin Mo had seen the same gesture by the gray door. Before the hand came down, a faint rectangle lit the wall, resembling the performances he had seen through screens.

It showed no face. Only a bed and a leg outside its covers. Toes slowly straightened in the light. Water gathered beneath the bed.

“Keep your leg still,” Lin Mo told He Cen.

His left foot stopped on its way to the floor. Yu Mei lifted it onto the bed, then turned toward Lin Mo. His own bed's foot was sinking, as though someone were climbing its legs and bearing down.

He didn't try to brace himself. Raising his left shoulder hurt. He pressed his back to the boards and asked Yu Mei which leg had sunk.

A hand's shadow gripped the foot of his bed.

Its fingers tightened. Iron creaked. The solid bed leg still rested on an intact tile, but the shadow dragged it half an inch toward the sinks.

Yu Mei caught the headboard and braced on dry ground. The bed paused.

“Fang Ji!” she shouted toward the maintenance passage.

No answer came.

The stranger pushed himself up on his right foot and reached for Lin Mo's arm.

“Stop!”

He recoiled. Red letters seemed to scatter against the wall behind him.

“The frame. Don't grab me.”

After hesitating, the man braced his shoulders against dry wall beside the sinks. His left leg couldn't hold him. Planting his right foot, he gripped the outer frame with both hands. Yu Mei told him to bring it toward her, without lifting.

The legs scraped with a mounting squeal. Lin Mo's right knee jolted; pain whitened his vision. He bit his lip rather than urge them faster. The man couldn't pull the bed and support his leg. If Yu Mei released it to help his knee, it would slide again.

He Cen found a long dry cloth beside his pillow.

“Underneath.”

He turned to pass it over, went dizzy, and caught the bed's edge without standing. At Yu Mei's instruction, he threw it onto dry floor, half an arm's length from her. She hooked it with her foot and worked it under the moving bed leg.

As soon as it soaked through, the hand appeared in it.

“It's not working!”

Lin Mo could see. Blocking light wasn't the same as breaking contact with water. The saturated cloth still connected to the flooded floor. He told her to pull it away, not add more absorbent things. Her heel dragged it back, leaving a bright streak.

Footsteps finally sounded outside.

Fang Ji stopped at the flooded entrance. He took an empty bucket from the cleared stack and upended it between dry floor and bed. Before stepping onto its base, he paused.

“That'll slip.”

He put it back and squeezed along the remaining dry tiles by the headboard. Yu Mei made room. He caught the frame's other end and helped the stranger drag it toward the dry wall.

Less than a foot of movement brought its legs clear of the puddle. The shadow let go.

Lin Mo breathed as though he'd crawled through a narrow pipe. Fang Ji ignored the hand retreating beneath the sinks. He pushed the bed until all four legs stood dry, then crouched to check Lin Mo's knee.

He Cen's bed needed moving too. They shifted the bottles and had him lean against the headboard, holding his gray jacket. The stranger sat again, his palms shaking too much to grip his trousers. Fang Ji and Yu Mei slowly pushed from either side until the beds stood close, leaving only enough space to pass sideways.

The picture on the wall dimmed. Two more claps sounded.

Lin Mo watched fresh moisture beside the bed leg until it stopped moving. Then he asked Fang Ji to turn the clip-on lamp down. No footprints appeared on the dry tiles.

“The room I checked is dry,” Fang Ji said. “Shouldn't have kept worrying about basins once the water started here.”

“Breathe first,” Yu Mei told him.

He sat by the bed's foot, right palm flat, left hand clenched around the phone. Lin Mo asked him to put it facedown before the cracked edge cut him. Only then did Fang Ji notice his grip. His fingers stiffened as he released it.

“I just—” the stranger began.

“I know you helped pull.”

They looked at one another across the foot of the bed. Lin Mo thanked him, quietly but clearly enough to hear. The man seemed about to add something, then turned away.

Yu Mei didn't finish the exchange for them.

Fang Ji described his inspected route. Beyond the blue door, dry floor led around the backs of the seats without steps. The repair room had a level entrance and broad benches. A narrow passage along its inner wall ended at a closed iron door. He'd seen it, hadn't touched it.

“At least we'll stay dry tonight.”

Lin Mo checked his phone. It probably wasn't dark outside yet; here the light couldn't tell them day from night. He didn't dispute tonight. He asked whether there was somewhere he could lie down.

“Put the benches together, yes.”

“Then let's move.”

He Cen said he could walk, without standing to demonstrate. He waited until Yu Mei offered her forearm, sat upright, and tested his feet against the floor.

In the water beneath the sinks, the unlit lamp shone again.

Yu Mei retrieved their shoes from under the beds and shook water from the soles. She hung Lin Mo's cloth bag inside the wheelchair. Its strap had twisted through the buckle; she unfastened it and tried again. Fang Ji waited until it sat well clear of the wheel before bringing the chair alongside.

This time, nobody waited for the light to finish.
