# Chapter 124: What He Thought Was a Beam

Before leaving, Lin Mo stayed in bed another fifteen minutes.

His shoulder still hurt, but his left heel no longer burned. He waited until Fang Ji and Yu Mei were in position before lowering his foot for the transfer. Yu Mei supported his right leg, keeping it clear of the bed's edge.

He Cen watched from his pillow as they secured the bags. He slowly unscrewed his water bottle and drank, telling Yu Mei to stop looking back.

“Remember my jacket.”

“Gray. Black button.” She left the door half closed.

The laundry's back door opened onto a short outer passage. Lin Mo had thought it ran parallel to the scenery corridor. At its end, he saw the routes diverge outside the hall. Their original path went right, toward the white light. This one went left around the sunken orchestra pit.

Half its ceiling had collapsed. Rows of seats leaned into darkness, a broken pipe protruding between red backs. Water fell slowly from it into the depths. On a farther wall, a blue door stood beside a narrow strip of gray light.

Fang Ji pointed. He Cen's direction.

“The old walkway's gone,” Yu Mei said.

Broken ends projected from the passage, a long stretch missing between them. Through the railing, they could see the old planks in the pit, unreachable and useless for the wheelchair.

Fang Ji unfastened the empty bucket from his waist and set it safely aside. Two solid bricks had served as bucket supports. He tested them underfoot, then asked Yu Mei to stay against the wall.

From the passage's wider corner, Lin Mo saw a shallow slope below the broken walkway. It curved along the wall toward the blue door, dust covering its surface, dark crosspieces showing at the edge. The level difference wasn't great. Its entrance lay just beyond the corner.

“We can go below.”

Fang Ji inspected it. He hadn't been that way. Thick dust concealed a slope that rested half against the wall and half over the pit. Its underside was hidden.

The clip-on lamp reached only the front wheels. Lin Mo asked Yu Mei to use his phone's flashlight. Squinting into its beam, he saw a dark, regular-edged brace beneath the first board. It looked like iron.

“Keep to the wall. That beam goes into it.”

As he spoke, he thought he should ask someone to examine underneath. But his chair would have to move to change his viewing angle. Fang Ji had already turned toward a cleaner line along the slope, as though repeated footsteps had wiped away its dust.

“Test it first,” Lin Mo added.

Leaving Yu Mei with the chair, Fang Ji stepped onto the slope. A light step made the boards creak without sinking. A heavier one dislodged dust beneath a crosspiece. Before his third, he looked back.

“Not shifting here.”

Lin Mo checked the dark strip by the wall. He'd found footholds among collapsed beams before. The familiar shape reassured him. He nodded, telling Fang Ji to stop before the next joint.

Yu Mei led with the phone. Fang Ji pushed, leaving the bucket by the bricks to collect if the route proved passable.

The first meter was slow. Casters brushed dust away, exposing dark red paint like the backs of old theater flats. Lin Mo looked sideways for the beam again.

A crack sounded behind him to the right. The chair dropped sideways.

His braced wrist struck the armrest as he instinctively reached out. He recoiled. His right leg shifted slightly out of position on its support, driving a sharp pain into his knee.

“Stop!”

Fang Ji was already pulling backward. The chair back rose, but another split opened below, dropping the right rear wheel farther. Yu Mei set the phone on the concrete edge ahead and seized the frame.

“Don't pull! It's coming apart!”

Fang Ji stopped. The left rear wheel remained on the boards. Both front casters had passed the joint onto a small concrete platform. The right rear wheel was trapped in the break, darkness beneath its axle. Falling fragments took a long time to strike anything.

Lin Mo pressed into the chair instead of reaching again. His weight dragged sideways. His shoulder support slipped; Fang Ji caught it with a few fingers but couldn't reposition it.

“Steady the chair first,” Lin Mo gasped. “Leave that.”

Yu Mei planted herself on the platform and gripped the front frame. Only then could Fang Ji see beneath the sagging board.

The iron beam was wood.

Black paint covered a thin, hollow shell. Split open, it revealed rotten strips. The whole slope was fallen scenery resting on a ledge beside the wall, with only a few framing pieces inside.

Lin Mo had mistaken it for structural support. No footsteps had cleared that narrow line either. Water dripping along the wall had washed the dust away.

“I got it wrong.”

Fang Ji's jaw tightened. He didn't answer.

Yu Mei told Lin Mo to lift his left foot. He'd wanted to brace it against the floor, but it would only press on the splitting boards. He withdrew it and let them hold a body that couldn't rise from its seat.

He could tell them what hurt and what mustn't move.

“Keep the right leg supported. Don't pull my left arm.”

“I know!” Yu Mei's voice shook.

She wasn't impatient with the repetition. Her knuckles were white, her right foot pressed against the platform's edge. No railing stood behind her. He waited for her to secure her footing.

Fang Ji locked the left rear wheel. When Yu Mei said she could brace the front, he left her holding the chair and edged backward along the wall. He avoided the black strip Lin Mo had identified, using the raised concrete ledge at the wall's foot to return for the bricks.

Lin Mo listened to soles scraping against the wall, afraid Fang Ji would slip and afraid to urge him faster. Old training habits couldn't lift him from the seat or give them another working hand.

Fang Ji placed the first brick behind the right front caster on the platform, preventing rollback toward the break. He set the second aside, then edged along the solid ledge behind the chair and swept debris from beneath the platform with his shoe.

A broad concrete footing projected roughly half a foot from the wall. He felt its edge before laying the second brick flat on it. Its whole base rested on solid concrete. Its upper face nearly met the platform, filling the short gap before the rear wheel.

They couldn't pull backward, he told Yu Mei. They must lift the rear wheels clear of the broken wood, then advance onto the platform. The casters already had firm ground; the large wheels needed only a short move.

Their first attempt lifted the rear crossbar slightly. Lin Mo cried out at the pain in his knee.

Fang Ji stopped. Yu Mei saw the shifted leg support, lifted his calf, and settled his heel on the padding. She freed his trouser cuff from the metal edge. He waited through the pain without asking them to continue.

With both feet placed one ahead of the other on the concrete ledge, Fang Ji braced against the wall. He gripped solid frame tubes below the seat, keeping pressure off his palm wound. Opposite him, Yu Mei stayed on the platform and took the lower rear crossbar on the left. A small lift only: Fang Ji would advance the chair; she would steady it.

“Ready?” she asked.

Lin Mo nodded, then said yes.

The rear wheels slowly cleared the broken wood. They moved the frame just enough to align the right rear wheel with the brick already supported by the footing.

It settled onto the brick. The left wheel still rested on an uncollapsed board edge. Fang Ji caught his breath while Yu Mei adjusted her stance and secured the frame. After confirming the first brick still prevented the front caster rolling backward, he released the left rear brake.

Together they moved the frame forward. The front caster left its chock behind; the large wheels rolled from brick and board onto concrete. The chair back finally straightened.

Lin Mo couldn't draw enough air.

Yu Mei checked his leg and the unbroken support, then replaced his shoulder cushion. Sweat from her palm soaked the fabric. She didn't stop to wipe it.

A long tearing sound came behind them.

The scenery flat pulled away from the wall and tilted into the orchestra pit: its end first, then the narrow dusty route. A wooden frame snapped against a seat. Dust rose through the gaps.

Their way back was gone.

Remembering his confident instruction to keep to the wall, Lin Mo felt his stomach tighten. Fang Ji crouched on the platform. Red seeped through his fresh palm dressing. He pressed it with his other hand without looking up to curse.

“I thought scenery framing was a beam,” Lin Mo said. “I should have had you look underneath.”

“I tested it too. But I only said those two steps didn't shift.”

“I told you to go forward.”

“Light the way first,” Yu Mei interrupted. “We still need a route back. He Cen's waiting.”

The phone lay facedown at the platform's edge. When she lifted it, a glass fragment fell. One screen corner had gone black, but the flashlight worked. She showed him rather than saying it was fine. He asked her to hold the undamaged back, avoid the cracked edge, and wait for somewhere secure to put it away.

His notebook remained in its cloth bag, the strap intact. Fang Ji checked for new dampness, then shifted it inward from the wheel to avoid another snag. The water bottle was there too, barely making a sound when moved.

The platform lay below and right of the blue door. Three low steps joined it to the passage outside that door. The wheelchair still couldn't climb them. Fang Ji didn't suggest another lift. At the platform's opposite end, a gray soundproof door stood at floor level, without steps.

Yu Mei climbed two steps and stopped. The blue door stood half a person's height above them. A broken corner narrowed the steps' outer edge. Carrying Lin Mo and the chair would leave each helper with one foot planted, the other searching over empty space.

She returned without calling Fang Ji to try.

“We can't get up. Another lift won't fix it.”

Lin Mo nodded. He wouldn't say “try it” again and leave someone searching for a foothold.

From the platform's edge, Fang Ji called toward the laundry. No answer crossed the pit and intervening turns. He called again: they couldn't return yet; if He Cen heard, he should wait, not come looking. Nobody could confirm he'd heard.

“The door's that way.” Fang Ji pointed upward. “We've gone around. Looks close, but we can't go straight there.”

Lin Mo reconstructed the route. The laundry stood higher. The broken walkway had followed the pit's outer edge toward the blue door. Their fallen scenery ramp had delivered them to a platform below it. The gray door shared this lower level.

Thinking only of the blue door's direction, he'd overlooked height. Even without the collapse, they'd have needed another route upward.

Yu Mei urged Fang Ji back from the edge and freed his jacket from a protruding iron tip. Her shaking fingers needed two attempts. Once she stood securely, Lin Mo explained the positions he'd established, including which doors concealed unknown ground.

Barely more than ten minutes since leaving the bed, his phone said. His shoulder felt as though he'd traveled for hours. He needed somewhere to recline, or at least help adjusting his position.

A short knock came from the gray door.

Yu Mei stopped. After a pause, two more knocks. Closer than He Cen's description, but closeness didn't make the source an ordinary person. Fang Ji positioned the chair beside the door, clear of its opening, without immediately touching it.

“Someone in there?”

The knocking stopped. Pale red flickered twice beneath the door. Lin Mo's pounding chest quickened again.

“Move the light,” someone said.

Fang Ji didn't. He asked where they were hurt. A strained gasp answered, then scraping behind the door, like a hand struggling to move across the floor.
