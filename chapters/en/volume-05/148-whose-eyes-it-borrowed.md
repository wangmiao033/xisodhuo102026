# Chapter 148: Whose Eyes It Borrowed

Lin Mo put the transcription back in the notebook.

He would not answer the question from across the passage. Say yes, and the voice would offer Keep Watching. He could not make himself say no. He flattened the paper's corner and asked Yu Mei to drink.

She said she would in a moment.

After a while, she asked whether the person knocking had been a man. The hand had looked broad, Lin Mo said, but he hadn't seen a face. He knew nothing else. She nodded, then asked about the sleeve.

“Gray.”

“That's what I saw, too.”

She laid her hands on her thighs. He did not ask whom she was thinking of. Once they left the slit, the picture could continue inside them. The less it showed, the easier it became to fill the gaps with somebody they knew.

He Cen pushed himself upright, complaining that his neck was stiff. He laid his glasses on his coat, found a rolled fold behind his collar, and slowly flattened it. A scrap of gray cloth, pressed against skin long enough, could hurt like another hand. Only after replacing his glasses did he ask Yu Mei for help.

She supported him while he changed position against the wall. When she returned, her bruised shoulder was too stiff to lift freely. Lin Mo told her to stop. He shifted against the wheelchair back, barely moving, and still felt it pull through his right knee.

“That's enough,” he said before she touched him.

She left him there. They sat quietly. Fang Ji listened by the doorway and reported that the knocking had returned: the same three blows, pause, three blows, but now followed by panting.

“At least it can replay the beginning,” Lin Mo said.

Yu Mei nodded without loosening her grip on her clothes.

They shared more water, leaving less than half the fourth bottle. The seatkeeper moved the sealed fifth bottle farther along the ledge, where He Cen wouldn't knock it over if he stood. Then he leaned back without offering his empty hands for some other job.

Lin Mo asked him whether the pictures used to identify their sources.

“Sometimes there was a scene title. Sometimes nothing.”

“Did you look for one?”

The man shook his head.

Lin Mo remembered accident clips on his own phone. A title announced that something was horrifying. Comments argued over whether it was staged; someone claimed that fakery made it harmless to watch. He had scrolled down too, looking for a judgment that would let him continue comfortably.

Now uncertainty about the person behind the door had become another reason he couldn't look away.

Yu Mei asked whether the connection was still pending.

“We can check once. Then back. If your shoulder needs longer, we wait.”

She raised her right arm only as far as the pain allowed. She could push on level ground, she said. No lifting. Fang Ji asked to inspect the lines near the doorway. He would not move equipment or touch controls. Lin Mo agreed and told He Cen they would remain within calling distance.

The writing beside the gray door had changed.

[CONNECTION AVAILABLE]

A fine line ran from the glass, across a pale flush panel in the floor, to a low metal frame against the wall. There was no ordinary screen inside it, just a vertical sheet of dark, dusty glass. Along its base were the words Live Reference.

Lin Mo did not approach immediately.

Yu Mei went around the pale panel and checked behind the frame. Bolts secured its feet to concrete. Behind it was wall, not a cavity, and there was room for the wheelchair on the right. Both fasteners at the panel's edge were present; pressing its edge with her foot did not tip it. Only then did she bring the chair over.

Fang Ji followed the thin line with his eyes. It first entered a row of channels low in the wall, rather than running into the central darkness. He could not squat. After bending slightly, he straightened and admitted he couldn't see inside.

“That's enough,” Lin Mo said. “Don't put your wounds down there for a better look.”

Yu Mei set the brakes before the frame.

Lin Mo's face emerged in the glass, then gradually changed into the face he used to have. Short hair, a broader jaw, sunburn at the neck. His shoulders widened. His right hand lay whole against his thigh. No splint.

His actual hand had not moved.

He stared. The image wasn't perfect. It had the tiredness he remembered beneath the eyes, even a patch of stubble he had missed shaving. Those flaws made it easier to believe in than smooth, unwounded skin.

[KEPT FOR YOU]

The gentle voice said that if he went on watching, he needn't lose this version yet.

His throat felt packed with dry cloth.

Yu Mei called his name. He blinked before turning toward her.

She did not ask if he wanted his old body back. She looked at his real wrist. The support hadn't loosened; his sleeve held the same creases. Following her gaze, he slowly found his breathing again.

“This isn't a reference.”

The old face frowned a fraction before he did.

Fang Ji directed his attention to the frame's foot. The line forked there. One branch entered the low channel; another ran up the frame's back toward a bright slit overhead. Beside the frame was a sliding cover. Its front edge said Display. Live Reference was behind it.

Yu Mei first inspected a small catch. It secured only the thin outer frame, not the floor panel. An arrow marked Slide Aside showed the travel direction. Once they could see that her hand need not go behind the glass, she released it gently.

The outer frame moved half an inch. His old face shifted with it.

Lin Mo almost told her to stop. Nothing dangerous had happened. The half-face was leaving his sight, and the movement looked too much like someone turning away. His lips moved. What he finally said was, “Slowly. Don't strain your shoulder.”

She slid the frame into its side channel. The image remained bright, like an advertisement set aside. Behind it was a narrow panel without a reflection, a manual slider and the final part of his source reference.

[LIVE DISPLAY: ON]
[CONNECTION AUDIO: NOT STARTED]

Separate controls.

He asked Yu Mei to turn the display off. Halfway through reaching, she stopped. Would that shut his sister out too?

He indicated the second line. They had not connected to her. This controlled the display going outward from their side.

The slider settled into Off.

A slit below the rail, showing his wheelchair's footrest, immediately darkened. Another lit with an image of him staring at his old face. That look was stretched out, as if he had already agreed to something.

“There's another,” Fang Ji said.

“The old picture's still there. See whether the new one has stopped.”

Yu Mei stood beside the frame, raised an empty hand, waited, and lowered it. The dark slit stayed dark. In the old picture she remained behind the chair. Fang Ji moved from the doorway to the edge of the pale panel. His recorded self did not move.

He reached toward the slider, but Lin Mo stopped him.

“What are you doing?”

“Checking whether it came back on.”

“It's still off. Don't keep moving it.”

Fang Ji withdrew his hand. He had used only the left, but the movement had pulled at his shoulder. He stood pale-faced against the wall. Yu Mei offered help. He refused; he could lean, and she should keep the wheelchair in sight.

His hand cast a shadow on the glass, not a fingerprint. Lin Mo used to do much the same thing in his unit: someone said a lock was secure, and he would pull it anyway. Here, unseen lines ran beyond a simple switch. Flicking it repeatedly would not make them understandable. It would only give the place more chances.

He asked to check the reference again. The number had not changed. Audio remained unstarted. Nothing said they had abandoned the query. Yu Mei held the paper against a draft from the wall. Lin Mo noticed that the cool air did not follow the central surface's breathing.

At his request, she held the sheet upright, its blank edge toward the center. It inclined to the right and stayed there, rather than rocking with the dark surface.

“The wind comes from the wall,” she said.

He told her to put it away. Not every chill meant the thing was approaching. Beside his chair there were still walls, vents, and a passage whose width could be measured. That small discovery let him lower his chin.

His old face kept watching from the displaced glass. He raised his left hand. The injured finger wouldn't close with the others. The reflected old hand curled neatly, unwounded, correcting the uncooperative part. He looked back at his actual fingers and did not repeat the test.

“Don't hit that glass when we leave,” he told Yu Mei.

She glanced at him.

“Broken glass will damage the tires,” he added.

She noted the projecting corner without teasing him for his hesitation. His shoulders eased. He did not need to give that moment a more respectable investigative purpose.

They had verified only this one outlet.

The central darkness suddenly sank a little, like a breath that hadn't quite filled the lungs. Its thin openings spread. The knocking stopped. A distant cough arrived, followed by laughter and impatient calls. The surface swelled again.

In a broad upper slit, someone held a phone.

The owner's face was absent. A thumb reached the end of a video of someone being hurt, moved halfway away, then returned to drag the progress bar backward. Each return raised a fine crease in the darkness, as though something were being stitched into it from far off.

“Is that person being filmed too?” Yu Mei asked.

Lin Mo did not know. The picture changed. A hand eased a door open while someone inside bent over a dressing. The watcher held their breath. The more carefully they kept quiet, the clearer that patch of the dark surface became.

A car window followed. A stairwell corner. A comment box refreshing again and again.

Some places resembled the theater; others did not. Lin Mo saw no shared admission ticket, no single command. Small pauses, backward drags, and stolen looks poured toward one place. Entangled with the suffering, they kept it breathing.

His palm went cold.

Fang Ji said those people might not know this place existed. Lin Mo remembered how he had stopped before the knocking hand. He did not pronounce ignorance innocent.

“It borrows their eyes.”

The central surface rose.

Something like a woman sat up from the darkness, head lowered, as if she could finally hear him properly. The outline was incomplete. A door crack made her left shoulder. Phone light opened and closed in her cheek. Many people held one breath inside her chest.

Lin Mo recoiled against the chair. The brakes held. Pain in his knee brought him back.

The figure spoke close to his ear.

“You can see me.”

Yu Mei returned to the handles. Fang Ji backed away from the pale panel. They left along the floor they had checked, without waiting for the figure to finish standing. The old face still looked toward Lin Mo. His parting glance did not move the display switch back to On.

In the passage he asked whether Yu Mei had seen a person.

“Something like one,” she said, breathing hard. “With other things inside when it rose.”

Their one slit remained dark. Farther away, more light gathered. The breath did not follow, but its voice reached the shelter's doorway, almost comforting.

“Don't be afraid. You aren't the only one who wants to look.”
