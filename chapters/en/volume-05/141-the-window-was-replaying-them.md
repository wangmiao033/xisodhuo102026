# Chapter 141: The Window Was Replaying Them

The bright point sank inside the window and illuminated He Cen's face.

The real He Cen sat to Lin Mo's left. The one behind the glass had his head down and mouth open, about to cough. He Cen clapped a hand over his mouth. His eyes reddened with the effort.

“Don't hold it in,” Yu Mei said.

The cough escaped. His image coughed too, then coughed again. He Cen had stopped, but the face in the window kept bending lower until its forehead almost touched the bottom of the frame.

Soft applause came through the door. The counter changed: twenty, twenty-one. A new line appeared beneath it.

[Catch-up viewing queued]

“It's replaying what just happened,” Lin Mo said.

The glass changed. The man with the red lettering recoiled, struck his injured leg against the brick corner, and opened his mouth. His hand and the lifted edge of his dressing were exactly where they had been earlier. The man himself remained against the far wall, motionless.

“For whom?” He Cen asked, struggling for breath.

The bright point withdrew. A deep room appeared behind the glass, full of square frames suspended at different angles. The nearest contained the moment Lin Mo had bitten his cheek. Another showed the same instant from below, as though someone had crouched beside his chair to watch his teeth.

No seated audience was visible.

From the side, Fang Ji said he could see Yu Mei hitting the wall. He shifted right. Her strained face shifted with him, blocking whatever lay deeper inside.

Yu Mei slipped her hand into her torn sleeve instead of rubbing her shoulder. In the window, a white line traced the bruised area beneath the cloth.

Lin Mo remembered the recorded breathing at the load-bearing rig. There had at least been a visible speaker to unplug. Here, their bodies remained on the platform while their pain entered someone else's queue.

“Move beside the window.”

Yu Mei took the handles. Her injured shoulder tightened as she pulled the chair back, and the glass immediately displayed her flinch.

“Slowly,” Lin Mo said. “We don't have to race it.”

She guided the wheels around the bench legs and into the blind space to the left. Fang Ji and the red-lettered man followed the wall. He Cen tried to stand, but his legs shook.

Yu Mei parked the chair and went to him. In the glass, he was already standing, knees bent as though he were about to collapse.

“It shows things that haven't happened?” Fang Ji said.

He Cen froze.

“Look at me,” Yu Mei told him. “Keep your feet steady.”

She supported his forearm and helped him sit again. He did not fall. His image continued folding at the knees, then vanished below the frame.

A disappointed intake of breath sounded behind the door.

[Continuation not received]

It was drawing the preceding moment out, waiting for him to supply the ending it wanted.

He Cen removed his glasses. “I'm staying here. Don't drag me around because of that.”

“We'll cover it first.”

Two grooves bordered the window. A rolled sheet of metal protruded on the right. Yu Mei had noticed a low handle there without touching it. She settled He Cen against the wall and put the third bottle within his reach.

Fang Ji inspected the handle without raising his injured arm. A short parallel rod lay below it. He pointed with his chin.

Yu Mei opened Lin Mo's notebook to the exposed working face of the technical pages. The private fold stayed closed. The half-page diagram included a similar window, with two opposing dark blocks labeled inspection shutters.

The diagram said nothing about stopping all viewing. The shutters belonged to this particular opening. Lin Mo looked from the printed blocks to the actual grooves, making himself attend to the metal instead of the expressions caught behind it.

“Try that. Leave the main door handle alone.”

Yu Mei pushed the low handle. Metal slid across one corner of the window, cutting Lin Mo's displayed face in half. The applause grew abruptly louder, like an impatient audience demanding that someone move out of the way.

Then the handle jammed.

A flattened paper cup had lodged in the lower groove. Its rim was black with grease. Each retraction of the shutter must have crushed it farther inward.

Fang Ji offered to work it loose with his shoe. Yu Mei shook her head; his foot would land on the short rod. She crouched and took the exposed bottom of the cup between her fingers.

A hand appeared in the window.

It reached over her shoulder and stopped beside her neck. No corresponding hand occupied the platform, but the hairs on her nape rose. She spun around, struck her already sore shoulder against the doorframe, and dropped the cup.

The laughter thickened.

“There's nothing behind you,” Fang Ji said at once.

“I know.” Her breathing was ragged. “I couldn't help it.”

The hand remained against the inside of the glass, its fingers slowly curling with her breaths. Lin Mo wanted to check behind himself. The urge pricked along his spine, promising that one delayed glance would cost him something.

He forced his attention back to the groove.

“The cup's halfway out. Rest first. I'll watch behind you.”

She sat against the wall. Nobody took over the unfinished job. He Cen drank, tapping the bottle against his teeth. The two small clicks came back amplified from behind the door. His hand stopped.

“Let it listen,” Lin Mo said. “You still need the water.”

He Cen glared at the door and took another swallow. He choked, but kept hold of the bottle. Fang Ji swore. That sound was not replayed, which gave Lin Mo a strange measure of relief.

When Yu Mei returned to the groove, she positioned herself to see the platform. She drew out the crushed cup by degrees. Its base tore, leaving a scrap caught in the metal. Using the stiffer rolled rim, she dislodged that too.

The handle traveled all the way left.

The shutter covered the glass. The applause muffled as though someone had pressed a pillow over it. Twenty-seven remained on the panel, but the echo of He Cen drinking stopped.

Lin Mo waited several breaths before settling against his cushion. His mouth still tasted of blood. His shoulder hurt enough that he could not tell whether he, too, had tried to turn.

She had closed it, yet nobody immediately spoke of being safe. The narrow strip of metal had interrupted something close enough to hear their teeth knock against a bottle. That was enough for Lin Mo to want several quiet breaths before asking anything more of her.

Yu Mei held the handle until Fang Ji pointed out the open notch supporting its end. She released it slowly. The shutter stayed closed.

“Let me sit awhile,” He Cen said.

They did.

A black mark on his hand frightened him until Yu Mei pointed to the flaking paint on the bench. He rubbed it repeatedly, then hid his hand beneath his jacket. Every so often he looked up at the shutter seam.

Lin Mo did the same. Each drawn-out sound beyond it made him wonder whose injury had been touched without his noticing.

“Some of those aren't us,” Fang Ji said.

A woman was swearing behind the door. Water rattled into an enamel basin. There was no basin here and nobody with that voice.

“Could she be alive?” Yu Mei asked.

They listened. The water cut through her final word, followed by slippers scraping the floor. When the water returned, the word broke in exactly the same place.

“We'll remember it,” Lin Mo said. “If we can see where it comes from, we'll look.”

Yu Mei tightened the knot in her torn sleeve. “That face in the window. Was it hard for you to look at?”

Lin Mo studied the fingers emerging from his splint. Even his clean new nails had an unfamiliar shape. His clenched face in the glass had almost made him hesitate to recognize himself, delaying his notice of the blood.

“It's my face,” he said. “I don't want them using it like that.”

She brought the water close and let him take a small sip without brushing the sore inside his cheek.

“I don't even like showing people my ID photo,” He Cen muttered. “Here anybody gets to order a program.”

“Charge them next time,” Fang Ji said.

“I'm still not selling.”

Fang Ji's brief smile faded. He sat carefully, finding a position that let his injured shoulder rest.

The sounds behind the door remained distant. Lin Mo did not ask anyone to reopen the shutter as a test. Those frames might still be waiting for somebody to look up in pain.

The red-lettered man had been staring at the floor.

“There used to be catch-up viewing,” he said eventually. “Late arrivals could watch what they'd missed.”

“Did you use it?”

“Yes.”

His fingers rubbed the seam of his trousers. He described small boxes beneath the image and a control that let viewers jump to whichever part they wanted.

“Did it add hands like that?” Lin Mo asked.

“I never saw it add things.”

“You have now.”

Lin Mo did not ask whose worst moments he had watched. Making everyone listen to those stories would accomplish little here. Instead, he asked the man to draw the entrance he remembered in the dust, without touching the notebook.

Using his shoe, the man drew three boxes, crossed out the middle one, and said he had remembered wrong: the control had been on the right. Fang Ji watched without helping him explain away the error.

The crooked drawing opened no doors. But Lin Mo found a similar circular symbol at the right edge of the actual panel. Beneath it, a narrow mechanical slot ran toward the door's side latch.

Once rested, Yu Mei inspected the back of that latch.

[Open door after inspection shutter closes]

Rust obscured the line above, but this one remained legible. She opened the broad door a finger's width. No wind or person rushed out. A level passage followed the wall, wider than the wheelchair. Opposite stood the black frames they had seen through the glass.

Fang Ji could see a clear patch only a few steps inside. He stayed outside it.

“Can I rest a bit longer?” He Cen asked.

“We'll wait,” Lin Mo said.

Yu Mei eased the door shut without latching it, leaving the inspection shutter closed, then sat to recover strength in her hands. They used the phone only to photograph the mechanism's actual position. When she extinguished the screen, the battery read seventeen percent.

Lin Mo closed his eyes briefly. When he opened them, the red-lettered man was watching him, apparently waiting to learn whether he would still be allowed along.

“Remember the entrance you described,” Lin Mo told him. “Point it out if you see it inside.”

The man nodded.

A delicate scraping sound came through the unlatched door. Through the remaining gap, Lin Mo saw the nearest black frame illuminate.

It contained no face. Five blurred backs occupied the positions where they were sitting.

The viewpoint was just inside the broad door.

They had covered one window. Another had found a view from behind.
