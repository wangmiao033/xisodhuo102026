# Chapter 132: A Way That Spared the Shoulders

Fang Ji rested until he could open his own bottle. He still didn't raise his left arm.

He handed Yu Mei the phone. He'd stay by the rear door. If anything went wrong, she should call immediately, not wait to understand everything before returning. When she asked whether he still wanted to follow, he shook his head and put the broken broom back against the wall.

“This won't hold that beam up.”

He Cen finally agreed to take the vacated benches. Yu Mei helped him sit upright and waited for his dizziness to ease before supporting his forearm. He planted both feet, turned partway, and paused. After resting, he lowered himself onto the bench's edge. Fang Ji didn't lift him. His gray jacket padded his back again, the lens cloth in its breast pocket.

Lin Mo watched from his chair. In hospital, moving from bed to chair had felt like something accomplished in a sentence before the real journey began. Watching He Cen settle, he knew this step counted too.

The stranger placed the old bottle beside He Cen without touching his arm.

“Thanks. Other side. Left hand.”

He moved it and returned to his stool. No forgiveness passed between them. They didn't leave the bottle out of reach for want of forgiveness either.

Yu Mei came for the handles.

“Only the right side. Rear door stays open. We keep going while they can answer. If they can't, we return.”

Lin Mo agreed, then explained that the chair would be harder to hold on a descent. Stop before the slope. She listened and retied his loose left shoelace, shortening the ends so they wouldn't catch the caster.

Fang Ji leaned in the room's rear doorway, able to see both He Cen and the iron entrance ahead. The inner turn was hidden from him. Yu Mei had Lin Mo report their position every short stretch and Fang Ji answer, checking only that they could still hear each other here.

The barrier on the right remained down.

Yu Mei pressed the red footplate. The left shoulder pads rose close to the beam, empty leather swinging beneath it. Nobody occupied the male lane. No weight appeared on the floor, no new notice sounded.

She released the plate. The pads slowly descended.

A second press produced the same movement. Lin Mo saw the red lever pull a short chain along the axle inside the wall. The chain disappeared behind a partition. Seeing that segment couldn't prove everything beyond it worked through ordinary metal parts.

“I'll walk in. Watch whether it drops.”

She backed the chair into a secure position outside the right entrance and locked the brakes. Then she stepped along the female lane's boundary. White light passed over her; the label lit. The pads didn't fall. She tapped the rail with her shoe. Solid iron, no movement.

“Can walk without holding on here. Need to see the next bit.”

Lin Mo reminded her not to go beyond the mesh gate. She acknowledged him and approached it slowly. A loose nut wedged beneath its crooked lower edge left only enough room for someone sideways.

Rather than squeeze through, she kicked the nut into a clear corner. The gate opened onto a level landing before the downward slope. On the right wall, an open iron cover exposed another section of red lever.

“Still level here. Haven't gone downhill.”

Fang Ji answered. Hearing him nearby loosened something in Lin Mo's back.

Yu Mei returned for the wheelchair. The lane allowed only a few fingers of clearance beyond its wheels. She kept both off the boundary instead of hurrying. The right leg support passed the rail first, then the frame. Machinery whispered beside Lin Mo without pressing him down.

Female lane lit again.

A gasp came from beneath the left beam.

He turned. Empty pads, dust falling through light. The gasp repeated, followed by a man's indistinct “Can't hold it,” distant yet lodged inside the metal.

Yu Mei stopped.

“Someone there?”

Lin Mo saw no person. Half a cracked speaker protruded behind the beam, a black wire entering the wall below it. Sound came from there without answering. After a pause, the identical breath and the same unfinished ending of “Can't hold it” sounded again.

He asked Yu Mei to retreat half a wheel's length. The small light on the right dimmed, and the speaker grew quieter. They stopped. It repeated without becoming Xiao Lu or answering their words.

“Old voices,” Yu Mei said.

“This part sounds repeated.”

Lin Mo didn't ask her to advance so they could hear the next sentence. The man's breath was breaking. He could imagine someone calculating how much longer he would endure. Imagining it would only make Lin Mo hurry.

Yu Mei asked whether to continue.

“Only to the landing.”

She tightened her grip and pushed the last stretch. The gasping remained on the left, inaudible by the time they reached level ground.

His knee hadn't been compressed. No new pain troubled his shoulder. He'd crossed seated where Fang Ji couldn't readily walk. That was true. He didn't have to find a pleasant explanation before using it.

He let out his breath and called Fang Ji.

“Made it?”

“Level ground. Haven't gone down.”

“Then don't.”

Lin Mo's mouth twitched. Yu Mei checked the surface and parked against the side wall before examining the open cover. It contained wires of different thicknesses, gears, and a small gray display rather than a wall of screens.

Print remained beside the red lever.

Body-shape sampling

The next line flickered twice and settled on Female lane. Several short terms below were crossed out. Body outline shone in the last column.

Lin Mo stared. The machine had ignored what he'd said about himself. Now it exposed its chosen criterion as though no explanation had ever been necessary.

Yu Mei lowered the phone so he could see.

“Goes by your shape?”

“This field says so.”

His voice was level. His fingers tightened on the armrest. This wasn't the pleasure of finally being seen, nor entirely the anger of being misidentified. Its judgment had let him reach this place. Fang Ji's shoulder still hurt, but from inside Lin Mo could examine how to stop the beam.

“Photograph it.”

Afterward, Yu Mei returned the phone to the outer pocket beside the chair, lens inward to protect it from the metal cover. She didn't make him stand beneath the label or ask whether he'd admit it now.

Looking at the old burns on her hand, Lin Mo asked whether pushing while steadying herself on the rail had tired her.

“Yes.”

“Rest, then.”

She sat on a low concrete ledge, hands on her knees. Carrying water earlier had left a wet patch at her elbow. The display exempted her from a load. Her hands still shook. The machine hadn't saved her the effort written across her face.

“Those signs never care who's really carrying things.”

After resting a little, she asked whether the people waiting outside for Lin Mo would still be there.

He looked back the way he'd come. Repair room, blue door, collapsed scenery, the receiving hall beyond sight. He couldn't erase those walls and imagine Yuan had stood at the entrance for only a moment.

“They agreed to wait until the time we set. If something happened, they'd seek help as arranged.”

“Would they know if you came out?”

“If I actually reached that side. My phone hasn't had a signal.”

“Could the wall have opened again?”

His fingers paused. He remembered the gray wall. He'd also put it aside as an unsolvable problem while searching for water, then looking deeper. Her question made him realize he hadn't mentioned going back to check.

“Possibly. I don't know.”

“What if we could get back?”

“He Cen sees a doctor first. Fang Ji's hand and shoulder need one too. If you want out, we look for the way together.”

“Thought you'd only go farther down.”

Lin Mo studied the descending passage. Pausing hadn't added information to Xiao Lu's transfer record. Her death record outside still existed. If he found a door and retreated again, how would he tell the voice still waiting at the hospital that he hadn't found the answer?

“I want to go down,” he admitted. “Doesn't mean I want to shut your way out.”

Yu Mei nodded without using his sister or the people outside to decide for him. The scenery passage to the hall still had white light, she reminded him. The laundry was flooding. A return route needed inspecting again. Trusting a previously walked path had already failed them at the walkway.

Lin Mo kept it in mind without declaring that they had an escape plan. He asked her to tell He Cen afterward that he'd willingly recheck the original entrance. First they had to discover what remained along the route.

Behind them, Fang Ji asked why they'd stopped calling their positions.

“Still on the landing. Resting,” Yu Mei answered.

He acknowledged her without urging them on. Lin Mo heard him turn toward the room: they hadn't descended; they were still there. The awkward system worked only between these open doors. It felt more like someone waiting than any sign claiming a route was passable.

While Yu Mei rested, Lin Mo examined the wiring. One lower wire had broken before. Fine copper wrapped its join, insulated by a scrap of cloth yellowed with heat.

Someone had worked here, some time ago.

Inside the lever lay a curved slot with a cross-pin at its end. Perhaps it had once prevented the lever springing back. Now a film of rust seemed to hold it out, leaving half a rounded head to grip.

After resting, Yu Mei tested it with two fingers without touching the plate.

Nothing moved.

“Need pliers to force that.”

Lin Mo asked her to wait. Each time the red lever went down, the gray wheel turned halfway and the pin's position shifted too. Perhaps the lever held the pin under pressure rather than rust locking it solid.

Yu Mei depressed the inner footplate, connected to the same axle.

The lever reached the slot's bottom. The cross-pin loosened. She pushed it inward with two fingers. When she eased her foot slightly, the lever caught against the pin instead of immediately returning upward.

The shoulder pads stayed raised.

Keeping her foot partly down, she called for Fang Ji to check outside. He could see the upper parts hadn't fallen, he reported, but the detection plate below remained lit.

The pin had gone in less than a finger's depth. A shallow groove still showed behind its head, suggesting it needed to go farther to hold securely.

“Press it again. Don't leave it caught halfway.”

She complied, removed the pin, and let the lever return slowly. Fang Ji reported the pads descending. Nobody had been sent inside to add a living weight to the experiment.

Two old scratches marked the cover's inner corner, one long and one short, beside the pin's second groove. Lin Mo asked Yu Mei to retrieve the phone and light what looked like a stain.

It wasn't a stain.

Three small penciled characters. The first was half worn away; the other two remained clear.

Get the person clear first.

Yu Mei looked up. Lin Mo studied the gray strokes, suddenly reluctant to announce what came next. Someone had stood here before, found the cross-pin, even left instructions to get the person out first.

A tiny paper corner protruded beneath the cover's lower edge.

Yu Mei brought the light closer. Dried oil stuck the paper to the cover, and a narrow retaining strip ran across it. She didn't tug. Beneath the strip, Lin Mo could make out a penciled arrow pointing toward the pin's second groove. Its other end remained hidden behind the panel.
