# Chapter 126: The Knocking Behind the Door

The dragging shoe stopped below the slope.

Lin Mo waited until the red glow began climbing the wall again. The man braced with his right hand. Whenever his left foot touched down, one corner of his mouth twitched. Fang Ji kicked a loose piece of metal away from the wall to clear his path.

“Sit here first,” Yu Mei said.

He shook his head, took another half step, and nearly buckled. He slid down against the wall at the top of the slope. Dirt marked the dressing's edges, but no fresh red spread through its center.

Lin Mo asked where the glass outside the blue door had come from.

“Broken when I got there.”

“Did you walk through it?”

“No. I heard knocking and tried another way.”

“Then your leg?” Fang Ji asked.

The man pointed downhill. After a pause, he said a falling seat had cut him. He closed his eyes before anyone could ask more.

The blue door stood a few steps beyond the corner. Jagged glass edged its broken window; more covered the floor below. Kicks had dented the lower panel. Two knocks came from behind it, followed, after a pause, by two more.

“Is that what He Cen heard?” Lin Mo asked.

Yu Mei had only heard his imitation. Fang Ji shone the phone beside the door. A gray jacket hung from a broken hook, its sleeve trailing through glass. Below the collar, a black button showed.

“Still here.”

Lin Mo almost smiled. They had something to bring back. Finding it hadn't shortened the route he'd sent them down.

Fang Ji pulled the door. Glass scraped beneath it; it opened only two fingers wide. Yu Mei told him to stop pushing with his injured palm and shone the phone through the window.

Lidded white water containers stood on a rack in the small outer room. A box of bottles sat against the wall. Beyond an open inner door, the thick pipe bent toward an upright old tank, water seeping from its base.

The knocking resumed. Something black rose beside the tank and fell. Fang Ji called through the window. It struck twice without answering.

“Stop shouting,” the red-lettered man said.

“What do you know?” Lin Mo asked.

“The noise brings things up from below.”

“Have you seen that, or are you guessing?”

His jaw tightened. “I've heard footsteps.”

Lin Mo heard them too: faint tapping deep in the pipe, faster than the knocks, like hard soles running across an empty stage. He couldn't abandon the water on this man's guess. Dismissing the uncertainty wouldn't make him clever either.

“Open the door first. Stay out of the inner room.”

Fang Ji found half a bamboo broom wedged behind the bucket rack. Bracing the rack with his shoe, he pulled it free with his left hand and swept glass into a recess by the wall. Fine fragments stuck to the wet floor.

Yu Mei took down the jacket. A sharp sliver fell from its cuff. She rolled the sleeves inward and put it on a dry ledge.

“We're taking that back. Don't use his clothes under the wheels again.”

Fang Ji worked the broom's broken wooden edge beneath the door. An old glazing strip, still studded with glass, had jammed in the gap. At his second attempt it shifted. The door sprang outward and struck his knuckles.

He jerked back, swearing against the wall. Lin Mo's knee tightened at the impact. There was no fresh blood; Fang Ji shook his left hand while Yu Mei took the broom and pushed the strip inward.

The opening widened. The knocking stopped.

Nobody moved. Lin Mo watched the dark shape hanging beside the tank, pointed end over the standing water. Several breaths later, water surged down the pipe. The shape lifted, struck the tank, and fell.

Twice.

“A handle,” Yu Mei said.

She held the light steady. A small float chamber filled beside the tank, moving a short rod. Its broken end had released the metal handle that should have been attached there. Now the handle hung loose and struck the side with each movement. Lin Mo could see that much. The darker pipes beyond remained hidden.

“Patient little bastard,” Fang Ji muttered.

He stepped inside to hold the door while Yu Mei lit the room. Beside the rack stood a wide bench with two rusty crossbraces. He tested its weight before bringing Lin Mo's chair onto dry floor. The red glow remained outside. He told its owner to wait clear of the glass.

Cool damp air met Lin Mo's face. Leaning back eased the spasms in his lower back. Yu Mei tucked the rolled jacket beside his waist, over his own coat, keeping the glass-marked cuff away from skin.

“He can lend it for a moment.”

Lin Mo nodded. Sweat and old soap lingered in the cloth. In hospital, he'd resented how slowly the carers changed his clothes. Now he wanted this little thickness against his back to stay.

A curled notice hung above the containers. Fang Ji made out DRINKING and reached for a lid.

“Wait.” Lin Mo indicated the bottom.

A long hairline crack ran down the white plastic. Water stains extended to the rack's leg. The lid was still fitted, but the container's mouth was damaged. Yu Mei moved it enough to smell something sour and closed it again.

The second container was empty. Water in the third reflected an overhead light.

The room's overhead light wasn't on.

Fang Ji withdrew his hand. The hard-soled footsteps paused inside the pipe. A faint laugh rose from beneath the tank. Lin Mo clenched his teeth. In the laundry drain, the lights had fitted inside a small ring of water. Here they had a whole container.

Yu Mei backed away without tipping it. Her heel struck the box. Plastic bottles knocked together.

“What about these?”

The cardboard was sodden, but the bottles stood upright. She turned one beneath the phone. Its sealing ring was unbroken, its sides undistorted, the water clear. The label said drinking water, one liter.

Fang Ji felt through the box with his left hand. Five sound bottles. A sixth had a crooked cap and black mud underneath. They left that one.

“Take one outside before opening it,” Lin Mo said.

Yu Mei glanced at him, as though afraid he'd substitute more watching for a drink. She took two bottles. Fang Ji moved an empty box obstructing the chair.

“Don't touch that patch,” the man outside said.

“Stop,” Lin Mo told Fang Ji.

Half a wet footprint lay beneath the box, leading toward the tank although its toes pointed into the outer room. Fang Ji's sole already overlapped its edge. He kept still. When Yu Mei lowered the phone, a narrow bright rim appeared inside the smooth mark.

Like the feet in the white light before the seats.

“You saw this earlier?” Lin Mo asked the man outside.

“I saw it pass beneath the glass.”

“Why only tell us now?”

“I didn't know it was still here!”

As his voice rose, the footsteps rushed closer through the pipe. Fang Ji hissed for him to shut up. Yu Mei held the light steady. Lin Mo watched the print stretch until it touched a rack leg, then shrink back.

The handle knocked twice.

The print shivered with it. Fang Ji's shoe slid slightly forward. He grabbed the doorframe, shoulder tipping as something pulled, unable to bear down with his injured palm.

Water was creeping over the threshold toward his foot.

“Lift that foot. Put it on the dry patch beside you.”

Fang Ji transferred his weight to the frame. Yu Mei steadied his left elbow. He pulled his shoe from the print and planted it on a dry tile. The moment his sole left the water, a heavy step struck inside the pipe, like someone missing the last stair.

He stayed upright, face pale.

“No water from those containers,” Yu Mei said.

Nobody argued. Lin Mo didn't send anyone behind the tank because one instruction of his had worked. He asked Fang Ji to draw the chair back beside the door, freeing the dry floor. Yu Mei brought out the remaining three bottles, one wedged beneath her elbow and two against her chest.

Then she crouched beside Lin Mo, eased the gray jacket from behind his waist, and rolled it again. His back lost its little support. He waited against the chair while Fang Ji steadied it.

Outside, the sealing ring snapped as Yu Mei opened a bottle. She drank a small mouthful, waited, then gave Fang Ji a turn. Lin Mo didn't take that sip as proof of safety. They had intact seals and clear water to judge by, and no better source available.

After Fang Ji drank, she held the bottle for Lin Mo's two mouthfuls, then offered it to the man on the floor. When he reached for it, she kept hold. A few swallows, then she capped it.

“Five bottles. One open. We'll divide them back there.”

An old carrying bag had lain beneath the empty box Fang Ji moved. He packed three bottles into it. One handle's stitching had gone; he would have to support the bottom. Yu Mei took the other two against her left arm and touched his sleeve with her free hand.

“Did that pull hurt your shoulder?”

“Arm's tired.”

“Then sit. Don't take me down with you.”

He sat on dry floor with the bag between his feet. His wet sole left a mark facing his toes. After watching it, he rubbed it out with his shoe. The ordinary moisture smeared away. No extra foot appeared.

“It wasn't like this before,” the stranger said.

Fang Ji looked up. “Neither was I. Which part did you mean?”

The man looked at Lin Mo instead.

Lin Mo rested his tingling left fingers on the armrest. With water past his throat, he could manage a full sentence.

“Do you mean you could fetch water without being dragged away? Or that you didn't have to fetch it yourself?”

“I didn't come to argue.”

“I didn't ask you to.”

Red flickered beside the man's neck. He rubbed a muddy clot from his trousers. Dried blood mixed with it, spreading the stain.

“It came to our seats.”

Yu Mei held the bottles closer. Fang Ji studied the broken words beside the stranger's shoulder and asked what kind of seats.

“Where you watch people perform,” Lin Mo said.

Fang Ji looked down at his shoes.

Lin Mo didn't describe Old Chen's disappearance. An admission didn't oblige him to lay every detail before this man and wait for him to choose a part that had hurt him too. The hand that had grown lighter in Lin Mo's grasp couldn't be made to explain anything for him.

“Do you still think they can stop?” the man asked.

Lin Mo looked toward the blue door. The handle kept knocking. Water seeped over the threshold, still separated from Fang Ji by dry floor.

“I've seen people stop. I've seen people refuse.”

“Later, nobody listened to me—”

“Tell me later. First finish telling us where you've been.”

The man fell silent. Fang Ji drew the bag closer rather than handing it over. Lin Mo knew more remained unsaid. He didn't want it now, and had no strength for it. A time when commands might have failed was something he could ask about separately from this man's refusal to withdraw.

Yu Mei folded the gray jacket with its black button showing and asked how much longer Lin Mo could sit.

“I need the bed.”

Fang Ji rose slowly, waiting for his arm before taking the handles. Water had eased the rawness in Lin Mo's throat. His chest remained tight.

“Let's find the way back.”

Below the slope, the gray door led to the collapsed platform. On the blue door's other side, the thick pipe passed through the wall and continued left, higher up. Beneath it stood a narrow dark door edged with pale bricks.

Yu Mei approached, holding the jacket.

A hinge gave a long squeal. Then someone swore.

“Stop pushing! There's a bucket behind it!”

Lin Mo lifted his head. Hoarse and muffled by two walls, the voice still carried He Cen's impatient ending.
