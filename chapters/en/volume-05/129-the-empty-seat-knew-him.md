# Chapter 129: The Empty Seat Knew Him

They collected the drinking water first.

The carrying bag's stitching was giving way. Yu Mei left only two bottles inside and looped its doubled handles over one wheelchair push handle, keeping the bottom clear of the wheel. She, He Cen, and the stranger each took another. Lin Mo's two old small bottles came too, every opened cap checked so they wouldn't arrive carrying only wet clothes.

Fang Ji waited behind the chair. Yu Mei supported Lin Mo's calf. He planted his left foot on dry ground and stopped halfway forward, in pain. The jolt at the bed's foot had done more than he'd felt lying down.

“Slowly,” Fang Ji said.

Lin Mo meant to say he knew. Only breath came out. They waited until his foot was secure, then helped him turn into the chair. His heel settled on the padding. Briefly he felt lighter, before his body sank heavily against the backrest.

Yu Mei adjusted rolled clothing at his waist, an old garment from beside the bed. He Cen wore his own gray jacket, sleeves rolled above hands reddened by fever.

“You first,” Lin Mo told him.

“Get your chair through, then the door's clear. I'll follow.”

Fang Ji ended the debate by entering the maintenance passage. Yu Mei followed and looked back halfway. He Cen kept one hand on the wall and held the bottle with the other, stopping every few steps. He could still hear and answer her.

The red-lettered man came last, edging along the opposite wall. He didn't bargain away his bottle for support. Where the passage joined the ramp, Fang Ji waited for all five before following the dry route he'd inspected.

The canvas remained in the laundry. Water sounded behind them. Yu Mei looked back without returning for the strap. She'd brought only the drier patchwork covering from He Cen's bed. What they couldn't carry kept dripping.

Water had spread outside the blue door. Fang Ji kept to dry tiles opposite, lighting only their feet, avoiding the containers with reflected lamps. The tank handle knocked faster, like someone losing patience.

He Cen paused beneath the hook where his jacket had hung.

“That close.”

Yu Mei urged him away from the water. He followed without asking why he hadn't found the shortcut before. The question could be put to Lin Mo, to anyone trapped here for so long. Asking wouldn't give them another sound tile.

The wall curved inward, revealing two rows of seats with their backs toward them. Most had toppled. One stood upright against the wall, taller than its neighbors, a dark screen set into its back. No light followed them. No applause came from the hall.

Fang Ji stopped two meters away.

“Around the back. You can see the door.”

The unpainted door stood open, a bench corner visible inside. Its approach was wide, though broken wires crowded beneath the seats on the right. Lin Mo asked Fang Ji to take He Cen inside first. He could wait on dry ground.

“I can see it,” He Cen said.

Then he spent a long while catching his breath against the wall. Yu Mei took his bottle, freeing his other hand, and led him slowly across. Lin Mo watched the gray jacket disappear inside. He looked away only after she confirmed there was somewhere to sit.

Red brightened beside him.

The stranger had stopped too. His broken name stretched outward, as though a thread pulled it toward the upright seat. He tried covering it. His hand passed through the letters and lit half his face red.

One faint character appeared on the black screen.

Seatkeeper's missing first character.

The man recoiled, his left leg buckling until his back struck the wall. The character dimmed. Red gathered beside his shoulder again, its beginning still absent.

Fang Ji raised the phone silently.

“Was this your seat?” Lin Mo asked.

“Not this one.”

“Why does it know you?”

“Seats in the same row can connect.”

He stiffened at his own answer. Something besides fear entered his face. His eyes moved between screen and armrest. Fang Ji warned him to stay back, but he took half a step forward.

The screen relit. A narrow thread drew his broken name from his shoulder. Its last characters returned to one line. Stroke by stroke, the missing first one assembled.

Seatkeeper

Half a smaller line remained beneath it.

Local physical entity handover

Lin Mo's throat dried. Those three complete Chinese characters were more familiar than this dusty face. When they had hung on mirrors, Old Chen would check Lin Mo's sleeves before asking, through the pain in his legs, whether he'd got out.

“It recognizes the name,” Fang Ji said. “How do we know about the person?”

“I haven't said we know everything.”

The stranger reached the seat's back. Without sitting, he touched a pale recess at the armrest's front. The screen flashed twice. Crowded old lettering appeared; three lines at the bottom remained legible.

Source owner in breach

Rack C, Code Seven

Applicant: Seatkeeper

Lin Mo started lifting his left hand. Shoulder pain forced it down. He asked Fang Ji to let him see the phone without handing it over, keeping both man and screen in sight.

“What comes below?”

The man's hand stayed still.

“The line underneath.”

“Look yourself.”

His tone was sharp, but his finger shifted enough to expose the covered text beside a worn pale patch.

Withdrawal: Not executed

Yu Mei emerged and stood beside Lin Mo. Seeing his face, she set her bottle on dry ground without asking whether this was worth stopping for.

He asked Fang Ji to photograph the visible lines, including the screen's cracked edge and the finger's position. No cropping everything down to words. He didn't want only his memory to recount it later. Fang Ji steadied the phone with his left hand, took the photograph, and showed him the thumbnail.

“This is an old application you've connected to now,” Lin Mo said. “Are you still saying you could withdraw then?”

“I said I could before the last moment.”

“Did you press it?”

The man watched Not executed without answering.

Lin Mo remembered people moving panels before the cabinet, Xiao Lu telling him not to reach inside again with his injured hand. They'd all searched for a way to get that body out. They had even checked the soles of their shoes in case they'd missed a thread.

“I thought he'd give in,” the man said.

“Do what?”

“Admit he'd crossed the boundary. Say he'd leave you alone.”

Lin Mo's back tightened. The man stared at the screen, as though speaking faster might make it turn the page.

“Then you kept pulling. Finally the button went gray. When I—”

“Don't put the part where you didn't press it after the part where we tried pulling him out.”

His mouth stayed open. No more words came. The screen lit his damaged eyebrow like an ordinary man before an overbright machine. He could no longer retreat behind a faceless name.

Yu Mei touched the chair back. Only then did Lin Mo notice how far forward he'd moved. He leaned back, still breathing hard.

“I won't have someone hit you for me. I won't write that helping move my bed settled this either.”

“What do you want me to do?”

“Stop rewriting that moment.”

Fang Ji quietly asked whether they were going. Lin Mo nodded and waited rigidly while he turned the chair toward the door. The stranger didn't follow.

Two little boxes flashed at the top of the screen. One retained the character for exit; a broken line occupied the other. His fingertip moved beneath exit. After several touches, the dark screen hummed.

“Leave it,” Fang Ji said.

“I used to leave through here.”

Lin Mo looked back. The man pressed into the recess until his finger whitened. A fresh notice slowly appeared.

Remote seat invalid

The same as beside his shoulder.

He released it and pressed again. Half the screen lit. Rack C, Code Seven remained at the bottom, an old account he couldn't turn past. He flattened his whole palm against it, veins rising across his hand, and said something inaudible.

No door opened.

Yu Mei approached but stopped two steps away. She told him to take his water. He looked down as though only then remembering he'd left the bottle beside the chair leg.

“What happens if I sit?”

Nobody answered for him. Fang Ji hadn't sat there and wouldn't try it for his benefit. Lin Mo studied the dark depression in the cushion, remembering the suspended seat in the receiving hall.

“If you want to try, say what the risks are. Don't make us stand here while you keep pressing.”

The man looked toward the open room. Yu Mei had returned to help He Cen lean against the wall. Someone asked where the water was, as ordinary as a visiting neighbor.

He slowly withdrew his hand. Twice pain stopped him bending before he retrieved the bottle.

Lin Mo didn't urge him faster.

As Fang Ji pushed past the screen, the complete name dimmed with the man following behind. The bottom line of the old application shone a moment longer.

At the repair room's entrance, He Cen asked what they'd seen.

Lin Mo didn't announce a complete catalog of guilt. Uncertainty didn't erase everything either. He described how the screen had reacted to the man, the words actually displayed, and the failure to withdraw he had acknowledged.

When he reached the other man's disappearance, He Cen lowered his head and unrolled his gray cuffs.

“What was his name?”

“Old Chen.”

“Surname Chen?”

“He asked us to call him that. I don't know his full name.”

Bitterness filled Lin Mo's mouth. After all the counters visited and numbers checked since returning, someone asked a name and he still had only Old Chen. His ignorance differed from the stranger's. Inside the same answer, both sounded empty.

“He has a son. He was looking for his name too, and an answer he never heard. I promised, if I found it, I'd keep the actual words.”

Yu Mei moved an overturned lampshade so He Cen could stretch his legs. What answer? she asked. Lin Mo said only that the theater had supplied a false one, made someone speak a dutiful child's words. Old Chen had wanted the original.

“What if the original hurts?” He Cen asked.

“Then I can't make it nicer for him.”

The man by the door still held his bottle. Lin Mo didn't look at him. He'd been clear enough without pursuing his face for a reaction to every sentence.

After a silence, He Cen asked whether the man would stay tonight. He addressed Yu Mei. Lin Mo didn't decide for the whole room.

“There's dry ground. I won't stop him leaving. If he stays, he leaves other people's things alone.”

“I didn't say throw him in the water.”

“Neither did I.”

Fang Ji took the two bottles from the push handle and put them with the other three. Lin Mo's old bottles went separately beside his bag. Four new one-liter bottles remained sealed. The shared fifth was still more than half full. He Cen's old bottle held less than a third; a little water covered the other's bottom.

“Tell us when you drink.”

Nobody gave the man with the red letters extra. Nobody secretly poured his share away. Yu Mei gave He Cen the open bottle. He drank too quickly and coughed. The water didn't immediately cool his forehead.

Once the coughing stopped, Lin Mo asked Fang Ji to move his chair farther from the low stool by the entrance.

“I don't want to sit beside him now.”

Fang Ji nodded and moved him.
