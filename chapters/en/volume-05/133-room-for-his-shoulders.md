# Chapter 133: Room for His Shoulders

Yu Mei left the paper alone.

She pointed from the groove still exposed on the cross-pin to a chipped socket inside the cover. Pushed fully home, the rounded head should fit there. With the lever pressing against it, they'd only managed half the distance, at an angle.

“Lever needs to go lower.”

Something hard blocked the footplate's underside. Oil had darkened the wood until Lin Mo mistook it for the floor. Yu Mei nudged it with her shoe. A broken packing block slid free.

It fitted exactly beneath the plate's lowest point. Pressure had cut a deep curved mark into its top; the lower half was crumbling. Someone had put it there, for an unknown purpose. Now it prevented the last little movement.

She set it beside the wall and pressed again.

The lever went slightly farther. The pin's second groove emerged completely. The display flickered and dimmed without declaring this safe.

“Might work.”

“Don't release it yet.”

Lin Mo asked her to ease the plate back and watch for catching. It returned, the pads descended, and no unexpected sound followed. She depressed it fully again. The pin shifted slightly. Someone still had to push it at the same time.

Keeping her foot down while bending sideways left Yu Mei awkwardly positioned. Her sleeve touched a raised metal edge before she could reach the inner pin head. She withdrew.

“Can hold it. Can't reach that way.”

“How far can you release the plate before it rises?”

“Any. Needs holding.”

“Much pressure?”

She changed feet to judge. Not very much, but constant. Lin Mo looked at his newly tied left shoe. The rubbed heel was still inside it.

He didn't immediately extend his foot.

“Lower it first. I'll try holding it. Keep your hand clear. If I say no, take over.”

She looked at his knee. Left foot only, he explained. His right leg wouldn't move. Lock the chair first, and don't pull him forward if he couldn't reach.

“See if you can reach.”

She moved him half a foot closer, both rear wheels on level ground, and locked the brakes. His sole reached the plate's rear half without his shoulders leaving the cushion.

Yu Mei pressed the front half down, then gradually removed her weight. A steady upward force traveled through his shoe to the back of his knee. He could hold it, but not easily. After a dozen or so breaths, his heel began to burn.

“Take it.”

She did immediately. He withdrew his foot. Neither touched the pin.

The pads hung on the left. Nobody was beneath them. He didn't have to wait until he truly couldn't hold on before calling a stop.

Yu Mei moved the chair back and suggested water, then remembered they hadn't brought any. She called for Fang Ji to put the opened bottle by the rear door and went to fetch it herself. Lin Mo remained on the landing within hearing distance, phone on the armrest lighting the cover.

The old gasping resumed.

This time he heard half a laugh after “Can't hold it.” At first he thought it belonged to the person applying pressure. On its second repetition, it sounded more like someone laughing to appease through fear, cut short by his own breath.

He didn't want a third.

When Yu Mei returned, he asked whether she could remove the loose plug beside the speaker, silencing only this sound. She inspected the wire. It ran to the broken speaker, not the lever. After asking Fang Ji to watch the pads outside, she eased the plug out.

Silence.

The frame hadn't moved, Fang Ji reported. She left the plug in view without severing the cable or claiming she'd released the recorded person.

Lin Mo drank twice. When Fang Ji asked about his foot, he said it still moved. Holding the plate had made his heel hot. He'd stopped.

“Don't put your foot where my shoulder was.”

“That's why I stopped.”

Yu Mei's fingers were clear of the dangerous gap. The lever had returned. Stopping had actually ended the attempt; nobody remained beneath it waiting for his next decision.

She capped the bottle and sat on the ledge. Metal had rubbed a small patch of her palm red. When she spread her fingers, the old burn tightened with it.

One glance told her what Lin Mo was wondering.

“Burn's old. Hot oil.”

“You cooked?”

“Sold breakfast. Fried food. On my feet before dawn.”

Lin Mo remembered his uncertain answer when Fang Ji asked what shops outside sold. White and brown shapes in a plastic bag beside the car. He'd only resented the delay. He hadn't even noticed whether the brown things were fried dough.

“That breakfast shop I mentioned to Fang Ji probably had an oil pan too.”

“Plenty of shops. Who knows which.”

She didn't invite another description or promise to cook for everyone if they got out. Looking at the scar, Lin Mo realized how nearly he'd taken a profession and assigned her all their future breakfasts.

She flexed her fingers.

“I want to get out and sit somewhere. Eat what somebody else made.”

“Mm.”

“Don't just grunt. Don't tell me they're all waiting for me to heat the oil.”

He finally smiled. “Won't. I'm waiting for someone else's cooking too.”

She glanced at him without smiling and rested her palm on her knee. Neither touched the lever. Without a voice hurrying them, they discovered how slowly a few minutes could pass.

Lin Mo said they could return for the day if she didn't want to continue.

“I'll tell you,” she said impatiently. “Every time I help, I don't need another speech about how I don't have to. When I say rest, let me rest.”

“Then we rest.”

Her face eased. Water shifted inside the bottle at her feet. The lever remained still. Lin Mo didn't replace his permission with more thanks. He waited until she stood before studying the distance between plate and pin again.

She rolled her sleeve farther back, exposing the burn she'd brought from an ordinary life. He kept her wish to eat someone else's breakfast alongside Fang Ji's question about the streetlights.

After resting, he proposed another attempt. Turn the pin head into reach before lowering the lever. He would hold the plate only long enough for her to move her hand. If it didn't align, release immediately. No stretching it with just a little longer.

Yu Mei turned the head outward. It had some play inside its collar; turned this way, she didn't need to reach deep into the cover.

“I'll push. Doesn't fit, we stop.”

She depressed the plate, waited for his left foot to take over, then stepped onto firm floor beside it. The red lever trembled. Only after he quietly confirmed he could hold it did she reach for the pin.

It missed on the first attempt.

“Stop.”

She caught the plate as he lifted his foot and let the lever return slowly. Neither hurried into another attempt on the theory that faster might work. Crouching, she found hard oily dirt beneath the pin. She gently scraped it with the packing block's straight edge rather than probing the gap with a knife.

The second time, the pin passed through the second groove. Its rounded head met the socket.

A click.

Lin Mo kept his foot down. Yu Mei placed hers at the plate's edge, ready to catch a rebound, and lightly tested the pin with her right hand. Once sure it wouldn't slide out by itself, she had him ease off.

The lever caught on the pin and stayed lowered. The plate was released. The pads remained raised.

Gray text lit the display.

This frame locked for maintenance

Lin Mo waited for a change. The text stayed. The pin didn't withdraw. He'd brought his foot back; his heel was hot without the wet sting of freshly broken skin. Yu Mei told him not to reach down and examine it himself. Let the foot rest first.

Once the lever clearly wasn't rebounding, she looked again at the shoe rubbing his heel and decided to crouch and loosen his left laces. Lowering the sock a little revealed the old red mark, skin intact. She spared their dressings, moving the shoe's back clear of the irritated spot instead. No pushing against the floor with that foot for now.

Fang Ji called to ask whether he could approach the entrance.

“Entrance, yes. Keep off the plate.”

He followed the wall and studied the line that had caught him. Male lane still shone. The raised beam now left half an arm's length above his shoulder. He didn't enter simply because it looked empty.

Lin Mo described exactly how they'd locked it and what they'd seen.

“No person underneath.”

“I heard.”

“The notice says this frame. Says nothing about farther down.”

“Wasn't planning to carry you down there at a run.”

Fang Ji looked toward Yu Mei. She stood beside the inner plate, ready to catch the lever. Lin Mo's chair had moved clear of the retreat route. The pin engaged its second groove. The pads were raised. Every precaution they could take was visible; he wasn't being asked to trust an unseen button.

“I want to cross,” Fang Ji said. “We'll need this route again. Can't always wait for you two to fetch water.”

He wasn't volunteering on Lin Mo's behalf. He touched his right-palm dressing, lowered his aching left shoulder, and took a small step carrying nothing.

His sole reached the male plate. Red lettering lit.

The beam stayed up.

He waited several breaths on that first step before bringing his other foot forward. His face was pale. Nothing pressed his shoulder again. He could stand upright without pushing against leather.

Yu Mei remained beside the plate. Only when he cleared the male lane and reached the landing did Lin Mo unclench his left fingers. He hadn't noticed tightening them.

“Across.”

Fang Ji didn't smile. He sat against the wall and released a long breath. His shoulder still hurt, his palm remained dressed. Crossing hadn't made either injury trivial.

Lin Mo told him to rest, in no hurry to continue himself. Listening to distant water, the three of them could finally distinguish their own breathing without the old recording.

Yu Mei checked the pin. Its head still met the socket. She showed Fang Ji what to inspect on returning. The maintenance lock wasn't a promise of permanence; raised pads alone weren't enough to check.

He nodded and walked back by the same route, still carrying nothing. At the repair room's rear door, he told He Cen both companions remained inside. He'd crossed without being compressed again.

“A way out?” He Cen asked.

“Haven't seen one. Got past this frame.”

Hearing that through the doorway eased Lin Mo's shoulders. Nobody announced that every deeper door would work this way. Nobody made a successful crossing prove Fang Ji's earlier pain worthwhile.

Pushed fully home, the pin head no longer blocked the cover. Yu Mei supported it and tried opening it farther. The retaining strip stretched across its lower edge loosened.

Several old sheets stuck to its back came into view.

The outermost held grids and arrows, with erased and rewritten words at its foot. Lin Mo noticed a handwritten instruction, more writing folded out of sight beyond it.

This stops the frame. The classification is still active.
