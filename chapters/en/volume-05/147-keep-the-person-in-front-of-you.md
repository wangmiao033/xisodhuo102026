# Chapter 147: Keep the Person in Front of You

Fang Ji interrupted the voice.

“Next comes a wall.”

He did not even look around. The voice drifted along the passage, asking how he knew there wasn't someone behind it. Fang Ji shifted his bandaged palm onto a clean part of his clothes and said nothing more.

Lin Mo found the copied reference in his notebook. He would not put the whole book on the tray. His sister's photograph was near the front, Old Chen's private fold farther back. Read only or not, he refused to offer one opening everything he carried.

Yu Mei found a page with blank space and asked if she could tear it out.

“A finger's width from the binding. Don't pull the pages before it.”

She creased it with her nail and tore slowly. The edge came away uneven. Lin Mo tried to hold the pen. His injured index finger would not grip properly; the tip dragged a diagonal line across the paper. Anger rose in him. Even copying a few characters had become something he had to do with an audience.

Yu Mei did not take the pen away. She asked whether he wanted to rest or have her write.

“You write. I'll read the numbers and check them afterward.”

She rested the sheet on the concrete ledge. He divided the long reference into groups, wary of mixing it with the fragments that kept appearing on the screens. After each group, he checked her writing backward against the original. Near a final dash her shoulder pulled painfully. She stopped with the pen still touching the paper, leaving a dark spot of ink.

“Wait.”

He swallowed the next words. He Cen opened his eyes at the sudden quiet and asked what had happened. Yu Mei explained that her shoulder hurt. He made room beside him. Neither addressed the voice outside.

Watching the paper, Fang Ji said there was someone he wanted to search for, too.

Lin Mo looked up without asking a name. When they became separated, Fang Ji said, the person had still been able to walk. Later he had seen half a face in a window. He had never dared identify it. He stopped there, as though waiting for someone to promise that more details would produce a result.

“Do you have a reference?” Lin Mo asked.

Fang Ji shook his head.

Lin Mo turned the original page so he could see that the search was not by name. This reference had come from the basement record; its successive entries could be matched. They had found no equivalent route from a face or a familiar form of address. He did not want Fang Ji to give away a piece of his past in return for more talking windows.

Fang Ji studied the paper, then said he understood.

Disappointment showed as his left hand released his clothing. Lin Mo endured the uncomfortable silence. He did not promise that finding his sister would automatically make him able to find everyone else.

“If I see another way to search, I'll tell you.”

“That'll do,” Fang Ji said. “Don't ask what they looked like.”

He Cen glanced toward them and closed his eyes again. Beyond the room, the voice was still asking which one they wanted. The little shelter felt narrower. Each person carried something that would not fit on the tray. Lin Mo's better documentation did not make anyone else's missing person unimportant.

He waited until Fang Ji looked away before asking Yu Mei to continue.

When the copy was finished, Lin Mo checked it three times.

The source reference, the forced-preservation entry, the end of the arrival receipt: all there. No new name, no added claim of kinship, none of Old Chen's papers. It was a fresh transcription, not a system-issued credential. He wrote COPY in the corner. The crooked letters remained legible.

They did not leave immediately. Yu Mei put the pen back and rested for another ten minutes. Lin Mo told He Cen they would go to the gray door again and return if it demanded an object be retained or a living person connected.

“You make every trip sound like you're going onstage,” He Cen said.

Lin Mo stared at him.

“Just tell me where you're going. I can hear you. Every step doesn't have to be a guarantee.”

Lin Mo nodded. Buried in his careful account was a need to convince them he would not make another mistake. No promise could unmake what he had said about that load-bearing beam.

Fang Ji remained where he could see both ends of the passage as Yu Mei pushed the wheelchair out.

Old Chen had vanished from beneath the sign. An empty hospital corridor had replaced him. A trolley rolled across it, its wheels mixing with the wheelchair's quiet noise. Lin Mo watched the real gray door and asked Yu Mei to stop in their previous position.

The tray was empty.

Another line emerged on the glass as they approached.

[TRANSCRIPT ACCEPTED / SEARCH DOES NOT CERTIFY OWNERSHIP]

Lin Mo checked that Yu Mei held only the copied sheet. She laid it in the shallow grooves, keeping her fingers out. One small plate said Read. At the other end was Return. Nothing descended when she released the paper.

“Read it.”

She pressed the plate. The ink flashed; groups of characters appeared in the glass. One group was missing. Lin Mo called a halt. Yu Mei pressed Return, and the paper moved outward half an inch. She waited for it to stop before taking it.

The ragged edge had left the last characters outside the readable area.

He told her to straighten the sheet, leave space above, and avoid pushing it deeper. This time the complete reference appeared. He checked it from beginning to end before she selected Search.

The central dark surface rose slightly.

Lin Mo kept his eyes down. The known final transfer appeared, with Core Backup still named as receiver. A dark box beside it said current access had not yet been verified.

[SEARCH MATCH]
[READ EXISTING ITEM]

Below was a larger, brighter option.

[RELATED VIEWING]

“Look first,” the voice murmured. “Then you'll know whether she's still there.”

Yu Mei's hand paused. Related Viewing occupied most of the panel. Read Existing Item was cramped into a corner, almost an afterthought.

“Bottom left,” Lin Mo said. “The current item.”

Her fingertip left a damp mark on the glass.

The dark box did not brighten immediately. Instead, Related Viewing unfolded into rows of small windows: his sister, Old Chen, Fang Ji's injured hand, even He Cen's fingers protruding from the gray coat. Lin Mo heard himself swallow. A thin line grew beneath the windows toward the center of the room.

Yu Mei said his name.

He realized he had been staring at his sister's box. Not even her whole face was visible, only hair and shoulder. He wanted to know whether she would turn. That patch of light had almost made him forget to check the result of the option he had actually chosen.

“Close this row.”

Yu Mei touched the small collapse symbol at its end. The pictures shrank away, revealing new text in the dark box.

[CURRENT ITEM AVAILABLE FOR QUERY]
[CONNECTION PENDING]

No failure. No requirement to watch first. The pictures had simply stood in his way, waiting for him to be unable to look past them.

He asked Yu Mei to photograph the panel. Twelve percent battery. She took one photograph and turned the screen off. He did not spend the remaining power chasing the central shape. First, something they could take away and check.

The tray returned the sheet intact. Yu Mei placed it in an empty outer page of the notebook, separate from the original record.

A quiet knocking began deeper in the room.

Three blows. A pause. Three more.

In a slit below the stone rail, someone's hand struck a door. The knuckles were red. Water rose beneath the door; each knock lifted the central surface a little higher. Lin Mo could not see a face.

Yu Mei took half a step and stopped.

“Is it happening now?”

He did not know. No date, no recognizable object. The knocking varied, urgent and slow by turns, but the absence of an obvious loop did not prove a person was there now.

“Can you hear me?” he called.

The hand paused, apparently listening, then knocked harder. His left hand had left the armrest. The notebook began to slide from his knees. Yu Mei caught it without approaching the rail.

“Lin Mo. The brakes are still on.”

He looked down. He had actually tried to push the wheelchair forward with his feet. His right knee hurt so sharply it felt hollow. The footrest had not moved. He settled his foot again, his mouth sour.

A small label appeared beside the picture.

[KEEP WATCHING]

Yu Mei stiffened. She remained halfway through that step.

“Find somewhere to talk to them,” Lin Mo said. “Don't select that.”

They searched the surfaces visible from nearby. There was no call control. Looking from the side, Yu Mei found that the line beneath the slit joined the viewing row, separate from the thinner line at the gray door. There was no handle to open anything. The door in the picture did not exist on this wall.

Lin Mo waited, then asked the person which way their door opened.

A high voice cried for help. Three adjacent slits lit with the same hand from closer angles, as if someone had moved a camera to give him a better view of the injured knuckles.

No answer.

“This doesn't reach them,” he said hoarsely. “Remember which opening it is.”

Yu Mei watched a long time before stepping back. What if someone really was waiting?

He did not stop her with a claim that everything was fake. He was afraid of that too. But this button promised only more watching, not that anyone behind the door would hear them.

Fang Ji called from the passage. He Cen wanted water. Yu Mei turned at once.

Lin Mo looked at the hand one more time.

The central surface lifted, briefly showing his face. Younger than his reflection, mouth open, as though he stood outside the door waiting for something worse. He wanted to deny that he had come for that. The button stayed lit. He was still looking.

“Back,” he told Yu Mei.

She turned the chair. The knocking followed them over the threshold and, in the last few inches, became extravagant laughter. Lin Mo did not take that as proof no one had suffered. A real event could be made into a joke. The worst part was his inability to ask the person here.

He Cen's bottle stood beside him. He hadn't asked Yu Mei to tighten it again. He had called once, and Fang Ji, not quite hearing, had repeated the call.

She steadied the bottle while he drank, then sat.

Lin Mo watched the water descend and thought of a door somewhere he could not locate. This drink did not prove he had saved everyone. Nor would he miss He Cen's question about the search because of the distant hand.

“We found somewhere to ask about now.”

The new copy was thin. Yu Mei had shown him that it came back without anything stuck to it. He studied its edge as the voice changed its tone.

It no longer urged him to watch.

“Don't you want to save him?”
