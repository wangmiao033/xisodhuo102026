# Chapter 125: The Red Words Near the Floor

Fang Ji told the person inside to move his hand back.

The door opened inward. Pushing could crush someone lying behind it. After several gasping breaths, the man dragged himself away. The red glow beneath the door shifted with him.

Fang Ji opened a narrow gap.

An overturned padded chair stood inside. Beside it sat a man with half a trouser leg darkened by blood. His back rested against the wall, his left hand pressed to his calf, his right braced on the floor. Dust stuck to his face. A patch was missing from his right eyebrow. He looked somewhat younger than Fang Ji.

Lin Mo had never seen him.

Luminous red words hung beside his shoulder, close to the wall, as though something meant for a screen had been left in the air. They didn't illuminate the floor near Lin Mo, but laid a narrow dark-red edge across the man's clothes.

Yu Mei pointed the phone at the ground. The writing dimmed. The top line held three character positions. The middle meant seat; the last looked like a character identifying a person. A vertical black gap interrupted the beginning.

A shorter line remained below.

Remote seat invalid.

Lin Mo's back tightened.

He'd seen that red before the mirror, seen it cover pleas to stop. As Old Chen's hand vanished from his palm, a name had remained above the screen, every stroke unchanged, as though waiting for them to call him back.

“You know him?” Yu Mei asked.

“I know that kind of writing.”

The man looked up, from Lin Mo's face to his braced right wrist.

“You're Lin Mo.”

Fang Ji turned. Lin Mo didn't acknowledge the name. He asked how the man knew.

“I watched.”

“Watched what?”

A glance at the red beside his shoulder. His mouth moved without making a smile.

“Your last show.”

Many people had watched it. Recognition didn't establish which name this stranger had once sat behind. Nobody had even established whether these words belonged to him.

Lin Mo asked Yu Mei to light the wall beside the man, avoiding his eyes. The letters sharpened, though half the beginning of the top line remained absent.

“Did you use the name Seatkeeper?”

The man's fingers tightened, creasing the cloth beneath his palm.

“Some water.”

“Answer first.”

Fang Ji stayed by the door without reaching for the bottle, his injured hand against his stomach. Yu Mei crouched a little way from the blood beside the stranger's foot.

“You're still bleeding. Don't move yet.”

He looked at her and finally answered.

“That name was mine.”

Lin Mo heard his own breathing. He'd imagined countless times what he might seize if a real person existed behind those red words. This man had none of the mirror's enormous hands. He wasn't as tall as the barrier. Sitting on the floor, his head was lower than the wheelchair's armrest.

“Clothes Mending Four.”

The man didn't move.

“Did you select enforcement against that source owner?”

“I did.”

The answer came so quickly that Lin Mo waited. The man lowered his eyes. Red flashed before the gray wall without repairing the missing part.

“Remember what you wrote?”

“Selection stands.”

Lin Mo's right hand drew inward. The brace stopped his wrist. Beneath the skin remained the injury where Old Chen had gripped him. He didn't need to touch it to recall the nails.

An admission, matching what he'd witnessed. But others had watched the enforcement. Someone willing to impersonate Seatkeeper could learn the words. He didn't immediately insert this face into every old scene.

“There was another line.”

The man briefly closed his eyes.

“Source owner in breach delivered for enforcement.”

“And after that?”

“I won't withdraw.”

A tiny sound came beside the wall. Thinking somebody stood behind another door, Yu Mei raised the phone. Lin Mo watched the stranger's trembling jaw, his teeth softly touching.

“Could you have withdrawn?”

After staring at his hand, the man said, “Before the very last moment. Yes.”

Pressure crushed Lin Mo's chest. He couldn't verify the claim here. Yet it was the answer that most readily forced every old moment before him. Old Chen had been half a step away. The barrier was up. Nothing pinned his feet.

Somewhere Lin Mo couldn't reach, there might truly have been a hand that could stop it.

Fang Ji said his name softly.

The chair hadn't moved, but Lin Mo had edged forward on its seat. Shoulder pain made him lean back. He didn't ask Fang Ji to push him closer or seize the stranger's throat for him.

“What was that person's name?”

“Clothes Mending Four.”

Lin Mo stared, lips pale.

“His name.”

The man had no other answer.

Silence filled the room behind the soundproof door. Lin Mo didn't know what he might do if he could stand. The thought disgusted him. It didn't stop the wound bleeding.

Yu Mei handed Fang Ji the phone and gestured for the stranger to lift his hand from his leg. A long cut lay beneath the trouser fabric, bleeding most at the outer calf. No bone showed, and nothing deeply embedded was visible. She looked from beside it without tearing away the stuck cloth.

When she began turning back the torn edge, he seized the fabric.

She stopped and asked whether it hurt more there. He looked past her at the phone. Its oblique light exposed his lower leg, including every involuntary tremor.

Lin Mo remembered the chair before the mirror. The demand to put his clothes on it. He'd refused, but every light had stayed on him, waiting for the next vote.

“Lower the phone.”

Fang Ji directed it at the floor, using reflected light from the wall. Yu Mei asked whether she could fold aside only the loose outer edge without pulling on the wound. After a delay, the man released the cloth.

Lin Mo kept watching. He wanted to see what this person did. He didn't ask for another inch of clothing to be lifted or make endured pain an answer to other questions.

The stranger watched her hands. Residual red lit his own knuckles. Before she touched the injury, he inhaled, reached into the air, then withdrew his hand to grip the overturned chair.

“Any clean cloth?” she asked.

He shook his head.

Yu Mei looked toward Lin Mo's small bag. Eventually he nodded. One dressing. Leave the rest.

Fang Ji helped open it without speaking. Packaging rustled clearly. Lin Mo remembered Old Chen asking not to be collected yet, the warmth dwindling in his hand.

Nothing he gave now could reach that hand.

Yu Mei pressed the dressing over the wound and had the man hold it with his own palm. He bent forward in pain. The red line beside his shoulder moved closer to the floor.

At last Lin Mo could see that it followed the man. At least now, it didn't stay on the wall.

“Water,” he said again.

Very little remained in their bottle. He Cen waited in the laundry, and both guides had sweated on the broken boards.

“One mouthful.”

Yu Mei held the bottle to the stranger's mouth. When he tried to tip his head for more, she stopped him and waited for him to swallow. He stared at the bottle but didn't grab it. His throat moved.

She replaced the cap and returned it beside Lin Mo's chair.

“We still need water.”

“I know.”

Lin Mo asked where the man had come from. He pointed down the short passage behind him. Another door stood open, blood trailing across its foot.

“Above.”

“There are steps. I can't climb them.”

“Not those three outside. A shallow ramp in here goes behind the blue door.”

He glanced along his route. The floor did rise gradually, but a wall concealed its upper end and the blue door beyond. Beside a broken handrail, a deep dragging mark cut the dust. Blood lay in the mark.

Fang Ji didn't hurry inside. Their way back had just collapsed, he explained. Someone remained in the laundry. They needed a route around.

The stranger frowned at laundry.

“I don't know it. I came down from the seats.”

“Where the audience used to sit?” Lin Mo asked.

Instead of nodding, the man lifted his unbloodied hand toward the words overhead.

“I could leave before. At the end, the mirror fell. I couldn't get back out.”

His nail scratched his palm as though something once remained there to press. Nothing appeared. The red stayed beside his shoulder.

Lin Mo didn't supply the missing sequence. During the collapse, he'd seen the front stage and the people leaving around him, never what happened behind the red words. The man said he'd been stranded. His blood was real. His history still required other evidence.

Pointing up the ramp, he kept his other hand on his calf.

“Water containers in the blue door's outer room. I couldn't get them. Broken glass across the entrance. Someone knocking inside.”

Yu Mei and Fang Ji exchanged a look. He Cen's blue door, outer room, and knocking had appeared in another account. The details matched; they didn't justify rushing a wheelchair through.

Fang Ji would check only the visible top of the ramp. Yu Mei stayed by the chair while he took the phone. He moved debris aside with his shoe, stepped onto the slope, stopped to inspect the ground, then continued.

Lin Mo didn't choose his footholds for him.

At the first turn, Fang Ji swept the light around before returning. Concrete at the top. A blue door, with a rack for empty buckets visible outside. The wheelchair could reach the turn. He hadn't gone farther.

“There first,” Yu Mei said. “Find somewhere you can lie down.”

Lin Mo nodded. Pain was returning, his right knee growing hot. Recognizing familiar names couldn't keep him upright indefinitely.

The man on the floor asked to come.

Fang Ji examined his leg before answering. Bracing on his right foot, the stranger tried to rise against the wall, barely daring to lower the left. Yu Mei stopped him, checked that blood wasn't spreading beyond the dressing, and secured the loosened cloth strips.

“Nobody here can carry you.”

“I can walk with something to hold.”

None of the broken handrail would come free easily as a crutch. Lin Mo didn't ask Fang Ji to dismantle it. The stranger would have to use the wall, inching along his own drag marks.

“Stay where we can see you,” Lin Mo said. “Don't touch my bags. Don't press anything for us. At a junction, distinguish where you've been from what you're guessing.”

Something ugly crossed the man's face before pain overtook it.

“Afraid I'll call another vote?”

“I'm telling you how you can come.”

Yu Mei didn't supply a reconciliation. She briefly supported his right shoulder by the sleeve until he settled his weight against the wall, then returned to the chair.

Taking the handles, Fang Ji asked whether Lin Mo could travel now.

“Yes.”

Enough for this inspected slope. That mouthful of water hadn't purchased the water ahead, a route back to the laundry, or the deeper doors.

Passing the stranger, Lin Mo saw the broken name beside his shoulder. Its beginning remained missing. Remote seat invalid shone clearly below.

“You said that name was yours. I'll check again.”

The man breathed against the wall without arguing.

Fang Ji pushed up the ramp. Behind them, a heel dragged, stopped, dragged again. Lin Mo turned. Red moved with the man, pausing wherever he paused to endure the pain.

At the first corner, Lin Mo asked Fang Ji to wait.

The man hadn't caught up.
