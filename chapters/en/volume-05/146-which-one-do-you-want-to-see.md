# Chapter 146: Which One Do You Want to See?

Lin Mo did not answer.

He asked He Cen what he had heard. Still holding the bottle, He Cen said someone had asked why Lin Mo had come only for his sister. Fang Ji had heard the same thing. Yu Mei said the voice had sounded as though it came from behind her. She had already looked. The corner was empty.

The seatkeeper drew his injured leg closer. His sole scraped the floor.

“You're pressing on the wound,” Yu Mei told him.

He stopped. Lin Mo watched him take his hand off his knee before calling down the short passage.

“Who do you want me to find?”

For a while, nothing answered.

A narrow image brightened beneath the sign. Old Chen sat by the old water station, the rim of his cup against his lower lip. The picture withheld most of his face. There was the wound along his neck, a piece of collar. Lin Mo heard him swallow and leaned forward before the pain in his left shoulder drove him back.

Yu Mei kept her hands on the push handles.

“Do you want to go over?”

Nothing spilled from the cup. Old Chen kept drinking, one swallow after another, as if that unfinished mouthful would last as long as Lin Mo watched.

“Not yet.”

Someone laughed softly in the passage.

“Haven't you always wanted to know what happened to him?”

The cut inside Lin Mo's cheek began to ache. He had seen enough of what happened to Old Chen. He had missed enough, too. He remembered the feet beyond the grille, his own sweating hand pressing the white handle. The question squeezed those separate moments together, making it seem that admitting he wanted an answer would put him in debt for a viewing.

He looked away from the cup and asked where the image came from.

“Which one do you want to see?”

Old Chen folded sideways. Another strip of light opened: his sister outside a hospital, a bag on her back, looking up at a window. Lin Mo could not tell whether the strap had been repaired. Recognition hurt before he had enough detail to recognize anything.

He remembered the day he had failed to go with her.

Or perhaps it was another day. He could see half a wall and no date. His mind was already supplying the weather, the payment window, the call he had taken, even her tone when she said he needn't come.

He Cen suddenly held the bottle toward Yu Mei.

“Help me put the cap on. My hand's shaking.”

Plastic rasped against the neck. Lin Mo turned. Water rocked inside the bottle; He Cen stared only at the loose cap. Yu Mei tightened it and set the bottle beside him.

“Who did you hear?” she asked.

“My father.” He coughed. “Asking whether I'm coming home.”

Lin Mo looked up. His sister's silent image had been the only thing in the passage.

Fang Ji had heard no father. The seatkeeper said he had heard a woman asking whether he was finished watching. None of them could point out the opening that carried another person's voice. Lin Mo stopped asking them to repeat it.

He Cen's fever had not broken. Wanting to check what he heard, he had almost stood; his knees had already moved away from the concrete ledge. Now he planted his feet again, his gray coat crushed in his grip.

“Don't answer for me,” he told Lin Mo.

“I won't.”

Only after saying it did Lin Mo realize he had meant to ask the voice whether it was He Cen's father. As though a sufficiently precise question would let him bear someone else's answer. He let out the breath he had drawn for it.

The hospital image faded. Old Chen's cup returned.

This time Lin Mo did not call his name. He asked Yu Mei to open his notebook to the copied source reference, leaving the photograph and folded paper where they were. An edge caught his left index finger. He flinched; she stopped until he said to continue.

The receipt named Core Backup as the receiving destination. The voice had provided neither the matching reference nor a new receipt. It could make him remember two people. It had not proved either was speaking now.

“What if it's real?” Yu Mei asked quietly.

“Then let him answer this. Stop showing him drinking.”

He called down the passage. “Old Chen. Put the cup down.”

The image kept swallowing.

“Don't tell me whether you want to. Just stop first.”

The rim rose and settled again. The gentle voice said he could keep watching. There was plenty more. No one answered his request. No cup touched a table.

Lin Mo closed the notebook.

His hand was shaking harder. The failure relieved him, and an ugly disappointment climbed through his stomach. He had wanted Old Chen to snap at him: Let me finish my water, will you? Knowing that the place could exploit the wish did not let him throw it away.

Fang Ji sat down nearby without announcing that it had obviously been false.

“I'll watch the sign. Give your eyes a rest.”

Lin Mo turned toward a crooked smear of cement. At its widest it was barely a finger across. He studied that gray patch, which had no story to offer, and waited for his shoulder to settle.

The seatkeeper spoke from the corner. After a selection, he said, more little boxes used to appear beside it. Sometimes they showed another scene with the same person. Sometimes another person's injuries. He had clicked once, initially. Then he wanted to know how it ended. There was always a little more.

“Did you know you were choosing each time?” Fang Ji asked.

“Yes.”

“Then don't make it sound like it clicked for you.”

The man lowered his head. He remembered making the choice. He had even set his cup aside so the hot water wouldn't spill over his hand.

Lin Mo turned toward him.

Hot water. Old Chen had been hurting in the picture, and the person outside it had first protected himself from his own cup. That small, ordinary precaution made the man more real than his red lettering. Neither Fang Ji nor Lin Mo demanded a more painful confession.

Yu Mei asked whether he wanted water now.

He looked at her as if he did not understand how the question could follow. She pointed at the fourth bottle. If his mouth was dry, he should say so before he stood and became dizzy. He took two small mouthfuls and set it back, capped.

Lin Mo's disgust did not diminish with the water level. He did not think the man should have been refused those mouthfuls, either. He could not turn a bottle into his own permission to decide how much another person ought to hurt.

The passage made no room for their conversation. Old Chen was still swallowing, suspended forever before the end of a drink. They had barely heard one man finish speaking before the place offered another person's ending to fill the silence. Even checking facts could keep Lin Mo watching indefinitely if he forgot to look at the living people beside him.

They rested for roughly fifteen minutes.

The fourth bottle was still more than half full; the fifth remained sealed. Lin Mo asked to see the time. Thirteen percent battery, no signal. He did not open the photographs. His exhausted face floated briefly in the glass. He knew whose face it was without repeating his name.

“I want to check the gray door. Find out what the tray takes.”

“Don't write us into it,” He Cen said, indicating the notebook.

“Only the record we already have. Nothing goes in until we've read the requirements.”

Yu Mei inspected the passage first. The image beneath the sign did not cover the real floor; the dust by the threshold was undisturbed. She rested again before pushing him over. Fang Ji stayed where he could see both them and He Cen. The seatkeeper remained behind.

As they passed beneath the sign, the cup turned toward Lin Mo.

“Just one more look,” said his own voice.

Even its hesitation sounded right, including the little rasp between breaths. He did not argue with it. He asked Yu Mei to stop outside the circular room.

The tray beside the gray door had two shallow grooves. From a distance they suggested a place for a hand; close up, paper fibers clung to the edges. The narrow glass behind it displayed two new lines.

Yu Mei checked below. A small arrow at the front pointed outward, beside the word Return. Neither of them put fingers inside. When she straightened, her shoulder trembled. Lin Mo told her to lean against the wall while he read.

The dark surface at the center lifted slightly.

“Take it out,” the gentle voice said.

He stayed still. The notebook contained his sister's photograph and Old Chen's unopened fold. An instruction that vague would not choose what he handed over.

Under the glass lay an old scrap of paper bearing half a printed character. It had not been pulled in. The grooves ran to the outer edge. Yu Mei pointed this out without trying to retrieve it.

A scraping sound came from behind them, like a chair leg moving in the passage.

“He Cen's still sitting!” Fang Ji called. “That sound came from in there!”

Lin Mo had already turned. Yu Mei had almost left the handles. They waited until He Cen called that he was there before facing the door again. The dark surface rose when Fang Ji shouted, then subsided. In a nearby slit appeared the hand He Cen had held around the bottle cap.

“It's waiting for us to turn,” Yu Mei said.

The enlarged hand never quite managed to tighten the cap. A second slit showed Yu Mei's half-turned face. Together they made a neat little story: He Cen needed help, and she cared only about taking Lin Mo to his sister.

His stomach contracted.

“I tightened it,” Yu Mei said, her voice changing.

“I was there.”

He did not ask her to do it again for the picture. Fang Ji looked in and asked whether they were staying or retreating.

“Back.”

They had read what the tray did. They did not need to wait while the room chose its next way of using them.

The writing remained as the wheelchair left the threshold. Again the voice asked which one he wanted to see.

This time Lin Mo heard the quieter sounds beneath it. Many people were asking what happened next. Someone wanted a closer view. Someone kept asking what the other face looked like. Their voices and demands differed. Together, somehow, they made the question sound tender.

Back in the room, Yu Mei looked first at He Cen. He raised the capped bottle a little and set it down.

“I didn't ask you again,” he said.

She nodded and sat, holding her shoulder. Lin Mo kept the closed notebook on his lap. Gray light reflected in the dark phone screen. Beyond the passage, that gathered breath was still urging him forward.

It did not say his sister's name this time.

“You have to see what comes next.”
