# Chapter 145: The Empty Space Was Breathing

He Cen slept for less than twenty minutes.

Waking, he found his glasses but did not immediately put them on. He listened toward the short corridor and asked who was smacking their lips. Yu Mei said they had not looked yet. He should have some water first.

They shared the last of the third bottle. The Seatkeeper crushed it and put it with the empties. Fang Ji nudged the bag closer to the wall with his shoe.

Only then did Yu Mei break the fourth bottle's seal. At the snap of its ring, everyone glanced toward the passage.

No answering snap came.

Lin Mo exhaled slowly. He was beginning to attach every ordinary sound to that held breath below, and the habit exhausted him more than an actual echo. He watched the fresh water and reminded himself to swallow instead of examining even that for evidence of being watched.

The fifth bottle remained sealed at the inner end of the concrete ledge.

“I'll look by the sign,” he told He Cen. “Just along this corridor. If a door blocks us, we stop.”

He Cen put on his glasses. “Tell me before you move. Don't let the wheels wake me.”

“I'm telling you now.”

“I heard.”

Fang Ji stood at the recess opening, where he could see both the resting people and the passage. He kept his injured palm against his chest and chose a section of wall that did not press his shoulder against a protruding brick.

Yu Mei left the fourth bottle with He Cen and took the handles. The notebook and phone remained in Lin Mo's bag. She pushed the few level meters, parked beneath the sign, and inspected the threshold.

The door was open.

Its thick gray slab had withdrawn into the wall, exposing half a round, dusty handle. Nobody needed to grip it to pass. The floor beyond was level with the corridor, its nearest section plainly visible.

A ring-shaped chamber opened before Lin Mo.

A broad walkway encircled a lower central space, bordered by a waist-high stone parapet. There were no audience seats along it. Narrow openings joined the chamber from every direction: at floor level, at shoulder height, close beneath the ceiling. It was as though a building had delivered all the gaps that should never have connected to the same place.

Each opening carried sound.

Someone wanted a curtain lifted farther. Someone demanded the ending, complained that there had been no follow-up. Laughter dropped from above and collided with an urgent voice asking people to stop filming. Every unfinished sound bent toward the dark center.

Lin Mo smelled heated dust and damp cloth.

Yu Mei remained on their side of the threshold. She called their position to Fang Ji, describing the level walkway. He answered, and He Cen's more distant voice said he could hear.

Lin Mo held on to that answer before looking down.

No enormous screen occupied the center. There was only an uneven surface whose depth he could not judge. Light fell from a slit above, touched it as though touching a fine layer of water, and changed color. It might have been hanging fabric or something much closer.

Whenever fresh laughter arrived, it swelled a little.

“A draft?” Yu Mei whispered.

Dust on the threshold did not stir. Neither did the hair beside her temple. The surface rose and fell on its own, barely moving, like a chest hidden beneath a thick quilt.

A fine line brightened along the nearby wall.

[Lower Convergence]

It traveled right to a small gray door. A receiving symbol matched the one on their new receipt. Beneath it, lettering read Core Backup Access.

Lin Mo's heart jumped. Then he noticed the width: narrower than his wheelchair. A dark seam dipped inward beneath its sill.

No voice of Xiao Lu's came through it.

He asked Yu Mei to illuminate that area with the phone. The battery had fallen to fourteen percent. She kept the light low and photographed the wording together with the joint beneath the door.

There was no ordinary keyhole. A narrow shelf protruded below it, resembling a tray for receipts. Its smooth surface held nothing.

[Awaiting access item]

Lin Mo stared. The words seemed to expect him to put a living person there. The thought sent him looking at his own hand before he remembered that no such demand had actually appeared.

“Does it say what it wants?” Yu Mei asked.

“Not yet.”

He asked her to move the light away. The receipt and route had brought them to the matching symbol. He would not fill the empty requirement with whatever he feared most, or have her test the door because of it.

A wheel knocked against something deeper in the chamber.

Lin Mo went rigid. It sounded like his small front wheel catching a joint in the receiving hall. Fang Ji's breathing followed. Then his own voice said that the beam ahead could carry weight.

He remembered saying it. The scenery still wore its black paint then. Fang Ji's hand had not yet begun bleeding again during the rescue.

Now the words came through a slit in the ceiling, slowed and softened as though someone were studying the exact way he had been wrong.

Yu Mei looked at him.

Splintering wood followed, stopping just before his body would pitch downward. Several openings inhaled at once, waiting.

Fang Ji's footsteps approached from the passage.

“I heard it too,” he said from outside.

“I misjudged it,” Lin Mo answered, his mouth dry.

“You've already said that upstairs.”

Fang Ji leaned by the doorway without reaching out his injured hand. He had recognized his own breath, he said. It sounded awful. Lin Mo did not need to let the recording finish on that account.

Lin Mo nodded after a moment. Being witnessed in the instant of his mistake had made him want to ask the person beside him for reassurance that he had been forgiven. Fang Ji did not supply it. He merely stood in the present doorway, where the man who had rescued Lin Mo still hurt.

The voices from the ceiling continued downward.

A stranger asked people to move so he could see what was happening. A younger voice asked whether anyone had called for help, its final words submerged beneath demands for clearer footage.

Lin Mo remembered standing beside a road, looking past several shoulders at an overturned electric scooter. He had wanted to know whether the injured rider was being helped. He had also stood for several seconds with his feet obstructing someone behind him.

The memory did not appear on the walls.

He recalled it himself when he heard the words move aside, along with the petrol smell at the intersection. He refused to turn that recollection into proof of manipulation and looked back at the actual shoes by the door.

He Cen called Yu Mei from the recess. She answered immediately.

“No need to come back yet,” he said. “All those voices. Has anyone real answered?”

Lin Mo studied the black surface. Something resembling part of a mouth had seemed to form, then became a fold before he could distinguish it. Nobody approached, although the sounds grew denser.

“We haven't found anyone.”

He Cen said he would keep sitting. He had saved half the water for them. Lin Mo knew he was calling a few missing mouthfuls half, but did not correct him across the passage. The bottle remained within reach. The words had been meant for living people.

Yu Mei backed the wheelchair half a foot out of the light.

The central surface suddenly rose. Beneath the parapet, innumerable thin points shone.

They did not look like eyes. Each narrow piece opened and shut: a phone's edge, a crack in a door, the long strip exposed when a finger held a curtain aside. Their light changed. People cried or sat blankly. Some did nothing at all, yet the pictures refused to leave them.

One contained Lin Mo's footrest.

Others held beds, table corners, closed car windows. These places did not form a theater he recognized. Whenever a new image illuminated, a tiny sound came from beneath the stone, and the dark surface seemed to thicken.

One bright strip rose, showing Lin Mo's profile as it had been long ago. His jaw had not been reshaped. His hair was short; he remembered the small sunburned patch beside his neck.

The image split down the middle. Half became his present face. The two halves pressed together, their eyes looking in exactly the same direction.

For a moment, he desperately wanted to reach out and keep the old half.

The chair stayed where it was. Yu Mei stood steadily behind it. He withdrew his left hand from the armrest's edge and unfolded his fingers.

That face belonged to him. So did the man sitting here, breathing, his shoulder aching. He did not have to choose an image to lay on the tray before he was entitled to carry his name farther down.

Both mouths opened in the light, inaudible. There were already too many voices. Even his old face had to compete with laughter.

He looked toward the real doorway. Fang Ji lifted his chin, asking whether he wanted to retreat.

“Let me see where the door is. Then back.”

The words emerged from his own throat, faint and rough. Fang Ji heard them and made room for the wheelchair to return.

Yu Mei kept her hands on the handles.

“Does all of this come here?”

Lin Mo remembered residual viewing requests and his assumption that someone, at least, must be seated beyond that first window. Cable ducts entered the walls at different heights. None led to a visible master plug.

“What we can see is going toward the middle.”

The nearest opening shut.

One breath disappeared from the room. Before he could identify whose it had been, an adjacent slit illuminated and resumed it. Not every opening needed to remain available. When one closed, whatever was waiting to watch could press in through the next.

“Come back first,” Fang Ji said.

Lin Mo agreed. He asked Yu Mei to remember the little gray door's location, leaving the empty tray untouched.

As the wheelchair crossed the threshold, he realized he had been pressing his tongue against the cut inside his cheek. A faint taste of blood returned.

“Are the pages still in the bag?”

Yu Mei checked. “Yes.”

“Nothing unfolded?”

“No. You know that.”

He did. He needed something that had not been taken, replayed, or extended with another half-sentence. Old Chen's private fold stayed closed. His sister's photograph remained between its familiar pages. He could not protect them from this entire place. He had not relinquished them yet.

At the recess, He Cen pointed out the water. The Seatkeeper leaned against the wall, looking past them toward the passage. His expression differed from the fear he had shown at the unfinished-viewing screen, though Lin Mo could not tell what he recognized.

“Seen it before?”

The man slowly shook his head.

“I only ever watched the front.”

A new voice traveled along the corridor.

No cough preceded it, no static or repetition like the preview. Soft and unmistakable, it came from the circular chamber and spoke Lin Mo's name.

All five went still.

He watched the Lower Convergence sign, waiting to hear his name again. Yu Mei did not turn the wheelchair. He Cen kept hold of the bottle.

After a pause, the voice seemed to understand what they were waiting for.

“Why have you come only for her?”
