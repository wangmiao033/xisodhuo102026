# Chapter 149: The Audience Without Seats

He Cen was awake. He asked why they had taken so long.

Lin Mo described the new frame, the separate controls, the figure rising in the center. When he reached the thumb dragging a video backward, he faltered. How could he explain, to someone who hadn't seen it, the way fingers became a shoulder?

“Pictures pasted onto a person?” He Cen asked.

“They don't stay pasted. A picture changes, and that part changes with it.”

He Cen took off his glasses. A single streak of dust marked one lens. He wiped it for a long time before saying that he didn't want to look.

He didn't have to, Lin Mo said. They hadn't started the audio. Next time, they would first see whether the speaking point could be placed by the door.

Yu Mei sat against the wall, her right hand limp beside her thigh.

The seatkeeper asked whether the thing had red lettering.

Lin Mo shook his head. No badge, no seat number. When the figure first rose, it hardly seemed to occupy any single spot. Looking toward the center, he had felt it was below him. Yu Mei, farther to the side, said it seemed to peer from cracks in the wall.

The man's lips moved. He had once heard a term: the Mother Seat.

“Who said it?” Fang Ji asked.

“Someone transferring me between scenes. Said there was a Mother Seat farther back. I never went.”

Fang Ji stared at him. The man did not claim to have known all along. Rubbing his trouser seam, he said he'd assumed it meant someone seated higher. He had wanted to move there, too. Later scenes had never offered the choice.

Lin Mo remembered the words without fastening the name to the figure. He asked whether the man had seen viewers without seat numbers.

Sometimes a picture would suddenly become popular, the man said. Comments poured into a corner without faces attached. No extra seats appeared. He'd thought the people were in another hall. That was only what he had thought at the time.

Then he stopped and studied his right hand. He raised one finger and made a small tapping motion in the air.

“Sometimes you didn't have to choose. Stay there, and the next part started by itself.”

“Why keep clicking, then?” Fang Ji asked.

“In case it picked something I didn't want.”

Nobody spoke for a long time. Lin Mo had always imagined someone at the back issuing orders. However terrible things became, surely a particular person pressed the first button. Yet this man had mostly sat and waited, occasionally correcting what should follow. Often he hadn't needed to move a hand to make another person's pain go on.

Yu Mei rubbed her palm and asked whether he used to think leaving ended it.

He said he wasn't sure what ending meant. He had closed pages and returned the next day. Once, a clip he wanted was gone, and he'd been angry. Realizing what the admission meant in front of Lin Mo, he pressed his lips shut.

“If you're finished, rest,” Lin Mo said.

He didn't want to hear more. Not because he had enough truth. Every detail made him imagine Old Chen's fifteen years as a place other people could visit again tomorrow. His next question might become a demand for a more agonized face to prove the confession genuine.

The voice outside had also fallen quiet, as if holding its breath for him to continue. Lin Mo closed the notebook. The small slap of paper startled even him.

He Cen put his glasses back on.

“I've watched things like that on my phone.”

No one asked which things. After a while he explained. Someone fell; several accounts argued about who had filmed it first. He had opened it intending to criticize them. Halfway through, he wanted to know whether anybody helped. He looked toward the passage, paler than before.

“Does that mean I belong here too?”

Lin Mo watched the sweat on his forehead. The place hadn't shown them a list, he said. They had seen those lingering looks gather. That did not let them certify somebody's membership from one memory of using a phone.

He Cen wiped his nose. The answer did not comfort him. It let him remain seated instead of getting up to prove which side he occupied.

Yu Mei took one of the last mouthfuls from the fourth bottle and saved some for Lin Mo. The bottle sounded hollow when she set it down. She left the fifth sealed and helped him drink without catching the cut in his cheek.

“Look at me next time we go in,” she said.

He raised his eyes.

“When you were staring at your old face, you missed the first time I called.”

He nodded. He remembered the second call, not the first. She did not demand that he promise never to look again. If she called, he should look at her before deciding to continue. Something they could actually do, simpler than swearing at the doorway that they would never be deceived.

They rested for more than twenty minutes.

He Cen's fever persisted. He could hold his water, but getting up still required pauses. Fang Ji checked his dressing without peeling it back; there was no fresh blood. Lin Mo briefly slept and dreamed he was standing to reach a cup on a high shelf. When he woke, his shoulder still hurt. The cup had not followed him out.

He told Yu Mei he was awake before asking whether she could push him again.

The seatkeeper wanted to move to the passage entrance. Not into the circular room. He could watch where people came out and relay He Cen's calls. Fang Ji indicated a ledge beside the wall and told him to move slowly, keeping clear of the wheelchair's path.

Lin Mo did not stop him. The injured left foot still dragged. The man sat as soon as he reached the ledge, fingers trembling. Agreeing to help had not given him a sound leg.

The display by the gray door remained off.

Audio was still unstarted. A new line said access depended on the receiving end. Lin Mo asked Yu Mei to leave it alone while they found exactly where speaking would happen. He did not want everyone in the shelter turned into part of a scene merely because he opened his mouth.

A short metal tube ran along the wall from the frame. Following it, Yu Mei found a movable arm near the threshold. At its end was an opening the width of a thumb, labeled Near-End Audio Port. Marks on the wall showed its range: from the frame to the doorway, not farther down the passage.

“We can speak here.”

She moved it carefully. The stiff arm stopped halfway. Lin Mo told her not to force it. From outside, Fang Ji noticed a catch underneath and asked her to return it to its starting position.

It went back quietly, revealing an upward arrow beside the catch.

Yu Mei lifted the catch with her thumb and supported the arm with her other hand. This time it moved easily along the marked arc. She lowered the catch again, fixing the port a little more than a foot inside the threshold. No one had to hold it up. The wheelchair could remain outside.

She went to the wall to rest, pale with shoulder pain, rather than immediately reaching for the chair.

Lin Mo studied the little hole. The room's other voice had no fixed source. The thing through which they might genuinely attempt a conversation was so small it couldn't contain a face.

The central figure rose again.

It had no complete head. A bright corridor made one side; fingers covering a mouth made the other. Beyond the rail were crying faces, faces that merely didn't want to be seen, and several bearing a familiar anticipation.

The seatkeeper was among them.

Not the wounded man on the ledge. A comfortable seated figure set a cup on a small table beyond his armrest and leaned forward. A red line beneath the picture brightened. Lin Mo couldn't make out the whole lettering. He thought of hot water.

The man in the passage gasped.

Before he could cover his face, the picture turned to show it. Rounder than now, unsmiling, astonishingly intent. No monstrous grimace. Just a person afraid of missing something, hardly blinking.

“Don't look at me,” he said.

The image continued. Old Chen's neck wound appeared beside the face, the two pictures waiting for them to choose a nicely balanced punishment.

Fang Ji turned away first.

“Move over a little. Keep the doorway clear.”

The man stared, then edged along the ledge. Fang Ji did not ask how it felt to see himself. He did not call He Cen over to make the exposure worse. Lin Mo watched Fang Ji's back until his shoulder met the real wall, then looked away too.

Murmurs crowded the center.

Let him try it. Don't block the view. Was there more of Old Chen? Different voices from different directions forced their way through the unfinished body. Lin Mo could almost feel a ready-made satisfaction opening before him, requiring only his agreement.

Yu Mei called his name.

He looked at her.

She stood pale against the wall, pressing her bruised shoulder. She offered no moral answer. She only asked whether the wheelchair should stay where it was. He said yes. She moved aside instead of making herself push immediately.

The seatkeeper bowed his head. He did not look absolved. His old image remained in the center; so did what he had done. Lin Mo was simply, for now, refusing to trade the man's shame for his own relief.

A faint ring of lettering appeared beneath the figure.

It lay along the inside of the stone rail, previously hidden by the dark surface. Now the nearest section was exposed. Yu Mei could read some of it without leaning over, but only the words Aggregation Source were clear.

Lin Mo asked her not to approach. Fang Ji shifted his view from the doorway.

He moved two steps left, keeping off the pale panel. The inscription followed an arc beside many small openings. It named no controller. There was no enormous chair.

[PRIMARY DESIRE TO WATCH]

The dark surface covered the rest again.

Cold spread through Lin Mo. The words seemed less an invitation than a maintenance label identifying what entered this place. The cracks supplied more than people being watched. They brought in separate desires to keep looking. Gathered together, those desires had produced a voice capable of calling their names.

He did not call that a complete explanation. Yet when the thing bent toward them again, the corridor on its left changed into a phone screen without leaving any part missing. Another watcher could take over, and it retained the same leaning posture.

“Could we block the openings?” Fang Ji asked.

Lin Mo surveyed the room. Some were near the floor, others above the ceiling slab. A few flashed and appeared elsewhere. They had needed stages to pass one heavy door. They had neither the reach nor the materials to seal this ring.

“We can shut the one we found. Leave the port by the door. Back out first.”

Once Yu Mei had recovered, she backed the chair outside the threshold. The port was fixed by the door. The copied sheet was safe. Their live display remained off. They had accomplished something possible, without declaring a victory over the room.

The figure approached the rail. Its edges scattered, like people leaning from many separate windows at once.

“You always need to see something before you'll believe.”

Lin Mo looked up as the surface uncovered the last stretch of inscription.

The arc around Aggregation Source had no endpoint.

It circled the whole room and joined its beginning.
