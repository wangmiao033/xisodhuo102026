# Chapter 136: Check the Way Back First

A rolling bottle woke Lin Mo.

It crossed the threshold, struck a caster, and stopped. Water did not roll in after it. It had already gathered in a thin line beneath the wooden door. That strip had been dry when he fell asleep.

He raised his head. Pain pulled tight behind his neck. Yu Mei crouched at the door, pressing an old cloth into the gap. It darkened quickly. She checked both ends and did not bother adding another layer.

“Wake up. It's coming from the front.”

Only two hours had passed on his phone. He hadn't slept deeply, but he hadn't heard rain either. Beyond the repair room's high window, the pale wall still glowed, giving no clue whether it was day or night. He started to lower his feet. When his left heel touched the back of his shoe, he remembered Yu Mei telling him not to scrape it along the floor and kept it on the footrest.

Fang Ji sat opposite him, left arm hanging, the edge of the cloth around his right palm curling up. He nudged the bottle aside with his shoe. Shallow water crept along the wall outside, carrying a small tuft of lint.

“From the laundry room,” Fang Ji said.

“Did you go and look?”

“Saw the lint. I didn't go over.”

Lin Mo nodded. The water level they'd seen on their way in no longer told them much. He asked Yu Mei to open the door wider. Damp wood scraped the floor. Farther away, the feet of an overturned seat still stood above the water.

The high-backed chair's black screen flickered.

The man beside the doorway immediately drew his legs inward. Light caught the outer dressing on his left calf, then died. The broken red lettering by his shoulder remained.

“Will it come in too?” He Cen asked.

He had put his glasses on. His gray jacket trailed from the joined stools, one hand clutching its hem. Yu Mei lifted the fabric clear and touched his forehead. He turned away irritably, then had to grip the stool to sit up.

“I meant the water.”

“I know. If it keeps rising, we'll move.”

Lin Mo looked toward the narrow passage at the back. The load-testing frame was still pinned, and an unexplored maintenance route lay below it. Going deeper could so easily sound reasonable: there were papers, arrows, his sister. But before sleeping, exhausted in much the same way, he'd said they should check the way back first.

He asked for the notebook and opened his own rough map, leaving Old Chen's pages alone. Fang Ji pointed out the receiving hall's direction. It was one turn off from the opening Lin Mo remembered.

“That's scenery. The middle section collapsed. The outside wall is behind it.”

Lin Mo studied his line. He had drawn the narrow route they'd taken as the only route there was. It was what he could see from the wheelchair. After everything that followed, he'd begun treating the unseen strip along the wall as nonexistent.

“Could we follow the outside wall to the hall?”

“I've been partway. There's a fallen cabinet. Don't know if the chair fits.”

Yu Mei stood at the door. A dry margin ran between two rows of overturned seats, disappearing beyond the high-backed chair. She didn't promise it would work. She asked whether Lin Mo could sit steadily.

He settled against the cushion and breathed out. “Once my leg's positioned.”

She adjusted the right leg rest without lifting his shoulder. He waited out the pulling pain behind his knee and looked up. His vision stayed clear. He told her, and she put the notebook away, hanging only their opened second bottle of new water beside the push handle.

“We'll look. If we can't get through, we'll come back. You stay here.”

He Cen wanted to know how long. Lin Mo checked the time and said half an hour. If they reached a point beyond shouting distance, they would return with what they'd actually seen. They weren't going below on this trip.

“Leave the phone. I'll time you,” He Cen said.

“I need pictures of the entrance.”

He Cen glanced at his hands and didn't argue. He pointed at the stopped wall clock, muttering that keeping it up there was just spiteful.

Fang Ji shifted to where he could see both He Cen and the front door. He wasn't coming to lift the chair. Lin Mo didn't ask him to endure a little more.

Before they moved, He Cen pointed at the clip light. Its beam fell on Lin Mo's right shoe; the outside of the caster was dark. Yu Mei crouched, secured the clamp, and tested it. Fang Ji nudged the empty bottle against the wall to clear their turn.

Every small thing kept them there longer. Watching the water, Lin Mo grew impatient and failed to hide it.

“You'll be coming back in that same chair,” He Cen told him. “Let it light the road.”

The beam finally reached the brick joints. Lin Mo realized he'd been staring at the white patch on the far wall as though it were an exit. He said he understood. Yu Mei checked a bag strap before pushing. It stuck out toward the door hook; she folded it inside the armrest.

The man with the red lettering watched the lamp, the bag, then Lin Mo's hands.

“What?” Lin Mo asked.

“They didn't show this on the screens.”

The man lowered his head. Lin Mo's fingertips went cold. The screens had shown his pain, his kneeling, his altered voice. They hadn't made anyone seated in front of them wait for a lamp to be straightened or a strap to be kept out of a wheel. He almost asked whether that made it easier not to care. He refused to spend the strength left for the trip on that man.

Yu Mei had both hands on the handles. “Ready?”

He turned away from the red letters and answered.

The right caster entered shallow water at the threshold. His chest tightened. Yu Mei paused, straightened the chair, and eased it over. Water barely reached the tire, but two glossy tracks trailed behind them. Fang Ji found a brick fragment and set it inside the doorway.

“Don't bring the wheel over this side coming back. There's a piece missing.”

Lin Mo noted the place, finally looking at something nearer than the distance.

“Don't go in front of that seat,” the man called.

Yu Mei stopped.

“Nobody asked you to go near it,” Lin Mo said. “Tell us what you saw.”

“Someone stepped into that bright strip before. The chair turned. I don't know if it still does.”

A narrow metal plate lay under dust, directly before the seat. Yu Mei had meant to go behind it anyway. She moved another hand's width toward the wall and avoided the plate without testing it with anyone's foot.

Fine grit crackled beneath the wheels. He Cen's coughing faded. When Lin Mo looked back, Fang Ji was still inside the door. He didn't raise even his less injured arm to wave, only tipped his chin.

The outer wall was more intact than expected. Plaster had peeled away in sheets, exposing brick, but a firm strip of concrete ran along it. Toward the center, seat frames lay on their sides. A strap stretched between two chair legs. Yu Mei stopped and used a thin piece of wood to lower it to the floor.

Something shone underneath.

A small circle of light turned beneath a seat, passed over a screw, and withdrew. Lin Mo told her not to move the wood again. They waited several breaths. The strap stayed down. The circle didn't come toward them.

Yu Mei stepped back and straightened the chair.

“The footrest will hit it if we go straight.”

“Back up and go around.”

“That puts us back near the plate.”

He studied the wheels and wall. Once, carrying a load through a narrow alley, he could turn a shoulder to get past. Now the single word around had to accommodate four wheels and two footrests. He asked her to back up to the clear patch, then inspect ahead on foot instead of trying the width with him in the chair.

She set the brakes and walked three steps. Behind a seat, a short broken board could be lifted away. There was no wire beneath it. She laid it aside, made room for the footrest, and returned.

They got through. Sweat chilled Lin Mo's back, but nothing new was hurt.

A fallen tool cabinet blocked the corner. Its doors gaped; screws covered the floor. Yu Mei swept the sharp ones aside with the stick, then tested the cabinet. It wouldn't move. She could squeeze between it and the wall. The wheelchair couldn't, even without its footrests.

Bitterness filled Lin Mo's mouth. The possibility of going back had opened a fraction, only to be blocked by his body again.

“Look beyond it. I'll wait.” The words came too quickly.

Yu Mei looked through the gap at something bright farther on, then crouched beside him and lit the cabinet's base. Half a hinge was trapped underneath. The cabinet wasn't resting flat.

“If it tips, I can't get back, and you can't back away.”

He swallowed just a second before he could say it.

She examined the other side. What he had taken for the outer wall was a thin partition, ending half a meter short of the actual brickwork. She walked to its end without leaving his sight and pointed behind her.

“Through here.”

She backed the chair up and took it around the partition. They left the cabinet where it lay.

A familiar row of long lights appeared.

The receiving hall. Nobody stood beneath the lamps, but a gray-white column slid across the floor, turning dusty wheel tracks bright. Lin Mo remembered his chair veering toward an empty seat and Fang Ji catching the handles.

Yu Mei immediately braked in the wall's shadow.

“This is far enough.”

He agreed. The narrow passage at the other end was close, but the moving beam lay between them. Their half hour wasn't up; they still hadn't found a route He Cen could walk out along.

Yet the passage was real. It was still there.

As a child, his sister used to wave an ice pop from the far end of their alley, urging him over. The plastic wrapper swung. He could always run straight to her. What he remembered now was syrup dripping over her fingers and the frown when he'd bought the wrong flavor. Back then, a short distance had seemed impossible not to cross.

“Go back first?” Yu Mei asked.

Along the hall's edge, a low raised strip followed the wall. Its shadow ran from here toward the passage, but a fallen curtain hid the farthest section.

Lin Mo photographed their position and asked her to turn.

“Tell them the hall's there. Next trip, we check the edge.”

As they turned, the beam brushed the spot they'd occupied. The chair didn't move. He let Yu Mei set the pace. Behind the cabinet, he admitted that he'd nearly sent her through that gap.

“You asked. I didn't go.”

“Yeah.”

“Think through the rest of the sentence next time.”

Fang Ji leaned out when the repair room came into view, checking the wheels first. Lin Mo told him not to move anyone yet. He Cen immediately asked whether they'd heard anything from outside.

“We haven't reached the entrance.”

Twenty-six minutes. Lin Mo showed him the photograph without enlarging it until only the promising-looking passage remained.

He Cen's finger lingered at its edge. Could they keep this picture? It was saved, Lin Mo said, without asking why. The passage was tiny, easy to miss. For someone waiting here, it was a piece of outside he could hold in front of him.

Yu Mei shifted a stool leg inward, dragging a wet mark. She wasn't taking her old place. She was leaving the farther, drier end for He Cen.

The cloth beneath the door was soaked through.
