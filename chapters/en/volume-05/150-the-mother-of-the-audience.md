# Chapter 150: The Mother of the Audience

Outside the threshold, Lin Mo did not ask Yu Mei to take him straight back.

From this angle he could see more of the inscription inside the stone rail. It ran around a narrow whitish rim. As the dark surface withdrew, successive sections emerged, like an old machine finally losing its casing.

Yu Mei saw it too. She wiped sweat from her temple without taking her eyes off the letters.

[AGGREGATION OF THE PRIMARY DESIRE TO WATCH]

The smaller text below was damaged in two places. Lin Mo could make out collective unconscious, continuous generation, and front-stage structure. No individual's signature. No suggestion that a viewer could take the highest seat and assume control.

“The line above,” Fang Ji said. “Far left.”

Lin Mo followed his indication. Deeper lettering sat within the curved black border.

[MOTHER OF THE AUDIENCE]

Below the name was a shorter line.

[THEATER GENERATION SOURCE]

His back pressed slowly into the cushion.

Behind so many doors, he had tried to imagine who built the theater. The highest-seated spectator. A hidden administrator. Someone with a red ID. Even when he imagined several people, there had always been a hand responsible for pressing a button. Now countless hands extended beneath the words. Each lifted a curtain a little, replayed a moment, left a comment asking what happened next.

Gathered together, they had a name of their own.

The figure raised its head. Its face changed three times: a gentle woman, then only an eyelid, then overlapping mouths that spoke the same words.

“At last you're willing to call me.”

Lin Mo had not called. He had read a name; the voice was already treating sight as a response. He looked at the chipped rim below the letters. Old paint had flaked there. It did not resemble a title that had appeared merely to welcome him.

Yu Mei touched the handles. “Back?”

“Yes. We've read it.”

She withdrew the chair. Fang Ji followed slowly. Passing the seatkeeper's ledge, he did not make the injured man stand to clear the way. Yu Mei watched the real floor as the wheel passed a hand's width from his shoe.

In the shelter, He Cen asked what the name had been.

Lin Mo told him. He Cen began repeating it, stopped halfway, and asked whether some woman had given birth to the thing they saw.

Lin Mo shook his head. The inscription referred to the collective unconscious. Many people's desire to pry into others' suffering gathered here, continually generating the theater in front of it.

He spoke carefully, separating interpretation from what he had read.

They had seen watching enter through the cracks, the figure changing with its sources. The name and Generation Source were written on the rim. When it had first appeared, how many places outside connected to it: he did not know.

“What about the people who sat out front?” Yu Mei asked.

“What they did is still theirs.”

The seatkeeper heard from the passage entrance and did not raise his head. Lin Mo would not let a larger thing erase the choices made by particular hands. Nor would finding a name mean that everyone behind every door had to do as it said.

Fang Ji came back and compressed the light bundle: three empty large bottles, two old small ones. The dry crackling made Lin Mo thirsty. Yu Mei divided the fourth bottle's last water between him and He Cen, wetting her own lips with a little. It was empty.

She passed it to Fang Ji and opened the fifth.

At the snap of the seal, the seatkeeper looked over. She let him reach for it himself, then put it beside He Cen after he drank. Nobody announced that the last bottle belonged only to a chosen person. Lin Mo nevertheless registered its weight. They could not spend an entire day here looking at a face.

“If it lives on that, will it disappear if we stop looking?” He Cen asked.

Lin Mo remembered other light taking over after their own display went dark.

“We stopped one new feed. The others are still coming in.”

He did not make closing his eyes another job He Cen must do to save everybody. The feverish man finally let himself rest, asking only to be told about the next trip.

Yu Mei wondered if the gray door was another route the thing had deliberately offered.

Possible, Lin Mo said. But the records matched in sequence, and querying the existing item had not required more viewing. If fear of the room made them discard everything they could check, they would again have only a voice directing them.

He asked for the phone.

Eleven percent. The receiving symbol, source reference, and query result were still there. In the latest photograph, Read Existing Item crouched at the lower left of Related Viewing, almost missed. Yu Mei enlarged it. He checked the groups to ensure they had not been led to a similar but different reference.

“Audio only this time. Don't let it see us first.”

She asked if she should write that down. On the copy's blank space he suggested two short notes: near-end display off, audio can be disconnected. The second had not been tested. She put a question mark after it.

He saw and quietly agreed.

He had nearly written a hoped-for escape as an inspected one. She put the pen away without declaring either of them more careful, and made sure he drank before they went. His mouth still hadn't healed.

Fang Ji asked whether, if someone kept talking after they connected, Lin Mo would simply never come back.

He stared. Every plan had stopped at making contact. Beyond that lay a blank well. He had thought of hearing his sister, not of Yu Mei's shoulder or how long He Cen might wait on the concrete.

“First we establish whether it's her. Then I come back and tell you.”

“Don't promise for us that she'll wait.”

Lin Mo nodded. He had no such authority. If a person was there, waiting might be the hardest thing for her. He would explain that people here were injured and needed pauses. He would not leave the connection hanging without a word.

Yu Mei said she would tell him when she couldn't manage.

“Don't wait for me to notice.”

“You have to listen when I say it.”

He agreed. The word was harder than he expected. Before even seeing his sister, he was afraid an ordinary pause could lose her. He let the fear press inside his chest without using it to ask Yu Mei for one more effort.

Drowsy, He Cen had forgotten to cap the water. Fang Ji came over, steadied the bottle low with his left hand, and called quietly. He Cen woke enough to tighten it himself. Nobody rushed to complete every movement for somebody else. Lin Mo watched until Yu Mei chose to stand.

Then they returned to the gray door.

Fang Ji remained midway along the passage. The seatkeeper had returned to the shelter, giving He Cen two people he could call. The fifth bottle and notebook stayed inside. Lin Mo carried only the query transcription; Yu Mei had the phone in her pocket. Old Chen's private fold remained closed, not offered up for a faster route.

The little port was fixed by the doorway. The wheelchair could stay outside.

Yu Mei examined the audio controls. A Disconnect button was tucked underneath the metal arm, on a different surface from Start. She showed Lin Mo and pressed it once. The panel replied that no connection existed. It demanded no retained object or continued scene.

That proved the button responded, not that disconnection would always be possible after contact. He asked for a photograph of its position. The battery fell to ten percent. He left the remaining power for something they genuinely needed to carry away.

The transcription went back on the tray.

They checked the complete reference, selected Read Existing Item, and collapsed Related Viewing when it expanded automatically. The live-display slider remained off. Yu Mei moved the illuminated old face to the end of its side channel so it could not obscure the actual controls.

[READ-ONLY CONNECTION]
[RECEIVING END MAY DECLINE]

The second line released something in his chest, then tightened it again. Somebody who could refuse was somebody worth waiting for. But writing could lie. He could not call that brief relief his sister's return.

“Start it.”

Yu Mei pressed the control.

No one immediately called to him. A faint noise came first, like paper shifting in a distant room. Lin Mo held his breath until the sound of his own heart grew louder.

The central voice intruded.

“What do you want to hear her call you?”

He looked up sharply. The gentle figure inclined toward them, all its mouths moving together, prepared to supply the word he wanted most. Heat rushed into his eyes. His body recognized expectation before his mind could stop it.

Yu Mei called him.

He turned. Beside the near-end port, she pointed at the metal arm. No speech to persuade him, just the actual place from which the answer should come.

The central voice was overhead. The little opening had said nothing.

He settled against the chair and slowly released the breath that hurt in his chest.

“I'm waiting for her to speak for herself.”

He addressed the small hole, not the center. Yu Mei's hand remained beside Disconnect, neither pressing it nor leaving.

The background noise stopped.

A thin line lit on the glass, running from the gray door toward the port. Existing Item Responding appeared beside it. The final groups of the complete reference aligned in turn. Their transcription stayed outside the slot, unchanged. It did not crumble or become a paper sister who could walk out.

A faint voice came from the opening.

“Who's outside?”

Lin Mo's vision blurred.

He knew sound alone was insufficient. The roughness, the falling ends of sentences: he had heard them in so many old clips. This time, though, it had not called him brother first. It had not told him to reach out or arranged how he should cry. He held an empty stretch of armrest. His index finger hurt, distinctly and presently.

“Lin Mo. I've come to query this preservation record.”

Silence.

He wanted to add that he was her brother, to cram everything important after his name before the doorway swallowed it. Yu Mei neither supplied the words nor urged the person inside to recognize him.

Several breaths passed. Then the little hole spoke again.

“Can you stop now?”

He didn't know whether she meant walking, speaking, or some process he couldn't see. He left that uncertainty intact instead of rushing to say yes.

“What do you want me to stop?”

A little breath moved in the port.

“Don't open the picture yet.”

Yu Mei checked the slider. Still off. Lin Mo said they hadn't opened it. He was outside in a wheelchair, connected only by sound. His throat tightened at wheelchair, but he did not list his injuries as if the person inside owed him repayment for everything that had hurt along the way.

“All right,” the voice said.

One word. No prepared celebration followed. The line remained lit, the full reference present. Lin Mo did not immediately touch Disconnect, nor did he ask Yu Mei to open the narrow door for him.

Beyond the rail the central shape leaned closer, many mouths opening soundlessly.

It wanted to watch them recognize each other.

He kept the wheelchair outside the threshold and gave it no new picture. Looking at the little port, he held back the words he wanted to hear and waited for the person inside to choose the next ones.
