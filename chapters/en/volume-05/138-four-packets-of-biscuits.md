# Chapter 138: Four Packets of Biscuits

Fang Ji had searched the low cabinets at the front.

He'd assumed the metal one in the rear passage held wiring. Loose cable covered half its chipped sign, leaving only part of the word maintenance visible. Lin Mo asked him to move the cable first and not pull the door with his injured palm.

Yu Mei got up and shifted the hanging loops.

[MAINTENANCE SUPPLIES]

The catch was rusty but unlocked. She pressed it with the short stick. The ring didn't lift; the door knocked inward with a hollow sound. Fang Ji reached toward it. She looked up, and he withdrew his right hand.

“Push it in, then lift. The door's bearing against the catch.”

She did, gently raising the ring with her other hand. The door opened.

Its edge brushed a cable coil. An empty spool rolled against her shoe. She stopped to settle the coil before searching. Lin Mo watched her legs trembling slightly from crouching. When he asked for a look at the lower shelf, she told him to wait while she changed feet. He waited rather than trying to see over her shoulder.

The cabinet wasn't full of medicine and food. Its top shelf held empty spools, a torn glove, and old nuts in a tray. On the lower shelf, however, stood a metal lunch box. No water had reached its edges. A folded piece of card lay on its lid.

Yu Mei brought it to dry ground by the doorway and opened it.

Four flat plastic packets nestled together. Ordinary compressed biscuits, according to the wrappers. Their seams were still sealed. The outsides were dusty; no damp patches showed within.

He Cen looked up, glasses sliding down his nose.

“Really?”

She brought one closer. Lin Mo's stomach cramped before he remembered to check the date. The wrapper's use-by date was later than the date on his phone. In this place, that wasn't enough for him to promise that everything matched the real world.

“Dry. Seals intact,” Yu Mei said. “I'll open one and look.”

She tore the corner. A thin crack appeared in the biscuit, shedding dry crumbs. No sour smell, no unexplained shine like the water tank's. Fang Ji swallowed visibly while watching it.

Lin Mo didn't ask her to take the first bite as proof for everyone else. He explained what they could and couldn't judge, and said he would eat. The others each agreed. He Cen said his main fear was still being hungry afterward.

Fang Ji brought the cleaner lunch-box lid. Yu Mei wiped away loose dust and broke the biscuits into roughly equal pieces. Four packets for five people wouldn't divide neatly. The last one's edge crumbled. She kept the broken portion. Fang Ji reached with his left hand to exchange two pieces, his bandaged right palm drifting toward the lid to steady it.

“Don't take nothing but crumbs.”

“Keep your dressing off the food.”

He paused, set the pieces from his left hand on the lid, and drew his right palm away, letting her exchange them. Small movements filled the room with sounds that normally wouldn't deserve notice: plastic tearing, wrappers crumpling, teeth breaking hard biscuit.

Lin Mo ate slowly. The first bite was so dry it lodged at the back of his tongue. He didn't try to gulp it down. Yu Mei gave him water to soften it first. The second new bottle emptied. He Cen drank the last water from his old small bottle, and the little remaining in Lin Mo's old bottle was used as well.

Yu Mei opened their third new bottle. Two remained sealed.

Looking at his portion, Lin Mo remembered when his sister hid her medication. She often left some lunch, then told him she'd already eaten. The more urgently he questioned her, the firmer her answers became. Once, he'd simply set his own lunch box by her bed. She stared at it for a long time before asking whether he could take out the green peppers.

He'd been angry. How could she still be picky under those circumstances? Now his mouth was full of dry crumbs, and complaining about peppers seemed remarkably like something a living person ought to do.

“A bit salty,” He Cen said.

Fang Ji laughed. “Still picky.”

“I said salty. I didn't throw it away.”

Lin Mo smiled and bit into his second piece. Nobody told He Cen to be grateful. Nobody required the man with the red lettering to explain why he deserved food. All four packets had been divided up. Yu Mei collected the empty wrappers and bottles; there was no secret fifth packet for anyone.

He Cen's forehead stayed hot. Fang Ji didn't raise his left arm just because he'd eaten. Hunger loosened its sharpest grip, and the room's other problem grew clearer again: water was edging toward their feet.

Yu Mei tipped the final crumbs into her palm and ate them, then wiped the lunch-box lid with a wrapper. Fang Ji offered his left hand to take it. She kept it away. That hand still had doors to open. It didn't need a coating of greasy powder.

“Why keep it?” He Cen asked.

“For the rubbish. Unless you want it by your feet.”

He offered his flattened wrapper. His hand shook midway. She took it without saying the food would make him strong. A little skin had peeled from his lower lip. When Fang Ji brought him water, he drank in several small swallows instead of forcing it down at once.

The red-lettered man ate slowest. His share wasn't bigger. He held the last piece between two fingers as though finishing it would remove his only evidence that another mouthful might still be given to him. Lin Mo neither hurried him nor watched until he swallowed to prove the distribution fair.

Yu Mei checked the glove in the cabinet. The fingertips were torn and its lining had stiffened together. She put it back rather than wrapping anyone's wound in it. The cabinet was large. So far, its usable contents amounted to four packets they'd already eaten.

Fang Ji tapped the bottom, hearing a hollow space beyond the back. Yu Mei warned him off using the heel of his hand. He switched to the stick, probing a corner and lifting an old label. A cup was drawn on it. Beneath it lay dust, no box.

“Thought there might be something to drink.”

“We have water,” He Cen said.

“Something hot would've been nice.”

Nobody answered immediately. Lin Mo remembered the hospital corridor's water dispenser, which people complained took too long. Press its button and it first coughed out air. He'd complained too. Remembering that small cough made the biscuit salt stronger on his tongue.

Yu Mei set the third bottle near the back door. Imagining hot water didn't warm what they had. She reminded everyone to drink before moving; stops to unscrew caps would be awkward on the road. Lin Mo waited until she'd drunk, then asked for the notebook.

As she stood, the cabinet door swung slowly inward. Light crossed the back panel, catching a copper edge.

Half a copper plate had been hidden behind the lunch box.

Yu Mei lit it with the phone. Bent double lines marked a maintenance cart lane on one side and a small drawing with shoulder supports on the other. Short crossbars indicated levels. Someone had crossed out the uppermost outward-transfer opening.

“Same road?” Fang Ji asked.

Lin Mo asked for a photograph of the whole plate before comparing it with Old Chen's half diagram.

They weren't identical. Copper preserved the route's bends. The paper added little boxes labeled current section, pending transfer, and fragment. Its torn edge fell beside the lane. What was missing wasn't a straight arrow to his sister. It was two switchbacks.

Yu Mei pointed to the plaque they'd photographed. The arrangement of its lettering matched the drawing, as did a horizontal notch at the corner. Lin Mo traced the double line upward and found another entrance, not at the receiving hall, but beside the mesh gate beyond the load frame.

“Another door next to the steep slope?”

She considered it. Wire mesh, with a panel. She'd taken it for a barrier to stop someone falling.

The gentler route branched away from the steep one, bent around a square room, and reached the same level. Earlier, he'd stared at lower-level maintenance and felt himself dropping down that impossible slope. He hadn't examined the wall beside it.

The handwriting they thought was Old Chen's crowded the paper's upper margin.

[DON'T USE A PERSON AS A CART.]

Two arrows ran below it. One pointed to a black hand-gripping rectangle labeled hold. The other led to a small circle marked bypass. None of the writing entered the folded private portion. Yu Mei could read it without opening that corner.

Lin Mo remembered Fang Ji's hand bracing the scenery. It was convenient to treat a person as a component for lifting weight, especially when he always reached out first. Now that palm was bandaged, and his left shoulder injured too. The error in the diagram had acquired a weight of its own.

“Keep this open. Compare it at the door.”

Fang Ji leaned closer without reaching for the pages. He understood the gentler route and asked whether it led back to the entrance. Lin Mo pointed out the crossed-out outward-transfer opening. They'd only seen the other end near the hall; that didn't make it an exit.

“But there's dry ground,” Yu Mei said.

Beyond the second bend, the copper diagram showed a small square platform, perhaps somewhere to park equipment. They would have to see whether it still existed. At least it gave them somewhere to check beyond the cloth softening in the water here.

He Cen smoothed a wrapper they'd missed, running his thumb along the compressed folds. Its breakfast picture showed milk in a cup with a spoon beside it. He studied it, turned the picture away, and asked whether just the two of them were going again.

“First we check if the chair fits,” Lin Mo said. “First landing, then back.”

He Cen looked at the chair without claiming he'd find walking easier. He put his gray jacket on and tried standing beside the joined stools. His knees weakened. He sat again. Yu Mei told him to rest rather than exhaust himself before the road had been checked.

The man by the door asked whether they needed him. He recognized certain lights, he began, until Fang Ji looked at him.

“Stay there and explain what you've seen.”

Lin Mo didn't add forever. Once they knew the route, the man could decide whether to follow. His claim to recognize lights didn't justify taking someone else's legs along to find out.

Yu Mei returned the diagram to the notebook, leaving the inner fold untouched, and photographed the copper plate. They left the heavy lunch box beside the cabinet.

As they moved toward the back, a faint noise came from the front. Not a bottle. Water had reached a cabinet leg and floated an old sweet wrapper free. Fang Ji blocked it with his shoe, as though unwilling to let it slip beneath the stools, then laughed at himself and lifted his foot.

Lin Mo noted the drifting paper. “How deep now?”

“Still below the shoe sole. Slow rise.”

“I'll ask again when we're back.”

The short rear passage remained dry. Shoulder supports hung overhead in the frame, the locking pin's round head snug against its seat. Yu Mei checked before bringing Lin Mo through the right lane onto the level ground beyond the mesh gate.

The steep slope still lay ahead, broken railing leaning downward.

This time he didn't dwell on its sign. He asked her to light the right side. The beam passed a wire panel and found a low copper plaque, almost gray with dust.

Wiped clean, MAINTENANCE CART LANE matched their photograph.

A black handle beside the door was shaped to receive an entire palm. Small letters above it read MALE POSITION, beside peeling red paint. Lower down, a round iron cover was half buried in dust.

Lin Mo rested the notebook on his lap, open beside Don't use a person as a cart.

“Don't grab the handle yet.”

Yu Mei's hand had already risen. She stopped and slowly lowered it.

Only a few fingers' width separated her cuff from the hollow. The biscuits settled heavily in Lin Mo's stomach. He turned the light toward the lower round cover, away from the part that looked most like an ordinary door handle.
