# Chapter 122: What the Canvas Was For

The white light stopped at the doorway.

Fang Ji waited until the sweat cooled on his shoulders. The beam never swept away. Two footprints remained in it, one ahead of the other, blocking the fallen lighting stand.

“It wasn't doing this when I came through.”

Lin Mo looked up. The lamp trembled, and each movement shifted the footprints forward a fraction. Its bent pivot had caught against a dangling metal strip. It was trying to turn, trapped facing the narrow doorway.

“Don't ask me to climb,” Fang Ji said. “That beam's cracked.”

“Look down here first.”

Lin Mo indicated the floor with his chin. A crossbar on the fallen stand cast a finger-width shadow through the light. Where the footprints met it, their toes appeared cut short.

Fang Ji put a scrap of wood into the beam. Nothing dragged it away; no third footprint appeared. He tried the shadow, waited, then withdrew it.

“Wood's fine. It's people.”

“The chair moved when the light reached my legs.”

Fang Ji draped a corner of canvas over his hand, keeping his skin out of the beam. Its shadow covered a footprint. The dusty floor beneath cleared. When he lifted the cloth, half a foot slowly reappeared.

“We need it high enough to cover the chair.”

Metal clattered beyond the doorway. A woman stood there carrying two empty buckets. Their rims knocked together as she walked. She saw the canvas, looked relieved, then noticed Lin Mo.

“What have you picked up now, Fang Ji?”

“A person.”

“I can see that.”

She set down the buckets. Her left sleeve was rolled above several old burns; she held a folded cloth in her right hand.

“Yu Mei, stay there,” Fang Ji said. “Don't step in the light.”

She looked up at the lamp and withdrew half a step.

“I could get through before.”

“So could I.”

He worked at the old strap around the canvas, tugging twice at its knot. When it wouldn't loosen, he began tearing along a frayed edge.

“That has to cover the beds,” Yu Mei said.

“We need it here first.”

“And afterward? How do we get it down?”

Fang Ji rubbed the knot with his thumb, then rolled part of the canvas back up.

Lin Mo pressed into his backrest. Had he changed his mind? He didn't know how long this patch of shadow would last. He was about to ask Fang Ji to stay when the man pulled the strap from beneath the roll.

“Keep this. Whatever cloth we get back will need tying.”

He pocketed the strap and spread the canvas. It was gray-brown, ragged in places but thick through the middle, enough to keep water off half the sleeping space in the laundry room.

The cloth Yu Mei held had a wet patch. It had soaked through against her clothes. The canvas had somewhere it was needed.

“Could I wait in the shadow while you find something else?” Lin Mo asked.

She examined his legs. “How long have you been sitting?”

He checked his phone. Less than twenty minutes since entering. But holding the chair with one foot was different from sitting on a level floor. His lower back had begun to twitch.

“The way he came through has closed,” Fang Ji added.

Yu Mei pressed her lips together and left the buckets behind. Lin Mo thought she was refusing. Fang Ji crouched beside the stand, picking tangled wire away with his scrap of wood.

She returned dragging a wide panel: the bottom half of an old door, wrapped in black cloth. It was a handspan wider than the space between the chair's large wheels.

“From under the bed. Leave it covered for now. There's good thick wood underneath.”

Fang Ji brought it out through the shadow and stood it between the lamp and the wheelchair. Its shade reached Lin Mo's knees, but light still passed over it onto his backrest.

“Could I duck?”

“And when your shoulder hurts and you straighten up?”

Fang Ji spread the canvas over the panel. It shielded the whole chair. Most of the footprints vanished.

He tested the shadow with a shoe, then shifted his weight into it. His foot stayed put. Only then did he ask Yu Mei to support the other end.

The panel was too low, the canvas heavy. When she held the panel and he took the wheelchair, the cloth sagged. Twice a bright gap opened, and Lin Mo felt his legs tighten.

“Stop a moment.”

Fang Ji released the handles to straighten the canvas. Lin Mo kept his right leg on its support and his injured hands still. The stand was within reach of his shoe. Once, he could have seized it and pulled. He had to keep himself from trying.

Yu Mei spotted an iron hook.

“Hang it there. Panel against the crossbar. Use that brick to stop it sliding.”

They shifted the fallen stand about half a foot, enough to clear the chair's path, and braced a remaining upright against the wall. Canvas above, panel below: the footprints disappeared from the doorway.

A bucket toppled. Yu Mei bent to catch it just as light leaked past the canvas and grazed her hand.

Her hand jerked outward.

Fang Ji lunged to hold the cloth. She stumbled into the shadow, fingers spread, while the bucket rolled toward the upholstered seats.

This time Lin Mo saw what had happened. The bucket hadn't moved until her involuntary jerk sent it away. Once released, it simply rolled; it didn't follow the turning light.

Yu Mei kicked the other bucket clear.

“Leave it. Go.”

Fang Ji eased the wheelchair behind the screen. Hot canvas smelled of old oil. Lin Mo held himself rigid until the rear wheels passed through the doorway.

The corridor was darker than the hall. Wooden frames lined the right wall, remnants of colored paper still attached. The left wall recessed farther in. After a few paces Fang Ji stopped.

A gap cut across the floor. He could step over it, but the chair's fist-sized casters would drop straight through. Broken timber edged the opening; beneath it was empty space.

Yu Mei was still holding the canvas at the doorway.

They needed the door panel.

Fang Ji went back to explain. She looked down the corridor, then upended her remaining bucket beneath the cloth. It wasn't tall enough. She let the canvas trail and moved the brick onto its lower edge to hold it down.

Fang Ji dragged the panel over and peeled back its black covering. The wood was sound, the heavy edges intact. He checked the floor on both sides of the gap, cleared loose splinters, and laid the panel across.

The opening was less than a palm wide. The panel rested on solid flooring for more than two handspans at each end. He tested both sides with his weight, then had Yu Mei tuck the loose cloth clear of the wheels.

There was room for both casters, with space to spare. Fang Ji lined up the chair while all four wheels remained on solid ground. Yu Mei crouched on the far side to watch its course.

“A little lip at this end,” Lin Mo said. “Slowly.”

The casters rose onto the panel. His supported knee tightened at the slight tilt, and he asked them to stop. They waited while he settled against the backrest.

The small wheels crossed the covered gap and reached the far side. Only then did the large wheels climb onto the panel.

Fang Ji's hand slipped.

The chair jolted and stopped. Yu Mei held its front frame, both casters secure on the far floor. Fang Ji changed his grip. A cut had opened across his right palm.

“Where did you do that?” she asked.

“The hook.”

He pressed his hand against his trousers, then used the uncut side of his palm. With Yu Mei steadying the frame ahead, they rolled both large wheels across. Neither relaxed until the chair was completely clear.

Sweat covered Lin Mo's forehead. He didn't ask them to hurry. Fang Ji was dripping blood, and Yu Mei still had to retrieve the bucket and canvas.

A crackle came from the doorway.

Sparks appeared on the cloth.

Yu Mei ran back, lifted the bucket, and tugged. An upper corner was caught on the hook. A burning scrap fell at her feet. She snatched off her light jacket to beat it out.

Fang Ji shouted for her to come back.

“The bottom's still good!”

“Yu Mei!”

She seized the torn lower edge and ripped. Most remained on the hook. She dragged less than half through the doorway, smothered the embers with the bucket's base, and gathered the remnant to her chest.

The footprints returned.

Through the remaining cloth, Lin Mo saw toes reaching toward the threshold. Fang Ji pushed him away before they finished that step. Yu Mei caught up and shouldered a crooked door shut until only a finger-width gap remained.

The applause receded behind it.

They stopped in the laundry room at the corridor's end. Old sinks lined three walls. Two low beds stood beside them. An adult man lay on one, facing the wall beneath a patchwork covering.

Yu Mei checked his forehead before putting the salvaged canvas at the bed's foot. Water dripped from overhead beside the bundle.

Watching the wet patch spread, Lin Mo began to understand what getting him here had cost.

Fang Ji locked the wheelchair brakes. Following Lin Mo's instructions, they supported his lower back and right leg, let him plant his left foot, and eased him onto the empty bed. His vision darkened as he reached its edge. Both waited until it cleared.

The mattress was thinner than the hospital's. A join in the boards pressed into his back. When he tried to shift, pain caught his left shoulder.

“Wait.”

Yu Mei rolled old clothes beneath his side while Fang Ji supported his legs. Together they moved him off the join. He could finally let out his breath.

She crouched at his feet and loosened his laces.

“Can I take these off?”

He nodded. Supporting his heel, she eased off the right shoe. Two pebbles fell from his sock. All that way, he'd thought the pain came from his injury.

“Let's look at the other heel.”

His left sock was damp. Holding the chair against the floor had rubbed a red mark into his heel, though the skin hadn't broken. She wiped away the dust and placed both shoes beneath the bed, toes pointing where he could see them.

Fang Ji lingered beside him.

“You can get back in the chair, can't you?”

Lin Mo understood what worried him.

“I need a rest. I know you can't keep carrying me.”

“I didn't mean now.”

Fang Ji moved the chair against the wall, leaving space to reach the sinks, and checked both brakes. Being able to lie down didn't make any of that something Lin Mo could take for granted.

His shoulder still hurt. He saw Fang Ji hide his bleeding hand behind his back.

“Let me see. I brought clean dressings.”

Yu Mei opened the small bag beside the wheelchair and found two bottles of water. Her eyes stopped on them, then moved past Lin Mo to the other bed.
