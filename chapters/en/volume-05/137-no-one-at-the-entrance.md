# Chapter 137: No One at the Entrance

He Cen enlarged the photograph with his thumb. The edge of the white beam filled half the screen.

“What's that?”

Lin Mo told him how the wheelchair had been pulled toward an empty seat. He Cen's thumb stopped. He reduced the picture to its original size and handed the phone back without asking why they hadn't simply crossed.

Yu Mei rested on a stool for ten minutes and drank before standing. She left the second bottle with Fang Ji, roughly a third of its water remaining, and took the phone and the short stick from their previous trip. Lin Mo asked whether she wanted to bring some.

“We've seen how far it is. I'll drink when we're back. No bag swinging into cabinet corners.”

They retraced the strip by the wall. The strap still lay on the floor; the cabinet hadn't moved. Each familiar, unchanged thing let Lin Mo relax a fraction. Then the receiving hall's long lamps appeared, and the relief tightened inside him again.

The column of light was moving toward the narrow passage.

Yu Mei braked in shadow and walked along the wall to the fallen curtain, keeping her toes inside the raised strip. Rather than pull it, she folded a loose corner inward. Dust rose, each grain startlingly clear in the light.

Concrete continued behind it, wide enough for the chair. A broken hanging rod pinned the other end. She couldn't remove the curtain, only fold enough aside to make room.

“We wait for it to turn back.”

Lin Mo watched the light. It didn't return according to his counted breaths. Near the passage, it lingered as though illuminating a guest who hadn't yet sat down. One sweep was not enough to establish a reliable cycle.

Yu Mei returned to the handles. They waited in the wall's shadow. Lin Mo heard his own quick breathing and loosened his jaw. He almost mentioned waiting during training. The old experience wouldn't give him working legs now. Saying it might only suggest that she ought to trust he had an answer.

At last, the beam turned toward the center.

Yu Mei moved up to the curtain, stopped, and waited for the light to travel farther before proceeding along the cleared concrete. Lin Mo kept his shoulders against the cushion. His footrest passed within inches of the bright edge. He watched his left shoe without reaching out a foot to hurry them.

A crack passed his ear, then old nail holes, then a boarded window. The lamps hummed behind them. Yu Mei stopped abruptly.

A caster had rolled onto a fold of cloth.

She held the handles. The chair neither advanced nor tipped toward the light. Lin Mo could see the fabric's other end beneath the rod. Pushing would wind it tighter around the axle.

“Back first.”

She eased the chair back half a hand's width. The cloth loosened. With the brakes set, she crouched in front and slowly drew out the freed section. The rod gave a small sound behind it.

“Stop.”

The white beam was turning their way.

She left the final piece where it was, tucking the corner against the wall to keep it clear. The hum seemed to climb through Lin Mo's teeth. His body tensed before he could decide what to do.

Light brushed the outside of the raised strip.

The chair shifted outward. Locked wheels scraped sharply. Yu Mei was already behind it, holding it toward the wall. Her shoulder struck brick with a dull thump that clenched Lin Mo's stomach. He didn't shout for speed. Watching the front, he told her the caster was still on concrete.

She pulled back a little. The chair straightened.

The light passed.

Yu Mei stood still, sweat covering her forehead. She rolled her shoulder and winced. It hurt from the impact, she said; she could still grip. Lin Mo didn't ask her to move immediately. They waited until the beam had gone well away before covering the remaining distance.

There was no door at the passage mouth.

Inside, the lamps quieted and the floor's pulling sensation vanished. These were the same narrow bricks he'd seen coming in, smelling of damp lime. A corner was missing on the left, where Fang Ji had warned him not to drop a wheel. Yu Mei stopped short. Lin Mo pointed out the iron edging.

The covering layer filled the corridor's end.

Gray-white and flat, much like an ordinary wall. Two rows of screw holes still framed it. A deep scrape marked one place the chair had caught. He held the phone close and compared an earlier photograph from the real-world side. The position matched. The light did not.

He couldn't see Mr. Yuan.

Or the iron door by the real stairwell. It hadn't been directly ahead of the anomalous passage, but off to one side. For a moment he thought a sharper angle might reveal someone's shoe.

He remembered Mr. Yuan standing on the right while the other transport worker steadied the chair. Someone had warned him to keep his sleeve off the edging, in an ordinary voice, as though there were other jobs after this one. The gray wall blocked more than two people who could help him out. It blocked the kind of day in which finishing one task meant going on to another.

He'd checked their contact details repeatedly before asking them to wait, yet hadn't retained the second worker's name. He'd expected a short separation. Now he couldn't call him anything but sir. He opened his mouth, then closed it.

Yu Mei stayed beside him without rushing to knock. She shone the phone over a wheel mark in the dust and a pale scuff from broken brick. They made the entrance feel unmistakably real, and the absence of an answer harder to bear.

Lin Mo asked for the old photograph again. The iron edging had the same irregular rust gap. Beyond the second hole on the right, the same diagonal scratch remained. At least they hadn't mistaken a similar opening for their way in.

Knowing that didn't help.

Yu Mei crouched and used the stick to shift dust beside the chipped brick. Fibers left on their arrival were caught in the joint, now wet. She left them there, showing him how water was gathering where the wheel could most easily slip.

“He Cen couldn't stand here long either.”

Lin Mo looked at the narrow patch of solid ground. If everyone came, those at the front would knock while the others waited near the long lamps. He couldn't line their bodies up and expect hope to shield the last person from the light.

“No,” he said quietly.

Still, he didn't want the chair turned. His fingers curled against the armrest. The skin under his wrist support itched where he couldn't scratch. His back and shoulder ached from sitting. He waited for a wave of pain to pass, then forced himself to look away from the wall, at Yu Mei's feet and the space they needed to retreat.

“This is the place?” she asked.

“We came through here.”

She moved him near without crossing the iron edging and knocked with her knuckles. A dull sound shook flakes of dust onto her hand. She didn't pound repeatedly or touch the holes.

Lin Mo called Mr. Yuan's name.

The passage pressed his voice back against his ears. He gave his own name and location, then waited. No answer came through the wall. The phone had no signal.

He could estimate the elapsed real-world wait from its clock. He couldn't know whether the men had left as agreed, gone for help, or could no longer see the entrance at all. He refused to imagine someone still planted on the same brick just so there would be hands ready when he returned.

Yu Mei listened against the wall and finally shook her head.

“Try somewhere else?”

His left hand rose a little from the armrest, then settled. The surface was almost within reach, yet he couldn't stand and trace its screw holes. He hated that. Having Yu Mei pick at it for him wouldn't make the hatred useful.

“Photograph it. Tell them it didn't open this time.”

His dry throat hurt. His voice diminished on didn't open.

She took a wide shot, then the edging and chipped corner, and stepped aside to make space for the chair to reverse. Lin Mo kept looking. For a while, his sister had been frightened whenever he left, asking how long even when he went to the bathroom. He'd always said soon. Only later had he realized soon meant hearing his slippers return beside her bed.

He couldn't even send that word through this wall.

Yu Mei laid the notebook on his lap and recorded the condition and time in a single line. He read it, nodded, and asked her to put it away.

An opening on the passage's inner side had been hidden by fallen scenery on their arrival. Viewed from this direction, two shallow tracks led through it, like marks worn by countless carts.

They ran toward the rear side of the hall.

Yu Mei went to the opening, remaining within sight, and raised the phone. No white beam. A waist-high railing stood inside, beneath a copper plaque with black dust along its lower edge.

[MAINTENANCE CART LANE]

Lin Mo looked at his bag. A double line ran to the torn edge of Old Chen's half diagram. He'd concentrated on lower level and hadn't considered the other line as a usable route.

Yu Mei didn't enter. She photographed the tracks and sign together. From the opening, she said, the ground sloped down much more gently than the stretch beyond the back gate.

“It might go around.”

“Might,” he said. “Let's compare the diagram first.”

He spoke the qualification quickly, afraid a slower version would sound to him like finally. They'd already been away from the others for a while. They had a wall that hadn't opened and a road whose destination they hadn't seen.

Back in the hall, Yu Mei waited for the beam to pass before following the shadow. The tucked curtain hadn't fallen beneath the wheels. She still checked. Looking at the shoulder she had struck, Lin Mo didn't ask her to risk retrieving the last corner.

Water was slightly deeper outside the repair room.

Fang Ji lifted the soaked cloth and squeezed out gray water. Seeing their faces, his movements slowed. He didn't begin by asking whether they'd found an exit.

Lin Mo showed everyone the entrance photographs.

He Cen stared at the screw holes for a long time. The red-lettered man's lips moved as though he might say it had never been intended for them. He kept silent.

“It didn't open on this trip,” Lin Mo said. “We don't know whether it will later. We can't have you go there and wait now.”

Yu Mei brought up the cart lane. They'd checked the sign, not the end. She left the phone on Lin Mo's lap and sat, rubbing her shoulder.

He Cen saw the dusty mark and asked what had happened. She briefly described the curtain and beam. Fang Ji tested the water depth outside with the stick, then propped it against the wall.

“We can't stay here forever either.”

Water slipped beneath the door, curving around a stool leg. He Cen slowly lifted his feet. Yu Mei gathered his jacket clear.

Lin Mo opened the notebook to the half diagram. The copper plaque's photograph lay beside its unread double line, a drop of water from Yu Mei between them.

He waited for it to be wiped away. He Cen was still watching the entrance photograph. He said he'd left an umbrella outside, thinking he'd fetch it soon. Fang Ji asked what kind. Black, with a broken rib. He Cen shook his head, as though the description were useless.

Lin Mo remembered it. If they reached outside again, he wanted enough time to ask whether he'd found the umbrella, rather than remember this man only by his temperature and how many steps he could walk.

He looked toward the rear passage and asked whether anyone had checked its cabinet.
