# Chapter 144: Behind the Curtains

Yu Mei drank, returned the bottle to He Cen, and stayed seated.

Lin Mo waited. Eyes closed, she pressed her bruised shoulder while her other hand lay empty on her knee. A few minutes earlier, on a screen, that hand had been attached to somebody else's collar.

“Did I say something harsh?” He Cen asked.

“It wasn't you.”

He tightened the cap and leaned the bottle against a bench leg. Less than half remained. Lin Mo watched the surface and swallowed without asking for more.

Fang Ji picked up the light bag of empties and indicated the slope behind them.

“Two bends back up. Then the wet room.”

The Seatkeeper sat just inside the door, his left leg extended. He mentioned that the seat upstairs could illuminate too, then fell silent. He did not turn several glowing screens into proof that down was their only direction.

Yu Mei opened the route photograph. Beyond the transfer room's rear-right opening, a path followed the wall. The outer side was labeled display; a narrow inner line was labeled maintenance. A solid roof marked the block at its end. There were no symbols promising beds or food.

“I'll inspect that stretch,” she said. “You stay here. No helping me hold things down with your foot.”

Lin Mo nodded. She took neither the water nor the notebook. He could still see half her body after she passed the table. Fang Ji relayed their calls from the doorway, his voice hoarse.

A light door sounded, followed by slow footsteps. Yu Mei reported a descending route, then paused before adding that a separate walkway ran beside it. She could reach the first bend.

“Coming back.”

Lin Mo watched her emerge beside the table. White dust marked her knuckles where she had checked the brickwork. She reported a roofed recess beyond the bend, with a broad concrete ledge to sit against. It lay about two turns away; she had not yet reached the final portion.

He Cen lowered his feet.

Yu Mei supported his forearm as he rose, waiting for the dizziness to pass. He moved along the wall into the broad doorway, then rested while she returned for the wheelchair. Fang Ji carried the light bag, keeping his injured palm against his chest. He left the two sealed bottles hanging inside the push handles. The Seatkeeper took the third bottle and followed He Cen.

At the table, Lin Mo heard that voice again.

“Brother.”

The preview was closed. This identical voice came from another frame. He Cen stiffened and glanced back, as though afraid it might pin the chair to the floor.

Lin Mo watched the real threshold.

“Right,” he said.

Yu Mei pushed. The edge of a frame passed slowly before his eyes. He did not look up again before they reached the opening. His left hand remained open on the armrest, free to tremble.

The corridor was wider than the diagram suggested.

Thin curtains hung along its outer side, gray with what looked like smoke. Tall figures brightened behind them. When one bent down, its shadow covered the wall, smaller shadows moving across its back. Laughter, whispers, and hurried breaths overlapped in the gaps, leaving no voice room to finish.

The cart lane descended beside the curtains. Two shallow wheel grooves converged toward a dark opening.

Yu Mei did not take it. Against the brickwork, partly concealed by a curtain edge, ran the gentler maintenance path she had inspected. Its intact concrete was wider than the wheelchair. Lin Mo recognized her handprint in the dust.

“Stay by the wall.”

He Cen led, moving his palm across two bricks at a time before pausing for breath. The Seatkeeper followed half a step behind without reaching forward to hurry him. Yu Mei pushed the chair. Fang Ji came last, the empty bottles clicking in their soft bag.

“He's falling!” someone shouted behind the cloth.

He Cen was upright. The voice made him flinch, and his feet actually tangled. His left hand shot toward what appeared to be a rail beside the curtain.

“Don't grab it!” Lin Mo shouted.

His hand stopped. Yu Mei had set the brakes and was about to leave the chair when the Seatkeeper put down the bottle and extended his right forearm between He Cen and the cloth.

He Cen did not drop his weight onto it. Seeing the arm, he stopped short, found the brick wall again, and slid down against it. The Seatkeeper withdrew rather than bracing on his injured left leg.

The curtain moved. Its apparent rail bent with it.

Only then did Lin Mo recognize a bright image on the fabric. The actual cart-lane edge lay below, with part of its lip broken away. A step in that direction would have found empty space.

“I saw a rail,” He Cen said after a long time.

“So did I,” Fang Ji answered.

Yu Mei crouched beside him instead of immediately helping him stand. The Seatkeeper retrieved and opened the bottle, putting it within reach. Watching the curved projection, Lin Mo remembered the painted scenery he had mistaken for a supporting beam.

Behind the curtain, the voice shouted falling again. This time it was laughing.

Lin Mo looked toward it.

“He didn't. You waited for nothing.”

The laughter paused briefly, then resumed farther along, as though his reply had been passed to another listener. He had won nothing. At least the anger had somewhere to go. He Cen swore under his breath too.

Yu Mei asked whether he could reach the bend, away from this curtain. He shook his head first. Only later did he nod, drawing his feet closer before standing against the wall. She held his forearm until he steadied, then returned to the chair.

Every trip between them cost her effort. Lin Mo did not disguise the remaining distance as almost there. The route photograph could wait until the bend. For now they needed the wall and floor directly before them.

The first turning was level and wide enough to turn the wheelchair. Yu Mei parked it facing the way back, set the brakes, and sat against the wall.

Two empty small bottles rolled out of Fang Ji's bag. He stopped them with his shoe instead of bending his injured shoulder after them. The Seatkeeper nudged them back toward the opening and leaned against the opposite wall.

He Cen looked at him. “Thank you for that.”

The man answered quietly. Fang Ji kept watching the path. He neither joined in nor interrupted.

The red fragments at the man's shoulder flickered. An outer curtain displayed his kneeling shadow, its leg uninjured, apparently bowing to somebody. Lin Mo looked away. He would not supply a suitably satisfying punishment scene for the man here.

After resting, Yu Mei went around the last bend. Two taps on the brickwork let them hear where she was. She called back that the recess had a roof and dry ground, a wide enough opening for the chair, and nothing visibly glowing inside.

Lin Mo did not take that to mean nothing could ever glow. He waited for her return and the details of the last few meters: a gentler slope, flush joints, and loose cloth by the entrance. She had checked beneath it; the concrete had no hole.

They started again.

This time nobody warned that someone would fall. Quiet inhalations stirred behind the fabric, like unseen spectators leaning forward, afraid a sound might disturb what happened next.

Lin Mo watched the floor until the wheelchair passed the final flush seam. His knee took no jolt, but friction against his shoe made his left heel burn again. Yu Mei stopped at the opening and eased the shoe edge away. No wetness marked the sock. His foot stayed on its support.

The recess was dry.

A solid concrete slab roofed it. The broad ledge grew directly from the floor, with no hinged underside like the bench. He Cen sat, leaning his head against the wall. His eyes closed and reopened until Yu Mei spoke to him. Then he slowly gathered his jacket into his arms.

Fang Ji left the empties in a corner. The Seatkeeper passed Yu Mei the third bottle. She gave Lin Mo a mouthful and drank herself, leaving a shallow layer at the bottom. Without opening another bottle, she took the two sealed ones out of the damaged bag and stood them against the wall, relieving the handles of their weight.

Lin Mo's lower back had stiffened badly. He looked at the ledge several times, wanting to lie down. It was too far below the wheelchair seat, with insufficient room for two people to stand around a transfer.

He asked only to change position. Yu Mei eased his cushion higher so he could loosen his back without leaving its support.

The splint rasped against his clothes. He tried relaxing his left shoulder, but pain pulled through toward his chest. When he said stop, she stopped, without another tug to make him look comfortable.

“Better?”

“I can sit. It isn't pressing in the same place.”

She flattened a fold in his trousers beside the protected knee without undoing its support. Watching her bowed head, he remembered clamping his mouth shut in the Theater to keep anyone from seeing pain. Now asking someone to straighten his clothes required its own accumulation of strength.

When she sat again, she wiped her face with her sleeve.

“Don't start apologizing.”

“I was going to ask whether that wall is pressing your bad shoulder.”

She blinked, then shifted to a position that spared it.

“The wall isn't going to move for us,” He Cen muttered with his eyes closed. “Make yourselves comfortable.”

Fang Ji gave a short laugh.

Lin Mo kept his phone dark. The wheelchair's dim clip light went off too. Gray illumination leaking from behind the curtains was enough for nearby objects. They did not have to spend their remaining batteries making every corner visible.

“This counts as farther down?” He Cen asked.

“Yes.”

“I'm not doing this to help find your sister.”

Lin Mo looked up. The man's eyes remained closed.

“I know.”

“I moved because I couldn't sit back there. When you go looking for her, tell us where the water is and which way you've gone.”

“I will.”

He Cen asked for no assurance that everyone would escape together. His hand still trembled, and the walk had not left his fever upstairs. But after stating his own reason, he seemed to relax a little, relieved of being made braver in somebody else's account.

“Could Old Chen's things be farther down?” Yu Mei asked.

Lin Mo glanced at his bag. All three technical pages remained inside the notebook; only the edge of his own route notes showed.

“If we find something, we'll check. We haven't yet.”

“Don't listen to another preview like that for an answer.”

He nodded. The memory of Xiao Lu's face chilled his fingertips. He laid his left hand flat on the armrest instead of hiding it.

An opening on the other side of the recess led to a short level corridor. Yu Mei could see its end from where she sat. A dark sign stood there, its lettering divided by shadows from the curtains.

When a shadow moved, Lin Mo could finally read it.

[Lower Convergence]

He did not ask anyone to get up. He Cen's breathing had slowed; old blood still marked Fang Ji's dressing. Yu Mei leaned against the wall with damp hair covering one side of her face.

A drop-like sound came from the far end of the passage.

Not water. After several repetitions, Lin Mo recognized the gentle smacking of lips in the dark. Another sound answered from farther away. More tiny noises gathered below, merging into a single breath that had not yet been released.
