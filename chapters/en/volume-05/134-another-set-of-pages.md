# Chapter 134: Another Set of Pages

The handwritten warning crowded a gap in the old print.

Yu Mei opened the cover farther. A paper corner lifted, revealing fine lines running through several boxes. Words filled spaces beside them; two had later been struck out. At the top stood a heading rather than a person's name.

Low-level classification transcription

Lin Mo asked her to wait before turning another page.

They'd locked only the load-bearing frame. The right-hand display still separated male from female. The handwritten warning matched what they could see. That didn't vouch for everything else. Yu Mei brought the phone closer. Fainter writing showed through the thin paper from its reverse.

“Machine instructions?”

“Possibly the print. Handwriting's separate.”

Binding holes lined the lower edge, without thread. A hinged metal strip held the sheets against the cover's back; oil stuck only their outer edges. Supporting the loose corner, Yu Mei gently worked her fingertips along the paper, stopping wherever it resisted.

The outermost sheet finally lay flat.

Printed boxes contained English and short symbols. Lin Mo recognized simple words such as body and path. A page wouldn't let him explain an entire program. Handwriting filled the gaps, thin strokes changing direction as though someone had copied a little at a time, afraid they wouldn't recognize later what they'd seen.

Yu Mei read one annotation.

“This field changed. The name didn't.”

An arrow led to the printed field body_shape. F stood beside it; another line read return. The marks varied in darkness. Someone had traced the original lettering before adding Chinese alongside.

Inside the cover, Female lane still shone beside Body outline. Its relationship to these words seemed similar. Similarity helped them interpret what was here. It couldn't establish that every door marked with that letter would admit him.

“This too.” Yu Mei lowered the light.

I've told it three times. It doesn't read what I say.

Lin Mo's throat tightened. He'd just told this classifier he was a man and received no answer. The writer had arrived earlier and recorded the number of attempts, finally circling only the field for body outline.

He almost asked who had written it before realizing Yu Mei couldn't know.

A narrow old label lay beneath the paper's lower corner. Its right edge was damaged. On the left, small print read Transcribed by. Three handwritten characters followed.

Clothes Mending Four

Lin Mo sat upright so suddenly his left shoulder hurt. He kept staring as the diagram and Yu Mei's hand briefly blurred.

“Wait.”

She went still.

This designation had appeared on other people's papers before: the Original Materials Room, the sound box, the red-edged cabinet. The empty seat had displayed its associated reference, Rack C, Code Seven. Seeing a designation couldn't call back a voice that wasn't here.

But this label said Transcribed by.

He asked Yu Mei to put his notebook from the bag on his lap and find the earlier mention of Clothes Mending Four. He'd copied it himself. A corrected stroke remained beside it. Rack C, Code Seven was there too. Old Chen's bodily disappearance hadn't turned the page blank.

Yu Mei held it where he could see, without pressing his own handwriting against the label.

“Same designation?”

“Yes. Doesn't prove he wrote every stroke here.”

Keeping his left arm supported, Lin Mo touched his notebook's corner instead of reaching for the pages behind the cover. He remembered Old Chen's handwriting approximately. Not well enough to certify each oil-stained mark.

The urge to recognize it was stronger than he wanted to admit.

Once he'd leaned back again, Yu Mei asked whether this was the man he'd mentioned.

“Old Chen.”

She studied the paper. “How did his notebook get here?”

“It's not that notebook.”

He answered too quickly, then slowed. Sun Ning had last carried Old Chen's broken-strapped bag, leaking cup, damaged booklet, and old tag. Lin Mo hadn't recovered them. These were equipment diagrams on old paper, binding holes at their corners, fixed behind a machine cover. They weren't the thin booklet with its sewn corner he remembered.

Wanting to find someone couldn't turn lost belongings into things found elsewhere.

“Maybe separate copied pages he left. Read what these say first.”

Yu Mei closed his notebook with the photograph still in place. Supporting the outer sheet, she unfolded part of the exposed page beneath, leaving its tightly stuck corner alone.

More writing. Interpretations alongside English lines. Twice, Can pass had been crossed out and replaced with Only tested this frame. A tiny sketch showed the shoulder pads. The person in the first drawing hadn't moved clear. A heavy cross marked the second.

A short note beside it looked hastily written: pin halfway in, thought it held, dropped again after the foot came off. Three slashes followed. Neither Passed nor Success covered what had happened.

Yu Mei breathed in softly.

“Like our first attempt.”

“We didn't leave someone inside.”

“Did he?”

Lin Mo didn't nod. A person was drawn; the note described the drop. It didn't say who had been hurt, or how badly. He read the visible words again without inventing the rest for the blank face.

Yu Mei touched the removed packing block. Had the writer put it there? Lin Mo asked her to keep it, but proximity didn't prove the same person had placed both objects.

“I nearly thought that when I saw Clothes Mending Four.”

Looking at the heavy cross, he remembered pointing out the false beam beneath the scenery. A familiar shape had reassured him then. Afterward Fang Ji's hand had bled over broken wood.

He wouldn't use a familiar name as another beam.

“Even if these are his, we check each part.”

“He knew this kind of thing?”

“I saw him take notes. Keep what he'd actually seen separate. Saw him choose a wrong old door too, misremember where to collect something.”

Yu Mei stopped asking whether Old Chen understood machines. One English letter had been copied incorrectly and corrected in paler strokes. Beneath it: Can't see clearly. Leave blank for now. Whoever left these sheets had struggled to see things too.

Lin Mo asked her to include that in the photograph.

She drew the phone back. The clearest arrow had filled the image before; now the little note about leaving a blank entered the frame. He didn't want a record already tidied into answers.

Fang Ji coughed outside. He Cen wanted to know whether the pages showed where food was kept below.

“Not yet,” Yu Mei called.

“Then don't call searching on an empty stomach clever,” He Cen's distant voice answered.

Fang Ji laughed softly. Lin Mo's mouth moved too, then settled. The person associated with these pages couldn't hear that complaint. Those who could still waited for water and food. He returned to the operating diagram before an old name made him forget to go back.

The cross connected to the warning outside the cover. Creases interrupted a further sentence.

Get the person clear first. Then engage the pin. Don't leave him underneath waiting for you.

Remembering the mark on Fang Ji's shoulder, Lin Mo felt a faint touch inside his chest. This writer hadn't recorded only how to succeed. Something they'd done wrong remained too. The sketch had no face. No name identified who had stood below.

“He'd been here?” Yu Mei asked.

“The paper's here. We don't know yet whether he wrote it at this machine.”

Lin Mo feared sounding unwilling to believe. He also feared filling that empty face with someone familiar the next moment. Near the margin, the character for Chen had been traced heavily. He studied it in silence.

Old stood in front, strokes crowded into a diagram line. Yu Mei adjusted the light until both emerged. They sat beside a form of address someone had altered. Crossed-out strokes concealed the original, leaving only part of the character meaning older sister.

Old Chen

Lin Mo returned his left hand to the armrest and slowly curled its uninjured fingers.

He remembered the person collecting sound boxes at the water station calling him Sister Chen. Just call me Old Chen, he'd answered. Still clutching his torn bag, cup rim protruding, he hadn't chosen a more convenient name when someone addressed him wrongly.

The evidence now went beyond one designation.

“That sounds like something he'd do.”

Heat rose behind Lin Mo's eyes. The paper hadn't brought Old Chen back. His absence made it impossible to hold it up and ask who wrote a line, why it was crossed out, what happened to the figure beneath the heavy mark.

Yu Mei didn't declare this good news. She steadied the light.

Fang Ji called to ask whether they'd found something. Lin Mo explained and told him to stay by the rear door rather than follow another familiar name inside. He heard Fang Ji report only maintenance notes possibly connected to Old Chen. Not Old Chen himself.

Lin Mo released the breath he'd held.

Several paler, less legible lines occupied the paper's bottom. Crowded into a margin, they were later additions rather than part of the classification diagram's original explanation.

“Turn it?” Yu Mei asked.

Lin Mo looked at the inward fold. Before leaving, Old Chen had still been telling him not to go through his notebook. Lin Mo hadn't agreed to turn every unread private page into material for finding freedom tickets. Absence didn't cancel that refusal now.

“Only the exposed operating notes.”

“There might be a name inside.”

“Or something else he didn't want people reading.”

She nodded and let the corner settle without taking his hesitation as permission to unfold it. In the exposed text, a separate box held two legible English words.

missing

empty

Beneath the first, someone had written missing segment in Chinese. Beneath the second, empty segment. A heavy diagonal separated them. Lin Mo could read the Chinese beside it unaided.

A missing segment isn't an empty segment.

He immediately remembered the answer described on the hospital paper's reverse: it hadn't been entered with the original segment.

That record said only it hadn't come through with the original. He'd resisted calling what he couldn't read nonexistent. Now operating notes possibly left by Old Chen himself distinguished those things beneath a piece of old code.

He asked Yu Mei to photograph the whole page, including the folded unread edge, without opening it. When he returned to the residual voice still asking for its own name, he didn't want to bring hope clipped from its context.

She photographed it, then laid the paper flat against the cover again.

“When you say return,” she asked quietly, “can he still hear you?”

Lin Mo watched the name at the margin.

“There's a place outside where I can hear the voice he left. Sometimes it answers what I say now. Sometimes it can't follow. He didn't come with me.”

Yu Mei stopped glancing into the darkness behind them. Lin Mo didn't call his name and wait for an echo. The axle stayed still. The maintenance-lock notice remained. Outside, Fang Ji asked whether He Cen wanted more water.

That was a voice someone here could answer.
