# Chapter 131: Assigned to the Right

Fang Ji took his hand off the doorframe after reading the last line.

“More looking?”

“Once we've seen it, back we go.”

The beam remained still. Two deeply hollowed shoulder pads hung beneath it. Lin Mo imagined all the shoulders that had fitted there before heading downward. He couldn't see what weight lay beyond the beam, only a black rod running along the wall into the floor.

No matching rod stood on the right.

He asked Fang Ji to lower the phone, looking for the place where the lanes divided. Fine dust covered the threshold. Part of a painted line showed on the left, resembling an ordinary trip-hazard marking. The wheelchair's front wheels stood to the right. Fang Ji was behind and left, an armrest between them.

From a few meters away, Yu Mei asked whether they were coming back.

“Yes.”

To straighten the chair before reversing, Fang Ji stepped left. His sole touched the dusty line.

The shoulder pads dropped.

He ducked too late. The left pad struck his shoulder; the other missed, its metal edge scraping his clothes.

Lin Mo shouted for him to stop.

He'd already released the handles. His body bent beneath the pressure, both feet on the ground, as though something invisible had filled the pad on his left shoulder. He tried withdrawing a foot. As his heel lifted, his knee folded forward.

“Don't force it!”

Sweat appeared on his forehead. He kept his right hand against his chest and braced his left on his thigh.

“Can't get back.”

Yu Mei hurried to the door and steadied the wheelchair before Lin Mo could lean out. She reached for Fang Ji's collar. The pad sank farther as soon as she touched him.

“Don't pull him. Find what releases it.”

Fang Ji said the red lever beside him had moved. It stood inside the right lane, connected at its base to a broad iron footplate less than half a meter beyond the threshold.

Yu Mei locked the chair and reached with her toe. The wall clicked when she pressed the plate.

The pad rose slightly.

Fang Ji drew breath and tried moving backward. The threshold caught Yu Mei's heel; she couldn't depress the plate fully. Changing her footing released it for a moment. The pad immediately descended.

“Let me get steady.”

Fabric stretched tight over Fang Ji's left shoulder. Lin Mo told him to wait until it lifted properly rather than pull backward. Watching the lever, he directed Yu Mei's foot an inch farther in.

She entered the right lane, her whole sole on the plate. The red lever lowered. A gray wheel above it made half a turn. The beam stopped, and the shoulder pads retracted.

Fang Ji felt for clear space over his shoulder before withdrawing the foot inside the line. On dry ground outside, he leaned so heavily against the wall that his forehead struck it softly.

Yu Mei held the plate until even his loose clothing was clear. The pads rose to their original position when she stepped off.

He Cen called from the room. She shouted that Fang Ji was out and he should stay seated, then crouched to inspect the shoulder.

“Don't pull my clothes.”

She waited while he eased his collar aside. A long red strip marked the skin. He could slowly rotate the shoulder; raising his arm halfway hurt. He didn't dismiss it as a little pressure. Lin Mo didn't ask him to prove he could still push.

“Back to the room.”

Fang Ji nodded, feet still trembling. While he rested against the wall, Yu Mei caught the chair's front frame to straighten its casters.

A narrow white light appeared on the right wall.

It swept from Lin Mo's shoes to his shoulders.

He stiffened, remembering the receiving hall's beam dragging his wheelchair. Here there was no applause. The wheels stayed still. Light crossed his chest and settled on his face. The screen displayed:

Female lane

Yu Mei stopped with her hands on the frame and looked up at him.

His jaw ached from clenching. He'd just told them he was a man. Both had heard. Now a screen barely larger than a palm assigned him elsewhere with practiced lettering.

“I'm a man.”

The text didn't change. Rollers whispered inside the wall. Light traveled down his collar, reached the armrest, and turned back. He waited. No host answered. No vote opened. This machine simply repeated its assignment.

Against the wall, Fang Ji painfully lowered his left arm. His sharp breath made Lin Mo look away. He'd almost asked for another scan, ready to argue his name, his former body, this judgment. Fang Ji still hadn't sat down. Yu Mei was holding the chair. None of them owed him a vigil while he waited for a machine to listen.

“Let him rest first.”

The light moved off Lin Mo.

A small barrier ahead of his right caster sank into the floor, revealing a narrow route without shoulder pads. The left marking lit where Fang Ji had stepped.

Male lane

Now they could see the dusty line's full boundary. The entry plate extended slightly beyond the threshold. Fang Ji's step aside had landed on its end. Being outside the door hadn't guaranteed safety; he'd stood on the detection plate while backing up the chair.

“That counts as going in?” he muttered.

Lin Mo stared, stomach cold. Recognizing the hazard didn't feel like a clever discovery. A little sooner, and Fang Ji needn't have bowed his shoulder beneath it.

Yu Mei lit his feet and waited until he stood at the wall, clearly beyond the painted boundary, before pulling the chair back. The right barrier stayed down. Female lane remained illuminated.

“It let you through too,” Fang Ji told her.

“Nothing pressed on me.”

Her footprints showed clearly in the dust, from outside to the red plate. Level ground, no invisible weight, no pads forcing her to bend.

Lin Mo's whole body had been changed. The records outside had changed with it. He couldn't yet tell whether this machine examined bodies alone or consulted something else. His hatred of its classification wouldn't temporarily undo those changes.

He was still Lin Mo. Still a man.

The facts collided in his chest. Neither made way for the other.

Fang Ji looked from the screen and swallowed something he hadn't quite said. He didn't tell Lin Mo it was lucky he was like this now.

“Back?” Yu Mei asked.

“Yes.”

She took the handles, avoided the left detection plate, and reversed onto the level passage. Fang Ji followed slowly. As he passed the place where the light had scanned Lin Mo, the screen chimed and the left pads trembled.

He stopped. Yu Mei could see he hadn't stepped onto the plate. The pads swayed with the light without falling. They stilled when he moved away along the outer wall.

“Have to step inside for it to drop.”

“That's what we saw this time.”

Fang Ji didn't object to the qualification. He watched his shoes on the inspected tiles, avoiding a small patch of pale dust.

He Cen sat with his bottle beside his knee. First he looked at Yu Mei, then at Fang Ji's lowered shoulder.

“Thought you were looking at the door.”

“Stepped on something trying to leave.”

Fang Ji didn't blame Lin Mo or argue as though the question were an accusation. Yu Mei had him sit by the cupboard, put down the phone, and wait until his hands stopped shaking before taking water.

The stranger shifted to make room. Fang Ji chose another stretch of wall.

Lin Mo asked to remain in the wheelchair. He'd only just got up, and Fang Ji had a fresh injury. Another immediate transfer was too much. He rested with his leg supported and the rolled clothing adjusted against his waist.

“You can go through the right?” the stranger asked.

“Do you want to?” Yu Mei said.

“I'm asking.”

He'd asked whether the route worked, not how badly Fang Ji hurt. Lin Mo noted that without erasing the help he'd given at the bed.

“Nothing pressed on us there. We haven't gone farther.”

“Then he could just—”

Meeting Lin Mo's eyes, he stopped.

After waiting, Lin Mo asked who he meant by he.

No answer. Female lane still filled his mind. He wasn't sure one casual suggestion to just go wouldn't bring all his anger down together. He settled against the chair and waited until he could speak normally.

“If I go, I decide.”

Yu Mei brought water to his lips. He choked after a mouthful and coughed twice. She lowered the bottle until he recovered.

After sitting quietly for a long time, Fang Ji said they'd at least found what released the pads.

“Next time, hold that down before checking the left.”

Lin Mo didn't agree to a next time. The red mark on Fang Ji's shoulder deepened. He remembered the old theater giving him tighter clothes, again and again, while calling everyone else's part merely cooperating.

He couldn't repeat that sequence with someone else in his place.

“See how you feel after resting.”

Fang Ji laid his right hand flat on his thigh and turned the left shoulder slightly backward. He stopped at once. Asked where it hurt most, he pointed behind his collarbone without repeating the movement. Yu Mei could inspect his skin and see how far he could move. She couldn't judge what damage lay beneath.

Lin Mo almost told him to get a doctor outside to look at it. The habitual thought rose too quickly. He swallowed it and asked whether his fingers were numb.

“No. Just can't put strength into it. Does yours hurt like this?”

“Some of it. Similar doesn't mean the same.”

“Didn't ask you for a prescription.”

Lin Mo's mouth moved without quite smiling. Fang Ji pushed the bottle toward Yu Mei's foot. Sweat marked his grip; a shallow dent remained in the plastic.

“Don't stand in there to test it,” he said suddenly.

Lin Mo looked up.

“You kept staring left. Like you needed it to press on you too.”

“I wanted to know how it sorts us.”

“Find out another way. Being hurt again won't make anyone remember what you used to look like.”

Lin Mo stayed quiet. The reasons he'd prepared stopped before that dangling arm. He hadn't actually stepped inside. That didn't mean the thought hadn't drawn another look toward the left.

“I won't have you test it for me again.”

“I mean you.”

Yu Mei offered the bottle once more, waiting for him to swallow slowly before taking it back. He Cen had been listening.

“If the right doesn't press down, could we find something there to get everyone through?”

Fang Ji looked toward the door. So did Lin Mo. The white glow hadn't followed them inside.

He remembered the low red lever. They'd seen it raise the pads once. They needed to examine what released weight mechanically and what still depended on the door's classification, with nobody trapped beneath it.

“We can look there first,” he told He Cen. “Without putting anyone on the left.”

Outside, the right barrier remained sunk into the floor. A narrow white strip reached from the corridor beneath the rear door they'd closed.
