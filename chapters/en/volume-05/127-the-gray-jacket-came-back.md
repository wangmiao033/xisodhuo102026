# Chapter 127: The Gray Jacket Came Back

Yu Mei already had her hand on the handle when Lin Mo stopped her.

The relief left her face.

“I know his voice.”

“I've heard voices I knew too.”

He didn't explain what had happened afterward. She studied him, lowered her hand, and braced a shoulder beside the door. First she asked where the person inside was.

“By the sinks. Where have you all gone?”

“We're near the blue door. How far between us?”

A pause.

“Then you're pushing the wrong door.”

Fang Ji came to look. The dark door opened outward. A rag was caught beneath it, no bucket. He used the broken broom to move the cloth and pulled the door open.

A narrow passage followed the thick pipe. At its far end, another sheet-metal door stood open about a foot, blocked by a bucket. Light beneath it outlined the round base.

The person and his voice remained beyond that second door.

Yu Mei crouched and aimed the phone along the floor. The bucket shifted. A familiar chipped bowl appeared in the opening, followed by a hand.

“There. Show you this too. Satisfied?”

“Put the bowl down. Don't come out.”

Her voice rose sharply, then dropped. Leaving the bottles by the entrance, she stepped onto the first brick. Fang Ji lit both ends. Between pipe and wall ran an intact concrete maintenance passage, with no gaps patched by old boards.

Lin Mo watched her reach the far door and squeeze sideways through. The shadow behind it retreated.

“You've got a fever! What are you doing moving buckets?”

“The door kept rattling. Couldn't lie there waiting for it to open itself.”

He sounded breathless. Lin Mo didn't ask for old memories to prove his identity. He waited until Yu Mei had seen him and looked back through the doorway.

“It's him. This is the door behind our sinks. We never used it. All that stuff was in the way.”

Fang Ji gave a brief whistle without smiling. They'd traveled all that way, nearly fallen into the pit, and found a return route a few steps from where they'd begun. Lin Mo had remembered only the walkway He Cen had used. Nobody had moved the empty buckets stacked to catch leaks behind the sinks.

“Stop counting the extra steps,” Yu Mei called. “Come and help.”

Fang Ji looked at Lin Mo.

“I'll wait here. Brakes on. No wet prints.”

He locked both wheels and pushed the door fully open so they could see one another. The red-lettered man reached the wall beside Lin Mo without entering the passage.

“This goes back where you're staying?”

“Let them clear it first.”

Buckets knocked against the wall. Yu Mei sent He Cen back to his bed while Fang Ji passed out the containers. Lin Mo recognized the welded patch on one bucket's bottom. Old rags underneath caught water from a pipe joint. The buckets were empty; the cloth was soaked and heavy.

He Cen explained he'd moved only the outermost two. Yu Mei told him to drink before finishing.

Overhead, the pipe from the blue room's tank ran through the passage and vanished into a dark recess behind the laundry wall. Knocking traveled through water and metal. Standing inside, someone could easily think there were people moving outside the door.

“Could you hear us?” Lin Mo called.

“Bits. Swearing first. Then someone talking about bottles.”

Lin Mo didn't ask which swear. The stranger was looking at the pipe too, gray-faced. They had found one visible source of knocking. The nonexistent lights in the water and the footsteps beneath it still resisted an explanation involving ordinary echoes.

Fang Ji returned for the supplies. He took the bag across, then the two bottles and the jacket, and came back for Lin Mo. The wheelchair would just fit. Its right leg support had to follow the wall; halfway through, they wouldn't be able to turn.

“Did you look underneath this time?” Lin Mo asked.

Fang Ji looked down at him.

“Yes. Including the parts you couldn't see.”

Lin Mo nodded. He didn't brush it aside with a promise to remember. Fang Ji set the phone beside his left fingertips, most of its weight borne by the armrest, its beam on the casters. He kept to the exposed concrete beside the wall. At the far end, Yu Mei held the door's corner clear so the leg support could pass between the bucket shadows.

He Cen sat on his bed, sweaty hair against his forehead and dusty glasses in his hand. Less than a third remained in his old water bottle. Its cap rested on his knee. Seeing the red glow behind them, he raised the glasses and squinted.

“Another one?”

“Leg wound,” Yu Mei said. “Sit by the door. Keep off the wet patches.”

The man settled onto a low stool, back against the wall. Red letters hung before flaking whitewash. He Cen stopped with the glasses halfway to his face.

“Someone from the audience?”

Nobody answered immediately. Fang Ji brought Lin Mo beside the empty bed. Yu Mei positioned the cushions for his transfer. They waited for his left foot to settle before supporting his back and hips off the seat. Only when he lay down did the sweat at his temples run into his hair. He closed his eyes as the boards creaked beneath him.

He Cen asked again.

“He says he is,” Lin Mo answered.

The man by the door lowered his head. Fang Ji finished supporting Lin Mo's leg, then asked He Cen what else he recognized.

“Someone selected standing for me once. A whole night. Letters were that red.”

He lowered the glasses into his lap. He didn't accuse this particular man. Lin Mo glimpsed an old discolored ring around his left ankle before the trouser cuff covered it, as though it wasn't something he wanted shown again.

“Remember a name?”

“I was worrying about my legs.”

A short, ugly laugh escaped him.

“Might never have finished reading it.”

Yu Mei laid his jacket on the bed. Several seconds passed before he reached into its pocket for the lens cloth. One corner was wet. He used another, swore when it smeared the lens, then turned it over.

Watching him polish, Fang Ji eventually asked whether he wanted anything else brought back.

“Yes.”

“What?”

“You lot.”

Fang Ji turned away and placed three sealed bottles beneath the bed. Yu Mei left the opened one within He Cen's reach and the fourth unopened bottle beside the wall where Lin Mo could see it. All five were there. Nobody called them a water supply they could replenish.

He Cen drew the jacket over his shoulders. The black button tapped his glasses. He felt its dry half and finally stopped asking why they'd taken so long.

Lin Mo had meant to wait until the pain eased. Hearing the bottles touch the floor made him speak.

“I sent us the wrong way.”

Yu Mei stayed crouched at his feet.

“Mistook scenery framing for a load-bearing beam. The chair went through. They got me out. The whole route collapsed, and our bucket's still on the other side.”

He Cen looked at Fang Ji's dressing. Fresh red filled its center without reaching farther beyond the edges. He pushed the new bottle toward him.

“Who told you it was a beam?”

“Nobody. I got it wrong.”

He Cen turned to Fang Ji. “Will you believe him next time?”

Fang Ji's fingers paused on the bag's loose stitching.

“Depends what he says.”

Lin Mo watched a fine crack along the bed board and waited.

“Hands off. Don't pull the injured leg. I believe those. Says he can see through dust and the floor will hold? I'll look myself.”

“Same here,” He Cen said.

There was no gentleness in it, but Lin Mo could breathe. He didn't need them to rewrite the broken route as nobody's fault so he would feel better. It still hurt to hear them mark out this part of him they couldn't trust.

“That place had been hollow for ages,” the man by the door said.

Yu Mei looked up. “Did you see him go onto it?”

He shook his head.

“Then what's the use of telling us now?”

He fell silent. He Cen put on his glasses, looked at the cuts on the stranger's face, and slowly removed them again. Lin Mo didn't know which night he remembered. He wouldn't ask him to be forgiving.

Yu Mei put an old basin nearby and an upturned bucket beneath the man's injured leg, with rolled cloth under his foot. He had to support and move his own leg. When pain stopped him, she waited rather than forcing it. Once he was settled, she examined Fang Ji's hand.

The laundry had held four voices. Now a fifth person's breathing was louder than any of them.

Yu Mei took Lin Mo's cloth bag from the chair and asked whether he wanted it by his pillow or farther onto the bed.

“Inside. Opening toward me. Don't bend the photograph.”

Her hands paused before placing it as he asked.

“Your sister?”

“Yes.”

She didn't reach inside. After a while, he asked her to take out the photograph and hold it facing him. It had been protected between the notebook's pages; even the fall hadn't creased it. His sister still stood in ordinary daylight.

Yu Mei didn't ask whether she resembled him now.

“How old?”

He began giving the age he remembered, then stopped. The hospital outside said she had died a year ago. Bringing her food felt as close as yesterday. Both crowded his mind. He couldn't pick the easier version for someone else and let it erase the other.

“That year is how I remember her,” he said at last. “The records outside don't match.”

Yu Mei waited. He told her briefly about the changed patient in bed six and the death record he'd found afterward. She kept her fingers clear of the photograph's face. When he finished, she asked what had told him to come back inside.

“I found a record of her transfer. Followed it as far as the entrance. Nothing beyond that yet.”

“Then catch your breath before looking again. You nearly coughed on your water.”

He wanted to say his breathing was fine. A spasm caught his shoulder and back before he could. He closed his eyes through it. When he opened them, she was still holding the photograph.

“Put it back. You need a rest too.”

She laid it flat where it had been. He Cen didn't ask her to bring it over for him to inspect. He spread his jacket aside, clearing a dry place on his bed for her.

She finally sat, leaning against dry wall beyond the sink, a hand still beside the water. Fang Ji rolled back her wet cuff. She opened her eyes to say she could manage; he let go.

Listening to their quiet argument about that sleeve, Lin Mo stopped searching every sentence for directions. The red-lettered man had closed his eyes with a hand on his calf. Nobody had turned the photograph for him to see.

Overhead, water pooled in the canvas and dripped from its charred edge into the basin. The cloth had been taut when they left. Now its middle sagged, and a fresh wet line ran down the back wall.

“Is the leak worse?”

Yu Mei pushed up the shallow pocket to guide water into the basin. The strap rasped against its pipe.

“The pipe moved earlier,” He Cen said. “Thought that was you knocking.”

Fang Ji raised the phone toward the wall behind the sink. The elbow connected to the blue room's pipe trembled in little pulses. At each movement, water emerged from the crack in the wall.

Lin Mo remembered the wet print pulling at Fang Ji's shoe.

“Don't pour it down the sink.”

Yu Mei moved the nearly full basin onto the floor and put an empty bucket beneath the leak. Fang Ji shifted the drinking water farther onto the beds, away from the sinks, and left the maintenance door open wide enough for escape.

Holding his jacket, He Cen edged toward his pillow.

“Can we still sleep here?”

Nobody answered. Lin Mo's shoulder hadn't eased. His body was too heavy to want to move. Thinking up a place that might hold them wouldn't slow the water coming through the wall.

The man by the entrance raised a hand.

“Before I came here, there was a dry room behind the lower row of seats.”

Lin Mo turned toward him.

“I stayed there,” the man added. “I'm not guessing.”
