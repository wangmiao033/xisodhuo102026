# Chapter 142: The Seatkeeper Entered the Picture

Lin Mo stopped looking through the gap.

He asked Yu Mei to open the broad door while he watched the floor. Small stones remained embedded in its concrete surface; the wheels would not sink into them. She walked to the nearest black frame, inspected its back, and returned. There was room to stand, but no handrail, only vertical cable ducts.

“I'll stay by the door,” Fang Ji said.

He left the light bag of empty bottles beside the bench and stood slowly. He Cen kept his glasses on. Yu Mei told him they were going only through this doorway, close enough to answer a call.

“I'm awake,” he said. “Just tell me where you are.”

She pushed Lin Mo inside. His left foot remained on its support. The wheels crossed without jolting, but his right knee cramped because he had braced it in anticipation.

When he looked up, several frames lit simultaneously.

Every one contained his profile. Each Lin Mo turned in a different direction, searching for a hidden corner. The real man remained still.

Fang Ji swore softly. A frame by the hinge enlarged his mouth, with his bandaged palm crossing the bottom of the image.

The frames had rough black metal backs, not mirrors. Suspended halfway up the room, they staggered across the space, creating a winding route that passed every illuminated face.

“You can see more here than through the window,” Yu Mei said.

“It wants us looking.”

A low table stood at the far end. Shallow wheel marks approached its edge, and its shape resembled the transfer station in the diagram. Only a few meters separated them from it, filled with faces.

The red-lettered man appeared at the doorway. His right hand was empty now; the third water bottle remained with He Cen. As he dragged his injured leg across the threshold, the rightmost frame made a sharp sound.

Lin Mo's face disappeared from it. An empty seat took its place, lettering slowly illuminating the backrest.

[Seatkeeper]

The man stopped against the doorjamb. This time he did not touch the red fragments beside his own shoulder.

“They still have your seat,” Fang Ji said.

“Not the same one.”

A second line appeared.

[Unfinished viewing]

Below it, a narrow gray box held a tiny image. Someone knelt on the floor, arms shielding their face. Dark red lettering pressed across their shoulders. Lin Mo thought of Old Chen and the Original Materials Room.

No identifying number appeared. He could not see the person's face. The Seatkeeper, however, stepped closer, his shoe scraping loudly.

“You recognize it?” Lin Mo asked.

“I watched it.”

“Who?”

The man raised his hand without answering. Two thin black plates unfolded beneath the screen, one at palm height, one lower. The upper plate carried a single word.

[Continue]

“Put your hand down.”

“I only want a clearer look.”

Lin Mo clenched his left hand, felt pain spear through the old finger injury, and released it. He knew that phrase. People had wanted clearer looks before, then brighter lights, lower collars, a mirror pushed a few inches closer to his face.

“So do I,” he said. “That window was waiting for He Cen to fall so it could keep watching.”

The hand lowered, though the man stayed beside the frame. He said the broadcast had already started when he arrived. What had happened was already happening. He had never touched their bodies, never given the first order.

“What are you touching now?” Fang Ji asked.

A fine red line had extended from the fragments at the man's shoulder to the screen, hanging in the air like a cut. He backed away. It lengthened, and the empty seat moved forward inside the frame. A dark patch appeared on its cushion, shaped like the dressing on his calf.

From the platform, He Cen asked why nobody was talking.

“We're all here,” Yu Mei called. “Stay seated.”

Several frames lit to their left. He Cen's gray jacket filled one; another enlarged the sweaty strip of neck above its collar. Fang Ji stepped across the actual doorway. The displayed sweat kept running.

“Blocking him doesn't help.”

“Don't hold yourself like that,” Lin Mo said. Fang Ji's injured shoulder was creeping upward. “We only need to hear each other.”

Fang Ji leaned back against the wall. Yu Mei angled the chair away from the Continue plate, exposing the lower one beneath its dust.

[End local continuation]

A shallow slot ran beside it. The Seatkeeper saw it too.

“Will that erase the original?”

Lin Mo wanted to ask why he had not worried about what continuing might do. Instead, he read the wording. It referred to ending this man's continuation. A record strip remained alongside it. Nothing here described where the original segment was stored.

“It doesn't clear your record.”

“I didn't say that.”

“Then don't wait for me to press it for you.”

Yu Mei stayed behind the chair. The kneeling figure suddenly raised its head. Its features remained blurred, but its mouth moved distinctly, seeming to call a surname.

Lin Mo almost said Old Chen's name.

The name never left his mouth. As his own breathing quickened, a border illuminated around the gray box. The image expanded. He did not have to reach for it. Staring was enough to bring it gradually closer.

“Turn me around,” he said.

Yu Mei did. He faced a rough wall with half a white pebble embedded in its cement. If that really was Old Chen, could looking away cost him the last trace of the man's original name? The question kept returning until the pebble itself blurred.

Behind him, the Seatkeeper said, “I'm not continuing.”

A plate clicked.

Lin Mo waited until Yu Mei told him the red line had withdrawn before turning partway back. The empty chair had receded into the frame. The kneeling figure was gone.

[Local continuation ended]

The panel had not become blank. Several gray lines from the old request remained, along with the man's ID. He watched them with his right hand against his chest, as though he had just put down something hot.

“Can he go now?” He Cen called.

“This has stopped,” Lin Mo answered. “He still has to walk slowly on that leg.”

The Seatkeeper slid down the wall, sweating. After a while, he said he used to think it made no difference when he watched a segment that was already available.

“Once I left it running while I went for water.”

When he returned, everyone in the comments was laughing. He dragged the recording backward to see what he had missed. His gaze settled on their water bottle.

“Did you hear what was said?” Lin Mo asked.

“I don't remember.”

“You remember the laughter.”

The man did not deny it.

Lin Mo expected anger. Instead, something hollowed out beneath his ribs. He had forced so many words past his teeth, hoping someone would retain even half of them. Someone else's reason for replaying them had been to catch up with a joke. Afterward, the words themselves had not survived.

A red fragment trembled above the man's head. Lin Mo did not turn his forgetfulness into remorse on his behalf.

At the doorway, Fang Ji's dressing brushed the frame. He flinched away.

“There's a nail,” Lin Mo warned.

Fang Ji looked down and shifted half a step, finally letting his shoulder ease. He neither helped the man on the floor nor blocked his way out. Yu Mei started toward the table through the space they had made.

A frame beside the extinguished one lit up.

The same kneeling figure returned. Scrolling gray dots replaced the Seatkeeper's name above it. A second frame illuminated, then a third showed a lifted hem, as though other viewers were receiving the part he had declined to continue.

“I stopped,” the man said.

“We saw.”

Lin Mo did not make him press again. His own red line was gone. Ending it had not put the other illuminated spaces under his control. Whispers moved between the frames, with nobody visible to be told to shut up.

Yu Mei listened, then gently pushed one frame aside.

It rotated halfway on an axle, exposing its dull back. She had pulled no cable. The turn simply revealed more of the wheel track beneath it.

Lin Mo had been so busy watching what the screens contained that he had forgotten they occupied the actual route.

“They turn,” she said. “We don't have to watch the whole row.”

She rotated only the two obstructing the chair, keeping their edges clear of her forearm. The second caught halfway around. Rather than wrench it, she adjusted the chair's path. Fang Ji nudged a loose severed cable aside with his shoe.

No new door welcomed them. They crossed several meters of concrete and reached the table, its dusty edge fringed with scraps of paper.

Yu Mei set the brakes. Lin Mo checked the doorway. He Cen sat with the bottle between his legs, cleaning his glasses with his jacket. The Seatkeeper remained on the floor just inside. Fang Ji could see both sides from the doorframe.

Only then did Lin Mo examine the table.

A dark image still played beneath the upper glass: someone peering through a curtain. Yu Mei found an ordinary hinged catch at the side and lifted the glass frame. Another flat panel lay beneath it, with several rows of dusty keys.

[Transfer verification]

Lin Mo smelled warming dust. The words offered none of his sister's voice, but here at last was something they could begin without first making somebody cry.

Yu Mei wiped the lower slot with her sleeve. The torn edge caught on a projection. She stopped and used an intact portion instead.

Beside the slot, the label read source association.

“The front of the notebook,” Lin Mo said. “The entry from the basement.”

She secured the three technical pages at the back, preserving their closed fold, then turned to the earlier receiving record. One edge of Xiao Lu's photograph appeared. Half an eye in sunlight looked out at Lin Mo, and suddenly he wanted no screen to see it.

“Keep the photograph covered.”

Yu Mei flattened the old page over it, leaving only the complete source association exposed. The transcription had no missing characters. The linked forced-preservation record they had checked earlier remained beside it.

Lin Mo examined each segment. Even the separators seemed unfamiliar until he silently read them again. He had carried this precise chain of information so far that encountering somewhere to use it frightened him almost as much as finding nothing would have.

“Have you found your sister?” He Cen asked.

“A place to check the old transfer.”

“Then check. I'm here.”

Lin Mo lowered his eyes against an unreasonable sting. He had made He Cen wait several times already. This time the feverish man did not tell him to hurry. He merely moved the bottle farther along the bench, leaving somewhere for Yu Mei to sit when she returned.

The old panel slowly brightened. Its input field was empty.

Lin Mo did not enter the familiar nickname. Beginning with the first segment, he read Yu Mei the original association he had brought all the way here.
