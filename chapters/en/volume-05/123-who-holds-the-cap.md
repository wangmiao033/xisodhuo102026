# Chapter 123: Who Holds the Cap

Yu Mei took out a water bottle.

She looked at Lin Mo without opening it. The man on the other bed heard the plastic crackle and pushed himself upright, his blanket slipping from a faded shirt.

“Just a mouthful.”

“Lie down,” Yu Mei said.

“Then I'll choke and you'll shout at me.”

His voice was hoarse, but he was alert. Fang Ji pulled the blanket over his legs and introduced him as He Cen. He'd tried to fetch water himself that morning; Yu Mei had stopped him at the door.

Lin Mo looked at Fang Ji's right hand. Blood had reached his little finger. He was pressing his palm into his dirty trousers. The care pouch held clean dressings, but Lin Mo couldn't open them with his braced wrist.

“Rinse his hand first.”

Yu Mei glanced at the bottle.

“There's water up there.”

She meant the leak. Beneath the surface dust, the basin water looked fairly clear. Lin Mo followed the damp trail upward. It emerged from blackened filling inside the broken ceiling and ran over rusted reinforcement.

Fang Ji hid his hand.

“This doesn't need a whole bottle.”

“Nobody said a whole bottle.”

Yu Mei tightened her grip. She neither opened it nor gave it to him.

Drops struck the basin. Lin Mo lay on a bed they'd cleared for him, their salvaged canvas still wet nearby. One of the people who'd helped him was bleeding; another was watching his water. It had been easy to say the wound should come first. There was also a man waiting with a dry mouth.

“Let him drink from the other bottle,” Lin Mo said. “Don't pour it all out. We don't know when we'll find more.”

Yu Mei placed both bottles on the dry edge of a sink and opened one for He Cen.

Fang Ji went to pull up a chair. His palm hurt as he gripped it; he switched hands. Hearing his gasp, Yu Mei promptly took the bottle from He Cen's mouth.

“Slowly. What happened to one mouthful?”

He swallowed and reached for his chipped bowl. She kept the remaining water in the capped bottle, within his reach.

The other bottle was for Fang Ji's hand. Lin Mo asked him to stop pressing the wound into his trousers. Yu Mei found a dressing and supported his wrist over the sink. Water carried blood from his palm.

Then Fang Ji told her to stop.

A cracked drain opening lay below. Instead of draining, water gathered around its edge in a thin ring. A row of bulbs shone in its surface.

Lin Mo looked up. There were no bulbs overhead.

Yu Mei righted the bottle. The reflected lights came on in sequence. A muffled laugh rose through the drain, faint as a voice several rooms away.

“Don't pour any more down there,” He Cen said.

Yu Mei fetched a chipped enamel basin to catch the last of the rinse. Lin Mo watched until the reflected bulbs went out and the bloody water finally receded. He didn't ask anyone to probe the hole.

Fang Ji's palm was dressed at last. Yu Mei covered the part likely to touch things without wrapping his whole hand. He flexed his fingers. A small red spot appeared, then stopped spreading.

“Bring the bottle here,” Lin Mo said.

She held it to his lips for two sips before replacing the cap. Less than half remained. He watched her return it to the chair's pouch, without asking for the other bottle back.

“Leave that one here.”

Yu Mei's lips moved. All she said was, “I'll keep track of it.”

She turned to the rescued canvas. Its edges were blackened; hooks had torn long slits from two corners. Folding the burned parts inward, she tried to tie it to an overhead pipe. It wouldn't reach.

Fang Ji joined her. She immediately warned him about his dressed hand. He used his left to steady the pipe while she threaded the old strap through a hole. Eventually they stretched a small, sloping roof between two pipes.

Drops ran down to its lowest corner. Yu Mei moved the basin beneath it, leaving each bed a little dry space. When she stripped away a wet sheet, the padding underneath was damp too. She hung the sheet on a wooden frame away from the sinks.

Now Lin Mo understood why Fang Ji had insisted on saving the strap. They used everything to its last possible purpose, then found another.

Yet he'd almost said: I have water for you. Will you take me farther in?

Fortunately, he'd kept it to himself.

Fang Ji set Lin Mo's food on clean wrapping paper and pulled the package open at its notch, keeping his dressed hand away from it.

“Eat now, or after you sleep?”

“Now. The other portion's for you.”

Yu Mei didn't refuse. She divided it, giving some to He Cen and Fang Ji, keeping the smallest piece. He Cen broke off part of his and put it back.

“Don't wait till I'm asleep and give it to me again.”

She ate it without answering.

Halfway through his food, He Cen reminded Fang Ji about a coat hanging beside the blue door. If it was still there, bring it back. Fang Ji waved acknowledgment, his mouth full.

“That's what you said last time.”

“I didn't get there last time.”

“My lens cloth is in it.”

Yu Mei impatiently told him to eat. He persisted: gray coat, two pockets, one button replaced with black. A pair of glasses lay beside his bed, one arm missing, lenses tied together with string. Dust clouded the glass.

“Water comes first,” he added. “The coat if you have a hand free.”

He put the glasses beside his pillow, then reminded them to bring the coat itself, not just the cloth. Lin Mo nearly smiled. Fever hadn't reduced this man's life to a bed and a drink. He still had an old coat worth arguing over.

Lin Mo chewed slowly. The food stuck in his mouth. He struggled to swallow but didn't ask for water. Seeing his throat work, Fang Ji brought the bottle anyway, tipping it only slightly.

“Enough,” Lin Mo murmured.

“Take your time, young lady,” Yu Mei said absently, reaching to wipe crumbs from his chin.

He moved back a little and thanked her.

“Just call me Lin Mo.”

Her hand stopped.

He didn't hide his face. He knew what they saw. Explaining would mean recounting things they hadn't lived through, and he couldn't manage that much now. He repeated his name instead.

Yu Mei handed Fang Ji a tissue.

“There's still some on Lin Mo's chin. Help him.”

Fang Ji asked first. The rough paper tickled. Lin Mo closed his mouth and waited.

“If you faint from thirst, that's more work for us,” Fang Ji said, capping the bottle.

He Cen studied Lin Mo's wrist and asked if there were still doctors outside. When Lin Mo said yes, he touched his own forehead and turned away.

Lin Mo remembered the prescribed medicine in his care pouch. Each packet had its instructions. He didn't know how long He Cen had been feverish, or what else might have happened to anyone here. He didn't offer a pill on the strength of perhaps.

He asked Yu Mei to get his own scheduled dose, took it with her help, and had the packets put away. He Cen watched without asking for any. Loosening his collar, he asked whether Lin Mo had heard of others getting out.

Lin Mo told him about Wang Hai and Sun Ning: they'd crossed the door ahead of him; he'd lost contact afterward. He kept their names together with what he didn't know.

Fang Ji shook his head. Yu Mei hadn't met them either.

Neither name found anyone in the laundry room.

After a while, Lin Mo explained that his sister was called Xiao Lu. She'd last been transferred to Core Backup. He didn't say it as though everyone should know the place. Yu Mei paused over the cloth she was folding.

“I don't even know what's below this room.”

“Did someone tell you she was down here?” He Cen asked.

“No. A record gave her destination. I traced the acknowledgment to the entrance. I haven't been farther.”

“Then you'll have to take it a section at a time.”

“I know.”

He Cen grunted and lay back. Lin Mo could hear the doubt. There was no way to prove how far a man who couldn't get out of bed unaided would go.

Empty containers stood nearby: two with lids and a bucket patched across the bottom. Fang Ji nudged them clear of the walkway.

“Where do you get water?” Lin Mo asked.

Their stored supply had run out. The ceiling leak wasn't drinkable. He Cen had carried a bucket from beyond the listening corridor, Fang Ji said, but hadn't managed another trip.

The corridor lay across the sunken side of the hall, beyond the old orchestra pit. A walkway had led to the blue door. Following a large wall pipe brought you to the water room. He Cen described only where he'd actually fetched water; he hadn't investigated the stairs beyond.

Fang Ji wanted that route. Yu Mei didn't trust the floor. They'd already argued before Lin Mo arrived. That was why the empty buckets had been by the door.

“If we don't go today, we'll have to tomorrow,” Fang Ji said.

Yu Mei looked toward the bed. He Cen picked up the bottle and held it against himself.

“I've got this. Look at the route. You don't have to sit vigil.”

Lin Mo didn't immediately volunteer. He inspected his legs. His knee dressing showed no fresh dampness; the red mark on his left heel had faded. Lying down had stopped his back spasming. The shoulder still ached. Hope couldn't certify him fit to get up. He needed more rest.

Yu Mei took his phone from his jacket and put it by the pillow. Its flashlight worked; nobody would replenish the battery. With his left fingertips, he dimmed the screen and left the fruitless signal search alone.

“Once I've rested, I'll come. You look for water. I'll see how far the route goes.”

Fang Ji went to the wheelchair, tried its caster over a narrow floor seam, and set it down again.

“If it won't pass, we stop.”

“We stop.”

“Don't agree now, then ask me to try again.”

Lin Mo looked at the clean dressing on Fang Ji's palm and nodded.

Yu Mei left the remaining water between the beds and stretched out on a narrow bench by the door. Her eyes stayed on the leaking canvas. Fang Ji sat against the bucket rack, his injured hand flat on his knee.

Lin Mo closed his eyes. He expected to think about the door he could no longer see. Instead, Fang Ji's face returned, asking whether anyone outside still sold food.

He wanted to tell Xiao Lu about the breakfast shop. It opened when morning came. Knowing she couldn't hear, he saved the words and let his stiff back settle into the mattress.

When he opened his eyes again, Fang Ji had tied the empty bucket left by the door at his waist. Yu Mei tested the door beside the sinks. Its hinge squealed.

A second squeal answered, farther down the corridor.

He Cen raised his head.

“At the blue door, don't follow the knocking inside. Last time, I only took water from the outer room.”
