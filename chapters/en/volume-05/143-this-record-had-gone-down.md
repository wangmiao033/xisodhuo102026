# Chapter 143: This Record Had Gone Down

Halfway through entering the association, Yu Mei stopped.

One key had failed to rise, producing the same character twice. She lifted her hand, teased the edge of the key up with her nail, then deleted the duplicate.

That position contained only one character in the notebook. Recognizing the earlier portion had almost made Lin Mo say the entry was ready.

They checked the whole association again, including the separators. Yu Mei placed the linked forced-preservation record beside it and pressed the verification key.

Something clicked inside the table. Every black frame went dark.

Before Lin Mo could relax, someone nearby called him.

“Brother.”

The voice came from behind the table, low enough not to wake anyone. His left hand rose. Pain struck through his shoulder before the arm could straighten, whitening his vision.

Yu Mei caught the notebook rather than his arm.

“Lean back.”

He heard her but did not move. A pale collar appeared in the darkness, followed by a face. Xiao Lu sat low to the ground, looking up at him, her hair against her cheeks as though she had just woken.

He recognized the tilt of her head.

“Can you hear me?”

Her mouth moved. She told him not to hurry. The closeness of the voice blocked his throat. He remembered things that had impersonated her before; remembering did not make this face easy to refuse.

Yu Mei kept her hands on the handles without pushing closer.

He asked where she was.

“Brother, reach out your hand.”

A fine white line illuminated the table edge opposite his right wrist. His fingers tingled beneath the splint. He wanted to hide the hand beside his leg, then feared that withdrawing it would leave her behind again.

“My right hand doesn't work,” he said.

Her eyes never moved toward the splint. Her lashes trembled, and her head tilted once more.

“Brother, reach out your hand.”

The breathlessness within the sentence was identical.

He waited for a third repetition. A black speck beside her pale collar rose and fell along the same path. Dust captured in an old image, perhaps, or a flaw in the picture. He followed it until the familiar words emerged again.

“Stop it.”

Yu Mei found a broad button along the edge. “The one that says Return from Preview?”

He saw it then. He had been looking only at Xiao Lu.

“Yes.”

She pressed it. The face disappeared, leaving half a word behind in the darkness.

Lin Mo sat with his mouth open, unable to draw air, as though a long thread had been pulled out of his chest. Yu Mei did not tell him the whole thing must have been false. She returned the notebook to his lap and waited through his rapid breathing.

“Who was that?” He Cen called.

“A preview using her image.”

Lin Mo's voice changed on the word image. He did not know whether the small segment had come from the real Xiao Lu, whom she had been reaching toward, or how much had been removed before it was brought to him. During those three repetitions, however, she had never heard him say that his right hand could not move.

The Seatkeeper spoke from the doorway. “They used to show someone close to you first.”

“Why didn't you tell me?” Lin Mo snapped.

“I didn't know this station did it.”

“Tell us the rest of what you've seen, then.”

The man tried to rise against the wall and sat again. Some windows, he said, began with a segment that made people want to enter. Later they could extend what came before and after it. What he had seen had never been this clear.

Lin Mo's anger stayed, but the man genuinely seemed unable to point to a particular control.

“Loosen your hand,” Yu Mei said. “You're gripping the book.”

His injured finger ached when he released it. The paper had buckled beneath his grip. The photograph remained covered.

Suddenly he wanted to see it.

Not to compare eyebrows or the shape of her eyes. He wanted his sister to occupy a whole place, with ground beneath her feet and a wall behind her, where the person holding the camera knew what shoes she wore. Somewhere a single word had not been cut loose to wait for him to offer his body in return.

Yu Mei turned to the old page. His sister was not smiling broadly. Her eyes looked faintly impatient, as though whoever took the photograph had been slow about it.

The ache deepened. At least he could breathe.

“Cover it again.”

The screen returned to the verification field the preview had obscured. Complete match illuminated the first line. Two entries below corresponded to the notebook: the payment-source association and the linked forced-preservation marker.

Lin Mo read them from beginning to end. He did not start a new search under Xiao Lu's nickname.

The original record had reached this station.

A small downward arrow accompanied the last-transfer field. When Yu Mei opened it, the screen showed an image resembling an old receipt. One corner was missing, but the source association remained complete.

[Core Backup]

The receiving field bore those words.

Something inside Lin Mo touched solid ground again. This was neither the person beckoning from the preview nor an unnamed arrow in Old Chen's diagram. The association he had carried from the hospital and the basement appeared on a further arrival receipt.

Yu Mei lowered the phone's brightness and photographed the whole receipt, then the panel around it. His pale chin reflected in the glass, with the wrist splint at the edge. He did not ask her to crop them out.

“So she's there now?”

“This records arrival there.”

The word records felt cold beside his sister. Yet something cold could remain where it was instead of learning to nod just because he wanted an answer.

“We still have to find the original segment.”

The phone showed fifteen percent before Yu Mei put it away. There was only a receipt in the new photograph. Lin Mo would not reopen the preview to obtain something that looked more like a reunion.

A narrow field illuminated beside the table.

[Access route: Lower Convergence]

Its diagram led from the rear right of the transfer room, descending around a bend. The arrow stopped at a heavy line outside the box marked Core Backup. It did not enter the box.

Lin Mo looked toward the actual wall. Suspended frames concealed the opening, but a sill showed beneath them. The wheel marks turned there.

“A route name this time,” Yu Mei said.

He nodded. His heartbeat made his injured shoulder throb. He wanted her to push him straight toward it. But He Cen still sat outside, Fang Ji was still leaning by the door, and everyone else's thirst and exhaustion had continued during these minutes.

“Back first.”

“We found it, and we're going back?”

“To tell them how far we've got. You need water too.”

She studied her dusty hands and nodded.

As she began turning the chair, the frames lit again. Xiao Lu's half-face occupied the nearest one, her lips opening. Before any sound emerged, Lin Mo's own face replaced hers.

It showed him hearing that first word.

His chin was raised, his eyes full of something he had not had time to hide. The image stopped at the worst half-second and multiplied. One frame enlarged his eyes; another isolated the right hand he had wanted to raise.

Fang Ji swore from the doorway. Lin Mo did not ask Yu Mei to smash anything. He turned his face away and let her push.

At the first suspended frame, a voice seemed to stifle a laugh.

“Almost.”

It was not Xiao Lu.

Cold spread down his back. Yu Mei stopped, her hands clamped around the handles. He thought she had heard the voice too. Then he noticed what she was watching.

In a frame near the door, a woman gripped somebody's collar.

The face resembled Yu Mei's. Below it was He Cen's gray jacket. Someone beyond the image was saying to leave him; pushing him along would be faster.

“Don't watch,” Lin Mo said.

“I didn't pull him like that.”

“I know.”

“How? You weren't there when he got the fever.”

Her sharpness brought him up short. He could not testify to days he had never witnessed. The hand in the picture was still gripping the collar, its knuckles white. It was the hand she had just wrapped around the wheelchair handle, attached somewhere else.

“That mark between your fingers came from the keys,” he said. “You were beside me the whole time.”

She looked down at the fresh streak of gray between two fingers. Slowly she released the handles. Her forearm relaxed, while the displayed hand continued clutching.

She stepped back and called to the real He Cen. He had not seen the screen. He was adjusting his collar and asked if she wanted water.

Yu Mei's eyes reddened.

“Wait till I'm out.”

Lin Mo had been about to ask her to turn all the frames away. She had just escaped an invented action; now she still had to push him through a row of her own faces. He swallowed the request.

“Half a foot clear on the left. Same route as before.”

She resumed pushing. The wheels crossed the familiar embedded stones. She turned no more screens and did not quicken because of their shouting. In the last frame, she still clutched the jacket. Outside, the real He Cen held a water bottle toward her.

Lin Mo never located whoever had said almost. The frames had empty backs, and no visible cable led to a seat.

Yu Mei parked him beside the bench and sat. He Cen handed her the third bottle, then took his glasses cloth from his pocket and put it away again, unable to settle his hands.

Lin Mo told them what the receipt said, line by line. He did not play the preview or make He Cen comfort him for nearly reaching toward it.

“I want to check Lower Convergence. There's a route behind the room, on the right. We'll inspect the slope and floor first.”

“How long this time?” He Cen asked.

“Only as far as somewhere we can rest and still communicate. If it doesn't connect back here, I'll return.”

Fang Ji asked whether finding a passable route meant everyone would go. Lin Mo knew an answer solely about his sister would not do.

“I haven't seen an exit. If you stay here, we still need a way to keep what's inside away from you.”

A crisp cough sounded beneath the bench.

He Cen looked down. There were only the fixed iron supports. He lifted his jacket, revealing a light the size of a fingernail beneath the seat. A tiny He Cen occupied it, bending forward to look underneath.

Yu Mei found a loose black metal cover and pressed it back. The light vanished. Whispers shifted toward the platform's right side, like a crowd rising onto tiptoe to discover another opening.

He Cen looked up at Lin Mo.

“Find somewhere I can sit without being watched. Don't count this bench as rest.”
