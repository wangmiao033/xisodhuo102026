# Chapter 140: Someone Was Waiting Below

Water reached the spot where He Cen had kept his shoes.

He stopped trying to escape it by lifting his feet. Yu Mei draped the gray jacket over him and helped him shift to the other end of the bench, where the floor was still dry. Fang Ji brought the two sealed new bottles toward the back door, carrying them briefly with his left hand before setting them down to spare his shoulder.

The third bottle was partly used. Yu Mei tightened its cap and asked the red-lettered man whether he could carry it in his right hand while keeping his left against the wall. He tried and agreed. The two sealed liter bottles went into the old torn carry bag, secured inside the wheelchair's push handles. Fang Ji held a light bundle of flattened empties: two liter bottles and their two old small ones, wrapped in dry cloth.

“We'll sort it properly on dry ground,” he told Lin Mo. “Don't reach for it now.”

Lin Mo's left fingers had already risen. He lowered them and asked Yu Mei to check the papers at the back of the notebook. Old Chen's three copied sheets were secured, their inner fold untouched, a blank page separating the oily paper from his sister's photograph. He didn't take the whole thing out to count again.

He Cen's legs weakened when he stood.

Yu Mei steadied his forearm until he could lean on the wall. Fang Ji stepped aside instead of trying to catch him with two injured arms. The man with the red lettering eased his left leg forward from the doorway, sole whispering over the floor.

“Which way do you want to go?” Lin Mo asked.

The man glanced at the water, then the rear passage. “With you. To dry ground.”

“Wait at the back door first. Don't touch any handles.”

He said he understood. Lin Mo still explained: the first frame's crosspin stayed where it was; the next gate's bypass was already open; nobody touched the black palm hollow. The man's face darkened, as though receiving entrance instructions was uncomfortable for a former spectator. In the end he only asked whom he should wait for.

He Cen went first.

He wasn't testing the road. Yu Mei helped him the few meters to the iron gate and left him leaning against the wall before returning for Lin Mo. Fang Ji followed with the light bundle of empties, watching the floor's edge. The red-lettered man came last. When they gathered by the gate, they could still see a little water at the repair room's front door.

The load frame's crosspin hadn't moved.

Yu Mei stood inside while Fang Ji passed slowly through the male lane, the shoulder supports held overhead. He Cen followed in small steps. Whenever he looked up, he had to stop for breath. Lin Mo saw tendons stand out in his neck and told him to watch the solid ground ahead rather than every sound of iron above.

The man with the red lettering hesitated longest.

A line of light crossed his shoe. He put his right foot forward and withdrew it. Fang Ji didn't hurry him. Yu Mei pointed out the pin, still seated. At last he moved through the male lane with one hand on the wall. His left calf shook visibly. He sat as soon as he reached level ground.

He wasn't punished again. Neither did he become a different kind of person.

Lin Mo passed through the right lane. FEMALE POSITION still shone. Nobody teased him with the word or asked him to smile. When his shoulder left its white light, a little breath trapped in his chest finally escaped.

The wire door stood open to one side.

Yu Mei helped He Cen onto the flat patch beyond it, then brought the wheelchair over. The two white marks aligned. The black hollow was dark. Fang Ji looked at the strip of sleeve trapped in the edge without asking why she hadn't retrieved it.

Beyond the doorway, He Cen glanced back. The mesh obscured the repair room now; only a faint mark Fang Ji had left beside the wall was visible.

“Forget something?” Lin Mo asked.

He shook his head. “Just remembering where I came from.”

Fang Ji traced the route in the air, keeping his hand low. He showed He Cen the gate opening, then the railing beside them. It connected one stretch at a time. He didn't need to memorize the whole way to the hall at once.

Lin Mo didn't write that down. The places where people had actually stopped reminded him more clearly than a straight map line that retrace our steps wouldn't accomplish the return by itself.

As Yu Mei started to push, the carry bag brushed the foot of the door. She stopped, untied it, and secured it higher. A full bottle knocked dully against a handle. He Cen jumped at the sound, then managed a small laugh.

“Like collecting rubbish.”

“Sell it when we're out,” Fang Ji said.

His smile faltered. He didn't add a promise that they would. Yu Mei settled the two full bottles well clear of the axle. Fang Ji retrieved his bundle of empties from the wall. The opened bottle in the other man's hand was capped too. Nothing had been left for somebody to go back and find.

Yu Mei paused as she straightened. Lin Mo noticed her clenched jaw and suggested a rest. She accepted. Some stood, some leaned; He Cen sat again. An open door didn't oblige anyone to descend immediately.

Only when she said she was ready did Lin Mo look down the slope. A fine reflection followed the railing and broke at the turn.

The first stretch took a long time.

He Cen inched along the wall. Behind him, Yu Mei held the wheelchair with both hands. She couldn't also support his arm. Fang Ji and the red-lettered man waited where they could be seen above, moving down one at a time once the others reached the landing.

Lin Mo counted breaths, then stopped. He didn't want every slowing step to sound like time stolen from his sister. The feverish man knew nothing of that private urgency. He was searching for the next place to lean.

Halfway down, He Cen's hand reached a joint in the railing and jerked back. A metal edge scraped him without breaking skin. He didn't dare grip it again. He stopped on the incline. Behind him, the wheelchair stopped too. Nobody had two spare hands to move him.

“Palm against the wall first,” Lin Mo said. “Don't rush your feet.”

He Cen leaned his shoulder into the wall, each breath lifting the gray jacket. At last he edged half a step around the joint and took the smooth rail beyond it. He looked back and cursed whoever had fitted it. Quietly, but Lin Mo relaxed on hearing it. Cursing a particular thing was better than silently pitching downhill.

At the first bend, He Cen sat against the wall.

“Here. That's enough.”

Yu Mei braked. When the red-lettered man reached the turn, she took the opened bottle from him and gave He Cen a drink. Lin Mo didn't disguise the next bend as nearly there. They hadn't checked beyond it. He would wait here while Yu Mei looked. She rested until her hands stopped aching before going farther.

Shoes scraped beyond the next corner. Her voice followed.

“A platform. Somewhere to sit. No steps.”

Lin Mo couldn't see her. His chest tightened, and he asked how far. She came back into view and pointed along the railing. There was a metal joint to cross squarely with the casters, then a broad wooden bench fixed to the wall.

“Any doors?” Fang Ji asked.

“One wide one. Shut. I haven't touched it.”

They waited more than ten minutes at the first bend. He Cen's forehead stayed hot after drinking, but his breathing slowed. He said he could reach the bench. Yu Mei let him go ahead along the railing. Fang Ji followed, the empty bottles tucked lightly against his side, stopping when he stopped rather than trying to hold him up.

Once He Cen reached the bench, Fang Ji waited beside the joint, leaving space to bring the casters across squarely.

On the second slope, Lin Mo heard laughter.

Very faint. Many people breathing out together behind thick cloth. Yu Mei paused too and asked whether he'd heard. He had. He told her to square the wheels with the joint before looking up.

The metal strip stood slightly proud of the floor. She backed up a little, aligned the chair, and moved slowly. Each caster clicked over, followed by the larger wheels reaching flat ground. The jolt tightened Lin Mo's knee. His teeth caught the inside of his cheek; pain spread along his jaw.

The laughter behind the wide door became distinctly clearer.

He Cen's face changed on the fixed bench. Yu Mei didn't move the chair toward the door. She parked beside him and set the brakes. Fang Ji and the red-lettered man joined them. All five were on the same platform.

The ground was dry.

First Lin Mo saw the wet prints from He Cen's shoes, then everyone else's. They ended before the bench instead of being erased by water behind them. He finally dared let his heel ease out of its tense position.

Two thick pipes crossed the ceiling, dusty and not dripping. The bench was fixed to brick. Yu Mei pressed its supports and checked that its bolts remained before allowing He Cen to lean back. He clutched his jacket, watching the door, unwilling to close his eyes.

Lin Mo's phone had eighteen percent remaining.

The wheelchair's clip light had dimmed too. He asked Yu Mei to switch off the phone's flashlight. A weak overhead lamp gave enough light to see their feet. Once the phone was back in the bag with its screen dark, the laughter seemed nearer.

“Another recording?” Fang Ji asked.

Lin Mo studied the door. No exposed speaker. Its small window was black, but a dusty display sat beneath it. The laughter wasn't repeating the same ending. Wanting it quiet wasn't enough reason to declare it a recording.

“Don't open it yet.”

Yu Mei sat against the platform wall, hands flat on her knees. The little knot in her torn sleeve showed. Red pressure marks crossed her palms from gripping the handles. Lin Mo swallowed his request for her to light the display immediately and waited.

Fang Ji examined the outside without touching the lock. No water beneath the door; no broken hinge. The display was low enough to see while seated. He pulled a corner loose from the cloth around the empty bottles and, keeping his left hand low, wiped a line of narrow lettering clear.

[LOWER-LEVEL FRAGMENT TRANSFER]

Lin Mo asked him to take out the phone for a photograph. Fang Ji held it up briefly in his left hand, then lowered it and passed it to Yu Mei. She had rested. She photographed the whole panel and opened the notebook to the partial equipment diagram.

Its transfer box matched a short code above the display.

The printed diagram finally had a destination they could identify. They'd seen a sign beyond the load frame and come down a different lane. Here was the lower transfer point. The operating notes they thought Old Chen had left hadn't returned them to reality or promised his sister beyond this door. They had brought people unable to manage the steep slope this far.

Lin Mo brushed the paper's blank edge with a fingertip, remembering Old Chen beside the water counter, crossing out something he'd remembered incorrectly. He didn't call his name into the room. It caught in his throat, then settled back into the notebook.

A line below the display looked newer.

[RESIDUAL VIEWING REQUESTS]

A small number changed slowly. Lin Mo saw seventeen, then eighteen. Laughter rose behind the door, like people turning their faces in the same direction.

The red-lettered man recoiled. His left leg struck a brick corner, and he cried out.

The number jumped to nineteen.

Lin Mo looked at him. The man had seen it too. His lips paled; his hands clamped over his knee. No voting cards, no white scrolling comments. Just a few people crowded onto dry ground, with a panel apparently waiting for their next sound.

Fang Ji drew his foot away from the door. Yu Mei laid the phone facedown beside the notebook.

The number didn't fall.

Lin Mo stared at the black window. The laughter had sharpened when he'd bitten his cheek too. Two sounds couldn't explain the entire mechanism. But he already knew he couldn't bear anyone here praising fear as a good performance.

He Cen gripped the bench's edge.

“Are there people sitting in there?” he asked slowly.

Lin Mo didn't answer.

Deep in the black window, a small patch brightened, like an eye just opening.
