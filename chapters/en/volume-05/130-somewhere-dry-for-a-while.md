# Chapter 130: Somewhere Dry for a While

Lin Mo didn't lie down immediately.

Fang Ji cleared old lamp housings from the benches and pushed them together. A narrow seam remained. Yu Mei spread the patchwork covering, pressed down, and felt the legs rock.

“This end isn't level.”

Fang Ji crouched. Neither foot was cracked; a hard lump of cement raised one side. He shifted the benches half a foot and sat to test them. Wood creaked without wobbling.

Seeing both helpers wait for his agreement, Lin Mo suggested letting He Cen lie down first.

He Cen opened one eye against the wall.

“So you keep hurting in that chair till we come back to lift you?”

“You've got a fever.”

“I've got a mouth. I'll say when I can't sit.”

He spread his jacket beneath himself and found a position that didn't dig into his back. Insisting would only keep everyone standing over a bench.

Yu Mei locked the wheelchair and had Fang Ji move around to the leg support. They followed the transfer sequence they'd used before. Lin Mo pressed down only briefly through his left foot. When his back touched the covering, he discovered how hard he'd clenched his jaw. It ached when he tried opening his mouth.

“No questions about routes yet,” Yu Mei said.

“I haven't asked.”

“Your face has.”

He closed his eyes. Fang Ji's laugh sounded tired. Sitting beside the wheelchair, he removed the dirty outer cloth from his palm without pulling the dressing stuck in the center. Yu Mei checked the remaining care supplies. Enough to reinforce the edges. She secured it again and told him to stop bracing things with his palm.

The stranger needed a different position too. Dragging his left leg and bending for the bottle had strained the wound. He loosened the over-tight cloth strips himself. Seeing no further soaking through the dressing, Yu Mei told him to keep pressure there rather than uncover it repeatedly.

Lin Mo smelled dust and old wood. The repair room had no sinks, no laughter from drains. A broad overhead beam joined both walls intact. Gray light like sunshine fell through a high window onto empty cable reels.

It wasn't sunlight. There was no visible sky beyond the window, only the white face of another wall.

But nobody told him to look up and smile.

He woke once to He Cen asking whether messages had reached the phone. Fang Ji said there was no signal. Yu Mei said Lin Mo should see any messages first anyway. Silence returned.

When he woke again, the cloth beneath his cheek was warm. Four sealed new bottles stood together by the wall. The opened one sat between the benches, a little emptier. Everyone remained. Nobody had gone to press the seat while he slept.

Fang Ji reported that water still sounded outside but hadn't reached them. He Cen had drunk several times. He remained hot and grew dizzy after sitting too long. With Yu Mei's support, he'd moved along the wall to dry floor farther from the entrance. His jacket padded his back. He still refused to surrender his glasses.

More than three hours had passed on the phone.

Lin Mo asked Yu Mei to adjust his right leg. Sleep had put the pain far away; a small movement brought it all back. He didn't call this recovery. Fang Ji could still move his hand, he said, but didn't want to strain it.

“Then don't lift me yet.”

Yu Mei gave Lin Mo two mouthfuls of water and capped the bottle. Seeing her dry lips, he told her to drink. She rolled her eyes and told him to leave her mouth to her.

She drank anyway.

The stranger drew his hand from his knee, as though suppressing a request. Yu Mei asked whether he wanted some. He nodded, took a small drink, and put the bottle where everyone could see it.

Lin Mo still didn't want to look at his face. He could keep himself from watching the bottle.

Fang Ji found a flat reflector inside a cabinet door. Intending to inspect the high window, he turned and flashed light across Lin Mo's face. When Lin Mo squinted, he immediately turned it against his knees.

“Caught you?”

“Keep it out of my face.”

Fang Ji nodded. After a while, he asked the stranger why he'd recognized Lin Mo's injured wrist from their first meeting.

“I watched the broadcasts.”

“Were you like this inside too?” Fang Ji asked Lin Mo.

His eyes traveled from Lin Mo's face to his body, then away. Lin Mo didn't pretend he meant only the brace.

“Not when I arrived. They changed it, over and over. Getting out didn't give it back.”

Fang Ji looked up, finally seeming to understand why Yu Mei's young lady had been interrupted. He asked Lin Mo's former name.

“The same.”

After a silence, Fang Ji straightened the patchwork corner lying across his wrist so it wouldn't creep into the brace.

“All right here?”

“Yes.”

Near the pale scar, he stopped pushing it farther. Lin Mo watched his thick-knuckled hand and remembered his own old palm holding a pen. Writing a few words with his left hand was difficult now. He could still identify the injuries and forced changes responsible. He wouldn't hand over his past self to fit someone else's familiar form of address.

“I'm a man. You can use my name.”

Fang Ji acknowledged it without demanding an identity card.

The stranger looked up. Lin Mo didn't turn. He hadn't said this for the person who'd voted in red letters. That man didn't have to agree before the words could remain in the room.

Yu Mei took the reflector with its back toward Lin Mo and directed the high window's light into the cabinet. She'd heard everything. She asked only whether the black lump in the corner was cloth.

Fang Ji teased it apart. Tangled old wire.

“Thought we might find something softer for you.”

She stood the reflector against the wall, sharp corners turned inward. Lin Mo watched its covered bright face until Fang Ji sat again. He didn't explain further why he kept his name.

Rest had sharpened their hunger.

Fang Ji opened a low cupboard. Flattened shades, short wires, black dust. He lifted the top metal box to check beneath. A hardened sweet wrapper stuck to its base, the printing worn away.

“Even kept this.”

He Cen asked whether he'd found food. Fang Ji showed him. After a long look, he shook his head and told him to put it back.

Both portions Lin Mo had brought were eaten, shared on first reaching the laundry. He couldn't turn crumbs in wrappers into another meal. Knowing there were steamed buns outside wouldn't sustain anyone here for an extra day.

“Somewhere else people collected supplies?”

The stranger looked up. This time he didn't immediately repeat that they used to deliver to his seat.

“The passage behind us leads to the lower maintenance room. People brought equipment up from there.”

“You've been down?”

“No.”

“Food?”

“I don't know.”

Fang Ji closed the cupboard without swearing. He'd inspected only the dry rear passage, leaving its iron door untouched. That remained their possible next step. Lower room stirred something in Lin Mo's chest. Then he saw Yu Mei placing He Cen's water within reach.

He didn't put his sister behind that unknown door.

“Look at the door first. Decide when we can see.”

“You stay there,” Fang Ji said. “I'll look.”

Lin Mo opened his mouth to say he could look too. He paused, then asked Fang Ji to bring back the actual wording of any notice, and not test anything unclear on his behalf.

“Think I want to keep doing that?”

Fang Ji took the phone down the rear passage. The door was close enough for Lin Mo to hear his footsteps stop, a metal ring tap twice, and a hinge turn very slowly.

He didn't call anyone inside.

When he returned, he held a photograph before Lin Mo. A wide iron door stood open. Level ground split beyond it into two lanes. One had a shoulder-high crossbeam; the narrower one had a red-painted lever at its side. Farther on, a passage descended beyond the light's reach at the first bend.

“Taken from outside. Door wasn't locked. Half the writing catches the glare. Can't get all of it from outside.”

Lin Mo examined the low red lever, its upper end connected to an axle inside the wall. It resembled a pedal for moving equipment. He'd seen similar ordinary machinery. This time he withheld the conclusion.

“Chair fits?”

“As far as the door. Haven't checked inside.”

Enlarging the photograph revealed only male lane. Glare obscured what followed in the photograph; the doorframe hid its other end. Fang Ji had also seen a line beginning female lane. He wouldn't pronounce a route passable on half a sentence.

The stranger sat straighter.

“I saw them come back out of there.”

“Who?”

“People moving equipment. One man couldn't lift his arms. Another shouted at him for coming back.”

He began pointing at his own shoulder, then stopped. Lin Mo waited to hear whether he'd seen what happened inside. He shook his head. He'd been in his seat and heard one long noise.

Fang Ji put away the phone without treating that as a full set of rules.

When his back and shoulder eased, Lin Mo proposed going to the doorway. No descending the passage. Read what they could, come back if they couldn't. Stop before anything requiring him to leave his chair. He told both Fang Ji and Yu Mei.

“Where will you sleep afterward?” she asked.

“Here.”

“And if neither of you can manage the transfer, what do I do?”

He couldn't answer immediately. She was reserving enough help for a real task they would have to perform, not inventing an obstacle to his decision.

“Only to the doorway. Fang Ji doesn't touch the beam. Either of us starts hurting, we retreat.”

“I'll help you to the rear door,” she said. “Within calling distance. Nobody rounds a bend I can't see.”

He Cen drew his water closer and indicated the space beside him. He could reach it; nobody needed to watch every sip. The stranger adjusted his wounded leg and sat securely against the wall. He hadn't been appointed He Cen's keeper. They would simply remain in the same dry room, each with water and somewhere to sit.

The transfer still required two people. Fang Ji waited until Yu Mei supported Lin Mo's leg before helping him sit. Curiosity about a male lane didn't make his shoulder fit to brace his weight. Once back in the wheelchair, he closed his eyes. When he opened them, Fang Ji was still waiting.

“Now.”

The cloth bag returned beside the chair. Fang Ji had meant to leave it and save weight. Lin Mo needed the original records. At the door, he couldn't rely on someone else's memory to recognize a number. Fang Ji tucked the strap inside the armrest without searching the bag. The water remained behind. This short trip didn't need all five bottles.

The rear doorway was indeed close, only a few meters along a level passage. A raised voice carried easily into the room. From its entrance, Yu Mei could still see He Cen. Fang Ji brought Lin Mo to the iron door and kept both rear wheels outside the threshold on dry ground.

Two old shoulder pads hung beneath the beam, their leather worn into deep hollows. The narrow lane beside it had an ordinary support rail and no matching pads. Ahead, the lanes joined at a half-open mesh gate. The descent began beyond it.

Lin Mo looked up.

Male lane: Carry the load through.

Female lane: Return without a load.

Dust obscured part of the final line. Fang Ji nudged dry grit outside the door with his shoe, keeping his hands clear of the interior.

“Can you read it?”

“A few words missing.”

He angled the phone down. Shadows picked the dusty lettering out at last.

Once in position, do not release the load yourself.
