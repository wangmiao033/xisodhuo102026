# Chapter 50: The Stool Reserved for Her

“No,” Xiao Lu said.

Before Xu could explain, Old Chen raised his palm toward him.

“Nothing more to discuss about that.”

“She wouldn't actually be your child. Another child finishes the scene. Just the one word—”

“I understood.”

He didn't turn to Xiao Lu for an approving nod. He moved the bag from between his knees to his feet, leaving his hands free.

“I wanted that word before. Heard it and wanted another sentence. Preferably one that made what I'd done all right. Give me another person and I'll start asking her.”

Xu leaned back and asked about the remaining notebook.

“Not the whole book. Don't wait until she's gone to ask again.”

“If I hadn't come, would you have agreed?” Xiao Lu asked.

“Possibly. That's why I said he shouldn't wait.”

“Then don't tell me later you missed hearing your child because I wouldn't call you Dad.”

“I can't say that.”

She didn't smile to make it easier. Lin Mo remembered Old Chen seeing the torn notebook and asking why she hadn't caught it. If he wanted to remember this as saving somebody, he'd have to include her objection.

Xiao Lu relaxed her fingers on the script. She moved aside, leaving Old Chen a clear view of the box and the corridor.

Lin Mo expected him to get up. Instead, Old Chen rubbed his left knuckles with his right thumb. Lin Mo recognized the hesitation. Approaching that word in the recording, his own hand had rested on the button before he'd pressed it.

“Old Chen.”

“I know you're calling me.”

“Still want to hear?”

“His name. Afraid you'll all leave and I'll come back secretly.” He met Xiao Lu's eyes. “So I'm saying it while you're here.”

Xu lifted the half-closed lid. Only a little remained. Hearing it might tell them whether the original was worth finding. Lin Mo asked which words came from Old Chen's pages.

“I supplied the child's answers. The adult voice is what he performed then. Calls the child next. Listen for yourselves.”

“Play it,” Old Chen said.

A chair scraped. The old male voice scolded someone for breaking what he'd repaired. Said he'd deal with him when he got back. Footsteps followed, louder than the child had been. Chen, then a cough obscuring what came after.

Old Chen's chin moved forward.

Xu held his finger beside the switch, apparently waiting for a request to repeat it. Lin Mo knew he'd ask to hear something he'd missed. He took his left hand off the wood and pressed it against his knee, keeping it from reaching for the switch on Old Chen’s behalf.

“What follows?” Old Chen asked.

“The child says he'll remember.”

“You wrote that?”

“Yes.”

Old Chen closed his eyes briefly.

“Turn it off.”

“Almost finished.”

“Turn it off anyway.”

The footsteps stopped. Old Chen's hand still shook. He asked to close the lid himself. Xu moved the box closer.

His fingers paused at its edge. Lin Mo left them there. Eventually Old Chen pressed down until the catch clicked, then coughed, wincing as it pulled at his shoulder scrape.

Xiao Lu took out her cup and offered water. He shook his head. After the coughing passed, he said the sentence was still in his throat.

As Xu put the box away, Old Chen asked why he had to finish the scene to get its number.

“You wouldn't get it. We'd be continuing the scene. I could request the earlier material.”

“You used to say you were gathering everything.”

“You asked about your notebook. You wanted paper. What use would a number have been?”

“Let me try.”

Xu turned the box across his knees, exposing a narrow slot underneath. Sweat had faded the last section of the label.

CLOTHES MENDING FOUR
ORIGINAL WITHDRAWAL: RACK C, CODE SEVEN
RETURN TO ORIGINAL MATERIALS ROOM

Lin Mo leaned forward. Pain stabbed his left shoulder. He stopped rather than brace his injured right wrist against the ledge. Xu tilted the label farther out so he could read it.

“That's a room?”

“Handles old material. Ask with the number. Whether they let you look is their business. I received separated pieces. Never searched inside for yours.”

“But you said his name might be there.”

“Might. Didn't promise.”

Lin Mo wanted to keep the box. At least stop another lead from leaving. Old Chen only asked to copy the label. Xu said he didn't care. Lin Mo opened a blank page near the back of his booklet without removing any of the older inserts. Old Chen checked his copy and pointed out a missing stroke in one character.

“I can leave things out myself. Don't do it for me.”

Xiao Lu laughed softly. Old Chen smiled too, then looked back at the lid. He hadn't counted that single Chen as recovering his child's name.

Xu asked how much longer they meant to sit. He ought to take something usable back from this visit.

“Which part do you want?” Old Chen asked.

“Lived this long. Must know something people want to learn.”

“Wanted another listen. Closed the box myself. Will you write that?”

Xu glanced at his injured neck. Closing a box wasn't much beside a rescue. The girl holding the fabric off his face instead of catching the notebook: that was worth using.

“You asked me,” Old Chen interrupted. “Write about me. Ask her for her part.”

“I'm not finishing it for you,” Xiao Lu said. “Not doing it again either.”

Xu held his pencil without writing. Old Chen's face reddened. Lin Mo could see him approaching another place where a sentence might sway him. He'd come to ask who took his belongings; now he had to prove he still had something worth taking.

“Then go back empty-handed,” Old Chen said.

Xu pocketed the pencil. Old Chen's eyes followed it. Lin Mo suddenly understood that having nobody willing to record him frightened him too. After years of preserving himself, he still wanted someone besides paper to retain a sentence.

“I remember you closing it,” Lin Mo said. Before Old Chen could answer, he added, “Also remember you wanted to hear more. Both happened.”

“Write it down?”

“Not that part, not now. My hand needs a rest.”

Old Chen looked at his right wrist and nodded. Lin Mo was surprised himself: he could remember without instantly reaching for a pen to guarantee somebody's survival.

Xiao Lu rested her cup in her lap. Xu gathered the pencil shavings. The stool he'd pulled out for her remained in front of them, and nobody filled it.

A man in gray protective sleeves called to Xu from a low cart loaded with slotted racks. He asked for the sound box without looking at their faces.

Xu handed it over.

“Couldn't make it work. No continuation.”

The man slotted it into a rack and fastened the catch. Old Chen's hand lifted an inch from his knee, then settled. They didn't get to keep the box. Xu hadn't become friendlier because they'd refused.

“Where's the Original Materials Room?” Old Chen asked the collector.

“I'm not returning there. Outgoing material comes from that way. Collection goes elsewhere.”

He indicated a narrow passage beyond the water station, gray fabric hanging at its entrance. Someone was pushing an empty rack out. Not Jiang Jian's window, nor through the dining room.

Old Chen half rose. Lin Mo stopped him.

“We said no following carts.”

“Not following. Finding out where to stand.”

He sat and asked if they could reach it from the waiting area. Along the outer corridor, the man said; ask at the far desk with the withdrawal number. See what happened from there. He promised no access to an inner room and pushed his cart the other way.

Lin Mo watched people moving past the gray curtain. A particular place at last, one they could find without following calls of Dad. His legs ached. Xiao Lu still held her cup. Old Chen had just let the voice leave.

“Back first,” Lin Mo said. “Tell them what we found. Then return.”

After studying the empty space where the box had been, Old Chen agreed.

Xu stopped Xiao Lu. He produced a thin recording plate. With no continuation, he could return the cup movement along with the yellow bowl so the next person didn't use it to recruit her. Two small images showed the wrapped cup rim and the bowl.

“Don't need your script. Just asking if this is the movement.”

She didn't take it. Pointing to the first image, she asked whether it included her letting go.

“Ends with the turn.”

“I let go afterward. He drank by himself.”

Xu paused. He inserted the plate into a low stand beside the water station and turned a switch. The scratched black screen lit with her hand moving wet cloth. No sound. Then the edge of a yellow bowl.

For the first time, Lin Mo saw Jiang Jian's account for himself. Old Chen's neck injury flashed in a corner. Even the moment Xiao Lu's thumb stopped in empty air remained. The clip didn't wait for Old Chen to hold his cup steady.

Xu tried moving it forward. It always ended there.

“Arrived like that.”

“Mark it declined,” Xiao Lu said. “Not waiting for her to come back.”

He attached a strip marked NO CONTINUATION and placed the recording in the returns slot. She didn't mistake that for all the footage being withdrawn. She asked where the original came from. Xu turned it over to show the source field.

The Original Materials Room again.

Lin Mo wrote the cup clip's reference separately beneath Clothes Mending Four. Two different pieces had passed through the same place. This time they had two objects, not two people remembering a table.

Xu left carrying the remaining rack. They stayed. Tightening her cup lid, Xiao Lu gave a brief laugh and pressed her lips together.

“That sentence from when we ate. I've got it.”

Lin Mo didn't lift his pen.

“What?”

“Swallow before you yell at me.”

His booklet knocked against his knee. Once, his sister had saved half a bowl of noodles. He'd eaten hungrily while asking why she hadn't eaten her own before they cooled. Chin in hand, she'd said those words.

Xiao Lu noticed.

“You've heard it too?”

“Yes. I remember it.”

“Don't tell me yet.”

He closed the booklet. She'd felt a little angry, she said, and amused, impatient with waiting for the other person to finish. No yellow bowl. She couldn't see clearly who held the chopsticks.

“That's all I want to keep for now.”

“All right.”

She put her cup back in the bag before writing in her own script. He didn't look at the words. His chest felt warm, as though she'd just scolded him. He didn't ask about noodles or decide whom her irritation belonged to.

Old Chen stood on the other side of the wood with his damaged bag. He hadn't heard the whole name, he said. He'd probably keep thinking about it when they returned.

“Then say you still want it,” Xiao Lu told him.

“Can't make you sit watching me forever.”

“Good that you know.”

Lin Mo rose slowly. Xiao Lu made room for him to plant his feet; Old Chen steadied his left elbow only when asked. At the trough's far end, another rack emerged through the gray curtain and turned into the corridor.

Old Chen didn't turn with it.

“Always made me wait.”

“For what?” Lin Mo asked.

“Gathering the pages. Next performance. Child finishing his sentence.” He secured the bag and headed toward Sun Ning with them. “Their turn. I'll eat, rest, then come and ask.”

Behind them, the cart brushed the curtain aside. Through the gap, Lin Mo saw an enlarged image hanging on the wall.

Xiao Lu was handing him a water bottle.

He still held the bread paper. Half of Sun Ning's chair showed beside him.

The place where they'd just eaten.
