# Chapter 56: A Hundred Thousand, All at Once

As the guard lifted, a fine spring slipped behind its pivot.

Lin Mo heard it strike inside the partition. Before he could let go, the edge caught his left sleeve. The whole strip jerked inward, dragging his forearm an inch forward.

Pain shot down from his shoulder. He braced his feet and instinctively grabbed the sleeve with his right hand. Turning the injured wrist made his fingers spring open. His booklet slipped between his legs; he caught it with his knees before it fell beneath the iron.

“Stopped?” Old Chen asked.

“My sleeve's caught.”

He couldn't pull hard. His skin lay beneath the edge. The thin gray fabric stretched tight; his whole left arm was going numb. Old Chen turned slightly. The black shutter descended, pressing against the back of Lin Mo's hand and making it shudder.

“Don't turn!”

Old Chen straightened. The shutter stopped, without rising fully.

Shao hurried from the track, peered beneath the beam and reached for the outside reset handle. Lin Mo saw only her work sleeve.

“Pull your hand out so I can retract it.”

“Can't.”

“Lower it toward you. Don't pull up.”

He tried. Some fabric slipped free, but a notch tore the worn sleeve, raising gray threads. Bare metal scraped his forearm. A faint red streak marked the white guard. He swallowed his breath. Recoiling to shout would wrench his shoulder again.

Shao released the reset handle and told Old Chen to hold his face steady while she removed the side drive catch. How long? She didn't answer. She dragged her ladder behind the cabinet, her shoes clanging against the track.

Braced in that sliver of space, Lin Mo finally saw the light behind the iron.

The guard he'd tilted had jammed its sloping edge in another groove, exposing a narrow strip of the maintenance side. The uppermost words were half hidden. Below, he could clearly read CORE VERIFICATION and GENERAL PERFORMER DEPARTURE CONDITIONS. A separate box said QUERY SUBJECT: CURRENT SOURCE OWNER. Material return and a person's departure had separate fields.

“Old Chen,” he whispered. “I can see.”

“See what? Tell me.”

He leaned forward. His shoulder hauled him back. Only four digits showed in the light, the rest covered by the guard. He wouldn't guess from a few zeros. Bending his knees, he edged his feet forward half a palm's length and lowered his body.

The whole line emerged.

DEPARTURE CRITERION: DURING THIS LIVESTREAM, 100,000 ELIGIBLE VIEWERS MUST SIMULTANEOUSLY MAINTAIN THEIR SELECTION OF FREEDOM.

His mouth stayed open. He almost forgot to breathe.

A hundred thousand.

He'd imagined an exit behind a door, a key in somebody's hand, or enough existence value to buy his way out. Now, faced with the words, he thought first of the white comments that couldn't fill a corner of the small screen outside.

“How many?”

“A hundred thousand.”

“Existence value?”

“People. A hundred thousand eligible viewers.”

Silence, then Old Chen's muffled cough. He kept facing forward this time, face flushed. Lin Mo couldn't see his hands, only the mouth in the glass, its corners tightening and slowly easing.

More text remained below. He lifted the booklet with his right hand. His pen rolled from the pages, struck the bench and fell beside his foot, beyond reach.

“Read first,” Old Chen said. “Read it to me.”

Lin Mo followed the maintenance display word by word.

ONLY IDENTITIES CURRENTLY PRESENT AND QUALIFIED TO VOTE AS VIEWERS COUNT. LEAVING THIS SESSION OR WITHDRAWING THE SELECTION REDUCES THE COUNT. REPEATED SELECTIONS DO NOT ADD TO IT. COUNTS DO NOT CARRY BETWEEN SESSIONS.

Currently pressed close against the guard. He changed his angle to make sure he'd missed nothing, then repeated the line, stressing currently and reduces.

“One leaves, one vote goes. Can't have a group vote and leave, then bring in another.”

Did everybody have to agree? He searched farther. Only after finding UNANIMITY NOT REQUIRED in the next box did he answer: no, but a hundred thousand had to remain present, all still choosing freedom at the same time.

Saying it made the difficulty real. No list of a hundred thousand names, no tally that rose forever. Any number could come to hear people suffer. Without that choice, their presence was useless.

A screw fell behind the cabinet. Shao swore and told them not to lift the guard again. The trapped spring end had bent; prying upward would jam the inner gate too.

Lin Mo shifted his weight onto his feet and gently withdrew his arm. A little more sleeve came free. Scraped skin pressed against the metal, blackening his vision with pain. He waited, eyes still on the words.

“Can we vote too?” Old Chen asked.

Lin Mo remembered the accompanying-participant seats. Wang Hai and Sun Ning could watch and speak there, but weren't ordinary viewers. He searched the display for eligible identities. At the bottom:

PERFORMER AND ACCOMPANYING-PARTICIPANT IDENTITIES DO NOT COUNT TOWARD VIEWER VOTES. VERIFICATION APPLIES ONLY TO THIS SESSION'S DEPARTURE APPLICANT.

“We don't count now. Neither does Xiao Lu.”

He repeated the last sentence to Old Chen. Even reaching the number wouldn't, according to anything here, release everybody in the theater. He couldn't take it back as a promise that one victory would settle everyone's account.

A small line underneath gave the application route.

APPLICATION: START BROADCAST PAGE / DEPARTURE APPLICATION FOR THIS SESSION.

His pulse moved visibly beneath his wrist. He didn't touch the display; he couldn't reach it. The maintenance side was marked read-only. The comparison still concerned Old Chen's single source number. Beside it: departure application not established.

“Have to start a broadcast.”

“Your own?”

“They verify whoever applies. This hasn't opened one for me.”

He pushed the booklet upward. His jacket couldn't support it; the spine slid again. He nudged the pen closer with his socked toe and reached down. His hip pressed the bench corner so painfully his fingers curled. He stopped, leaving the pen beside his foot.

“Get the number down first.”

“Just a hundred thousand gives the wrong idea.”

At last he caught the pen and laid the booklet sideways on the exposed corner of the bench. His left arm couldn't move; his right wrist wouldn't turn. He angled the paper against his knee and wrote same session, simultaneously, then a hundred thousand. One cramped word resembled another. He rewrote it underneath rather than leave himself guessing later.

He wrote FREEDOM largest. After it he squeezed: leaving reduces; withdrawing reduces; no carryover to next session. At viewer eligibility, his pen punctured the thin paper. He stopped. Pain couldn't be an excuse to merge two different identities.

“Performers don't count,” he said as he wrote. “Accompanying participants don't count.”

Old Chen repeated it quietly. The words gave neither of them a vote.

Lin Mo lifted the page and checked each point against the display. General conditions. A hundred thousand. Same session, still present. He'd omitted the lack of a unanimity requirement; he added not everyone required before lowering the booklet. His notes weren't complete, but he knew what he'd preserved. He hadn't invented a comforting set of rules around one number.

“Show Wang Hai when we're back.”

“Yes.” Only then did Lin Mo hear the return he'd assumed. Old Chen remained in the glass, leaning slightly against the rail beside the footprints for support.

“Your feet okay?”

“Numb.”

“Should I stop?”

“Get that hand out first. I'd like to sit down and hear about it.”

Old Chen inhaled as though to laugh. A hoarse breath emerged. All these years, he'd worried about finding enough pieces to complete the notebook's first half. Now, for the first time, he knew there was a much larger number to reach.

Before Lin Mo could answer, the screen above the entrance turned red.

It faced outward. He saw only a reflected flash in the glass. Old Chen could read it clearly. His lips moved, but he didn't immediately speak.

“What?”

“Get your arm out first.”

“Read it.”

Shao reached from behind the cabinet and laid a loosened catch on the iron. Only the source-owner match now held the inner gate up. If Old Chen left position, it would fall. The reader had to withdraw first. She didn't suggest they could brace it with a body.

Old Chen asked if they could withdraw once he'd read the line. Shao didn't answer. He asked again.

This time, Lin Mo heard the screen's alert.

READING LIMIT EXCEEDED. ASSISTANCE IN BREACH REGISTERED AGAINST CURRENT SOURCE OWNER.

Red words followed. Lin Mo could make out only the reversed name. Old Chen read the rest.

“Seatkeeper says, So you're finding someone else a way out.”

Lin Mo understood. Each line he'd read had prompted Old Chen to ask or repeat something. The face being sent to the continuation had a mouth. The pickup light beneath the glass flashed with Old Chen's voice. A hundred thousand. No accumulation. He'd spoken all of it.

Another alert.

CLAIM AGAINST SOURCE OWNER: REQUESTING VIEWER SEEKS PHYSICAL SEVERANCE OF ORIGINAL OWNER'S BODY. SCOPE: CURRENTLY MATCHED SOURCE OWNER.

Old Chen read it correctly. He didn't leave out physical.

White comments squeezed beneath the red border. Why keep this person when the carry-test participant had left? Bring the source owner back to the empty table; don't stop the story here. No poll appeared. Shao watched the current-requester field.

“This claim is against you,” she told Old Chen. “It doesn't wait for their votes.”

Lin Mo's back met cold iron, compressing his injured shoulder. He turned his head toward the white handle, but his body still faced the maintenance side. He had to free his left arm before he could turn.

“Leave first.”

Old Chen stayed. The lowered shutter hung above Lin Mo's hand, its edge still touching the cloth.

“It'll come down.”

“I know. You—”

“I don't know what it'll do to you. Don't tell me protection makes it safe.”

Lin Mo stopped. His protection remained; he knew that. It hadn't prevented the white scar on his wrist or given his shoulder another inch.

Shao approached Old Chen, hand raised as if to push him out of alignment. He lifted his elbow to block her and immediately recoiled in pain. Unable to push her away, he could only repeat: wait until the person inside was out.

“Keep standing there and recall can lock onto this number.”

“I can see.”

His voice shook. His jaw was so tight. Nothing about him looked like a man certain of every consequence.

“I don't want to stay. I want to leave right now.”

A small noise beside his feet. Lin Mo thought he'd moved and ducked to protect his arm. The shutter stayed still. Old Chen had only touched the small shelf with his toe, making his tag jump.

“Hurry.”

Lin Mo nodded, eyes burning and blurred. He pushed the booklet inside his jacket, then pressed his right palm against the trapped sleeve. He couldn't twist his wrist. With the heel of his hand, he eased the cloth outward.

Gray fabric slid from beneath the edge, pulling at the scrape. He clenched his teeth without yanking. Outside, Shao held the loosened drive catch steady. The sloping guard stopped trembling. His left hand finally cleared the narrowest point, leaving two lines of blood along his forearm.

Half the inner light went dark.

He quickly repeated the conditions: a hundred thousand, same session, currently eligible, still choosing freedom. Old Chen echoed a hundred thousand, making no promise of perfect recall.

The handle remained behind him. Lin Mo moved his feet toward the bench and turned, half his body scraping the partition as he bent his painful knee. His pen was clipped into the new notes. He held his jacket closed with his right hand to keep both from falling.

“The paper?”

“On me.”

“Then get out.”

His fingers found the white handle. Before he could press, two narrow grilles dropped outside. Their sound was lower than the shutter's: one behind Old Chen's feet, another across the space between his position and the door.

Old Chen remained whole in the glass. He looked down, then lifted his face before the matching light slipped away. His mouth trembled too badly to finish a sentence.

CURRENTLY MATCHED SOURCE OWNER LOCKED.

Lin Mo forced the white handle down. The narrow door slowly retracted. At last he could see his wood outside, leaning against the wall with the carved end upward, only two steps from Old Chen.

Between those steps, an isolation grille had fallen all the way to the floor.
