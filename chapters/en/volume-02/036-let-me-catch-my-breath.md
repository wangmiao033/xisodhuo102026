# Chapter 36: Let Me Catch My Breath

Lin Mo didn't bend his finger.

On the left, the narrow window. His sweat still marked the metal beneath it. The black glass on the right had no frame; Wang Hai's face had appeared only when it lit. He knew which one had a man behind it.

But both hands moved again.

Wang Hai lowered his finger and pressed his palm downward. The other man did the same, wood brushing his clothes, broken end pointing the same way.

Lin Mo's eyes drifted right. That glass was brighter. No frame obscured Wang Hai's creases; he could see his lips clearly.

He was already waiting for that one to tell him what to do.

He turned back to the window and stepped closer, resting his left shoulder against the metal. Pain caught in the altered joint. He'd misjudged its width again. He used the pain to stop, keeping away from the brighter Wang Hai.

Short lines extended from both floor frames to his socked toes.

[ENTER THE CORRESPONDING FRAME TO CONFIRM YOURSELF.]

Recorded material began playing at the stage front. His left palm stood upright before drawing back to his abdomen. They hadn't erased the beginning.

He edged forward to see it better. A fine line lit at his feet. He withdrew his foot; the light retreated. Before he'd entered, his outline appeared inside, old shoulders filling the jacket as though he'd been waiting there all along.

[TIME REMAINING: 01:46.]

In the live frame, the figure also wore gray, but its hands hung down and its shoulder seams sat lower. Its head turned with his. He stepped left. It followed; the recorded hand stayed against its abdomen.

Knowing which was new didn't make his feet choose correctly. The recorded frame was nearer the door. Those familiar shoulders made him want to put himself back inside them.

Outside, Wang Hai pointed at his feet.

Lin Mo started lifting his left foot toward the live frame. Then Wang Hai pointed at the metal. He'd meant find support first.

Lin Mo put his foot down without pretending he'd understood.

The other Wang Hai also pointed at his feet, but the next movement never came. Its hand paused, then rose to shoulder height. The real Wang Hai's was already at his waist.

Lin Mo breathed against the window. Mist spread across half of Wang Hai's collar. He wrote nothing in it, waiting for it to recede and the man outside to respond.

Wang Hai bent and breathed against the same spot.

Two layers of mist. Now neither could see.

Lin Mo hesitated, then moved his face sideways. Wang Hai moved the same way. They met again across the glass. His mouth opened. Lin Mo heard nothing, but the expression hardly suggested praise.

A little breath of laughter escaped him, whitening another patch.

Wang Hai closed his mouth and waited.

Lin Mo stepped back, exhaled slowly, then pointed to clean glass. He waited instead of breathing on it at once. This time Wang Hai stayed back too.

The man in the black glass had already bent, beginning a breath that hadn't happened yet. Mist showed there: the previous breath, its outline across the collar unchanged.

Lin Mo glanced over without trusting that delay to work next time. At his fingertip, he breathed once, briefly, then stopped.

Wang Hai waited for it to clear before answering lower down. At last they left each other's faces visible.

Lin Mo pointed to him, then to the live frame. Wang Hai shook his head and raised his palm.

Lin Mo stopped.

Wang Hai had told him: no gesture meant it was safe inside. Again he'd tried to make a response into permission nobody had given.

He relaxed his finger, brought his left palm to his chest, and slowly pressed downward. Wang Hai didn't finish it for him. He lowered his hand and stayed to watch.

A new proposal appeared.

[FEMINIZING ADAPTATION: RIBCAGE, WAIST AND BACK. MATCHING DEMONSTRATOR MOVEMENT MEMORIES.]
[NAME AND SEGMENT DURATION UNCHANGED. IMPLEMENTATION AT 60% APPROVAL.]

The recorded shoulders stayed as they were. A new white outline appeared alongside, waist and back drawing inward, fabric lifting slightly over its chest. They placed that body inside the live frame, leaving him a space to stand.

His right wrist pressed against his jacket. He could keep his feet outside the line. He couldn't push the proposal away.

[Can't he choose himself?]
[The old one looks more like him.]
[The new one fits. Just add the movements.]
[Don't choose for him. Show both in full first.]

Approval climbed from fifty-four. He watched the recorded palm rise, withdraw, settle against his abdomen. Then the sequence became a beckoning hand. Nothing labeled the opening refusal. Nothing in the ending had become consent.

Watching Xiao Lu smile onstage, he'd wanted to decide which version was really her. Now it was his face. He'd made every movement himself; spliced together, they could lead a stranger inside.

He raised his left palm toward the audience and held it. His shoulder quickly ached. Instead of drawing his hand inward, he bent his knees, letting it descend with his body, palm still outward.

One comment asked whether he felt unwell again. Another said not to rush him. They hadn't read the same meaning into it. Approval held at fifty-eight.

He crouched too low. His bruised knee hurt. He laid his left forearm against the frame to rise, but his waist and back tightened first.

Sixty.

The thin execution light passed around the frames, regardless of where his feet stood.

His breath caught halfway in. His ribs drew inward; the support behind them shifted lower. Where he'd once held himself upright, there was suddenly nothing. He unclenched his teeth and breathed out in stages, abandoning the effort to force his chest to its old width.

Beneath his jacket, the blue garment settled against his altered chest. His clothes hadn't changed. The broad belt's knot remained, but a little slack had appeared beside it. He left it alone and planted his heels.

His left arm shook. The heel of his palm slid down the metal. He stopped halfway into a crouch, keeping the full weight off his injured wrist, and eased his back toward the wall.

Wang Hai had turned toward the door. Lin Mo didn't breathe against the glass to summon him back.

They'd agreed: if he couldn't hold himself up, Wang Hai could find the door.

A narrow corridor appeared in his mind, one he'd walked countless times. He seemed to know to leave his badge facedown at shift change. If someone grew impatient outside the window, he should bend until his face fitted the little frame. He even remembered smiling as he taught a newcomer not to breathe on the camera.

The newcomer's face was blank. The instructing hand was his.

He almost bent to match. His fingertips were about to tap the metal twice when they touched moisture that hadn't dried.

Wang Hai had waited for him. There'd been no newcomer.

He pressed the damp spot. The familiarity wouldn't leave, but he kept his hand still.

Thirty-six seconds.

He drew a little breath, made sure he could release it, then tried again. The touch of cloth against his chest was unfamiliar. He glanced down without shielding the camera or arranging his collar to match the outline.

Why had he come in again?

To see how they matched movements to a person.

His old shoulders turned toward the exit. Inside the live frame he still crouched. He could leave both unselected. He owed the audience no gracefully posed answer.

He edged his left hand higher along the metal, moved his heels forward, and waited for his knees to support him before leaving the wall. Rising took longer now. His jacket brushed his calves. His body wanted to settle its weight into the new curve of his waist.

He spread his feet farther apart.

Wang Hai returned, right hand on the door handle, making no gestures. Lin Mo didn't ask him to confirm anything. He approached the live frame.

At its edge, the figure lurched as he did, just as ungainly. He stopped, left palm against his chest. Until his breath caught up, he wouldn't lift the other foot.

Twenty-one seconds.

At last both socked toes were inside.

[LIVE: CONFIRMED AS SELF.]

The recorded frame remained unoccupied. The beckoning hand stopped. Small text appeared beside it.

[RECORDED ACTION: UNCLAIMED.]

He stared at UNCLAIMED. He didn't touch the second frame or mistake the word for nonexistent. The footage remained. This time, at least, the two hadn't been combined into one answer from him.

He lowered his hands and waited for the door.

In the last ten seconds, the route to shift change lit in his mind again. The rear door was behind him, yet he turned half a step the other way, as though waiting for his replacement.

Outside, Wang Hai touched the chair leg to the doorframe.

Lin Mo couldn't hear it. He saw the crooked carving, stopped, and turned toward the real door.

When the light went out, Wang Hai pushed it open.

"Don't ask yet." Lin Mo gripped the wood. "Let me catch my breath."

Wang Hai didn't pull. He stepped back to let him cross slowly, then braced the wood once both socked feet were in the corridor.

Lin Mo finished exhaling.

"I'm out."

"I see."

"That movement wasn't asking you back to the window."

"I know. Couldn't open the door before time was up. Went back to see if you were still on your feet."

Lin Mo nodded without claiming Wang Hai's return as obedience. At the counter, the woman pushed the new badge halfway out. His name was unchanged. On the back, separate live and recorded fields; only the first was marked.

"Can I take this one?"

"Completed review badge. Yours."

He found the last dot of his own handwriting, then took it by the edge, exchanging nothing from his pockets.

"What about that?" Wang Hai asked.

The small screen still beckoned.

The woman gave no answer. Without smiling, Lin Mo turned the badge for Wang Hai, pointing at the recorded field.

"If anyone says I claimed that gesture, ask them to show me this side too."

Wang Hai examined it before looking away.

"You'll have to remember to turn it over."

Lin Mo slipped it into a blank booklet page, away from the pocket holding the fragment. Inside the waiting room, He Jing was still reading her badge. At their footsteps, she tucked her feet back.

"Figure it out?"

"I made the movements. Didn't accept what they made them say."

"Still leading people in?"

"No."

This time she nodded without checking the screen.

They retraced their route. On the second stool, Xiao Lu looked up, saw his hand on the wood, and drew her script closer. He only said they were going to rest. He didn't show her the badge to verify or ask what she remembered.

Wang Hai stopped at the full-length mirror.

"You asked me to remind you."

Lin Mo faced the glass. Gray cloth hid most of the blue across his chest, but shoulders and waist had both departed from their old shape. Before reciting, he thought of his name. Then he spoke in the voice he had now.

"I'm Lin Mo. I'm a man."

Wang Hai waited beside him.

"Again?"

"Not yet."

He remembered his sister. He remembered which door he'd entered. The extra corridor remained in his head; saying the words easily didn't mean everything he remembered was right. He let Wang Hai go half a step ahead and followed the carved end of the wood.

Sun Ning's door was shut.

He gave both names as agreed and heard her answer. But his left hand was already reaching for a patch of peeling paint. He wanted to put the review badge there.

There was no slot.

Wang Hai watched his hand.

"What are you looking for?"

"Where we change shifts."

His own answer stopped him. The door opened. Sun Ning stood inside, right arm against her side, left hand on the door.

"Weren't you just going to look at the entrance?"

He took his hand off the wall. He knew to tell her he'd seen it. He knew who'd walked back with him.

But when Sun Ning stepped aside and asked why they'd taken so long, he almost answered with a phrase he knew by heart.

The next shift isn't here yet.
