# Chapter 54: The Apology Beyond the Glass

The screen enlarged Old Chen's hand. Dark marks beside his nails overlapped the table's outlines for needle and pliers.

Lin Mo moved aside, hoping the camera would show the suspended schoolbag. It followed Old Chen instead.

The attendant held the removed plate beside its slot.

“Source-owner dispute means filming the person being checked. Getting in front won't help.”

Lin Mo stepped back. His shoulder burned, and he'd blocked Old Chen's view of the door.

Tao told the thin man the case was going to the red-bordered cabinet. No putting it back in the continuation slot. The man turned his empty rack, complaining that after the longest wait he'd leave without a complete delivery.

“You'll get your credit. I'll accompany the label.”

Tao returned for the case. Beyond the barrier, the viewing light still shone on Xiao Lu's paired plates in the returns tray. The yellow bowl remained separate. Lin Mo took nothing and didn't demand another intervention for her.

Old Chen folded the receipt behind his tag and tried lifting his bag. Pain in his neck stopped him halfway. He set it on a stool.

“I'll carry it,” Wang Hai said.

“You just held that schoolbag.”

“Didn't kill me. Carrying a bag isn't carrying a person.”

Old Chen kept it. Wang Hai sat instead, right hand hanging beside his knee. After a while he slowly rotated his wrist.

“Planning to finish the ledger before we go back?” Lin Mo asked.

“I'd like to.”

“My legs won't last indefinitely.”

“Then ask how long before agreeing.”

Tao returned with the case and wrapped its long label around it. The cabinet could receive this item now. They could come to the outer edge and ask; the people there decided whether it opened. An old tag alone granted no entry.

Old Chen hugged his bag against his abdomen and asked Lin Mo to come that far.

“To see where it is. If there's a wait, we go back first.”

He didn't address the camera. Old Chen nodded only to him.

The passage was hotter. The floor-level delivery track descended past a half-open metal panel. Red light fell around their shoes. The rack scraped the panel's bottom; Xiao Lu stepped back twice, keeping her bag clear.

The red-bordered cabinet had no service window. Its open rear revealed upright frames above a polished rail. A woman in dark work clothes stood on a stepladder changing a lamp, two screws between her lips.

After Tao explained, she ordered them outside the yellow footprints. She climbed down, pocketed the screws, and took the long label between two fingers.

“Person?”

Old Chen raised his tag.

She compared the case number to the current continuation label before taking anything from him. Two narrow plates went into the cabinet's side. A small window flashed his old face from the mending session, then his present one.

His tag touched the side slot. The light stayed yellow.

SOURCE-OWNER NUMBER MATCHED. CURRENT DISPUTE PENDING CONNECTION.

“The participant withdrew?” she asked Tao.

“Yes. Both objections are on this.”

She inserted Duan Qin's withdrawal plate into a second slot. Yellow changed to white. Old Chen kept his tag. Its old percentage opened no other cabinet.

“Shao,” the woman said. This shift, this row of cabinets; other racks weren't her responsibility. She held the painted-over return field beneath the lamp.

“You want the source material back?”

“My pages. The answer after I asked for the name. First, where they are.”

“This is return of material. Doesn't cover getting you out.”

Old Chen nodded. Lin Mo looked harder at the small window. Rack C, Code Seven remained there, beside changing fine print half obscured by a metal shield.

Master Shao explained: current use hadn't been released. The outer desk could show separated pieces. Black meant prohibited withdrawal, not missing material. She could open a comparison strip for this man's number, showing where the answer segment connected and who currently held it. No promise of returning pages now, no stopping every old clip.

“The participant left,” Lin Mo said.

“She withdrew herself. The source is still committed to this session.”

A red line lit beside the window. Bay Two appeared: schoolbag on its stool, red text against the table.

Seatkeeper: Retain continuation by the source owner. I'm not withdrawing my selection.

Shao tapped the cabinet.

“I need room for the next item. If you only want to argue with him, this goes back.”

“How long for comparison?” Old Chen asked.

“Stand long enough to read one pass. Finish and leave, material returns. Can't stand, it returns early.”

She slid a narrow gate aside, exposing back-to-back panes of glass, one toward Old Chen, the other toward a wall-side recess. Two shallow floor grooves ran on either side of a chest-high metal partition.

Old Chen stepped forward. Shao stopped him.

“Marks first. Outside source-owner position only. Matching a tag and checking the present person are separate.”

He set his bag on dry ground and entered the worn footprints. His face appeared in the window. An inch-wide white strip emerged to the right.

Lin Mo couldn't read it. Old Chen turned; the moment his shoulder shifted, a black shutter dropped between the panes. The strip retracted.

“Face moves, it closes. Old number still needs a live person.”

His hand went toward it. Shao slapped the partition.

“Don't pry. Doesn't wait for your fingers either.”

He withdrew his hand and straightened. The strip returned, still facing away. He tried reading its reflection. Got one character, then another wrong.

“Connection,” Shao corrected. “Not return. Haven't reached return yet.”

Old Chen smiled painfully.

“Thought my eyes had learned to read too.”

“Your side watches you. Reader enters from beside you. One person.”

Wang Hai inspected the recess. A low beam barred the entrance; getting in meant turning sideways, bracing against the wall, and descending two steps to a fixed bench.

He pressed his right palm to the beam. His wrist gave before he could try. Lin Mo kept hold of his own wood. Wang Hai retreated himself, swore, then stopped shaking his hand when that hurt too.

“Can't do it. Might get in. Wouldn't come out looking human.”

“You are human,” Old Chen said.

“Let's preserve that benefit.”

Xiao Lu looked at Lin Mo without immediately taking the place for him. He asked what the reader would see. Shao indicated the strip: junctions, current holder, this inquiry's reply. It passed outward and returned to the cabinet. They could read aloud or make notes, not take it.

“What about farther inside?”

She closed the gate and tapped the partition.

“Maintenance side. Reader can't see it. No reaching through. One number opens one strip.”

Could he try getting down first?

“Before reading starts. Exit toward the white handle. Don't push the inner partition.”

Wang Hai took the wood, freeing his left hand. Lin Mo braced against the beam and turned sideways, right foot first. Halfway down, his knee met an edge. He'd forgotten his changed hips no longer fit where they used to. His body descended while the fabric caught above, tightening the broad tie against his side.

“Don't force it,” Xiao Lu said.

He stopped and freed his hem. Trying to turn at once struck his left shoulder against the wall. He drew a breath, retreated half a step, and made the turn in two movements. At last he sat.

Only the lower half of Old Chen's face showed outside. They'd seemed so close. Now the partition hid his arms; Lin Mo couldn't even tell if he was reaching.

A line awaiting confirmation appeared on the glass. Lin Mo couldn't shift inward. Leaning toward the cold pane made it clearer; farther forward brought metal beneath the bench against his calf.

“Can you manage?” Wang Hai asked.

“Yes.” Then Lin Mo located the white handle, behind the entrance step. He'd have to leave the seat and turn again to reach it.

“Getting out takes time too.”

Shao didn't operate it for him. He pressed it with his left palm. The narrow door blocking half the exit slid into its slot. Using the beam, he climbed one step at a time. Wang Hai returned the wood without mentioning his first answer.

“Someone else?” Old Chen asked.

“I want to see it myself. But my agreeing doesn't make it easy.”

Old Chen nodded without invoking youth as a guarantee. Lin Mo steadied the wood. Sweat cooled on his back as he watched that handle he couldn't reach without turning.

The glass darkened. Old Chen left the footprints and bent for his bag. A cough stopped him halfway. Xiao Lu approached; he waved her back and braced against the beam until it passed.

A child's voice came from the window.

“Dad, you fixed my bag. I know.”

Old Chen straightened instantly.

Closer than Xu's box, the voice seemed to belong to someone behind the glass. The screen showed only the Clothes Mending Four schoolbag, labeled REQUESTED ANSWER PREVIEW.

Shao swore. She didn't control previews. She offered no congratulations on finding it and didn't close the window for him. The red name glowed below the image.

Seatkeeper: Come back and sit properly. Let the child finish this time.

Old Chen moved a step closer.

Lin Mo called him. No answer. Xiao Lu took out her cup, then stopped. Nobody tried covering the words with an offer of water.

The child said he shouldn't have slammed the door. He knew how hard his father worked; he'd never had the chance to say so.

“I slammed the door,” Old Chen said quietly.

The child had accepted even that, but Old Chen didn't ask it to stop. Tendons stood out on the hand gripping the beam.

“Is that the separately stored answer?”

Shao turned the case toward him. The original-junction field remained blank.

“Comparison hasn't started. Haven't even read you where it connects.”

“Who wrote what's playing?”

“Requesting viewer supplied it. Ask him.”

Lin Mo's chest tightened. The voice gave no name, yet knew which regret a father would most want to hear. Old Chen's lips were pale. His hand stayed on the beam.

“Write that too,” he said.

Lin Mo reached for the booklet, thinking he meant the words. Old Chen shook his head.

“I wanted to sit down again. It wasn't that I didn't hear you.”

Lin Mo left his hand at the pocket.

“And now?”

“Still do.”

Another Dad. Old Chen coughed, eyes full of water when he lifted his face. He pointed toward the outer footprints.

“I'll stand here. Read my own number first.”

Shao asked him to confirm his reader. Old Chen turned to Lin Mo.

“If you can't make something out, leave it blank. Don't guess a sentence to make me feel better. Can you?”

“Yes. But I need to rest first. Tell Sun Ning and the others where you'll be.”

Old Chen looked at the speaking window and slowly released the beam. He bent and lifted the bag in both hands.

“I'll go back with you.”

Shao closed the cabinet opening, clipping the current dispute beside Rack C, Code Seven. Return before the next rack, she said. Miss this cycle, wait until the old material was in use again. No pass to open it whenever they wished.

Lin Mo noted the empty trolley beside the entrance and asked how to recognize the next rack.

“White frame means changeover.” She pointed down the track. A gray frame waited ahead of it. That nearby order was visible; she didn't know how many stood farther away.

Old Chen's name remained lit beside the window. He hadn't accepted the viewer's request. Refusing hadn't instantly silenced the preview either.

As they withdrew, Lin Mo looked back. The white strip had vanished behind the shield. The bench stood empty beneath its low beam. Inside, even turning around took two movements.

Old Chen waited by the door, holding the bag low to spare his shoulder.

“Next time you read. I'll try to stand long enough.”
