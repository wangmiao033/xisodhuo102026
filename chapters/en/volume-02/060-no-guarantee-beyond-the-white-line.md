# Chapter 60: No Guarantee Beyond the White Line

“Even if you come back?” Wang Hai asked.

Even then. The page had been clear: once entering the formal area ended his protection, returning to the beginner side wouldn't restore it.

He and Xiao Lu had just reached the rest room. Sun Ning made them sit. Lin Mo described his broadcast page, the empty stage, the mirrors and the white line, leaving the termination of protection until last. He heard his voice sink as he said it.

“Don't put the thing that scares you most after you've finished explaining,” Sun Ning said.

He began again.

“Entering the formal performance area ends beginner protection. After that, my existence value could reach zero. They could permanently erase my identity.”

Wang Hai looked down at Old Chen's bag. Lin Mo thought of the cabinet too, without turning one claim against a source owner into a punishment everyone faced everywhere. What he'd witnessed there was frightening enough.

Xiao Lu took out her cup, drank twice and offered it to nobody.

Did the other area guarantee an audience? Sun Ning asked. No. The public directory would make his session available to anyone who wanted it. Nobody had reserved a hundred thousand viewers for him.

“People watched your other sessions.”

“I kept waiting for them to give me something to do. This time I want them to see what I want before they decide to stay.”

“What do you want?”

“Their votes for freedom. And for them to see that making me into the person they want is a choice they're making.”

Wang Hai looked up.

“Some will enjoy hearing that.”

“Yes. They've watched me hurt already. Acting more obedient hasn't stopped them. The next session needs something they haven't seen.”

He made no claim that new proposals didn't frighten him. His shoulder hurt whenever he moved; the scrapes hadn't closed properly. His body remained as the last execution had left it. He had to pull his hem clear just to sit steadily. He couldn't promise another alteration wouldn't matter.

Sun Ning watched his hands.

“Don't use Old Chen's last moments as your preview.”

Lin Mo went still. Red words on the screen, empty footprints. He could build a frightening opening from them. He hadn't mentioned it, but he'd thought of using the man's last pleas to hold people's attention.

“I can say they removed his body. I won't act out him begging for help, or open his notebook for them.”

“That's your promise.”

“I promise.”

Wang Hai pressed a loose corner beneath the bag's severed strap. If he stayed here, could Lin Mo reach the stage? Yes, slowly. Xiao Lu had seen him do it.

“Don't make her guarantee it. Tell me yourself.”

“I can walk. I need to stop on the ramp. I can't climb over the platform edge or pull anyone.”

Wang Hai leaned back.

“I'll come and watch. You keep the wood. I can't hold it up for you all the time. If someone needs picking up, I haven't got another pair of good hands.”

Lin Mo nodded.

“Not yet. There's the bag.”

Could he leave it with Sun Ning until he returned? She agreed not to open it, but told him to keep it away from her feet. He supported its base with his right hand and slowly shifted it to an empty patch by the inner wall, releasing it only when it stood securely.

Sun Ning hadn't agreed to come.

“I don't want to go onstage. I don't want to wait beside one for somebody to tell me how it ends. The application you found is yours. I understand that.”

His chest tightened. He wanted to promise he'd get everyone out eventually. Thinking of the page with a hundred thousand, he left it in his booklet.

“If there's a result, I'll come back and tell you. I won't sign you up.”

“First be able to come back.”

It wasn't a curse. She asked Wang Hai to move her water closer. He put it where she indicated, leaving the empty stool within her left hand's reach. She tested its stability herself, then told them to stop standing around watching.

Xiao Lu still hadn't said whether she'd come. At the door, she screwed on her cup's lid.

“What do you want me to do?”

He started to ask her to come with him, then stopped. She'd already shown him the entrance. She meant more than the walk.

“I want you there. Not to recognize the past for me or answer for someone else.”

“What if you keep looking at me, waiting for me to call you that?”

He couldn't immediately say he wouldn't. She lowered the cup into her bag and let his silence stand.

“I'll want it,” he said. “That doesn't mean you have to give it to me.”

She closed the bag.

“I'll go as far as the side. I haven't agreed to be filmed or stay all session. Tell me before you change the shot.”

“All right.”

She glanced at his left sleeve and told him to free the torn edge folded inward, so it wouldn't keep rubbing as he walked. He adjusted it himself. Wang Hai had already moved outside, leaving room for him to turn with the wood.

They passed the water station slowly. This time Old Chen wasn't behind his left shoulder. Lin Mo's arm lifted a little toward that space, then fell.

Xiao Lu saw. She didn't fill it. She walked ahead and to his side where he could see her, waiting before each turn for him to catch up.

The public entrance was quieter. One title had vanished from the directory and another appeared with zero beside it. The attendant leaned against the mirror rig.

“Into the formal area this time?”

Lin Mo opened his draft first. FREEDOM remained in the lower left. The applicant was his channel alone; the session was unconfirmed. He showed Wang Hai, then showed Xiao Lu the shot's boundaries. She pointed to a place at the side, away from behind the rig.

The attendant said it was on the waiting side. Staying short of the white line kept her outside the current performance arrangement. Wang Hai chose the same spot, by a wall and two stools. They could see the stage but couldn't end the broadcast for him.

“You have to press it yourself,” Wang Hai said.

Lin Mo expanded the execution rules. Body-modification and cognitive-overwrite proposals would execute at sixty percent approval. Ending the broadcast wouldn't reverse anything already executed. He read the lines aloud, his finger lingering over sixty. He'd suffered under that number before. A new program name didn't lower it.

Could he exclude one category? Wang Hai asked. Lin Mo searched without finding any way to remove his body or memory from proposal targets. He found only End Current Broadcast. Using it would end the session's count; another broadcast would begin at zero.

“You need to be able to reach that.”

From the waiting-side preview, Lin Mo located the performance controls beside the low platform, fixed independently of the mirrors. A separate button started the broadcast. Looking across the line, he checked the distance from the ramp and told the attendant to keep the wheels clear of that route.

The attendant crossed alone, rolled the rig onto level ground in the formal area and placed the stool by the ramp. He pressed both brakes, showing Lin Mo the wheels had stopped before letting go.

The page requested a name for the directory. After thinking, Lin Mo entered: This Time, I Tell It.

“You can't tell everything,” Xiao Lu said.

“Then I'll admit what I haven't told.”

He added no promise of truth, completeness or the only account. He wasn't exchanging one infallible page for another. This time, he would leave the choices visible within the story.

Beside the saved title: PUBLISHES WHEN BROADCAST STARTS. No audience had heard it. No countdown hurried them toward a final second.

The boundary light came on. Lin Mo approached, and a notice rose before him. He expanded it with his hand, keeping the wood away, and read to the bottom.

PROTECTED PERSON: LIN MO. BEGINNER PROTECTION CURRENTLY ACTIVE.

SCOPE: DURING THE BEGINNER STAGE, EXISTENCE VALUE CANNOT BE REDUCED TO ZERO AND IDENTITY CANNOT BE PERMANENTLY ERASED. BODY ADAPTATION AND PERFORMANCE PENALTIES ARE NOT EXEMPTED.

Then the termination clause:

PROTECTION ENDS AFTER CONFIRMATION AND DEPARTURE FROM THE BEGINNER PERFORMANCE AREA. RETURNING DOES NOT RESTORE IT.

Currently active held him longest. It had remained through the wrist pain, the unfamiliar body, the times he couldn't recognize himself. It had stopped none of those. Without its two guarantees, he didn't know whether he'd have reached this place at all.

His sister had given up all her existence value. At the end, she'd said wait.

He remembered the hand extended in that recording. His own now hovered before confirmation. Slender knuckles, the wrist scar, fresh blood along his left sleeve.

Wang Hai didn't hurry him.

Lin Mo withdrew his hand.

“I want to sit a little longer.”

The attendant stepped aside. He sat on the stool before the line. The draft remained his. Nobody took away his eligibility for pausing.

He told Xiao Lu about the guarantee. He feared crossing would be throwing away what his sister had paid for. Only after saying it did he notice he'd been looking at her throughout.

She didn't answer for the person in the recording.

“I can't tell you whether she'd want this.”

He nodded.

“Do you want to cross?”

“Yes. I'm afraid too.”

“Then count both. Don't wait for me to remove one.”

He sat and slowly opened his right hand. His palm had long since dried; none of Old Chen's sweat remained. The nail's scrape across his wrist scar still hurt. He wanted to keep the protection and learn what lay beyond it. At this threshold, he finally couldn't take both with him.

When he stood, Wang Hai only nudged the displaced wood closer. He didn't lift him.

Lin Mo returned to the notice. He read again that the departure application verified him alone. Counting hadn't begun. Losing protection didn't authorize him to leave the theater. Beyond the stage was still more stage. He would start at zero.

Xiao Lu asked suddenly: if hardly anyone came, would he drag her into a story he'd promised to leave alone, just to keep them watching?

He looked at the empty space beside the stage. He could imagine the temptation. One recording of her calling him her brother, and he could keep everyone waiting for the next. Move the camera closer whenever she hesitated.

“If you say stop, I'll stop. I'll ask again before putting you on camera.”

“I'll say it.”

He didn't put her words into the preview or ask her to stand under the light. Turning back to the notice, he checked that he remained the sole applicant. His choice hadn't drawn either waiting companion into it.

“I confirm.”

He pressed his confirmation. The light beside the white line changed to white. TAKES EFFECT UPON CROSSING. There was still that final distance he could choose not to walk.

He set the wood beyond the line and checked the ramp's entrance before stepping over with his right foot. His left knee trembled as it followed. He paused for a breath, then brought that foot fully into the formal area.

BEGINNER PERFORMANCE AREA EXITED. BEGINNER PROTECTION TERMINATED.

The voice was level. No strength poured into him. His wounds didn't suddenly hurt more. He stood beneath the light, hearing his own breathing, until the words withdrew into his status panel.

He looked back. Wang Hai still leaned against the wall on the waiting side. Xiao Lu made no reassuring gesture.

Following the route they'd left clear, Lin Mo reached the stool and sat, returning his booklet to his jacket. The main camera's preparation light came on. The frame held only him and the narrow mirrors. No new body or cognitive proposal had executed. Protection was the field that went gray.

He bound the departure application to his own session. The small FREEDOM box confirmed its lower-left position: zero votes, broadcast not started.

He turned a side mirror slightly outward, leaving the camera a direct view of him. The brakes held against his pressure.

His hand moved to Start Broadcast. He didn't look back for anyone to press it for him.
