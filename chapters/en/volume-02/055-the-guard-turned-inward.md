# Chapter 55: The Guard Turned Inward

Back at the rest area, Lin Mo called out his name before setting the wood against the wall within Wang Hai's reach.

Sun Ning still faced the inner wall. Only after hearing all four return did she turn a little. He told her the water clip showed her left sleeve and the chair back, not her face. They hadn't obtained the remaining footage or discovered where it had been filmed.

Had anyone been made to perform it again?

No. He told her about Duan Qin's carry test. At the scraped palm, Sun Ning's left hand stopped on her injured wrist.

“What about you?”

“No fresh cuts. Legs need a rest.”

“Then sit before you tell me.”

Sitting revealed that his broad cloth tie had twisted again. He moved his hem himself. Wang Hai stayed by the door, resting his right hand. Old Chen set the broken bag beside his feet but didn't let go.

“Watch this for me. I want to come back and drink from that cup.”

Wang Hai lifted a corner of the bag's mouth. The old notebook rested on the cup. He lowered it.

“Don't make it sound like collecting your effects. Just say you'll be back.”

“I'll be back.”

Old Chen released the bag, keeping only his tag and viewing receipt. The severed strap remained wound around the opening, unrepaired.

Lin Mo rested with his eyes shut until his fingers stopped trembling with his pulse. Then he noted the cabinet's location. Hearing about the low beam, two steps and white handle, Sun Ning asked who could get him out if it closed.

“I have to reach the handle myself.”

“Can you, now?”

“Tried once. Slowly.”

She neither forbade him to go nor assured him that slowly was enough. He wrote that beside the location. Old Chen was watching the door.

The gray frame hadn't moved through. They'd checked the track's corner both ways; the white frame still waited behind it. Nobody knew how much time remained.

Xiao Lu would accompany them to the outer desk and wait where she could see the return route. She hadn't agreed to approach the cabinet. Wang Hai needed to rest his hand. Sun Ning asked for an honest account afterward, without volunteering for another scene.

Old Chen reached to help as Lin Mo retrieved his wood, then stopped to ask which side.

“Left elbow. Let go before the narrow passage.”

They went slowly. Back at the outer desk, Tao had resumed using his knife.

“Thought you weren't coming.”

“Changeover hasn't happened?”

“Gray frame caught on an edge. Don't thank it. Someone's gone to push.”

He pointed toward the glass door. Xiao Lu found a stable stool outside, sat briefly, then cleared an empty box from their path.

“I'll wait here. Whoever comes back, call me first. Don't just shout run through a wall.”

Shao was folding her ladder. She checked Old Chen's tag and receipt, told him to put the tag away, and turned the reader's field toward Lin Mo.

He gave his name and wrote it where she indicated. She slid the paper into a side slot.

READER FOR THIS PASS: LIN MO.

He reached toward another gate. It wouldn't move.

“This one.” Shao tapped the original low beam. “One strip doesn't open a row.”

The little screen still showed Bay Two's empty table. As Old Chen approached the footprints, a smaller image opened beside it, displaying his present face. Lin Mo had thought they'd left the camera behind. He drew his foot back.

“That person can see this too?”

Shao turned over a label beneath the screen.

VISIBLE TO CURRENT REQUESTER: ORIGINAL-OWNER COMPARISON POSITION.

“You stopped the paired segment. This item's being matched to its owner. The requester's still holding it.”

“You didn't say.”

She pointed to two black heads no bigger than rivets beside the glass. During his trial entry, he'd watched the low beam and missed them. The label specified the visible area, she said. He could still withdraw as reader.

Old Chen raised his tag before his face. The small window darkened; the source-owner light blinked twice. Lowering it steadied the light.

“Can't read with it covered?”

Shao nodded. One number, and only while its owner could be matched. She could turn off their little monitor and silence the comments. His image would still be sent to the current continuation.

Lin Mo left it on. Better to see the red words than mistake one less light for the viewer's absence.

Old Chen studied his own face.

“Do I look that bad?”

“You're tired.”

“I mean, can he see I want to listen?”

Lin Mo couldn't say no. After a moment, Old Chen brought the tag to his chest and wiped sweat from his upper lip.

“He probably saw already. I still want to check.”

Would Lin Mo appear too? Shao marked the glass's boundary with her palm. Only a corner of Old Chen's shoulder disappeared; Lin Mo's recess stayed out of frame. He remembered the range he'd actually seen. It guaranteed nothing about going unnoticed behind the partition.

He tucked his booklet inside his jacket and left the wood outside the entrance, touching its notches once more. Old Chen stood in the footprints. His tag and receipt lay on a small shelf beyond the marks, inserted into no lock.

“I can't see your hands,” Lin Mo said.

“I can see half your head.”

“Tell me before you move.”

Old Chen agreed. Lin Mo descended sideways, planted both feet and lowered himself onto the bench. His shoulder cleared this time. His hip still pressed the iron partition; room for his arms seemed narrower than he remembered.

Shao pulled the connecting handle outside. White light made Old Chen squint. The first section slid toward Lin Mo.

SOURCE-LEDGER COMPARISON: RACK C, CODE SEVEN.

He read it aloud. Old Chen corrected one sound and waited for him to repeat it before agreeing. Beneath Clothes Mending Four, separate lines listed the original segment and answer.

The original ended with the question about the name. No child's name appeared under the answer, only old junction numbers. One box read Window Screen After the Mending.

“And the promise to find it for me?”

Lin Mo looked farther down. The original-answer field was blank. Small print beside it said:

NOT ENTERED WITH ORIGINAL SEGMENT.

After he read it, silence lasted outside.

“Doesn't mean it isn't there,” Old Chen said.

“Only says it didn't come in with this segment.”

“I understood.”

Lin Mo offered no comfort. His pen faltered in the cramped space; he'd nearly written missing. He crossed out the first stroke and copied the wording exactly.

The junction strip advanced. Current use lit: Continuation Bay Two, requester Seatkeeper, original participant withdrawn. The source-owner field still showed comparison in progress.

Old Chen coughed, turning from the glass. The black shutter slammed down, its cold edge touching Lin Mo's raised left hand. He snatched it away.

The strip retracted halfway. When Old Chen straightened, it emerged again with the same words.

“Caught you?”

“Touched me. Hand's outside.”

He lowered his arm. The shutter sounded much louder in here, dropping before his nose. Light still burned behind it, without showing through a single crack.

Shao told Old Chen not to suppress his cough. Face moved, reading stopped; they could resume when he steadied. She didn't promise that a reaching hand would get out in time.

Lin Mo set down his pen.

“How much longer?”

“Return and exception fields left. Your choice whether to continue.”

Old Chen pressed his sleeve to his mouth and said continue. The strip turned. Black still covered the original-owner return field, but short text showed beside it.

CURRENT USE UNSETTLED. SOURCE-OWNER DEPARTURE CREDENTIAL NOT ESTABLISHED.

Lin Mo leaned closer, unsure he'd read the last words correctly, then spoke them. Old Chen asked whether leaving meant getting his pages back.

“Don't know. Doesn't say.”

He called Shao. From outside the entrance, she explained that the credential only lifted this prohibition on withdrawal. Return still depended on whether the material survived and had other uses. Scattered pages wouldn't grow back because a box turned white.

“How do you establish it?”

She pointed behind the partition.

“Departure verification. I read source material here. Don't open that section.”

A fine reference line at the return field's end continued beneath an inner metal edge. Lin Mo pressed closer to follow it and met only the reflection of his nose.

“Can I stop it here?”

“Press the stop-reading tab below. Only stops this strip. Don't lift inward.”

He found the flush tab and pressed outward. The strip stopped. His right wrist hung above his knee; holding with his left left him unable to turn his booklet's pages. When Old Chen asked, he could only repeat the departure-credential line.

This ledger contained no exit, but it referenced somewhere else. He remembered the protection he'd read on arrival: his existence value couldn't be reduced to zero, his identity couldn't be permanently erased. It had never promised to get him out.

“You want to check how to leave,” Old Chen said.

Lin Mo looked up at the only part of him visible: his mouth.

“Yes.”

“Then say that. Don't call it finding my answer.”

His face heated. He'd nearly borrowed Old Chen's wish to make looking farther sound like something good for both of them.

“I want to know how to leave the theater. Don't know how much is behind this, or what happens if I touch it.”

Old Chen was silent. The preview's opening notes sounded. Shao cut them off at the cabinet and called to someone farther away: this line was being checked; stop sending answers into it.

Red words remained.

Seatkeeper: How much longer? You've seen the person. Get on with the performance.

Old Chen ignored them.

“Can you get out now?”

“Let go, turn, pull the white handle. Yes.”

“Don't reach in yet.”

Lin Mo released the tab. The last box passed, followed by small print: MAINTENANCE-SIDE VERIFICATION REFERENCE. Its tail vanished behind the iron. Old Chen told him to wait and asked Shao what crossing the limit would mean.

She straightened from the jammed gray frame.

“You opened an original-owner dispute. Cross the reading stop, it goes against this item's source owner. Requester's still holding it. Can pursue the claim. Don't think I'm taking it for you.”

“Against who?”

She pointed to Old Chen's face in the glass.

“The person matched now.”

He glanced at his shoes. The white light nearly slipped from his face; he raised his head without leaving position. Lin Mo finally understood the tightness outside his recess too. The more precisely Old Chen stood, the more precisely the account could find him.

“Let's finish,” Lin Mo said.

“Did you see how to leave?”

“No.”

“Then who are you speaking for?”

Lin Mo gripped the booklet on his knee. He couldn't immediately say you, or leave out his own fear of being caught. Old Chen waited, the red marks on his neck bright under the light.

“I'm afraid I won't get out. Afraid I'll leave you here too.”

“So am I.”

Metal clanged near Shao. The gray frame, straightened, began sliding slowly forward. The white frame followed behind. She called for them to withdraw once they'd finished reading, before changeover.

Old Chen shifted his feet toward the centers of the marks.

“Can you reach it?”

“Have to get off the bench. Lean inward.”

“Read. Don't keep me waiting while you invent good news.”

Lin Mo didn't move at once. He opened the booklet across his legs and clipped his pen into a blank page. His right hand supported the cover. His left found the tab again. It pressed only outward, but another guard beyond its pivot connected to the inner maintenance side.

He tested the outward pressure, stopping the strip, then braced his left forearm against the iron. His old shoulder injury stabbed. He paused for two breaths, planted his feet and slowly raised himself.

Old Chen's face stayed in the glass. He didn't ask Lin Mo to call his name again. He watched the black shutter that would close if he turned.

Lin Mo's fingertips passed the normal reading position.

He lifted the outward-facing guard half an inch inward.
