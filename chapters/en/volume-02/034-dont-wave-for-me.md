# Chapter 34: Don't Wave for Me

Wang Hai stayed.

He glanced at the words beneath the window, shifted the chair leg right, and stepped closer. His lips moved behind the glass. Lin Mo couldn't hear a word.

His rough right hand remained raised, palm inward.

Lin Mo slowly lowered his own. The shadow at the stage front swayed once more, fingers spread in the light, as though reluctant to let the man outside leave.

He shook his head at it. The screen stayed unchanged. The lit square extended toward center stage, where a white human outline held its right arm at shoulder height.

[SEGMENT: FAREWELL. TIME REMAINING: 02:00.]

That first movement had already chosen for him.

He didn't enter the square. Turning to the door behind him, he felt its smooth panel. No inside handle. The timer changed to 1:58. The light above the exit stayed yellow.

He'd checked before entering. It opened afterward.

Outside, Wang Hai pressed his palm downward. Lin Mo lowered his shoulders. His breathing was still fast, clothes rising and falling across his chest. He began pointing at FAREWELL, then stopped halfway.

What if it took that too?

Comments scrolled across the stage front.

[Is she facing the wrong way?]
[He's watching the person outside.]
[Don't count leaning on the glass as a gesture. Wait for him to move.]

“I didn't say goodbye,” Lin Mo mouthed. His throat vibrated, but he heard nothing. There was no pain, and nobody spoke for him.

He pressed two fingers to his throat, confirming the vibration. He didn't try shouting. In the earlier silent room, making a sound was forbidden; even knocking counted. Here he could open his mouth, but no sound emerged. The entrance diagram had crossed out a mouth. It hadn't said where the sound went.

He mouthed his name to Wang Hai and waited. Wang Hai frowned and shook his head. Lin Mo tried again, slower. Wang Hai pointed at his ear, still shaking his head.

Lin Mo closed his mouth. He couldn't expect Wang Hai to read the particular Mo that meant silence through glass. Even a correct guess wouldn't mean his voice had returned.

He breathed gently toward the window. Too far away: nothing. He edged closer until his nose was a hand's breadth from it, then exhaled. A small patch misted white.

Wang Hai immediately turned, seizing the nearby doorframe with his right hand.

He'd misunderstood. He thought Lin Mo couldn't breathe.

Lin Mo hurriedly raised his left palm between them. Wang Hai had already turned toward the entrance; the hand stopped him. His lips moved. Without striking the glass with his injured hand, he released the doorframe and pointed at Lin Mo's chest.

Lin Mo looked down at his moving jacket, then rested his left palm on his upper abdomen, without pressing his chest. He slowly breathed out and in, letting Wang Hai see. He stopped deliberately opening his mouth to breathe.

Wang Hai returned to the window and wiped his face with his right palm.

Both looked tired.

Lin Mo pointed at his eyes, then looked toward the panel below the window. Wang Hai followed his gaze and shifted aside. The goodbye message was still there.

Lin Mo shook his head.

This time Wang Hai didn't copy him. He watched, waiting for him to finish.

Lin Mo pointed to his own chest, then raised his left palm and held it still. Wang Hai slowly nodded, then pointed to his feet. Neither shoe moved.

He would stay.

Lin Mo closed his eyes briefly. When he opened them, 1:27 remained.

The white outline waited in the square. Its shoulders were narrower than his; its raised arm formed a soft curve. A new proposal opened beside it: FAREWELL POSE.

He took two steps closer and stopped at the small print.

[FEMINIZING ADAPTATION: SHOULDERS AND ARMS. CLOTHING AND NAME UNCHANGED.]
[IMPLEMENTATION AT 60% APPROVAL. CURRENT: 53%.]

His right wrist rested against his jacket. He wanted to fling his arm away, show the white figure his refusal. He started, then restrained himself. A swinging arm was precisely the pose it offered.

He kept both hands down and stared straight ahead.

[How do you perform a farewell without waving?]
[Let her get comfortable. She could barely lift her arms.]
[Are they changing the injured one too?]

At the last comment, Lin Mo eased his right sleeve upward. He didn't roll the sleeve all the way up, exposing only a little of the white scar beside the cuff. He pointed with his left finger, neither smiling nor offering the scar toward the audience.

They'd already seen this injury.

The number dropped to fifty-one.

He glanced outside. Wang Hai gripped the chair leg with his right hand, his other cuff held against his abdomen. He couldn't come inside and raise Lin Mo's hand for him, or add a vote to the scrolling words.

Lin Mo moved aside to reveal the closed exit. He pointed at the door and lowered his hand, hoping someone would understand he was waiting for it to open.

The projected figure looked back over its shoulder.

[He wants one last look.]

Lin Mo clenched his jaw. He pressed his left palm toward the floor once and stopped gesturing. Question marks appeared, followed by a comment telling them to leave him alone. Fifty-one held, but fell no further.

He lowered his sleeve. That movement had left his aching left shoulder trembling. The broad cloth belt remained securely knotted. He neither loosened it nor adjusted his clothes to match the outline.

1:08.

New comments covered the old.

[Just change the upper part. Leave the scar alone.]
[You get to keep your name. Still not good enough?]
[He hasn't agreed to anything.]

Lin Mo looked up at the last line. It slid offscreen. He couldn't know whether its writer was still watching.

A scope clarification appeared beneath the proposal. The injured wrist was excluded; the shoulder girdle and upper arms remained highlighted. Approval began rising.

He stepped backward. Wang Hai immediately raised his hand. Lin Mo stopped at the square's edge, socked heels still inside the yellow boundary. Drawing his hands to his sides, he touched the booklet's hard corner through his jacket.

He could take it out. Write for them.

His fingers gripped the pocket opening. Then he remembered the woman's rule: belongings must remain inside clothing. He let go. The pen was right there. He could feel it, and still couldn't use it.

With his left finger, he slowly traced 不, no, on his gray jacket. At the first vertical stroke, the projection showed only a hand held to his chest. It omitted the remaining strokes.

[She's saying she can't bear to let go.]

He stared. His breath caught.

Sixty.

The proposal's border lit. The number stopped at sixty-one. IMPLEMENTING appeared beside it.

His right shoulder sank first. Instinctively he tried lifting it with his old strength, but something behind his shoulder blade slowly shifted. Taut muscles couldn't find their former purchase. His elbow slipped along his waist. The width that had filled out his jacket was gone.

He gritted his teeth without trying to wrench his shoulders back.

The left side tightened next. An ache dragged from his neck into his upper arm. Contours changed beneath the skin; his sleeve settled against his arm, though the fabric hadn't grown. His left arm, which had barely supported him before, drew inward. Reaching for the wall, his fingertips fell short of where he expected, touching only the window's metal frame.

He felt down it until he could brace the heel of his palm, then slowly bent his knees.

Outside, Wang Hai half crouched too. He raised the chair leg crosswise, then stopped before the glass. Looking at the barrier between them, he stood the wood back on the floor.

Lin Mo could still bend his right wrist. The white scar remained. But when his arm hung down, his palm met his thigh in a different place. He drew back the right hand he'd meant to brace against the floor. Instead, he rested his left forearm against the frame and settled his weight onto his feet in stages.

Thirty-seven seconds.

The shadow finally matched his shoulders. The white outline still held its arm up. He didn't copy it. Both hands stayed low until the pulling through his shoulders and back subsided.

[BODY MODIFICATION: 48%. COGNITIVE OVERWRITE: 42%.]

He was still struggling for a full breath when the figures appeared. He could see where forty-four had changed. The other number hadn't moved, yet images of himself waiting in a hospital bed still flashed through his mind. An unchanged number was no reason to trust that bed.

He pressed his left finger against the real floor beside the hem of his jacket. Leaving the metal frame, his fingertip made a damp mark on the ground.

There was no bed here. He crouched by the window. Wang Hai crouched outside.

Wang Hai pointed toward the rear door, then raised his right palm. He didn't urge Lin Mo to straighten.

Lin Mo nodded and stayed in that undignified crouch. He owed the white figure no graceful turn.

The timer reached zero. The yellow light went out. The door released outward.

He didn't lunge for it. First he braced his left palm against its edge, moved his heel half a step, and waited for both legs to support him. Then he slowly rose. Wang Hai came around to the exit and offered the chair leg's carved end.

Lin Mo gripped it. His first exhalation finally made a sound.

“Didn't say goodbye.”

“I could tell.”

“You nearly left.”

“I was looking for the door. Thought you couldn't breathe.”

“I wanted you to see my breath.”

Wang Hai looked at him.

“Saw it. Scared me half to death.”

Lin Mo began explaining the mist on the glass, then stopped. They'd practiced only one gesture: stop. Once inside, he'd added a movement neither of them had ever tried.

“I'll tell you first next time.”

“You'd better.”

Wang Hai didn't touch his shoulder. He steadied the wood and waited for Lin Mo to come out himself. His gray jacket's shoulder seams hung lower. He drew the pocket forward so the booklet's hard corner wouldn't keep striking his hip. The broad belt retained its original knot. He hadn't used the confusion at the door to change his clothes.

The woman collected his channel badge and stamped a small blank space. Supported by the wood, he approached. SEGMENT COMPLETED now marked the back.

“Your name hasn't changed,” she said.

“You wrote a goodbye for me.”

She pointed at the window.

“He's still here, isn't he?”

Wang Hai opened his mouth. Lin Mo turned the badge faceup first. His handwritten 默 remained, its final dot blurred with ink where he'd pressed too hard.

He raised his left palm toward the woman.

She leaned away.

“Put your hand down. Don't block the people behind you.”

He obeyed, watching her.

“You understood that?”

She looked up.

“Understood what?”

“You didn't take it for goodbye.”

Her fingers left the badge. She glanced at the rack. The preprinted 林茉 badge still stood one empty slot away. He neither reached for it nor asked again about changing it back.

Wang Hai steadied the chair leg closer. Only then did Lin Mo realize he'd been bearing against it with his altered shoulder, which was starting to tremble. He lowered his grip, widened his stance, and straightened the badge again.

“He stayed. I didn't send him away and bring him back.”

Her hand paused, but she didn't erase the stamp. She placed the badge in a shallow slot beside the counter. A white line flashed underneath.

Lin Mo freed one hand to catch the badge's edge, then stopped.

The line led inward, much like the one at the receiving-and-dispatch counter.

“What did that just collect?”

Before she answered, a small screen lit across the corridor. Lin Mo turned and saw his face. In the image he stood straight, his shoulders still as they had been when he entered, his left hand slowly beckoning inward.

It was the hand he'd raised to stop Wang Hai. The beginning, when he'd held it upright and still, was gone. They'd joined it to the later movement, drawing his hand toward his abdomen.

Another channel appeared beneath the image.

[FRONT-OF-HOUSE GUIDANCE: PLEASE FOLLOW HER INSIDE.]

Behind the screen was a waiting-room door. An adult woman with her sleeves rolled high rose from a stool, looked at the image, and approached the corridor.

Lin Mo immediately raised his left palm.

Seeing it, she relaxed a little and quickened her steps.

Onscreen, Lin Mo held that same hand up, waiting for her.
