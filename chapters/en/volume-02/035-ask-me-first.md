# Chapter 35: Ask Me First

"Don't come over."

The woman kept coming. She glanced at Lin Mo's feet, as though looking for a line she shouldn't cross.

He lowered his hand.

"I didn't call you inside. That's me on the screen, but they spliced the gesture together."

She stopped two steps away. About thirty, short hair tucked behind her ears, sleeves rolled high. She held an unfolded channel badge.

"You just told me to follow."

"I was asking him to stay." Lin Mo glanced at Wang Hai. "Did you hear me say follow?"

"No. It said so up there."

She looked at the screen again. The recorded hand drew inward. Both his hands hung down. His left shoulder already hurt; he kept them where she could see without demonstrating again.

"Ask me first."

"Ask what?"

"Whether I can take you where you want to go."

She frowned. "I want to go back where I came from. Can you get me out?"

His throat tightened.

"No. I'm still looking."

The relief left her face. Wang Hai drew the chair leg aside, clearing the corridor without barring her way.

She stayed, pointing beneath the image.

"Where exactly is front of house?"

The woman behind the counter took a badge from the rack.

"Through the door."

"Not the exit?"

"It says come inside."

The newcomer rubbed her thumb over her badge's bottom blank, then raised it.

"You told me to choose someone familiar to guide me. This is what you showed me. Then she's standing here. What was I supposed to think?"

Lin Mo could see two separate fields on the back: entry and companion confirmation. Neither bore a fingerprint. Watching the footage hadn't entered her into a segment.

He addressed the counter woman. "Take mine down."

"The badge has been processed."

"For the goodbye. Not recruiting people for you."

She tapped the shallow slot. His handwritten badge showed at its edge; the white line underneath had gone dark. She neither pushed it farther nor touched the preprinted 林茉 in the rack.

"We only collect here. To see what was kept, go to review."

"Where's that?"

She turned a small board. Two faces confronted each other on its reverse, one eyeless, one with small frames around its eyes.

[IDENTIFY YOURSELF.]

Below: adaptation continued during the segment. He left it untouched, looking instead at his badge. Its little teeth had caught inside the slot. Pulling might not free it.

Wang Hai touched his sleeve.

"Deal with her first. She's still standing here."

The newcomer watched the wood Wang Hai offered him and stepped back.

"Do you get out if you bring someone else in?"

Wang Hai's grip tightened. Lin Mo shook his head before he could extend the wood toward her.

"I just came out. Time ran out, the door opened. He waited outside."

"Of course you'd both say that."

Instead of taking the path they'd cleared, she raised her badge to block the onscreen face. Lin Mo waited without reaching for her hand.

"You said to ask you. Can that one answer?"

"I don't know what else it might play."

"Then turn around."

Leaning on the wood, he turned slowly. His weight wouldn't settle where it used to. He edged a socked heel outward before giving her his back. Onscreen, his face still looked ahead. The old left hand beckoned again.

Silence behind him. Then, "All right."

He turned back. She hadn't approached.

"All I know is it doesn't follow what you're doing now. That's all I'm willing to believe."

"That's enough. Don't treat it as now."

The counter woman tapped a badge against the slot, signaling them to move. The newcomer started handing hers over, then stopped.

"Mine goes here too?"

"That one's finished."

She snatched it back. Its corner scraped the slot and folded along a white crease; the fingerprint fields stayed empty. Lin Mo reached, missing her. Pain twisted through his shoulder. He gripped the counter until it eased.

"Let her finish asking," Wang Hai murmured. "She hadn't thrown anything away. You nearly threw your arm after it."

She pinched the folded corner, offering no thanks, and turned the badge toward herself.

"You recognized my face," Lin Mo said. "They edited what I did."

"So how do I choose?"

"Don't choose me yet."

"I wasn't going to."

He stopped and made room. At the counter she turned her badge over, keeping her fingertip outside the companion field.

"What if I don't know anyone?"

The woman indicated another slot beside the rack.

"Enter alone."

"Why didn't you say that?"

"You asked which person."

Lips pressed together, she held the badge to her chest and backed toward the waiting room.

"I'll read it again. Don't press anything for me."

The woman didn't reach after her.

Lin Mo was about to suggest checking the exit when she turned.

"I'm going back to sit down. Don't raise your hand again."

Wang Hai coughed out half a laugh. Lin Mo pressed his already moving left finger against his jacket seam and nodded.

She returned through the waiting-room door, ignoring his onscreen guide toward the stage. She dragged her stool out of the projection's reach and read her badge line by line.

Only then did he lean toward the wall. His jacket seams hung on his upper arms. There was empty cloth where he'd once found support, but the bone hurting against the wall was still his.

"Lower down," Wang Hai said, standing the wood beside him. "You want to go back in today?"

"See who it kept."

"You. She recognized you."

Onscreen, his unchanged shoulders filled the jacket. His raised arm stayed steady. He couldn't hold it up that long anymore. The image never tired.

"It uses old movements in other segments. That badge they collected is connected to this screen. I want to see how it matches the movements to a person."

"Will that stop them using it?"

"She hasn't promised."

Wang Hai looked at his socked toes.

"Then don't tell yourself you're going in to get it removed."

Lin Mo nodded but stayed against the wall. His left hand still hovered, fingertips curled inward. He touched his thumb to each finger, letting them straighten.

"If you stay here, I'll stay while you rest," Wang Hai said. "Holding up the wood doesn't mean you have to go again."

Lin Mo took it and sat on the narrow bench. He breathed until his shoulders stopped rising with every breath. The counter woman didn't hurry him. The footage started over.

From the waiting room came a call.

"Don't practice along with it."

He looked up. His hand had returned to his abdomen.

"I'm not."

"Good. Learn it, and I really won't know which is which."

She resumed reading. He pressed the carved 默, an old notch scraping his fingertip. He didn't take her words as an offer of help, or resent them.

He had just been thinking he might feel better standing the way the image did.

A faint face reflected in the bright corridor's glass, hair ends distinct against its cheeks. He supported his right elbow with his left palm, then tried the reverse. That pulled his left shoulder. He released it at once. Moving both arms didn't mean he could put his old strength through them.

"Say something," Wang Hai said.

"What?"

"Anything. Your mouth was moving."

Lin Mo couldn't remember whether there had been words.

He looked at the glass.

"I'm Lin Mo. I'm a man."

He rested his left palm on his thigh. The adult woman in the glass lowered hers. His shoulders didn't change back, but he'd heard the words leave his own throat.

Wang Hai didn't repeat them for him.

After resting, he returned the wood and braced himself on the bench. His left arm began aching first. He shifted his feet twice instead of pushing upright at once.

"What do I perform in review?"

The woman pushed the board closer. Under its transparent sheet: two minutes; belongings kept inside clothing. The same adaptation threshold applied. Body and cognitive proposals passed at sixty percent approval.

"You see only your live image and recorded material. Choose what you want to identify."

"I'm not accepting the past you wrote me."

"Nobody asked you to take a permanent-resident badge."

She placed a blank one at the counter's other end, away from 林茉. He kept his pen in his pocket and pointed to the witness window.

"Can he stay where he was?"

"Yes. Witnesses aren't available for selection. You're selecting yourself."

"What if I choose wrong?"

She lifted the board's corner.

[SELECTION MARKERS WILL BE RETAINED IN THIS SEGMENT'S RECORD.]

He asked about leaving. She traced the two-minute limit, adding no conditions. The stage door had closed again; its light was dark.

"If I don't choose, will you keep me inside?"

"Time's up, the door opens. No selection means no selection."

Wang Hai read the last line himself.

"You can go in and choose nothing. Heard that?"

"Yes."

Before touching the confirmation field, Lin Mo took him to the window. Both stayed in the corridor while he breathed lightly against the glass, leaving a small white patch.

"That doesn't mean I can't breathe. It means look here."

"What if you really can't?"

He paused.

"I'll hold the frame and crouch. I won't keep doing this. Go find the door; don't wait for another signal."

"You crouched before."

"Yes. You were right to look for the door."

Wang Hai studied him without another reprimand. He tapped twice against a clear patch beside it. Lin Mo tapped twice too, then stopped.

"Don't knock," Wang Hai said. "Mine makes a noise out here. I won't hear yours once you're inside. Even if I could, how would I know who did it?"

Lin Mo lowered his hand. They agreed to watch each other finish before responding with one movement. If unclear, stop. No elaborate code of long and short signals.

Wang Hai slowly bent his index finger. Lin Mo copied him. The second time, Wang Hai stopped halfway, but Lin Mo had already finished.

"Ahead of me again."

"Habit."

"Try being less helpful in there."

This time Lin Mo waited until Wang Hai's finger had straightened before lowering his hand. They switched twice more. Finally Wang Hai stopped frowning. Across the corridor, the recording played on. Neither showed it what they'd learned.

Lin Mo returned to write his name. He paused after the last stroke, letting the ink dry, then pressed the entry field.

"What are you going in for?" Wang Hai asked.

"To see how they put the live image and recorded material together. No permanent identity. No following the image."

"Time's up?"

"I come out."

The woman collected his badge. Wang Hai went to the witness position with the chair leg on his right. The newcomer looked up from the waiting room but stayed seated.

Lin Mo stopped where she could see him.

"I didn't ask your name."

She hesitated.

"He Jing. Jing as in quiet."

"Lin Mo. Mo as in silence."

She looked at the window, then at them.

"Names fit this place."

He didn't smile. Outside the door, he said his name once more. Wang Hai tipped his chin at her and took his place.

The yellow stage light came on.

Lin Mo crossed inside, shoulder cloth brushing the door. Sound disappeared as it closed. He kept his hands down and found his balance where he could see the window.

Two small floor frames lit, one ahead of the other. LIVE and RECORDED. Both empty for now. His old shoulders emerged in the projection, its sleeve poised before the arm rose.

He stayed back. First, Wang Hai.

The narrow window lit. Outside, Wang Hai raised his right hand and slowly bent his index finger, stopping halfway.

Black glass beside the window also lit.

Another Wang Hai stood there, chair leg on his right, injured left hand concealed beneath his sleeve. His right index finger was half bent too.

Both waited for Lin Mo to finish the gesture.
