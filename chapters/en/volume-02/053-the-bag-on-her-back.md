# Chapter 53: The Bag on Her Back

The woman pushed the strap again.

It slipped off one shoulder. A thin cord caught the schoolbag from behind and jerked it upward, tightening the other strap beneath her arm. She rose onto her toes. Her protest broke into a different pitch.

“Don't pull back!” Xiao Lu called.

The narrow rack blocked Lin Mo. He tried moving a wheel, but touching the metal folded his injured wrist inward. Wang Hai braced the front wheel with his right foot.

“Move. She's being lifted.”

“Test tether. Keeps it off the floor.”

“It or her?”

The thin man retreated. Tao kept the case on his counter and pushed the glass door fully open.

“Stay off the delivery track. Walk beside it.”

Xiao Lu went first; Lin Mo followed with the wood. Old Chen stayed behind them, hugging his bag. His tag tapped the viewing receipt with quick, small sounds.

Continuation Bay Two was barely half a room. Spare schoolbags hung on a wall. Beyond the bright line stood a low table marked with positions for needle and pliers. The woman's bag connected to an overhead bar. A pedal beside her feet was slowly rising.

An attendant in black sleeve guards crouched by the table, prying open a cover.

“Loosen the other strap,” Xiao Lu said.

“It won't.”

The tether trapped its lower end. Reaching tightened the strap around the woman's elbow. Lin Mo almost pulled the bag downward.

“Don't push it for her,” Old Chen warned. “It's winding in underneath.”

“I know,” the attendant snapped. He exposed two connecting rods without stopping them. “Where's the accompaniment? It lowers when she hears put it down.”

“Lower it now,” Xiao Lu said.

“Interrupt the carry test, they mark me for a missing segment.”

Whenever the woman's heel lowered, the pedal pushed her up and the overhead bar reeled in more cord. She changed feet twice, trying to escape. Twice the bag rose.

“Don't change feet again,” Lin Mo said.

She flushed, hearing another order to stand correctly.

“I said I'm not playing his daughter!”

“I heard. Your right toe's against the edge. Moving again pulls it higher.”

He planted the wood beside his foot and pointed with his left hand. His shoulder wouldn't let it rise past his chest. Xiao Lu crouched, followed his gesture, and told the woman which foot to keep still.

The attendant threw the cover aside.

“You touch it, I still have to report it.”

Tao passed the long label through the doorway, keeping the case.

“Source owner is here. Number's still being checked. I haven't released it. Stop the drive. Put the missing segment on me.”

The attendant read the bottom, glanced at Old Chen, and moved a short handle beside the rods. A click overhead. The cord stopped rising; the bag remained suspended.

“Spring's still loaded. Don't let go.”

Xiao Lu already supported the bottom. She immediately warned the woman not to give her its full weight. Wang Hai pushed upward with his right palm and winced.

“Can't hold this long.”

Lin Mo nudged a low stool closer with his foot. Its leg caught the raised line. Unable to bend and lift it, he stepped aside for the attendant. By the time the bag rested on the stool, Xiao Lu's fingers were white.

The woman freed her arm. Sweat soaked her clothes. Stepping backward, she caught the pedal with her heel. Lin Mo's warning came too late. It struck her calf and she fell, scraping her left palm across concrete.

She shoved Xiao Lu's reaching hand away.

“Don't touch me!”

Xiao Lu crouched a step away and withdrew. The woman saw the marks across her palm but still wouldn't let her closer.

Lin Mo leaned against the table, knees shaking beneath his skirt. Those few steps had set his shoulder burning. He hadn't caught her. Telling her not to be frightened wouldn't help her stand.

The attendant retrieved the cover, brushing dust onto his trousers.

“Just say it doesn't rub. We can settle the round.”

The woman swallowed.

The prompt board facing Lin Mo read CARRY TEST. He reached left-handed to turn it over; the attendant blocked him. Xiao Lu went around and read the back.

“Thank you, Dad. I know you meant well.”

The woman's face reddened slowly.

“They didn't show me that side coming in.”

They could record it separately, the attendant said. Start with Thank you. Beneath the board, a pickup light glowed green as the woman opened her mouth.

“Don't answer for her,” Lin Mo said.

“Teaching her how to make this easier.”

The woman slapped her right palm against the floor, raising dust. She addressed the green light. She'd tested the left strap. It rubbed below her collarbone. She hadn't said thank you and didn't intend to. Her voice frayed until the last words were barely audible.

Lin Mo nearly asked her to repeat them. Seeing her heaving chest, he asked the attendant whether they'd been recorded instead.

The man glanced at the light and turned the board facedown. He neither confirmed receipt nor deleted what it had captured.

A wall screen lit. White comments crowded the corner.

Why'd the bag stop?

get on with it

Was she nearly hanging just then?

A red name appeared above them.

Seatkeeper: The father hasn't told her to put it down.

Beside the name, a narrow box read REQUESTING VIEWER FOR THIS CONTINUATION. The attendant immediately reached to steady the bag.

The left of the screen showed an empty table. No one sat behind it, but an old male voice leaked from the speaker, telling the child to try the bag.

Old Chen's hand stopped at his bag's mouth.

The accompaniment case remained on Tao's counter. Lin Mo looked back: the thin man's hands were empty.

“Why's there more here?”

The attendant pointed beneath the screen.

“Opening's already in the slot. We're missing the ending.”

The line repeated. On the floor, the woman looked at the empty place as though someone waited there. Covering her left palm with her right, she murmured that she shouldn't have thrown the bag down.

“You didn't,” Xiao Lu said. “I held it. We put it on the stool.”

“He doesn't like me throwing things.”

Old Chen approached the table, stopping outside the line.

“I performed that line. A long time ago.”

She stared. His present face belonged so little to the voice that a faint, angry smile touched her mouth.

“Sending somebody else to persuade me?”

He held his tag beside the screen for the attendant to check, without thrusting it at her.

“I'm not asking you to wear it. That was an old recording. I'm standing here now.”

Blood beaded on her left palm. Dust clung to her knees. Her lips kept moving, as if she were fitting her words to another tone.

Seatkeeper: So the original is still here. Finish it yourself.

The attendant turned the long label toward Old Chen.

“You continue, the original participant can leave. Faster than waiting for checks.”

Old Chen looked at the table. New schoolbag, needle, pliers. Even the chipped paint on the chair resembled the old scene. Lin Mo feared he'd sit, make a choice for the woman that looked almost reasonable.

“Old Chen.”

“I hear you.”

“They didn't say you'd only have to sit.”

Old Chen didn't agree. He asked the woman why she'd come.

“Carry test. Repaired bag. Walk to the end of the line, say where it rubs. I did.”

“Then?”

“They changed the sign. Said wait for the father. Borrowed his part at the beginning, had to give him an answer at the end.”

“Instructions included accompaniment,” the attendant said.

“Accompaniment. Nothing about recognizing somebody.”

Her voice strengthened, though her shoulders stayed drawn in. Old Chen studied his tag. He'd lent his voice and waited for an answer. Now that waiting rested on someone else's shoulders. She couldn't even put the bag down.

“It still counts when I don't perform at that table anymore?”

“The source works, we can use it. Leaving regular sessions doesn't cancel your old number.”

Tao offered no wider explanation.

“I want the source ledger,” Old Chen said.

“Finish this first.”

“I want to know which words of mine you're using to keep her waiting.”

Red covered a white question about her injury.

Seatkeeper: Don't turn this into an argument. I want her to hear the child being good.

The attendant glanced toward Old Chen. Lin Mo understood who the viewer meant. He looked up at the black camera. Before he could swear, his dry throat made him cough.

The woman tried rising on her right hand. Xiao Lu moved the free stool from the table's other side.

“Here?”

She nodded, pulled herself onto her knees using the seat, and kept her left hand closed. Blood smeared between her fingers.

“Duan Qin,” she said suddenly.

Old Chen looked up.

“You keep calling out without asking. My name's Duan Qin. Nobody needs to finish for me.”

Seated, she nodded toward the exit. Could she leave?

“The continuation isn't settled. Stopping the mechanism isn't settlement.”

The small ease in her gaze tightened again as she looked at Old Chen. Lin Mo moved aside on his wood, exposing the label.

“You said the original participant could leave. She finished her walk. Who do you need here to check the number?”

The attendant hesitated. Tao turned the label to the painted-over return field and circled the active number.

“Case is with me. Source owner disputes the connection. She says she never accepted the second half. Keep matching them now, the wrong material doesn't go against me alone.”

The attendant glanced at the screen, then the drive handle. He pulled a small box from beside his feet and removed a narrow plate. The old voice stopped repeating. The empty table stayed onscreen.

Duan Qin waited, then stood. At the door she turned back for the short jacket on a wall peg and draped it over her shoulders.

“Don't make that the child's too.”

Nobody stopped her. The thin man cleared the threshold. Xiao Lu stayed an arm's length away until she crossed safely, then followed.

“Going to wash my hand. Don't need company.”

Duan Qin moved toward the water station along the wall. Two steps later, she stopped.

“Will you give me another thank you?”

Tao made no promise. He turned over the removed plate and showed its participant field, now marked WITHDRAWN.

“I can send this in for comparison. Don't have everything sent out before.”

She stared at the empty table.

“Do I have to stay watching to know whether you change it?”

Lin Mo looked at her closed hand. Blood no longer dripped, but grit remained between her fingers. Before he could suggest washing it, she raised it to inspect for herself.

“Forget it. I'm washing my hand. That doesn't mean I agree.”

“I heard you,” Old Chen said.

She didn't turn back. She disappeared around the corner, leaving a little tracked-in dust inside the bright line.

When Lin Mo looked back, Old Chen's hand had appeared at the edge of the onscreen table. The camera was turning.

ORIGINAL PARTICIPANT WITHDRAWN. SOURCE-OWNER DISPUTE AWAITING REVIEW.

The attendant returned the handle to its slot without replacing the plate. He handed Tao the long label.

“Only this one stopped. Cabinet rejects it, I withdraw the same material again.”

Old Chen stood before the camera, a corner of his shoulder injury visible beyond his collar.

Seatkeeper: You owe the ending. Pay it yourself.

He gathered his tag and viewing receipt.

“She's gone,” he told Lin Mo first.

Then he turned toward the lit screen.

“Now check mine.”
