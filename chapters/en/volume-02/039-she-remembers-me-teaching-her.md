# Chapter 39: She Remembers Me Teaching Her

"He's not a doctor," Lin Mo said.

The woman in short sleeves held the green door, looking from Wang Hai's left hand to the chipped wood.

"I wanted someone who could open a door."

Wang Hai remained wary. "Which door?"

"The one back there." She pointed behind her. "Weren't you outside the whole time?"

An empty paper rack stood inside, with a narrow passage beyond. Lin Mo couldn't see the door she meant. Without leaning in, he asked whether she could breathe now.

She nodded impatiently, then shook her head.

"I'm out now. I couldn't find you before."

"I was helping get someone's hand free." Wang Hai showed her the broken end. "I wasn't watching your door."

Her face darkened as she stared at the fresh chip.

"So what you promised yesterday doesn't count today?"

Lin Mo held back an immediate objection to yesterday. He asked where they'd been, what he'd worn, what he'd said. She answered quickly: beside the glass, gray jacket, find Wang Hai if you couldn't breathe. Asked which hand he'd pointed with, she stopped.

"You expect me to remember that?"

"My right wrist's injured. Some movements are difficult."

She glanced at his hand. "The left, then."

"Don't fill it in. You couldn't remember. Leave it for now."

"You think I'm trying to get something out of you?"

"I remember working at this door. But the papers inside say that experience can be given to someone else."

Her fingers tightened on the door. Unconvinced, she turned over her settlement badge.

"My name's here too. You're saying it's all fake?"

Zhou Ling was on the front. A small table with a date field filled the back. Before making out the date, he saw the fine print beside it.

[ACQUAINTED: YESTERDAY. GUIDING DEMONSTRATOR: LIN MO.]

In the thumbnail below, he held his abdomen while Wang Hai turned toward a door.

Wang Hai pointed with his right hand, stopping short of the paper.

"I know where that was filmed."

He briefly described the recognition segment from entrance to exit, without deciding when Zhou Ling's yesterday had been. She shook her head until he mentioned Lin Mo crouching and the door he couldn't open. Then she brought the badge closer.

"You didn't go in to help?"

"No."

"I remember you did."

Wang Hai lifted his left hand slightly, leaving the gauze in place.

"Sometimes I remember this hand isn't hurt. Still can't hold anything."

She stopped arguing. Her hand dropped without loosening its grip.

Suddenly she pressed the badge facedown against her chest and pointed at Wang Hai.

"Cover your name."

Startled, he covered his factory badge with his right palm. She shut her eyes and could still recite his name. Opening them, she almost smiled, then looked at Lin Mo.

"I didn't learn it just now."

"I believe you remember it," he said.

"Then why say you never taught me?"

His fingers found the hard edge of his review badge. He left it pocketed. She'd had a piece of paper too.

"I need to find out where it came from. I know you can say his name now."

She turned her badge over, abandoning the test. Wang Hai's palm was sweaty when he uncovered his name. He wiped the damp away with his thumb, as if something might disappear if he kept it hidden.

Asked where she'd come from, Zhou Ling pointed beyond the paper rack. The continuous demonstration station. They'd made her follow the person in the glass until she reached the door. Something had tightened around her waist. She'd opened her mouth to call, but couldn't hear herself.

"It said follow someone familiar. I thought someone would at least be waiting."

Lin Mo had no answer. Wang Hai stood before her now. That couldn't make up for those minutes.

She pushed the door wider and came outside. Wang Hai made room without helping her.

"Where do I go now?"

Lin Mo raised a hand toward their route back.

"Tell me. Don't do that movement you taught me."

He lowered his hand. Past the badge rack were waiting benches, he told her. He had someone to find elsewhere.

"Will you teach me again, then?"

"I never taught you that."

Anger showed in her eyes, but more than anger, confusion.

"But I remember you teaching me."

She walked toward the benches without waiting for a comforting explanation.

At Sun Ning's door, Lin Mo announced both their names. She called that it was open. He pushed gently with his left hand, without waiting for her to rise.

Her right forearm rested on a folded coat, her cup on the left.

"How's your arm?"

"Worse than before you grabbed it."

He stopped. She indicated a chair with her chin.

"Sit. I didn't ask you to stand there paying for it."

He sat a little farther away and asked what he could fetch. She wanted the water from the table's other end. The cap was already loose. He put it by her left hand without trying to support her arm too.

"About what I did earlier."

"Go on."

"I mistook you for someone in my memory, waiting for me to lead them. But you let me in. I grabbed you and hurt you. That was me."

She touched her elbow beneath the cloth before answering.

"Next time you remember something, sit down first. Don't make me prove you're wrong again."

He agreed. A wooden chair leg scraped as he shifted back; she still flinched. He saw and offered no further assurances.

They rested until Wang Hai could open his right hand fully. Only then did Lin Mo unfold the narrow slip before him and explain about Zhou Ling and the station.

"You're going again," Sun Ning said.

"I want to see where it turns today into yesterday."

"Then what?"

He'd meant to say finding the source would let him stop it. The water beside her caught his eye.

"First, find out who's identifying people for us there. If they only show me the same door, I'll come back."

Wang Hai frowned. "You haven't read the sign. Don't tell us how easy coming back will be."

Lin Mo folded the slip.

"I'll read it first. If there's a performance, I'll come back and explain."

This time he really did stop at the entrance.

The station stood on the other side of the green door, opening onto the outer corridor. There was no need to pass through the materials passage. A long mirror hung outside, a support rail beside it. Wang Hai read the conditions with him: four minutes, sitting permitted, belongings could stay in clothing.

Below the sample diagram beside the mirror were three separate buttons: beginning, middle, end. Wang Hai pressed the middle one. Only that section lit.

"Can I view mine out here?" Lin Mo asked.

The attendant shook his head. These were test buttons. Personal samples could only be adjusted inside.

Lin Mo didn't ask to escape the cost. He noticed an option to preserve the joins and requested a demonstration. The man pushed aside a slider by the buttons. Narrow black lines separated the three images, each retaining its own number beneath.

"If I leave that on, will what I take away show it too?"

"This session's receipt will. The originals stay unchanged."

Wang Hai held the slider where it was.

"At least I'll be able to tell which piece is which. Don't just tell me you understood it."

Lin Mo tested it. One left finger could move it. Then they returned without taking a badge.

Hearing their names for the second time, Sun Ning glanced at his hands.

"Didn't go in?"

"No. It's a four-minute segment. Physical and cognitive proposals still pass at sixty percent. They can make further proposals during it. No guarantee of withdrawing old footage. I can see how my continuous sample is assembled and take this session's assembly receipt afterward."

He explained where he'd go and what he could and couldn't obtain. She neither dissuaded him nor told him to go without worrying.

"Leave that chair here. Don't make me get up when you return."

He moved the empty chair beside the door, within her left hand's reach. He left hers alone.

Passing the long mirror again, he stopped.

"I'm Lin Mo. I'm a man."

The adult woman in the glass moved her lips with his. Wang Hai looked up from the rail's screws.

"If you remember, save your breath."

"I almost remembered making Zhou Ling a promise."

Wang Hai waited. Lin Mo recalled where they'd actually met until the wood outside the green door returned to the scene. Then he turned to collect his badge.

The short-haired attendant checked the slip's sample number and returned it. Lin Mo wrote his name on a blank badge and asked where his witness could stand. The man indicated the side window and brought a movable low stool to the entrance for him to see.

At Lin Mo's request, he put it by the window. Lin Mo wouldn't have to stand throughout.

He left the wood with Wang Hai. Bracing himself against the outside rail, he untied the broad cloth belt, took up some slack and retied it over his jacket. It took two attempts. Wang Hai waited until his fingers were clear of the knot.

"Too tight?"

Lin Mo breathed in. "All right for now."

The door shut, cutting off sound.

The stool sat below the left window. A rail ran along the wall toward three narrow screens: the green door, himself holding his abdomen before the glass, a faceless outline. Black gaps separated them. Small source numbers scrolled below.

Two steps along the rail, the second image moved behind the first. His recorded self turned, reaching toward the door slot. An unscarred hand joined his sleeve.

Lin Mo stopped.

[CONTINUOUS SELF-DEMONSTRATION.]

The composite walked around the green door and repeated his recent movement, pressing his abdomen. As its back bent, the third outline approached. An assembled Wang Hai stood opposite, seemingly summoned to help.

Zhou Ling remembered someone going inside to support her.

This person really did reach out.

Lin Mo moved closer along the rail to read the source numbers. They shrank into the black joins, leaving a single experience field.

[YESTERDAY. BEFORE SHIFT CHANGE.]

He reached left-handed toward the screen's edge but found no switch. The footage continued. Retreating to where Wang Hai could see, he pointed his left palm at the screen, then toward his eyes.

Wang Hai mimed holding a badge. He thought Lin Mo wanted the slip.

Lin Mo shook his head. He didn't summon him inside. Slowly, he opened his left hand toward the window, then moved it from Wang Hai's face toward the screen.

Watch. Don't finish it for me.

Wang Hai lowered his hand. He couldn't hear, but this time he stopped searching for something to fetch.

Comments crowded the screen.

[Walk doesn't match. Won't join up.]
[Changed the waist. Lower half hasn't caught up.]

[Checking's performing too. Stand up.]

A proposal opened beside the third outline: pelvis, hip and leg contours, coordinated gait, matching continuous-demonstration experiences. Name unchanged. Sixty-percent implementation. Lin Mo used the rail to sit. He wouldn't walk over just to make the footage resemble him.

Approval passed sixty percent.

An ache spread across him beneath the skirt's waistband. His knees drew inward while his heels stayed in their old positions, pulling his inner thighs tight. Releasing the rail, he braced his left palm on the stool and slowly moved one foot outward, then the other.

The weight-bearing points in his pelvis pressed farther apart. Fabric tightened around his hips. The newly tied belt constricted his breathing. He picked the knot loose left-handed without pulling at the join between blue top and skirt. The cloth loosened but stayed around his clothes, clear of the floor.

Wang Hai's right hand struck the window frame. Lin Mo heard nothing. He couldn't lift a hand to answer.

Suddenly he remembered Zhou Ling complaining about her aching legs at shift change.

He remembered fetching her a stool, telling her to stop pushing herself, her thanks after sitting down. A face now occupied that formerly empty passage. A voice too.

But they'd argued outside the door. She hadn't thanked him.

He unclenched his teeth and breathed against the glass. Wang Hai bent closer, waited until Lin Mo looked up, then answered with a short breath elsewhere.

Lin Mo held on to that order of events, trying to separate their argument from the obedient shift change.

His lips moved first.

Zhou-jie.

The familiar address came so easily. He'd never asked her age.
