# Chapter 41: Call Me Old Chen

Wang Hai didn't call a fourth time.

He lowered the wood slightly. Lin Mo's left hand sank with it; only then did he realize he was still leaning on it. The woman in the mirror leaned too, the same white scar visible on her lowered right wrist.

"You know who I'm calling now?"

"Yes."

"Don't rush."

Lin Mo looked at him. Wang Hai wasn't smiling or offering his factory badge as proof. His bandaged hand rested against his belly. The other gripped the wood tightly.

"Me," Lin Mo said again. "But I was waiting for someone else."

"Who?"

The demonstrator inside the green door. He almost said it, then realized that the person who carried stools and talked to Zhou Ling was also Lin Mo in his memory. Neither name was wrong. He'd somehow stepped outside it.

He explained. Wang Hai looked worse.

"I could say mine too. Badge went blank, you told me, I could repeat it. Didn't feel anything to do with me."

"You got it back."

"Mine. Don't go looking for a bucket."

Lin Mo hadn't thought of the bucket until that moment. Now Door 08 appeared. He understood why even Wang Hai's warning had come reluctantly.

"I won't."

He loosened his left fingers. His damp palm stuck to the carving. He knew his name, recognized his ID, knew he was a man. Yet he needed Wang Hai's eyes to connect that me with himself again.

Nearby, someone screwed a cup lid on with a squeak.

"Rest. That bit of wood doesn't look strong enough for two."

A woman sat by the water station beside the mirror: short hair tucked behind her ears, worn cloth shoes beneath a dark skirt. A tin cup rested on her knees, cloth wrapped around its rim. Following her gaze, Lin Mo saw Wang Hai shift the strain onto his right wrist.

He eased his weight at once. Wang Hai braced the wood harder.

"Wait till I say I'm tired."

The woman moved along the bench without coming to help. Lin Mo sat, the skirt pulling at his hips. He separated his knees a little before settling.

"How long were you listening?"

"Since the second call. Thought the first might be for me."

Wang Hai eyed her warily.

"You're Lin Mo too?"

"Chen. Call me Old Chen."

A woman's voice, low and rough. Ink marked her knuckles; a corner was missing from one nail. Noticing Lin Mo's gaze, she rested the lid on her knees.

"Came here a man. Still consider myself one. Old Chen will do."

Wang Hai swallowed whatever he'd been about to call him.

Lin Mo didn't ask how much they'd changed. First he asked whether Old Chen worked here.

"Used to perform. Now I find places to sit. You?"

"I'm not staff."

Too quick. The green door lit in his head. Old Chen didn't make a joke of it.

"Where were you going?"

"Rest area. Someone's waiting."

"Remember that step, then. Stand at a mirror till you look like yourself and your legs will give out first."

Wang Hai repositioned the wood, broken end outward. Its scrape reminded Lin Mo of the chair by Sun Ning's door. That had a history he could follow. She'd asked him to leave it because she didn't want to get up for them again.

He rose and nodded to Old Chen.

"I'll go back first."

Old Chen nodded and blew on his water. No warning that this was Lin Mo's only chance to find him.

At Sun Ning's door, Lin Mo announced Wang Hai's name. His own caught.

"And Lin Mo. Me."

Silence inside.

"It's open."

He pushed left-handed. Wang Hai followed with the wood. The empty chair remained where he'd left it. Sun Ning stayed seated, her gaze traveling from his neck to the shoulder seam that had slipped lower.

"Changed you again?"

"Twice. Same session."

She cradled her arm without reaching for his face.

Settled in his old chair, he asked about her arm. His voice was thinner now. She frowned, as though first needing to find him in it.

"No better. Haven't moved it."

"Enough water?"

"Enough. See to yourself."

He took out booklet and pen, describing the session and his failure to answer his name. At the part about nearly crossing out his notes, she moved the pen farther across the table.

"Show me before you change anything earlier. Might not know if it's right. At least I can ask why."

"Every sentence?"

"Important things about yourself. Don't ask where to put every cup."

Wang Hai clamped his bottle between his knees, started opening it, and stopped.

"This spot doesn't need approval?"

She ignored him. Lin Mo almost smiled. This time no shift-change memory supplied someone smiling too.

He laid out the new receipt, exposing only its three source fields. The fragment stayed in the booklet's separate pocket. Sun Ning read and pushed it back.

"They can make you remember working. Can they make you think we're in your way?"

He looked at her arm.

"I did think Wang Hai shouldn't leave."

Wang Hai offered no gentler explanation. After a pause, Sun Ning moved her cup left.

"If you think I'm blocking you, say so first. Don't move me with your hands again."

"All right."

He didn't add a promise that it couldn't happen again.

Later, Wang Hai mentioned the person by the water station. Was he an attendant? Both shook their heads.

"He says he remembers before," Lin Mo said. "I want to ask how."

"Can you still walk?"

"I'll ask if he'll come," Wang Hai said. "Sit outside. If either of you doesn't want him, never mind."

Sun Ning waited for Lin Mo's nod before agreeing. Wang Hai propped the wood on Lin Mo's left and moved the empty chair within reach before going alone.

Old Chen arrived with his tin cup. Wang Hai fetched a corridor stool, remaining standing. Settling outside, Old Chen noticed Sun Ning's arm and asked only whether he'd block her way.

"Nobody's going anywhere fast."

He shifted the stool a few inches outward.

Lin Mo introduced himself again. The name still felt empty. Old Chen didn't ask him to repeat it.

"The words haven't changed. Why do they sometimes stop being mine?"

Old Chen looked into his cup without offering a reason. From a cloth bag at his skirt, he took a worn badge. Keeping a finger over the channel number, he exposed one completed field.

[BODY MODIFICATION: 100%.]

Lin Mo stared.

"They finished."

"Long ago."

"Your memories?"

"Some remain. Some I only remember having remembered."

He pocketed the badge before it could be asked to prove anything else.

"Fifteen years. Counted the days myself, using the calendar I knew. How long it's been outside, I don't know."

Wang Hai slowly straightened away from the frame.

"Fifteen years here?"

"Never got out."

Lin Mo's fingers left his booklet. He'd wanted to ask how not to forget. Against fifteen years, the question felt terribly small.

"You write every day?"

"Wrote a lot at first. Waking up, after each performance. Once they took the front half. Left holding the back, I couldn't even be sure who 'he' meant."

"Did you find it?"

"No. Tried. Picked up more memories somebody else supplied. I've kept less than I started with."

His open bag revealed a thin booklet, corners repaired with stitches. Lin Mo didn't ask to see it. Old Chen didn't offer his private life as an example.

Sun Ning watched the bag.

"Fifteen years. You'll frighten him worse."

"Frightens me. Can't knock ten off to make myself useful."

Lin Mo pressed his booklet through his clothes. For a moment, seeing one hundred percent, he'd hoped Old Chen would say that finishing meant nothing more could be lost. He hadn't. This person had survived a long time and still had to account for what he couldn't recover.

Wang Hai coughed.

Lin Mo raised his hand at once, thinking he should pass over the receipt. Wang Hai looked at his empty right palm.

"Dry throat."

Lin Mo lowered his hand. Old Chen didn't ask which shift he'd remembered.

"You stopped."

"He told me."

"Remember that part too. Don't write it down later as something you worked out alone."

Wang Hai drank and clamped the bottle between his knees to close it. Lin Mo neither took it nor asked whether he needed help to prove he'd understood. Thirst could end at a drink of water.

Old Chen sipped, frowned, and lowered his cup.

"Cold again."

"Talk slower, it'll get colder," Wang Hai said.

"Wasn't blaming you."

A brief smile. Lin Mo watched the cup before asking, "Who do you remember?"

"My son. Mending his schoolbag. Needle stuck; pulled it with pliers. Made the patch too thick. He came home complaining it rubbed his back. I called him ungrateful."

"Afterward?"

"Can't be sure whether he kept using it. This place gave me a happy morning, him going off to school. I won't add it."

"Because it's too nice?" Sun Ning asked.

Old Chen shook his head.

"Because I remember the argument. Wanting forgiveness doesn't mean I remember him giving it."

Lin Mo thought of Zhou Ling's thanks. He'd never helped her at the green door, yet he even felt guilty about not coming sooner.

"How do you know the argument's real?"

"No sentence guarantees it. Who I told, when something new appeared, I try to keep those apart. Leave gaps when I can't. Gaps feel awful. Your mind wants to finish them."

He indicated the booklet. Lin Mo could keep it on his lap.

"Write what you promised before leaving. Whether you still want to do it."

Lin Mo wrote: Announce my name when I return. Then: Don't make Sun Ning worry.

She saw it.

"Didn't ask you to promise that second one. Can you decide whether I worry?"

His pen stopped. Old Chen didn't rescue him.

"Something you hoped for?"

"Yes."

"Put it elsewhere. Don't squeeze it beside something already said."

He struck through the second sentence lightly and rewrote it below. Sun Ning confirmed the first, then returned her attention to her hand.

"Enough today. Don't test everything I've ever said."

Old Chen nodded.

"Enough. I asked too much too."

Cart wheels scraped past the stool. Old Chen drew in his own feet. Watching him tighten the lid, Lin Mo considered someone wholly changed who still knew what he disliked and could admit uncertainty. That completed badge hadn't answered everything for him.

On a fresh page he wrote: I heard Wang Hai call three times. Answered the third. I want him to call me that now.

He didn't write that he was cured. Resting the pen across the booklet, he waited for his fingers' numbness to ease before putting both away.

As Old Chen left, Lin Mo rose against the chair back, intending to see Old Chen to the door. Sun Ning pointed left-handed at the door.

"Leave it open. Could use some air."

He stopped and left it wide.

Other footsteps stopped outside.

Xiao Lu stood in the corridor with her script. Her gaze moved from Wang Hai to Lin Mo's face, as though trying to identify what had been lost this time. He didn't invite her in.

"Getting water," she said.

Old Chen made room. Lin Mo moved his left hand from chair to frame. Pain caught his shoulder; his body tipped forward.

Xiao Lu's hand was already reaching for his right wrist.

Before touching the scar, she abruptly turned it and caught the gray jacket instead.

"You can't pull there."

Quickly, as though correcting someone reaching beside her.

But nobody else had reached.
