# Chapter 51: The Unfinished Drink

The wood slid another half inch after Lin Mo stopped.

Xiao Lu turned, looking first at his legs. He braced the wood and pointed toward the gray curtain, already hanging still.

“There's a picture on the wall inside. You handing me Sun Ning's bottle. I'm holding the bread paper. Half her chair shows.”

Old Chen turned too late.

“Moving?”

“Didn't see long enough. Didn't hear anything.”

His right hand was gripping the inside of his coat, near the booklet and its hard-won directions. He wanted to lift the curtain. At least see what else hung there.

Xiao Lu shifted her bag.

“Tell Sun Ning first.”

“Yes.”

He let go. They turned away from the wheels, passed the waiting-area sign rack, and found Sun Ning still seated, right arm against her side. Wang Hai had stretched his feet outside the doorway, leaving room for carts.

“Lin Mo. Xiao Lu and Old Chen. We're back.”

Wang Hai drew his feet in and waited. Lin Mo lowered himself onto the chair's edge before handing over the wood, carved end first.

“No new chips.”

“In you or in this?”

“This.”

“Thought so. You're not that reliable yet.”

Lin Mo's laugh caught on a stab of shoulder pain. He rested his right wrist on his thigh. Wang Hai set the wood aside and waited while he pulled the trapped hem from beneath his hip.

After hearing about the water station, Sun Ning asked whether Old Chen had heard the name.

“Just Chen. Xu wrote the child's answer. They've taken the box back.” His grip tightened on the bag, but he went on to give the Original Materials Room reference.

Then Lin Mo described the picture.

Sun Ning withdrew her left hand from beside the bottle.

“Am I in it?”

“Half the chair back. Didn't see your face.”

“Doesn't mean it isn't there.”

“No.”

She looked above the door, then at the bottle. Lin Mo followed her gaze. Lampshade, screws, peeling plaster: everything seemed capable of hiding an eye. He couldn't identify a camera. Tearing down one thing wouldn't prove the others clean.

“I want to sit farther in.”

Wang Hai stood to move her chair. She pressed her left palm against the seat.

“Let me get up first. Don't carry me with it.”

Lin Mo had already reached out. Her shoulder drew back, and he returned his hand to his knee.

“Tell us where.”

“Bring that empty stool over first.”

Xiao Lu put it on her left. Sun Ning pushed against it to stand. Her right arm barely moved; her face went white. Wang Hai turned the original chair toward the inner wall and waited until she was seated before stepping back. The bottle stayed within reach of her left hand.

“Will that keep them from filming?” he asked.

“I don't know,” Lin Mo said.

“Neither do I.” Sun Ning leaned back. “I want to look at the wall. Is that all right?”

“Yes.”

Lin Mo stopped searching overhead. He sat slightly behind her, clear of the space between her and the door. Xiao Lu stood with one hand pressing the script inside her bag before finally sitting.

“That sentence. Was there writing on the picture?”

He knew which sentence she meant.

“Didn't see any. You said it at the water station. The picture was from eating.”

She nodded and took her hand off the script. He didn't mistake that for reassurance. Not hearing sound proved nothing beyond the instant he'd been there.

Old Chen coughed in the doorway. The corner of a loose page slipped out of his damaged bag. He trapped it with his elbow; the cup lid knocked inside. Setting the bag between his feet, he eased the page back himself.

“If you go, I'd like to come.”

“Sit properly first,” Sun Ning said.

He did.

Lin Mo rested his head against the wall and closed his eyes. Soon he opened them. Had Sun Ning answered when he'd announced their names? The more he searched that small blank, the larger it became. Sitting here began to feel like a step he hadn't finished taking.

He asked.

“I heard. You said yourself first, then the others.”

“Afraid I'd missed it.”

“You can say a missed sentence again. Coming back isn't an exam.”

Her left hand gripped her hem whenever the right arm hurt. Instead of assuring her he understood, he asked whether she wanted the stool moved.

“Left a little. Clear of my legs.”

Xiao Lu shifted it. Sun Ning supported her right wrist with her left palm, taking two attempts to settle. Lin Mo stayed seated. He didn't arrange her arm where he thought it ought to go.

“If you don't want to hear about the picture—”

“I do.” She looked up. “Don't see me angry and decide next time you shouldn't worry me.”

He remembered Xiao Lu asking about that at the water station. He didn't turn toward her.

“I'll tell you what happened.”

Two groups passed outside to collect water. His shoulder still ached, but the trembling in his legs stopped. Old Chen loosened the wrapping around his cup. A wet corner fell beside the lid. Xiao Lu didn't help this time; he folded it back himself.

When he finished drinking, Lin Mo proposed visiting the outer desk. Ask about the eating picture, then check the original references for the mending and cup clips.

“Don't have me stand up again to test whether they're filming,” Sun Ning said.

“We'll look at what's already there.”

“If you see my face, tell me. Don't agree to where they can put it.”

He promised. She remained facing the wall, asking for no guarantee that everyone could be protected. That made the weight in his chest heavier. He needed to return with something beyond ignorance without pretending a little meant everything.

Wang Hai agreed to come this time. He passed Lin Mo the wood.

“I can walk to the desk. I'm not carrying anybody back.”

Sun Ning wanted the door left open. Xiao Lu moved the stool clear of the space she'd need to stand alone.

An empty rack now stood beside the curtain. Wang Hai ignored the wheel marks and asked a woman in a gray apron for the outer desk. She pointed down the corridor.

“Don't touch the curtain. Racks come through.”

Lin Mo stayed outside the line and described the picture. She glanced behind the cloth: a sample awaiting sorting. Ask at the desk.

“Is that one still there?”

“The water bottle?”

“My hand,” Xiao Lu said. “He's in it too. We want to see what's already recorded. We won't repeat it.”

The woman studied them, pausing at Lin Mo's wrist. She went inside and turned a low screen toward the passage. They could see without going behind the curtain.

A gray image shook. His face appeared in profile, narrower than the face in his mind, mouth open as though waiting to be fed. Bread paper covered his knees. Xiao Lu extended the bottle.

It touched his fingers. The picture restarted.

No sound.

Before the second loop finished, Wang Hai swore softly.

“Where's the rest? He held it himself to drink.”

The woman turned a knob behind the screen. Back to Xiao Lu collecting the water, forward to their hands meeting. Nothing beyond.

“That's all on this one.”

Lin Mo swallowed. The bread was long gone, yet there he sat, mouth opening over and over. He wanted to raise a bottle now, show someone he could drink. His hand held only wood. He'd nearly supplied another movement for them.

“Can we stop where you pick it up?” he asked Xiao Lu.

“Yes.” She asked the woman herself.

The picture froze. Below the bottle, a crosspiece of Sun Ning's chair showed, with part of her left sleeve at the edge.

Wang Hai bent closer.

“I cleared that space for her bottle. You'd just come back with the bread.”

Xiao Lu pointed to the other side. She'd held his wrapping paper down; when she fetched the water, one corner had curled between his fingers. He remembered pulling at it, afraid of getting paper in his mouth. The fold remained onscreen, the corner still caught.

He watched again, following his own hand. Loose fingers began curling around the bottle. Just before his grip steadied, the picture cut. Paper, fingers, chair: the same sequence, three times.

This sample used their recent meal. He couldn't see what else had been taken from it.

“Put the part where I let go back in,” Xiao Lu said.

“I only sort what arrives.”

“Who can?”

“Whoever has the original.”

As she reached to put the screen away, Lin Mo asked to see the reference label. She pulled a rigid plate halfway out from the back. Its thumbnail stopped at the offered bottle too.

ORIGINAL MATERIALS ROOM / EXCERPT

No filming location. No sound field.

She looked past them and told them to clear the way. Lin Mo moved half a step. The wood caught a brick joint, and his legs weakened. Wang Hai supported his back with his right hand, leaving the injured wrist alone.

When the dizziness passed, the woman was asking which session the excerpt came from.

“Eating.”

“Which meal session?”

“No session. I couldn't swallow. She brought water.”

Her pencil stopped. She rubbed out the character she'd begun, leaving a gray smear.

Old Chen stared at it.

“If someone uses this to say he was fed like this years ago, will your desk tell the difference?”

“They check incoming material. Ask the person about their past.”

“And if he's forgotten?”

She didn't answer. Wheels approached behind them. Old Chen stepped against the wall, hugging his bag. Lin Mo let the question go. It reached far beyond that drink, and she didn't even have the drink's ending.

Wang Hai asked her to switch it off.

“Hasn't been racked yet. Can leave this one facedown.”

She reversed the plate in its slot. The screen went black. A cart bell rang; she turned to lift the curtain, no longer listening to questions about other pictures.

Xiao Lu waited a moment before holding her script against her chest.

“One plate. She turned one over.”

“I saw.”

Wang Hai didn't tell them it was safe to eat now. He moved beside Lin Mo, right arm held across the path of the empty rack until its wheels passed.

“Come on. Standing here won't get the ending back.”

The outer desk was smaller than Lin Mo expected. Behind a wooden barrier, a gray-haired man scraped glue from a plate with a knife. Flakes clung to his cuffs. Each scrape ended with a flick toward a waste dish.

Lin Mo opened the back of his booklet and gave Clothes Mending Four and Rack C, Code Seven. The man told him to keep the page open rather than hand it over, then asked whose cup clip they wanted.

“Mine,” Xiao Lu said. Old Chen indicated his cup.

The man put down the knife and drew up two stools. One had a crooked leg. He pushed it back and found a sound one.

“Sit. To check the original source, I'll need more than a number.”

“What else?”

“The recorded people. Come for someone else, I can only tell you where it was sent.”

Old Chen smoothed his skirt. Eventually he took his old tag from the bag. Lin Mo had seen it before, but never with the fingers moved off its channel number.

“That's me in the mending.”

The man's eyes went from tag to face and back.

Old Chen extended it farther.

“Don't look at me now. Match the number.”

The man entered it on a small keyboard beneath the desk. A narrow strip of paper emerged. Before Old Chen could take it, he circled the rightmost field.

RACK C, CODE SEVEN / IN USE

“They're still using it.”

Lin Mo had worried the old tag might no longer work. Now that fear had found its opposite. The tag had worn pale. The person had changed. Someone was still taking pieces of the performance.

Old Chen's hand hovered beside the paper. After the number he'd thought worn out long ago came a location from today.
