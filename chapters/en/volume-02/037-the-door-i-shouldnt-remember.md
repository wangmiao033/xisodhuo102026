# Chapter 37: The Door I Shouldn't Remember

"The next shift isn't here yet."

The words slipped out after all.

Sun Ning stopped, her hand on the door. Lin Mo stood outside the threshold. Only when her expression changed did he hear what he'd said.

"What shift?"

He wanted to say there wasn't one. But the narrow corridor glowed in his mind. A green door stood at its end, three patches of paint missing near the bottom. He knew to leave his badge facedown, hold the door with his left hand, and let his replacement pass on the right.

Sun Ning stepped right.

He caught her right arm and pulled her behind him.

She gasped, jerking her shoulder back. He let go immediately, but she'd already struck the door. Her left palm braced against the wall. She couldn't straighten.

Wang Hai thrust the chair leg between them.

"Back off."

Lin Mo retreated. His socked heel caught the threshold; he nearly fell. He gripped the frame without reaching for her again.

Sun Ning bit her lip until the pain eased.

"Why did you grab me?"

"I thought you were going through."

"I was letting you in."

She cradled her right arm, moving more slowly now. He stared at his empty hand. The newcomer in his memory followed without questioning his grip, without that sharp gasp.

He remembered leading someone across the threshold. Remembered a smile. The blank face was beginning to resemble Sun Ning; as her features settled into place, even the sound of her striking the door threatened to join it.

He clutched his sleeve until his knuckles whitened. She'd pulled away because he'd hurt her. He held on to that, refusing to explain the smile.

"I'm sorry. I... remembered the wrong person."

"Look at me. Don't tell the floor."

He raised his eyes.

"I know your arm's hurt."

"You didn't just now."

He couldn't argue. She returned to the table and pulled out her chair left-handed, without his help. Once she'd settled, Wang Hai stood the wood where Lin Mo could reach it.

"Do you want to come in now?"

"Yes."

"Then come in. No shifts to change here."

Lin Mo crossed the threshold and sat against a chair back. His body remembered sitting differently: waist tucked in, hands on his knees. He planted both palms on the seat's edges and let the awkwardness stay.

Sun Ning asked where they'd been.

He began with pressing his thumb to the first entry badge. Wang Hai added only that both times, Lin Mo had chosen to enter.

"You were going to look at the entrance."

"After that, I wanted to see inside."

"How many trips should I count on next time you say you're going to look?"

His throat dried. He'd meant to explain how the recorded footage drew people in. That could wait.

"Two. I should've sent Wang Hai back to tell you."

"I didn't come back either," Wang Hai said.

She glanced at his injured hand and let it rest, drawing her cup closer with her left. Her right elbow stayed by the table's edge.

Lin Mo set down his review badge, then immediately turned it facedown.

Wang Hai pressed the table's edge.

"Who's that for?"

Seeing the stamp on its back, Lin Mo withdrew his hand.

"Turned it over again."

Sun Ning looked without taking it. She saw the live field's stamp and the empty field beside it.

"This didn't fix you."

"No. The hand outside's still beckoning."

Wang Hai started pointing to the live field, then stopped. Sun Ning shifted her chair halfway round, bracing a table leg with her left foot to keep her arm clear.

"You brought back a piece of paper. I was waiting for two people."

Lin Mo drew the badge closer. All the way back, he'd planned how to explain the unclaimed footage, as though nothing mattered more tonight. He couldn't even say how late they were. There was no clock outside. He hadn't counted how often she'd opened the door.

She indicated her arm.

"That's enough for today."

He remembered the two pieces of material separated on the narrow workbench. He'd followed that voice calling him brother all this way, then forgotten a living person's injury. He put the badge back between the same blank pages without showing it again.

Wang Hai tried opening the water bottle. Its cap slipped against his right fingers. Lin Mo reached automatically, but Wang Hai clamped it between his knees and unscrewed it himself.

"Don't need anyone taking over my shift."

Lin Mo's hand stopped. Wang Hai drank, screwed the cap back on, and set the bottle at the table's other end.

Sun Ning's mouth twitched.

They sat for a long time. Footsteps passed several times without a knock. In the dark glass beside the table, Lin Mo found his reflection. He silently recited his name and reminded himself he was a man, asking neither of them to listen.

The green door remained.

Eyes closed, he even knew how cool the air felt inside. Every remembered handover went smoothly: badge down, door open, someone taking over. He couldn't recall which had happened before lunch, which on a day his legs hurt. Those practiced afternoons had neither mornings nor nights.

He tried remembering his first arrival. At once his hand rose, opened the door, placed the badge perfectly. Earlier than that? The same beginning.

He opened his eyes, saw the bottle, and shut them. Now the faceless newcomer held a bottle too.

He stopped trying. Things he'd just seen could slip in.

He took the pen from his booklet. Sun Ning's chair scraped back. Without raising his hand for her inspection, he wrote: green door; three bare patches near the bottom; low slot on the right. Finally: badge first, then open door.

"I want these written down."

"Afraid you'll forget?" Wang Hai asked.

"Afraid whatever I see next will feel familiar."

Wang Hai shifted his stool and read.

"I'll look."

Lin Mo raised his head.

"Stay here. I haven't grabbed her. My legs work better too."

Sun Ning pointed left-handed at the wood. This time Wang Hai waited.

"What does he lean on if you take that?"

He propped it beside Lin Mo's chair, reconsidered, then retrieved it and dragged an empty chair close, their backs touching.

"This won't lead you anywhere."

Lin Mo made no joke. He tested the chair back and agreed to wait.

Wang Hai left. Sun Ning slowly uncurled her right hand, the index finger last. Lin Mo almost asked several times whether it hurt more.

"It hurts," she said.

"I haven't asked."

"You're staring. Same thing."

He looked away.

"If I say anything like that again, keep away from me."

"I did. You need to learn not to follow."

He set down the pen. When the door opened, both hands stayed on his thighs.

Wang Hai stood there, still sweating.

"It's there."

"What is?" Sun Ning asked.

"Green door. Three missing patches. One shaped like a tooth."

Lin Mo covered the last line of his notes.

"Which side's the slot?"

"Right. Can't see it until you're in the short corridor, past the badge rack. It's a side passage for carts. I stayed outside the yellow line."

Lin Mo's stomach sank. He'd never taken that passage. Both times he'd left the stage, he'd gone toward the rest area. Wang Hai had been with him.

"Inside?"

"Didn't go in. Says materials transfer. Don't deliver yourself."

Wang Hai laid down a slip from the counter woman without taking Lin Mo's booklet.

[IMPLEMENTED SAMPLES: VERIFY NUMBERS AT THE TRANSFER WINDOW. ORIGINALS MAY NOT BE BORROWED.]

"Asked whether you'd worked there before. She wouldn't answer. Wanted the number first. I said you had the badge. Only then did she give me this. Empty-handed, I could stand outside and look at the door. That was all."

Lin Mo's first badge remained in its slot. He'd brought the second back. He drew it halfway out and turned it over. A tiny number stood beside the live stamp.

"Would she tell anyone that?"

"Didn't ask for anyone else." Wang Hai sat. "No performance this time. Look through the window, stay outside the yellow line. That's what she said."

Sun Ning didn't tell him to go.

He watched her awhile before understanding she wouldn't choose for him either.

"I'll check the number. Only from outside."

She took her left hand off her arm and touched the empty chair.

"I'm staying. And I won't open the door again. Sort out what you're saying before you come in."

"All right."

He waited until his legs stopped shaking. He didn't reach out to her for forgiveness. Before rising, he circled the line in his notes and added: The door is real. I may not have been there.

He crossed out the second sentence. Wrote: I have never been there.

The route he'd walked today with Wang Hai could establish that much.

Back in the short corridor, the recorded hand still beckoned. He Jing sat inside the waiting-room door, her thin badge frayed from turning it over.

"Where now?"

"Checking a sample number."

"You're not guiding people?"

"It's my first time too."

She held her badge up to block the onscreen hand.

"It's getting harder to tell you two apart."

He stopped.

"Which part?"

"That one's waiting to lead people in. You say it's your first time." She lowered the badge. "Which one am I supposed to listen to?"

Its folded corner had worn rough. After all this reading, she still hadn't pressed a fingerprint. His two visits hadn't spared her that decision.

"Look where you want to go. That one can't take you." He pointed at the screen, then lowered his hand. "I haven't found the way out either."

She moved her stool back without asking for promises.

He turned toward the green door and nearly kept going. She watched without following. Wang Hai went ahead to ask directions. Lin Mo waited until the woman actually pointed to the passage on the right.

The green door was narrower than he remembered. The three bare patches matched almost exactly, old wood showing through. He even remembered which way the tooth-shaped flake curled.

Unable to crouch, he bent at the yellow line. Dust lay in the low slot on the right; its bottom sloped inward. Beside it, three iron trays stood behind a transfer window.

His memory told him to drop his badge facedown into the slot.

He gripped it tighter, approached the window, and held it outside the glass. Beyond a vertical bar, the apron man looked up. The same tape covered his chin, red showing at its edges.

The upper pane was fixed. Below it, narrow rails ran through a shallow tray hatch, with a sliding gate at either end. A connecting rod joined them: when one rose, the other fell. Each now left a gap only two fingers high, an iron tray spanning the space between them.

The man recognized him and saw the wood. He didn't open the green door.

Lin Mo indicated the number. The man leaned closer, then reached for a tray. Wang Hai asked something through the fixed pane. The man pointed at his ear, shook his head, then indicated the little sign overhead.

[NO AUDIO INPUT AT THIS WINDOW.]

Lin Mo didn't shout. He signaled wait, showed the name side, then the number again, making clear he wouldn't hand it over. The man nodded, took out a roll of gray paper, and flattened it in the tray.

A green door was drawn on it. Even the missing paint was printed there.

A hand reached beside it. Short nails. No white wrist scar.

The demonstrator's hand in his memory was uninjured too. He'd remembered it as his own.

The man gripped the tray's front rim and slid it forward. His right fingers passed under the outer gate and out beyond it. At the foot of the gray sheet, words came into view.

[SHIFT-CHANGE DEMONSTRATION. FIRST-PERSON ADAPTATION.]

Lin Mo tightened his grip on the badge. Wang Hai cursed softly. He heard but couldn't answer.

The man lifted the paper's edge. Another sheet lay beneath.

Same door. Same hand.

Different name field.

Before Lin Mo could read what was on the second sheet, wheels sounded down the passage. The cart attendant saw the exposed tray, hurried over, and shoved down the outer gate's handle.

The apron man's hand was still on the tray's rim.

Lin Mo reached for him. His palm met solid, cold glass.
