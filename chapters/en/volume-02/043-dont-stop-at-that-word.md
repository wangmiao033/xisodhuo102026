# Chapter 43: Don't Stop at That Word

After the cart passed, Lin Mo still held his jacket hem.

The three-field receipt lay between him and the empty seat. He slipped it behind the review badge and closed his booklet. Where Xiao Lu's cup had stood, a ring of water was sinking into the wood.

Wang Hai rested with his eyes closed, right hand on the wood.

"Stop counting."

"I'm not."

"You breathe in every time you hear footsteps."

Lin Mo slowed his breathing. Soon he was watching the corner again.

"If they stop her—"

"She didn't ask you to follow."

"I know."

Wang Hai opened his eyes.

Lin Mo touched his knee. He wanted to stand, but he also wanted Wang Hai to suggest checking on her. Then following would be something they'd decided together, rather than his refusal to listen.

He admitted this. Wang Hai considered him, then moved the wood to his right.

"Then I won't suggest it."

Lin Mo watched the water ring fade. He stopped looking for a better excuse.

He'd promised to come back and explain before. Xiao Lu should be allowed the same promise. Trusting her to return was harder than following her. He settled the booklet inside his clothes without writing down that she'd been gone too long. Wang Hai closed his eyes again.

First came the clink of a tin cup against a bag. Old Chen rounded the corner with Xiao Lu beside him, script in her arms. Nobody hurried her along.

"I didn't go in," she said. "Didn't confirm anything for you."

Lin Mo made room. She stayed standing, took her cup, and drank before explaining.

Post-session verification occupied a recess in the outer corridor. No entrance to a performance. You could look up your own completed sessions without handing over a recording piece. To hear both sides of a joint session, both people had to come and choose separately. Come alone and you could hear only your own side.

"You said I'd need a settlement badge," she told Old Chen.

"I did, back then. Good thing you asked."

He set down his cup. A damp ring spread around its base.

The attendant had let her search under her own channel for the most recent joint ending. The screen listed Lin Mo and the name from another session. She'd seen Lin Mo's entry but hadn't opened the audio.

"I didn't get the whole thing. We won't know whether both sides are there until you come."

"Another performance?"

"They don't take new sessions there. The sign says viewing only. No reexecution. There's a separate entrance for reenactments."

Her grip tightened on the script. Old Chen added that the old votes wouldn't be carried out again. Whether listening might bring something back, nobody could guarantee.

"You don't have to want this because I do," Xiao Lu said.

"I want to."

Too quick. She studied him.

"All the way through?"

"All the way."

Wang Hai stood and flexed his right hand before offering the wood. Lin Mo waited. Xiao Lu screwed the lid onto her cup and put it away without hurrying them or reaching to support him.

Before leaving, Lin Mo and Wang Hai returned to Sun Ning's door. The others waited at the water station.

Sun Ning heard where they were going.

"Do you want to watch, or are you afraid she won't come back to you afterward?"

Holding the empty chair, Lin Mo thought about it.

"Both."

She let that stand. Her right arm rested on folded clothes; her left drew her cup closer.

"Tell me how far you got when you come back. Don't just say she called you brother again."

"All right."

The four of them followed the outer edge of the waiting entrance. Old Chen reached toward a narrow door, caught himself, and stepped back. Xiao Lu pointed farther along.

"Used to be here?" Lin Mo asked.

"I used to come out this way. Went wrong earlier too."

"Thought you'd been here fifteen years," Wang Hai said.

"Time enough for them to move some stools."

Wang Hai looked down at the wood. His mouth eased a little.

No long mirror at verification. Three low partitions enclosed a table open to the corridor light. Two small speakers, a wide screen, a pause button on either side. Behind it, a man in a brown vest wound cloth around a frayed cable.

Xiao Lu pulled out a stool so Lin Mo could approach without turning. He chose the corridor side. Bracing against the edge, he waited for Wang Hai to withdraw the wood before sitting, careful not to put too much weight on the narrow table.

Above them hung the sign. Beneath viewing only, another line specified that this station held only the images broadcast during the session and the corresponding audio. Unbroadcast material wasn't available here.

Lin Mo read the word broadcast. He didn't announce that they'd found the original footage.

The attendant asked for their channels and gave them separate selectors. Xiao Lu found their session on the right. Lin Mo saw the same pair of numbers on the left. Only after both chose did the screen open.

His neck hadn't yet changed into its present shape. The gray jacket's shoulder seams sat higher. Two microphones stood side by side; the right light was off. His body suddenly felt lighter, as if moving his legs the old way would let him rise.

He kept his feet on the floor.

The attendant indicated pause, then exit. They could stop anytime. Leave the table and it would close. No countdown. No request to repeat their old words.

Wang Hai started outside the partitions.

"You were there," Xiao Lu said. "Will you watch with us?"

He checked with Lin Mo first. At his agreement, Wang Hai stood the wood on his right and took a place at the corner where both could see him. Old Chen stepped back too.

"And you?" Lin Mo asked.

"I'll sit over there. Family business. Don't need to hear every word."

Xiao Lu let him go. He settled on a stool across the corridor, within sight but beyond the lowered speakers' reach.

She pressed play.

It began with their separate confirmations. No skipping straight to the clasped hands. The dark microphone came first, then the seated synthetic face. Lin Mo heard himself asking why they had to put her back on a screen when she was standing right there.

Xiao Lu rubbed the script's cover with her thumb.

Onscreen, she finally came into view beside the seated synthetic version. Xiao Lu raised her right hand, then lowered it. He didn't ask whether she remembered. He waited for the hand to settle before looking back.

When the live two-channel option won, both speakers came on. The dining-table sounds mixed between them made his knees jerk forward. He gripped the stool with his left hand. Wang Hai reached as far as the table corner and stopped.

"You're sitting here," he said.

Lin Mo nodded. Xiao Lu turned the volume down one notch and checked whether he wanted to pause.

Onscreen, he finally asked what she wanted to ask first, then touched his inner pocket, ready to stop her with another piece of evidence.

He moved his present hand to his lap.

The masked figure said she would recognize someone eventually. Lin Mo looked at Xiao Lu. She was watching her recorded self hold the script in front of the synthetic face.

"I was scared."

"You told me."

She shook her head. "I can see my hand shaking. You telling me I was scared isn't the same as seeing it."

He swallowed his explanation.

Onscreen, Wang Hai leaned against the railing. Ask him and he'd say leave. Ask her and listen to her answer.

The man at the corner coughed. Lin Mo saw his thumb picking at the wood beside its splintered end.

"You'll get a splinter."

Wang Hai withdrew his hand.

"Should've tried harder," he said eventually.

Xiao Lu paused. Both speakers fell silent.

"Tried to tell me what?"

He nodded at the screen. "To leave. That's all."

"Now you've seen what happened, you think you should've chosen for me?"

"Didn't say that."

"When you both start regretting it, you make it sound as though I was wrong to stay and ask."

On the frozen screen, Lin Mo was looking back at Wang Hai, waiting for a solution that wouldn't require asking her. He'd done it again beside the water station, with no camera to show him.

"I wished I'd pressed end sooner too," he said.

"Your button alone wouldn't have worked. I can see that."

She pointed through the screen at their separate controls, leaving the real ones untouched.

"I'm not saying that so you'll guess what comforts me."

Wang Hai let go of the wood. His right hand stayed open a moment.

"I'd still tell you to leave."

"You can."

"I'd still worry if you didn't."

"I didn't ask you to be happy about it."

He slowly nodded. He didn't take back his advice or insist he should have brought her down himself.

She resumed. Her recorded voice said she wanted to finish this round of questions. Quietly enough. Beside Lin Mo, she moved the script to the table, leaving both hands free.

When she asked what would happen if she never remembered, he heard how long he'd paused. Onstage, he'd thought his answer was clear. Here he could see himself watching her afterward, waiting for her to promise him a chance.

Xiao Lu turned. He expected a question about whether he still meant what he'd said. She was looking at his shoulder.

"Lean back."

"Nothing to lean on."

She glanced at the low partition. Her hand stopped.

"Then let's pause."

She stopped the recording and moved her stool aside, giving him room to rest his left forearm flat on the table. Wang Hai waited until he'd repositioned his feet before shifting the wood.

"I'm not leaving," Lin Mo said.

"Didn't say you were. Your shoulders are up around your ears."

He breathed out slowly. She waited until his hand stopped trembling before continuing, without turning her help into another question about memory.

At last came the question about whether he'd eaten.

He'd thought he would look at her. Instead, he watched the screen: the tears still gathering, the hope he hadn't let go. Xiao Lu asked about the pill pack, corrected the memory of wiping away ink, told him to hear the parts that were wrong too. Each sentence came through both speakers, the painful half intact.

"I'm afraid you'll be angry," he said.

She turned.

"Seeing how I kept pushing you."

"You still want me to watch?"

"Yes."

She studied him without saying whether she'd be angry.

The proposal field lit up onscreen. He remembered every movement afterward: pointing to the end button, her fingertip stopping beside his white scar before that word.

His right wrist grew hot.

"You can't pull there," Xiao Lu whispered.

He looked at her. She was watching her own hand. She didn't say she'd remembered. She turned her palm up on her lap while, onscreen, that same hand closed around his.

"You're my sister."

His own voice.

Then the word he'd waited for.

He pressed pause.

His left finger barely touched the round button. Silence. The image stopped just before Xiao Lu raised her hand. Her eyes still faced him, no host's smile on her lips.

Wang Hai said nothing. Behind the table, cloth whispered against the damaged cable.

Lin Mo knew what he'd broken. Still, he couldn't move his finger. It had been so long since he'd seen her recognize him this clearly. Here he could keep that moment, without the lights carrying them onward.

The Xiao Lu beside him gently nudged his finger off the button. He let her.

"I want to hear the rest."

"You tell me to let go."

"Then let me hear it."

At last he looked away from the face held forever on the edge of changing.

Xiao Lu rested her hand on play.

"You promised to watch it all with me."
