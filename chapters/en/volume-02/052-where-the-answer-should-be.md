# Chapter 52: Where the Answer Should Be

“Where are they using it?” Old Chen asked.

The man kept the paper and slid his tag back.

“Looking at the old material or asking about the new version?”

“Mine first. Questions afterward.”

Speaking too quickly pulled at Old Chen's neck. He rested his fingers on the counter until the pain eased. He still wanted Rack C, Code Seven: the speaker in Clothes Mending Four, not the child added later.

Lin Mo folded his booklet to leave the reference exposed. The man checked it, then opened a shallow drawer crowded with thin, tape-edged cases.

“Tao. You can't keep calling me Hey.”

“Haven't called you anything yet,” Wang Hai said.

Master Tao kicked the crooked stool against the wall.

“Saved you a word.”

Seated, Lin Mo noticed a wired-glass door behind the barrier. Figures carrying racks crossed a narrow passage beyond it. A low slot beneath the door joined the delivery track under the counter.

Tao selected a case marked Clothes Mending Four. Leaving the old label intact, he turned it over for Old Chen to check the channel number.

“Yes.” Then Old Chen leaned closer and read it digit by digit. A scuffed character stopped him. He compared his tag.

“Half of this is still here. Not that other one.”

Tao angled the lamp, agreed, and rewrote the character clearly. Only then did Old Chen exhale, as though the tag had finally connected to something real.

The cup was harder. Lin Mo had a source, no complete reference.

“Don't substitute the mending number. Who held the cup? Who reached for it?”

Xiao Lu identified herself. Old Chen raised his wrapped cup without putting it in the slot.

“Keep that. Couldn't drink from it if you handed it over.”

Old Chen lowered it to the bag's mouth. After hearing about the wet cloth, neck injury, and cut to the yellow bowl, Tao searched another tray. Out came Xu's returned plate, still marked NO CONTINUATION.

“I want to see after I let go,” Xiao Lu said.

“Which first?”

Old Chen stared at the case. His lips moved without a sound.

“Mending,” Xiao Lu said, putting her script away. “My hands could use a rest.”

Tao fitted a polished shield beside the screen, leaving passersby a view of its black back. Could the others watch? Old Chen looked at Lin Mo and Xiao Lu before agreeing. Wang Hai remained behind the stools.

“I'll ask to stop where I need to.”

Tao moved the stop button closer and took his hand away.

A man appeared, bent over a table.

Lin Mo noticed the hands first: broader, knuckles prominent, the needle always angling outward. A very new schoolbag lay beside them. After two attempts to push the needle through, the man used pliers to pull it out. The thread went taut.

Old Chen inhaled.

“Needle was too thick that time.”

The sound came only when Tao raised the volume. The man swore at the material. His low voice resembled the one in Xu's box. Beside Lin Mo, Old Chen touched his throat.

Lin Mo knew what his palm would find. Since his own neck had changed, he sometimes still swallowed expecting a vanished prominence to catch his collar.

“Put it on. See if it scratches,” the man told the other side of the table.

The chair was empty. Someone offscreen told him to look lower. He repeated the line, eyes lowered. This time a child answered that it didn't.

Old Chen pressed stop. The empty chair remained.

“When I heard it, it felt like someone was sitting there.”

Nobody supplied an answer. Xiao Lu stayed where he could see her, without moving into the child's place onscreen. Wang Hai drew his feet back to leave him room to stand.

Eventually Old Chen let it play.

Needle, schoolbag, the child's answer. A few seconds later, the man stopped acting. He put down the pliers and asked someone offscreen whether they'd read the pages he'd given them.

A voice told him not to rush the next part.

“You can at least give me the name first, can't you?”

Lin Mo leaned forward.

The picture ended.

No child. No answer from offscreen. Half of Old Chen's former face remained, turned aside, mouth just closed, eyes still waiting.

“Farther,” Old Chen said.

Tao moved a slider. Black changed back to that bright half-face. Farther along, a small end light came on.

“I asked. He must have answered.”

“This one ends here.”

“But I remember someone agreeing.”

Lin Mo wanted to say the answer might be elsewhere. Then he remembered the single Chen he'd heard from the box before the cough obscured what followed. He asked whether Tao had a connecting piece.

Tao removed the case. A patch of black sealant covered the connection at its trailing edge, distinct from the yellowed tape.

“Beyond the seal, you need the original junction. Can't get that here.”

He pointed to the end label.

ANSWER SEGMENT STORED SEPARATELY. SEE SOURCE LEDGER FOR JUNCTION.

Old Chen touched the corner. Tao held it down, preventing him from taking it. Neither hand pressed harder, but the red mark on Old Chen's neck deepened.

“I won't open it. I want to see exactly where this one stops.”

Tao loosened his hold, supporting the other end beneath the lamp. Old Chen left the sealant alone. His fingertip followed the hard edge to the end and returned to the label.

Could they write it down?

Lin Mo waited for his nod before adding a line beneath the references. His right wrist hurt against the counter, so he held it clear and wrote slowly. Xiao Lu pushed the booklet half an inch closer without turning the page.

“For now, don't count that promise as something I heard,” Old Chen said.

Lin Mo looked up.

“Don't write that it never happened either. Perhaps he said something else and I remembered it that way.”

“Only writing where this piece ends.”

Listening to the pen, Old Chen said the notebook's front half hadn't originally been so thin. Its first page sprang shut. He'd weighted it with an enamel dish; later they'd made a prop of that too.

“I kept asking for the paper. They showed me things on the table. Asked if they looked right.”

Lin Mo stopped writing without adding that.

“Which do you want back now?”

“Paper. The name. Just now, I wanted my face.” Old Chen touched the cup lid. “All of them. Get close, sometimes I forget which I came for.”

Lin Mo had been comparing the old brow and chin to the person beside him, as though a closer inspection could better prove his past. Old Chen turned the shield aside. Light withdrew from his face.

“Enough. Finish that line and put the pen away. Don't wear out your hand on me.”

Tao reached for the case. Old Chen looked at the cup plate.

“She hasn't had her turn.”

“I know. You're still holding this.”

Old Chen looked down and withdrew his hand. Embarrassed, he lifted the bag to his lap, found it too heavy, and lowered it again.

Tao covered the yellow-bowl thumbnail and followed its return label to a wider source plate. He connected one section: surrounding movements survived there, but the tray held no other parts.

Xiao Lu and Old Chen agreed to watch.

Heavy cloth lay beside a doorway. Half of Lin Mo's knee appeared against the wall, then the tin cup. Xiao Lu moved the wet wrapping. Her thumb paused in empty air.

Before, that moment had cut to the bowl. Now her hand kept withdrawing, curled into a fist, and opened.

Old Chen held the cup throughout. He lowered his head to steady it. Xiao Lu returned the lid and drew her hand entirely back. Cut bag strap lay in a small heap on the ground.

No voice came from the narrow picture. Tao raised the sound, producing only a low hiss.

“If it's not there, leave it,” Xiao Lu said.

She asked to go back to the release and watched again.

“That's the ending I want kept.”

“Taking it where?”

“Nowhere for a performance. If someone uses the first part again, let them know the same section shows him holding it himself.”

“I can clip this ending to this one. Can't recall everything that's gone out.”

“Start with this one.”

Tao fastened the short clip and source plate's labels together with a broad clip, removing the yellow-bowl plate. Xiao Lu watched him finish before settling her empty hands on her thighs. She didn't have to reach into the picture to prove she'd let go.

A bell sounded behind the glass.

Tao put the paired plates in the returns tray and opened the door. A tall, thin man waited with a narrow rack.

“Bay Two's waiting for the mending accompaniment. Why are we a rack short?”

“Viewing request. I'll put it back.”

The man stayed, front wheels against the threshold, catches rattling. Tao bent to move a wheel; the man braced it with his knee. The person at Bay Two had waited so long she wouldn't wear the schoolbag. Much later, and someone would have to persuade her all over again.

“Let her take it off,” Wang Hai said.

“Tell them. I deliver a full load.”

Lin Mo tried to rise. His knee weakened and he sat again. Wang Hai reached, but Lin Mo shook his head, drew the wood closer, and planted his feet. Beyond the glass a woman asked how much longer. He couldn't hear the reply.

Old Chen stood with a hand on the counter.

“Whose voice are they using?”

The man reached for the case without answering. Old Chen didn't stop his hand. He placed his old tag beside it.

“This person's?”

Tao glanced at the tag and told the man to wait. He drew a twice-folded label from beneath the case. The session title had faced outward. Unfolded, it revealed more lines, ending in a destination.

CONTINUATION BAY TWO

“Today?” Lin Mo asked.

“The person's already in position. They're asking because we're short. You want it stopped, don't put a missing item on me.”

Tao blocked the reaching fingers with the back of his hand.

“Label's not checked yet.”

He promised no cancellation. Sitting again, he flattened the strip beneath glass.

“This leaves my counter, I can't send it incomplete. If the original number doesn't match, I need the exact field recorded.”

Old Chen's hand was shaking. Lin Mo remembered him before the box closed, knowing the answer might be invented, still waiting for the last sentence. This time there were footsteps beyond the door. A living person coughing.

“Where's the source ledger?”

Tao pointed inward, along the low track.

“Red-bordered cabinet handles original junctions. Ask about your own old number. This viewing receipt only proves you came. It won't open the cabinet.”

He tore off the receipt. It carried Clothes Mending Four, Rack C, Code Seven, and a mark showing this piece ended after the question about the name. No answer had been supplied. The cup ending stayed separately clipped.

Wang Hai pointed from behind Lin Mo.

“Why's that painted over?”

A return field sat above the destination. Black paint covered its middle box, leaving tiny lettering beside it. Tao moved the lamp.

RETURN TO ORIGINAL OWNER: WITHDRAWAL BARRED

“Who's the original owner?” Old Chen asked.

Tao didn't answer, but left the strip beneath the glass. Old Chen gathered his tag and viewing receipt in one hand, gripping the damaged bag's short mouth with the other.

The glass door remained open. At the passage's far end, an adult woman wearing a schoolbag was called toward a bright line. She pushed a shoulder strap off. Someone told her to put it back.

“Wait for the father's line. Rack C, Code Seven arrives, you continue.”

She looked toward the open door. Her lips moved.

“But I didn't come looking for a dad.”
