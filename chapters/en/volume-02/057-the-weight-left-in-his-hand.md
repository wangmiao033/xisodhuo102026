# Chapter 57: The Weight Left in His Hand

Lin Mo planted his right foot on the first step.

He couldn't brace with his left arm or trust his right wrist. Pressing his back against the low beam, he inched upward. The black shutter still hung behind him. Old Chen didn't hurry him; each breath sounded louder through the glass.

“Arm all the way out?”

“Out. Turn.”

Old Chen turned at once.

The shutter slammed home, cutting off the inner light. The isolation grilles stayed down. Old Chen stepped backward. His heel scraped the floor, but his body remained where it had been. He looked down and tried again. His skirt moved; his heel seemed sunk into the sole.

Lin Mo reached level ground. His knee buckled into the entrance wall. He found the wood and braced on its carved end to keep himself upright.

“Open the grille.”

Shao was already pulling the cabinet's connecting handle. The strip stopped. So did the white frame at the track's end. The source-owner light stayed on.

She pulled again, then removed the loosened catch entirely and threw it down. The last rotating sound stopped.

“Drive's disconnected.”

Old Chen watched his feet.

“I haven't moved.”

He gripped the crossbar with both hands and bent his knees, trying to pull free. His shoes creased. A thin bright edge appeared above his ankles. Lin Mo mistook it for a reflection until he bent and saw the white footprints through it.

“Don't pull up!” Shao shouted.

Old Chen stopped, afraid to lower his weight again. Suspended there, his collar rubbed his shoulder injury open. He couldn't hold back a cry.

The red name rose to the screen's top.

Seatkeeper: Execute this claim. Selection stands.

A single boxed line followed.

PHYSICAL SEVERANCE OF CURRENT ORIGINAL OWNER IN PROGRESS.

Lin Mo reached around the cabinet for a power cable. Shao blocked the one he indicated: the reading drive, already disconnected. She wouldn't let him believe pulling out more would unplug the claim.

“Where do we cancel it?”

She pressed the handle that ended comparison. Something sounded inside his former recess. His reader's name disappeared; Old Chen's source number remained outside. She pointed, keeping her hand on the handle.

“Reader's withdrawn.”

For an instant, seeing his name vanish made Lin Mo think one more person might leave. He reached for the other side. Solid iron. No second withdrawal handle for Old Chen.

“Before it locked, he could have stepped away.”

Old Chen heard. Still trying to move his feet, he had no strength left to blame anyone.

Shao indicated the requester on the screen, then crouched and pried a cover from the grille's base.

“Watch my hands. Release the barrier first.”

Old Chen called Seatkeeper. Quietly the first time; his voice broke on the second. He'd stop looking. Cancel this one. He could go back and compare something else.

No red answer.

Unable to wait, he said Lin Mo had reached inside. Then he looked straight at him.

“I'm not telling him to take you.”

“I heard.”

“But I said it.”

His mouth shook. He wanted to take it back and desperately wanted a reason he could leave. Lin Mo didn't tell him to be brave, or claim he'd choose the same gap again. He'd reached through. Old Chen had stayed until the lock took him. Both had happened.

Was this an act? one comment asked. Stop, said another. A third complained it was taking too long. Lin Mo extended the wood. Its end struck the grille with a hard, hollow sound. He wanted to speak to those words, but Old Chen called his name.

“Come here.”

He moved closer. A hand fitted between the uprights. Old Chen couldn't take a step; he turned sideways, shifting his upper body along the crossbar.

“Take this.”

Lin Mo passed the wood horizontally through. Old Chen gripped its end and tried to pull his waist outward. Lin Mo braced the other end in the wall's corner. Pain tightened his whole left shoulder. His legs shook. Old Chen didn't move out, but the bright edge beneath his skirt abruptly rose.

He let go.

“Don't pull! It hurts!”

Lin Mo released the pressure immediately. The wood dropped half an inch onto his stockinged toe. He couldn't tell whether he'd helped Old Chen stay or dragged him deeper into that emptiness.

Below, Shao cursed a rusted fitting and told him to call the outer desk.

He shouted for Xiao Lu and Master Tao: someone was locked at the red-bordered cabinet. He didn't just shout run, or replace the location with a cry for help.

Old Chen kept one hand on the crossbar. The other reached toward the shelf beyond the footprints. He picked up his tag. The receipt stuck against his palm; he couldn't gather them together.

“Wrong person, maybe?”

Lin Mo looked at the glass. Same source number. Old Chen's face still in the frame.

He couldn't say yes.

Old Chen understood his silence. Looking down, he worked the receipt behind the tag with a fingernail.

“Take them. Check again.”

He passed both through the bars. Lin Mo's right hand barely caught them; the tag nearly slipped through his fingers. He pressed them against his chest with his palm's heel, then tucked them inside his jacket, in front of his booklet.

“It's your number.”

“I know.”

Old Chen shook his head, dissatisfied with his own answer. He held out his empty hand.

“Pull me. Not like before.”

Lin Mo set down the wood and took his hand with his right. Warm fingers, slippery with sweat. A chipped nail pressed his thumb. Closer than the wood, but there was nowhere to exchange his hurting body for another, no way out that needed no force.

“I'm holding on.”

Footsteps stumbled outside. Xiao Lu entered first and stopped at the sight of Old Chen. Tao followed, still holding his glue-scraping knife.

“Don't cut cables! Turn this!” Shao shouted.

He threw the knife onto the empty ladder and crouched to take the exposed spindle. Together they turned it. The exit-side grille rose a little. Xiao Lu bent to lift its bottom edge; Shao stopped her and had her pass the loose catch from the floor instead.

By knee height, Old Chen's upper body projected half a step beyond the grille. Lin Mo retreated to give him somewhere to fall.

The rest of that step never came.

Dark fabric below Old Chen's waist seemed misaligned. On the left it still followed his body. On the right, gray tiles showed through. He leaned forward, but where his legs had been remained inside the footprints. Part of his shadow was missing.

Lin Mo's hand jerked downward.

He fell to his knees without releasing Old Chen. Xiao Lu supported the outstretched forearm. There was weight beneath her palms, then a sensation like fine sand sinking inward. Startled, she opened her hands and immediately closed them again.

Old Chen looked at them. Tears suddenly fell.

“I counted fifteen years. I don't want it to end with today.”

Lin Mo couldn't hear clearly and asked him to repeat it. He shook his head, panting as though he'd run a great distance. He kept saying he wanted to get out.

“I know.”

“You don't. Don't tell me you know.”

Lin Mo closed his mouth. He didn't know how each of those fifteen years had passed, or what losing weight from the soles upward felt like. Old Chen wouldn't look down through the pain, yet still strained toward the open grille.

Tao had raised it fully. Shao put down the loose catch and reached behind Old Chen's back. Her hand made him sway forward, but his shoulders stayed within the glass's boundary.

She stopped with her hand in the air.

“Nothing underneath is pinning him.”

Xiao Lu crouched and looked beneath his shoes. Could he move his knee forward without lifting his foot? He tried. Half his body leaned toward them; below the knee, his extended leg broke into a thin shadow through which the floor showed. He snatched it back.

“Don't try that again.”

She stopped. Having thought of another way didn't entitle her to make him keep trying. She moved her hands to the elbow she could still feel. Did supporting it hurt? He first shook his head, then said yes. She eased her grip. Afterward, she couldn't find the same boundary again.

Lin Mo saw it too. Open grille. Stopped track. No cord or plate beneath the shoes, which were thinning from the heels. They'd moved every mechanism he could see. Old Chen still couldn't leave.

“Withdraw it!” Xiao Lu shouted at the screen.

Seatkeeper answered at last.

Seatkeeper: Source owner in breach delivered for enforcement. I won't withdraw.

An obscenity rose in Lin Mo's throat. The name in the red frame had no face, no hand they could catch.

“My bag?” Old Chen asked suddenly.

“With Wang Hai. Cup, notebook. All there.”

“Tell him not to look through it.”

“He hasn't.”

“How do you know?”

Lin Mo stopped.

“I'll tell him.”

“And that answer. Did he ever answer me?”

The promise to find his child's name. Beyond the partition, everything was dark now. Lin Mo remembered the stroke he'd almost turned into missing.

“Not entered with the original segment. That's all I saw.”

Old Chen searched his face for something withheld. Lin Mo waited, refusing to fill the silence with perhaps it's on the original paper. They hadn't even finished this small inquiry for him.

Old Chen nodded, then shook his head hard. There was plainly more he wanted to say. His lips moved. Chen came first, followed by a long blank.

“The child's name?” Lin Mo heard himself ask.

Old Chen looked almost angry.

“I can't remember.”

No second word followed. The preview's opening notes sounded. Tao crossed to the cabinet and held down the sound button before the voice that had apologized for the child could enter.

It didn't comfort Old Chen. With his remaining strength, he gripped Lin Mo. His nail scraped the old wrist scar. Lin Mo gasped without letting go.

“Don't take me yet.”

The plea reached nobody who could stop the execution.

His hand grew lighter. Lin Mo followed forward, fingers closing tighter. First he could still feel the joints; then his thumb pressed his own forefinger. Warmth remained in his palm. Even the sweat was still wet.

Xiao Lu's hands fell empty too.

Old Chen's head dipped. Lin Mo instinctively raised his left arm to catch it, pulling open the two bloody scrapes. No weight met him. The face, still frightened, thinned away from the glass. His voice broke in an unfinished breath.

Lin Mo remained kneeling, hand held where theirs had met.

No shoes fell. No clothes. No body they could carry back.

The source number went dark.

BODY REMOVAL COMPLETE. ORIGINAL-OWNER NUMBER DEACTIVATED. NO RETURN TO THE STAGE.

Shao lowered the hand supporting his back. Tao kept pressing the sound button, letting no child's answer fill the quiet. The white frame knocked again at the track's end. Nobody went to receive it.

Lin Mo called Old Chen.

No reply. He searched the small patch of tile, then bent low to peer beneath the track. Dust, screws, cabinet legs. What he couldn't reach remained out of reach; not seeing behind it didn't let him claim somebody was hidden there.

“Where did he go?”

Shao watched the deactivated number without answering. Could they reconnect the body they'd just lost? Her finger lingered beside NO RETURN TO THE STAGE, then dropped.

Xiao Lu crouched beside him. Thinking she meant to help him up, he shook his head. She didn't touch him. She retrieved the wood that had slid down the wall and placed it within reach of his left hand.

“Master Tao says we need to clear the track. I heard him. I don't want to go either.”

Lin Mo lowered his head and felt the hard tag inside his jacket.

They'd both held it. With a grille between them, they'd still been able to speak. Now the tag remained, and the viewing receipt. The only weight in his hand was his own.
