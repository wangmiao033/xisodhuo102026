# Chapter 59: Let Them See Zero

Only after the water carrier passed for the second time did Lin Mo reach for the wood again.

His forearm had stopped dripping, though the sleeve still stuck. He left it alone and planted his feet. Pain caught in his shoulder as he rose, sending him back into the chair. Wang Hai watched without jumping up.

“Trying again?”

“When this passes.”

He waited. Outside, Xiao Lu was flattening a curled corner of her script with her palm. Her bag rested at her feet. Old Chen's broken bag stood on the other side of the threshold. Neither had been touched.

When Lin Mo asked about the Start Broadcast page, she glanced first at his legs.

“I know the self-directed entrance. The page they gave me might not be the same as yours.”

“Let's look at mine.”

“I'll take you to the entrance. That's all.”

He agreed and told Sun Ning and Wang Hai. Sun Ning said he wasn't to finish looking and immediately start a broadcast. If he intended to go somewhere new, come back and explain. Wang Hai would stay again. Take the wood, but don't try prying open another door.

“It didn't open the last one,” Lin Mo said.

“I wasn't praising it.”

His mouth moved without managing a smile. This time he planted each foot firmly before rising. The wood steadied his first uncertain moment. Xiao Lu waited until he reached the doorway, then picked up her bag.

They turned away from the Original Materials Room. Beside the waiting-area sign rack ran a wider passage; he usually saw more people coming out than going in. Xiao Lu followed the wall past a KEEP CLEAR DURING SCENE CHANGES sign and pointed toward an open low platform.

Two headings marked the entrance: GUIDED BEGINNER SESSIONS, with awaiting segment assignment underneath, and PUBLIC SELF-DIRECTED SESSIONS. Program titles scrolled up a small screen beside the second.

Some had zero viewers, others a handful. A red title promised Just One Last Nod. Beneath it, someone waited under a light, face partly hidden. Lin Mo stopped. Don't take me yet. His fingers tightened around the wood.

Xiao Lu didn't steer him away from the picture.

“Still want to look at the entrance?”

“Yes.”

Nobody here collected old tags. An attendant wearing gray elbow guards pushed a mirror rig past and told them to shift a stool out of the wheels' path. Lin Mo moved aside. Asked where to find the broadcast page, the attendant pointed to a short arm projecting from the wall.

“Bring up your current channel yourself. I just deliver the equipment.”

Lin Mo sat on the fixed stool beside the arm, its screen level with his chest. The page requested his channel number. He found the earlier entry in his booklet, entered each digit and checked the displayed name and performer identity.

His channel. His alone. No source number for Old Chen, no cabinet door opening beside it.

He closed the booklet on his knees. Xiao Lu stood behind and to one side, able to see without blocking him from rising.

“Here.”

Departure Application for This Session was near the bottom of the page, lower than he'd expected, beside instructions for storing belongings. He opened its narrow collapsed panel. The first line identified the applicant by his channel. Below: BROADCAST NOT STARTED. COUNTING INACTIVE.

He didn't submit it. Enlarging the preview, he found the words he'd read behind the cabinet.

DURING THIS LIVESTREAM, 100,000 ELIGIBLE VIEWERS MUST SIMULTANEOUSLY MAINTAIN THEIR SELECTION OF FREEDOM.

No gap waited to catch his hand now. His fingertip rested beside the number, but his eyes stung so badly he nearly lost the last word. Old Chen had waited all that time and never got to sit and listen. This time Lin Mo even had a seat.

“Can you scroll down?” Xiao Lu asked.

“Yes.”

She left the controls to him. Currently present, qualified to vote, leaving or withdrawing the choice reduced the count. Repeated selections added nothing; nothing carried between sessions. He remained the applicant. Performers and accompanying participants still didn't count. An easier page to open made none of the conditions easier.

He held his booklet beside the screen, the cramped word he'd rewritten at the cabinet in view. He could check the additions from memory too. Both the route and the verification scope were right. Beside them he wrote: Seen on my own Start Broadcast page.

“This page is what I've seen,” Xiao Lu said.

He nodded. She couldn't replace the witness who was gone.

A demonstration sat below the rules. They read its explanation first: simulated identities, no actual viewers, no votes generated. He pressed Start. Three gray figures appeared. Two bore FREEDOM labels; the count was two.

He dragged one of those two out of the session box. The number dropped to one. Its FREEDOM label went dark.

He'd thought he understood already. Watching the space empty still stopped his finger. He closed the demonstration rather than keep playing with figures that couldn't hurt. The real count beside it remained zero.

“Someone can choose it and still get tired of waiting,” she said.

“I have to make them want to stay.”

“They might stay to watch you lose.”

On the scrolling entrance screen, somebody was crying. The viewer count changed, then fell back.

“I know. More people watching doesn't mean more votes.”

He returned to his application preview. Beside zero, counting remained inactive. Another part of the page offered the audience view. He opened it: a dark stage awaiting its start, with proposals, comments and session options along the bottom.

FREEDOM was hidden in a collapsed options panel.

Tapping it brought up a preview warning: this action submitted no vote. PERFORMER still appeared beside his identity. Seeing the audience's screen hadn't made him a viewer.

“Can I put it outside that panel?”

The attendant, returning the mirror rig to the passage edge, glanced over.

“You can adjust positions in the preview. Don't cover the proposal panel.”

He neither made the selection nor promised to change any rule. Lin Mo searched until he managed to move the FREEDOM voting option to the lower left. It couldn't overlap audience proposals and remained small, but no longer needed unfolding. He took two steps away in the preview. The camera followed; zero stayed in place.

Xiao Lu moved into his former position.

“Am I in it?”

Her sleeve showed at the right edge. He narrowed the frame until she disappeared. She checked her position and moved another step clear.

“Tell me where you're filming first when it's running too.”

“I will.”

He saved the option's position in the session layout. BROADCAST NOT STARTED remained above it. There were no real votes. Moving an image hadn't delivered a single viewer.

Were they using the mirror rig? the attendant wanted to know. Otherwise, clear the way; somebody else needed it. Three narrow mirrors stood on a low wheeled frame. The side mirrors pivoted, with foot brakes underneath.

“What kind of session is it for?”

“Your choice. Mirrors, low platform, stool. Available equipment's listed. Don't use the rig as a ladder.”

Lin Mo returned to the venue preview. One arrangement offered a fixed seat; another, the movable rig and low platform. A public self-directed session allowed him to arrange these during preparation. The performance boundary and central proposal screen remained fixed.

“Can I try walking through it first?”

The attendant looked at his wood, wheeled the rig onto an empty patch and locked both sides. This was a trial arrangement on the waiting-area side. Beyond the white line ahead lay the formal performance area. No broadcast needed to start for this, and he couldn't roll equipment into other people's paths.

Lin Mo rose and went behind the rig. He turned with his old stride, but his hip and knee failed to follow the angle his memory supplied. His balance shifted first. His left sleeve brushed a mirror corner; pain in the scrapes sent him lurching the other way.

Xiao Lu reached out, stopping when he caught himself on the wood.

Once steady, he tried pushing the rig a little farther out. The locked wheels didn't move. Release the brakes first, the attendant said. Lin Mo couldn't crouch; Xiao Lu asked if she could press them.

“The right one. I can manage the left.”

Together they freed the frame. He moved it outward, had the attendant lock it again, and tried the walk once more. This time his hip cleared. The wood could land without threading between the wheels. Only after measuring the space his body actually needed did he ask Xiao Lu to step outside the rig.

His face came back from the mirror. Torn gray clothing, a neck slimmer than in the old photograph, fabric lifting over his chest with each breath. He knew the body he had now. He also knew that gaze was still waiting for him to accept an identity somebody else had chosen.

He left the reflected face untouched.

The attendant brought a low stool beside the trial platform. Lin Mo put his wood on the raised edge, intending to step up with its support. The broken end skidded on the smooth surface. He drew his foot back at once.

“Not that side,” Xiao Lu said.

She found the short equipment ramp on the other side. Its slope was gentle but only wide enough to wheel the rig straight up, with no handrail. He tried it slowly. Friction warmed his stockings against the ridged surface. At the platform's edge he stopped, unwilling to throw his weight forward to cover the last step.

The attendant moved the stool closer. Lin Mo sat with his hip clenched in pain, pulled his hem clear and reset his feet. Xiao Lu waited for his breathing to settle before asking whether he still wanted the platform.

“Yes. Mirrors on level ground, stool by the ramp. If I can't get up, I'll sit here.”

Was he sure he didn't want the central seat? It faced the main camera. No walking required; his face would stay visible throughout. From below, Lin Mo could see how much easier it would be. Then he imagined that chair holding him before the lens all session and shook his head.

“Not the fixed seat.”

Xiao Lu looked at him.

“I don't want another family table arranged for me, waiting for them to say who I should thank.”

“They can change you while you're walking.”

“I know. I want room to turn a mirror away.”

He pushed himself up from the stool and pivoted a side mirror inward. His face retreated. Old sheet metal faced the camera, repair welds clear along its edge. The attendant told him not to obstruct the main screen; he stopped while it remained visible.

Xiao Lu checked the preview. Half mirror back, half Lin Mo.

“That's what you'll show them?”

“That I can turn it away. They want my face, they can look at me.”

“What if they're bored?”

He studied the metal, unwilling to pretend this alone deserved a hundred thousand people's time. He didn't know. Perhaps he could ask, on camera, how many lives they'd written for him and which part they'd change next. He could bring their choices into the light. He couldn't demand every arrival agree with him.

“If you edit your own words down to the flattering parts, I'll say so,” she said.

“Say so.”

“I haven't agreed to stay all session. Keep that part too.”

He nodded and turned the mirror back for transport.

The public directory listed only confirmed sessions: titles, brief previews and actual attendance after starting. Nothing guaranteed an audience in exchange for payment. The attendant said self-directed stages often stood empty. Some never gathered more than a few people.

Lin Mo looked at the unlit platform. A hundred thousand began with one person willing to stay. He didn't even have that one.

He saved the movable-mirror arrangement in his draft without submitting the session. Asked whether to retain his guided beginner destination, he opened the boundary information instead.

Beyond the white line was the formal performance area. Confirming entry and crossing the line would end beginner protection. Returning wouldn't restore it.

His finger stopped. Xiao Lu watched without choosing anything for him.

“Let's go back first.”

He closed the audience preview. The draft still showed zero votes, broadcast not started. Before leaving, he looked again at the small box he'd brought out of the collapsed panel.

“That stays visible.”

“What does?” the attendant asked.

Lin Mo pointed.

“Let them see zero too.”
