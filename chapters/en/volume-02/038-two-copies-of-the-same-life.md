# Chapter 38: Two Copies of the Same Life

The trapped fingers twitched beneath the glass.

Lin Mo's palm lay against the fixed upper pane. He couldn't reach them. The tray hatch below stood barely a finger's width open. The apron man's right hand gripped the front rim of the iron tray, fingertips extending past the outer gate, nails squeezed white.

The cart attendant still held the outer gate's handle.

"Let go," Lin Mo said.

"Push the tray back first."

"His hand's underneath."

The attendant looked down and released the handle. The gate sank a little farther under its own weight. Behind the glass, the man's shoulders jerked taut. His mouth opened. No sound reached them.

Wang Hai wedged the chair leg's broken end into the gap beside the fingers. Pushing down with his right hand, he raised the gate a little.

The man tried to withdraw his hand. The tray shifted with it; its rolled rim had caught his fingertips. His left hand reached sideways. The attendant seized the lever outside.

"Can't push it back! The inside gate hasn't dropped!"

Lin Mo saw the second gate hanging behind the first. A rod linked them. As the outer gate rose, the inner one descended toward the trapped wrist.

He raised a hand to stop Wang Hai. Pain caught in his shoulder. Wang Hai, watching the gap, missed it.

Lin Mo pressed his left forearm against Wang Hai's right elbow.

"Hold it there. No farther."

The wood stopped. Sweat ran down Wang Hai's nose. He kept his injured hand against his belly, making no attempt to help his other hand.

"This thing bites harder than a door."

The attendant bent to pull the tray. Lin Mo blocked him.

"His hand's still in there."

"I know!"

"You didn't before."

The attendant fell silent, still gripping the lever. He watched the half-lowered inner gate as though calculating whose feet the tray might land on once he'd wheeled his cart away.

Behind the glass, the man raised two fingers of his left hand.

Lin Mo knew what came next: two taps, withdraw the tray, let the next shift take over. His fingertips reached the window before he noticed that the man wasn't tapping. His fingers were spreading apart.

He needed more room.

Lin Mo pointed left-handed at the tray, then outward. The man shook his head urgently and flattened his trapped hand. His face had drained white.

Wrong again.

Lin Mo put his hand against his ribs. He drew a small breath and let it out slowly. He needed that moment before he started deciding for someone else between gasps. Seeing his chest move, the man tried lowering his own shoulders. His right elbow stopped jerking.

Lin Mo gave no further directions. He stepped aside so the attendant could see the man's open left hand.

The attendant looked, then nudged a short pin beside the tray with his foot.

"Sideways. It slides sideways."

Lin Mo had thought the pin was a lock. He reached to test it, but the hard edge hurt his right wrist. He supported an exposed corner left-handed instead. The attendant pushed the pin aside with his shoe. The tray shifted right less than a finger's width.

The fingertips came free of the rim.

He drew his fingertips behind the outer gate's downward path. His wrist still couldn't pass beneath the inner gate. He raised his left palm and slowly pressed it downward.

Lin Mo didn't tell Wang Hai to release the wood yet. He bent to check that both fingers were clear of the outer gate, then pointed to the inner one and waited for the man's nod.

"Ease off slowly. The outside drops, the inside lifts."

Wang Hai gradually eased the pressure. The outer gate descended and the inner one rose, at last letting the man draw his wrist straight back. Two fingers hung against his palm, blood seeping beside the nails.

Wang Hai still held the wood down.

"Is he clear?"

Lin Mo checked above the tray.

"He's clear."

"Check again. My arm's about done."

Lin Mo looked and nodded. Wang Hai released the last of the pressure. The outer gate settled against the tray, the inner one rising to the top of its track. Another chip broke off the wood and lodged in a rail. He withdrew the chair leg, checked its carved end, then leaned against the wall to straighten.

The attendant tore off some clean wrapping paper and slapped it against the outer gate. It couldn't reach the man. He stared and swore at himself. Using his left hand, the man reset the connecting rod to its middle position and locked it with the flat key from his waist. Both gaps were two fingers wide again. Only then did the attendant feed the paper through.

The man wrapped his fingertips left-handed. He kept his head down, offering no nod of thanks.

Lin Mo didn't ask him to continue.

The two gray sheets still lay in the tray. Blood marked one corner of the first, clear of its number. The attendant began rolling them up, but the wrapped hand stopped him at the rim.

The man looked at Lin Mo's review badge.

Lin Mo held its number against the fixed pane, keeping it out of the hatch. The man's left finger moved between the matching numbers on badge and sample. He turned the first sheet over, revealing a small IMPLEMENTED stamp.

The second had none.

Lin Mo moved aside for Wang Hai to look before offering an explanation.

"What do you see?"

"Same place twice."

"The person?"

"Just a hand in each."

Lin Mo swallowed. He'd seen the door and assumed someone stood beside it. He'd almost pointed out a sleeve. There was no sleeve. The printed hand ended at the wrist. His mind had supplied the rest.

A thin white strip covered its name field, one end curling up. Printed brackets showed beneath, waiting for another name. Both doors had exactly the same missing paint. Even the shadows between the knuckles matched.

He remembered standing there countless times.

Here was one moment, printed twice.

He pointed to the unscarred wrist, then his own. The man understood and drew a thinner backing sheet from beneath the first sample. No figures, just text.

[STANDARD SCENE: SHIFT CHANGE 6.]
[ACTION SEQUENCE RETAINED. EXPERIENCE ASSIGNED TO SELF.]
[REPLACEMENT WORKER: UNASSIGNED.]

Lin Mo stared at the last line. The newcomer in his head had no face. Sun Ning appeared in a doorway and he wanted to put her there. Just now, he'd almost tapped twice for the man behind the glass.

The attendant held down the half-rolled sheets.

"Numbers match. Seen it? Then go."

"Who wrote it?"

"I just deliver."

"Said that last time," Wang Hai said.

The attendant looked up, saw the wood, and looked away.

"Last time was a provisional assignment coming back. This time it's used material. I don't collect it, there's no room in the unassigned tray." He indicated the three iron trays.

"So you slam the gate down?"

He released the lever. A red line crossed his palm.

"Thought he'd moved his hand."

"Did you look?"

The attendant picked the wood chip from the rail without answering. Lin Mo remembered reaching for Sun Ning. He too had thought he knew what someone would do next.

That didn't excuse the attendant. Two fingers were still bleeding behind the glass.

Lin Mo could now read the trays' narrow labels: UNASSIGNED, IMPLEMENTED, RECLAIM. The man pushed the stamped sheet toward the middle tray and tucked the other beneath a blank name strip in the outermost one.

The same door and hand could be given to someone else.

He reached toward the gray paper, stopping at the hatch. His wrist still bore its scar, yet his memory accepted that smooth hand as his own. Taking the sheet wouldn't undo what he'd done to Sun Ning's arm.

"Which part of this did I actually do?"

The attendant only looked at him. Inside, unable to hear, the man reached to roll up the first sheet. Lin Mo pointed to SELF on the backing, then the door, and spread his hands.

The man shook his head, holding down the paper. He thought Lin Mo wanted it.

Lin Mo pocketed his badge, showed both empty hands, and pointed again at the printed wrist. He couldn't make a question of it.

"Not sure he understands what you're asking," Wang Hai murmured.

Sweat shone on the man's temple. The red stain spread through his wrapping. He didn't owe Lin Mo an explanation.

Lin Mo lowered his hands. He was about to leave when the man hooked a white lead with his left finger and moved it onto a nearby ring, away from the slot beneath the gray sheets. He inserted a thin piece marked with a white dot into the white socket beside the reclaim tray and pointed overhead.

The small screen lit.

Lin Mo saw himself at the recognition-stage window, hand against his upper abdomen, lips moving. Wang Hai turned toward the door and returned. His own channel number remained in the corner above the brief clip.

The first gray sheet hadn't moved. The new piece connected only to the white socket beside it. The yellow socket was empty.

The attendant blocked the outside of the pane with his palm. The man slid the screen sideways. Lin Mo shifted half a step to see past the hand.

Beneath the picture ran a row of empty frames. Only the first held a tiny image of Lin Mo. Above them was one line.

[LIVE MATERIAL: AWAITING ASSEMBLY.]

His hand, Wang Hai's turn toward the door: any of it could go into those frames.

Lin Mo looked at the man. No nod came to answer everything he hadn't asked. The man removed the white-dotted piece and switched off the screen.

This time Lin Mo left the empty yellow socket alone.

"I was there when that was filmed," Wang Hai said.

"You weren't at the green door."

"Didn't see you go there either."

Lin Mo pressed the booklet through his pocket and took two careful breaths. He couldn't say how much of his memory came from this place. But the green door could no longer prove he'd lived that life. Even its peeling paint was ready-made.

The man pulled a narrow slip from the counter and wrote slowly with his left hand. His two stiff right fingers couldn't hold it steady. He tucked one corner beneath the iron tray.

The attendant waited without grabbing.

Lin Mo let the man's hand withdraw completely before taking the slip. It bore the sample number and STANDARD SCENE: SHIFT CHANGE 6. Below, among printed return destinations, a short stroke marked one box.

[CONTINUOUS DEMONSTRATION STATION.]

Inside, the man rolled each sheet into its original tray and pointed at the slip's number. A receipt for checking the match. The originals stayed here.

"Can I see how they put it together there?"

No answer came through the glass. The attendant stowed the trays beneath the cart and fastened the crossbar.

"They take people there. Want to go, read the sign yourself."

Wang Hai tightened his grip on the wood. Neither followed. They waited outside the yellow line until the cart turned the corner. Lin Mo filed the slip beside the review badge, leaving the fragment's separate pocket untouched.

"Let's go back."

"Already?"

"Sun Ning's waiting. I need to tell her they can give this to other people."

Wang Hai stayed where he was.

"Ask about her arm first."

Lin Mo tucked the protruding slip fully between the pages and closed the booklet.

"Her arm first."

"What if she doesn't want to hear the rest?"

"Then I won't tell her."

He put away the booklet, then reached for the wood. Wang Hai held it steady. As Lin Mo turned, he wanted to bend and center his face in the window, smiling before the shift changed. He stopped. Instead, he indicated the man's injured hand, lowered his palm, and stepped away first.

The man didn't smile. He turned to fetch gauze.

As they passed the green door, it opened a crack.

Lin Mo stopped, gripping the wood. A woman in short sleeves stood inside with a completed badge in her right hand. Her glance passed over his face. She searched beside him.

"Where's Wang Hai?"

Wang Hai said nothing. He moved a little closer to Lin Mo.

She looked up at last, saw the factory badge on his chest, and relaxed. Lin Mo's fingers tightened one by one.

"You know him?"

"You told me about him." She looked at Lin Mo. "Yesterday. You taught me to find Wang Hai when I couldn't catch my breath."
