# Chapter 42: Her Hand Moved First

The jacket pulled taut in Xiao Lu's fingers. Lin Mo didn't fall.

He shifted his left foot forward and found his footing, right hand still hanging beside him. Wang Hai retrieved the wood from the chair and offered the carved end beneath his left hand.

"Hold this."

Only when he had it did Xiao Lu let go. Creases marked the gray fabric beside his elbow. She stared at her fingers as though unsure what she'd caught.

"Did I pull you?"

"My jacket. Didn't hurt me."

"I saw the scar."

He wanted to say she'd avoided it before, but waited. She'd supported his left forearm and seen his wrist. The response could belong to what they'd experienced here.

But whom had she been warning?

"Do you remember who—"

He stopped. Her uncertain face tightened.

"You want me to recognize someone again?"

"No. Who were you saying that to?"

"I don't know either."

She drew the script against her chest. Old Chen stayed back. Inside, Sun Ning tapped the table with her left hand.

"If you can't stand, sit. She's saved you. Does she have to stand here as punishment?"

Lin Mo returned to the chair. Xiao Lu lowered her hand at last. Wang Hai propped the wood and cleared her way, but she remained.

"I wanted to block something. Not because I thought you'd fall. It was..."

She gestured toward his wrist without touching it. Whatever familiarity had accompanied the reaching hand seemed gone now.

"Someone was going to grab there. It hurt."

"Your hand?"

She looked down, then shook her head.

His stomach tightened. Hospital room, payment window, his sister seeing an injury: any of them could fill her silence. He even knew how to ask so she'd choose one.

Old Chen touched the cup lid, making a soft clink. Lin Mo looked up. No warning shake of the head. He was simply waiting.

"You don't have to work it out now," Lin Mo told her. "I won't record this as you recognizing anyone."

She studied him.

"Didn't say I could go with you either."

"I know."

"I'm getting water."

She walked toward the water station without hurrying. He stayed seated until the dark script disappeared, then unclenched his right hand. The white scar still burned.

"Were you about to mention the hospital?" Wang Hai asked quietly.

Lin Mo nodded.

"Don't think she guessed because I did. You told me."

The words struck cleanly. Lin Mo swallowed his next remark: She almost remembered.

Old Chen sat outside again.

"She's the person you're looking for?"

Lin Mo didn't simply say yes. He described his sister's disappearance, the coat, the word brother in their joint session. Then the overwrite. He left the pill pack hidden and kept the fragment's pocket closed.

"I heard her. It wasn't a screen speaking for her. Then she didn't know again."

Old Chen offered no assurance she'd say it again.

"I chased a sentence once. The more I wanted to hear it, the easier it was to finish it for the speaker."

"You wanted someone to recognize you?"

"Yes. Eventually they avoided me. I blamed them."

Wang Hai looked away without asking who. Lin Mo rubbed his booklet's cover. Xiao Lu had been staring at his wrist, and all he'd wanted was to know whether it proved something about himself.

Sun Ning shifted her injured arm on the folded coat and inhaled softly. He looked up.

"Water?"

"Room for my legs. Move out of the doorway."

Wang Hai moved the stool. Old Chen stood. Lin Mo shifted against his chair and drew back the leg nearest the door. At last Sun Ning could stretch into the open space.

Once she'd settled, he asked whether he could go sit outside.

"Sit where you want. Names when you come back."

"Just the water station. No performance."

"Clear in advance this time."

Wang Hai waited until he could stand before steadying the wood. Lin Mo walked even more slowly. Each turn reminded him that his changed hips and legs couldn't follow his upper body as they used to. Old Chen went ahead, waiting when footsteps stopped. Fifteen years of experience couldn't lift Lin Mo's feet for him.

Xiao Lu was already seated on the water-station bench, script on her knees, cup in hand. Seeing him, she moved the cup farther away.

"I didn't call you over."

"I want to sit. This end all right?"

She glanced at the space and moved one corner of the script.

"Don't block my way out."

He left the corridor end free. Wang Hai sat on his other side, wood leaning against his right knee, its splintered end clear of anyone's legs. At the water station, Old Chen tightened his cold cup's lid and left it closed.

Lin Mo couldn't relax his left shoulder. He tried leaning back, but the bench had no backrest. He reached for the wood again.

Xiao Lu raised her script across his reach.

"Standing up, or can't get comfortable?"

"Can't get comfortable."

"There's a wall."

Half an arm's length behind him. Old Chen's former spot lay closer to it, empty. Lin Mo had remained perched on the edge, ready for someone to demand a demonstration.

Wang Hai offered the wood. Instead of taking it, he asked Xiao Lu to move her cup. She put it on her other side. Bracing his left palm against the bench, he edged over until his back touched the wall.

The jacket folded behind his neck. Xiao Lu reached to straighten it, then stopped.

"Want me to?"

He nodded. She lifted the outer collar and smoothed the fold without pulling his blue top. The scar lay between them; she didn't keep testing how to avoid it.

"Better?"

"Yes."

"I remember you needing support on your left side onstage. I remember that." She withdrew her hand. "What I said at the door, I don't know. Don't make them the same thing."

He watched her palm return to the script.

"All right."

She waited for the slow answer before turning a page. Wang Hai laid down the wood and loosened the edge of the cloth at his left wrist without exposing the injury. Lin Mo realized how little rest that right hand had had, even while drinking.

"Lean back too."

"I am. Wall's not reserved for you."

Lin Mo shifted to give him room without arranging his hands for him.

Xiao Lu opened her script, closed it, and eventually asked, "What did that person teach you?"

Old Chen stood nearby. She asked Lin Mo.

"Remember what I choose to do now. Leave old words unfinished if I'm not sure."

"Learned it?"

"Almost failed just now."

Her mouth twitched.

"Don't pay him yet."

"Nobody's paying," Old Chen said.

Wang Hai wiped sweat from the wood. "Came out carrying that cup. Thought he might be collecting water donations."

Her smile faded quickly. She reopened the script, pausing between pages without inviting anyone to search.

"There's a part I can't connect."

Lin Mo stayed back.

"Which part?"

"Our session. I remember you held my hand. I said it hurt. You let go. Before that..."

She stared into her open palm.

"I wanted to ask something. My mouth was open. Don't know what it was."

Lin Mo heard his own breathing. Brother, you—still there in his head. He didn't say it, but she'd seen that he knew.

"Don't tell me now."

"All right."

She held the page against her knees and wrote. He looked away until she turned the script toward him.

[I WANTED TO ASK. DIDN'T FINISH.]

No form of address. Wang Hai saw too, without confirming or nodding. Xiao Lu turned the book back, pen poised.

"Even your silence makes it feel like I got the answer right."

Lin Mo watched her writing hand.

"All I know is that you remember an unfinished question now. What I remember can't go on your page instead."

After a moment she wrote again.

[AT THE DOOR, FIRST WANTED TO GET MY HAND IN FRONT OF HIS. DON'T KNOW WHY.]

The writing was fainter. Reading the uncertainty, Lin Mo felt his urge to make her try again ease. She'd left the gap. He needn't fill it with a hospital room.

"If I ask later, tell me first that it's what you remember."

"Then?"

"Then I'll listen. Don't count hearing your version as me remembering."

He wanted to record the agreement and asked her first. She indicated his booklet.

"In yours. Leave my two lines here for now."

He stopped before turning his page.

She closed the script, her left hand automatically covering the place where her thin recording piece lay.

"Have I heard everything this could play?"

"You can't assume that. At the transfer window we saw an experience at one door, printed twice."

She looked up. He described the gray sheets and live material awaiting assembly, without claiming to know where every memory came from. Wang Hai added that his filmed turn had been made to look like entering to help someone.

"You went to check?"

Lin Mo nodded. Taking the new receipt from his booklet, he placed its three source fields between them. The pill pack and fragment stayed put.

Xiao Lu touched one black join without taking the paper.

"Separate these, does it separate them in your head?"

"No. I still felt I should report for a shift."

"Why go, then?"

He remembered returning twice from the entrance.

"I didn't want only somebody's explanation. Now I know that this time they joined things that never happened together. I can't take the memory out of Zhou Ling's head."

She withdrew her finger without asking him to repeat who Zhou Ling was.

"I want to see our session."

His left fingers almost pinned down the receipt. He knew how badly he'd want a response from her if they played brother again.

"Which part?"

"All of it. From pressing start to pressing end myself. Don't choose one sentence first."

Wang Hai straightened. Old Chen lifted his cup from the counter.

"What's in your script, you kept it yourself?" Old Chen asked.

Her fingers stayed on the cover.

"Mine. Not lending it."

"Don't give it to me. I used post-session verification. Asked with my own session's settlement number. How much they can check depends on what's been kept."

"Another performance?" Lin Mo asked.

"I checked numbers then. Read the sign they have now. I haven't agreed to anything for you."

Xiao Lu didn't immediately agree either. She asked where it was, which route came back. Old Chen indicated two points on the counter with his cup's base: around the outside of the waiting entrance, not through the green materials door. Directions, no claim he could make anyone open a cabinet.

She rose. Lin Mo began to move too.

Her script blocked him.

"Stay seated. I'll look at the entrance."

"I can—"

"You need help standing. I want to check my question. Why are you rushing ahead for me again?"

His hand stopped on the bench and slowly withdrew. The wood stayed beside Wang Hai's knee.

"Come back and explain after looking. If it needs a performance, we'll discuss it."

"I won't press anything for you."

She turned. Old Chen waited until she asked whether he'd show her the way, then followed with his cup.

Two of them remained. Wang Hai rested against the wall, raising his bandaged hand a little higher. Lin Mo watched where Xiao Lu had gone. Her retreating steps paused.

"Lin Mo."

He looked up.

At the corner, she pointed to the loose jacket hem beside him.

"Tuck that in. Cart coming."

He gathered the fabric onto his knees. Only after the wheels passed did he realize how quickly he'd answered.

Wang Hai didn't ask her to call again.

Neither did he.
