# Chapter 33: Lower Your Hand First

“Use this.”

Lin Mo pulled the pen from his booklet. Sun Ning didn't take it. Her left finger stayed on the packing paper's small print.

“Will they let you bring it in?”

The pen stopped in midair.

“Haven't asked.”

“Then don't answer my question for me yet.”

She pushed the paper back and made room. Lin Mo sat, knocking his bruised knee beneath the table. He shifted sideways, still holding the chair leg. It struck the table's edge, nearly sweeping the paper off.

Wang Hai held out his right hand.

“Give that back. You take the pen, I'll take the wood. Before you decide to take me again.”

Lin Mo released it. Wang Hai propped it beside his leg and sat by the door. His left hand stayed tucked away, fingers curled beneath his sleeve.

Lin Mo wrote his name on a blank page, carefully tracing the last character, and showed Sun Ning.

She nodded.

“What if their badge has the wrong name?”

He drew a cross beside it.

She studied it.

“You don't want this name?”

Lin Mo turned the booklet back. The cross sat right beside 默. Even to him, it looked as though he'd rejected what he'd just written.

Wang Hai laughed.

“Lovely. They haven't started yet, and you've already canceled yourself.”

Lin Mo pointed the pen at him, unable to laugh. He couldn't tear out the page: the pocket behind it still held the fragment. He began adding words beside the cross, then stopped.

What if they only let someone glimpse it?

Sun Ning gently lowered the booklet.

“First let me see what you're refusing.”

She pointed at the door with her left hand. Lin Mo followed her finger. Wang Hai had already moved aside.

“I didn't tell you to leave,” she said.

He sat straight again.

“Then why point at me?”

“I pointed at the door.”

“I'm in front of it.”

Sun Ning closed her eyes. Lin Mo finally let out a breath of laughter. It pulled at his chest and quickly stopped. Setting down the pen, he pointed his left finger at the paper and pushed his open right hand outward.

Sun Ning offered him the paper.

He didn't take it.

“I meant I won't accept it.”

Her hand stopped too.

“I thought you wanted it.”

Silence. Footsteps shuffled past in the corridor, briefly blocking the light beneath the door.

Lin Mo slowly drew his right hand back to his chest, palm outward. His left finger pointed only at the paper. He watched Sun Ning's eyes and waited for her to move.

She put the paper down.

He nodded, then lowered both hands.

“That one,” she said. “Don't change it halfway through pointing.”

Wang Hai copied him with his right hand. Before his palm was upright, he glanced left, as though wanting his other hand to indicate something. Finally he jerked his chin toward the door.

“What about me?”

Lin Mo looked at his injured hand.

“Don't try two gestures yet.”

“You're going inside. If you start doing a whole string of them, how do I answer with only this hand working?”

Lin Mo crossed out the other gestures he'd written down, keeping only the last one. He raised his hand and held it. Wang Hai raised his too. Neither moved. Eventually Sun Ning turned the packing paper faceup.

“You can't stand there till tomorrow.”

“Better to stop than go wrong,” Wang Hai said.

Lin Mo lowered his hand. He didn't write that it worked. He circled the cross and noted: Misread today.

Sun Ning saw, but left the pen alone.

“You're still going over?”

“To look at the entrance.”

“And afterward?”

His fingers stopped on the pen. Through his jacket, a corner of the original form pressed against his chest. He remembered the two pieces in the narrow slot, his knees bending with the recorded image. That had been only a short replay.

“I want to know what makes people follow along once it's installed.”

Wang Hai leaned back.

“That man just showed you.”

“The recording. Not how the stage recognizes people.”

“So you want to stand on it.”

Lin Mo didn't answer immediately. Sun Ning pulled the packing paper aside, leaving his marked-up page before him.

“Decide when you get there. Don't wait until the doorway to tell him you planned it all along.”

Wang Hai watched him. Lin Mo capped the pen.

“Yes. I want to go in. But first I'll see how to get out.”

Wang Hai picked up the wood without urging him to stand.

Sun Ning stayed in the rest area. Lin Mo closed the booklet without exposing the fragment. Outside, he stopped and told her they'd come back if Wang Hai couldn't see him from the entrance.

“Didn't you just say that?” Wang Hai asked.

“To her.”

Wang Hai said nothing more.

The Silent Theater arrow led toward the other side of the waiting-room door. Xiao Lu still sat on the second stool, script across her knees. Seeing Wang Hai's wood first, she tucked her toes beneath the stool.

Lin Mo stopped two steps away.

“We're going around the other side.”

She looked toward the arrow.

“You accepted?”

“Not yet.”

She held down the script as though afraid he'd open it. He kept his booklet at his side.

“What played by the door was something I said inside. I didn't ask them to send it to you.”

“I knew it was your voice.”

“I mean, I wasn't calling you over.”

Her fingers shifted from the book's corner.

“What about when you do call?”

He couldn't tell her to trust his voice. That white cable had run into the wall throughout.

“See me first. Then ask who I'm calling.”

“And if you tell me what I ought to remember again?”

The question came quickly. His right wrist pressed against his clothes, its white scar still there. He swallowed the word sister.

“You don't have to come with me.”

Xiao Lu neither nodded nor offered the script. She moved her stool toward the wall to let them pass.

The entrance was a short corridor ending at a chest-high counter. Behind it, a woman in sleeve protectors slotted thin badges into an upright rack. The top one bore Lin Mo's channel number.

The name was still 林茉. The same sound; the wrong final character.

“The box was returned,” he said.

She withdrew her hand, checking the number, then his face.

“We haven't received a returned badge.”

Wang Hai stood the wood beside him, keeping it off the counter.

“We haven't received notice we changed our names either.”

She ignored him. Lin Mo unfolded the packing paper to show only AWAITING PERSONAL CONFIRMATION.

“I haven't confirmed. I'm not taking that.”

She pinched the badge and moved it down one slot. It neither shattered nor vanished. Beside it stood blank badges with tiny notches on their backs.

“Handwritten ones go below. No familiar movements for your first segment. You find your own way.”

Lin Mo kept his hands back.

“And if I go wrong?”

She turned a display board toward him. Three simple pictures: a crossed-out mouth, feet inside a lit square, a door pushed open after the square went dark.

The small print was clearer than on the packing paper.

[YOU MAY LEAVE AFTER ONE SEGMENT. ADAPTATION VOTING CONTINUES DURING THE SEGMENT.]

His eyes stopped on ADAPTATION.

“A blank badge still means body changes?”

“You asked not to accept a proposed identity. Who said you were exempt from performing?”

Wang Hai looked at him. Lin Mo met his eyes.

“I didn't say exempt.”

He folded the paper back, pressing his handwritten 默 on its reverse. The worker had returned the box, but that return didn't reach this rack. If Lin Mo went back to the rest area, the badge bearing someone else's name would still be waiting for the next trolley.

He wanted to pull it out. The woman covered the rack. He stopped without touching her.

“Don't use that one yet.”

“If you don't take it, it stays here.”

Half of 茉 showed beneath her hand. Her answer told him nothing about tomorrow. Today, he could only watch his own slot. He asked her to separate the badges. Only when the blank one lay at the counter's far end did he loosen his grip on the packing paper.

She lifted a transparent sheet beside the display to show its reverse. Personal belongings had to remain within one's clothing. Communication was restricted to one's own range of movement. She pointed at his booklet.

“Keep it put away inside. You can't hold it up.”

He returned it to his jacket. He still had paper and pen, but couldn't show his written name to the stage. He traced a character in the air with his left hand. She didn't look at his fingertips; she was waiting for the next badge.

Wang Hai tapped the counter with his right finger until she looked up.

“Where do I watch?”

She pointed to a narrow side window overlooking the stage's edge. A dark panel sat beneath it.

“Witness position. Watch only. You can't answer for him.”

“Can he hear me?”

“Not once he's inside.”

Through the glass, Lin Mo saw a yellow boundary and an open door behind the stage. He raised his palm toward the outside. Beside him, Wang Hai raised his right hand. Standing outside the window, they finished rehearsing before the glass could separate them.

The woman tapped the rack.

“Coming?”

Lin Mo stayed. Shoes had scuffed a patch of wall white beneath the window. He crouched, needing his left hand against the wall as soon as his knee bent. Wang Hai bent too, finding only a metal channel extending from under the door.

“What are you looking at?”

“How the door opens.”

“Planning to crawl through here?”

“Seeing if it locks.”

Wang Hai braced the chair leg on the floor for him. Lin Mo gripped it and rose, socks sliding slightly. His altered toes had drawn his stance too narrow again. He spread his feet and slowly settled his weight.

“Looking underneath won't help,” the woman said. “That channel doesn't open it.”

“Show me once.”

Her face darkened. He neither moved closer nor offered the confirmation box.

Eventually she took a stiff card bearing only an arrow from the rack's side and inserted it beside the door. The yellow light went out. The rear door opened outward onto a passage returning to the short corridor. She removed the card; the door stayed open.

“Of course it opens now. Nobody's inside,” Wang Hai said.

The woman's finger rested on AFTER ONE SEGMENT.

Lin Mo hadn't checked every mechanism inside. At least the exit wasn't painted on a wall, and it didn't lead down an unknown route. He moved along the corridor far enough to see its outer handle, then returned to the window.

Wang Hai took back the wood.

“If you raise your hand, I'll stay. What if they tell me to leave?”

Lin Mo studied the narrow window. The hand he'd used to trace letters already ached. Even outside, his body wanted somewhere to lean.

“Look at me first. Not the badge.”

“And don't take my nod to mean it's safe. I haven't seen everything in there.”

Lin Mo nodded, then stopped.

Wang Hai sighed. “That nod I understand.”

Lin Mo returned and asked the woman to turn the blank badge over. No FEMALE. No PERMANENT RESIDENT. Only three fields: CHANNEL, NAME, ENTRY FOR THIS SEGMENT. He wrote his name, put away the pen, and held his fingertip over the last field.

“After this segment, I can leave through that door?”

She pointed to the third picture.

He looked at Wang Hai. Wang Hai's hand was down. He didn't nod for him.

Lin Mo pressed his fingertip into the box.

The yellow stage light came on. Leaving the 林茉 badge in the rack, the woman inserted his handwritten one beside the door.

Lin Mo moved his lips, finishing his own name outside.

He crossed the yellow boundary. Cold floor met his socks. The door closed behind him. Wang Hai came to the window and raised his right hand.

Lin Mo immediately raised his palm to show he'd seen.

A small floor square lit beside his feet. His hand's shadow appeared at the front of the stage.

[FIRST VOLUNTARY ACTION: WAVING.]

His hand froze. He tried to shout wait. Breath rushed from his mouth, but he couldn't hear even his own voice.

Beneath the narrow window, the dark panel lit up for Wang Hai.

[SHE HAS SAID GOODBYE TO YOU.]
