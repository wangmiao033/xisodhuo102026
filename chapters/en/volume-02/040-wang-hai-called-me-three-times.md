# Chapter 40: Wang Hai Called Me Three Times

Lin Mo shut his mouth, keeping Zhou-jie behind his teeth.

Outside, Wang Hai pointed at him, then nodded toward the door. Lin Mo almost agreed, thinking he meant to fetch Zhou Ling. But one hand was still bandaged, and the right wasn't beckoning. He was asking whether Lin Mo could walk.

Not yet.

He lowered his left palm: wait. Wang Hai misunderstood and took two steps toward the door. Lin Mo stopped gesturing and retied the loose cloth belt with a roomier knot. His lower abdomen and hips still hurt. He shifted his socked feet, seeking purchase beneath the stool.

[TIME REMAINING: 02:31.]

Wang Hai returned, shaking his head. Still locked. He held the wood upright against the glass, carving toward Lin Mo, without pointing out which screen to choose.

Looking at Mo, the character for silence, Lin Mo remembered Zhou Ling insisting she remembered. He'd been angry: once he explained the source, she ought to admit there'd been no yesterday. Now he knew he'd never carried her stool, yet his shoulder held the ache of carrying it. He could see her sitting down. Had he heard her thanks or supplied them himself?

He didn't force the scene to continue.

A thin line flickered where the screens joined. He stood using the rail. His right stride had shortened; the left foot followed as before, almost knocking his knees together. He stopped, separated his feet, and turned them with his body instead of forcing the turn through his waist as he used to.

The stool stayed under the left window. He kept its location in mind, refusing to substitute the stool at shift change.

The controls were beneath the front screen, less than half an arm's length from the rail's end. He followed the rail with his left hand, steadied himself at its end, and pressed the middle button.

Both sides went dark.

Only he remained, before the glass, hand on his upper abdomen, lips moving. Wang Hai turned toward the door. Nobody approached him. Nobody was helped away.

The clip they'd seen at the transfer window.

He selected the beginning: green door, unscarred hand setting down a badge, door opening. The ending showed Zhou Ling's face. Below her shoulders, her body faded to blankness.

But Zhou Ling wasn't standing here now.

The beginning bore SHIFT CHANGE 6, the middle his channel number, the ending another number. Each section stopped independently. YESTERDAY stayed fixed in the experience field.

He pushed the preserve-joins slider with his left finger.

The screens lit side by side. YESTERDAY moved above all three, beside none of the original numbers. The hand vanished at the first image's edge. In the next, Lin Mo still held his abdomen. Blackness lay between.

He hadn't taught Zhou Ling. Separated, the images couldn't even complete the movement of teaching her. They said nothing about when she'd actually entered the theater.

He pressed the middle button again. His actions looped after Wang Hai left. Selecting the ending, he found Zhou Ling's face waiting unchanged. She'd been looking down. Placed after the other footage, she seemed to watch a hand reaching to support her.

He raised his own left hand to that height. His shoulder allowed barely a breath before it dropped. The woman kept looking down, never following his real hand.

When they'd met, he'd asked which hand she remembered. Only after seeing his injury had she answered. His question might have helped fill the gap.

He stopped demonstrating, lit all three screens, and moved back until the camera could see both the images and his lowered hand.

Leaving the slider there, he pointed at the recorded Wang Hai, then outside. Unable to read the tiny lettering, Wang Hai shifted left for a better view. Now he could see that his recorded self never reached anyone.

His lips moved twice. Lin Mo couldn't guess the words. Wang Hai dropped his right hand, then raised it, empty palm toward him, holding still.

This time Lin Mo didn't finish it for him.

[Who's supposed to understand it like this?]

[So he never helped her up.]

[Add a clip where he does. Don't leave that gap.]

[The face and hands should match up too.]

A proposal appeared above: further feminization of face, neck and arms, with permanent-demonstrator experiences. Continuous standing posture as the demonstration template. Existing injuries excluded from repair.

Lin Mo read the last line, finger still beside the slider. He'd preserved the joins. Voting remained open.

Fifty-seven.

He opened his hand toward the camera, leaving the finger beside the control to show he'd chosen this. Someone asked why he had to make the footage so ugly. Another said let him finish checking. The number dipped, then rose under more comments.

[She must know this job pretty well.]

[She will once they've finished.]

He shook his head, opening his mouth to say he didn't work here. No sound came. Breath whitened a corner of the screen.

Sixty-one.

The side of his neck tightened as though his collar were pulling upward. He lowered his chin without tugging at his clothes with the injured hand. The line from collarbone to neck grew unfamiliar. Near the screen, his cheek touched it in a different place. Fine, prickling pain spread through his forearm. His hand narrowed inside the sleeve; the old scar still burned along his wrist.

Reaching for the rail, he missed.

It hadn't moved. The point he'd reached from had.

He looked down and placed his left fingers on the metal one by one, shifting his heels back to bring himself closer. Shoulder pain flared. He bent his knees rather than surrender all his weight to that arm.

Wang Hai went to the door, taking the wood.

Lin Mo knew he was testing it. Then he thought: the next shift isn't here. Why's he leaving already?

An ordinary thought, faintly aggrieved.

[BODY MODIFICATION: 70%. COGNITIVE OVERWRITE: 50%.]

Afternoons filled his head. He waited inside the green door. Zhou Ling arrived, calling him jie, a familiar address for an older woman. Wang Hai carried things for them. Once, in the rain, all three had moved the stool inside.

What was the weather outside?

He couldn't picture a window or where the rain fell. Trying to trace it back brought him to the assembled, smiling face.

He opened his mouth, exhaling slowly.

I'm Lin Mo.

No sound. His left hand held the rail; his right rested loosely against his jacket. The wrist injury wouldn't let him make a fist. Through the cloth, paper pressed against the bases of his fingers.

He reached for his ID, stopping at the pocket. Cameras remained. The paper couldn't decide what he called himself.

He shuffled backward, left foot, then right, releasing the rail only when he reached the stool and sat. Taking out his booklet, he shielded the hidden pocket with its cover and opened to the green-door entry.

[I HAVE NEVER BEEN THERE.]

He recognized his handwriting. His mind immediately supplied an explanation: he'd written that on his first day. Of course he'd been here many times since.

The explanation came more easily than the words on the page. For a moment he wanted to cross out NEVER.

He held the pen away from the paper.

Wang Hai returned and crouched at his level. They breathed across the glass, neither immediately telling the other what to do.

Wang Hai breathed once against it, briefly, then stopped. Watching the mist recede, Lin Mo remembered practicing today: they'd moved to the same side, fogging the glass until neither could see.

Remembering Wang Hai's expression loosened his mouth a little and pulled painfully at his neck.

This memory was no prettier than the rainy afternoon.

He wrote what was before him: I'm sitting. Wang Hai is outside the window. Then he added his name, and man. His fingers were numb. The last stroke trailed crookedly. He left it.

[TIME REMAINING: 00:24.]

The screens still showed the joins. Neither modification had reset the slider. Below, a small RECOMMENDED MERGE box lit, waiting for him to press it.

He stayed seated.

Wang Hai pointed below the window, then toward the door. Once he saw Wang Hai had the wood steady, Lin Mo put away his booklet and pen. Rising, he felt the gray jacket's shoulder seam slip lower, the skirt tighten along his changed hips. He steadied himself at the rail and divided his previous step into two.

At zero, Wang Hai pushed the door open from outside.

Sound returned. His breathing was high and short. Wang Hai offered the wood to his left hand without pulling his arm.

"Slowly. Door's staying open."

He pressed down firmly before stepping out. Sitting against the corridor wall, he finally said the whole sentence in his present voice.

"I'm Lin Mo. I'm a man."

Wang Hai didn't tell him he'd said it already. He angled the wood's broken end away from Lin Mo's socks.

Later, the attendant brought the receipt. Three source numbers occupied separate boxes with black strips between. Under THIS VIEWING: SEPARATE SECTIONS, JOINS PRESERVED. His handwritten name remained unchanged.

"Will this change Zhou Ling's?"

"Only settles your session."

"She'll still remember me teaching her."

The attendant pointed at the old sample number without answering. Keep the original narrow slip too. Nobody came to collect the yesterdays already sent out.

Lin Mo kept the receipt. He showed Wang Hai the three sources before filing it behind the review badge, avoiding the fragment's hidden pocket.

"You saw?"

"Saw I didn't help her. Rest was too small."

Lin Mo pushed it closer. Wang Hai read and nodded, without claiming Zhou Ling understood now. Returning it, he rubbed his forearm with his right hand.

"While you were sitting, you smiled at the door."

"Did I?"

"Briefly. Wasn't at me."

Lin Mo remembered the rain and stool. He described the added memories, including Wang Hai carrying things for them. After listening, Wang Hai paused.

"Was I happy about it?"

"I remember you never complained."

"Doesn't sound much like me."

Lin Mo tried to laugh and couldn't. Wang Hai looked no easier. He moved the wood away from Lin Mo's knee, keeping it on his right until Lin Mo asked to stand. Only then did he offer it again.

"I wasn't waiting there for you to call me," he said. "I came because I wanted to."

"I know."

"You know now. If you don't when you come back, ask first."

Lin Mo nodded. Before the separated images, he'd thought he could pull truth apart from falsehood. Now he wondered how much of that quick I know would remain.

They rested a long time. His first attempt to rise pitched him forward with his new center of gravity. Wang Hai didn't catch him; he braced the wood so he could sit back down. The second time, Lin Mo placed his feet under his knees, waiting for his left leg to stop trembling. They returned along the corridor, slower than before.

The long mirror appeared again.

Lin Mo stopped, remembering the sentence he had to say each day. He knew his name, why he'd come, that his pocket held clues to finding his sister. Yet the reflected neck and hands seemed to belong to a colleague he'd just met. Even the habit of turning seemed more familiar to that person than to him.

Ahead, Wang Hai looked back.

"Lin Mo."

He heard and waited. The person who trained newcomers should emerge from behind the door and take what Wang Hai carried.

The second call came closer.

Lin Mo looked up at the mirror without turning.

The third time, Wang Hai stood beside him. He didn't grab him, only brought the carved end into view.

"I'm calling you."

Lin Mo looked from the wood to Wang Hai. Cold sweat slowly spread across his back.

"I know that name."

"Then why didn't you answer?"

He gripped the wood. Only as his left fingers tightened did he feel the sweat in his palm.

"I thought you were calling someone else."
