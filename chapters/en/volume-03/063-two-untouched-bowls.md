# Chapter 63: Two Untouched Bowls

Lin Mo studied the two untouched full bowls.

“At last, something that won't get smaller while we wait.”

“Won't get any more edible either,” Xiao Lu said.

The woman laughed. At least nobody needed telling to eat before it cooled. She closed the box and helped wheel the rig away. The low crossbar finally left the floor clear.

Xiao Lu remained standing. Bending over a bowl, she gathered her hair before it touched the rim.

The arrangement's easy completeness troubled Lin Mo. Equal portions. Parallel chopsticks. Plastic creases pressed where they wouldn't obscure the decoration. Someone only had to arrive and resume a moment that had never gone wrong.

The background lit up.

A wider table appeared on the panel. Behind it sat a man with Lin Mo's former face, chopsticks in his right hand, no scar on the wrist. Steam obscured the person opposite. A gray sleeve rested by their bowl.

The man drew that bowl closer.

“I'm home. You won't have to wait anymore.”

Lin Mo tightened his grip. The lower voice, even its pauses, resembled what he remembered. The tenderness of the sentence came from nowhere. He couldn't place a day when he'd said it, or see which old recording supplied the scene.

The man pushed up his sleeve. A tear in the gray left sleeve carried a loose thread like the one Lin Mo had freed beside the brake. Lower down, a black mirror-rig wheel showed beside the table leg.

Lin Mo looked beneath the real table. No wheels. Those had left with the rig. Something present moments ago had been spliced into this supposedly distant room.

“That's the rig from here,” he told Xiao Lu. “And this sleeve only tore here.”

“They gave you your old face as well.”

“We can't treat it as one intact scene from the past.”

His hand stayed tight. Knowing the mixture contained recent objects didn't stop him wanting another look at that uninjured wrist. Xiao Lu waited without demanding he appear unmoved.

The figure opposite smiled.

“As long as you're home. I'm not hungry.”

“Who put that person there?” she asked.

He found the small gray text. The empty-table equipment was installed. The recommended background still included preset characters. Their speech wasn't registered as the performers' live answers.

“They came with the image. I thought empty table meant an empty background too.”

He searched for Pause. She stopped him.

The figure pushed both pairs of chopsticks toward the man, saying the noodles were still hot and they'd already eaten. The picture moved toward the bowls. Both remained full, chopstick tips spotless.

Xiao Lu pulled one real bowl closer.

The plastic beneath dragged free, warm and damp against her forefinger. She didn't shake it off. She watched the film cling to her fingertip, then slowly released it.

“That wasn't how I waited.”

Lin Mo didn't ask who she'd waited for.

She drew a little more plastic from beneath the bowl, touched the heated base and withdrew. He moved half a step closer. She looked up: warm, no burn. He stopped without taking the bowl away.

“Could you sit farther off?”

He dragged his low stool to the table's side and sat down, leaving the chair that seemed reserved for the returning man.

“Here?”

“Yes. When you're directly opposite, I start wondering which line I'm supposed to answer.”

He steadied the wood. She studied her hand again. Behind them, the man still waited for someone to say they weren't hungry. He resembled Lin Mo, minus the aching shoulder and wrist that couldn't bear careless weight.

“I was hungry,” Xiao Lu said. “Sick of waiting. A skin had formed on the soup. I'd part it and it would close again.”

“Don't force yourself to remember.”

She looked up.

“I'm not forcing it. I'm telling you.”

He swallowed his concern. The warm background seemed built for him to be a caring brother inside it. Yet the moment she spoke, he wanted to dictate how she could safely think.

“I don't remember someone telling me not to go hungry,” she said. “I got tired of waiting and wanted to eat.”

He looked at the untouched model bowl. Half a bowl of noodles hovered on his tongue. He held it back, leaving her room to remember the next mouthful herself.
