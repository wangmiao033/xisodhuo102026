# Chapter 77: The Light Wouldn't Hold His Weight

Only once he'd risen clear of the narrow seat did Lin Mo realize how much weight it had carried.

His right knee wouldn't straighten immediately. He planted the wood ahead, waited upright, then eased his heel away from the seat leg. End glowed behind him; the slender microphone glowed ahead. No one came to meet him between those lights.

His first step settled. Xiao Lu said she could see him.

At the second, she didn't repeat it. His shoulder was drifting from the center of her fixed camera feed. Along the picture's lower edge, only the upper part of his support remained. She kept her hand beside Send, watching his mouth, ready to press when she had something to say.

He followed level floor along the channel's right side. Beside his left foot, it slanted away. Toward the far end, its silver edging approached the reflected light in the glass. Another person there leaned on wood: the same softer face, torn left sleeve, footsteps always late.

He remembered not to use it for directions.

Once walking, his eyes followed the brightest place more readily than memory.

At the third placement, he avoided a patch of whitening silver. The wood tapped solid floor. He waited until his stocking rested on that same surface before bringing the other foot forward. He wouldn't stride across the deceptively narrow channel.

Sweat stuck his clothes to his waist. Despite his adjustment, the notebook's stiff corner nudged his ribs with each movement. He couldn't spare a hand, only straighten slightly so it didn't scrape one spot repeatedly.

“The microphone…”

“Still ahead. I can only see the top.”

He nodded. She didn't say two more steps or arrange his feet by the distance in her picture.

At the channel's end, a row of lights came on beneath the glass.

Nothing pursuing him. Lights connected along the fixed capture frame, illuminating the disputed-statement position and giving the silver strip a second edge. As he narrowed his eyes, his delayed reflection was lowering its wood.

The reflected patch looked wider than the actual floor.

He'd already shifted a fraction forward when he felt the support fail to settle. Its end struck the silver edging and slid diagonally into the channel with a sharp scrape.

His right knee buckled.

Xiao Lu saw his shoulder drop and half his face vanish beyond the picture's edge.

“Ge!”

He didn't answer.

He didn't grab for the reflected wood still standing straight, or drive his left shoulder into the hard edge beside him. He spread his left foot outward onto solid floor he could feel, bending both knees slightly to draw his falling weight back. Pain shot from his wrist toward his elbow. He held his breath, refusing to suspend himself from the sliding support by that injured hand.

The wood stopped diagonally against the channel's bottom, still in his grip.

What held him was the foot he'd just planted.

Xiao Lu heard a sharp intake of breath, then rubbing fabric. She'd half risen, knocking her knee beneath the console before remembering she couldn't reach him from here.

“I can't see your feet.”

No commands to go left or right.

Watching the actual silver strip, he drew his right foot back half a palm's width. His stocking scraped the floor. Pain numbed his knee, but it finally stopped folding.

“Don't… talk yet.”

She released Send. Backstage fell abruptly quiet, her own breathing too loud.

He waited longer.

The crouching figure in the glass finally steadied too, reenacting his near fall. He didn't watch it finish. Looking at his hand, he found he could relax his fingers slightly rather than grip until the joints whitened.

First he returned his weight to his feet. Then he lifted the wood along its original path. The end had caught beneath a burr on the silver edging; he couldn't wrench it free. He tilted it gently inward, released the snag, and drew it up inch by inch.

A dark mark remained at the end.

He planted it beside his stockinged toes and listened for the solid sound before slowly entrusting it with some weight again.

“I'm steady.”

Xiao Lu missed the chair slightly as she sat, its leg clattering. Ignoring that, she asked whether he'd fallen.

“No.”

“Hit anything?”

“No. Old pain.”

She closed her eyes briefly. His face finally returned at the picture's edge. Sweat ran down his forehead. He started to wipe it, touched his sleeve, then abandoned the attempt.

She didn't say lucky. What she hadn't been able to see made that word too light. She said she was still here, told him to wait until his legs steadied.

After a while, she asked whether he wanted to return to the seat.

He looked back. The route remained open, End still lit. He wanted to sit, wanted his knees relieved of deciding where his upper body might land. Returning also meant retracing the steps he'd tested.

Having come this far didn't oblige him to finish.

With the wood secure, he eased the load on his right leg and slowly settled his left heel. Both feet could support him. He could now see the channel's end clearly. The microphone was closer than the seat.

“Can still stand. I'll go forward.”

“Don't judge by what I can see.”

“By what's under… my feet.”

She acknowledged him. He hadn't promised he wouldn't sway again; she hadn't demanded that promise. He'd described a step presently possible, signed nothing for whatever followed.

The freedom field continued refreshing. Comments urged haste or called for attendants. He didn't turn to read them, didn't know which figures had changed during his near fall.

No one emerged at the far end.

He could at least refuse to follow that same misleading light again.

He lowered his gaze to his toes, choosing the point where wood met floor rather than the brightest silver. The channel's actual end was fixed. Double images shifted, but a tap couldn't make empty space solid.

Probe, then foot. At the end, he changed direction in two stages along level ground. Each turn protested in his hip, so he divided the movement further. Better to feel awkward as something difficult to carry than let another person drag his body across.

His face disappeared from Xiao Lu's camera. Only a corner of clothing remained.

“You're out of my picture.”

“Listen.”

“I am.”

She heard the wood strike three times, long intervals between, a faint exhalation after the last. She didn't convert the sounds into steps remaining. Each meant he was still moving.

He finally saw the round base beneath the microphone stalk.

He didn't lean on it. The stalk was thinner than expected, its base too small. Being upright didn't make it capable of supporting him. He stopped outside the position, found the last level patch with his wood, then entered the half-lit boundary.

The existing camera beneath the glass lit, taking over the present-statement feed.

His face returned to Xiao Lu, closer and more frontal. He wasn't straight; his left shoulder hung low, his hand still grasped the support. His eyes remained open, fixed on the restored audio beyond the camera.

“See you now.”

He nodded without announcing his arrival again. He arranged his feet where he could hold them, wood ahead on his right, its end clear of the microphone's round base.

This microphone received him more easily than the seat's. An experimental breath moved the capture bar. He didn't need to raise his chin to chase it.

His written statement appeared beside his face in the correction field.

He saw the marker identifying his new voice, then Seatkeeper's challenge: was the present speaker still the person who'd wanted to leave?

He didn't begin with that larger question.

“First correct… what you cut.”

The red name stayed overhead.

“I said I'd go in. Into… this judgment room. Then I said, keep speaking.”

He spoke in two parts. The microphone recorded the pause without bridging it with a chime to make a smoother sentence.

The original capture appeared again. Referral result first, his decision to continue speaking afterward. Opening the End confirmation, backing out to continue his statement: each action remained in its original place. Beside it, he watched the whole sequence, obscuring none of the words with his body.

“I didn't say… I wanted to stay.”

Xiao Lu watched his mouth. She wanted to add that he'd said this countless times, to make everyone understand what reaching this position had cost. Instead she left Send untouched, giving him a statement finally unobstructed by another voice.

He raised his hand toward the handwritten word wanted, its missing stroke supplied afterward. He didn't take out the notebook again. This captured page was enough. Every fresh challenge needn't shake loose everything kept before it.

“Words haven't changed.”

The verification frame enlarged the thumbnail. He could see where wrist pain had stopped his pen, could see the sentence predated the backstage correction. His refusal hadn't been put into his mouth through Xiao Lu's newly connected voice.

He returned his hand to the support, slowly. At least he no longer needed to hold up paper while fearing the cover would slip.

The verification light paused on the original audio, then the written thumbnail, then his current statement.

Not identical wording. None said he wished to remain in the theater.

THE TRUNCATED SEGMENT DOES NOT ESTABLISH VOLUNTARY STAY. THIS CITATION EXCLUDED.

The brief I'll go in withdrew from the montage awaiting acceptance, returning to the verified record with its complete ending. Beside the crossed-out subtitle, CORRECTION PENDING gave way to an exclusion result.

He watched the space actually empty. For a long moment he didn't move.

Previously, the silent montage had joined that nod-like motion to another smile. Now it stopped at the gap. Only a link to the complete material remained beside it; the half-sentence could no longer be hauled back into the sequence.

An automatic replay queued for the next round became invalid. Gray lettering appeared in the waiting area. Watching it disappear, Xiao Lu understood this wasn't another explanation added beside an error for nobody to read. That half-sentence couldn't perform as before.

The whole room hadn't gone dark. Nothing done to him had been undone. But this particular agreement, attributed to him moments ago, could no longer bear down on him.

Backstage, she pressed the back of her hand to her mouth, muffling a trembling breath. She didn't touch the gray permissions or declare the whole judgment won.

“That segment's out.”

He nodded at the empty space.

“I see.”

His new voice hurt. Both legs trembled. He gave the wood more weight and waited for his breathing to slow. For the first time, crossing the room felt like something other than supplying the glass with a more pitiful pose.

FREEDOM reached 4,274, paused, then fell to 4,269. Some still argued that the crossing was staged. Others said the changed result was what mattered. He couldn't catch every comment.

Xiao Lu asked whether he could stand a little longer.

He consulted his knees before answering yes.

The citation was excluded. The overall presumption that he wished to stay remained. Other smiles, selections, and touches of Continue faded across the glass. At its center appeared a statement for the present applicant to verify.

The first half confirmed his present performance appearance. Immediately after it came continued stay.

One confirmation served both.

He raised his eyes.

The figure in the glass did the same, still a beat late.

He looked at that altered face, which had hurt alongside him all this time, and didn't step back.
