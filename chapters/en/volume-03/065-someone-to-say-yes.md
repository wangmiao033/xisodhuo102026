# Chapter 65: Someone to Say Yes

He saw it. His left fingers remained extended toward the camera. In the final second, he still wanted one more sentence to turn it back. The timer reached zero. A tone sounded.

APPROVAL: 62%. PASSED. EXECUTING.

His hand landed on a blank part of the panel.

The cold edge became a bedrail. He remembered standing by his sister's hospital bed, pushing paper toward her. Save one of them first; they'd find a way afterward. He knew each word he'd spoken, how he'd lowered his voice so the neighboring patient wouldn't hear.

She nodded. She'd made up her mind.

Relief filled him. They'd discussed it. She held no resentment. He hadn't been thrown into the aftermath unknowing. He'd even smoothed the paper for her, reminding her to keep her signature on the line.

He jerked his hand back.

The backdrop remained empty. The bedrail existed only in memory. Standing at the panel, he heard his own mouth say, “You agreed quite clearly.”

Xiao Lu didn't approach.

“I heard. That's you speaking now.”

He wanted to explain the vote. True, but he'd directed those words at her. Searching for the nod, fitting her present face into the memory, he found even her anger an inconvenience acquired later.

“Don't put the paper away.”

Her hand closed over her bag's opening beneath the chair.

He stepped toward her. His left knee weakened; the wood dragged. He didn't fall, but his right foot crowded the table leg and the broad waist tie pulled tight. Pain whitened his vision.

Wang Hai called his name.

“The bag's hers. Your own papers are in your clothes.”

His left fingers reached toward Xiao Lu, curled to receive an agreement that wasn't there. She lifted the bag behind her chair and met his eyes.

“Stop there.”

He withdrew his hand. She meant his approach. He didn't turn it into another question about continuing the broadcast. Braced on the wood, right palm lightly against the table, he placed his feet where he could see them and waited for his knee to stop folding inward.

“Light by the bed made me want to cover my eyes. I was angry. I was the sick one, but whenever somebody said they meant well, I had to reassure them first.”

The person who'd consented so readily still sat quietly in his head. Looking at Xiao Lu, he heard two utterly different feelings at once. One spared him trouble. The other was angry with him, or with someone whose face she hadn't recovered yet.

She turned her hand over, studying her palm.

“When the paper came afterward, signing wasn't my first thought.”

He waited. The water holder beneath the bowl clicked softly. She looked up as though the sound had come from far away.

“I wanted to call him back. And I didn't want him to come.”
