# Chapter 75: The Words They Cut Away

Xiao Lu turned the receipt over. No other entrance on the back, only a faint crease. She turned it faceup and matched its four code sections to the four empty fields. The console slot didn't read paper. Retrieving the sheet she'd almost fed into it, she began entering the sections with her finger.

The first two verification lines turned green. In the third was a mark so fine it resembled a scratch. Only after squinting at it did she discover the onscreen option to enlarge her own receipt.

“Could've said.”

Her complaint earned no extra use. She checked all four sections and the current objection number appended to them before reaching for Confirm.

The purpose remained original-capture verification and correction of this same objection. Her published retention request occupied the evidence field. No new demand for her name, no further agreement page, no promise this would get her brother out.

She pressed Use.

UNUSED remained printed on the receipt. The screen changed: CREDENTIAL CONNECTED. CHANNEL OPEN. It would be consumed when this channel closed. She laid the paper over the index and held its corner.

In the judgment room, the false consent abruptly lost its sound.

The picture continued, Lin Mo's mouth opening and closing as though reluctant to surrender the last word. That cropped face was still his. He glanced instinctively toward End. Still lit; the broadcast hadn't stopped.

RELATED CITATION REVIEW CONNECTED. LOADING ORIGINAL CAPTURE.

A timeline appeared across the distant glass. A white line marked the cut. Beyond it, the darkness slowly gave way to footage that hadn't been shown.

He saw himself just before standing.

No different, braver performance. He remained seated, right knee trembling, wood against his foot. Xiao Lu held her bag; the attendant stood before the ramp. He'd opened the End confirmation and backed out.

The original audio played.

“I'll go in. Keep speaking.”

At the restored ending, his right hand loosened.

He'd expected anger, an urge to demand whether everyone supplying his lines had heard. Instead, his shoulders slowly settled, as though someone had removed a weight he'd held too long.

His voice had been so quiet.

Quiet enough for those last words to vanish, leaving what sounded like unhesitating agreement. The difficult breath was there too, before Keep speaking. The picture supplied no declaration that he wanted to stay.

The timeline extended backward to his withdrawal from the End confirmation. Continuing this broadcast preserved its current freedom count. That was what his selection had done.

He leaned toward the seat microphone.

“That's it.”

The amber light came on. His new words appeared beside the original audio, each with its own timestamp, not assembled into another continuous sentence.

CITATION TEXT DOES NOT MATCH ORIGINAL CAPTURE. DISPUTE MARKER RETAINED.

PERSONAL CHOICE TO REMAIN IN THE THEATER didn't vanish. A fine line crossed it out; CORRECTION PENDING appeared beside it. The repeated montage froze on its final frame, deprived of the voice answering for him.

The line barely covered the words. At least they could no longer hang there unblemished, as though nobody had objected.

Backstage, Xiao Lu saw his hand relax before verification passed. Sweat had stuck the receipt to her palm. She peeled it free and smoothed its corner, leaving the gray controls untouched. The code had added no votes. His figures remained seventy-four percent body modification, fifty-four percent cognitive overwrite.

The audio input, however, lit.

No local test this time. RELATED COLLABORATOR CONNECTION FOR THIS OBJECTION, with the judgment seat as receiver. She checked the number, then Send, half expecting the little speaker to answer for him again.

She pressed and held.

“Ge. Can you hear me?”

He looked up too quickly, pulling his left shoulder. He stopped, eyes still on the glass that had shown the old recording, then turned to the side panel.

“Yes.”

She drew an audible breath.

“Don't move yet. I only have your upper half.”

He left the wood where it was. He'd called her through the closed door without receiving an answer. This was Xiao Lu now, quick to stop him when worried, rather than a sentence preserved from her past.

“Where are you?”

“Backstage station. Sitting down. Got my bag.”

She'd meant to explain everything. He nodded first, as though sitting down and having her bag established that she still occupied a place of her own.

“That paper. You…”

“I chose that little segment. The one you saw.”

His lips moved. She waited without preemptively forbidding thanks or demanding to know whether he'd meant to refuse for her.

“I saw.”

She acknowledged him. He lowered his gaze and settled his palm heel against the wood.

“Thank you.”

Quiet words that didn't turn her old request into a voucher for more of her past. She shifted the receipt to a dry patch.

“Let's get this sentence corrected first.”

He watched the small light beside her name.

“Back then, did you…”

He couldn't finish. Not because his voice failed, but because he didn't know whether to ask if she'd been frightened, or where she'd waited after pushing away that sheet.

Her finger pressed the crease in the receipt.

“Don't ask now.”

He looked up.

“I've only just seen it myself. I want some time to think.”

He nodded slowly. Through the connection came a chair leg scraping, a cup lid knocking as she drew her bag closer. For once he didn't urgently interpret every little sound as trouble. She could still rearrange her own things.

“All right.”

She straightened the receipt and stopped pressing its already flattened crease. Once she'd left a sentence in the old record. Now she could keep a little silence for herself.

“Look at the upper left.”

He turned his head left. She hurried to add, “Of the screen. You don't have to turn yourself.”

He stopped halfway and murmured acknowledgment.

“I'm still in this chair. Haven't moved into your left ear.”

His mouth twitched; he didn't force a laugh. A small audio-track icon occupied the panel's upper left: VERIFICATION ATTACHED TO STATEMENT. Finding it, he looked back at the crossed-out words.

The viewers were arguing already.

he still said he'd go in

Going in to speak means moving in permanently now?

Some asked whether an administrator could change FREEDOM directly. Others demanded the code onscreen as proof this wasn't staged. Xiao Lu read them without moving her hands from the console.

“It can't change votes. It can check this cut against the original audio. You've heard it.”

then guarantee as an administrator you haven't hidden anything else

“I'm not an administrator.”

Lin Mo heard her steady answer. A truth already played didn't need another private page opened to support it every time somebody asked.

He indicated the restored portion of the timeline.

“Again.”

She didn't reenter the code. The material was already available within the open channel. She moved the pointer before his pause, replaying more than the isolated missing words.

I'll go in. Keep speaking.

This time someone noticed he'd checked the consequences of ending. Obviously he couldn't bear losing the freedom votes. Another answered that this wasn't the same as being unable to bear leaving. More people struggled to pin him down with an interpretation of those two sentences.

He didn't deny his reluctance to lose them.

“I wanted to keep… my chance to get out.”

Too long. He paused before chance; the higher voice scraped his throat, its final sound weakening. Xiao Lu watched the volume bar fall without supplying a more elegant ending.

“Hear the last part?”

“Your chance to get out. Yes.”

“Good.”

He rested against the narrow seat and swallowed carefully. Recovering the old audio hadn't eased his throat. The glass still echoed a moment late. All the places forcibly changed remained here with him.

FREEDOM rose from 3,107 into the thirty-three hundreds, lost several dozen, then reached 3,612. New arrivals could see the dispute marker and hear the replay. He couldn't take that as proof they'd all finally understood.

An unfamiliar name asked whether the lower-left choice remained available. Xiao Lu gave its location without promising thanks. Another viewer had chosen prescribed performance and now wanted to change after hearing the rest. The forced round had closed; its result couldn't be voted again. FREEDOM remained independently open.

Lin Mo pointed to the referral record.

“That vote… doesn't answer for now.”

He neither erased the earlier choice nor barred a change of mind now. Even as the figure rose, people were leaving; both movements hid within a single refresh.

The backstage Send control suddenly dimmed. Xiao Lu's chest tightened. Releasing it, she discovered only that this single press-and-hold transmission had timed out. The channel remained open. She pressed again.

“Still here.”

He nodded. He'd been watching that field too, without deciding a moment's silence meant she'd been taken away again.

“Have you got water there?”

He looked at the empty seat edge and shook his head. The cup remained in her bag. They couldn't pass it through the wall.

She touched the bag's opening, then brought her question back within what she could do.

“I'll ask less. You don't have to answer everything.”

He tapped the wood, a response she could see.

The light before the seat had warmed the small patch of floor beneath his stocking. He tried drawing his right foot closer. A spasm inside his knee stopped him. Instead he eased the wood along the seat leg, making space for his toes.

“Why've you gone lower?” she asked.

“Moving my foot.”

“More inward?”

He stayed still and looked up at the camera. Remembering she couldn't see his feet, she corrected herself.

“I can't see down there. Check for yourself before moving.”

He lowered his head, probed the channel's near edge, then brought his foot back along level floor. No triumphant shot marked a challenge overcome. A stockinged toe simply ceased hovering beside the silver strip.

Only when he sat straight again did she release her lower lip. She remembered that blind spot, rather than offer another direction to prove herself useful.

Deep in the room, half the boundary around the standing position lit beside the slender microphone. A correction field had appeared. He was no longer limited to resubmitting objections. The original false citation occupied one side; a space for the applicant's correction waited beside it.

He looked between them and understood what the code had actually opened. The original audio was back. Everyone had heard the missing ending. What remained was to remove that stubborn interpretation from beneath his name.

Red lettering covered a corner of the dispute marker.

Seatkeeper: An old receipt from someone who paid his bill, and a longer edit. Enough to prove who he is now?

Xiao Lu's finger stopped on Send.

He didn't wait for her. Resting his palm on the seat edge, he checked that the words were still attached to his statement, then leaned toward the microphone.

“I'm here.”

His voice wasn't loud. Both connected ends received it.

“Ask me.”
