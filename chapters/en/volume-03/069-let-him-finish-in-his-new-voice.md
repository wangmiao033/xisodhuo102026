He stopped the preview. His voice had already changed many times. Having a female voice wasn't what he objected to. But others were using that sample to decide how he'd speak. He had no say.

“Only this change?”

The same scope remained expanded. Cognition wasn't included. He showed Xiao Lu, stood and took the two steps to End Broadcast. The wood landed beside the fixed panel, outside the equipment lane.

Plenty of time remained.

Hand beside End, he looked at his wrist scar. No wagering the final second on a suddenly understanding audience. No expectation that defiance could shield his body.

“I want to keep this session.”

She looked at him.

“It may hurt again. My voice may not work properly. I can still stop. I want to continue, not pretend they'll definitely vote it down.”

“I can keep speaking.”

“I know. But you aren't taking the pain for me.”

“Of course not. Want to leave a few things you mean to say first?”

He understood. She wasn't offering a more beautiful sacrifice. She could speak, read, decide what to do with her hands.

Did she still want to remain for this part? Yes, to help him make his words clear. She hadn't requested an end or volunteered for the next modification.

He removed his booklet, leaving its front compartment closed. Drawing unused pages from the back, he kept old pages and receipts beneath the closed front cover, blank side toward himself.

With the table gone, he returned to his stool and laid the booklet across his knees. Pressing his legs together hurt. So did resting his right wrist by the paper. He turned the book, palm heel against his clothes, writing slowly with the fingers that moved.

Xiao Lu stood beside him without taking the pen.

Before he finished the first page, the back cover slipped. He tried his left elbow. Shoulder pain skewed the paper; the pen scratched a line beside still.

“Here,” he said, indicating the cover's outer corner. “Not my arm.”

She crouched slightly and supported only the hard cover. Moving his wrist hurt, so he turned the page toward himself, sparing the pen a turn. The writing tempted him to cross it out. She reported the time remaining without hurrying him.

Legible. He left it. Plenty mattered more than handwriting.

Wang Hai half rose beyond the line, then held his right wrist and sat again. Don't wait for him to help. Xiao Lu had the cover steady; both Lin Mo's feet touched the floor.

“Watch whether I cross my legs. Hurry, and I sit the old way again.”

Wang Hai checked. Uncrossed. His hand didn't need to become another table. His eyes could still do this much.

I STILL WANT TO GET OUT.

The characters were larger than usual. The final one slanted badly; Lin Mo rewrote it beneath, rather than leave a symbol to guess. He added that spoken statements counted only with his nod.

“Make me nod to every sentence that long, and my head will drop off first.”

“Sounds like you,” Xiao Lu said. “Regulating yourself to death.”

“Shorter, then.”

Leaving the first attempt, he began another line.

WAIT FOR ME FIRST.

Wait for him to finish or decide? Both. Slow speech didn't invite anyone to supply the ending. She agreed. Once he'd finished, she released the cover and moved her wooden chair slightly ahead and beside him, within pointing range of his injured shoulder.

She took her cup from her bag, drank, offered it. He preferred to wait until afterward. Clipping the pen by the page, he checked the proposal again.

No additions. At the deadline, approval stood at sixty-one percent.

The execution notice lit.

His throat forcibly narrowed. He tried to swallow. Sharp pain pulled beside his neck; breath reached his mouth without emerging as before. He bent forward. Xiao Lu set the cup at her bag's opening beside the chair, without trying to make him drink.

He raised a palm and pressed slowly downward.

She stopped. Didn't urge him upright.

The constriction gradually passed. His next exhalation made a thin, brief sound, then rose higher than expected. He couldn't find the familiar place to push from. Forcing it lower hurt more. He unclenched his jaw and let the breath finish.

BODY MODIFICATION: 74%.

Cognition remained fifty-four. Unaffected injuries hurt as before. His right wrist and left shoulder gained no strength.

As he straightened, the booklet tilted. He steadied its cover with his palm heel before trying to speak.

“I…”

The first sound cracked. He coughed, no blood, only dry pain pulling from throat to ear. Xiao Lu lifted the cup, waited for his nod, then offered it where he could hold it securely.

Both hands cupped it loosely, their heels supporting the weight. One small sip, without throwing back his head to force it down. Pain remained, dryness eased. He returned the cup. She fastened its lid and put it inside her bag.

“Who's your little sister?” Xiao Lu asked.

Despite the pain, amusement leaked out of him as breath. She turned back, saw him smiling and stopped looking for water.

He raised the new page only slightly, indicating its first line.

“I want to get out,” she read.

He shook his head, pointed into the sentence.

Brief and rough in the new voice. She checked the writing and read again.

He nodded. Only then did she support the hard back cover and take the booklet, showing the new page to the current camera. The front stayed closed. No old story exposed: just these newly written large characters, including the crooked one rewritten beneath.

“He just wrote this. I missed still. He corrected me.”

He took the book back onto his knees. She neither attached his old voice to the words nor repeated the painful little still for greater effect.

Still fierce after modification, one viewer said. Another had only wanted a nicer voice. Had they made him mute?

His lips moved. Xiao Lu thought he was about to say he was fine. Before she could warn him, he pointed to the short line.

Wait for me first.

He swallowed, letting breath emerge from its new place. No attempt to lower it.

She nodded and waited.

“I can… still get… some words out.”

He didn't know how long the pain would last. He meant only the words he'd managed already, no promise of a loud, fluent voice next time. His fingers trembled as they left the paper.

“I heard you.”

A viewer asked what recognition changed: hadn't she always read words for other people? Xiao Lu gripped her knee without arguing. Her face darkened. Lin Mo wanted to reach her, but the book lay between them and his shoulder wouldn't rise.

He tapped the page, drawing her eyes.

“Wait for me.”

This time he wasn't addressing the audience.

He picked up the pen. The first line was crowded; he turned the booklet and slowly wrote in the space he'd left.

SHE READ IT WRONG. I CORRECTED HER. SHE DIDN'T DECIDE FOR ME.

Her mouth twitched.

“Had to include my mistake?”

He nodded, finally forming a more complete sentence in his new voice.

“This time… it's evidence.”

She laughed. His laugh was only breath before pain stopped it. He didn't pretend he'd mastered speaking or call this higher voice someone else's. He'd wanted those words written. The defiance was his too.

FREEDOM climbed from seven hundred and sixteen into the nine hundreds, then past a thousand. Some voted to see how long he'd last. Others pleaded for no more changes: they'd thought it was just replacing an audio track. He couldn't match each stated reason to the count.

He divided what he could say into two parts. Xiao Lu read his new writing, then waited for his addition. When writing took time, she left the gap instead of borrowing an audience line to fill it.

“Twelve hundred and eighty-four.”

She read the number. He watched seven disappear, unable to identify the lost choices. The book stayed in his lap. He didn't shout again for those places.

“Rest your hand.”

This time he listened. Clipping the pen back, he closed the new page against the back cover and returned the whole booklet inside his clothes, old pages and compartment still unopened. He found the wood with his foot and settled beside it, watching the controls.

“Anything else you want to say?” she asked.

The new voice remained unfamiliar, his throat sore. But he knew not to force it toward the old position. She waited without counting down.

“What you voted for… I know.”

He paused until that wave of pain eased.

“I know her. She knows me. This round… didn't change that.”

She nodded without performing another reunion to prove it. They could both see the ordinary proposal: no cognition, no kinship terms. A body change had no right to take her brother away again.

White comments crowded upward.

One red line stayed where it was.

Seatkeeper: Nice cooperation. So you do know how to perform to the vote.

Lin Mo looked up.

The red name brought the warmth of Old Chen's disappearing hand back into his palm. He didn't rise to chase someone he couldn't see. His left fingers closed on the wood until they whitened.

Xiao Lu saw the name. Her smile vanished.

Slowly, in the voice they'd just changed, Lin Mo spoke it.

“Seatkeeper.”
