# Chapter 83: Make the Hand Bear on Its Own Frame

The beam settled, leaving a slit of light above Lin Mo.

He didn't run toward the newly enclosed wall. The outermost locking tooth still trembled. Put a stockinged toe there and it might catch. He stayed on the groove's right side, on floor he knew.

The giant's second hand braced overhead. Below, its damaged hand spread four intact digits along the frame, searching for another opening.

The metal tongue lay behind them. He glimpsed it before the palm covered the lower edge. Trapped mirror fragments crackled without disintegrating; one still held the seam open.

“Retainer underneath,” he said. “Hand's covering it.”

Backstage, the attendant set his narrow panel on the empty console and opened the inspection request. Another attendant answered from the front-stage duty point.

Xiao Lu held Send, letting the judgment room hear.

“Someone's outside your door.”

A heavy sound came through as the front attendant pushed the gray transfer panel. It didn't open. He checked the black frame's base and reported loaded floor catches and a ramp that couldn't be raised. The old transfer-interruption control couldn't help.

“What about the manual route?” Xiao Lu asked. “The one you recorded.”

“Checking the outer inspection cover. Can't stop the performance from there.”

She asked whether anyone stood where the structure might fall.

A familiar voice answered after a pause.

“On the bench. Haven't touched the door.”

Wang Hai.

Lin Mo looked toward the narrow stage window beside his seat. From here, it was only a bright strip. Wang Hai explained that the attendant had brought the panel over. He could hear. His hand was hurt; he hadn't stood.

“Stay clear of the black frame.”

An acknowledgment, then scraping bench legs. With the attendant's help, Wang Hai moved toward the stage's side, farther from the structure. Hearing him hadn't summoned him into the room. Lin Mo still faced the floor alone.

The lower frame jolted forward.

A thumb forced through the seam, reaching for the half-shoulder he'd exposed while speaking. He kept his wood low and offered its sound middle, not the split end. Contact jarred his injured wrist.

He loosened his grip, yielding with the blow. The wood slid across the floor; the thumb pursued it.

Lin Mo lowered himself the other way. The strike landed half a step off, flinging grit past his neck. Glass dust scraped the side of his left palm when it touched down, tugging the old wound beneath his torn sleeve. He shifted pressure toward the unbroken skin at the heel of his hand.

His sleeve and slanting wood remained in the lowest bright frame.

Overhead mirrors turned. Their images already showed him crouching. Before the thumb withdrew, its wrist began twisting, as though waiting for another picture to take control.

He pulled the wood back.

The thumb swept toward his actual position. Keeping low, he cleared his knee as its tip dented the groove's silver edge.

The upper eyes still saw.

He could move while this lower hand changed pictures, provided he moved before its next turn.

“Pauses before changing direction,” he panted. “Keep my picture there.”

Xiao Lu stopped searching for the inactive front camera and retained the side feed. She could preserve her own view, not turn off the mirrors above him.

“Same one. Only your sleeve now.”

The lowest mirror showed that same scrap. Its bright frame narrowed around the cloth while the thumb turned toward it. Higher images still held his whole body elsewhere.

He remembered the relationship. During the thumb's withdrawal, he regained the step he'd retreated, then moved closer along the groove. The microphone's round base passed behind him. At last, the lower frame lay within reach of his wood.

He wasn't testing whether they could see him. They always could. He needed to know which picture this protruding thumb would follow first.

He reversed the wood, keeping the split end toward him, and probed forward with the sound tip.

The black frame's casing extended below the glass. Near the floor, a lip projected outward. When the thumb withdrew, the lip shifted inward. When it emerged, its own weight tipped the lip slightly open.

He didn't put his fingers inside. His first probe missed; the second found metal.

The front attendant reported locating the inspection cover. Loaded structural parts lay beneath. He could inspect them, but couldn't release the loaded catch from there. Raising the cover slightly produced a dull knock.

The metal tongue beside Lin Mo's feet quivered.

“Touch it again. Lightly.”

Another knock. Another movement of the tongue.

The two ends belonged to the same frame. He no longer had to interpret a sliver of reflection as some mysterious mechanism.

“Heard that,” Wang Hai said. “On your side.”

“Don't pry inward. I'll move the hand.”

Lin Mo raised the wood into the side camera's remaining view. His shoulder stayed low, sleeve at the bright frame's edge. He angled the wood left, beyond his actual position.

The thumb emerged.

He waited until the lowest shoulder image brightened, then lowered the wood and withdrew toward the groove's right side. The thumb pressed along its previous direction. Its tip missed him. Its heavy base landed squarely on the black casing.

A dull crash.

Upper panels were turning already. The thumb tried to change course, but its own pressure had opened the lip. More of the metal tongue showed, its tail crossing a shallow slot.

He pushed the wood in.

The sound end passed beneath the lip; the shaft lay across the real silver edge. The damaged section remained outside. His right hand guided it while his left heel pressed the shaft toward the floor. He wasn't asking his injured wrist to lift the hand.

The wood caught.

The giant palm withdrew sharply. The lip failed to close, compressing the wood with a brief groan. Lin Mo released it and lifted his heel.

It stayed wedged.

The metal tongue lay exposed against deeper darkness. His walking support was trapped there too.

He sat beside the groove, facing away from the frame, and drew his right leg clear of fragments. The notebook dug into his waist. He nudged it back against his ribs with his hand's back. Pen still clipped, covers closed.

His wood had bought an opening that wouldn't immediately shut.

When the giant hand lifted, the shaft rose slightly. He didn't grab it. The metal tongue's tail lifted from its slot, exposing a worn line beneath. As the hand pressed down again, the tail settled.

That piece of metal was carrying a load.

He touched the exposed shaft with his right heel, testing a possible new direction. The split produced a sliver. He stopped. It hadn't broken yet; that didn't give him ten more tries.

There might be one useful effort left.

Overhead, the second hand pulled the beam lower. Images crowded together, a shoulder here, legs there. None offered an undivided measure of distance.

He ducked falling dust. Wang Hai asked whether something had collapsed.

“Not yet. Stay back.”

Scraping ceased on the other end. A person could hear him and stop. The hands in the mirror continued following their pictures.

Xiao Lu saw his familiar support pause at the screen's edge while his body dropped.

“Sitting,” he told her. “Wood's holding the frame. Still whole.”

“I know where you are.”

FREEDOM passed thirty-seven thousand, slipped, and climbed again. Some demanded he retrieve the wood. Others urged him to wrench the retainer barehanded or asked why the attendants merely watched.

The front attendant lowered the inspection cover. Raising it farther would skew the loaded casing. Wang Hai told him not to put a hand underneath.

A muffled curse followed. Lin Mo didn't know whose. For an instant, he almost smiled.

Someone outside remembered hands weren't replaceable.

Red lettering appeared.

Seatkeeper: An unfinished outline. How is it supposed to recognize you properly?

The giant kept moving. Its broken forefinger traced the lower frame while the thumb tried to reach around the crosswise wood. Lin Mo edged backward, with no wall to lean against, and braced one foot on the floor.

An ordinary proposal covered the glass's lower-right corner.

COMPLETE THE APPLICANT'S REMAINING PHYSICAL FEMINIZATION: 74% TO 100%.

NO COGNITIVE OVERWRITE INCLUDED. CURRENT EXIT STATEMENT AND FREEDOM CHOICES UNCHANGED. EXECUTION UPON APPROVAL.

Approve and Oppose appeared below. Approval required sixty percent of the proposal's valid choices at closing. Unselected positions didn't count toward approval. Departed and invalid choices were excluded. Votes could change before the deadline.

Ninety seconds began counting down.

BODY MODIFICATION remained 74%. COGNITIVE OVERWRITE remained 54%. His hand still shook from releasing the wood. The new wording hadn't yet given the pain another place to live.

“Read the scope.”

Xiao Lu read the bodily change, seventy-four to one hundred, then the exclusion of cognition. Her voice grew flatter. She didn't repeat the red lettering about recognition.

Would finishing mean they stop changing him?

This far already. Get it over with.

He said this was his body now. What's he afraid of?

Someone answered that he'd asked to leave, not asked them to finish him.

Lin Mo watched the wood. He feared the lip snapping shut. He feared another wave of pain would leave him unable even to sit. He didn't know where the remaining twenty-six would act. Across the glass, people were selecting approval on his behalf anyway.

“I didn't ask for this.”

Quiet, but audible.

I voted freedom. Approving this too. Nothing wrong with both, is there?

“You can choose both,” he said. “Still hurts me.”

He didn't demand withdrawal of the freedom vote as proof of anything. Both boxes existing together made that claimed kindness colder.

Backstage, Xiao Lu moved her bag strap from underfoot. She didn't search for exhausted paperwork. She asked the attendant to relay the proposal to the front and whether the inspection could pause its execution.

The answer returned through the panel. No. Site staff could clear people, inspect the frame, and guard routes. They had no control for stopping this proposal.

She told Lin Mo before he wasted the countdown waiting for a solution no one could provide.

“I'm here. People outside too.”

He looked up. The second giant hand slowly tightened on the beam. A pale compression mark spread across the wood below, but the metal tongue remained exposed. The new proposal hadn't undone what he'd accomplished.

He cleared small fragments with the edge of his hand, making a place for his shoulder and back to land, then moved his feet out of the wood's load path.

Without his support, he couldn't quickly return to the narrow seat. End still shone, and he knew the route. Reaching it now meant first lifting himself off the floor.

“Don't close that inspection cover tight,” Wang Hai said. “He's still got wood in there.”

Someone answered.

FREEDOM reached 43,180, then lost twelve. Lin Mo kept watching the gap.

Twenty-seven seconds remained.

Above the wood, the giant palm waited for him to move first.
