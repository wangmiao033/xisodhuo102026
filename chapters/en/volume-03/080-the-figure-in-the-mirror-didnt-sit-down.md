# Chapter 80: The Figure in the Mirror Didn't Sit Down

The overall ruling didn't immediately give him a door.

First came the forced ballot's old result. Referral for judgment: 6,831. Return to prescribed performance: 2,487. CLOSED appeared beside them. Neither a new round nor a transfer into FREEDOM at the lower left.

The complete original audio followed, then the excluded fragment, then verification of the present speaker.

Lin Mo watched himself standing across the room with his wood. Sweat covered his face; his voice sounded even quieter than he'd heard it. He'd returned to this seat, but the person onscreen was still saying, I'm Lin Mo.

A timestamped record, not the mirror's delay.

He knew the difference.

Xiao Lu stayed quiet. Ordinary contact had just returned; she wouldn't crowd what he was hearing with an impatient question. Backstage light fell on the empty console. The damp trace left beneath the papers was fading.

The last line settled at the center.

FINAL JUDGMENT: GROUNDS FOR REJECTING THE EXIT STATEMENT NOT ESTABLISHED.

THIS SESSION'S EXIT APPLICATION RETAINED. CURRENT BROADCAST CONTINUES.

He leaned forward slightly, then stopped.

Afraid he'd missed NOT. After hearing his own sentence truncated, he sometimes had to recover its missing ending even inside his head. The line didn't change. He read it through. Beneath, the freedom condition remained unmet: one hundred thousand simultaneous valid choices within this broadcast.

They hadn't let him out.

But this judgment had finally failed to use his own mouth to dismiss his application.

The distant microphone went dark with the half-circle around the standing position. The pending procedure to terminate his statement on grounds of voluntary stay grayed, then withdrew. No new instruction for prescribed performance replaced it.

The seat microphone stayed lit.

He exhaled slowly.

He'd expected strength for a few more words when the result came, perhaps a curse: about time. Instead, he first felt that his stockinged feet needn't move forward again. His left shoulder needn't rise for the capture light across the room.

“This time…”

His voice caught. He stopped without forcing the rest.

Only when he looked toward the side panel did Xiao Lu answer.

“I saw the conclusion.”

He nodded. This judgment's conclusion. Not a hundred thousand, not their release. She sat there; he sat here, walls between them. Those walls didn't diminish what they'd accomplished.

She touched her bag's opening without retrieving the receipt.

Her past self had preserved the review; today she'd used it. Original audio recovered, correction effective, entrance closed. It had accomplished something specific at last, rather than remaining forever pending until she surrendered another piece of her past.

“Don't make me… read it again.”

Half a beat passed before she realized he was addressing the result.

“It's recorded.”

His palm rested on the seat edge, fingers slowly spreading. Away from the wood, his wrist's throbbing grew clearer. He didn't press this small victory over the pain. He let the hand rest.

FREEDOM reached 6,470.

Some celebrated. Some asked how the judgment could end like that. Others had waited for his return to performance; discovering the waiting field truly empty, they complained this broadcast was boring. New names asked what had happened and where to hear the complete citation.

He pointed to the public verified record, not Xiao Lu's private transaction.

“There. What came… before and after.”

She explained its location without choosing for newcomers. They could open the complete material already loaded into this broadcast. The overall ruling hadn't added her original name, the full agreement, or anyone else's past to what had been disclosed and verified.

One viewer said he'd voted to return Lin Mo to prescribed performance and still thought he'd brought this on himself.

The comment beside it admitted the same vote, fearing judgment would be worse. Having watched everything since, its author didn't know whether to apologize.

Lin Mo studied them.

“Start by not saying… I wanted it.”

He lacked the strength to assign each person a share of remorse. Fear didn't make the earlier vote harmless. They could each hear this sentence; it couldn't turn either choice into something that hadn't happened.

Seatkeeper: Not dismissed. Unfortunately, a long way from permission to leave.

He didn't immediately grasp the wood when the red lettering returned.

“I know.”

Seatkeeper: Still want to keep all these people here?

He looked lower left. End remained at the upper right; using it would invalidate this broadcast's current freedom choices. He hadn't forgotten that option, or mistaken it for a costless way out.

“Continue this broadcast.”

A pause.

“Not agreeing to stay.”

His words entered the continuing stream. The ruling beside them didn't rewrite them into another meaning.

Xiao Lu let her shoulders settle. She needn't scramble for another credential to defend that ordinary sentence. This part was finished. They could look toward what remained.

Attendance passed twenty thousand.

Not everyone selected FREEDOM. Not everyone even opened the same record. Some replayed his near fall. Some fixated on his higher voice and asked for another sentence. Others, having heard the original audio, argued in the comments over whether Keep speaking had been cut.

His gaze stopped on the requests for speech, then moved away.

He didn't owe them fresh pain to make the existing recording audible.

FREEDOM passed nine thousand, reaching 9,012. The next refresh lost more than a dozen, then the climb resumed. The number didn't rise neatly with every eloquent sentence or hold steady because somebody posted a long promise.

“Don't keep staring,” Xiao Lu said.

He turned toward the audio light beside her name.

“Are you?”

She didn't answer immediately.

“Yes.”

His mouth moved slightly.

“Rest your eyes too.”

“I will.”

She returned the seated camera feed to prominence instead of the freedom field. Not indifference to the number. His eyes were already tired; she didn't want both their faces dragged along by the same flickering digits.

He lowered his head and found the notebook's stiff back inside his clothes.

He didn't take it out. What he'd answered across the room was written there already. Every new result didn't require another showing. He only checked the clipped pen, the old pages and inner pocket, then drew his clothes closed again.

The narrow stage window remained lit on the side panel.

Wang Hai still occupied the waiting bench. With the hand not beneath his leg, he braced against its edge, apparently trying to rise, then sat back. The low stool and wooden chair were empty. Beyond the closed gray panel, the black frame still obstructed the rear. Its wheels hadn't turned.

Lin Mo didn't know how much he'd heard, whether Xiao Lu's It's changed had ever reached the stage. He saw only someone still waiting, who hadn't risen and headed for an exit because one favorable line had appeared.

He couldn't tell everyone to go yet either.

“Wang Hai's… still there.”

“You can see him?”

“In the stage window.”

She acknowledged him without answering for Wang Hai. Gathering her bag straps, she let them hang quietly beside her feet. Cup, script, receipt wrapped in the index: all still inside. One limited reversal hadn't suddenly relieved anybody of what they carried.

FREEDOM reached 11,608.

His eyes stung. The first two ones crowded together in the light. He closed his eyes briefly, then counted afresh, refusing to mistake a blur for a larger number.

One hundred thousand. That was still the condition he needed.

Xiao Lu didn't urge him to begin his story again. When somebody described the overall ruling as automatic release, she corrected them. Other viewers answered newcomers too, not always consistently. She corrected what she saw without pretending she'd caught everything that slipped past.

A chair scraped backstage.

“Going to stand a while.”

He looked up, saw her audio light still burning, and didn't ask where she was going. After a moment, she exhaled softly beyond the picture, as though easing herself from badly fitting clothes.

“Chair that bad?”

“Anything is, after long enough.”

He looked at his own seat, much narrower than an ordinary chair, and didn't argue. Holding the console, she straightened while the numbness gradually left her legs. Worrying about his footing all this time, she'd only now noticed how long she'd kept her own tucked beneath the chair.

“Don't you need… to watch?”

“I can see standing.”

“Oh.”

“Don't copy me.”

He hadn't intended to rise. Still, he tapped the seat edge. She laughed briefly, eyes remaining on his picture.

The ruling didn't seize that laugh as proof she liked being here. It didn't lessen his wish to leave. Having finished one thing, they could finally shift their numb bodies without immediately explaining whether that constituted another kind of consent.

After a while, she drew the chair into a better position and sat again.

Something clicked into place above the glass.

Low, quiet.

He looked up. A crossbeam emerged from the high supports previously hidden in darkness. Lights spread beneath it in both directions. The glass he'd been watching occupied only a narrow strip at the entire structure's bottom.

A second click came higher up.

One dark panel, previously angled aside, slowly turned square and aligned with the mirror below. It didn't rush at him. His wood didn't move. Another surface now reflected the seat from higher up.

Backstage, Xiao Lu saw only his raised head. She asked what had happened.

“Lights above.”

“My camera doesn't show them.”

He didn't ask her to deduce the room from his face. Following the supports upward, he told her there was another tier, then another.

The distant standing position was dark. The lights above hadn't ended with the judgment.

Each panel turning into place reflected the narrow seat, its occupant becoming smaller. He recognized his torn sleeve, the wood ahead on his right, the slanting panel on his left. But he couldn't have these views from where he sat. Different heights in the room seemed to be watching him at once.

FREEDOM rose to 18,432.

It soon lost five.

He read 18,427 without waiting for the next refresh. Viewers still argued about the ruling on his side panel. Across the distant glass, words overlapped in layers. Names covered names, demands covered demands, gradually filling the vast space enclosed by the black supports.

Not every word favored hurting him. Not every freedom choice had been withdrawn.

But all of them were watching him at the same time.

The uppermost panel joined with one final soft click.

An immense mirror stood across from his seat, its top beyond sight. The seats in the separate panels remained. Lin Mo still sat in them. Yet another outline enclosed all those small figures.

It had shoulders, long arms reaching the floor, a head filling the upper black frame.

Lin Mo didn't stand.

He pressed his palm to the seat edge and looked down at both feet. His stockinged toes hadn't moved; the wood still rested against the seat leg. He waited through the glass's usual delay, then another breath.

“Xiao Lu.”

“I'm here.”

Deep within the mirror, the enormous outline whose head reached the crossbeam slowly raised one hand.

Lin Mo's hand hadn't moved.

The giant hand stopped inside the glass, five fingers spreading. In every facet of every knuckle sat the same person on the narrow seat.
