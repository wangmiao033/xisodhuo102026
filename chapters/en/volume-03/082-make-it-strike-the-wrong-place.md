# Chapter 82: Make It Strike the Wrong Place

The fingertip failed to grip the floor.

It slid along the silver edge, scraping a white line through the little seat reflected in its surface. Lin Mo recognized the sound from when his wood had slipped into the shallow groove. This time it lasted long enough to hurt his teeth.

He stopped thinking of the hand as a shadow that could pass through anything. It had weight. It needed somewhere to put it.

Four fingers held the lower frame inside the mirror. The forefinger protruded through a seam that hadn't fully opened. When it lifted, the surrounding glass bulged. When it pressed down, the silver floor edge rang.

Lin Mo moved along the groove's right side.

The finger turned toward where he'd been and struck the fixed seat leg. The leg held; a pale abrasion appeared on the finger pad. Impact shivered through his soles into his bad knee.

Don't run, he told his legs. You can't. Fall, and getting up will be worse.

Backstage, the attendant examined the cracked seat covering and the bottom of Xiao Lu's picture.

“Where's the upper frame?”

“Outside this camera. Can you stop it?”

He checked his narrow panel. Transfer complete. There was no ongoing handover to interrupt. He didn't pretend pressing an empty field repeatedly would help.

“Need the main frame's local status. Duty board's outside.”

Xiao Lu looked at her brother, almost beyond the edge of the picture.

“I'm going to the board outside the door with him. Leaving contact open, but I won't hear while I'm away.”

Lin Mo knocked the floor once. She picked up her bag and followed the attendant, neither surrendering her station nor ending collaboration.

A faded diagram hung beneath the duty light.

A black line ran from the front stage to the judgment room. Another passed backstage and branched toward several stations. The attendant placed his panel against a reader; lights beside the diagram came on in sequence. Xiao Lu found her console number. Its doorway remained immediately behind her, with no verification line between.

The main frame's light was red.

“Still carrying its load.”

The drawing showed several retainers gripping the frame. She didn't try to use an overhead diagram to tell her brother which one he could touch. She asked where people could go.

The attendant indicated a dotted route ending in TEMPORARY OBSERVATION HOLDING. Thick walls enclosed it on all four sides.

“Actual rupture of the main frame, affected stage positions canceled. Then this local safety route opens. It isn't open now.”

“Can't someone bring him out from here?”

He moved his finger to the solid line ending at the transfer door. Lin Mo's admission was complete; this panel couldn't reverse it as an unfinished handover. Another branch allowed an on-site manual inspection. The attendant recorded the pressure damage and said he'd check.

Xiao Lu wasn't satisfied. She repeated the station number anyway, making sure the inspection request named the right room. An inadequate answer was no reason to let the one available action disappear into the corridor.

Then she read the faded text beneath the diagram and stopped halfway through.

AFFECTED PERFORMERS MUST COMPLETE RELEASE FROM THEIR CURRENT PERFORMER-IDENTITY BINDING. FOLLOWING RELEASE, ORDINARY VIEWER REGISTRATION MAY BE REQUESTED INDIVIDUALLY UNDER THE CURRENT IDENTITY.

She flattened the diagram toward the light. The faded words lay beneath the same dust as the diagram. No one had just added a reward for her.

“So ending collaboration lets me go there?”

“No. Performers and accompanying performers don't count as viewers. Main-frame failure first. The affected positions stop, then performer-identity release. Closing contact or changing stations isn't that.”

He pointed at the walls around the dotted route's end.

“That's holding. The theater still owns the doors.”

She asked whether registration would let someone select this broadcast. He checked the sequence before answering: registration granted, the person maintaining valid current presence, then the same single choice as an ordinary viewer. No automatic enrollment. No votes cast for them. No line restoring a canceled account.

“First we need somewhere safe to put people. None of those conditions has been met.”

Xiao Lu didn't retrieve the receipt. It had finished its job. The cup knocked against her script inside the bag, still just two ordinary belongings.

She remembered the two turns before holding without trying its locked door, then returned to her console.

In the judgment room, Lin Mo reached the stretch where he'd almost fallen before.

The calf wound hadn't bled much. Blood clung to stocking fibers and stung when he bent his knee. He couldn't spare a hand to touch it. The wood already stood on the real right side of the groove.

In the glass, the silver line shone farther out.

The finger reached diagonally across the groove toward his image. A thin mirrored nail touched down first, pressing toward what looked like level floor. Its edge missed and dropped half an inch.

The hand hesitated.

Lin Mo stayed still.

The floor reflected in its pad hadn't registered the dip. Inside the distant frame, the picture still joined that area into one flat surface. The finger pressed along the wrong position, then adjusted, trying to lift the edge that had slipped below.

An upper pane shifted its angle.

He saw the top of his head in it. The forefinger veered right sooner than he'd expected, scoring dust where he'd intended to step. He kept his foot planted until it passed.

The higher panels watched too. A little delay didn't mean the room was blind.

He looked back toward the lowest bright rectangle. During that downward press, its silver edge had remained offset from the edge beneath his feet. That small discrepancy was all he could use. He dared assume nothing beyond it.

The first time, the finger didn't catch.

The nail scraped over the silver strip and lifted free. He shifted sideways, letting the point land outside his toes rather than waiting for it to clear the floor completely.

His wood struck the burr with a familiar click.

He'd struggled to extract it here before. The dark mark remained on the wood, and the narrow gap that had caught its end remained in the floor.

He crouched a little and angled the wood toward the groove.

He couldn't overturn the finger. His damaged hand barely held the newly stripped grain. He needed only to shift an edge that had already dropped into the channel a little farther sideways.

Xiao Lu's voice returned.

“Ge. I'm back. Same station.”

“Don't give directions.”

Seeing his face almost beyond the picture, she stopped what she'd meant to say.

“All right. Someone's here. Checking the room.”

The fingertip approached again.

Lin Mo touched the mirrored nail with the wood. No flinch of pain, only a scraping sound. Striking it wouldn't frighten it away.

The finger turned after the support visible inside the bright rectangle.

He drew the wood back a few inches and moved outside the actual silver edge. The notebook dug into his waist, reminding him that his torso hadn't turned far enough to let his shoulder bear weight. He didn't force the turn, only widened his stance.

His image lagged.

The finger pressed onto the flatness the reflection had invented. Its thin nail edge fell into the groove. Lin Mo brought the wood against its side without swinging his arm. He leaned in with the small weight shift he'd already prepared.

Less than an inch.

The sharp corner slid beneath the burr.

He released the pressure and tried to retrieve his support. At that moment the joint descended, trapping the wood between nail and silver edge. Pain tore through his right wrist.

He opened his hand.

The wood stayed in the groove. He fell sideways. His left palm met the floor; he couldn't save his old forearm wound from scraping through the torn sleeve, but he kept his shoulder from taking the full impact.

The giant finger lifted.

The burr couldn't imprison the whole hand. It held only the nail's corner. As everything else pulled backward, the thin plate and the heavy finger pad began moving in different directions.

The first crack was quiet.

Close to the floor, Lin Mo saw a white line race from the trapped corner across the first joint. Two more cracks followed. The four fingers gripping the frame inside the mirror shook.

He drew his feet away as fragments dropped where he'd been.

The outer joint split. Half the mirrored nail tipped into the groove. The long finger snapped back inside; the seam clapped shut, then a trapped fragment forced it apart again.

The wood sprang free and rolled against his right leg.

Xiao Lu called his name. Before answering, he pinned it down so it couldn't roll beyond reach.

“Still here.”

He stayed bent over for two ragged breaths.

The finger hadn't taken him with it. He hadn't won an intact piece of the creature, either. The half-nail in the groove was breaking into smaller shards. Little faces went dark across it until only gray-black backing remained.

A split ran along the wood's lower end, shorter than his palm. He tested it with a fingertip without deciding that it could still bear his full weight.

Xiao Lu didn't celebrate the cracking sounds.

“Where are you?”

“Right of the groove. On the floor.”

“I can't see you.”

He lifted the wood into the bottom of the earlier framing. A small length of scoured grain appeared on her screen.

“There.”

He lowered it, placing the unbroken side against the floor. With his left foot and right elbow, he gradually arranged himself to rise. White chips clung to his palm. He left the darkened nail alone.

Behind the glass, fresh pictures overlapped the finger's damaged tip without immediately rebuilding it.

FREEDOM climbed past twenty-seven thousand, then fell to 26,900. Some viewers left when he couldn't rise. Others finally found the choice. Broken glass didn't cast a vote for anyone or preserve their presence forever.

He stopped counting the flickering last digits and checked the path back to the seat, remembering three new sharp fragments in case he needed to retreat.

One comment asked whether he'd won. Another demanded he smash the rest. He looked at the main frame still rising from the floor into darkness.

“Finger cracked.”

His eyes were level with the silver strip. Following the direction of the finger's withdrawal, he found a narrow dark opening in the lower seam. A metal tongue held the bottom frame there. It rocked briefly, then a hanging reflection concealed it.

He remembered the position without reaching inside.

Xiao Lu relayed what she'd learned: actual main-frame failure, performer-identity release, temporary holding still locked within the theater. She didn't turn the dotted line into an open escape route.

“Only then can I request viewer registration. This identity, one choice. Not now.”

Lin Mo pushed himself halfway up before answering.

“Don't go wait there.”

“I didn't. Original console.”

Behind her, the attendant asked whether lower-frame damage was visible. She asked him to let her brother stand first. Once on his feet, Lin Mo reported a fractured finger joint and a main frame still standing. Those were different results.

A short alert sounded from the attendant's panel.

The safety route hadn't opened.

Both side columns in the judgment room began turning inward. The narrow faces previously aimed at the glass rotated into the room. Lin Mo watched the right-hand silver line disappear behind a descending black edge. Teeth locked along the floor, barring the level strip toward the wall.

He retreated one small step toward the narrow seat.

End remained lit. FREEDOM refreshed to 31,246.

The last overhead beam slowly closed. Inside the mirror, the giant with the damaged finger raised its other hand.

This time it didn't brace its own lower frame.

It braced the room closing above Lin Mo's head.
