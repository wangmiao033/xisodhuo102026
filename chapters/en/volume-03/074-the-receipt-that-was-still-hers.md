# Chapter 74: The Receipt That Was Still Hers

The hand onscreen put the pen cap on the wrong way.

Xiao Lu watched it pause, pull the cap off, and fit it to the other end. She almost laughed. So she'd always found useless things to busy herself with when nervous.

This wasn't the agreement sample Lin Mo had seen. PRE-SIGNATURE SUPPLEMENTARY RETENTION appeared at the picture's edge, under the protection-payment transaction number. The closed identity field was concealed. Only the link between its owner and her current performance number remained.

She couldn't remember the pen's color. Blue onscreen, then black when the light changed. She didn't force memory to choose, simply to make the past feel more convincingly hers.

A voice outside the shot asked whether she wanted to retain one opportunity to review cited evidence.

The person holding the pen looked up. The camera showed no face, only fingers tightening on the paper's corner.

“For whom?”

Her old voice. Not every word felt familiar, but the pause to ask before acting stopped her hand from reaching toward Fast Forward.

If the protected person's statements were cited as evidence that he wished to perform, the voice explained, the related party could use the retained receipt to check one citation against the original capture and enter the same objection's correction channel.

No additional protection period. No reversal of payment. No deciding for Lin Mo whether to stay or leave.

The hand remained still a long time.

“Keep it, then.”

“Once your original identity is closed, this won't form part of your ordinary account permissions. It must be sealed.”

“Seal it.”

Her finger moved to a small box: retain or waive. No second protection-payment agreement to sign. She selected retain and pushed the sheet forward.

“If someone says he's willing, let him speak for himself.”

The recording stopped.

Xiao Lu stayed seated without immediately pressing Next. She'd expected only an account of what she'd paid, how she'd disappeared. She hadn't expected this small thing her past self had tried to leave behind.

It couldn't take anyone straight outside. Might not even open a door. But the theater hadn't suddenly awarded it to her today for recognizing her brother.

The right was linked to the protected person's identity, not a particular performance. It applied only when his statement was cited and he personally objected. Comparing Lin Mo's registered edit point against those terms, she saw that this instance fitted their narrow scope.

The sealed acknowledgment bore the same transaction completion time. Original account closed; unused review credential retained with the transaction. She could find it now because her current performance number had led to her own linked record.

She paused on the finger that had selected the box.

“Couldn't you have asked a few more questions?”

The recording couldn't answer. She opened Scope and asked for her present self.

In the judgment room, Lin Mo faced another picture.

Himself again. A half-body shot on the low stool preserved his expression as Xiao Lu laughed, then joined it to his finger pressing Continue. Brief chimes bridged the scraps of speech, disguising the pauses.

I still want to speak. I'll go in.

Below: PERSONAL CHOICE TO REMAIN IN THE THEATER.

He saw himself smiling, saw his present reflection lift its head late. That softer face hadn't made this decision for him. The words beneath it already had.

“That isn't… what I said.”

The seat microphone registered another objection. The assembled picture remained, playing quietly before him.

Seatkeeper: Isn't that your hand?

He didn't deny it. His right wrist hurt inside his sleeve. The onscreen touch had indeed belonged to this hand.

“The hand's mine. The words… aren't.”

clicked it yourself. now you don't like the result

The referral record remained on the side panel, explicitly stating that the result granted no consent to past or future modifications. But the current falsehood concerned something before modification: his repeated wish to leave.

He needed to distinguish them. Difficulty speaking couldn't be allowed to merge them into one thing.

“I chose to continue… this session.”

Pain pulled down from his throat. He closed his mouth and waited for a complete breath.

“Not to stay.”

The reflected mouth closed later. Suddenly he hated the delay, hated how it prolonged each difficult opening of his mouth. He reached inside his clothes for the notebook. No old page would decide for his present self. Writing was simply another way available to him.

He laid its stiff back across his knees. The front cover secured the old pages and inner compartment; he opened only the new page from this session. I STILL WANT OUT remained there, including his correction beside the badly written character.

He studied it and slowly relaxed his right wrist.

The new sentence was short. In the remaining space, he wrote: I DID NOT SAY I WANTED TO STAY.

At wanted, the pen stopped twice. His wrist scar pulled tight. He didn't cross out the half-line and start again for the camera. He supplied the missing stroke.

He raised the page, keeping the other contents behind the front cover. The camera beneath the glass caught it. A thumbnail of the written statement entered the capture record.

The assembled picture didn't disappear.

At least his own different sentence was in the picture now too.

Backstage, Xiao Lu initially thought the raised notebook meant he was looking for her. She reached halfway toward the audio input, then stopped.

The local test wasn't her brother. She'd tried it. No point calling herself again.

She enlarged the feed until she could just read the line. His cover kept slipping as he awkwardly shielded the old pages. He pressed it back with his thumb without lifting his left shoulder.

“I know. Don't keep holding it up.”

The words weren't transmitted. He didn't lower the notebook because of them. He waited for the thumbnail to settle, closed both covers, clipped the pen back, and returned everything inside his clothes. The wood still rested against the seat's foot. He placed his hand on it again.

She watched a little longer, then returned to her sealed attachment.

Its title contained the words ADMINISTRATOR CODE.

She didn't rush to enter it anywhere. Beneath, the permission occupied one short line: RELATED CITATION REVIEW. Most neighboring controls were gray. Account restoration, performance commands, vote entry: none available.

The source field identified a restricted review credential issued to the relevant party in a backend administrative format. Generated during supplementary retention for the original protection transaction. Uses: zero.

It didn't mean she'd once been an administrator.

She laid the index beside the onscreen reference number. Index to old transaction; transaction to supplementary retention; retention to this same receipt. She followed the links before touching the final sealed marker.

The code didn't appear immediately. First came the applicable material she had to check before use.

Only relevant clause excerpts, visible to her, not sent into the broadcast.

Protection Payment, Clause Four: protection eligibility took effect before payment was executed. Specific identity protection during the beginner stage did not exempt its subject from physical adaptation or performance penalties.

Clause Seven: completed payment closed the original identity account and revoked real-world links. The protected person's memories were excluded from this payment.

She finished reading. Her own throat felt dry.

Back then, these words hadn't made every consequence feel real. Now her brother sat before her, his new voice hurting, old injuries unhealed. Protection having taken effect and these later harms could coexist on one sheet.

The credential added no exemptions to the agreement. These clauses stayed backstage for her inspection. Use would verify only the selected objection and purpose, requiring no additional surrender of her past. This narrow route ended at the sentence Lin Mo was contesting.

One final condition: when the correction channel closed, the credential was consumed. It could not reopen a second objection.

She kept looking. She knew there would be no second receipt after this one, and knew what it couldn't buy.

She closed Scope and selected only the current objection. Other private pages and performance records stayed untouched. No original name needed filling in. The supplementary requester's closed identity remained verifiably linked to the present Xiao Lu.

“Just these.”

No attendant stood beside her to nod.

The screen asked whether to extract the credential for checking. Actual use required separate confirmation at the linked-objection entry. Before extraction, she had one present decision to make: publish the selected retention-request segment to establish her link as its holder.

She opened the segment. No whole agreement, no name field. Only the hand with the pen, her voice agreeing to retain the review. She could cancel extraction and leave the receipt sealed.

Her hand rested on the console edge.

Remembering her past wasn't the same as letting all these people hear her then. The voice sounded fairly calm. But her fingers had creased the paper's corner, betraying what she'd tried to hide.

She checked the selected segment to its end and pressed Submit herself.

An old voice sounded in the judgment room.

“Keep it, then.”

Lin Mo looked up sharply, fingers tightening on the wood. The following picture was marked RETENTION REQUEST, dated to the old transaction. Xiao Lu's name stopped in his throat. This wasn't her speaking to him now.

“If someone says he's willing, let him speak for himself.”

The hand pushed the sheet away. The segment ended. Someone commented that she'd prepared her brother's way out long ago. Another demanded to know whether she had greater permissions.

Xiao Lu saw both comments, saw Lin Mo look again at AUDIO AWAITING CONNECTION. She didn't satisfy them with further attachments. This one small selection was already public; backing out wouldn't retrieve it.

She left their questions onscreen without opening another page for them.

Her linkage evidence passed. The extracted credential finally opened before her alone. The code itself didn't enter the broadcast.

Four sections: an administrative-call identifier followed by review, linkage, and single-use scope markers. Fine verification lines sat beneath each, as though one wrong copied character might lose even this narrow entrance.

A thin receipt emerged from the console slot. She caught it. The paper edge scraped her fingertip, its faint sting bringing her back.

No password she'd remembered. Something she'd once retained and now actually held.

She laid the receipt on the index. UNUSED was still intact.

In the judgment room, Lin Mo checked the waiting-audio notice again. He didn't know whether she'd encountered trouble. He didn't tell the camera to hurry her into answering for him. Watching the lit microphone across the room, he tried drawing his foot back from the channel before his seat.

The distant standing position finally acquired a name.

DISPUTED STATEMENT.

He didn't rush to rise. First he touched the wood to the level floor he could reach, established where his first step would go, then looked at the false consent still playing.

“I'll correct… that sentence.”

Backstage, Xiao Lu heard. Her feed carried sound from the judgment room, though her brother still couldn't hear her.

She brought the receipt beside the linked-review input and stopped to check its reference against his current edit point once more.

The finger that had once chosen retain would move no further in the old recording.

Now it was her own finger's turn.
