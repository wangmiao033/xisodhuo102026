# Chapter 86: Still Inside the Theater

The iron bent farther. Wang Hai's palm slipped with it.

“Let him take his hand away first,” Lin Mo said.

The front attendant steadied the outer edge of the cover. Wang Hai withdrew his palm, leaving the bent prop wedged against its underside. There was no room for another adjustment.

He shifted the bench back and sat with his right wrist held against his body. Red slowly returned to the white pressure mark across his left palm. His fingers opened one at a time.

“I'm clear. Don't wait for me.”

Lin Mo lowered the broken wood.

The pin had moved beyond its old wear mark. The sound end of the wood rested against its side, the real silver edging beneath serving as a fulcrum. He settled his heel into position. He knew how little distance his remaining weight could cover. His shoulder and back had just tested it.

Above, the hand with the broken index finger began to climb.

Its thumb left the lower frame. The thick knuckles passed another tier of mirrors and reached for the tilting crossbeam. The other hand still supported it; their images crowded together, faces and commands overlapping without a gap.

The thumb no longer pressed the base down.

The broad band lifted slightly. A thin gray line opened between the lower pin and its socket. The retaining tongue that had covered it lay in pieces.

“Let go of the rod outside.”

The attendant released it and stepped aside. His narrow panel struck the bench leg, briefly interrupting the sound. Backstage, the other attendant resumed relaying the front-stage audio into the microphone.

The straightened casing didn't immediately spring back.

Lin Mo pressed his heel down.

The split extended a little farther into the wood, but its end stayed against the pin. Metal slid. The broad band slackened, then snapped taut as both enormous hands pulled inward overhead.

Before he could lift his foot, Lin Mo let his body settle backward, sending the last inch of movement through his heel.

The pin fell.

Long and slender, it scraped the base and struck the channel. He drew both legs away. The wood rolled across his stomach. Metal rang three times against the groove, the last impact lost beneath a break high overhead.

The main tension band whipped straight upward.

Its upper end still gripped the crossbeam. With nothing fastening it below, the distorted frame dragged the upper anchor sideways. The black catch turned, exposing a pale inner face.

Then it broke.

The enormous mirrored shoulders split apart.

The lowest tier folded inward first. A diagonal fracture crossed the chest. Images once assembled into the same hand veered away from one another. Someone was still telling him to sit properly; the words fell into darkness with half a finger joint.

Lin Mo lowered his head. His right arm covered the notebook inside his clothes, his left shoulder finding the patch he'd cleared. Small fragments struck his back in a close, stinging shower. He didn't raise his face to find out whether he'd won.

When the first crashing subsided, he drew a breath thick with dust.

The side camera's light remained on.

The main frame leaned into the space ahead, but the camera's small wall bracket had stayed in place. Its wire ran along the wall toward the seat. Dust whitened the picture, then thinned to reveal the back of his neck. His cough sounded through the seat microphone.

The broadcast hadn't returned to a waiting screen.

BODY MODIFICATION: 100%. COGNITIVE OVERWRITE: 54%.

FREEDOM passed 76,400, then lost more than two thousand. Some thought it was over. Some couldn't watch more falling glass. New names arrived together, asking whether the person inside was alive.

“Alive.”

He raised one end of the wood into Xiao Lu's camera. His sister didn't answer. The attendant at her station acknowledged him and said the front had heard too.

Across the floor, some teeth had released halfway. Others still obstructed the room. The gray transfer door remained closed. Lin Mo dragged the wood back and cleared fragments from beside his legs, exposing his route toward the seat.

Something overhead was still moving.

After the main anchor broke, a side beam had trapped part of the giant's wrist. Scattered mirror pieces were joining along it, forming a thin surface with his face inside. It couldn't extend the enormous hand again, but its narrow descending edge could still reach the floor.

He moved his legs out of the bright patch beneath it.

A fragment struck his ankle. He pushed it away, then drew his leg close. Blood and dust had stuck to his stockings; torn fabric clung to the calf wound. He tried to lead with his left knee, felt his unfamiliar hip angle tip him, and planted the other foot first.

He worked backward to the far side of the old microphone's round base. It wouldn't stop the remaining mirror limb. It marked a patch of genuinely level floor he still recognized. Farther behind stood the seat, the panel's bright edge unobstructed.

He left the wood beside his right hand and eased his aching fingers open.

His reflection opened its fingers too. The broken mirror wrist kept gripping the side beam.

He didn't call anyone in to carry him.

Backstage, a row of red lights went out on the site diagram.

Xiao Lu couldn't see that. She heard the latch in front of her spring open. Holding the cart, she waited for the door to stop trembling before pushing it.

The gray-costumed woman's hands went still.

She released one finger, then another. No fresh pushing motion took over. The wheels no longer followed the retreating light on the floor.

She removed both hands and held them against her stomach.

“I can stop.”

Xiao Lu rolled the empty cart against the outside wall, clear of the doorway. The woman touched its handle once more, then deliberately let go. Nothing pulled her back.

Beyond the door lay more gray tiles.

Benches lined the wall. An overhead screen carried the same broadcast. Farther inside stood another heavy, closed door. The old arrow ended here: TEMPORARY OBSERVATION HOLDING.

Xiao Lu crossed with her bag, then held the door for the woman. Two more people arrived from affected stage positions, one covering his ears, another carrying a darkened sign.

The man covering his ears tried the inner door's handle. When it didn't move, he rested his forehead against it, then slowly stepped back. Breaking supports sounded duller through these walls. Xiao Lu looked for a passage toward the judgment room. There were only the two doors.

The site display confirmed the failure.

MAIN FRAME FAILED. AFFECTED STAGE POSITIONS CANCELED. SAFETY TRANSFER ROUTE OPEN.

The old terminal beside it offered the next step: affected current performers could apply to release their identity bindings.

For the first time, Xiao Lu saw her name beside an available entrance.

The gray-costumed woman went first. She placed her hand at the reader, studied her notice, and selected Release. The performer designation disappeared. Ordinary spectator registration became available.

She looked back at the cart.

“I don't have to fetch it?”

“That position's canceled,” said an attendant who had arrived. “We're still inside the theater. Stay clear of the inner door.”

She examined her hands beneath the light. Their color remained solid. Saying she needed to sit, she went to the bench. An available option hadn't automatically registered her or chosen FREEDOM.

Xiao Lu set her own bag beside her feet, its strap looped around her wrist.

The terminal recognized Xiao Lu, the person standing there now. Her canceled account didn't return. The used paper stayed wrapped beside her cup.

She touched Apply and waited for the complete notice.

The first lines matched the other woman's: release the current performer-identity binding; terminate related performance posts and collaboration permissions; end the existence maintenance supplied through that performer identity.

Then a yellow warning appeared.

CURRENT EXISTENCE REMAINDER INSUFFICIENT. INDEPENDENT MAINTENANCE AFTER RELEASE WILL BE UNSTABLE. DURATION CANNOT BE CONFIRMED.

Her hand stopped short of confirmation.

The attendant didn't hurry her. She pointed to the warning and asked whether holding could replenish what she lacked. He checked the available services and her current state.

“Nothing here can supply existence. We can seat you. You can register. It doesn't say how long you'd last.”

“Does everyone get this?”

He looked toward the woman on the bench, then back at the terminal.

“She didn't.”

Xiao Lu withdrew her hand.

What she'd given away long ago hadn't returned because a door had partly opened today. Sitting at the console, walking to its doorway, ending an ordinary collaboration: through all of those, she'd remained a performer maintained by the theater.

This confirmation would remove that support.

She looked at both hands. They were solid. The bag strap had left a red mark across her fingers.

If she didn't press, nothing would diminish this instant.

On the overhead screen, Lin Mo used the wood to clear another piece of glass. His right hand couldn't hold it firmly. When it slipped, he dragged it close and tried again. FREEDOM passed eighty-six thousand, then retreated.

Her single choice couldn't fill all those empty places.

She crouched and took out her cup. The lid was tight; she used both hands. The water had lost its warmth. She drank, screwed the lid back on, and returned the cup without laying her script or receipt on the terminal.

She still wanted to carry these things outside herself.

“Will I still be Xiao Lu after this?”

“Your current identity remains. We're releasing the performer binding.”

She read DURATION CANNOT BE CONFIRMED again. There was no promise of safety, no exchange field asking for a sacrifice. She could withdraw and keep the maintenance she had. She would also remain a performer the theater could attach to another post.

People were moving toward the benches. The gray-costumed woman made room and asked whether she wanted to sit first.

Xiao Lu shook her head, then said, “In a moment. When I've finished.”

She lifted the bag onto her left shoulder and returned to the reader. She wanted the designation she'd never chosen removed from beneath her name. When she left, she wanted to say she wanted to go, just as her brother had.

She selected Release.

It didn't become a vote. The performer marker disappeared first. Related posts closed, one by one. The ordinary collaboration at her original console ended with them.

In the judgment room, the relayed backstage voice cut off.

Lin Mo looked at the audio light. CURRENT COLLABORATOR IDENTITY RELEASED. CONNECTION ENDED. Xiao Lu's name remained, with no message and no assurance she'd reached safety.

“I'm still in this broadcast,” he said into the live microphone.

The holding screen carried his words. Xiao Lu looked up to answer. The terminal offered no way to send her voice.

She raised her hand toward its glass, then stopped.

The tip of her index finger had grown pale. Light shone faintly through a place beneath the nail that had blocked it before. She turned her hand over and pressed it against her other palm. She could still feel the contact, but something at the edge had thinned.

The next page appeared.

CURRENT PERFORMER BINDING RELEASED. ORDINARY SPECTATOR REGISTRATION AVAILABLE.

The broadcast's choices remained closed to her. FREEDOM had reached 94,210; now it slid to 93,762.

She adjusted the bag strap and stood squarely before the reader.

She was still inside the theater. Her brother's voice still came from overhead. This time, she was reaching for a place from which she could truly choose.
