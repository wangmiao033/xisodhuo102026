# Chapter 84: The Floor Had Shifted Beneath Him

Lin Mo kept his right heel beside the wood without pressing down.

The thumb still lay above it. Force now would break his support first. At the metal tongue's root, stress briefly whitened the surface; the giant's downward pressure hid it again.

Twenty-one seconds.

Footsteps joined the backstage audio. Xiao Lu asked the attendant to send clean dressings to the front duty point. Her brother's hand and leg were bleeding. He told the front station to fetch its reserve supply and added the location to the panel. He didn't promise passage through a closed door.

She could at least bring ordinary help closer.

I opposed it. Why's the percentage still so high?

Finish changing him, then let him go. Better than dragging it out.

Empty Cup: He said he didn't want it.

You never voted for anything before?

Empty Cup didn't answer. Lin Mo wouldn't spend the remaining seconds answering that history for him. The proposal's percentage shifted above sixty, then retreated slightly. FREEDOM climbed separately.

“Don't take it on your hand,” Xiao Lu said.

She saw him lowering himself and thought he meant to brace on his wounded palm. He turned the hand, resting its unbroken edge on the floor, right elbow close to his waist.

“Getting lower.”

He didn't want pain to throw him from a position he could barely hold. He folded the hem beside his hand beneath the injured skin. Dust immediately marked it.

Zero.

VALID CHOICES: 62.4% APPROVAL. SIXTY-PERCENT THRESHOLD MET.

Pending execution brightened below. BODY MODIFICATION still read 74%.

Half a breath later, it moved.

The first change came inside a breath he thought he'd learned how to take.

Air entered his chest, but his muscles failed to support it with their familiar pressure. He tried to exhale. His throat tightened first; the passage felt narrower, unfamiliar again. His right hand rose toward his neck. Halfway, he noticed the wrist scar and lowered it beside his waist.

Gripping his throat wouldn't stop this.

79%.

A slow pull began deep in his back, followed by pain beside his hip. Already seated on the floor, he nevertheless felt he'd missed his own landing. The angles between joints, which he'd painstakingly learned to manage, shifted away. His right leg drew inward, tracing a curve through the dust with its stockinged sole.

He braced the other foot, trying to distribute his weight as before. It landed too far away. His knee buckled.

The wood lay beside him. He didn't grab it.

It was holding the frame. Pull it free and the lip would close.

“Ge.”

He heard Xiao Lu call and tried to answer. The first sound was air. The second emerged short and high, ending against an unfamiliar wall. He didn't force it lower. He opened his mouth and let the next breath through.

“I heard,” she said, close in his ear. “Don't try again yet.”

Her fingers touched the cup lid in her bag, then stopped. The water was here. Telling him to drink couldn't remove the wall. She left the cup and asked whether the dressings had reached the front.

They had, the panel answered. On a clean spot outside the inspection cover.

An actual arrival sounded very small.

86%.

Lin Mo caught his lower lip between his teeth and felt their meeting point shift. He released it before adding another wound. Sweat ran down his jaw, falling closer than he expected. His hand rose to catch it and missed.

The lower images began slipping out of alignment.

The old crouched shoulder still shone, but the newly captured outline no longer fitted it. In the lowest panels, his shoulder and sitting posture separated into overlapping versions. The thumb moved forward slightly, then withdrew.

He saw it. He couldn't move.

Pain kept descending through his back. His attention broke into pieces: one tending his knees, one finding breath, one stubbornly watching the wood.

He feared wasting the hesitation he'd just witnessed.

COGNITIVE OVERWRITE remained 54%. The number offered no relief; intact memory didn't lessen bodily pain. But he knew why the wood lay there. He knew Xiao Lu was across the wall. He knew the people who had just voted hadn't asked him.

Those facts still belonged to him.

You'll look better finished. Don't be like this.

A comment crawled through a cracked mirror panel.

He couldn't read the next one. He watched the real floor instead, away from words forever finding purposes for his pain.

Backstage, Xiao Lu realized she'd been holding her breath until her chest hurt. She released Send long enough to breathe, then pressed it again.

“Still watching you.”

She didn't say it would be over soon. The display had reached 93%, but his braced hand was shaking harder.

The final change wasn't fiercer. It merely seemed to last longer.

The distance between feet and hips felt consistently shorter than he expected. He tried to move his right knee; his body tipped first, drawing his left shoulder down. The old injury caught. For a moment, pain blackened his sight.

When he opened his eyes, BODY MODIFICATION read 100%.

EXECUTION COMPLETE.

COGNITIVE OVERWRITE: 54%.

There was no shared applause. Some posted celebrations. Others asked what kind of completion left someone pinned to the floor. Some couldn't bear watching and left. FREEDOM lost nearly three hundred, then climbed beyond its pre-execution count.

Lin Mo stared at his knees. He couldn't immediately put his feet back where he'd learned to put them.

That position no longer worked.

He tried lifting his torso. Weight traveled through his back differently. His heel rose too soon and nearly pitched him onto the wood. He stopped and lowered himself again. The cloth beneath his hand crumpled.

No percentage taught him how to stand in this completed body.

He settled one heel, doing only that. Forward an inch: his knee drifted. Back halfway. A movement he'd once performed without thought now required him to watch his actual foot.

The old shoulder injury hurt in the same place. His wrist scar hadn't flattened. The cloth beneath his palm continued darkening. The display called the adaptation complete without settling a single injury.

He couldn't reach that brief opening with his old steps.

Watching the wood, he realized he didn't need to step onto it. He was already down. He knew where his shoulder and back could safely land. Unable to stand, he could send his weight from here.

He slowly turned his right foot toward the shaft until his leg stopped twisting after an obsolete habit. It still hurt. His sole found solid floor.

Xiao Lu saw his expression change. She didn't ask whether he was better. She watched the audio light and waited.

Fine clicks traveled overhead.

He looked up.

The giant palm was withdrawing into the glass. Small images slid apart across it, exposing dark seams. Its thumb left the space above the wood while a newer shoulder image took the old one's place. High mirrors still followed him, but no third hand emerged from them.

The metal tongue's tail lifted.

He recognized the movement he'd been waiting for. The loaded casing was pulling back; the wood held its lip open. Force traveled two ways, exposing the fine white line at the tongue's root.

Perhaps one breath remained.

He didn't try to stand.

His right heel lay just short of the sound middle. He slid his foot flat instead of trusting the distance he used to cover in one step. Stocking brushed wood. His heel settled where the split wouldn't take the first strain.

“Pressing now.”

His voice wasn't the one he'd prepared. Xiao Lu understood.

“Keep your hands off the cover!” she shouted into the attendant's panel.

Wang Hai repeated it. Hands withdrew outside. Only a sharp breath came through.

Bracing his left foot, Lin Mo sat backward. His weight drove his right heel against the shaft. The silver strip became a hard fulcrum; the sound tip beneath the lip rose.

Wood cracked first.

The split ran inward, throwing up a sliver. His foot slipped sideways. The newly altered relationship at his hip couldn't hold the direction he'd expected, and his whole body tipped.

He didn't snatch himself back with his injured wrist.

He let his shoulder and back descend onto the cleared floor. His heel stayed on the wood, bringing the rest of his weight down.

The giant palm was pulling backward too.

The metal tongue couldn't follow the casing. Lifted from its slot, it bent between opposing forces. The white line opened at its root, becoming a genuine black gap.

A hard, short report came through the floor.

Then the wood broke in two.

Lin Mo drew his feet clear. His stocking tugged the cut on his calf, sending numb pain upward. The longer section sprang from beneath the lip and landed beside him. The short, damaged end tumbled into the groove, stopping against dead mirror-nail fragments.

The tongue's broken head slid out and struck the silver strip with a small ring.

Smaller than he'd imagined: a gray-black piece, edges polished by use. Something that small had fastened all this weight together.

The lower frame lifted slightly.

The mirror didn't fall. Right-side teeth held the floor. Overhead, the second hand braced hard against the beam. Several high panels flashed white, taking the displaced load.

The room shook violently.

Flat on the floor, Lin Mo guarded his ribs with his right elbow so the notebook's hard corner wouldn't force its covers apart. The pen stayed clipped. Grit settled on his torn sleeve; he left it there.

The lowest retainer had broken.

The opening continued widening. Several narrow, taut bands extended through the darkness behind the frame. One trembled where its lower end had come loose. He could see them, but not where they led. He didn't reach inside.

A shout came from the front, followed by Wang Hai's rough voice.

“Cover's lifting! Don't push it down!”

Xiao Lu asked whether anyone was hurt.

“No. Everyone stood back.”

Breathless, the attendant reported one failed lower load point. The upper frame remained locked. The safety route hadn't opened. He didn't turn one fracture into permission to leave the theater.

Lin Mo opened the eyes he'd closed against falling dust. The side camera remained lit. So did the tilted control panel. Broken wood hadn't ended the broadcast.

FREEDOM rose to 55,109, then changed to 55,081.

Couldn't have done that before the modification, could he?

So you should thank the people who voted for it.

Watching those comments, he felt an anger straighter than pain. The shifting images had given him an opening. His body had also been forced to a completion he hadn't chosen. The first fact couldn't sign for the second.

“I used the opening.”

His new voice scraped through the room. His throat still hurt.

“You didn't save me.”

He wasn't addressing everyone. People who'd opposed the proposal, stayed, or still selected FREEDOM watched the same stream. But those waiting to collect thanks for voting to change him needed to hear where they stood.

Xiao Lu didn't ask him to explain again.

“Bottom's broken. Don't get up yet.”

He couldn't. The failed attempt remained in his knee, and the broken wood couldn't restore his lost balance. He dragged the longer section within his right hand's reach. He didn't mistake it for a full-length new crutch.

Outside, someone pushed the wrapped dressings closer to the inspection cover. Paper scraped the floor. Help had reached the other side of a frame that still held them apart.

Lin Mo turned toward the sound.

“Where's the next one?”

The giant's intact thumb descended toward the opening, trying to force the raised lower frame back into place.

The broken metal tongue didn't grow back for it.
