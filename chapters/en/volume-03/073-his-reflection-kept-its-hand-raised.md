# Chapter 73: His Reflection Kept Its Hand Raised

Lin Mo first noticed the hand still hanging in the glass.

He'd returned his own to the narrow seat's edge. His reflection kept two fingers beside its ear. After a moment it lowered them, like someone reluctantly obeying an instruction.

He raised his hand again, this time with one finger extended.

The reflection followed late. It added no second finger, didn't smile while he remained still. An amber light glowed beneath it, beside tiny lettering: REVIEW CAPTURE PLAYBACK.

He watched the light without assuming it already knew his fear. Then he slowly examined the room.

The narrow seat stood near the entrance. His left panel showed the same broadcast. Xiao Lu had disappeared from the thin stage view; her empty wooden chair and his stool remained beside the original controls. Farther away, Wang Hai sat beyond the white line, head lowered, seemingly awaiting news no one could yet deliver.

A shallow channel crossed the floor diagonally from the seat. He'd avoided its near end. A thin silver strip marked the edge; the far end stopped before a standing position for one person, with a slender microphone on a stalk.

Beyond stood the glass. Only its lower portion was lit. Black supports and dark panels extended above and to either side, their joints higher than the ceiling lights. They made no complete face yet. He already disliked the height from which they seemed to look down.

White lettering moved down one line.

APPLICANT'S STATEMENT: WISHES TO LEAVE.

A picture covered it.

Xiao Lu smiled. He heard his own voice: “Helps me sit.”

Nothing else. No approaching wheels, stool leg almost landing on his foot, demand to keep the way to End clear.

The next clip used his new voice.

“I'll go in.”

Keep speaking had been cut.

His reflection remained seated, face turning slightly in delayed light. A category appeared beside the images: VOLUNTARY CONTINUATION OF PERFORMANCE.

“I meant this room.”

His throat tightened. The capture light didn't change. A gray line stated that his voice had failed to meet registration requirements.

He tried drawing the left panel closer. It was fixed. Pain pulled through his wrist. Releasing it, he examined the seat edge. A tiny microphone opening faced forward, away from his lowered face.

He couldn't turn one objection into another way of hurting himself.

Fine mesh covered the opening; dim light shone inside. When he leaned close, the volume bar rose. When he retreated, it dropped, leaving only the hum from the joints.

He tested two positions without speaking a complete sentence. One twisted his shoulder painfully. The other required pressing a foot against the channel edge, leaving him unstable. He abandoned both and reconsidered the distance between the fixed seat and microphone.

A viewer said he was pretending not to hear, couldn't even object properly. He let the words pass. He still knew how to judge a position. He simply had more places that couldn't take being forced. Recognizing them didn't make him less familiar to himself than the face in the glass.

He squared his body, brought his back away from the seat, and stopped shrinking his throat into his collar. His right knee tightened. He drew the wood closer, planted it securely, then raised his chin.

“You cut it.”

The amber light flashed.

OBJECTION INSUFFICIENT. IDENTIFY THE IMAGE.

He didn't immediately try shouting again. He waited until the reflected mouth closed, then indicated the white line ending the second clip.

“Here. More… after this.”

The clip froze with his mouth not quite closed.

Onscreen, he seemed about to give some longer, gentler assent. Viewers supplied words: he was really afraid to leave, afraid of anyone seeing him as he was now. His left fingertips pressed the wood's rough grain.

“I'm afraid of pain.”

The amber light came on.

“Of falling. Not being able… to walk.”

He paused, giving his throat time to release.

“I still want out. You cut off… keep speaking.”

A thin marker appeared below the second clip. No approval, no compensation. Only an objection finally received instead of dismissed as indistinct breathing.

EDIT-POINT OBJECTION REGISTERED. ORIGINAL CAPTURE BEFORE AND AFTER THE CUT REQUESTED.

His hand stayed on the wood. A moment later the reflected grip tightened, performing his pain again.

Beyond the narrow backstage door, another sound had stopped Xiao Lu.

A warning kept chiming while the attendant walked on. She called him back and pointed at the red light overhead.

“Is that telling me to get out?”

“To come in and close the door.”

Her bag strap was caught in the gap. She pulled it free; the door closed. The corridor fell so quiet she could hear their footsteps.

“Sounds a lot like you're not welcome.”

“Could also mean the door's open.”

She let it go. Bag over her shoulder, she followed him past numbered windows. Empty chairs, lit screens with no one before them; one showed the stage's gray wall. She paused long enough to confirm Wang Hai remained where he'd been. He hadn't followed them in.

The attendant stopped at a shallow console and slotted his narrow board into its edge.

Two positions lit up: stage collaboration, leading back toward the door, and backstage picture handover. Xiao Lu was already listed, her number linked to this session's collaborator. No one demanded her old name. Calling someone brother hadn't opened other people's records.

“Can I talk to him here?”

“You can request a connection. No guarantee while judgment capture has the audio.”

“What can I do now?”

He showed her the picture, then the grayed-out audio input. This station could receive designated cameras and submit material checks relevant to the current statement. It couldn't answer for the applicant. It had no audience voting position.

Xiao Lu withdrew her hand and looked beneath the console. No straps to secure her in the chair. The narrow door beside her was still open. She set her bag at her feet and pulled out the empty chair herself.

“I'll stay here. Connect the picture first.”

She confirmed that station alone. Her name remained Xiao Lu; her role remained collaborator.

Lin Mo's profile appeared first. From the high angle, his right hand and half the wood crowded the bottom edge. His feet were outside the shot. She leaned closer, trying to see his knee, and found only her own brows and eyes reflected over the screen.

She sat back without shouting for him to move his leg.

The audio input briefly lit. She immediately called, “Ge.”

Her own voice returned from the small backstage speaker. LOCAL AUDIO TEST appeared beside it. She frowned and released the control.

“Who asked you to answer me?”

The attendant pointed to Send beneath it. Still gray.

She inhaled, drew back the urgency that hadn't yet become anger, and looked at Lin Mo again. He was studying his left panel. A connection notice informed him backstage collaboration had joined the picture; audio awaited connection.

He saw Xiao Lu's name.

He didn't know which room she occupied, whether she'd sat down, or that she'd called him. At least those words meant she'd found somewhere she could see him.

He raised a hand within the camera's view, no higher than his shoulder could tolerate. Held it briefly. Lowered it.

The hand in the glass lagged again.

Xiao Lu nearly waved back, then realized her station wasn't sending her image. She rested her palm on her knee and smiled faintly.

“Still know to test it first.”

Thinking she meant the audio input, the attendant said it couldn't connect yet. She didn't explain. She'd already noticed the edit-point marker Lin Mo had registered.

She found the same point in the material-check field and requested the original surrounding audio. The page revealed no stranger's past, only her permissions: current collaborators could annotate, not retrieve sealed original tracks.

“Won't even let him hear his own whole sentence?”

The attendant didn't answer for the theater. He pointed to RELATED-PARTY REVIEW.

“Verified parties can use this. Only goes further if there's an unsettled record.”

Her eyes remained on him.

“Whose record?”

“Choose yourself, and it checks yours. Don't try entering his name.”

Her hand rested on the console edge. The name field held only Xiao Lu and her present performance number. She remembered paper, remembered her own pen touching it. Plenty remained blank. Urgency couldn't justify filling what she couldn't recall with guesses.

She drew her bag closer. The cup sounded softly inside. When Lin Mo had last drunk from it, he'd been beside her. Now only she heard it.

She resisted calling Ge again. She didn't know whether backstage test audio was retained. She wouldn't keep repeating her fear to that little speaker.

First she enlarged the picture. His lips were dry; his raised chin looked stiff. The objection remained on his left-hand screen. The first useful thing she could do was find that cut-away piece of speech, spare him the effort of saying the same thing again.

She stopped before touching Search and checked her current number once more. Above was the collaboration she'd just joined. Below, a link to an old transaction. No field required her to turn Lin Mo's sister into an entry pass.

She'd only just remembered who she was. She didn't intend to use that fact to guess her way through every door.

She selected verification of herself through the current performance number.

A roller moved beneath the console. A thin index sheet emerged. She caught it: transaction category and sealed status, without the full agreement, an original name, or a secret that could open any door she liked.

PROTECTION PAYMENT ON ANOTHER'S BEHALF: LINKED MATERIALS.

ORIGINAL IDENTITY ACCOUNT CLOSED. RETAINED TRANSACTION AVAILABLE FOR REVIEW.

She turned it over. Blank.

The attendant had removed his board and was preparing to return to the stage. She asked whether Lin Mo's picture would go with him.

“You connected the station. Taking the board doesn't take the picture.”

She nodded without asking him to stay and search for her. As he reached the door, she stopped him once more.

“Wang Hai's waiting. Tell him to watch the original screen. Don't say we've got out.”

He agreed and left. She didn't know whether the message had reached him, couldn't nod for Wang Hai through a row of walls.

In the judgment room, the retrieval status finally appeared below Lin Mo's marker.

REQUEST RETAINED. ORIGINAL CAPTURE REQUIRES RELATED-PARTY REVIEW VERIFICATION.

Seatkeeper's name lit beside it.

Look how many people you need to answer for something you said yourself.

Lin Mo didn't set Xiao Lu's name against the accusation. Keeping his objection onscreen, he answered one word at a time.

“I said it. Play it… all.”

FREEDOM fell from the thirty-one hundreds to 3,082, then returned to 3,107. He saw without blaming the losses on her continued silence. He didn't even know how far she'd already walked.

The gray panels behind him stayed closed. The slender microphone at the distant standing position lit, but no one had called him over yet.

Backstage, Xiao Lu laid the index flat on the console. The screen asked whether she wished to continue into her sealed transaction. Ownership pointed to that same closed record, not an old account she could sign into again.

She looked at her brother's profile and pressed View.

The first thing to appear was a hand holding a pen.
