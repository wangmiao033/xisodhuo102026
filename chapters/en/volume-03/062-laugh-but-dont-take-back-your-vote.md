He turned slowly within the range his hips now allowed, rather than twisting as memory told him he could. The attendant stood by the stool's legs to keep it from sliding. He didn't lift Lin Mo.

Before he settled, his booklet slipped partway from his jacket. He reached to catch it; his wrist weakened, nearly taking his supporting hand off the seat.

People began counting steps faster than he took them. He didn't follow their pace.

The platform was low. His last foot still shook as though he'd traveled a great distance. He tested the wood on the level surface, then slowly turned toward the main camera.

He wasn't pleading. These few newly learned steps remained in his legs; an instruction from outside could change them at any moment. That frightened him more than the old face he'd offered as bait.

FREEDOM reached seventeen.

For a moment, he thought he should stay standing. A little longer might bring another vote. Xiao Lu warned that his left leg was shaking. He didn't answer, holding on for several more breaths before realizing he'd made the number an order not to sit.

“Laugh. Just don't take back your vote afterward.”

He nearly laughed himself and feared they really would. One comment called him greedy. Another said the vote belonged to its owner, who could withdraw whenever they pleased.

“Yes.” He backed slowly down the ramp. “All I can do is ask. I can't say you owe me.”

By the time he reached the stool, seventeen had become fourteen.

Three empty places. He laid the wood by his legs without rising to give those who'd left another performance. His wounds remained. If he fell into trouble again, Wang Hai couldn't haul everyone free with a right hand that hurt too.
