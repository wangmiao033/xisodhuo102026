# Chapter 85: The Strip Only Held This Side

Before the thumb came down, Lin Mo pushed the broken wood forward.

He kept it flat. Its sound end nudged the snapped retaining tongue away from his stockinged toes. The thumb scraped the silver edging, sending the metal fragment hopping into the channel.

The lower frame sank another fraction.

Behind it, narrow bands tightened. The broadest descended from above and doubled back at the base. Polished wear marks crossed its surface; its lowest section remained hidden inside the black casing.

Lin Mo shifted right, clearing the thumb's return path. He had found an angle at which his bent leg didn't immediately tip him sideways. That wasn't enough to trust it with walking.

“Can you see bands outside?”

Xiao Lu relayed the question. The inspection cover struck something. The front attendant said a transverse rod ran beside the base, with bands behind it.

“Don't pull.”

The man stopped.

As the giant pressed again, three thinner bands lit. Short position numbers traveled along their edges and vanished into the frame's sides. One matched the number beside Xiao Lu's station.

She heard a rhythmic tapping behind her.

Through the open doorway, a gray-costumed woman pushed an empty cart. One wheel had caught in a floor joint. Her hands kept driving forward, knocking against the handle at identical intervals.

“Stop pushing a moment,” Xiao Lu called.

The woman turned her face. Her hands went on.

“I can't.”

The attendant checked his narrow panel. A line on the old site diagram flashed to the same beat. He went out and lifted the trapped wheel. The woman advanced with the cart, then resumed the motion at the next floor mark.

“The stage positions are still attached to the main frame,” he said on returning. “The lower catch broke. The upper tension hasn't released.”

Xiao Lu looked at her brother lying on the floor. The structure above him was holding more than his ceiling. She told him what she'd seen, without claiming those few bands accounted for everyone in the theater.

He waited for the thumb to withdraw and touched the iron fitting beside the broad band with his wood.

A faint tap. The crossbeam answered overhead. As the band tightened, half a horizontal pin emerged at its base.

Something carrying the load above was indeed fastened below.

He didn't tap again.

The front attendant compared his inspection opening with the old diagram.

“Lower pin for the main tension band. The broken tongue covered it. Until that pin comes out, the upper anchor stays locked.”

“Can you push from outside?”

The man nudged the rod. Lin Mo's pin trembled sideways, then stuck. The inner and outer casing had twisted out of alignment. More pressure only narrowed the space beside the pin.

“It'll jam. Stop.”

Wang Hai braced his left hand on the waiting bench.

His injured right wrist lay across his lap. He could see the cover lifting, but not the attendant's hand beneath it.

“There's a prop inside the lid,” he said. “Folded against the left side.”

The attendant raised the cover slightly. A flat iron strip hung from a slot in its underside. Its bottom had sprung out of the catch and swung with every tremor.

“It holds the cover. It won't take the main frame.”

“Get your hand out first.”

The attendant detached the strip. Its tip had bent in the impact; reversing it, he braced the raised cover and withdrew his hand. The opening gained barely half a pinhead's width.

Hearing the man's relieved breath, Lin Mo briefly let go of his wood. His wrist had never stopped hurting. Other sounds had crowded over it.

“I need to turn the casing back,” the attendant said. “Can't manage both places alone.”

Wang Hai rose with his left hand against the bench, but stayed clear of the frame until he'd heard what was needed. Only then did he cross the cleared floor.

The attendant moved his bench beside the cover, out of its direct fall. Wang Hai sat with his right wrist protected on his lap. His left palm held the iron's outer face. It slipped inward. He braced a foot against the bench leg and shifted his palm sideways.

Nobody asked him to fill the gap with his injured hand.

He moved the wrapped bandaging cloth from beside the cover to clean floor behind the bench. Someone on the stage screen urged him to pass it through. He glanced at the closed transfer door and kept it away from the slit.

“Leave it here. Don't lose it inside the iron.”

His left hand still hurt too, though it lacked the stabbing injury that made pressure through his right wrist unbearable. The prop pressed a white line across his left palm. He changed to the heel of his hand, keeping his fingers outside. The attendant promised to lower the cover if he needed to let go.

Wang Hai nodded and took the small weight.

Their panel rested beside their feet, its sound relayed through the backstage console.

“I'm settled,” he said. “Go.”

The rod turned outward. In front of Lin Mo, more of the skewed pin emerged. Above it, the casing lifted enough to admit the wood.

He inserted the end. At his first push, it sprang back against his palm.

Pain shot up from his wrist. He released it, gaining nothing.

“Still loaded.”

The work outside stopped.

Seeing the pin didn't mean he could remove it. The broad band tightened and eased with the giant's movement, but the pin's underside stayed pressed against its socket. That weight had to lift first.

He looked up.

The other giant hand supported the crossbeam. When the highest mirrors turned, its palm drew inward, tipping the frame backward. Once, the lower pin had lifted briefly before settling again.

He would have to wait for that movement. The broken wood couldn't hold up what was above him.

Xiao Lu could see his chin now. He had eased from lying down to sitting sideways. She enlarged the existing picture and told him the camera still received sound. The people at the front could hear too.

He looked toward the narrow seat.

The familiar silver strip remained, with more fragments around it. He dragged the wood across his knees and cleared two pieces from the nearest floor. Then, alternating elbow and heel, he shifted backward. At the slender microphone's round base, he stopped for a long time.

He didn't have to stand to make his way back.

An upper mirror turned toward the entrance. The thumb below was still searching the split, but the higher image had found his retreating back. Seeing a bright edge approach in that image, he laid the wood beside him and drew closer behind the microphone base.

Falling glass grazed the wood and shattered beside his cleared path. The wood diverted one corner, nothing more. He waited for the noise to cease, picked a sharp grain from his stocking, and measured the remaining distance.

His reflection kept retreating while his body rested. The two sets of knees occupied different places. He didn't hurry to catch up with the image.

Wang Hai heard cloth scraping and asked what he was doing.

“Going back to the seat leg. Before I try again.”

“Take your time.” Then Wang Hai cursed softly: the prop was slipping.

The attendant steadied the cover while he reset his palm. Lin Mo waited too.

At last the fixed seat leg touched his lower back. Sitting sideways, wood across his thighs, he could steady himself against it. The tilted panel was close enough to touch. End remained at its upper right.

His fingertips found the edge. He didn't press.

FREEDOM: 63,927. Attendance: more than 108,000. New viewers saw another man holding iron outside the room and asked where this was. Others complained about seeing only half a face and told Lin Mo to move forward again.

He gave the microphone what breath he'd recovered.

“People are still inside. There's no way out yet.”

Xiao Lu checked her identity field, then the woman pushing the cart. The safety route remained inactive; its dotted line ended inside the theater's thick outer walls.

“I'm going to check that route. I'll take my bag. If the door's shut, I'll stop there.”

He turned toward her audio light.

“You won't hear us away from the console.”

“I know. The attendant will stay and relay the front.”

She lifted her bag onto her shoulder. The cup touched the script. The used receipt, wrapped in its index, stayed packed away. She tucked the chair under the console so the attendant could reach the existing microphone.

“You don't have to come back to this room just to hear me,” Lin Mo said.

Before she could answer, he added, “Find the route. Remember where it is.”

“I will.”

She released Send. The attendant held the same already-open control and relayed the front panel's sound into the microphone. Her station registration remained hers; neither performer nor collaborator status had changed.

Outside, she passed the old site diagram and took the first turn along its solid line. Two empty crates crowded the second turn. She dragged the outer one against the wall, clearing space for whoever followed.

The temporary holding door was shut.

Beside it stood an old registration terminal. Through the glass she could see benches, a screen, more gray theater walls. She touched the door control. Its inactive red light shone against her fingernail.

She didn't keep pressing.

The gray-costumed woman reached the turn, cart wheels following the lit marks. Xiao Lu held its side to guide it past the crate.

“Are you going this way too?” the woman asked.

“When it opens.”

“What about the cart?”

Xiao Lu looked at the doorway, then the worn wheels. She couldn't tell the woman to let go while the compulsion might still pull her off balance.

“Keep it beside the door. You can try letting go while someone's holding it.”

The woman studied her own hands and nodded slightly. Xiao Lu moved her bag to her other side, freeing the hand nearest the cart. The screen shone behind the glass. For now, both women remained outside.

In the judgment room, Lin Mo had worked his way back along the cleared floor to the lower frame. End was farther away again, but he'd just tested the entire route himself.

He placed the wood's sound end beside the pin, using his right hand only to guide it. The length lay where his heel could press.

Outside, the attendant held the rod turned; Wang Hai steadied the cover prop. The giant's upper palm drew inward another fraction. A gray line appeared beneath the broad tension band.

The pin moved.

Barely, but it didn't spring back. Lin Mo lifted his heel, preserving that small gain instead of forcing another push.

A high scrape came from above. The giant was bringing its other hand toward the load-bearing crossbeam, trying to square the distorted frame. With every inch it drew in, the thin prop outside bent farther.

“It's going to snap,” Wang Hai said.

FREEDOM reached 71,308, then dropped to 70,964.

Lin Mo watched the half-exposed pin. Outside, they'd held only one side open. Inside, he'd found room for only one heel.

If he withdrew to wait again, the gap might close on every hand they'd put there.
