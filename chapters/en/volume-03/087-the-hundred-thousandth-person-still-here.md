# Chapter 87: The Hundred Thousandth Person Still Here

Xiao Lu laid her paling fingertips on the reader.

The glass still supported her hand. The terminal recognized her current name, checked the performer binding just released, then offered ordinary viewer registration. Nobody else stood here in her place. Nothing in her bag was required.

She chose to proceed.

VERIFYING CURRENT IDENTITY AND PRESENCE.

The line moved upward. Her brother remained on the overhead screen, but the terminal's viewing area was dark. REGISTRATION IN PROGRESS. She held her hand steady. The bag strap weighed distinctly against her left shoulder.

The gray-costumed woman looked across from the bench.

“What happened to your hand?”

Xiao Lu held her other hand beside the pale fingers.

“When I released the binding, it said I couldn't maintain myself.”

The woman made room at the bench's near end. Xiao Lu thanked her, still looking up.

The person carrying the dark sign sat with it held against their chest. Eventually they discovered their arms could lower, and rested it on their knees. Old adhesive scraps clung to its back. A thumb rubbed one corner loose, then stopped, as though waiting for an order to put it back.

Xiao Lu looked at her name. The blank left by PERFORMER hadn't prescribed her next movement. She could continue here or sit on that bench. Both choices existed without a bright line pushing her toward either one.

She lifted her fingertips and settled them again, leaving a faint damp print.

Lin Mo was laying the broken wood across his legs.

The thin surface hanging from the mirror wrist had lengthened. It turned slowly along the side beam, carrying an imperfect face: his jaw from when he'd lain down, his eyes high on another fragment. Each turn swept a narrow white stripe across the floor.

The first stroke caught debris.

The second pressed against the microphone's round base. Metal tilted toward the groove with a grating screech.

As it lifted, he pulled his knees clear. The marker he'd used for level ground couldn't protect that position forever.

“Still a piece above.”

No answer from Xiao Lu. Her name remained beneath CONNECTION ENDED. He looked instead at his cleared retreat.

The fixed seat leg was lower than the tilted disc. Firmer, too.

He pushed a sharp fragment aside with the wood. Bracing on his right elbow, he dragged himself backward with his heels. The wood snagged his torn sleeve. He stopped, freed the fabric with the backs of his fingers, then moved again.

The mirror wrist twisted after him.

Caught on the side beam, it could swing through only a small arc. Reaching beyond that angle drove its root against metal and flashed a white crack through the glass. After seeing it twice, he shifted toward the arc's outer edge.

It could still reach him. Every extra inch would strain its own root first.

Someone asked where his sister had gone. Another said backstage had withdrawn; the broadcast would probably end too.

“She's taking her own route.”

He didn't know whether it was safe.

FREEDOM recovered past ninety-five thousand. Attendance exceeded 140,000. Some newcomers first saw blood on the floor. Others asked why they'd missed the body proposal. Old comments crowded the new. No single voice answered for everyone.

The thin mirror lunged.

Lin Mo flattened the wood against the floor and shifted his hips across the small space it supported. The sharp lower edge scraped his stocking. Fibers opened over his toes. No fresh blood.

He curled his toes and reached the seat's right side.

His waist kept tipping more readily than he remembered. He placed his left foot, then brought the right leg after it, refusing to let the onscreen image set the distance for his actual knee.

The notebook dug into his clothes. Keeping the wood beneath his elbow, he nudged its cover back and felt the clipped pen. His name was written inside; blank pages remained. He protected it without displaying any of them.

He intended to take it with him, along with the body that still hurt. There were things he had not written yet. The small stiffness against his ribs belonged to those unwritten pages as much as to the words he kept checking to remember himself.

His left shoulder couldn't lean against the seat. He turned until his waist met its fixed leg and rested his wrist on his thigh. The tilted control stood ahead and left. End remained lit. He touched the panel's edge, leaving a gray fingertip mark, then withdrew. The option was close enough to use. He needed to know that, even now, when every number beneath it made continuing seem like the only thing anyone wanted him to do.

New words appeared before Xiao Lu.

ORDINARY VIEWER REGISTRATION COMPLETE. CURRENT IDENTITY: XIAO LU.

One current registration. The viewing area came alive. The elapsed time behind her brother continued rather than starting from zero.

She selected his broadcast.

The verification border withdrew. LIVE VIEWER PRESENCE lit. His cracked lips and watchful eyes looked closer here than on the overhead screen. She wanted to tell him she'd arrived. Her fingers found only the audience choices.

She kept the words in her mouth.

The attendant checked the connection. Remaining personally present at this viewing point would keep her choice valid in the current count. He left the choice field untouched and went to help the person covering their ears sit down.

“Almost?” the woman on the bench asked.

“Not yet.”

FREEDOM: 98,106. The next refresh lost ninety-three, then recovered. A fine ring still moved around Xiao Lu's loading choice field.

She didn't withdraw her hand.

The transparency had spread from beneath the nail into her first ridge of fingerprint. She still felt the terminal's coldness and heard her cup touch stiff paper inside the bag. She drew the bag closer, remembering the water she'd drunk and her intention to carry these things out herself.

That intention hadn't changed. The woman on the bench had made room for her without asking for a reason or a performance. Xiao Lu could still sit there after this. She wanted that small, ordinary piece of the future too.

In the judgment room, the thin mirror descended again.

Lin Mo couldn't retreat behind the seat. The wall wiring ran there, leaving nowhere to put his legs if the glass followed. Instead he raised himself enough for the lowest remnant to catch his face, drew his feet inward, and shifted left along the seat edge.

The mirror wrist twisted toward that change.

Its root struck the beam. The first reach fell short. Fragmented faces crowded forward inside it, stretching the thin surface until its lowest corner crossed the cracked seat.

Lin Mo ducked.

Glass struck the seat's hard edge at an angle.

The damaged covering burst into white chips. Fine cracks climbed through the mirror. Lin Mo took the wood and pushed the thin corner hanging beside his knee, without swinging at it.

The sound end met the gray-black backing.

He pressed his forearm along the wood. No raised shoulder, no wrenching wrist. The seat leg steadied his back. The corner bent away, tightening the wrist root against the beam until the existing white fracture opened all the way through.

It broke.

Glass carrying part of his face struck the floor ahead. An edge hit the control's support. He released the wood and caught the panel with his palm heel before it could overturn.

End remained. So did FREEDOM.

99,732.

He eased the panel onto its support and heard how tightly he'd been breathing through his teeth. The wood lay outside his right leg, shedding a few more splinters from the broken end. Still the same piece.

Only an empty wrist shell remained lodged above. No complete hand could reach down from it. Small unsupported panes continued falling. He tucked his feet toward the seat leg. The ceiling wasn't safe. He watched one fragment tumble against another and change direction on its way down. The moving hand was gone, but the broken room still required him to pay attention. He kept his head below the seat edge.

Empty Cup: I'm still here.

Don't leave. It counts who's here now.

Haven't voted. Just want to see whether the door opens.

I approved the change and chose freedom. You don't have to thank me.

Lin Mo gave that last comment no nod. Its author would have to remain here with the earlier choice; selecting this box couldn't wash it away.

99,815.

He stopped calculating the gap and pointed toward the teeth still obstructing the floor.

“Outside. Keep your hands out of the inspection opening. Wait for this door.”

The front-stage screen still carried him. From his bench, Wang Hai told the attendant to move a shoe away from the cover. The clean cloth remained behind the bench.

Lin Mo's finger paused in the gray door's direction.

“When it opens, watch your footing. Glass inside.”

He was finding somewhere they could reach him.

Wang Hai lifted his left hand toward the screen, then remembered there was no return picture. He lowered it and indicated the cloth. The attendant picked it up and tore the wrapping, without crossing the teeth. Both waited on the side from which they could actually enter.

Xiao Lu's choice field cleared.

Her single option remained unselected. The valid-presence marker stayed lit above. Her paling hand hadn't caused someone else's name to replace her own.

She moved her right hand toward FREEDOM, steadying her thumb against the glass edge so the unfamiliar sensation wouldn't spoil her aim.

99,991.

The next refresh lost two. New choices arrived. She watched the final digits flicker without waiting for them to settle before finding the control. It lay beneath her fingertip. She'd read it clearly.

99,999.

Xiao Lu pressed.

She still felt a little pressure. The glass received it. Her empty field changed to SELECTED, followed by a short confirmation.

THIS FREEDOM CHOICE HAS FULFILLED THE EXIT CONDITION.

The count in the same picture became 100,000.

Almost simultaneously, a hundred thousand lit before Lin Mo. CONDITION NOT MET disappeared. No instruction told him to hold these people through another judgment.

ONE HUNDRED THOUSAND SIMULTANEOUS VALID FREEDOM CHOICES REACHED IN THIS BROADCAST.

APPLICANT: LIN MO. EXECUTING RELEASE.

Something sounded inside the gray door.

The row of teeth nearest its foot dropped at once, exposing floor tiles they'd long concealed. A narrow opening appeared along the door, admitting light from a different direction.

He didn't immediately smile or ask the audience to verify it again.

His palm slowly left the control.

In holding, Xiao Lu's choice remained lit. She knew she'd pressed that last box. Her brother's name now stood beside EXECUTING RELEASE.

The heavy inner door trembled. People beside the bench looked up.

The gray-costumed woman reached to support Xiao Lu's elbow.

She touched it. Then something empty slipped through the contact.

Xiao Lu looked down. Light passed through her knuckles onto the FREEDOM she'd selected. She withdrew her hand from the terminal and kept looking at her brother.

The door was really opening.
