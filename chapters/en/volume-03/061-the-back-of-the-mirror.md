# Chapter 61: The Back of the Mirror

Lin Mo pressed Start Broadcast.

The preparation light turned red. Zero stayed in the lower left. Only after waiting a while did he realize he was holding his breath, as though the light should have summoned someone out of the dark.

Nobody. Just him before the camera, the metal rig beside him and the narrow path he'd left himself.

He stood with the wood's support. The person in the side mirror rose slowly too. The gray jacket's left sleeve was torn; the blue garment beneath fitted his waist. He brushed hair from his mouth with the back of his hand.

“This session is called This Time, I Tell It.”

His voice went out. Nothing answered. He'd prepared another sentence, but the attendance figure stopped him. Wang Hai moved on the waiting side. Lin Mo didn't look back. A few stirring words for the companions who'd already come this far wouldn't produce a hundred thousand viewers.

Attendance changed to one.

what's this?

“The ultimate livestream.”

The words emerged drier than he'd expected. He licked his lips without trying again in a voice better suited to announcements.

“I'm Lin Mo. I've opened my departure application. I need a hundred thousand eligible viewers in this same session, all keeping FREEDOM selected down on the left. Leave or withdraw it, the count drops. It isn't clicking once and leaving your name.”

ultimate with one viewer?

“Before you arrived, there were none.”

The viewer said nothing else but didn't leave. Three more arrived. Lin Mo couldn't know whether the first had invited them. Four wasn't proof he'd captured anybody.

where's the host who did the questions with you?

He kept the camera away from the waiting area. Another comment followed: was he hiding her until the audience grew?

“She hasn't agreed to be filmed. You can't buy that moment.”

“And that's your answer.”

He sounded firm. Inside, he feared all four would leave. Xiao Lu was only steps away. Asking her to say something might make the empty frame easier to fill. He left the shot alone and walked into the vacant patch of light himself.

He followed the outside of the rig and pushed the pivoted side mirror a little farther. Welded metal entered the frame. Half his reflected face vanished.

“This one's been repaired. Not very well. You're welcome to look closely.”

He kept his face beside the mirror's back, unobstructed.

“I'm here. Want to see a person? Stop waiting for a mirror to arrange one for you.”

Attendance reached nine. Someone asked which sessions he'd performed in. Another recognized the white wrist scar and wondered how this beginner was still alive. His fingers stopped against the frame.

“Someone didn't survive. Old Chen. A viewer pursued a claim against him, and I watched his body disappear. I won't perform his last pleas. The person on camera today is me. Take it up with him.”

He heard his quickening breath. Challenging them hadn't stopped his arm hurting. He checked that the wheels still left the End button clear, then faced the camera.

An ordinary cognitive proposal appeared at the side: HAPPY WITH WHO YOU ARE. Sixty percent required. Few approvals yet; its timer ran. He pressed his tongue against his palate, checking he hadn't begun repeating the title.

“Say you like it first. Don't borrow my mouth.”

One comment said passing it would spoil the show. Another asked why they'd take his insults for nothing. The timer expired at twenty-three percent. FAILED appeared, and the proposal shrank into the sidebar. He didn't relax. The next proposal would still need sixty.

“Here's a bet.”

He tapped the mirror's back. Even that small blow hurt his palm.

“I'll turn this whole rig away and walk onto the platform. Tell me what would make me turn it back myself. My old face. My old voice. A nice story where I never did anything wrong. Have a guess.”

“Guess right, I'll answer a question about myself. Leave the people beside me out of it. I can't answer for them.”

“Losing won't press FREEDOM for you. That's your choice.”

Restore him. Show him his sister. Make him fall. He couldn't answer every suggestion. They guessed thoughts he hadn't voiced, chilling him. For the first time, he could also see they weren't all waiting for the same thing.

A comment from NoTearjerkers pushed through.

NoTearjerkers: You can barely walk. You'll need the mirror to see your feet.

He looked down. A low crossbar joined the rig's base, brakes at either end. The attendant had pressed them easily. Lin Mo needed to work around his wood to reach the right one. His foot fell short; he stepped closer.

“Keep the wood out of the wheels,” Wang Hai said.

He stopped and moved its broken end outside the rig. Wang Hai remained off camera. Lin Mo didn't pretend he'd thought of it himself.

“Someone just reminded me. I didn't suddenly get better at this.”

outside help? some bet

“The bet's about turning it back. Not dying clever and alone.”

He released the right brake, circled round and released the left. The rig moved more easily than expected. He checked his force, but it slid another half palm's length. The weld flashed under the light like teeth bared in darkness.

Bracing the wood, he pushed with the heel of his right hand. His wrist couldn't twist. He changed his footing instead, inching the frame around. Platform, stool, his own back passed through the reflection. The central mirror still faced the camera. Two more steps would turn it fully away.

More than twenty people watched.

The argument obscured part of the picture. He couldn't finish reading. First return the rig to level ground, lock it, then take the route he'd tested up the ramp. His left shoulder was going numb, but old habits urged him faster, as though a longer step could spare him the pain of those after it.

He took the longer step.

His hip cleared the frame. His stocking caught the crossbar. His knee failed to bend where he expected; the wood landed beside the wheeled base while his heel hung clear of the floor.

He grabbed the mirror's edge.

The pivot couldn't bear his weight. The side panel swung back and struck another mirror with a crack sharper than the comment alerts. His forearm slid down the rear frame. The torn sleeve caught, exposing a hot flash of pain.

The whole rig swung toward the ramp.

“Stop pushing!” Xiao Lu called.

He had. His chest now pressed the frame to keep it from rolling. The crossbar caught his shin; one foot stood ahead of the other. His wrist couldn't pull, his left arm couldn't brace. The central mirror passed before him, partly blocking his route to End Broadcast.

The route he'd cleared himself.

“Right foot back,” Wang Hai said.

“The bar's there.”

“I can't see inside. Don't force it because I said so.”

He didn't. Panting, he saw his feet in the side mirror: one behind the bar, one outside the wheel. That small reflection placed them more accurately than the old legs in his head.

NoTearjerkers' prediction still climbed the screen.

He wanted one last argument that he hadn't lost. The mirror had swung back by accident. Shut his eyes, feel his foot free, and the bet could continue. FREEDOM still showed zero, yet the thought forbade retreat as though a hundred thousand were waiting.

“Xiao Lu.”

“Listening.”

“I need to turn this panel a little more to see below. Will it catch you?”

She stood and took two steps toward the wall.

“Not now. No farther toward me.”

He agreed and eased the frame with his right fingertips. His face left the center; shin and gray crossbar came into view. He stopped turning, found the brake and pressed it down with his toe.

One end locked.

He drew his weight away from his chest. Unable to straighten, he lowered himself beside the rig. His knee hit the floor. The cry went straight into the broadcast, with no fine words to follow it.

His second foot finally came free.

He sat, afraid to lean back against the frame. The wood lay half an arm's length away. His reflection was pale, forehead wet. The blue clothing and altered waist and chest remained. His awkwardness didn't make this body belong to him any less. Calling himself Lin Mo hadn't protected it from impact either.

He pressed the other brake and tested the rig. It held.

“Do you want me to come in?” Xiao Lu asked.

He wanted to say yes. On the floor, his claim that he could walk by himself felt too fragile to touch. Looking at the crooked base, he asked whether she could see if its wheels still blocked the control-panel route.

“Half a wheel. It'll stop you going left.”

“Stay there for now. I need to settle it.”

“I meant helping you. Not protecting your bet.”

He looked up. She waited for him to separate those things.

“I know.” He paused. “I was still trying to say I hadn't lost. I'm not doing that now. Watch for me. Tell me what I shouldn't touch.”

She agreed to that and stayed outside the line.

“I lost,” he told the camera.

“Yes. Ask your question.”

He waited, right hand on the wood, left forearm resting on his knee. He expected his old face, a humiliating line, or the pleas he'd promised not to perform.

The comment was brief.

NoTearjerkers: If you reach a hundred thousand and she still can't get out, do you leave?

He looked beyond the camera. Xiao Lu stood where she'd chosen. She wasn't watching him on behalf of a vote, or waiting to answer for him.

He opened his mouth. The mirror had turned back. Its metal rear couldn't hide the question now.
