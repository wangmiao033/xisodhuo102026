# Chapter 79: After She Put the Paper Away

On the way back, Lin Mo didn't look at the microphone again.

He planted the wood outside the standing position and waited for its end to settle before moving one foot. At the level patch beyond the channel, he turned in smaller steps than before. The notebook pressed his ribs like another bone he'd carried all this way.

Xiao Lu didn't count for him.

The close camera kept his retreating back as his shoulder slid toward its edge. Watching the torn sleeve disappear, she lifted her finger from Send, then returned it.

“Nearly out of my picture.”

“I know.”

He looked down for the dark mark on the wood. That end was rougher now, resisting slightly against the floor. He lifted and replanted it instead of dragging it, without scraping at the burr that had caught him.

He didn't have to tear out everything that had hurt him before he could pass.

When the seat stood directly ahead, his right leg trembled harder. He stopped rather than join the last few steps together. His reflection still walked slowly behind him, no longer accompanied by the sentence saying he wished to stay.

Reaching the seat, he touched its edge with the backs of his legs before drawing the wood closer. Sitting pulled a dull ache through his old shoulder injury. He caught himself with his palm heel instead of falling backward, waited for the pain to settle, then eased his hand free.

The original side camera returned to Xiao Lu's screen.

“Seated?”

He tapped the seat edge.

She didn't ask again whether he wanted water. Another question couldn't bring the cup through the wall. Watching him settle the wood ahead on his right, its end against the seat leg, she slowly leaned against her own hard chair.

His palm touched the left control panel.

End remained. He didn't open it. Its familiar light helped establish that he'd returned to the same place.

BODY MODIFICATION: 74%. COGNITIVE OVERWRITE: 54%.

The camera change hadn't reversed the correction. The excluded fragment remained in the verified record; the current ruling still revoked the presumption of voluntary stay.

“Still here… on my side.”

Understanding, Xiao Lu checked her result page too.

“Mine too.”

A backstage notice said this review could now end. No countdown chased her finger. A short explanation stated that closure would end this objection's related audio and correction access; verified materials and effective rulings would remain.

Her fingertip hovered outside Close.

They'd finally heard each other through these walls. When he'd left the picture, she'd had only those few sounds of wood touching down. Closing would take that connection too.

She turned the receipt faceup.

UNUSED remained printed on it. Paper wouldn't rewrite itself to follow the screen. Touching the dent from the index's corner, she checked the usage record. The credential had connected. Once closed, its single use would be exhausted, whatever this unchanged sheet might say.

Seatkeeper: In such a hurry to put it away? Might need it later.

The red words appeared beside the public correction status. Seatkeeper couldn't see her actual code, but could see the review had reached its end.

She lowered the paper, presenting it to no camera.

“This one's finished.”

Wouldn't an open door be safer?

Lin Mo turned toward the seat microphone. He nearly told her she needn't keep it open for him, then stopped.

His sister was still sitting at the other end. Her hand would press the control.

“Can we still talk… afterward?”

She found the original collaboration entrance. Ordinary connection requests remained available, with the warning that judgment capture could prevent audio access. She'd been able to request that since taking this station. It didn't belong to the paper's single allowance.

“This connection will stop. I can request ordinary contact again. Have to try.”

“Yes.”

“Don't start walking across again.”

He looked at his legs. Fatigue breathed through his answer.

“Wasn't planning to.”

Her mouth moved slightly. She could imagine him saying it, but still checked the picture and his steady wood before stacking the sheets.

“I'm closing it.”

She wasn't seeking his approval. She was telling him what would happen, so the sudden silence wouldn't make him think something had gone wrong.

He lifted his fingertips toward the camera.

She pressed Close.

Sound went first. Her hand remained where it was as Send turned gray, its moving bar flattening. The correction entrance folded away. The administrator-format field retained THIS USE CONSUMED.

USES: ONE. REMAINING: ZERO.

She tried no other number, typed no current name into the gray fields. Her old account remained canceled; the ordinary collaborator field still read Xiao Lu. Using this chance to help him speak hadn't seated her among the voting audience.

In the judgment room, the familiar background noise vanished.

All this time he'd heard little sounds of paper being pressed down, an occasional chair creak when she shifted. Now only his breathing remained. One ring of distant capture lights went out. The light beside his seat stayed on.

He checked the ruling immediately.

THIS PRESUMPTION OF VOLUNTARY STAY REVOKED.

Still complete.

He pointed toward it for the current camera, held briefly, then lowered his hand. Xiao Lu saw and checked the same result on her closed review page.

Losing sound for a while hadn't cost them what they'd accomplished.

She folded the receipt along its crease, code inward, and wrapped the index around it. Opening her bag, she found a flat space apart from the script and put both sheets where the cup's bottom wouldn't crush them.

Once the bag closed, the console was empty for the first time.

The bare patch felt strange. Since seeing that old recorded hand, she'd kept pressing the paper, as though releasing it meant losing everything again. Now it wasn't beneath her palm. Her brother remained onscreen; so did the correction.

She rested both hands on her thighs, slowly opening fingers held curled too long.

Then she leaned toward the station and requested ordinary collaboration contact.

The request waited at its original entrance. No management-call prefix, no invitation to choose another sealed file. The status stayed pending. She still received the picture and sound from the judgment room.

Soles brushed the corridor floor.

She stood, stepped around chair legs and bag straps, and opened the door. The attendant who'd brought her was passing with his narrow panel. He stopped.

“Handing the station back?”

She glanced behind her. With the paper gone and the credential entrance closed, it did look as though a job had ended.

“No. I'm staying here.”

He looked at the pending request without reaching to cancel it.

“Ordinary contact. Wait until judgment capture releases the audio.”

“Can I sit and wait with the request pending?”

“Yes.”

As he turned away, she stopped him. Not to restore the code or carry a Ge across to her brother. Pointing toward the station's continuing picture, she asked whether surrendering it would mean applying again somewhere else.

That would be a transfer to another post, he said. Verification would have to begin again.

She released the doorframe.

“I'm not changing.”

The door closed without catching her bag strap, without a warning sound. Back at her chair, she carefully straightened its legs and settled into the small warmth she'd left. Choosing to stay couldn't light the gray entrance sooner. It did prevent an exhausted credential from being treated as her reason to leave.

Lin Mo didn't know which step she'd reached. He saw RELATED AUDIO ENDED and ORDINARY COLLABORATION REQUEST PENDING. He saved his breath instead of calling repeatedly into the thin line.

FREEDOM slipped from 6,181 to 6,044, then slowly recovered. Some thought an entrance disappearing meant his helper had gone. They urged him to say something quickly and keep people watching.

He swallowed the dry pain in his throat without immediately answering.

She'd said she would try.

Looking down, he nudged the notebook sideways inside his clothes without taking it out. The hard corner finally left his ribs; a distinct pressure lingered beneath the fabric. Unable to raise his left arm far, he gathered the torn sleeve lightly with the backs of his fingers, keeping its loose edge off the seat.

The microphone was close. He didn't explain every movement to the audience. Sitting hadn't stopped the pain. His legs still trembled; his throat remained dry. At least all those hurting places no longer had to keep him standing at once.

Backstage, the connection field became available. Xiao Lu checked its destination first. Judgment capture no longer occupied the distant statement audio; ordinary collaboration was admitted within this broadcast. Further capture could interrupt it again.

No new review authority. No restored account.

Before holding Send, she checked that the label wasn't LOCAL TEST.

“Ge.”

His eyes lifted from the line.

“Heard you.”

With her other hand, she drew the bag closer inside the chair legs.

“Paper's put away.”

He nodded slowly. This nod answered only what she'd just said. It belonged to no prescribed sentence.

“Keep it… secure.”

“I'm not carrying it up a mountain.”

He glanced at the wood beside his foot and didn't answer that. His mouth moved slightly. Seeing, she didn't urge him to laugh aloud.

Someone asked whether, now she'd stopped using the code, she could quit collaboration and cast a freedom vote like the people outside. She examined her status without mistaking quit for an answer.

Ordinary collaboration could end. That didn't mean a current performer could enter the audience. No entrance offered her that change, no qualification had been granted.

“Not now.”

Later?

She paused.

“I don't know yet.”

Not knowing rankled. She'd once surrendered what she could, believing she might shield her brother for a while. Now she finally occupied a position she'd chosen herself, yet a box other people could select so easily remained beyond her reach.

She didn't open the bag again.

Her brother no longer needed another promise bought with that paper. Proving she still wanted to help didn't require immediately setting out everything she had left.

Hearing her answer, Lin Mo didn't say having him was enough. He wouldn't promise everything would be restored after their escape. He remained in this room; a hundred thousand was still far away.

“Where do you want… to stay?”

“At this station for now. I can hear you here. See you too.”

“All right.”

“If you don't want to speak, tap once. I can hear that.”

He tapped the seat edge. A small sound. The ordinary collaboration's incoming bar moved one notch.

Xiao Lu saw. Her palm finally needed to hold down no paper at all.

Across the room, the disputed-statement position dimmed, leaving the slender microphone empty. The cropped smiles and nods withdrew from the center with the crossed-out caption, shrinking into the processed record. The middle of the glass showed Lin Mo sitting again.

THIS OBJECTION CLOSED.

Beneath it, the final judgment's overall ruling began to load.

He didn't rise to meet it. Keeping the wood within reach, he held his back an inch from the seat edge to ease his left shoulder. He waited where he was for the words to settle.
