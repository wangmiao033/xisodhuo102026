# Chapter 76: The Page She Closed

She moved her hand from the receipt to her knee. The hard chair had numbed her legs. She was about to stand when her brother leaned toward the microphone; she stayed to listen.

Her words reached the judgment room and the same continuing broadcast. She didn't flash other records to prove they existed, didn't offer her original name to silence suspicion.

Lin Mo slowly settled back.

“All right.”

He hadn't won her the right to say no. She'd already said it, as she'd once complained he walked too slowly, as she'd just asked for time to think. He could leave his acknowledgment beside her decision without stepping into her place.

He regretted losing them anyway.

There was nothing admirable about the feeling. His stomach seemed abruptly hollow. Looking away from the count, he studied the pale worn grain of his wood and waited until that emptiness no longer supplied the breath for a decision.

Xiao Lu didn't find the screen fair now. Those questions had left her hands cold for a long time. This late line couldn't remove them from her ears. She pointed out the result's location so Lin Mo could enlarge it himself, offering no assurances that the rules would protect them.

He touched his clothes. The notebook remained inside, its stiff back against his ribs, pen securely clipped. The screen used the thumbnail he'd already published. It hadn't opened the older pages.

He looked across the room.

No chair at the single-person position. A slender microphone stood outside its edge, with the delayed glass behind. The shallow channel ran diagonally between it and his seat. In changing light, the silver border shone, then seemed rubbed away.

Xiao Lu returned her feed to its original size, seeking another sliver of floor. Still only his upper body and half the wood.

“I can't see clearly under your feet,” she said first.

“I'll look.”

He stayed seated. Extending the wood, he slid its end across level floor to the silver strip and tapped lightly. The channel beyond sounded hollower; the far side, solid. He didn't need to put a foot into it to know where not to trust his weight.

His reflection extended its support a moment later. He drew his gaze from the glass to the piece of wood actually in his hand.

“Don't guide me… by the mirror.”

She paused, then understood. “I'm watching your live camera.”

“Just did.”

He acknowledged her without repeating it a third time. Her mouth twitched. Separated, they could finally complain of each other's repetition. The wall remained; neither had forgotten.

His panel specified the correction position, appending the existing objection number. Submission wasn't complete. Leaving the seat didn't accept any of Seatkeeper's interpretations. End remained in its original place. To use it, he'd have to return here.

He drew the wood closer and tested whether his feet would bear him.

His right knee tightened immediately. Old pain shot up from his hip. He returned his weight to the seat rather than force himself upright. Xiao Lu saw his shoulders lower and didn't tell him to try again.

Lin Mo instinctively wanted to warn him about his wrist. Then he realized he didn't know whether the stage could hear this objection-channel audio. He didn't call a string of names to test it. He only watched a moment.

Someone outside the door; someone backstage; someone on this narrow seat. Seeing each other didn't mean they could reach. He remembered the hand holding down the ramp mat and looked for support he could actually use here.

Slowly he spread his fingers. Sweat made the rough wood slippery. He wiped his palm on an untorn part of his clothes, then planted the support securely.

“Give me a moment.”

“I'll wait.”

The correction window hadn't closed because he'd sat back. No urgent countdown had appeared. The distant microphone simply shone, waiting for him to bring his still-hurting body across.

He used the pause to study the channel's end. Its silver border stopped short of the wall, leaving level floor before the standing position. The distance was hard to judge in the dimness. He'd approach along level ground and inspect the last stretch there. Looking wide enough wasn't the same as having tested it beneath his foot.

The red name lit again.

Seatkeeper: Audio can be supplied. So can paper. Show us who stands up and says it now.

He looked at his hand: altered joints, old wrist pain, injuries still beneath his sleeve. All remained between raising and lowering it. He wasn't standing to display a shape that better pleased anyone.

The correction entrance was open across the room.

He shifted the notebook inside his clothes so its corner wouldn't dig into his ribs. The wood landed first on nearby floor he knew could take weight. Right foot secure; left foot positioned without pulling his hip. He waited for a breath, then slowly leaned forward.

Backstage, Xiao Lu sat straight, watching the actual camera picture.

“I can still see you.”

He didn't turn to find the voice.

Steadying himself on the wood, he left the narrow seat.
