# Chapter 72: The Threshold Admits One

The first viewer to announce they'd voted didn't say for what.

They only demanded Lin Mo go in. Someone asked where: what if people had chosen differently?

The two tallies refreshed at intervals, their rising numbers attached to no particular comment. His right hand remained beside End. No new modification descended, but speaking had left his throat increasingly dry and sore.

Xiao Lu took out her cup and asked before offering it.

“Yes.”

“Heard that one.”

She unscrewed the lid. He supported the cup with his palm heels and sipped. His right wrist trembled as he returned it. She steadied the bottom, waiting for him to release it, then replaced the lid and put it away.

EmptyCup: Chose judgment. Still voting FREEDOM. Couldn't send you back, could I?

judgment too. that room hasn't opened in ages

The comments appeared together. Someone directed the second at EmptyCup: same motives, stop pretending. EmptyCup swore back. Another viewer demanded they stop arguing and let the broadcaster give thanks.

Lin Mo shook his head. Thinking he wanted her to skip it, Xiao Lu moved on. He tapped the panel edge.

“I want to answer.”

She pointed back to the comment, choosing no easier question for him.

“Same box. Doesn't mean… the same.”

then tell us who's good and who's bad

“Don't know. Haven't gone in.”

His new voice thinned at the end. He didn't force it toward its old position. He waited for the pain to ease.

“I know… I don't want to perform again.”

RETURN TO PRESCRIBED PERFORMANCE was rising too. Some demanded obedience; others feared judgment would hurt more, insisting the second choice at least kept him alive. One admitted misclicking and couldn't work out how to withdraw and keep watching.

Xiao Lu found the rule: choices could change before the deadline; no selection suspended the picture. She pointed it out without promising the first choice would bring anyone out alive.

staying on gray. caught your last sentence. choosing neither

Delayed among earlier comments, it received no follow-up. The blank count increased. Lin Mo couldn't tell whether the person remained or had closed the session.

Wang Hai suddenly leaned forward beyond the line.

“She asked if you're cold.”

Lin Mo looked around. Xiao Lu's hand had been resting on her bag for some time. A screen chime had covered her quiet question.

“Not cold.”

She studied his trembling right knee. Moving the stool closer hadn't given his feet strength. He started to press the leg still, but pain in his wrist scar stopped him.

“When we walk… slowly.”

“Think I'll drag you into a run?”

“You don't walk slowly.”

“I'll check the route first. Come back and complain how slow you are.”

She didn't leave the camera's view without asking. They waited together for the deadline bar to reach its edge.

The short chimes stopped.

FINAL JUDGMENT: 6,831.

RETURN TO PRESCRIBED PERFORMANCE: 2,487.

Blanks, departures, and invalid choices remained separate, colored for neither side. Judgment held the majority. The result entered the session record.

TRANSFER TO FINAL JUDGMENT. THIS RESULT DOES NOT CONSTITUTE THE APPLICANT'S CONSENT TO PAST OR FUTURE MODIFICATIONS.

Below, his exit application still showed no approval.

FREEDOM stood at 3,196, then 3,189. He looked from the result to those nine thousand valid choices without adding them to the shortage at the lower left.

Seatkeeper: The people's will.

“A majority.”

The red words didn't change.

“Some people… didn't answer your question.”

He didn't claim them all as supporters. Some were bored, frightened of choosing badly, or had closed the session and ceased caring. All he could preserve for them were those visible blanks, already listed outside victory and defeat.

A latch released at the black frame's base.

The gray-elbowed attendant left the wheels locked. Crouching, he unfolded a narrow ramp. The rear wall parted along its central seam, revealing a short, ceiling-lit passage. The frame's empty center finally led somewhere. No mirror; no hand reaching out.

A scanning light appeared at the threshold. The red border divided into front and rear layers. Another fixed camera inside the passage faced the stage.

The attendant displayed the transfer diagram. The broadcast would continue uninterrupted. The frame would check the applicant's number, and the judgment-room picture would succeed the stage feed. Until entry, Lin Mo could end at the original panel; afterward, at the synchronized controls left of the judgment seat.

“What if he falls halfway?” Xiao Lu asked.

The attendant looked up.

“I'm asking you. Don't look at him. He hasn't got shoes.”

He pressed down a slightly raised ramp edge, then laid a narrow nonslip mat into the passage. The board showed an emergency transfer-stop control for staff. Pausing the handover did not let them decide whether the applicant's broadcast continued.

Xiao Lu crouched and felt the mat's joint. Only when it lay flat did she stand.

“Now there's something.”

Lin Mo knew she didn't mean shoes. He planted the wood before his stool and set both feet securely.

Asked whether he'd enter according to the result, he remained seated. He opened End's confirmation page. Ending still invalidated the current freedom count. One final confirmation would spare him this doorway.

“Ge.”

Xiao Lu spoke softly, careful not to hurry him. Her bag was in her hand; her wooden chair stayed where it stood.

“I still want to come to the entrance. I'll look at the job beyond it.”

“Look first. Then say.”

She nodded.

He backed out without confirming End. He studied the ballot result and the still-lit FREEDOM count once more.

“I'll go in. Keep speaking.”

He rose on the wood. Hip pain caught first; his altered center of balance carried him farther forward than old habit expected, and his right knee wouldn't straighten securely. Xiao Lu stopped her reaching hand before touching his shoulder.

“Where?”

“Right… waist. Don't pull my wrist.”

She placed her palm behind his right waist, steadying that one sway. When his legs took his weight, he asked her to release him. He could make the next step himself.

Wang Hai had planted his feet before the waiting bench. Seeing Lin Mo steady, he drew back his hand.

“Wood on the mat first. Almost missed the edge.”

Lin Mo shifted its end inward. Those few inches looked insignificant on camera; under pressure, they became something that could actually bear him.

He nodded to Wang Hai.

The notebook remained inside his clothes, its stiff back against his ribs. Old pages, inner compartment, and the newly written sheet hadn't fallen out. He checked through the fabric with the back of his hand. The pen was still clipped in place.

“Want me to carry it?” Xiao Lu asked.

“I can hold it.”

“Meant the wood.”

He looked at the support he couldn't comfortably walk without, then at her.

“Can’t let go of… that one.”

Her mouth tightened around a smile, cracking her tense expression.

“I'll carry mine, then. Stop looking at me. Watch the floor.”

The attendant reached for Lin Mo's left shoulder. He turned slightly, enough to keep that spot clear.

“Not there.”

The hand halted. Perhaps the man only wanted the transfer over quickly. He didn't know what couldn't bear pressure beneath the clothes. Before Xiao Lu could speak, Lin Mo pointed to the unsecured mat edge beside the doorway.

“Hold that. Don't let it curl.”

The attendant crouched. Lin Mo waited until the hand had left his shoulder before moving. Cropped afterward, the movement could look like shrugging off help. That wouldn't lessen the deep ache beneath the shoulder.

Xiao Lu didn't explain him to the camera. She stood ahead and to one side, visible, letting him decide when to follow.

On the ramp he couldn't answer anything. His torn sleeve brushed the old cut on his left forearm. Each placement of the wood sent pain under his shoulder. He kept as much weight as possible off his arms: step, stop, establish the foot, then step again.

The comments became a small bright patch high to one side. Someone accused him of prolonging a performance at the threshold. Someone mentioned old injuries. He couldn't follow their argument, only Xiao Lu locating the next seam.

“Half a step.”

He began lifting his foot. She corrected herself immediately.

“Half a step to the edge. Don't step over.”

He planted it again and exhaled through his teeth.

“Say stop… first.”

“Stop. Stop now.”

She studied his face. He neither blamed her nor said it didn't matter. They corrected the instruction before looking together at the red line.

Only the applicant could cross. Collaborators could accompany him this far; continued participation meant the side passage backstage, with the position still unconfirmed. Xiao Lu's toes stopped on the stage side. She didn't reach for his waist again.

“Can she see… once I'm across?” he asked the attendant.

“The entrance first. Collaborator pictures need another connection.”

Xiao Lu shifted the bag between hands and glanced at the narrow door marked BACKSTAGE.

“I'll ask. Find you when I know.”

No promise that she definitely could. He nodded and put the wood across the threshold, followed by his left foot.

Red light scanned his clothes. No one took the notebook or demanded he leave the wood. His applicant number matched the original panel. He waited for the camera-transfer bar to fill and the second controls to light before bringing his remaining foot inside.

The judgment seat wasn't the raised platform he'd imagined. Two steps beyond the entrance stood a fixed narrow seat, its left panel angled toward him. End glowed at the upper right.

The floor dipped just before it. His toe had reached the edge when he probed with the wood. The sound was lower. He withdrew his foot, moved sideways along the level surface, and took the seat's edge.

BODY MODIFICATION: 74%. COGNITIVE OVERWRITE: 54%.

The transfer display flashed past. Neither number had changed. He still needed patience he'd never imagined to arrange his knees and hips for sitting. Unchanged figures didn't mean his body was fine.

He set the wood within reach at his right front, its end against the seat's foot. His notebook pressed his ribs; he shifted slightly to make room for that too.

Resting his palm heel on the edge, he confirmed this was still the same broadcast and slowly sat. Once his back stopped straining, cold sweat settled against the fabric.

The left panel retained the entrance view briefly. Xiao Lu stood outside with her bag. Farther away, a corner of Wang Hai's waiting bench remained visible. Lin Mo tried to see the route behind her, but the gray panels slowly closed at his back. The stage image on his left-hand screen shrank to a narrow observation window. Its sound faded too.

He called Xiao Lu. Only his own higher voice struck the room, painfully tight.

In the window, she watched the stage screen. She'd apparently seen his mouth move without hearing him. She pointed toward the side door and turned to follow the attendant.

He didn't mistake her gesture for a reply received.

Deep in the room, an upright pane of glass lit. Taller black supports disappeared into darkness around it, like an enormous mirror still being assembled.

Beneath the glass, white letters recognized his name first.

LIN MO: REVIEW OF INTENT TO LEAVE.
