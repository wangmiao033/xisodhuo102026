# Chapter 67: I Know You Now

Lin Mo turned toward her without rising.

Beside the scenic panel's narrow shadow, Xiao Lu studied the person close enough to hear breathing. She didn't search the screen for his old face, ask him to move his hair or find an angle resembling the past. Her gaze still seemed dazed.

“Closer.”

His stool scraped a little nearer. His left shoulder was raised. When she pointed, he stopped, thinking she meant don't move.

“Your shoulder. Lower it.”

“It hurts when I lower it too.”

“Then don't.”

She withdrew her hand and smiled. He wanted to smile back, afraid the breath might blow everything away.

Her eyes settled on his mouth.

“When you ate those noodles, you hadn't swallowed before you started lecturing me. I wanted you to choke back a few words. But I really was afraid you'd choke.”

She breathed in slowly.

“At first, all I had was that sentence. Then the mouthfuls I ate myself. Me sitting angry over the half-fastened lid. Now I know the person. You.”

His right fingers pressed the stool. He couldn't answer.

“I remember waiting for my brother. I remember why I bought protection for Lin Mo. Not that agreement they just put in your head. I didn't discuss it with you. I was afraid.”

His eyes burned. She lifted her chin, as though bringing the hospital room, that remembered breath and his present face into focus together.

“Ge. I know you now.”

Her voice was steady. Having said it, she didn't assume another pose or wait for her next line.

His mouth opened. Nothing came.

“I'm here.”

Finally, thin and trembling. He said it again in the voice they'd changed, without lowering it or imitating his former self.

“I'm right here.”

She nodded. Tears dropped from her chin onto her collar. Her hand waited between them, palm up. He placed his right hand in it, keeping the scar clear of her knuckles, closing his fingertips lightly.

She noticed and immediately loosened her hold.

“This one hurts too?”

“Neither's performing well.”

“Excellent timing.”

“Wasn't my choice.”

He tried to laugh and sobbed instead. The unfamiliar sound made him want to turn away. She touched his uninjured fingertips. He looked up: nose red from crying, she watched him without rejecting that voice.

The camera stayed lit. Both appeared in the live image, their joined hands unobscured by the FREEDOM count. Someone could take this moment too. He wanted to hunch around it, hide it as something only theirs.

Xiao Lu wiped her nose with her free hand and faced the camera.

“I called him that. Just now.”

None of it frightened her back into the host's smile. The sidebar still displayed the previous execution. Lin Mo's cognitive reading hadn't fallen. The person in his head who'd arranged everything beforehand wanted to claim her brother's place too.

His fingers tightened. He relaxed them himself.

“The false scene's still there.”

“I know.”

“I don't want to meet you with that.”

“Use this hand for now. Gently.”

He eased the fingertips pressing her palm. She didn't disappear, didn't become someone awaiting proof because he loosened his grip. She'd finished calling him her brother. She'd heard his answer.

Suddenly she covered her eyes with the back of her hand and folded forward. His shoulder tightened, fearing pain. She shook her head, breathed against her hand. Finally she said she'd really been able to finish this time.

He looked at their hands. Last time he'd heard that word, he'd immediately chased lights and the voice replacing hers. Barely caught the edge of happiness. Now he could wait while she wiped her face, cried, complained about her blocked nose, then looked up again.

“I was afraid you'd cry and grip me tighter.”

“I didn't.”

“I know. I pulled a little. You let go.”

Only then did he notice her fingertips resting outside his palm. No test. She'd released him when she wanted, returned her fingers because she still wanted the touch.

An unreasonable hurt rose through him. The tears wouldn't stop.

“When you didn't know me, sometimes I truly didn't know where to stand.”

“Neither did I. Every time you came, I was afraid one missing answer would lose you.”

“You don't have to make those times up.”

“Wasn't planning to.”

She turned her palm toward him. Temper returned before the tears had dried. Looking at her, he stopped trying to swallow his sobs into something dignified.

Beyond the white line, Wang Hai cleared his throat. After a while he asked, “Everyone hear the whole thing this time?”

His right hand had left the wall to cover his forehead, as though shading his eyes. Xiao Lu answered first.

“All of it. He answered twice.”

“Enough. My ears still work.”

He smiled and turned away. He hadn't cast a vote to confirm their kinship or settled every question about the past. He was nearby, finally spared the urgent need to extend the wood and catch someone stumbling out.

Xiao Lu withdrew her hand, rubbed the sweat from his fingers against her palm without wiping it off. Could she sit closer?

“Bring your chair. Move mine, I might have to learn sitting again.”

“Finally admit you're slower at something.”

She first moved her bag to the floor farther in beside the table, then carried the wooden chair around his wood. The red chair stayed empty, stiff fabric fraying under the light. She ignored it.

He moved his right foot to make room. The broad tie pulled at his hip. At his gasp, she stopped, waiting for him to free the trapped jacket hem before settling her chair. They couldn't sweep each other into an embrace, or even reach the same height in one easy movement.

She sat a palm's width from his shoulder.

“What are you looking at?”

“You.”

“Looking properly this time.”

He lifted his left hand toward her chin. His shoulder wouldn't let it reach. Resting it on his knee, he watched her wipe her own face. Her mouth slowly turned down.

“I really remember. But not the date of those noodles. Lots of faces missing. I'm afraid you'll get happy and ask me to find everything else.”

“No exam today.”

“Don't bring a test tomorrow either.”

“I never brought a test.”

She looked at him.

“Plenty of questions, though,” he admitted.

Laughing, she tapped her chair leg with her foot. The movement held her old temper and impatience he'd met only in the theater. He didn't single out either as the real person.

“You watched me like this in hospital. Fell asleep: there you were checking. Turned over: another look. I pulled the blanket to my ears on purpose. You thought I was cold.”

“You said you were cold.”

“How long would you have sat there otherwise?”

“I really thought you were.”

“Now you know.”

Something rose in his chest, mingling with unfinished laughter, quickening his tears. He'd thought he'd lost someone who accepted every kindness. She'd returned with a small occasion he'd misunderstood.

“You did your share of lying.”

“You added another blanket.”

“It was cold.”

“My face was covered. You kept piling them on.”

He remembered the quilt's corner, finally understanding her muffled laugh beneath it. He'd thought his company made her happier. Remembering the hospital afterward, he'd treasured that sound, as though keeping enough good moments could make her days less hard.

“I asked the nurse whether to turn down the vent.”

“That's why I threw the blanket off. See? Lying to you took effort.”

He laughed through fresh tears. No need to say she'd always opposed him. A living person opposed him now. Why cover her with another layer of the past?

She watched him.

“And the big one. Be angry if you're angry. I can hear you now.”

His fingers pressed the wood's carved marks. The heaviest things he wanted to say began with why. Even those already spoken wanted asking again. She didn't hurry him toward It's all right.

“I'm afraid you think paying down to zero stopped you being a person. That all I have left is a debt for protection.”

She shook her head slowly.

“When I signed, knowing you'd remember me seemed like something left. When I really started disappearing, I found it wasn't enough. I wanted myself back.”

“And now?”

She moved her bag closer to her feet.

“Still do. What I ate. What frightened me. How I learned to speak onstage. I'm keeping all of it.”

He looked at the bag holding her script. So many pages he'd had no right to open. Being her brother wouldn't suddenly tell him what they contained. For once, those unseen pages didn't make him anxious.

“Keep them.”

“And Xiao Lu.”

He looked up. She touched her collar. No former name had suddenly grown back there.

“I'm keeping that too. Don't rush off finding a new name tag now. I can use this one and know who I am.”

“Xiao Lu.”

She answered quickly, then frowned.

“Don't say it like you're learning.”

“Practicing.”

“You've said it plenty.”

“Always afraid you'd leave.”

She considered him.

“I can go away and come back. I fetched my bag, didn't I?”

He nodded. She steadied the wood without taking it away or leaving her hand permanently beneath his wrist. The path beside the table remained. Doors still existed beyond them.

“Me. Wait for the old face to answer, and you'll be waiting all day.”
