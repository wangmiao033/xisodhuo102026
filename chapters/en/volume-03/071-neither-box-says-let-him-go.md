# Chapter 71: Neither Box Says Let Him Go

When the first line appeared, Xiao Lu covered her mouth.

Lin Mo leaned toward her, thinking she'd bitten her tongue again. She shook her head and pointed without touching the screen.

“Don't read along yet. This isn't addressed to you.”

VIEWING CONFIRMATION: TO CONTINUE WATCHING, SELECT ONE OF THE FOLLOWING.

Two empty boxes lit side by side.

SUBMIT APPLICANT LIN MO TO FINAL JUDGMENT.

REJECT THIS EXIT APPLICATION. RETURN THE APPLICANT TO THE PRESCRIBED PERFORMANCE SEQUENCE.

His eyes stopped on the second choice. Prescribed performance sat quietly at the end, printed no darker than anything else.

He straightened against the wood. Pain pulled through his throat. Xiao Lu waited for his breathing to ease before reading on.

“Judgment… of what?”

An explanation unfolded. Final Judgment would review the applicant's wish to leave, conduct during the session, and FREEDOM support. Referral did not mean approval. Existing FREEDOM choices remained valid while the broadcast continued. Return to prescribed performance would terminate his statement in this application; he would perform the assigned sequence until another application session was permitted.

Nothing said when that would be.

Xiao Lu read the tally rule last. A majority of valid choices still present at the deadline would decide; a tie went to review. The ordinary modification threshold of sixty percent did not apply.

Lin Mo scrolled to the bottom. Beneath the choices was a smaller line.

VIEWERS MAY LEAVE BEFORE START. DEPARTURES, NON-SELECTIONS, AND INVALID CHOICES COUNT TOWARD NEITHER OPTION. AFTER START, VIEWERS WHO HAVE NOT SELECTED WILL RECEIVE NO PICTURE. THEY MAY STILL CLOSE THE SESSION.

“Again.”

She started on the majority rule. He tapped the bottom line.

“People leaving.”

She read it again. The viewer who'd asked whether leaving counted as opposition didn't reappear. Someone else asked if a black screen still meant they were sitting here.

He opened the tally explanation toward the camera. Non-selections were recorded as blanks, neither obedience nor referral. Continued viewing required a choice. Silence did not give anyone permission to fill a box.

NoTearjerkers: Then I won't watch. Neither choice says let him go.

“Because he's put them in separate places,” Xiao Lu said. “Your FREEDOM vote doesn't choose here for you.”

The freedom option remained at the lower left. Lin Mo didn't touch it. A broadcaster couldn't add votes for himself.

Two thousand four hundred and six became 2,391, then lost another dozen or more. Attendance changed by different amounts. He couldn't identify everyone leaving, much less find the person who'd just said they wouldn't watch among those falling numbers.

Seatkeeper: See? You've driven them away.

His first attempt produced only dry breath. He lowered his tongue from the roof of his mouth and waited until he could manage a short sentence.

“You gave them… two boxes.”

Seatkeeper: You can end it too.

End flashed under a red highlight. The button hadn't been disabled. Someone had polished the door handle expressly to see whether he'd take it.

CURRENT BROADCAST CAN BE ENDED. ENDING INVALIDATES THIS SESSION'S CURRENT FREEDOM COUNT.

“He wants you to tell everyone not to leave,” Xiao Lu said.

Lin Mo murmured assent.

“Will you?”

He didn't answer immediately. Those two thousand choices felt close enough to touch; he could follow every change in the final digit. A hundred thousand seemed too distant ever to have belonged on this wall. Ending would lose even what was close.

He rested the heel of his right hand on the cold panel edge, keeping his wrist scar from folding inward. End lay within reach of his fingertips.

Xiao Lu watched without restraining his hand.

Outside the white line, Wang Hai shifted forward, then stopped when his right wrist couldn't bear the pressure. Studying the boxes, he finally asked, “How long?”

There was no countdown. Lin Mo found the gray start field. It awaited the applicant's decision to continue the session. After that confirmation, the submitting viewer could initiate the special round.

“Let me finish reading.”

Wang Hai settled back, injured hand on his leg. He didn't hurry either person inside the camera's view.

Lin Mo scrolled upward. His gaze stopped on applicant. Xiao Lu pointed at herself.

“Stop looking. My name isn't there.”

“I know.”

“You look like you're trying to squeeze me into brackets.”

His almost-laugh became a muffled cough. He turned aside, recovered, and looked back.

“Looking for… where helpers go.”

Her sharp expression softened.

The collaborator rules were one level down. Only one exit applicant was registered. Others could withdraw before the transition or choose assigned front-of-stage or backstage support. They would not themselves become subjects of judgment, acquire viewer eligibility, or confirm the applicant's statement for him.

“So I can't sit there and answer in your place,” she said when he'd slowly finished reading.

“Better not.”

“Afraid I'd do better?”

“Afraid you'd… start swearing.”

“Haven't sworn yet.”

She answered faster than he spoke. Then she looked back at the rules, refusing to let familiar bickering agree to anything for her.

“If they send you there, I want to keep helping. I'll choose a position once I've seen what it is. You can't agree to a whole string of things for me now.”

He lowered the hand he'd raised.

“Wasn't going to.”

“I know. Saying it first.”

She gathered her bag's straps at her feet. She wasn't preparing to leave. Watching her hands, he saw the false image of himself holding down her paper beside the hospital bed. Here there was no bed, no signature sheet. She tied the straps, disliked the stubborn knot, and undid it herself.

He waited until she'd finished.

“Tell me… if you change your mind.”

“You too.”

He moved the wood an inch closer to his foot and planted its end. He didn't pull himself upright on the injured wrist, only steadied himself on the low stool.

Asked to simplify the choices, Xiao Lu didn't translate them into let him go and don't. She read both, adding that judgment guaranteed no release.

Lin Mo caught the shortest possible answer between comments already scrolling away.

“I want the first.”

“The second… won't let me speak.”

He drew breath. His higher voice thinned at the end, without changing back. He waited through the pain.

“I didn't write the question. Continuing doesn't mean… you have to stay.”

FREEDOM dropped into the twenty-two hundreds, then edged upward. Some accused him of wanting votes while playing generous. Others objected to making departure an apology owed to him. Someone asked three times whether they could vote freedom without watching.

Xiao Lu found the eligibility notice on the same page. FREEDOM still required an eligible viewer still present and maintaining the choice. Pausing the picture did not itself withdraw it; closing and leaving, or actively withdrawing, meant it no longer counted.

She looked at him before finishing.

“That makes it hard to close.”

“Yes.”

“I don't want to read it like a way around this.”

“Just read… what it says.”

She finished. Someone wrote understood. Other names disappeared. Lin Mo called none back. Nor did he hide his hand from End, presenting a false trap with no possible way out.

The attendant in gray elbow guards checked the black frame's base lights without moving it. A second worker waited beyond the short platform, holding a narrow board.

“Can you choose too?” Xiao Lu asked.

He shook his head and turned the board. Position assignments and transfer status; no viewer boxes.

“We wait for the result.”

“I wasn't asking where you stand.”

He didn't reply.

Lin Mo called him back. He pointed to End, then behind the frame. Before he could speak, the worker said someone would guide the referral route.

“Not asking… the way.”

His throat felt tightened by a fine cord. Xiao Lu had half risen, but stopped, allowing him to finish.

“Once I'm inside… where's this?”

The worker looked at his hand and reversed the board. On the transfer diagram, a line connected the front controls to those beside the judgment seat. The original panel would remain active during transfer. Once the applicant entered, controls to the seat's left would take over the same broadcast. Ending carried the same consequence.

Lin Mo asked him to hold that section before the fixed camera. Only after seeing both CURRENT BROADCAST and CAN BE ENDED did he look away.

planned your retreat already. still acting like you're risking everything?

“Yes.”

The comment vanished before he added, “I want out. So I check… the door.”

Xiao Lu smiled and hooked her bag inward with her foot. A strap caught the wood's end. His support tilted slightly.

They both stopped. She released the bag; he planted the wood again. Neither used the mistake to demand the other keep farther away.

“Your bag… pulled me.”

“I know. Wants to choose my job.”

She bent to free the strap, lifted the whole bag to the chair's other side, and asked how much room the wood needed before setting it down.

Viewers were demanding a start. Lin Mo still studied his stockinged toes. Moving the support had turned his right knee inward; he needed time to redistribute his weight.

“Not ready,” Xiao Lu told the camera.

“I am.”

“Wasn't asking you. He was rushing.”

This time he nodded without laughing aloud. His support was steady. He'd checked where the controls would be. That didn't make him safe. It only meant Seatkeeper wouldn't be spared questions he still had the chance to ask.

He looked beyond the white line. Wang Hai's face seemed gray at the edge of the lights. He shook his head slightly: no vote for him either. Lin Mo had never counted them toward the hundred thousand. Seeing those empty hands still dragged something down inside his chest.

Seatkeeper's red letters stood at the top.

Your decision?

Lin Mo stopped the page at the continuation confirmation and looked at Xiao Lu. She didn't nod on his behalf. She sat back enough for the camera to see his hand.

“I still want… to speak.”

He touched CONTINUE THIS SESSION. It accepted that one decision. End remained lit beside it.

A submission record appeared beneath the red name.

SEATKEEPER HAS INITIATED THE SPECIAL ROUND.

A brief bell sounded twice. Both boxes thickened. Picture reception split into selected and suspended counts; a deadline bar began extending to the right. The first choices arrived. The comments went briefly sparse, then surged back.

voted. now send him in

The black frame's red edge shone painfully bright. Its center remained empty.

Lin Mo watched the new valid-choice numbers instead of that blank space.

“Really started this time,” Xiao Lu said quietly.

“I can see.”

At the upper right, each box received its first mark.
