# Chapter 89: She Hadn't Finished Speaking

Lin Mo moved his hand toward Xiao Lu's elbow.

His fingers met her sleeve. Resistance, at last. He didn't lift. He rested the edge of his palm there until she looked down too.

“There's still something here.”

She eased her arm toward him. Her sleeve dragged faintly across the white cloth, and he shifted to make room on the cart. His right leg wouldn't move; he used the other foot to bring it over, clenching his teeth against the pain.

Her shoulder left the wall. One shoe reached toward the wheel, but its sole made no scrape against the floor.

Her elbow slipped through his hand.

His palm struck his own leg. The attendant beside her tried catching her back, held something briefly, then passed through the thinning cloth.

She didn't fall. She remained close to the wall.

A fall would at least have told them where to put their hands.

Lin Mo looked at his cushion.

“Use this. Support all of her.”

The front attendant held his lower back while he shifted onto bare board. Pain shot through his hip. The gray-costumed woman put down the cup, retrieved its lid and screwed it shut, then returned it to Xiao Lu's bag. Its small knock against the bottom was unmistakable.

With both hands free, she pulled out the cushion and laid it beside Xiao Lu.

“Lean this way.”

Xiao Lu turned. The attendant guarded the back of her head instead of gripping her arm. Lin Mo folded the dirty portion of the white cloth underneath and spread a clean section where her cheek might rest.

She leaned toward it.

A shallow hollow formed in the cushion.

Lin Mo watched it extend beneath her shoulder. Seeing it too, she drew her foot closer to bring her legs over.

“Don't lift yet. Let me turn.”

The woman waited, pressing the cushion's corners. For so long, that cart had dragged her along. Now another person could speak and she could stop.

Xiao Lu turned halfway.

The hollow didn't deepen. Her back remained above the cushion while the padding slowly regained its shape. The attendant raised his end experimentally. It passed through her shoulder. She stayed at the same height, as though some part of her had never received the instruction to leave with the others.

“Put it down.”

Lin Mo searched for resistance at her cuff. He felt the weave once. On his next attempt, his fingers went through.

The last traces of contact were retreating from every place where a hand might support a person.

“Lower,” Xiao Lu said. “I hadn't settled on it.”

They flattened the cushion against the floor. She bent forward, one arm across her body, finding a position with what she could still control. Lin Mo swallowed the instruction to take her time. She was already doing everything she could.

Her hem caught beneath one corner.

Instead of lifting, the woman slid the cushion toward the cart. Fabric rasped against tile. Xiao Lu's knee stayed behind. Her wet hem moved briefly, then the cushion slipped free beneath it.

The woman stopped.

Xiao Lu straightened slowly.

“Where else can you touch?”

The attendant tried her shoulder, then the space behind her waist. Finding nowhere that could bear weight, he rested his empty hands on his knees.

Dust fell overhead. Beyond the second corner, hurried footsteps slowed at a shout to keep clear of the doorway. Something crashed inside holding. One cart wheel rolled a fraction, and the woman braced it with her foot.

Lin Mo tucked both legs aside. He had made enough room for the cushion, enough room for his sister, but couldn't bring her onto the board.

“What about the desk she used?”

The attendant looked toward holding. Glass shattered near the registration terminal. Its overhead light flared once, then dimmed.

“It has nothing to replenish her with.”

“Her old station?”

His voice tightened, high and scraped raw. Xiao Lu looked back along their route. A fallen beam obscured the shallow console's doorway, leaving a chair leg visible underneath. Nothing was being transmitted from there.

“Ge. That's where I left.”

He lowered himself toward her voice.

“I know. Let's find somewhere we can support you.”

He didn't ask her to undergo another release or restore the performer designation she had relinquished. There was only the body before him, still trying to move toward his.

He held out one end of the white cloth for her to find her own grip. The other end lay beneath his hand against the cart's edge, unknotted.

Xiao Lu pressed down with the heel of her palm.

The cloth moved.

She leaned forward at once, reaching for the wall with her other hand. Lin Mo held the cloth steady with his injured wrist straight. The front attendant braced the cart so his weight wouldn't push it away.

Half an inch.

Her shoulder left the wall. Hair fell beside his outstretched hand. He heard her inhale and offered the crook of his left elbow, keeping his scraped palm away from her.

The cloth slid flat.

It passed beneath her hand without retaining a crease. Lin Mo pitched forward. The attendant caught his waist; his knee struck the board hard enough to darken his sight.

“Xiao Lu?”

“I hear you.”

Her voice sounded farther away. She hadn't moved back.

She withdrew her hand from the wall, then turned toward the corridor and extended one foot.

“Move the cart ahead. I'll follow.”

The woman looked at Lin Mo. He nodded. They returned the cushion to the cart; the cloth hung over his left forearm. The cart rolled only a little, stopping where Xiao Lu could see it without standing.

She reached forward with her foot.

Her shadow stayed behind.

A narrow edge of cold light stood before her, from shoe to knee. Her extending outline met it and was pressed back flat. There was no impact, no mark on the tile. The dust beside her hadn't moved, but her body occupied less space.

She tried the other foot.

The same edge waited there.

Lin Mo took the wood from his right and probed across it. The sound end passed through, knocking against the wall. Lower down, it struck tile. Whatever stopped her didn't stop the wood.

He laid it down and reached out.

She reached too.

Their hands overlapped at the light. He could see her nails and his own wounds, with nowhere to get a purchase.

Xiao Lu suddenly looked up.

“Move away from this wall. That panel's tipping.”

A narrow casing peeled from an overhead seam, its lower corner pointing toward his leg. The woman turned the handle while the front attendant guarded his knee. The cart swung a short distance aside.

The casing struck where the wheel had been, severing a thin wire.

Xiao Lu watched until they were clear, then looked down at her feet again. She still recognized danger and knew how to guide people away from it.

Lin Mo used the wood to clear the wire from the wheel. Those small, achievable things brought him no closer to touching her.

“I'm trying to move,” she said.

“I can see.”

She blinked. Moisture still shone in her eyes. His chest tightened. He raised a hand to wipe it away, then lowered it, afraid of passing through that small brightness too.

“I want to get out.”

“We're going toward the door.”

He kept the cart beside her. He wouldn't call his own half-step something they'd accomplished together.

The woman tightened her hands on the handle.

“I'm holding it. It won't roll.”

Wang Hai finally reached the corner, working his way along the wall. He saw the empty half of the cart, then Xiao Lu. His injured right wrist stayed against his chest. His other hand stopped beside a hem through which he could see the tiles.

“I'll call someone.”

He shouted toward holding. A voice answered that it was collapsing there too. Only a few people had come through; they hadn't seen anyone else who could help.

Xiao Lu listened to people calling for one another.

She wanted to tell her brother about her vote. Then she stopped. That was finished. What he couldn't hold was her, and a completed number couldn't answer for the body that still hadn't reached the door.

“Don't put my bag on your bad shoulder.”

Lin Mo looked down. Its strap dangled beside his legs. He gathered it onto the board and tucked it beneath the sound portion of the wood, clear of the wheel.

“It's right here.”

The cup shifted inside. Xiao Lu remembered the cold water she'd swallowed. Outside, she wanted to pour it away and refill it with something warm, unscrew the lid herself, and wait until she could drink without burning her mouth.

She hadn't found a place for that ordinary mouthful of water.

Following her gaze, Lin Mo touched the place where the cup rested. He didn't know what she was thinking. He gathered the bag's opening and left it between them instead of putting it behind his back.

Nothing answered from inside. The water didn't grow warm because she was looking at it.

She nodded, trying to see whether the lid was tight.

The cold light cut across her view.

Her brother's face appeared behind a closing membrane. His lips kept moving, but half a sentence vanished before reaching her.

She called him immediately.

Lin Mo heard one short “Ge.”

Then only her mouth moved.

He bent over the cart's edge, passing his left forearm through the light until his fingers found the wall behind her. The attendant reached from the other side. The same emptiness closed between their hands.

Xiao Lu's outline didn't scatter across the floor.

It narrowed inward, starting at her outstretched fingertips, withdrawing from a corridor that still held dust, blood and the sound of wheels. Lin Mo followed her face with his palm. The back of his hand scraped fresh red against the wall, but he couldn't touch her.

He kept calling her name.

She saw his mouth and tried sending her last sentence across. No sound reached him. It didn't strike the wall or make anything answer.

Then he couldn't see her anymore.

Xiao Lu still held her hand out.

There was no corridor around her, no cart. The last slit through which she could see her brother closed. She tried to turn, but her body no longer followed.

Words appeared before her.

INSUFFICIENT EXISTENCE. FORCED PRESERVATION TAKING CONTROL.

She opened her mouth to refuse. There was nowhere to send a voice, no empty box awaiting her choice. She tried curling her fingers into a fist that might knock against something.

Even that movement stopped halfway.

Another line settled into place.

CORE BACKUP: LOCKED.

No direction. No exit. The words couldn't tell her where her brother would go. They didn't promise she would ever open her eyes again.

Her lips were still moving.

She was trying to say, Take me too.
