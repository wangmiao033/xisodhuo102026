# Chapter 81: A Finger Through the Glass

The curved surface lunged forward, stopping less than an arm's length from the seat. No flesh, no arm freed from the mirror. Only glass forced outward, with a shadow behind it. A line of dust swept across his stockinged toes.

The seat jolted.

His left shoulder struck the back. White flashed through his vision. The wood nearly escaped him; he clamped his elbow to his waist rather than let his damaged wrist take the shock alone.

The reflected seat registered the change late.

The giant palm struck the middle first.

Glass slammed into the place he'd vacated. The seat held, but its thin covering split. White chips jumped onto his stockings.

Only when the small pictures shifted right did the forefinger follow.

Lin Mo tightened his grip.

As it turned, a fine rectangle lit inside the mirror, enclosing his face and shoulder. That looked like the framing Xiao Lu received. Many other versions surrounded it; this one shone clearest.

“It missed.”

“I saw the seat crack. Don't tip backward. I can't see behind you.”

“Went for where I'd been.”

He kept watching the little rectangle rather than the hand's enormous outline, trying to separate what he'd actually seen from what he desperately wanted to be true. One wrong guess could pin him to the seat.

His wrist and knee remembered that route. He wouldn't surrender it to the light again.

Keeping his feet still, he extended the wood leftward across the floor, into the bright rectangle. The fingertip turned slightly. He drew it back and lowered his head until half his face left the frame. Several conflicting pictures crowded the finger pad.

Then it pressed toward the last clear image of his shoulder.

He turned aside. The swollen glass skimmed the wood's upper half, stripping a line of grain. Fresh dust settled on his hand. He smelled raw timber, something solid being peeled by a hand that shouldn't exist.

“Following the picture. Those two times, anyway.”

“I can't see the hand. You'll have to judge.”

His yes came out as breath.

As the shadow withdrew, loose chips rolled across the floor. Something slipped beneath his right sole: a fragment of seat covering. He lowered his hips, divided his weight between left foot and wood, and slowly scraped backward where a shoe heel should have been.

He had no shoes. Stocking fibers caught the sharp chip and pulled into a thread before shifting it clear. Only when his sole touched the floor again did he unclench his teeth.

“Something underfoot. Cleared it.”

A moment later she called the station number again, louder.

He checked the control. It still rested crookedly on its original support. A narrow passage remained beside the seat. He swept loose white chips out of that route with the wood, careful not to sweep them into his next step. If he had to retreat, he couldn't afford another fall.

A black line appeared along the joint.

The wind stopped.

His eyes went to the dark line again. The panels on either side still held their separate reflections. He could see where the finger was pressing between them and where the wood lay beneath his hand. There was no room to mistake one surface for the other now.

Glass cracked around the fingertip.

The mirror didn't shatter. Through the seam between two bottom panes, one angular knuckle forced its way out, followed by the long finger still connected inside.

Its surface continued to reflect him.

Its weight was in the room.

The point scraped the wood, cutting away a thin sliver, then struck the floor with a heavy thud.

Glass splashed outward. One fragment sliced his right calf. A dark spot spread through the stocking. He drew his foot back; his knee shook with pain, but he kept the support.

Footsteps reached the backstage station. Xiao Lu called the attendant to the screen.

FREEDOM stopped at 22,106, then lost seven.

Lin Mo didn't look up to count them.

The finger through the glass lay against the groove. Slowly it bent, trying to grip the ground beneath his feet.
