# Chapter 78: The Person Before the Camera

The instruction first told him to look at the camera.

He already was. Set in a black housing beneath the glass, it was low enough to capture his face and shoulders. Beside it waited the full verification sentence, joining confirmation of his present performance appearance to agreement to remain. Only one pause allowed.

APPLICANT: REPEAT, THEN CONFIRM.

He didn't read it.

Standing had sent a fine tremor from his legs through the wood to his palm, like insects hidden in the grain. He shifted his right foot slightly outward, waited for his weight to redistribute, then spoke.

“Those are… two different things.”

The capture bar received him. The prescribed sentence didn't change to an accepted color. RESPONSE DOES NOT MATCH PRESET appeared below. It didn't say he wasn't Lin Mo, but kept his name waiting for verification.

Seatkeeper supplied the accusation.

Seatkeeper: You won't even acknowledge the person in front of you. Who are we supposed to believe is speaking?

The reflected face turned a little late. The finer jaw, sweat-damp hair against his cheek, unfamiliar habits of making sound: all clearly lit.

He looked without raising a hand to hide it.

No other creature lurked beneath his skin. He was the one hurting, the one unsteady, the one who'd followed the channel and learned where its rough edge caught. Other people had altered these places. That didn't entitle them to take away the person who remained afterward.

“This face… is me now.”

Slowly, without forcing his pitch downward. It still hurt; he didn't need another struggle against his throat.

“The second half… isn't what I mean.”

Verification remained pending. The complete recitation was unfinished, its confirmation still lit beneath both statements.

Backstage, Xiao Lu leaned forward. This camera showed his face larger, the exhaustion beneath his eyes heavier. His knuckles shifted on the wood. He hadn't suddenly become unwilling to speak. Standing here continued to cost him.

She didn't read the first half for him to hurry verification along.

Written statement, original audio, and live capture already sat together in the open correction channel. Within that same objection, she selected the bundled sentence and submitted an annotation: the applicant was denying its second half; incomplete repetition couldn't substitute for an identity finding.

No new code entry. She could identify this discrepancy within the existing correction, not command the screen to rule her way.

Only after submitting did she notice she'd been pressing both papers together. The index's stiff corner had dented a blank patch of the receipt.

She released them and laid them apart.

The hand that had once pushed the paper away didn't need to remain onscreen, guaranteeing every word today. Her past decision could recover his missing speech; her present self could identify what was wrong with the sentence before him. Both were her. Staying in this chair didn't require surrendering all her past again.

When the annotation reached Lin Mo, he saw her collaborator number, not her face.

“I marked those two statements. Still your same objection.”

He acknowledged her.

He hadn't asked for an old photograph, or for her to publish an old name and claim him as her brother. Continuous footage already ran from his entry into this session's room to now. There were objections he'd made, words he'd written, the route he'd walked. She shouldn't have to fill the remaining space with things fetched from her past.

Light cast his fingers' shadows across his sleeve. Around the old cut on his left forearm, skin pulled taut beneath its torn edge. The rubbing during his walk was only now beginning to burn. Instinctively he started to draw the sleeve up, then stopped.

Not every pain needed showing before he deserved to finish speaking.

He returned his hand where it could rest and found his breathing. Lowering his shoulders spared him forcing sound through a constricted throat. The once-unfamiliar face moved with each breath without nodding agreement to the sentence onscreen.

Seatkeeper: Let him speak for himself. Isn't that the favorite line in her old receipt?

“He is speaking,” Xiao Lu said.

She sent those words alone, then left the space to him.

He moved his gaze from the reflected mouth to the camera's black housing. He wouldn't wait for the delayed self to begin, or take his breath from the prescribed sentence.

“I'm Lin Mo.”

The verification light received it.

“I'm a man.”

Higher, still his own voice now. That fact didn't invalidate his name. He didn't need to make his present body a humiliation to hold on to it.

“My body's changed. My voice too.”

He stopped. Fine pain pricked his throat; his mouth had no water. Xiao Lu saw him swallow and kept her fingers against the console edge without urging the final line.

He waited for breath, then looked up.

“I still want out.”

Separate statements with their own pauses, rather than one continuous reading of the screen's sentence. Only afterward did he look at the glass. Its lips were still moving, as though the room could finally only follow his speech late instead of answering first.

RESPONSE DOES NOT MATCH PRESET remained.

Beside it appeared CURRENT FREE STATEMENT, preserving everything he'd just said. Xiao Lu's annotation lay between the two entries, separating failure to recite from verification of the speaker.

The composite caption that had covered the objection faded first. Records from the nearby capture camera joined the entrance's number check along one continuous line. He hadn't become another applicant who'd appeared without entering.

He watched his name finally move out of pending verification.

CURRENT SPEAKER: MATCH CONFIRMED.

The bundled sentence withdrew to one side. It could no longer serve as his confirmation that he would stay.

He closed his eyes briefly.

The light seemed colder when he opened them. He wanted to sit; every small tremor in his legs insisted. But a confirmed match didn't mean he could leave. He waited to see what happened to the line that had misrepresented him.

Seatkeeper remained.

The red name settled beside the other pictures. Selecting Continue, a brief shared smile, Helps me sit: all crowded together again.

Seatkeeper: What about these?

He studied them without asking Xiao Lu to open another original track.

The credential covered this objection. Material already retrieved didn't turn it into a key to the entire theater. He needn't deny every smile and every genuine movement to express his wish to leave.

“Sitting steady… lets me keep speaking.”

He looked at the low stool left on the stage. His voice softened.

“I smiled. Still want out.”

Xiao Lu remained smiling in the picture. His added words didn't wipe it from her face. Suddenly he was glad that moment existed. He didn't want to surrender every moment without tears just to have his wish to survive understood.

The audience's words split into several currents.

Some insisted it resembled a prepared show. Some thought continuing obliged him to bear any consequence. Others pointed out for the first time that the screen had bundled who he was with agreeing to stay. They'd just seen it happen.

A new name asked where FREEDOM was; another supplied lower left. Some selected it and withdrew. Others would wait to see whether he could change the ruling. He watched the current figure climb from the four thousands without attaching a vote to any particular comment.

EmptyCup: Mine's still there. You don't have to answer.

He didn't.

Neither anger nor forgiveness. He could barely speak now and saved his remaining breath for the next line of the result.

Xiao Lu saw he was waiting and stayed quiet too. Her cup remained in her bag. The receipt beside her had softened from its earlier crispness. She smoothed the fold without presenting it for credit.

The current correction began appearing alongside the original citation.

The excluded fragment couldn't return. The remaining continuation and brief expressions contained no statement consenting to permanent or continued stay. The applicant expressly rejected that interpretation. Failure to repeat the bundled sentence couldn't be supplied as consent.

The text arrived slowly. He read line by line, refusing to let his eyes finish at the earlier confirmed match.

Finally PERSONAL CHOICE TO REMAIN IN THE THEATER left the ruling field entirely.

THIS PRESUMPTION OF VOLUNTARY STAY REVOKED.

Below, the status of his exit statement lit again: REVIEW CONTINUES.

No release. No converting referral votes into freedom votes, no undoing previous modifications. But the route prepared to dismiss his application because he'd supposedly wanted to stay was now genuinely blocked.

He looked a long time.

Xiao Lu returned from the verification page to the current ruling and checked again. This wasn't a suggestion on her screen while his remained unchanged. In the room's live capture, the false interpretation was gone. The new status shone before him.

She'd prepared so many things to say. At the moment itself, she reached first to secure the paper on the console. A breath from the vent lifted its corner. The receipt was too light for something that had cost so much; it fell without a sound.

She didn't want to call it worth the price. What she'd surrendered hadn't returned.

Her brother was looking at an effective correction. He needn't waste another breath arguing with that error. This was true as well.

“It's changed.”

He nodded faintly, unable to produce a word immediately. The distant seat hovered at the edge of his sight, as though the room had finally allowed him to consider what his body needed after finishing this one thing.

“I want to sit.”

Her eyes grew hot. She lowered her head and held Send securely.

“Back to your seat.”

“Yes. Slowly.”

“Don't need to tell me that.”

He'd meant himself. She understood only after speaking, but didn't rush to correct it; neither did he. Both knew to go slowly. Neither had strength to divide every sentence so precisely.

He didn't turn immediately.

The dark mark from the channel remained on the wood's end. He shifted his hand along its rough grain, checking his grip wouldn't slip, then examined the route back. The real silver strip extended beneath the lights. Its reflected counterpart still lagged.

Xiao Lu warned that this camera still didn't show all the floor beneath him. He knew. A corrected record didn't make the ground level.

FREEDOM passed five thousand and reached 6,186.

Some chose when he said he wanted to sit, others before the ruling changed; people were leaving too. One refresh brought it down to 6,179. It paused, then rose two places.

He read the final 6,181 without adding the previous four thousand.

Still a long way from a hundred thousand.

At least these choices now stood beside a statement that no longer pretended he wanted to stay.

The backstage channel remained lit. Xiao Lu checked: original-audio verification and this correction result had been recorded; the credential awaited closure. No balance remained for another use. Helping her brother establish who he was hadn't provided her a seat among the voting audience.

She turned the receipt facedown on the index.

“Ge. I'm still here.”

He was slowly turning toward the way back. Her words made him pause half a beat.

“I'm listening.”

He didn't search the glass for Xiao Lu. It had never shown her sitting where she was now. The voice came from backstage, from the position she'd chosen for herself. Tightening his grip, he probed the actual floor first.

Behind him, the ruling remained lit.

THIS PRESUMPTION OF VOLUNTARY STAY REVOKED.

This time, no other line immediately covered it.
