# Chapter 66: The One Who Said Wait

Lin Mo didn't ask whether she'd meant him.

He shifted his right foot and turned the wood's broken end away from his stocking. The hospital room remained in his head, his sister nodding. The harder he tried to see her, the more Xiao Lu seemed to occupy the wrong place.

He looked at the model egg instead.

“You won't look at me now?”

“When I do, I try to put you in there. I need a moment.”

She rested her hands on her knees.

“Then listen.”

He agreed. Rather than continuing about calling someone back, she tilted her face away from the overhead glare. The shadow beside her nose changed sides.

“I want to sit somewhere else. Too bright.”

He indicated the scenic panel's edge, still within the shot, where the board cut off a strip of light. Measuring room for her chair with the wood, he found he'd left too little. Her knees would hit the panel.

“Farther out. Don't squeeze in where I pointed.”

She dragged the wooden chair over, placing its front feet before settling the back. Her bag followed from behind it; she lifted it to her side. He left the red chair alone and didn't move opposite her.

“No. Convenient for you. Bring one, and every bed she remembers has to pass through yours first.”

So they'd watch two people sitting?

“Yes. The most expensive thing in this show is already installed in my head. No plans to add furniture.”

Xiao Lu laughed, then pressed her mouth.

“What?”

“I want to laugh. Afraid I'll lose what just came back.”

“Laugh slowly.”

“How?”

“Never tried.”

The laugh escaped like a breath gone astray. Still frowning afterward, she looked at the noodle model, then away.

“The lid. I remembered it wouldn't fasten. Now I know why I kept touching it. I was afraid he'd lift it, see half gone, and claim he'd already eaten.”

Lin Mo looked up.

“However little I saved, he had to eat it. I fastened it toward me, hiding the raised edge so he wouldn't see straightaway.”

Her thumb moved slowly across her knee. He vaguely remembered slipping his nail beneath the lid on his sister's side. A possible reason for that movement emerged. He didn't offer it yet, afraid they'd hurry to smooth another join.

“It wasn't all for him. If he said he'd eaten, I'd have to finish it. Cold noodles. I didn't want them.”

“Thoroughly considered.”

“What are you laughing at?”

“Him thinking he'd got the better deal.”

Only when she looked did he realize he'd smiled. He let it subside without denying it.

Her fingers stilled, as though caught by his mention of that man.

“When he came back, he wouldn't sit.”

“Go on.”

“Stood saying he wasn't tired. But his hand kept leaning there. I saw. Made me angry. Everything was fine. Asked about money: enough. What's enough in numbers? Enough meant I couldn't ask again.”

Lin Mo pressed his lips together. He'd said enough many times, often followed by I'll take care of it. He'd really scraped money together, then kept quiet, fearing an explanation would leave her hearing only what she owed.

“Maybe he thought saying it wasn't enough would stop you eating.”

“I was already thinking about it. When people won't tell me, I have to imagine something worse.”

He neither claimed nor evaded a place among those people.

“I've done that.”

“You still do.”

The speed of her answer startled her too. The pause stretched. Neither produced the medicine foil to demand recognition from a spontaneous phrase.

She found her bag and took out the script. He looked away. She didn't write, just held the cover before closing it.

“I can't find the date.”

“Leave it for now.”

“I'm not asking permission. There are other things in there. The date's blank. They aren't.”

He gave up arranging her priorities. Anger, hunger, reluctance to admit she'd saved unpleasant noodles: nothing had returned in the order he'd once written down.

FREEDOM fell to eighty-nine, slowly passed a hundred. She didn't look. He didn't announce it.

Were they about to recognize each other? Comments ordered her to say the word. Xiao Lu glanced toward the camera, apparently realizing how much reluctance she'd revealed to people waiting for her to agree.

Xiao Lu drew her script closer from her knees without thanking him on cue. Could Lin Mo move the egg farther away?

“I want to laugh every time I see it. Infuriating.”

He pushed with the glued chopsticks. Their joined end turned in his palm; instead of gripping, they spun the solid model halfway around. The egg faced her again.

She stared, laughing back the moisture gathering in her eyes.

“Stop helping.”

He laid down the chopsticks. She stood and flipped the model over, flat plastic base toward the camera. No egg watching anyone now. Returning to the dimmer light, she caught her breath and continued.

She remembered a bedsheet seam under her knee. The thread didn't prick, but she kept wanting to pick it. After someone left, she'd bent over. While they talked, she'd held herself upright. Once the doorway fell silent, the tension left her belly and she wanted to collapse sideways.

“When you left, I still wanted to yell at you.”

His hand stopped.

She paused too, without changing you back into an unidentified person. She looked up, no pleasure at having supplied a correct answer.

“Saying that, I know who I'm talking to. The face is still confused.”

“I'm listening.”

“I wanted you to stop minding how much I ate and finish your own food. But you said you'd go downstairs a while, and left. Angry and relieved. I didn't want to keep pretending I felt better.”

Pressure gathered in his chest. The unregretful sister hadn't vanished from his memory. Hearing about pretending, he first wanted to tell Xiao Lu not to dredge up her pain. He pressed his tongue against his teeth and swallowed the advice.

She watched his mouth.

“Going to say it's all right again?”

“Yes. Stopped myself.”

“No reward this time.”

“I know. Not enough paper.”

She looked down, bending the cover slightly. After a while she returned the script to her bag, freeing both hands.

“When the paper came, I wanted to ask you first. Then I thought you'd only say you'd take care of it.”

He didn't ask which paper. She looked into empty space, fingers pressed tightly against herself, seemingly not following a distant playback.

“I was so angry. Lying there, and I couldn't even make you say you didn't know. Then they explained what would happen after I paid. That frightened me.”

Her breath grew ragged. He reached to find her water, bent his right wrist inward and froze at the pain.

“My cup's in my bag.”

He withdrew. She fetched it, unscrewed the lid and drank. The rim stayed at her lips a long time.

“I didn't want to die.”

He raised his head.

“When I heard zero, I thought, Why take everything? I wanted to push the paper back. Afraid they'd stop explaining if I did. I hadn't finished asking.”

He'd seen her pull back the paper and question the clauses. None of it was a newly revealed secret. But he'd never heard how two kinds of fear fought for her hand.

“I remember what I asked. Some you've told me. Some were spoken in old sessions. What I'm telling you now came before the questions. I resented them not even letting me think.”

She held the cup lid against her knee, seeking a place to steady her trembling hand rather than miming the signature.

“They read your name. That made me angry too. So quickly, as though getting it right finished everything. I was afraid they'd miss a character, then say I'd paid to protect someone else. I made them read it again. I wasn't calm. I was too frightened to know what else to hold on to.”

His throat tightened. The recording he'd watched finally had a voice from beyond its frame. No new signature to compare, no unalterable original in his hands. Only Xiao Lu explaining how she'd delayed while afraid.

“You weren't beside me.”

In the implanted hospital memory, he still bent over the paper's corner. He could see that hand, and his present right wrist hanging beside the table.

“I know what they just added is false.”

“I'm not telling it to correct you. I really wanted you there. Then I decided not to wait. Afraid if you came, I couldn't sign.”

She set the cup between her knees, shoulders drawn tight.

Anger flared in Lin Mo, briefly stronger than the memory's easy relief. How could she trade away her entire name? Knowing she'd been frightened made him want to ask even more fiercely why she hadn't thrown the paper away.

“I still want to yell about this.”

“I can tell.”

“I can't hear it and say, Thank you. You did the right thing.”

Her eyes were fiercely red.

“I didn't want that either.”

He let out the breath he'd held. Her shoulders dropped slightly, then tightened again. Her fingers felt along the hand holding the cup.

“That last wait. I know now what I was waiting for.”

He sat utterly still.

“Not for him to finish. I could see through my hand. Suddenly I really wanted to undo it. Take back what I'd just handed over. All those questions, and when it happened to me, I was still afraid. I called out. Nobody waited.”

Her cup tapped the wooden chair. Water shifted inside without spilling.

Lin Mo remembered Old Chen's hand growing lighter in his own. He kept the thought in his hands, rather than using someone else's final moment to speak for her.

“I heard you say wait. Now I've heard why.”

Her tears finally fell. She neither sought the camera nor hid her face. First she fastened the lid, needing two attempts to catch the thread.

“That was me.”

He looked up.

“The one who signed. The one who wanted to take it back. I'm not guessing for her. I remember the cup becoming harder to hold. I remember being afraid.”

She was silent a long time. FREEDOM stood at a hundred and twenty-seven. Viewers still argued: a new performance, some insisted. Others demanded removal of the no-regrets proposal. The executed entry remained.

Something had finally come within Lin Mo's reach. He didn't snatch it for her. The person before him was bringing herself out of a past he'd watched and she'd lost. She hadn't yet reached his side.

Xiao Lu wiped her chin and returned her cup to the bag. Her gaze passed over his present features, paused at his tight mouth, then took in his whole face again.

“I don't want to watch that signing again. Leave the light here. I want to look at you.”
