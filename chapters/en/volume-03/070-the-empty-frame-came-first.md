# Chapter 70: The Empty Frame Came First

Seatkeeper didn't answer to the name Lin Mo had spoken.

New voice. Still knows how to say thank you.

Lin Mo swallowed against the lodged pain. He hadn't thanked anyone for the change, yet almost searched his last few breaths for something he'd inadvertently said.

Xiao Lu waited.

“I didn't.”

Quiet, clear. He loosened his left fingers without releasing the wood.

“You changed my throat. I still said… I want out.”

Using it meant liking it, someone replied. He'd even laughed. Xiao Lu looked up from later comments.

“I laughed because he used the word I missed as evidence. Didn't hear it all? Ask. Don't invent another reason for my laugh.”

The red name caught his eye again. Those same letters had remained on the cabinet's screen above the order for physical severance. He didn't know who sat behind them. He remembered a selection never withdrawn, until his palm could hold nothing.

“You selected Old Chen too.”

Seatkeeper: Don't invite departed performers back onstage.

He leaned forward. Knee pain stopped him. Suddenly he wanted every word out at once, fast enough to catch the vanished hand.

“He didn't leave. He wanted to come out.”

He had to pause. Xiao Lu didn't fill the gap with Old Chen's last moments. She turned her chair slightly toward him, feet still on the floor.

Who was Old Chen? Could they replay the story? Others urged her to speak: she'd only just found this brother, and already he couldn't talk.

Lin Mo shook his head.

“He stayed so I could see the hundred-thousand condition. I came out. He didn't.”

“I can tell you what I did. What he said at the end… isn't payment for your attention.”

The count rose from twelve hundred and something past fifteen hundred, then fell. He watched it fall without knowing whether any of those commenters had left. No single lost vote told him who had heard what he'd said. Nor would he offer Old Chen's belongings to recover the loss.

Could Xiao Lu answer these while he rested? He nodded, indicating only the current screen. Reading upward, she passed over invented last words for Old Chen.

“You never heard him say that. I didn't say it either. You wrote it.”

can vote freedom but can't praise how we like?

“Get praise wrong, and I can say so.”

She answered someone genuinely asking where to vote: lower left, separate from ordinary modification proposals. Accused of merely collecting votes for her brother, she raised an eyebrow.

“He's this session's applicant. I haven't opened mine.”

Lin Mo set no date for her. Leaning on the wood, he slowly changed leg position, freeing his right knee from the stool's edge. She moved her chair feet outward to give him room.

EmptyCup: I haven't withdrawn FREEDOM. No thank you, though? Wasted vote?

She looked toward him.

He chose the shortest answer.

“Expensive way… to buy thanks.”

Question marks followed. Xiao Lu didn't understand either until he pointed to COGNITIVE OVERWRITE: 54%.

“You made me pay.”

Her mouth twitched. Someone said he'd pay even without that viewer's vote. Others cursed the voice proposal and warned against lumping everyone together. Comments jostled upward. The red words stayed.

Seatkeeper: So reluctant to lose this session. I'll keep it for you.

He lowered his pointing hand.

Wheels rattled across a joint beside the platform. The attendant in gray elbow guards returned with another worker. No dining-set cart this time. Between them stood a black frame, narrow red strips along its edges. Through its empty center, the gray wall remained visible.

No mirror or picture. Two heavy bases rested against the wheel assemblies. An outward-projecting corner entered Lin Mo's view as it approached his stool.

He braced on the wood, trying to move his feet.

“First…”

Breath caught. Xiao Lu touched her bag, thinking he needed water. He shook his head and pointed to the wheels behind her.

She turned, palm raised.

“Stop. How close is the base?”

The attendant stopped, crouched, checked. Not yet at the stool. No collision. The path to End remained clear. Watching the corner, Lin Mo slowly drew both stockinged feet back.

“Not… in the middle.”

“Which side?”

She started moving away from the controls before he'd finished pointing.

“Let me finish.”

She stopped, irritated. After catching his breath, he touched the wood's end to the space beside and ahead of him, then indicated the rear wall. The frame should stand behind them, visible to both, its base clear of their route to the panel.

This time she followed the whole gesture.

“That enormous sweep. Thought you wanted me back in the waiting area.”

A brief laugh escaped in his new voice.

“You move faster… than I talk.”

“I know. Next time, point first. Save me a trip.”

Checking the position diagram on the controls, the attendant said the frame could go there. Keep its front clear. Lin Mo indicated only the floor he'd chosen, without agreeing to place either of them inside it.

“Not in use yet,” the man said. “Preparing for next round.”

He directed his colleague behind them. Xiao Lu lifted her bag, preparing to move her chair too. Lin Mo first rose on the wood, slowly straightening his knees.

“I'll clear the stool.”

She waited until he was steady before lifting one end of the light stool toward the panel.

“Not beside his foot,” Wang Hai warned. “Stocking there.”

She paused with the stool still lifted. Lin Mo looked down: his right foot had sought its old position, toes too close. He stepped securely sideways, brought the wood along and stopped swaying before letting her set the stool down.

Wang Hai stayed seated by the wall, outside the line. Lin Mo nodded to him without forcing thanks through unsettled breath.

The stool now stood beside the controls. Sitting slowly, Lin Mo tested his right palm against the panel's edge. He could touch it without those two steps. Xiao Lu's chair moved to the other side, back clear of the screen, bag at her feet.

At the rear wall, the frame's wheels locked. The second worker removed its protective corner cloth, folded it under one arm and withdrew with the attendant. The empty frame stood behind them, visible through the gap between their chairs. Neither person occupied its center.

moving a stool won't help

He touched the fixed panel's edge.

“Helps me sit.”

Xiao Lu smiled briefly before looking back at the red name. He kept his palm against the side, clear of End. His wrist hurt. He withdrew it.

A narrow strip of light appeared along the empty frame.

A pending item appeared atop the control screen. Lin Mo expanded it before reading more comments. Submitted by Seatkeeper. Scope: this session's next round. A gray field beneath still lacked content.

SPECIAL ROUND: FORCED VOTING. NEXT ROUND AWAITING START.

Xiao Lu didn't read it as already active. He asked for the full wording, then checked End.

CURRENT BROADCAST CAN BE ENDED.

The same page still warned that ending would invalidate this session's FREEDOM count. The cleared path remained open; the waiting-side white line hadn't moved. No compulsory countdown had been issued to viewers. No new choices listed.

He scrolled. Awaiting start. No ballot content.

“Voting on what?”

Seatkeeper: Keep asking whether they want to watch or let you go, don't you? This time they'll answer properly.

NoTearjerkers: I'm not answering your questions.

EmptyCup: I already voted FREEDOM.

leave now, does it count as opposition?

start already. who wants to watch two people move stools?

At the question about leaving, Xiao Lu paused and pointed it out. Neither could determine from the empty pending field how withdrawal would count next round.

“All we can see is awaiting start. I don't know the answer to that either.”

Ordinary departures still appeared possible. FREEDOM lost a few, then climbed; attendance changed too. Watching, he promised none of those current options for the coming round.

His right fingers tightened beside the stool at the memory of Seatkeeper's keep.

The red name knew how to make him hesitate. The button worked. The route wasn't blocked. But ending now would lose the votes they'd gained. The siblings who'd just found each other would remain in the theater.

“Ge,” Xiao Lu asked quietly, “will you wait for it to start?”

His mouth was too dry to answer. This time he pointed to her bag and clearly said cup. She unscrewed the lid and offered it. He supported the weight with his palm heels, took one small sip and returned it without repeated swallowing.

“Stop the wheels before. Water now. Finally two different gestures.”

He watched her replace the lid. His throat eased a little.

“Keeping score?”

“Yes. Settle it when you can talk properly.”

The cup went back. He didn't rush to prove he could speak. He let his breath out slowly, waited until she looked at him again.

“Wait for them… to show the content.”

He indicated End, still lit.

“See it first. I haven't agreed to do it.”

Her fingers rested on the chair back. After a pause she said, “I want to watch a little longer. But I've just remembered wanting to take back my signature. Don't say I've agreed later because I'm sitting here now.”

He looked at the empty frame. The false hand smoothing her agreement flashed through his mind. He left it there, touching only the stool's edge with his real right hand.

“If you want to leave… I'll want you to stay.”

She looked back.

“But tell me. My guesses… aren't good.”

“That one's accurate.”

Her fingers loosened. She stayed where she'd chosen.

“Don't watch only him.”

She directed him to ordinary proposals, still open, still able to supply a more familiar danger. He nodded. He'd almost forgotten their division of work again.

He watched the control page while she read the moving comments. Her chair stayed angled, no return to a camera-pleasing pose. She could see his hand while reading.

Some had heard a red name was here. Others had just watched the reunion and didn't want to become evidence. Requests for another Ge vanished beneath new questions.

FREEDOM reached two thousand four hundred and six.

“Red name. Look here.”

He indicated the number without a victorious smile.

“They haven't heard your question. Don't count them… as people you kept.”

The red name flashed and returned to its place.

Seatkeeper: Once they see the question, let's see how many like you choosing for yourselves.

Xiao Lu turned her chair a little farther toward the screen. Her bag was closed. She didn't reach for the script.

“Who reads this time?”

“You. Slowly.”

“Slow enough for you to interrupt?”

He nodded. She agreed and waited for a first line that hadn't appeared.

The empty frame's inner red border lit inch by inch. Its center remained blank. End Broadcast still shone on the panel.

Only the notice waited before them.

FORCED VOTING: NEXT ROUND AWAITING START.
