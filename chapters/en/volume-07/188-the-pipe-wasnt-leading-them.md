# Chapter 188: The Pipe Wasn't Leading Them

Water reached the bench's front foot first.

Xiao Lu drew her shoes back and knocked a heel against the wood. He Cen opened his eyes, already bracing to stand. Yu Mei told him to stay seated and inspected the front doorway.

Lin Mo watched the old pipe bend at the corner. Beneath it was a black gap, partly hidden by the fallen seat frame. From his lower position, he could see a little farther underneath its back.

“Does the wall turn inward there?”

Fang Ji shifted without touching the frame. “A narrow opening. The pipe goes through.”

“Check the ground before following it.”

Lin Mo stopped. He sounded as though he'd been inside. Fang Ji merely pointed to a dry brick beside the door: that was what he meant to examine.

Yu Mei adjusted the wheelchair lamp, keeping its clamp clear of Lin Mo's arm. The light slanted through the doorway without turning water into solid flooring. Fang Ji brushed dust away with his shoe. A line of brick connected with the room. The opening lay beside the fallen frame, not underneath it.

He withdrew to let Yu Mei look. She turned sideways without pressing her shoulder against the iron.

“Solid ground. I can only see a few steps.”

Back inside, she compared the chair's width with the opening. People could turn sideways; wheels couldn't. Lin Mo neither offered to stand nor asked her to leave the chair behind.

“Wide enough?”

“The entrance. I can't see the turn.”

She rested. The gap remained, but water kept approaching the bench. He Cen refused an early move to the dry rear doorway.

“I can still get up from here. Don't help me stand just to leave me standing. Call me when you know.”

Yu Mei agreed. Fang Ji would inspect only as far as they could still exchange words, then return. She would watch his footing and the chair. Nobody would disappear along the pipe.

He tucked his injured hand against his chest and slipped through. Shoes scraped brick, followed by a quiet curse.

“What happened?”

“Broken bracket ahead. Didn't step on it.”

Unable to crouch comfortably, he bent from another angle. Eventually he found room around it. The pipe turned upward; the passage remained level. He returned with news of a junction, old wheel tracks to the right, and part of a maintenance label. Water concealed the left-hand edge.

Lin Mo smiled, pulling at the sore inside his mouth.

“It went upstairs.”

“What did?” Xiao Lu asked.

“The pipe. It wasn't taking us somewhere to drink.”

He Cen nearly laughed, then coughed. Nobody became comfortable. At least they didn't have to invent a waiting tank at the far end.

Yu Mei inspected the junction next while Fang Ji stayed within sight of the entrance. Lin Mo heard their short exchanges. She returned having recognized the back of the maintenance-lane sign near the receiving hall. Seat shapes were visible beyond.

“A low beam. The chair should fit. Whoever pushes has to bend.”

She didn't release the brakes on the strength of should. First they compared the chair's back with the beam from the side. Xiao Lu stood, swayed, and steadied herself before asking what she could do.

“Watch my sleeve there,” Yu Mei said. “From somewhere steady. Don't chase me.”

Fang Ji repacked the bag, notebook and cloth above the empty bottles, torn seam turned upward. He asked whether it could rest briefly on Lin Mo's lap.

“It would hurt my leg.”

Fang Ji withdrew it without arguing that it weighed almost nothing. He would carry it, lowering it at each dry stopping place.

They moved the others through first. He Cen followed the real wall and waited while Fang Ji indicated the bricks beside the broken bracket. The seatkeeper dragged his injured leg behind him. Xiao Lu waited near the junction, cradling her scraped palm rather than carrying someone else's bag.

Then came the wheelchair.

Its left rear wheel struck a lump of plaster. A scraping sound, a tilt toward the water. Lin Mo's left hand rose toward the pipe, then stopped.

Wet rust covered it. A crooked bracket held it up.

“Stop.”

Yu Mei already had. She backed slightly and straightened the chair. The obstruction was loose plaster, not masonry. She nudged it clear with her shoe.

Lin Mo returned his empty hand to the armrest, heart racing. He couldn't explain noticing the bracket before he touched it. No old scene returned. Only the habit of checking a connection before putting weight on it.

“What were you looking at?”

“It isn't fixed securely.”

Yu Mei glanced at the bracket. “Good thing you didn't grab it.”

Footrests went first, then wheels, without taking him out of the chair. The wall muffled the water. A sliver of the wet bench remained visible before the doorway cut that off too.

At the junction they rested. Fang Ji checked a low, dry base against the wall before He Cen sat. Xiao Lu felt grit inside her shoe. Seated at the other end, she shook out a dry flake, found no new wound, and slowly fastened the shoe again. Lin Mo didn't finish it for her.

“You watch as if I'll come apart.”

He looked away, then back. “I don't know how not to.”

“Then don't only look at my hands.”

He looked at her face: shadows beneath the eyes, dry lips, a little impatience. She waited for his answer, lightly tapping the low base with her shoe.

He couldn't remember whether she'd looked at him that way before. He remembered this time.

“All right.”

“All right,” said a voice from the maintenance passage.

It resembled his, with a slightly longer ending. Fang Ji turned across the junction rather than running toward it. He asked who was there. After a pause, it repeated the answer.

Xiao Lu looked at Lin Mo. His mouth hadn't moved.

“Don't ask again,” he said.

Fang Ji fell silent, frowning. Something had used a recent reply. That didn't tell them where it had been captured or where it would travel next. Lin Mo saw no speaker on the pipe and didn't order it dismantled to support a guess.

The seatkeeper suggested a sound left in the wall. Before he finished, another voice said, “You can go now.”

Nobody there had just said that.

He Cen adjusted his glasses. “Are we going or not?”

He'd half risen and sat again, angry now. Lin Mo asked Yu Mei to inspect the passage instead of waiting for permission from it. Both beam supports were visible. Floor joints continued between them, with room to turn on the right. The words hadn't created new clearance. Their silence hadn't made the posts fall either.

“Tell the people behind before moving,” she said. “Don't follow a voice from there because you think it's mine.”

“It can use yours too,” Xiao Lu said.

Yu Mei nodded and held her less painful arm where Xiao Lu could see it. They needed to watch the actual person as well as listen.

That wasn't a guarantee. A wall or another body could block the hand; pain could make someone miss a movement. Lin Mo asked Fang Ji to stop where both ends remained visible, instead of using voices like ropes to pull everyone around a blind turn.

Fang Ji looked at his injured shoulder. “Then I have to stand there a while. Let me rest first.”

They waited. The voice resembling Lin Mo's said all right again. Nobody answered.

Xiao Lu flicked a grain of plaster from the seat with her uninjured hand.

“Does hearing your voice over there make you want to take it back?”

“Yes. But I don't even know where it is.”

She planted her feet. “Then let's get this one across first.”

Fang Ji stood and took his visible position. He didn't prove his name to the dark. Those behind didn't use a question and answer to certify who he was.

The low beam was harder than expected. Lin Mo fit underneath, but bending to push strained Yu Mei's shoulder. She stopped beneath it, braked the chair, and stepped back. Xiao Lu noticed the torn sleeve knot protruding. Yu Mei tucked it closer and tried another stance.

Fang Ji offered to take the handles briefly. She refused because pushing here meant controlling the chair's forward weight; his injured palm couldn't do that. They divided the movement instead: pause before the beam, pause underneath, pause beyond. Xiao Lu watched the sleeve from the wall, not from behind the chair. The seatkeeper waited until the others cleared it before following.

Listening to their breathing, Lin Mo wished he could make himself lighter. He kept the wish to himself. When his knee began pulling outward, he told Yu Mei promptly rather than waiting until the move was finished.

Beyond the beam, she lowered herself against the wall. Her hand stayed on the brake lever until Fang Ji confirmed the wheels were on the level section. Only then did she let go. He brought the bag through separately, stopping twice, its base almost brushing the brick.

He Cen asked how much farther. Yu Mei pointed to the brighter end instead of giving a number. He looked, tightened his mouth, and rose without pretending the sight had restored his strength.

Xiao Lu waited for him. When he lost his grip on a rough patch of wall, she didn't catch his whole weight with her injured hand. She told him where a smoother section began. He moved his hand there and found his balance himself. Lin Mo watched the small change save them from a larger fall.

At the passage's end, parallel wheel tracks led beneath the sign.

MAINTENANCE LANE.

Dust clung around the old letters. No new arrow waited below. They'd approached from the side rear, not returned along the two bends of the lower ramp or circled back to the load-bearing frame.

Fang Ji stopped behind the sign and looked into the hall.

Between two bodies Lin Mo saw empty seats. White light slid along a distant armrest, drawing a long brightness across the water nearby. Nobody approached. The beam hadn't reached the chair, but his toes drew inward already.

Yu Mei parked in the solid wall's shadow and switched off the clamp lamp.

“We came from that side.” She indicated a darker narrow corridor across the hall. “We can't just push straight through now.”

He didn't ask why. A corner of old canvas still hung beyond a seat the light had passed, torn along its edge. The ground beneath remained darker than its surroundings. Uncrossed space lay between that patch and the corridor.

He Cen sat against the wall. The seatkeeper kept his bad leg dry. Fang Ji lowered the bag between them without letting a bottle roll into the light. Xiao Lu stood ahead and to Lin Mo's left, looking back to make sure Yu Mei had withdrawn behind the wall too.

He could remember the low beam and crooked bracket. The hall's older route still had to be assembled from other people's words.

White light moved slowly across the nearest empty seat. Nobody occupied it, yet the cushion sank a little.

Lin Mo watched without asking anyone to try sitting down.
