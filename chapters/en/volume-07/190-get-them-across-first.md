# Chapter 190: Get Them Across First

The person outside crouched.

Lin Mo saw a gloved hand, then a flashlight close to the floor. Its beam stopped at the iron strip instead of sweeping into the passage. Reflective bands marked dark work clothes; the man's bent posture concealed the lettering on his chest.

“Don't grab my hand. Where are the wheels stuck?”

“They haven't reached the edge.”

The man looked up, saw the chair, and told someone behind him there were six people inside. He spoke toward another part of the outside room, unlike the voice that always clung to their replies. That alone wasn't safety. Lin Mo asked him to turn the light toward his own feet so Yu Mei could see the landing.

The flashlight moved.

What the gray-white surface revealed changed with their viewing angles. Lin Mo could see part of a room. Fang Ji still described grayness; Yu Mei saw only the nearest shoes and hands clearly. They didn't pretend all six shared a view.

The man stayed visible instead of reaching into the gray.

“Zheng. Rescue crew. Don't move yet. There's room to receive you.”

He called a colleague. Outside, they checked the floor and shifted a short piece of warning tape beside the ordinary iron door without removing the farther barriers. They placed a short rigid transition board on supported ground, not across an unsupported void.

Scuff marks covered it. Somebody had brought it from nearby and fitted it to the actual floor. Lin Mo asked Yu Mei to wait while Fang Ji described the chipped inner brick. She relayed his explanation.

Zheng didn't tell them to rush. Those able to walk would cross first and move aside, leaving room for the wheelchair.

He Cen lifted his face. Yu Mei asked whether he could reach the strip. He said yes, then stood against the wall for a long time before moving.

“You first,” Xiao Lu said, making space.

At the boundary, Zheng supported only the forearm He Cen extended outside. He didn't enter the gray. Beyond the strip, the colleague helped He Cen sit. Someone said he was feverish and needed medical attention.

Yu Mei's expression loosened. Not recovery, but someone taking over a problem she'd watched without medicine to address it.

Fang Ji went next, leaving the bag on dry ground beside Lin Mo. He identified his injured palm and shoulder so the hands outside wouldn't grasp the wrong places. A folding chair received him. Under ordinary lighting his lips looked very pale.

The seatkeeper didn't follow. His left leg trembled. He asked whether everyone could cross. Nobody guaranteed the mechanism. Zheng asked about his injury and whether he could lift his foot, unaware of his old seat name.

The man looked at Lin Mo as if awaiting absolution.

“Let them see your leg first. The other things remain.”

He approached with his head down. Neither the gray layer nor the rescuers delivered a verdict. With his footing visible from both sides, he slowly crossed. His leg came through. An old red blur flashed beside his shoulder, then became indistinct as he moved toward the wall.

Lin Mo noted that without saying it had vanished.

Xiao Lu stood beside the chair. “Should I go first too?”

“There's somewhere to stand outside.”

“I'm asking you.”

He looked at her. This wasn't a question about the route. She'd just escaped another kind of waiting. Now she had to leave this small space they occupied together before he did.

“Go. I'll be behind you.”

A few steps, not an unknowable future. She didn't take his injured hand. At the boundary she explained her scraped palm and borrowed support on the other side. Her shoe paused above the strip, then landed on cement.

She turned and remained visible.

No metal arm repeated her words.

The chair moved last. Yu Mei released the brakes and controlled the handles. Zheng and his colleague supported the front frame low down, not Lin Mo's legs or fixed wrist. Beside the approaching wheels, darkness remained under the chipped brick. They kept clear of it.

The board received one small wheel, then the other. A jolt hurt his knee enough to close his eyes. When he opened them, the reflective stripe was still on Zheng's sleeve.

“Slow with the big wheels.”

Yu Mei paused for everyone to check their positions, then pushed. Once the rear wheels crossed, the crew took control and braked the chair. Asked whether he felt dizzier, Lin Mo said yes. Then he said he was thirsty.

“Medical staff are coming. Don't gulp anything yet.”

Nobody withheld water until he produced a complete history.

Yu Mei crossed last. On ordinary cement, her hands retained the shape of a grip for several moments. Asked whether anyone remained inside, she said all six of their group were out. She couldn't account for the deeper area.

The passage remained behind the shallow surface. Its sounds receded. No one switched off the Theater or showed Lin Mo proof that the continuing support had ended.

More footsteps filled the room.

Old Zhou approached from the ordinary doorway and called his name. Lin Mo's unfamiliar response changed his expression. He began to ask, but a medic needed room. Lin Mo said he knew the name without remembering their shared experiences.

“All right,” Zhou said. “After they check you.”

He didn't grasp Lin Mo's shoulder.

Zhou told the crew he'd reported the disappearance, as had the transport service. People had inspected the room. The pale surface stayed indistinct; calls received no answer. Just now, a small noise and a lower opening had brought those nearby back to it.

“We couldn't see you all along.”

Lin Mo didn't imagine them sitting endlessly in place, or ask why they hadn't entered sooner. While unseen, he hadn't been reachable through a known safe passage. Neither Zhou nor Lin Mo could explain why the gray had thinned now.

Zheng asked for the position to be recorded and the outer area kept closed. Rescuing six people didn't make it a place for spectators to test. The crew dealt with those they could presently see and reach. Sounds deeper inside required separate assessment, not injured survivors volunteering to guide another entry.

Xiao Lu stood on outside cement. She didn't ask Lin Mo to turn back, or declare that the part still inside no longer mattered. Continuing support remained unfinished. Crossing a doorway couldn't settle it.

His phone stayed in his pocket. Nobody needed its screen to prove his return. Fang Ji indicated the bag and asked someone to bring it rather than leave it beside the opening. It was set on dry ground near the chair. The notebook remained wrapped; the private fold wasn't opened for everyone during the handover.

A medic asked his name, injuries, and whether he'd lost consciousness. He said Lin Mo, described fainting inside and the absence of older memories after waking. Age and biographical facts remained answerable; that didn't make his memory normal. The medic recorded it and continued examining the support and knee, rather than ending the assessment at his name.

Near He Cen, someone asked how long the fever had lasted. Yu Mei gave what she'd observed without inventing exact hours. Fang Ji's dressing needed attention. The seatkeeper's leg was examined separately. Xiao Lu answered her own questions, repeating her name more clearly when it came out softly.

Lin Mo watched without finishing for her.

They didn't race up the narrow stairs carrying the occupied wheelchair. Crews arranged transport equipment and moved patients separately, handling the chair apart. During Lin Mo's transfer, the leg support had to be repositioned. He called for a stop when something pulled. The people holding him actually stopped, waited for him to explain, and adjusted before continuing.

Outside, night air carried the warm smell of traffic.

Someone raised a phone beyond the warning line. A worker asked them to move. Lin Mo didn't recognize every face, but a camera didn't mean the whole city was voting. The nearest spectator lowered the device and took two steps back, leaving space for a stretcher.

Xiao Lu waited with another worker, a newly supplied thin blanket around her. She showed Lin Mo a water cup in her uninjured hand. Someone had told her to drink slowly. She'd had a little, not water conjured from their empty bottles.

Later, after a medic checked him, Lin Mo received a few small mouthfuls. Water stung the sore inside his cheek. He swallowed and couldn't immediately speak, eyes fixed on the cup's rim.

Yu Mei sat nearby with her eyes shut. For once she wasn't opening them every few breaths to check He Cen. Someone else was doing that. Her shoulder still hurt, but her hands no longer held everybody's next move.

A belongings record needed checking. Lin Mo could name the notebook and photograph without explaining every old page. Fang Ji added which items he'd personally returned to the bag; the rest he didn't know. The recorder didn't assign the copied sheets a definite author to make the form tidy.

Lin Mo asked them not to loop the handle around his right-side support. They changed its position, leaving the bag nearby where he could see it. He watched the old cloth before looking away. Fear of losing the papers didn't mean reading them could immediately restore him. Emergency-room lights couldn't illuminate that absence.

At the hospital, the six were separated for assessment. Curtains and waiting stood between Lin Mo and Xiao Lu. He didn't know everyone else's results, only heard names called, trolleys, questions about people they could contact.

When Xiao Lu sat beside his bed again, his voice no longer sounded dry enough to scrape. His wrist remained fixed and his knee hurt. The doctor hadn't approved another dangerous excursion tomorrow. He acknowledged the advice rather than deleting it from his plans.

He asked whether the others knew where his bed was. The nurse said their locations were being coordinated, not that everyone had been cleared to leave. He could wait here without sending an injured friend down the corridor to search. For once, waiting did not require someone to hold a dangerous door.

Xiao Lu squeezed her empty paper cup and released it, leaving a shallow crease.

“This one's really empty now.”

Lin Mo remembered Fang Ji removing the empty bottle from beside the wall. Something from today, not evidence retrieved from broken years.

“We can ask someone for more in a while.”

She smiled, then looked at his supported wrist without touching it.

“Brother.”

He raised his eyes. The address didn't restore its old warmth. It was still this person calling him now. He answered so she would know he'd heard.

She set the cup on the bedside cabinet. Its base tilted as she let go. He asked her to straighten it, and she did. Outside the curtain, a trolley's wheels sounded twice and continued away.
