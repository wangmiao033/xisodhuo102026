# Chapter 182: The Name on the Notebook

Lin Mo relaxed as the wheelchair entered the little room, thinking they had arrived. But a front caster still rested on the shallow seam at the entrance. Yu Mei pushed a little farther. The jolt caught his right knee, his heel struck the footplate, and the breath he'd released lodged in his throat.

“A little more,” she said.

This time, he waited for the wheels to stop and the brakes to engage before releasing the armrest. Pressure marks crossed his left fingertips. The injured index finger looked darker. Instead of showing anyone, he checked that his clothes were clear of the wheel.

Xiao Lu stopped in the doorway behind them. He tried to move aside. The chair stayed still. He'd forgotten the brakes, and that his shoulder couldn't propel the whole thing sideways.

“You don't have to,” she said. “I can get through here.”

She turned, moving her hand along the frame and pausing at the footplate. Watching her knees clear its edge, he understood that she really had walked every step from the gray door.

He Cen took the part of the broad concrete ledge nearest the entrance. His glasses slid down his nose. When he lifted his supporting hand to fix them, he swayed. Yu Mei turned toward him, but he braced an elbow on the ledge himself.

“Don't pull yet.”

Once his breathing steadied, he adjusted the glasses and made room for Xiao Lu.

“No nails here,” he told her. “Cold, though.”

She felt the surface before sitting, leaving a small space between them. Rather than drawing her hands into her sleeves, she kept turning them over against the concrete.

“It's uncomfortable.”

Lin Mo couldn't tell whether she was complaining. Then he realized she could finally be angry about a real piece of uneven concrete.

Opposite them, Yu Mei raised a hand toward her shoulder, stopped, and rubbed lower down through her sleeve. She drew in a quiet breath. Fang Ji put the light bag down. Its bottles crumpled together, louder than their footsteps.

“Stop squeezing them,” He Cen said.

“Too loud?”

“It sounds as if there's water left.”

Nobody answered. Lin Mo's tongue touched the old cut inside his cheek. He opened his mouth at the sting. He'd known the injury was there; now he'd encountered it again. Neither kind of knowledge replaced the other.

He asked to have the bag moved farther away. Fang Ji set it against the wall without throwing any bottles out. The last one emptied hadn't been flattened much. Its upward-pointing cap made a bulge in the old dry cloth.

Looking away, Lin Mo noticed a worn notebook on the ledge.

His name was on its cover.

“Is that mine?”

Yu Mei nodded, then said yes. Her hand paused beside it, as though she'd nearly passed it over already.

“What shouldn't I touch?”

“Technical pages at the back, with a fold we haven't opened,” Fang Ji said. “Someone else's private business. There's a photograph too. Don't shake it.”

The practical warning reassured him. He asked for the notebook flat on his lap, clear of his splinted wrist. Fang Ji supported its lower edge with his left hand, then had to set it down again and move closer before completing the handover.

Even that small distance couldn't be dismissed as easy.

The notebook had a definite weight. Lin Mo recognized paper, and the characters of his name. The final stroke was cramped. Perhaps the pen had been difficult to hold. He couldn't find the table where he'd written it.

The harder he searched for that table, the more his knees seemed like borrowed supports for someone else's belongings.

“Can I leave it unread for now?”

“Yes,” Yu Mei said.

“What about Xiao Lu?”

She looked up. “I didn't ask you to read it either.”

He'd meant to ask whether she also had something she needed to recognize as her own. Explaining took too long; his throat caught. She waited for his breath instead of supplying a prettier sentence.

“I meant, did you leave anything inside?”

She looked toward the door.

“I don't know. None of my old things are here. I don't know where they are now. That doesn't mean they're gone.”

He nodded without recording her possessions or promising to search the gray door. Fang Ji lowered himself carefully against the wall, protecting his injured palm. At her answer, he stopped looking outside.

A corner of the notebook's cover curled upward. Lin Mo tried flattening it, hurt his index finger, and changed to his middle finger. The paper shifted, exposing a corner of the photograph near the front.

It looked ready to slip out. He stopped and asked Yu Mei to lift it free, leaving it on the notebook.

Two people were in the picture.

The woman nearest the camera resembled the face he'd just seen in the dark metal surface. The other person's hand was blurred. He compared her features with Xiao Lu's across the room. He could recognize a connection without finding a scene opening behind it.

“There's writing on the back,” Xiao Lu said.

“You want me to look?”

“I remember it has writing. I don't know whether I want you to.”

He asked Yu Mei to turn it over. The words elder sister remained where someone had written them. He understood the difference between sister and brother. Xiao Lu brought her knees closer together without asking him to choose the word that better matched the photograph.

“This isn't what you told me.”

“A lot of things were changed after you got out,” she said. “Waking up doesn't mean you suddenly have to live by them.”

“Got out where?”

Yu Mei began to answer, but Xiao Lu said the real world. Later, he'd come back for her. That was all. She didn't turn his return into a story he was obliged to find moving.

He studied the photograph. The woman's face wasn't a mistake he had to cover. The words on the back couldn't supply his missing past either. After a while, he asked Yu Mei to return it facing the same way as before.

“I'm not choosing something new today.”

Xiao Lu nodded. He didn't take that as approval he had needed. The notebook was easier to deal with than the absent answer. He asked Yu Mei to close it, rested a hand on the cover, and stopped searching its edges.

Near the door, the seatkeeper shifted his leg away from a raised patch. He moved as though ashamed of needing more space.

“What's your name?” Lin Mo asked.

The man's shoulders stiffened. “They said it already.”

“I heard. I'm asking you.”

“Seatkeeper.”

It still sounded unlike something written on a notebook. Lin Mo didn't demand another name. He said the man's heel blocked Fang Ji's way, then heard the command in his own voice.

“Could you turn it inward a little?”

The man tried and stopped in pain. “Wait.”

“All right.”

Lin Mo nearly added that there was no hurry. Fang Ji had already withdrawn his foot, making words unnecessary. They waited until the leg cleared the passage.

“He isn't simply another companion,” Yu Mei said quietly, looking directly at the man. “There are things he has to answer for. I can't explain everything now. Your forgetting doesn't make them disappear.”

The seatkeeper lowered his head without asking her to put it differently. Lin Mo wanted the whole account immediately, as though missing it disqualified him from judging anyone. But he hadn't finished examining this room. His eyes already ached.

“Later,” he said. “Don't leave it out because I don't know to ask.”

Yu Mei agreed to tell him what she knew. Nobody used that promise to begin an interrogation. Nobody demanded he choose a side first.

Xiao Lu moved her hands to her knees. Red marks crossed the heels of her palms.

“Cold?” he asked.

“Hard.”

He accepted the correction. She watched him, then asked why he hadn't passed her the photograph.

“You didn't ask.”

“You might have shown me before.”

He waited. She didn't say before had been better, or that he'd finally learned something. The word left a past he hadn't lived through in memory and didn't immediately have to prove.

“Would you like it now?”

“When my hands stop shaking.”

She turned them over for herself. He tried resting his left palm without pressing the injured finger. Neither entirely succeeded. Neither asked the other to start again.

Something clicked against the floor. While arranging the bag, Fang Ji had dropped the cap from an old small bottle. It rolled behind the wheelchair. His shoulder stopped him reaching it, although it lay less than a hand's width from his toes.

Lin Mo thought first of crouching. His leg began to draw back before pain stopped it. He left his foot on the plate.

“I can see it,” Xiao Lu said.

She didn't crawl beneath the chair. She waited for Fang Ji to move his foot, then eased the cap sideways with hers. Her first attempt sent it against the wall. It flipped back. She swore softly. The second brought it somewhere she could bend to reach.

Dust clung inside. She didn't wipe it on her clothes. She handed it to Fang Ji and warned him not to put it near anyone's mouth. He kept it with the small bottle, opening upward, until they could clean it.

“Nearly threw it away,” he said.

“When we find water, are you going to keep a hand over the opening?”

Sweat showed at Xiao Lu's temple as she settled back. Lin Mo remembered where the cap was. Her words didn't promise water, and he didn't congratulate her recovery. She rubbed her leg. That small task had cost her effort.

Yu Mei needed to rest her shoulder. Those few steps had carried the pain toward her neck. When Fang Ji offered to inspect the route, she warned him not to disappear alone merely because everyone else was sitting.

“Only the doorway.”

He stopped where she could see him and reported no one passing along the short corridor.

That wasn't the whole way back. Lin Mo understood.

He asked where they'd last found drinking water. Higher up, Yu Mei said, near the old blue door and the water-tank pipes. What they'd brought down was gone. The leaks along the route weren't drinking water.

He wanted directions, but his head sank first. The notebook began slipping toward his right leg. He pressed its cover, hurting his finger.

“Take this first.”

Nobody made holding it a task to keep him awake. Fang Ji returned and used his left hand for the brief, light transfer to the ledge. The photograph and pages stayed together. He opened nothing.

With his lap empty, Lin Mo realized how tightly he'd been holding his legs.

Xiao Lu called his name.

“I'm sitting here. You don't have to keep watching.”

He nearly said he feared she'd vanish if he closed his eyes. But that felt like something he knew he should fear. The actual fear was simpler: would he still understand these words when he opened his eyes? Would he remember her saying the concrete was hard?

“You said it was hard here.”

“It still is.”

He couldn't smile, but he remembered that answer. Before closing his eyes, he heard Fang Ji move the bag, Yu Mei slow her breathing, and Xiao Lu shift once more, finding a position she could bear.
