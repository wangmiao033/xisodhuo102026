# Chapter 198: The Door Can Be Closed

The lock took two turns before the door opened.

Zhou didn't shove. He eased the key back, straightened it, and tried again. Xiao Lu watched his hand until the blue plastic bucket inside came into view. Then she let out a breath.

“It's always like that,” he said.

“I know.” Her answer came quickly. She slowed down. “I wanted to watch.”

Lin Mo waited in his wheelchair on the level paving. Afternoon heat pressed against his back, and the cushion beneath his knee felt stuffy. He checked the space ahead of the footrests before Zhou brought him through.

A towel had fallen beside a chair. Xiao Lu picked it up, then reached for the bucket.

“Leave that,” Lin Mo said.

“It's in your way.”

“Zhou can move it. Once I'm in, go to bed.”

Zhou put it under the sink. Xiao Lu hung up the towel without stopping to straighten its crooked clips.

The room was quieter than the noodle shop. Outside, someone dragged a metal rack along the pavement. Xiao Lu glanced toward the window, then down at Lin Mo's phone, checking that its ringtone was still low.

“The screen will light up if the hospital calls,” he said.

She nodded and left it on the table rather than taking it behind the curtain.

Her narrow bed had come with the room, a block of wood beneath one leg. As she took off a shoe, her sock caught at the heel. Lin Mo almost told her to loosen the lace first. She stopped and freed the fabric herself.

He didn't have to explain every small movement to her.

“Want this closed?” Zhou asked, holding the curtain.

“Halfway.”

From the gap, Lin Mo could see the foot of the bed, not her face. After a moment she called him.

“Mo.”

“Yes?”

“Wake me before we leave tomorrow. Don't decide I can eat breakfast on the way.”

“We'll eat first.”

The curtain shifted, hiding her crooked shoe.

Zhou parked him beside the table and set the brakes. Did he want to lie down too?

“Let's look at the appointment papers first.” Lin Mo paused. “That bump on the way back still hurts. I'm worried moving to the bed will mean more of it.”

“Then let's make your leg comfortable. The papers aren't going anywhere.”

Zhou didn't touch the straps. Following Lin Mo's directions, he shifted the edge of the cushion half an inch. A fold of clothing came free beneath it. Lin Mo waited before saying the position was better.

Two paper bags stood on the table. One held hospital documents; the other contained the old book they'd brought back. Zhou lifted that one. Its bottom corner was worn pale.

“Take this too?”

Lin Mo looked at the cloth showing through the opening. Since leaving hospital, he'd known the book held things from his past. He had looked at the photograph. He hadn't unfolded every page. The thought of doing so tightened something in his stomach.

“Keep it separate from the medical papers. Over here.”

Zhou set it on a dry corner. He didn't ask why Lin Mo wouldn't read it. Taking a folded sheet from the other bag, he turned its face toward him.

“Your results. May I look? Just to find what you need tomorrow.”

Lin Mo agreed. They separated the appointment slip, examination reports, and payment receipts. Zhou held faint printing at arm's length, then angled it toward the window.

“They've saved more on ink than I have left in my eyes.”

Lin Mo laughed. The inside of his cheek pulled painfully.

“Don't make me laugh.”

“I genuinely can't read it.”

A blank receipt caught on another sheet. Zhou nearly treated them as one. Lin Mo stopped him. They checked dates and printed headings rather than deciding importance by thickness.

It took time. No bell demanded that anyone claim anything. A misread word could be read again. When Zhou put an old queue ticket aside, Lin Mo asked him not to throw it away yet.

“Even this?”

“If I look for it tomorrow, I want to know whether I left it behind or decided I didn't need it.”

Zhou folded it into a separate bag and wrote OLD TICKETS, not VOID.

The phone lit up with a hospital reminder. Tomorrow's appointment hadn't changed. Lin Mo showed Zhou, then lowered the screen so its light wasn't aimed through the curtain.

“I can come in the morning,” Zhou said. “Afternoon, I need to get back to my own place.”

They checked where to meet the car. The shortest route on the map included steps. Zhou crossed it off. They could go around to the front street; a wheelchair symbol didn't guarantee the actual pavement.

“Keep track of the fare,” Lin Mo said.

Zhou entered tomorrow's journey in his own notebook, leaving the amount empty until it existed. He didn't add a charge for the laundry shop after the fact.

A paper bag brushed the old bundle. Lin Mo lifted his left hand and found it wouldn't reach. Zhou steadied the book without opening it.

“Can't put anything on top?”

“There's a photograph inside. Someone else's papers too.”

The bedside cabinet was flat, but its door would strike Xiao Lu's bed. Instead, Zhou moved unused cups off the table and laid down a dry cloth. Lin Mo asked for the bag opening to face him. Not so he could read immediately—so he wouldn't have to wake someone when he wanted it.

A page edge showed above the book. DIARY ONE. Smaller writing beneath. He recognized the heading and left it alone.

“Want me to turn it over?”

“Not today.”

Zhou covered it. Nothing happened. Nobody behind the paper demanded an explanation for his delay.

Lin Mo kept looking at the cloth. The person who had left so much might have wanted him to understand quickly. That person might have been frightened. Wasn't he wasting someone else's effort by leaving it unread? He could think all this without recalling how the pen had felt in his hand.

“Read it if you want,” Zhou said. “Don't rush because I'm sitting here.”

“I haven't decided.”

“I'll wash the cups.”

Water ran at the sink. Zhou rinsed them and turned them upside down. He didn't make the silence into a ceremony in which everyone waited for Lin Mo to choose.

Lin Mo rested his left hand near the bundle, neither touching it nor pulling away. Eventually the tension beneath his shoulder eased. He picked up tomorrow's hospital bag instead.

The bed creaked behind the curtain.

“Is the water out there?” Xiao Lu sounded hoarse.

“Cool or warm?”

“Warm. Don't come in. I'll sit up.”

Zhou set a cup on the small stool beside the curtain and waited for her hand before letting go. She drank without coming out.

“Are you looking at the book?”

“No. Sorting the hospital papers.”

“Oh.”

Her hand disappeared, then returned with the cup.

“You don't have to call me when you read it.”

“I know. If there's something I want to ask, I'll ask whether you want to talk first.”

The cup touched the stool. Only then did he hear her quiet agreement.

Lin Mo moved his left foot, trying to settle farther back. His right wrist went instinctively toward the armrest. The support struck its hard edge. He stopped, sweat rising across his forehead.

Zhou didn't reach under his arms.

“What are you trying to do?”

“I'm too far forward.”

“How did they show you?”

There was a transfer board beside the bed. They'd been taught how to use it before discharge, but Lin Mo wasn't sure they could arrange it properly here. Zhou brought it over and showed him rather than sliding it beneath him at once.

They checked the positions they'd learned. From behind the curtain, Xiao Lu warned them not to pull his shoulder.

“We won't,” Zhou said. “Stay there. If we can't position it, we'll stop.”

The table blocked the direction they needed. They moved that first, instead of making Lin Mo twist to accommodate furniture. Cups and book stayed in the middle of its surface. The legs scraped the tiles. Xiao Lu frowned through the narrow gap.

“Sorry,” Zhou said.

She didn't say it was fine. She let the curtain fall.

When they were ready, Lin Mo discovered he'd been holding his breath. He asked them to stop and breathed again. Sorting tomorrow's documents hadn't accomplished this movement for him. He had to say what hurt and how much he could do.

Zhou waited, hands at the positions they'd checked, without catching the injured wrist or loading the knee. Lin Mo shifted only as far as he could tolerate. The movement was smaller than he'd expected, but the seam no longer pressed into the same spot.

Zhou returned the board beside the bed, not to an unreachable cupboard. Before they moved the table back, Lin Mo checked the footrest and his clothes.

“Tell me sooner next time,” Zhou said. “I can't guess that you're uncomfortable.”

Lin Mo told him which numb ache had eased. He didn't promise he'd always speak in time. Zhou left the folded towel nearby instead of pushing it behind his back. That small space stayed empty. Lin Mo let his breathing sound tired.

The appointment bag faced upward when the table returned. The cloth over the book was crooked. Xiao Lu didn't emerge to straighten it.

As Zhou put his shoes on, Lin Mo noticed that the outer door was still ajar. People in the passage glanced inside as they passed. A delivery worker stopped.

“Are you the two who came back the other day?”

Zhou moved into the gap.

“Who are you looking for?”

“Nobody. I heard—”

“Then not now. They need to rest.”

The footsteps retreated reluctantly. Lin Mo hadn't seen the man's face. He didn't need to chase him and explain that he wasn't being important.

“Leave it open for air?” Zhou asked.

“Close it.”

Traffic still sounded outside. Closing a door hadn't made the world safe. It meant the people in this room didn't also have to explain themselves to everyone in the passage.

Before leaving, Zhou wrote the morning time on the bag and read it aloud. He would eat before coming; they didn't need to save him breakfast. Lin Mo pushed the papers away from the edge.

“Anything else?”

He looked at the water, charging cable, and curtain concealing his sister.

“No. Go home.”

A few minutes later, the lock made another small sound. His back tightened.

The key didn't turn. Zhou spoke softly from outside.

“Forgot to tell you. Turn the knob on the inside. You don't need the key.”

Lin Mo answered. This time the footsteps continued away.

He asked Xiao Lu, if she was awake, whether she could turn it in a while.

“When I wake up,” came her sleepy reply.

“All right.”

The old book remained covered. Sunlight crossed the cloth and reached the new bag with tomorrow's time on it.

Lin Mo positioned the phone where he could see its screen, settled against the backrest, and closed his eyes for a moment.
