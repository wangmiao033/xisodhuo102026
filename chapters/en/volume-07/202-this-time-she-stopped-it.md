# Chapter 202: This Time She Stopped It

The mat moved slightly. Tang Jing began speaking faster.

Liang stopped the movement at once. Nobody had lifted the chair away. The crew released the pressure they'd just applied. Luo Huaisheng's right hand halted in the air, fingers trembling. A medic supported his forearm without forcing it down against the pull.

Lin Mo watched the chair legs. They'd moved the front corner of the mat, but the whole length tightened from behind, drawing Tang's right foot deeper onto the pedal.

“It continues underneath,” Xiao Lu said.

She meant the mat, not an invisible rope between the people. Light from the side revealed fabric curling around the controller's base, its farther end caught beneath a rear chair leg. Pulling from the front first tightened the loop beneath her foot.

Han Wei cursed himself quietly. He'd folded it twice to stop the equipment sliding, then forgotten to straighten it in the rush to screen the film.

Liang asked which folds. After looking carefully, Han indicated the back layer. Unfold the side first; don't pull from the front. Nobody refused useful information to make his admission of fault more complete.

Tang's intervals of silence shortened. Her gaze went to her cup, but she shook her head. The medic didn't immediately give her water, allowing her to recover her breath.

Could they let her see the left pedal instead of the screen? Lin Mo asked, then waited for Liang and Tang to establish whether she could change her view. Nobody twisted her neck for her.

Liang shifted aside, illuminating the floor. Tang lowered her eyes and drew her left foot back a fraction. No faster repetition followed; Luo's hand didn't rise higher.

She could still move that foot herself.

The mat's fold loosened slowly. One rescuer steadied the frame while another unfolded only the raised fabric at the side. They neither lifted the whole chair nor dragged Tang. A loose screw lay beneath the thick cloth. Han reached for it; Liang stopped him before his hand went under a load-bearing point.

The screw bore no emerging words. It was a screw in dust. Keeping it lit, they worked around the bump and flattened the fabric a little at a time. Most of the left pedal emerged beneath worn transparent tape.

Small letters read:

END THIS READING.

Han exhaled, started to reach, then stopped.

“Should I press it?”

Tang immediately shook her head, hard enough to break her voice into a rasp. Liang told her not to rush and moved Han back. Lin Mo didn't explain why she objected. She'd refused. She didn't owe them an essay before it counted.

But the large box changed.

PLEASE END ON THE SUBJECT'S BEHALF.

Luo's right hand moved again, as though the image expected one last declaration. Lin Mo watched the phrase on behalf of. The large display and the pedal said different things. One still recruited a person to act for somebody else; the other referred only to this reading.

“Don't use the big box for her.”

Liang passed that on. Nobody touched the central gray light. Xiao Lu remained visible by the side door instead of stepping in front of Tang for a closer look.

“Can your left foot reach?” she asked.

Tang tried moving her toe. It couldn't. She sat too far back, the controller too far forward. Leaning to reach would press her right foot harder onto the other half.

“Could we move the controller?” Han asked.

Its ordinary cable remained attached to disconnected equipment. Lin Mo couldn't assume the gray layer would move harmlessly because the power was off. They needed to inspect the base, not drag it beneath Tang's planted foot.

Liang crouched to look. Her right foot pinned the device; moving it would move her. He abandoned that approach. Could she put her left toe at the near edge and decide for herself whether to press farther, rather than reaching the front in one movement?

After looking carefully, she slowly closed her eyes.

The medic supported the side where she might lose balance, so her right foot needn't brace her whole body. That hand steadied her. It didn't push her leg toward the pedal. Tang set her left heel down and eased her toe through the space the flattened mat had cleared.

A white scuff showed along her shoe. After every small movement, she stopped to breathe. Nobody counted down three seconds in which she had to succeed.

Continue lit on the right again.

Her voice barely escaped her teeth. Luo suddenly offered to nod. Anything to stop her suffering.

Han lifted his head.

“Do you want to accept that statement,” Lin Mo asked the old man, “or do you want her to stop?”

“Her to stop!”

“Then that's what we're doing. You don't have to surrender your account of the past as well.”

Luo's lips moved. He didn't accept the raised hand as his answer. His left still gripped the chair; he settled back instead of wrestling the other arm downward.

Lin Mo couldn't promise the room would never splice these words into something else. He could separate the man's meaning here, in front of witnesses. No sentence would guarantee permanent immunity from misuse.

Tang's shoe reached the edge of the left pedal.

Liang explained that nobody would press it for her. If she wanted to stop her own reading, she could try. If her body couldn't manage, she could signal. Care wouldn't be withdrawn because she couldn't complete a movement.

She looked at Luo, then Liang.

Lin Mo suppressed the urge to hurry her. Waiting for a final action felt horribly familiar in his body. Familiarity didn't give him her time to spend.

Her left foot pressed down.

The first attempt caught only the tape's edge. The pedal didn't travel fully. Captions paused, then rolled again. Continue broke into fragments of breath. Tang shut her eyes, tears dropping onto her knees. The medic held her steady; nobody said one failed attempt meant that was all she could do.

Liang saw the slipping toe. From outside the pedal's edge, a rescuer flattened the loose tape without depressing the control. The tape had already curled and collected dust. It wasn't a seal that must remain untouched.

Tang looked down again.

This time she pressed slowly. Her shoe stayed in place. The left pedal descended with a small movement. Reading in Progress stopped in the narrow strip and became a motionless gray line.

Her mouth stopped before willing.

Lin Mo heard a deep inhalation catch painfully. The medic attended to her. She wasn't required to say I've finished immediately to prove it.

Luo's right hand settled onto his lap.

He stared at it and raised his left hand, but didn't immediately twist the sore limb. His fingertips moved. Did this mean he didn't have to answer? Liang said nobody here required him to answer that interview. Medical staff would first examine the places that had been pulled.

Tang could finally move her right foot away from Continue.

A little lift. A pause. No sentence forced itself out again. Only then did she place the foot entirely on the floor. Both shoes stood before her own chair. Nobody took over the right pedal to maintain some supposed stability.

Luo's central portrait darkened; the large box vanished. Gray remained along the screen's lower edge, like an old crease that wouldn't clean away. Lin Mo didn't ask Han to restore power and prove everything safe.

“Keep it closed,” Liang told the crew. “People out. Leave the equipment.”

Before moving Tang, they let her adjust her position and checked that nothing pulled her back into the reading. Her throat produced only a small breath of sound. The medic said she needn't force words and helped her communicate another way.

Han offered paper and a pen, asking first. Only when she reached did he give them to her. She wrote water, crossed it out, then wrote outside first. The crowded letters remained legible.

Liang arranged the transfer. They didn't postpone all care until she was outdoors; they attended to her while moving toward the exit she wanted. Nobody called her back to supply an ending.

Passing Han, she paused and wrote on the reverse:

Don't put this in the film.

He nodded. His lips moved for a long time before he managed, “Understood.” Lin Mo could see the paper. He heard no forgiveness. Somebody whose throat still hurt didn't have to make a working relationship tidy on the way out.

Tang pointed at the gray jacket beside her chair. Han lifted it, touched a pocket, then stopped and held the whole garment toward her. Was she looking for her keys? She nodded and indicated the right pocket. He retrieved them from there without emptying everything else to be helpful.

A metal charm touched the ring with a small sound. Tang's shoulders flinched; then she held the keys. They would let her into her home. They weren't the next switch in this room.

Watching her, Luo asked where his glasses were. They hung at his collar. He hadn't managed to reach them while his right hand kept rising. Someone pointed them out; he slowly removed them with his left, wiped the lenses and put them on.

“What did she write?”

Han didn't display her note for everyone to read. He explained that she didn't want this incident included. Luo nodded, adding that his original interview shouldn't be used yet either. He wanted to think after returning home.

Han almost explained how many days he'd spent editing. Instead, he agreed to stop for now. Liang asked him to record that request without editing the current nod into acceptance of everything that followed.

Lin Mo listened without taking charge of their film. Luo had a room and a son. Tang had keys and a cup with a rabbit sticker. Their lives after this room didn't belong entirely to the people who'd witnessed danger at its door. He left his next sentence unspoken, allowing Han to hear the old man's request and Tang a space without another demand for response.

Xiao Lu stepped back to widen the route. Her heel touched the small step outside; she steadied herself without asking Lin Mo to turn and catch her. Zhou moved the wheelchair early, clearing its footrests from the stretcher's path.

Only then did Lin Mo notice his left hand clenched around the armrest, fingertips numb. He slowly released it. Heat remained beneath his shoulder, his knee urging him away. He asked Zhou to take him outside rather than waiting to be told that today's sitting and looking had lasted long enough.

Air passed beneath the faded poster. Its smiling face still lacked a corner.

Liang approached the wheelchair to ask for a brief account. Lin Mo agreed. They'd established that this reading had stopped and both people could leave their positions. That didn't prove every residual connection severed. They hadn't dealt with the continuing support structure left somewhere else in his past.

Liang recorded it without demanding a declaration that the entire building was safe.

Tang kept the paper in her hand as she was taken out. Luo received attention separately, still asking whether his son had been notified. Someone said contact had been made. Lin Mo couldn't hear the details and supplied no reassuring ending to the call.

Han stood just inside, looking at his disconnected microphone. When Liang called, he first checked that he wasn't being sent back to restart anything. Then he crossed the threshold.

Zhou touched his inner jacket. The notebook with the copied diary lines remained there. The wrapped book hung behind the wheelchair, private fold unopened, photograph secure. Lin Mo reached toward the new notes, then realized he couldn't reach them.

“At home,” Zhou said.

He nodded. This time he didn't insist on reading everything at the door.

Xiao Lu came around where he could see her. Had the words made him remember anything?

He looked at her for a moment.

“I understood some of it. I haven't remembered.”

She lowered her gaze and moved a torn scrap of poster from beside the front wheel. She didn't add finally to what he'd understood.
