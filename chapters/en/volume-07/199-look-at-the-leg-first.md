# Chapter 199: Look at the Leg First

Xiao Lu tore the egg while peeling it the next morning.

A little white stuck to the shell. She scraped at it with a spoon, noticed Lin Mo watching, and put the egg in his bowl.

“No shell.”

“I didn't ask.”

“Your face was asking.”

He touched the crease between his eyebrows and couldn't feel a difference. Remembering what she'd said the night before, he divided the egg with his spoon and didn't urge her to finish her porridge.

They were still eating when Zhou arrived. He drank soy milk by the door and complained that it was too sweet. Why had he bought it, then? The only place open along his route. They argued about soy milk without losing track of the time.

The car was waiting on the front street. Zhou had asked about the wheelchair beforehand; now he checked the door and seat height again. They used the transfer method they'd learned before discharge. Lin Mo kept his right foot from bearing weight and the supported wrist close to his chest. Xiao Lu watched the board instead of lifting his injured shoulder.

Heat gathered at his temples. He asked them to wait.

The driver, who had been reaching to scoop him into the seat, drew back. Nobody complimented a former soldier on enduring pain. Lin Mo breathed until the ache eased enough to cover the remaining distance.

As they passed the street with yesterday's noodle shop, Xiao Lu opened the window a fraction.

The laundry wasn't on this side. Lin Mo didn't ask for a detour. Zhou didn't turn a medical appointment into an inspection of their work. There was no new message from Xu Qin; all they knew was that she and Sun Cheng had reached hospital yesterday.

The waiting area had filled before they arrived. A nurse asked someone to remove crutches used to reserve an empty chair. A child stared at Lin Mo's wrist, then was pulled away before he could say there was no need.

Xiao Lu collected their queue number and brought him a form.

“Your signature here. Read it yourself.”

He checked that it belonged to today's visit. His left-handed letters slanted. He didn't ask her to trace them into something neater.

The screen flashed before the loudspeaker called his name. An ordinary recorded voice, borrowing no familiar inflection from an old channel. Xiao Lu stood. He reminded himself that he didn't have to intercept every sound and decide whether it was dangerous.

The doctor read the papers, then asked about falls, weight-bearing, and pain that had worsened since discharge.

Lin Mo mentioned the jolt in the wheelchair and the long period sitting at the laundry. His shoulder had felt hot afterward.

“How long?”

“I don't know the minutes. I didn't lie down in between.”

The doctor looked up. Having helped someone didn't make that answer irrelevant.

As the examination began, Lin Mo tried to move his lower leg into a more convenient position. The doctor steadied it first.

“Don't test what force you can manage. Tell me where it hurts.”

He stopped.

Queue announcements came through the door. A fan lifted the corner of a sheet on the desk. The doctor asked him to distinguish the ongoing dull ache from the sharper pain during movement, rather than describing the whole leg as bearable.

Xiao Lu didn't answer for him. Zhou waited by the doorway, with the wheelchair kept clear of the passage.

“Here,” Lin Mo said at last. “That side hurt more just now. Yesterday too.”

The doctor noted it, ordered the necessary reassessment, and told him to continue keeping weight off it. Neither shoulder nor wrist could substitute for the leg during transfers. He wasn't to loosen the supports because nothing looked different from the outside.

“Can I sit and look at things?” Lin Mo asked.

“You're looking at things now.” The doctor handed him a sheet. “Do you mean ten minutes sitting down, or several hours without moving, followed by a rough journey home? The second isn't all right.”

Zhou gave a short laugh and suppressed it.

Lin Mo didn't laugh. He had wanted permission broad enough to cover anything he might later call sitting and looking. The doctor told him to allow sufficient rest, stop according to his actual condition, and seek help as instructed if there were significant new changes—not wait for the next appointment.

Xiao Lu entered the advice in the phone and showed him. He asked about a point he hadn't understood. They didn't shorten the instructions to fit what he'd hoped to hear.

While waiting for the reassessment, they sat by a window. Xiao Lu went for warm water. Zhou's phone rang.

“I need to take this outside.”

Through the glass, Lin Mo watched him stand at the end of the passage, one hand over his ear. His mouth moved several times, then stopped to let the caller finish. Lin Mo couldn't hear. A worried face didn't give him a death to imagine at the other end.

Zhou returned as Xiao Lu brought the water.

“Not the laundry.”

Lin Mo waited.

“An old community room. They're recording local people's stories to show the neighborhood in the evenings. Something happened during yesterday's test screening. The police have been called. The room's closed off. They want to know whether you'd listen.”

“Is someone still inside?”

“One person won't come out. Keeps talking. I haven't seen it.”

“Medical help?”

“There. Don't think nobody's dealing with it.”

Lin Mo settled his cup.

“Let them deal with it. I'm seeing the doctor today.”

The sentence sounded short even to him. He didn't justify the injury or promise to make up for it that evening. Zhou nodded without putting the caller on the phone to him.

Xiao Lu sat down. Water circled the cup. She didn't say thank goodness, or that they ought to go. She asked how much longer the results would take. He looked toward the desk. Not their turn yet.

After a while Zhou said he'd told them they weren't coming today. He hadn't given out Lin Mo's number. He'd kept himself as the contact and would ask before passing anything further along.

Lin Mo released his grip on the bag.

“Thank you.”

“Don't complain tomorrow that I lost you a job.”

“I won't.”

“I'll remember that.”

Zhou put his phone away and asked what fillings they didn't want in the flatbreads downstairs. Nothing spicy, Xiao Lu said. Lin Mo remembered the noodle shop and thought they could at least ask before choosing for each other.

A man near the desk raised his phone, sweeping its camera across the waiting area. Lin Mo saw it turn toward him. He asked Zhou not to move the chair, then lifted his left hand only as far as his shoulder allowed.

“Don't film me.”

The man said he was showing his family how long the queue was. He kept the phone raised. Xiao Lu stood, without grabbing it, and pointed to the notice about filming on the wall.

“I didn't get your face,” the man said.

Lin Mo couldn't see the screen. He repeated his request not to include the people waiting here, then asked a passing staff member for help. The phone finally came down. Muttering, the man moved away.

Once the staff member took over, Zhou went downstairs for the food.

Xiao Lu's hand shook as she sat.

“You don't always have to shield me,” Lin Mo said.

“I'm sitting here too.”

He stopped, then nodded. She had her own face, her own moment of not wanting it filmed. His thanks could swallow that as easily as anyone else's assumption.

“Yes. I was only thinking of myself.”

She didn't demand a longer apology. Finding her water cold, she went to add some warm. Lin Mo looked at the blurred window reflection. No lost face appeared there. Only people waiting, lights, and chair backs crowded together.

Zhou returned with the flatbreads and set them between them. Lin Mo explained what had happened. He hadn't seen whether anything was deleted; he couldn't say it was completely resolved.

“Want to ask again?”

He thought about it, then asked staff to note the time and location instead of sending Xiao Lu after the man. They heard what could actually be checked before turning back to the results desk.

The thinnest edge of a flatbread crumbled as they divided it. Zhou caught the crumbs in the wrapping and complained about hospital prices.

“At least it isn't spicy,” Xiao Lu said.

Lin Mo chewed slowly, keeping food away from the sore part of his mouth. Breakfast didn't have to prove that it had healed.

When the results came, they returned to the consultation room. No paper cleared him for every activity or promised no lasting consequences. The doctor reviewed the tests, adjusted follow-up arrangements, and kept the restrictions in place. Zhou clipped the new sheet at the front without throwing out the old reports.

Outside, Xiao Lu asked whether she should take a photograph.

“The new instructions. Not the whole bag in a message.”

She found a spot without glare, showed him, then saved it. Name and date were legible. A blurred screen didn't replace the paper.

While they waited for the car, Zhou received a written update. After reading it, he asked whether Lin Mo wanted the details that had actually been established. Listening wouldn't mean agreeing to go.

Lin Mo settled against the backrest.

“Go ahead.”

The trapped worker was Tang Jing. An older man, Luo Huaisheng, was still there too. During the test screening some subtitles had needed reading. Tang Jing had read them for him and afterward couldn't stop. Ordinary power to the equipment had been cut, but the same sentence continued. Zhou had not received the original record; he wouldn't turn every relayed detail into eyewitness testimony.

Xiao Lu's spoon touched her cup with a small sound.

“Accident,” she said when Lin Mo looked. She moved the cup farther away.

“Is anyone hurt?” he asked.

“They say her voice is hoarse. Medical people are checking. Nobody's calling it harmless or asking me to guarantee that.”

Lin Mo watched the road for the car. Saying he'd seen this before might calm someone for a moment. He didn't know his before. He hadn't seen this room. Procedural familiarity couldn't supply eyes several streets away.

“Don't ask anyone else to keep reading,” he said. “That isn't me knowing the solution. Don't put another person in it just to find out. The professionals there need to work from what they can establish.”

Zhou passed that on without saying Lin Mo had taken charge.

On the return journey, Xiao Lu left the window closed. She held the new paper bag on her lap until the car stopped.

Back in the wheelchair, Lin Mo's right leg felt as though it had stayed behind in the seat.

“Wait before we move.”

They waited. The driver shut the boot and offered help. Zhou thanked him; none was needed at that moment.

Nobody counted down the pause.

At home, Xiao Lu put water on to boil. Lin Mo asked Zhou to leave the medical instructions where he could see them, then asked whether he could come tomorrow. They didn't need to fix the time immediately.

“The community room?”

“First, the full account. If we go, we discuss transport, rest, who comes. Not today.”

Zhou agreed, reminding him that fees hadn't been discussed. Neither side could be committed on the other's behalf.

“I know. Rescue doesn't wait for me. If they need additional observation, we'll discuss it separately.”

Xiao Lu turned from the sink.

“I want to know what the place looks like first.”

He didn't take that as a promise to accompany him.

“We'll look at whatever information they can provide. Then you decide.”

She nodded and switched off the boiling kettle. Steam covered half her face, then cleared.
