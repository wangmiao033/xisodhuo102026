# Chapter 193: Don't Take the Jacket Away

The blue collar in the window was wider than Lin Mo's shoulders.

After watching it, he asked Xiao Lu not to cover the glass yet. Xu Qin stopped with a cloth raised. Qiu still held the hanger beneath the twitching sleeve. Nobody could keep both hands occupied indefinitely while they waited for an answer.

Lin Mo asked Zhou to watch the counter and Xiao Lu to move his chair a little left. The brake released; wheels crossed a tile joint. The reflected chair moved with them. The collar stayed where it was, separated from his face by half a hand's width.

It wasn't on him.

Xu Qin exhaled when he said so. Qiu continued frowning. The jacket's failure to find a new wearer didn't release the old one. Lin Mo examined the faded price list on the lower window. Behind it was the usual collection position, exactly where the collar appeared.

“Customers stand here to take their clothes?”

Xu Qin nodded. People used the right side of the narrow counter to avoid the dirty laundry bags on the left. She glanced right as though someone really were waiting to pay.

No face appeared. Only an empty collar.

Qiu inverted a spare basket at the counter's edge and rested the hanger across it, transferring the weight from his hands. Supported, the sleeve loosened the thread beneath the bell slightly. Nobody had to become a human clothes rack. Lin Mo asked him to release it gradually, stopping if Sun Cheng felt worse. Only after both sides understood did Qiu let go.

No fresh coughing.

Qiu chalked two marks beside the main basket and asked Xu Qin not to wipe them away. They showed whether it moved; they couldn't imprison the garment. He made that distinction explicit. Her left hand withdrew from the chalk. Lin Mo watched the white marks too. Position no longer depended on frightened recollections.

Xu Qin steadied the main basket left-handed. Shouldn't they return the jacket to the machine's intake? The instruction was there, the opening found, barely two steps from the counter.

Lin Mo saw the yellow and pink slips without their white copy. He couldn't guarantee it, he said, but moving the basket toward the machine's side of the counter might show whether direction mattered.

His familiar bodily certainty reached ahead of the evidence. Xiao Lu looked up. Would it pull Xu Qin?

“Only move it a little.”

Xu Qin agreed. Zhou used hangers through the handles; neither touched the jacket as they eased the basket left. Before it reached the counter's middle, Xu Qin's right hand jerked forward. She gasped. Her knuckles struck wood. The black thread shone taut, lifting a corner of her apron.

Sun Cheng gave a short cry.

“Back!” Lin Mo said immediately.

Zhou didn't panic and lift. He retraced the movement. When the sleeve settled onto its support, Xu Qin could pull her hand back. Clutching her wrist, she leaned against the counter, forehead wet. The thread hadn't snapped. A fresh red band marked her skin.

Xiao Lu crouched to inspect the struck knuckles. Could she open her hand? Xu Qin slowly did, trembling badly. Qiu called his colleague to bring the first-aid kit to the entrance while he watched the inner door. He didn't pronounce the injury harmless.

Lin Mo pressed his left hand onto the coat across his lap. A little movement hadn't meant a little consequence. Everyone looked toward the woman in pain. He remained facing the direction he'd wrongly chosen.

“I got that wrong.”

Xu Qin offered no comfort. Were they going to move it again?

“Not now. Your hand first.”

Zhou didn't say it was all right either. He secured the slippery contact between the two baskets, then returned. If the jacket couldn't travel, they needed the paper. A direction wasn't a route.

Lin Mo's ears burned. He nodded anyway.

Sun Cheng asked what had happened to his wife. She said she'd knocked her hand. Lin Mo explained the rest: moving the basket left had tightened the wrist thread; they'd returned it. After a silence, Sun Cheng said his shoulder had twisted the other way. His lack of complaint only made Lin Mo feel worse.

Cold-pack supplies came in from the entrance. Xu Qin applied them to her knuckles without forcing anything over the embedded thread. She could move her hand; further assessment would have to follow. Lin Mo asked her not to carry the basket's weight. Supports could do that. She needed only to report changes in her wrist.

Xiao Lu came beside him. “Did you remember something?”

“No.”

“Then why so fast?”

He thought. “It felt wrong to keep waiting.”

She looked toward the inner door and didn't press him. His reason sounded hollow. Waiting could hurt someone. So could haste. His body retained dangerous tasks; it didn't know the terms of every new object.

Qiu illuminated the counter's underside. When the basket moved, the thin thread had shifted sideways out of a small brass guide. Returned, it ran through the center again. A worn line connected that guide to the end of the track. Years of receiving returned garments had stripped the counter's varnish there.

Lin Mo didn't ask for another movement to confirm it. Qiu could inspect from the current position while he reviewed what had happened. Low placement pulled the man inside; departure from the handover position pulled Xu Qin. A basket could substitute temporarily for a receiving hand, but didn't cancel the handover. Perhaps what mattered wasn't who took the jacket, but why it was still being handed over.

Sun Cheng's COLLECTED remained at the collar, waiting for the final step.

“What if the white copy was blank?” Xu Qin asked.

“Then it can't explain this. Find it first. Don't decide what's written on it.”

She indicated the narrow doorway behind the counter. The paper had been left on the pressing table, but she'd swept afterward. Scraps accumulated behind the door. It might no longer be on the tabletop. When she moved to search, Qiu stopped her. She couldn't squeeze through that gap now. Sun Cheng agreed. His arm felt stretched across the space behind it; he didn't know what touching it would do.

Lin Mo asked Zhou to inspect another route into the work area. A low gate on the counter's left opened onto a level floor. Two laundry bags blocked it. With Xu Qin's consent, they moved the bags near the street-side cabinet. She identified their customer labels as they went; the unclaimed jacket wasn't mixed in. Zhou took the lighter bag, Qiu's colleague the other. The cleared passage admitted the wheelchair.

“You're going farther in again,” Xiao Lu said.

“To see under the door. Standing people don't have this angle.”

He didn't mean that only he could save the man. Qiu could crouch too, but was watching the relationship between door and body. Lin Mo proposed observing from level ground, not abandoning his chair.

Xiao Lu walked the route first, checking ordinary tiles and pointing out water along the wall. Zhou mopped it and shifted the bags farther clear of their retreat. Qiu moved one light toward the work area. Another remained on the counter; the jacket wouldn't vanish into an unwatched darkness.

At the gate, a fold of Lin Mo's coat caught against his right knee. He stopped Zhou and waited while Xiao Lu straightened it. Sun Cheng coughed twice in the intervening seconds. Lin Mo clenched his teeth but didn't ask to go faster. The urge to trade his own pain for someone else's time arrived almost without needing memory.

He didn't obey it.

Past the gate, Zhou paused beside a worktable. Yellow scorch marks covered the wood. Sun Cheng's handwritten schedule hung in the corner: midday deliveries, Wednesday electricity payment. Some items were crossed out. One had been circled twice. Following that circle, Lin Mo realized Xu Qin was looking here too.

“He says I always forget,” she said.

He asked why she hadn't followed, then noticed her hand still at the counter. Nothing visibly bound her there; she was afraid to leave. The failed move had made that worse. She wanted to watch the jacket in case it slipped away. A reassurance couldn't fix that.

Xiao Lu brought the old sheet and spread its loose edge beside the basket, ready to line it once the sleeve could be supported. She didn't remove the hanger. From between door and counter she could see both, she told Xu Qin. Report changes directly; don't run over first. Xu Qin nodded, shoulders lowering slightly.

Lin Mo leaned back with his shoulder throbbing and asked Zhou for his medication record. He checked the time. Too early for another dose. He didn't take extra to prolong his stay. Xiao Lu offered water as he put the record away. If the noodle shop closed, they'd buy steamed bread.

“I thought we agreed on hot noodles.”

“You said we'd see whether it was open.”

She remembered precisely. He drank. The man behind the door remained awake, the counter stable, their retreat clear. These present facts justified continuing. Forgotten debts didn't oblige him to stay.

At the inner door, his angle changed. A horizontal scratch crossed the lower panel, its wooden edge chipped. Something inside held it short of a palm-wide opening. Sun Cheng's left shoe showed at the bottom, its fabric side worn white. No rope encircled his ankle. Higher up, Lin Mo saw only a patch of gray undershirt and a taut forearm.

He called Sun Cheng's name.

“Can you see me?”

“One shoe. Don't squeeze forward to give me a better view.”

The shoe shifted and stopped. Lin Mo looked lower. The pressing table's front-left leg rested on a thin wooden shim with a curled edge. A corner of white paper lay caught between shim and iron. Too far inside for their earlier light, it showed no writing. It might be an ordinary scrap.

He asked Qiu not to probe for it yet. Was anything else obstructing Sun Cheng's feet? His left foot could move. His right shoulder was pulled backward. His left hand reached the table edge, but he couldn't bend. A sleeve seemed to hold his arm behind him, though his fingers found no cloth, no cuff to pull off.

Lin Mo slowly opened his left hand on his lap. Training still told him how to place people and preserve an exit. It couldn't cut the invisible sleeve off the man inside.

“Don't try taking it off yet. Let me describe what's on the floor.”

Xu Qin spoke from the counter.

Not her husband's name, not a scream for help. “It's sliding outward again.”

Lin Mo turned. The blue sleeve stretched longer under the light, its red clip tilted toward collection. The thread beneath the counter wasn't quite taut. Between the two illuminated areas, Xiao Lu asked Xu Qin what hurt before Lin Mo could speak.

Nothing yet, she said.

Lin Mo looked back at the paper corner.

“We need to see this one first.”
