# Chapter 187: The Bottles Were Still Empty

Dust covered the locking pin.

Lin Mo saw its exposed head first. The second groove lay beside the socket, distinguishable only from another angle. Yu Mei kept the wheelchair back and moved to the side, turning the lamp. The shoulder supports cast longer shadows. The classification labels still glowed on the floor.

Locking the frame hadn't removed the plate.

Fang Ji stayed outside its boundary. Lin Mo asked Yu Mei to check that the pin was fully inserted, not merely half caught. She bent to inspect it without pulling, then indicated the illuminated portion.

The second groove sat entirely inside the socket. Its position matched the diagram. Beneath it, the maintenance-lock indicator remained visible.

“This frame is locked now.”

That was enough for their next few steps. They didn't need a guarantee from the whole Theater.

Yu Mei stood near the old release pedal. Fang Ji crossed empty-handed and stopped beyond the raised shoulder supports. He Cen followed, watching the solid ground, then leaned against the wall. The seatkeeper waited a long time outside the plate before moving. His breath escaped as his dragging leg cleared the line.

Helping hadn't earned him a different route. Nor was extra pain imposed on him here.

Xiao Lu paused beneath the ironwork. Lin Mo didn't hurry her. She asked whether she must avoid touching the rods midway. Yu Mei told her to leave the locking pin and red lever alone and follow the checked passage.

She crossed slowly, arms close, scraped palm clear of the frame. Beyond it, she looked toward the wheelchair and made room without calling him to hurry.

Yu Mei returned for him.

The female-position label still glowed over the right-hand route. Lin Mo understood that it assigned passage through this machine. He didn't read it as a new name he had to accept upon waking. He rested against the cushion as the wheels passed beneath the supports. His shoulder bore no machinery, but its old injury still hurt with each jolt.

Beyond the frame, Yu Mei looked back at the pin. Nobody removed it to take along. Fang Ji retrieved the bag, pausing to rest his hand before carrying it to the lamp-repair room's rear doorway.

Lin Mo smelled damp wood.

Water lay closer than the room described by his companions had led him to imagine. It spread from the front entrance without covering the whole floor. A drier area remained beside the back wall. Both old benches stood inside. The front legs of one were in water; the other had only dark damp marks beneath its feet.

“Stay out of the water,” Yu Mei said.

She parked inside the back entrance, inspected the rear bench, and tested it gently. Nothing shifted beneath her hand. He Cen waited rather than dropping onto it. Once she'd checked the section he could use, she helped him over, leaving the wheelchair braked.

Xiao Lu sat beside him. The seatkeeper leaned near the doorway, his injured leg clear of the water. Fang Ji placed the bag at an unoccupied end of the rear bench. Its old cloth remained dry, the notebook clear of wet shoes.

All six had crossed.

Lin Mo didn't say they were back. He knew the room's earlier use only through other people's accounts. What he remembered himself was the succession of pauses since the lower bench. He Cen touched the seat and pulled his coat away from the wet edge. To him, this was more than a first sight.

As Fang Ji withdrew his hand, a bag handle caught the notebook's cover, pulling out a corner. He stopped and laid the bag flat. Its old torn seam remained. After so much carrying and lowering, the bottom had folded outward, an empty bottle pressing against the gap.

Lin Mo had asked whether the bag would catch the wheels, not considered that it needed checking on a bench too. He asked Fang Ji not to lift it. Yu Mei didn't rush to take over everything.

“Move the bottles first,” Fang Ji said. “The notebook hasn't torn.”

Using his left hand, he shifted an outer bottle, freed the notebook's corner, and spread the cloth underneath. His injured right palm stayed near his body. Halfway through, the working hand began shaking. Lin Mo asked him to pause.

Xiao Lu offered to hold a cloth corner. Fang Ji indicated the dry side. She used her unscraped hand to steady it while he turned the notebook. The photograph didn't slip. All three copied sheets remained inside the cover, the private fold undisturbed.

The bag hadn't become sturdier. Fang Ji laid its handle aside and said they'd inspect the bottom before lifting again. Calling it light couldn't guarantee it. He withdrew his left index finger from beneath the handle and slowly flexed it twice.

Watching their hands crowded together, Lin Mo realized he'd nearly forgotten they hurt too. He'd thought only of protecting the paper.

“It can stay here. We don't need to move it now.”

Xiao Lu withdrew her hand, cloth marks on her palm, and closed her eyes briefly. She didn't lean toward him for praise. He didn't make the gesture evidence that she was like her old self again. He knew she'd done it now and needed rest afterward. That was enough to see.

The water near the front door shifted.

Nobody had stepped into it. Lin Mo followed the ripples to a thin flow entering from outside and dividing around debris. A reflected light lay too low to match the lights overhead. He tried finding its source until Yu Mei told him not to lean forward.

He hadn't noticed his back leaving the cushion.

“I wasn't going to touch it.”

“I meant your shoulder.”

He settled back. The brightness remained, proving neither a real submerged lamp nor anything else he could name. He Cen moved his foot from an advancing damp mark, irritation briefly stronger than fear.

“Water right beside our feet.”

Nobody finished the thought with permission to drink. Lin Mo heard the dryness of his own swallowing, as though his throat contained a small piece of rough cloth. Xiao Lu opened her mouth slightly, then closed it. Her upturned injured palm rested on her lap, fingers too tired to straighten fully.

Yu Mei inspected the old supply area and found nothing more. Their return hadn't replenished four packets of biscuits already eaten. She looked for something clean enough to place under the papers, then came back empty-handed.

Fang Ji offered to inspect the short stretch along the old outer wall.

“Don't go beyond a corner we can't see,” Yu Mei said.

He followed the dry rear edge empty-handed. Lin Mo could still see his upper body and hear his shoes avoiding water. At the front entrance he stopped and looked down for a long time.

“We can't use it the same way.”

Yu Mei's shoulder was still shaking. Instead of joining him at once, she asked what blocked it.

“The dry strip along the outside wall breaks off. A fallen seat frame is leaning across it. Water underneath.”

He described only what he could see, not the entire route to the receiving hall. Lin Mo asked him to step back and clear the view. The chair stayed on dry ground.

A bent frame showed through the doorway, its feet intersecting shadows in the water. They couldn't flick it upright. One loaded side pinned an old cloth strip; moving it might shift something at the unseen end.

“Don't move it,” Lin Mo said.

Fang Ji's hands were already close to his body. “I wasn't going to.”

Lin Mo apologized. His voice had run ahead of another person's action again. Fang Ji didn't tell him it was fine. He stepped inward, struck his left shoulder against the doorframe, and briefly closed his eyes. Inches of missing foothold forced them back, not a contest in bravery.

For a while, water was the only sound.

“Does that mean we can't get out again?” Xiao Lu asked.

She wasn't crying or speaking loudly. The question still seemed to use up something she'd saved. Lin Mo didn't ask someone else to explain because he couldn't remember the past.

“This stretch isn't passable now. We haven't checked the rest.”

He Cen looked up.

“Don't immediately turn the rest into a way out either.”

“All right.”

Lin Mo accepted the unwelcome qualification. Yu Mei sat, her hand slipping from her shoulder. For now, nobody could go turn a possibility into a complete answer.

Fang Ji still watched from inside the front doorway. Without entering the water, he changed position and pointed to an old pipe high on the wall. Lin Mo saw peeling paint and a section extending outside, its upper half visible above the fallen seat frame.

“When we came back through that short corridor beside the water-tank pipes, they connected in this direction,” Fang Ji said. “This is all I can see from here. I don't know whether it's blocked farther along.”

Yu Mei told him not to call it a shortcut yet. Sealed bottles found near the tanks didn't mean any pipe would lead to more. He knew. He meant they could follow a physical connection during another inspection rather than step into reflected lights to work out the direction.

Lin Mo asked whether the receiving hall lay the other way. Yu Mei described the known outer-wall route. Two things needing inspection couldn't be merged into one destination. They needed something drinkable and a route the wheelchair could use. Finding either wouldn't automatically supply the other.

He left his phone in his pocket. Its remaining charge wasn't fuel to burn casually. Fang Ji didn't spread the technical pages beside the water to draw a neat map either. He returned to dry ground and described the doorway, fallen frame, and visible pipe for everyone to hear.

Lin Mo placed them after the short route forming in his mind.

Xiao Lu suddenly asked whether he remembered the hard concrete ledge.

He hesitated, then found their exchange in the little room. “This one's hard too.”

“I didn't ask you to guess about this one.”

“I remember. You picked up a bottle cap too.”

She lowered her head, breathing a little more slowly, without praising him for recovering. Those things had happened today. Remembering them and still lacking the older days could both be true.

Fang Ji moved the bag farther onto the bench. The notebook stayed above the ridges of flattened plastic beneath the cloth. He took out one large empty bottle, its cap tight, and placed it beside the drier back wall. Then he looked at it and put it away again.

“Not there. Looking at it makes me thirstier.”

Nobody laughed. He Cen, eyes closed, asked him not to crush the bag noisily. Xiao Lu cradled her scraped palm in her other hand. Lin Mo eased his injured index finger away from the hard armrest.

They had reached the lamp-repair room. Outside lay a pipe worth checking, a wall route they couldn't use as before, and distances they lacked the strength to finish immediately.

The bottles were still empty.

Yu Mei didn't promise water the next time she stood. She rested her shoulder first. Lin Mo watched the damp line spread slowly beside the front doorway and remembered how far it remained from the dry ground.
