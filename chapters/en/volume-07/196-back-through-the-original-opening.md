# Chapter 196: Back Through the Original Opening

Xu Qin didn't lift the basket.

From outside the counter, she pointed to the machine’s original intake, then withdrew beside Zhou, wrist clear of the jacket. Her retreat didn't make Sun Cheng collapse or sever the line beneath the bell.

The basket rested flat. Nobody had to inherit the job of holding it forever.

Xiao Lu laid the white slip beside the red clip without inserting it immediately. The yellow and pink copies had crumpled together, collected folded across another corner. She asked to separate them. Xu Qin pointed out the fastening from a distance.

“Don't tear away the later writing,” Lin Mo said.

“I wasn't going to.”

Her good hand separated only the paper protruding beyond the collar. A blue mark crossed the two copies; the white slip completed it. The perforations matched. They weren't writing a refusal over the later acknowledgment, but restoring the missing part of the original set.

Lin Mo disliked the contradictory words. He wanted one clean answer. Sun Cheng had signed; the refusal had preceded him. Fear couldn't make either event disappear.

Xiao Lu clipped the original back beneath the red fastener, refusal facing outward.

Sun Cheng's breathing paused.

“Where does it hurt?” a firefighter asked.

“Not more. Something's a little looser.”

Nobody pulled him. His right arm remained behind him, left foot in place. Xu Qin started his name, then pressed her mouth with the back of her hand and waited.

“I'm not out,” he said.

The low intake had a removable support board, normally used for bagged clothing. Not for anyone to stand on. Zhou and the crew checked its supports and leveled it over the small step at the opening. It didn’t span the two paces from counter to machine. They would have to carry the basket level across that gap.

Xu Qin suddenly offered to give up the machines, anything to stop her husband's pain. She took out keys, clutching them as though surrendering every possession might purchase the next safe step.

Zhou asked her to keep them. This wasn't a sale. The crew didn't accept them either. They needed working space; payment wouldn't decide whether rescue continued.

Her fingers struck the drawer as she replaced the keys. She wanted to stand beside her husband, yet feared leaving nobody to watch the jacket. Xiao Lu said she could see the basket from the side, Zhou from the other. Xu Qin wasn't its only guard.

She stepped back. Her hands no longer had to demonstrate she hadn't abandoned Sun Cheng. He didn't summon her back.

Lin Mo remembered Xiao Lu's empty hospital cup. A memory from days ago, not a recovered piece of an older life. These people also needed someone to take over one small thing they could no longer manage.

He kept the chair to one side. Xiao Lu watched the basket with him. Zhou and a firefighter moved its base; Xu Qin described the original receiving position rather than lifting with her painful wrist.

The faded jacket lay flat. Torn left cuff turned inward, right sleeve intact, red clip at the collar. Lin Mo noted the positions so nobody would think the whole garment had crossed while leaving a sleeve outside.

Zhou and the firefighter carried the basket level across the short gap and rested it on the board. Xu Qin reported no tighter stitches; Sun Cheng could still breathe. Only then did they slide it toward the opening.

“Collected,” a quiet voice said beneath the bell.

Xu Qin jerked her head up. It resembled the end of something Sun Cheng had said, not a full statement. The real man remained visible in the doorway. He shook his head: he hadn't told anyone to take it away.

Lin Mo asked her to watch him instead of completing the counter's fragment. Her shoulders lowered, though she still hardly blinked.

The basket struck the intake's hard edge and stopped.

An old hanger lay crooked across the track, one end caught underneath, the other against dark cloth. Pushing would tip the basket. Zhou withdrew his hands and asked about removing the hanger. The firefighter couldn't see its farther connection and didn't grab blindly. A normal retrieval rod nudged its visible end a fraction.

Sun Cheng coughed. Everyone stopped.

“Your shoulder?” Xu Qin asked.

He shook his head after two breaths. “Cotton. Still in my mouth.”

The crew helped him keep a breathing position and spit out the material near his lips. Nobody told him to suppress coughing for the basket's sake. Damp white fibers fell onto the tile and were collected aside, not used to fill some required box.

Lin Mo studied the hanger again. The dark strip was a torn intake lining with its severed end visible. It hadn't tightened with the nudge. The hanger lay across the track by itself.

That didn't prove every subsequent movement harmless. He indicated only the end he could see. From a better angle, the firefighter checked that nothing else, no part of a person, lay underneath, then slowly turned the hanger aside.

It was placed nearby without a declaration that the entire rule had failed.

The basket approached again. Its leading edge crossed smoothly. The collar and red clip went first, all three slips on the same side. A slight downward slope shifted the jacket forward. The torn left cuff caught on a rough plastic edge.

Xu Qin called for a stop.

Xiao Lu didn't pull the sleeve. She directed the light. Lin Mo saw fabric snagged outside while the collar and paper approached the inner surface. Continuing could leave it in pieces.

“Back a little first.”

Zhou glanced toward Sun Cheng. His shoulder had lowered fractionally. Would reversing tighten it again? Neither Zhou nor Lin Mo knew. They explained the intended movement and kept the crew watching the actual response.

Sun Cheng asked to wait. He wasn't choosing to keep the jacket; coughing had darkened his vision. He needed to see the people at the door again.

Zhou stopped with his hands on the basket. The bell's line promised no pause, but they couldn't call every action necessary while the man couldn't speak.

Xu Qin called his name. He opened his eyes and asked them not to pull his right arm, or lift it suddenly if it loosened. The firefighter repeated both requests and shifted where he could see Sun Cheng's face. Only then did Sun Cheng agree to a very slow attempt.

Lin Mo didn't treat that as permission never to stop again.

The basket retreated a fraction. Sun Cheng said it hurt, without another violent constriction, then asked them to stop. They did. The collar remained visible outside the opening. Nobody snatched it from farther within. Xiao Lu saw tension leave the caught cuff.

From the side, the firefighter freed the snag with the blunt retrieval tip, rather than ripping it loose. The old tear didn't mend. It simply left the rough plastic and was eased back into the basket.

Lin Mo noticed his clenched teeth. He released them, tongue brushing the sore in his cheek. His own small pain couldn't pay for Sun Cheng's.

This time the garment advanced flat, no trailing sleeve. All three slips stayed clipped; no new name made Xu Qin a replacement recipient.

The basket crossed the marked original intake. An old track scraped. The jacket dipped inward.

Nobody dropped the entire basket. The crew stabilized its outer edge while Zhou kept his hands retrievable. The work jacket left the basket along the receiving surface: collar, clip and slips, whole right sleeve, torn left sleeve.

The outer basket was empty.

Lin Mo watched where the last cuff vanished. No human hand followed, nobody supplied the weight missing from an empty garment. Zhou started to advance the basket farther. Lin Mo asked him to hold. What needed to cross had crossed; another step would put hands somewhere unseen.

Zhou slowly withdrew it. Ordinary plastic scraped the support board. Xu Qin seemed to wait for that noise to become a command too. When it ended, she looked back at her husband.

The clapper touched brass without completing a ring. The fine line sagged. Collected didn't speak again.

Xu Qin inspected her wrist. The black stitches hadn't become unmarked skin. They loosened, leaving a broken length at her sleeve and fine red traces beneath. Someone stopped her picking at them and asked her to let medical staff examine them. Remaining pain wasn't an error to dig out with a fingernail.

Sun Cheng's right arm slowly lowered.

Not restored movement all at once. He tried leaving the table, folded forward in pain, and the crew supported him appropriately. Asked whether something still pulled backward, he said that force was gone. His own arm hurt.

Qiu eased the door outward a little. Sun Cheng confirmed it no longer pulled his neck or shoulder; only then did Qiu open it wide enough to help him through. They seated him on a stable chair near the door, without demanding raised arms or a twisted neck to prove recovery. His shoes left the trapped patch of floor. His back left the table. He still coughed, but his movement no longer followed the jacket's sleeves.

Xu Qin cried standing still, as if she'd forgotten she could walk. Xiao Lu cleared the passage and showed her she could approach. She didn't fling herself against his painful shoulder. She held an uninjured part of his left hand.

“Do I have to sign anything else?” he asked.

Nobody put a pen into his hand.

Qiu crouched where Sun could see his face. “Medical staff will ask your name. That isn't this shop asking you to collect anything. You can ask them what each form is for.”

Sun looked from him to the counter. He didn't answer immediately. Xu Qin began to supply his name, then stopped when he lifted his left fingers. He said it himself, hoarsely, without turning toward the intake.

Lin Mo waited through the pause. A rescued person didn't have to become easy to reassure. Qiu repeated where they were taking him, and let him hear the answer twice.

Lin Mo looked at the intake. The jacket had passed beyond the visible section. That didn't establish its destination or prove every abnormality had left the shop. He asked the crew to keep the equipment isolated, not let anyone feed in more clothes as a test.

The shallow basket was set aside with its rough edge intact. Xu Qin had never noticed that burr before. Lin Mo didn't promise noticing it would prevent future trouble. He asked her to lower her hand and have the red marks treated.

Xiao Lu stood beside the chair.

“Do you really want to know what else is inside?”

He watched the dark intake for several breaths. “Yes. Not now.”

She nodded and cleared the route before his footrests. Zhou waited behind him until he could see them clearly, then released the brakes.

As Sun Cheng was helped outward, Xu Qin glanced at the empty counter. She didn't turn the sign back to open. She left the keys for those securing the scene to check and explained that the power remained disconnected, so nobody arriving later would restore it by mistake.

At the doorway, Xiao Lu waited for room to cross the threshold. Lin Mo didn't hurry her. Outside air moved an actual old apron hanging near the door. It wasn't waving on anyone's behalf.

This time they took away a person seated in a chair.
