# Chapter 185: Uphill Took Something Else

The metallic knock came again.

Lin Mo watched the ramp, trying to find someone approaching beyond its dark bend. No footsteps followed. A long silence preceded the next knock, so faint He Cen's breathing nearly covered it.

Fang Ji offered to look. He left the bag beside the bench. Yu Mei told him to stay within the lamp's reach, and aimed the wheelchair's light up the lower slope. He walked empty-handed to the edge of the beam, looked down, and returned.

“A thin metal edge sticking up. I can't see beyond it.”

He hadn't vanished around the corner in pursuit of a complete answer. Irritation showed in his face. Those few steps had pulled the pain in his left shoulder toward his neck; he pressed it briefly.

He Cen didn't prepare to rise. Wasn't there water up there? Why go back? Fang Ji began explaining that water leaking into the lamp-repair room wasn't drinking water. He Cen shook his head.

“I asked why we're going back. Not whether we can drink it.”

Lin Mo looked at Yu Mei. She was watching the slope, not pretending she'd decided for everyone.

“We've checked the route to the receiving hall from there,” she said. “At least we know where the entrance is. Looking elsewhere down here means a route we haven't used. We still have to check how high the water's risen, and whether the entrance has opened.”

“What about where you found the bottles?”

“We brought everything we could use. I can't promise another five.”

He Cen removed his glasses and pressed the bridge of his nose. He didn't accept an imagined supply of water, but neither did he ask to be carried deeper in. His gray coat's hem brushed the floor as he sank lower on the hard bench.

“I'll go up. But at the lamp-repair room, water and the way back come first. You can't expect me to follow every time someone points out another line going down.”

Yu Mei agreed. Fang Ji nodded too, accepting his share of the promise. He Cen replaced his glasses, waiting for things to become clear before turning toward the wall. Xiao Lu didn't suggest they'd done enough for her already, or remove herself from their number with a plea to leave her behind.

Lin Mo asked whether He Cen needed his coat gathered out of the way. He looked down and slowly freed it from beneath him so he wouldn't tread on it when he stood.

“I'll ask if I need help. Let me try.”

Lin Mo waited. He couldn't remember earlier agreements about returning, but he'd heard this one clearly. Knowing him didn't oblige He Cen to follow his direction forever.

Yu Mei braced herself on the bench before standing. When Xiao Lu rose too, she asked her to wait while she turned the chair toward the ramp. Lin Mo disliked everyone clearing space for him again. But the wheels needed it. He didn't turn his discomfort into a polite demand that they stop helping.

The light revealed a strip of metal against the wall, one end curled away from a rusty screw. Air from above lifted it and let it tap the hard edge beneath, a short sound like teeth clicking.

“At least there's something making this noise,” Yu Mei said.

Lin Mo nodded without extending that reassurance around the unseen bend.

They didn't tear the strip off to make a wheel chock. It was too thin to grasp with injured hands or trust beneath a tire. Yu Mei slowly pressed it toward the wall with her sole and watched after lifting her foot. It sprang back a little but stayed out of the wheels' path.

Dust softened old wheel tracks on the rougher slope. A dark streak followed the wall. Yu Mei illuminated it and said it might be wet; they should avoid it. Lin Mo saw no droplets. If he stared too long, his mouth would begin inventing a taste.

“Up is harder than down,” she said.

He nearly answered that he knew. Knowing spared her shoulder nothing. He asked where they could first stop.

“A wider spot before the bend. Let's see whether we can straighten the chair and stop safely there. We'll deal with the second bend later.”

He Cen stood, paused at the bench with a hand against his forehead, then followed the wall. The seatkeeper let him pass and dragged his own sore leg after him. Xiao Lu moved between them, keeping her scraped palm off the rough surface.

Fang Ji collected the bag last. He didn't try carrying it while helping push. He kept it low on the wall side, putting it down after a few steps to wait, dividing a light burden into short distances.

Yu Mei began to push.

The slope was gentle, but the chair's changed angle was immediate. Lin Mo's weight settled backward. His right knee felt as though it would slide over the footplate. He tried tightening his leg, met pain, and told Yu Mei his foot wasn't comfortable.

She stopped a little way onto the slope, controlling the chair until she could secure the brakes. Before letting go, she checked that the tires weren't sliding. Then she moved beside him.

His right shoe remained on its plate. He'd said his foot had slipped when what he needed was for his whole body to stop bracing itself. Once he explained, she didn't pull his leg. She let him settle against the cushion and wait for the sudden tension to ease.

“Back to the platform?”

The bench was still close. He wanted to say they needn't stop so often. But she'd asked about his body, not whether he could seem less troublesome.

“Wait here. Let it settle.”

She gave him the time. He Cen stopped against the wall. Xiao Lu stayed below rather than squeezing past. Fang Ji lowered the bag, looking back as though searching for another place to sit.

Eventually the sharp pain receded to its familiar dullness. Lin Mo said they could continue, but Yu Mei kept the brakes on.

“I need another moment.”

His turn to wait.

When they moved again, she used shorter efforts, setting her feet before each push. She kept the knot in her sleeve clear of anything that might snag it. Through the chair, Lin Mo felt her strength fluctuate. She wasn't a machine set to a constant speed.

He wanted to catch the wheel rim. His left shoulder hurt as he began reaching, so he kept his hand inside the armrest. Helping couldn't mean giving her two bodies to steady.

At the first wider spot, she straightened the chair onto more level ground before releasing the handles. She eased down the wall a little without sitting, eyes closed as she caught her breath.

He Cen looked ill in the low light. He said he wanted water, then waved Fang Ji away from the bag. He knew what was inside. The request still escaped him.

Xiao Lu licked her lips and frowned. A dry white line marked their edge. Lin Mo's hand stopped on its way to his pocket. He had no tissues, nothing to hand over that would solve it.

“Did I always have something for you?”

She took a moment to understand he meant his empty hand.

“Not always. Sometimes you'd say, when we get back.”

He waited for more. She looked up toward the bend without demanding he honor the old words. They could have been a promise, or the postponement of someone who could do nothing then. He couldn't choose the more flattering explanation for a self he didn't remember.

“I don't have anything now either.”

“I can see.”

She didn't ask him to turn out his pockets. She found another piece of wall to lean against.

The seatkeeper arrived last, scarcely bending his left leg. He saw Yu Mei resting, opened his mouth, and didn't offer to push. Eventually he warned them that the gate above sprang shut. They mustn't hold it open with a person.

“I know,” Yu Mei said. “Don't walk under it to demonstrate.”

He nodded. Useful advice didn't make him their guide, or make his leg hurt less.

After the rest, Fang Ji advanced a few steps inside the bend, keeping the others in sight. The old painted arrow pointed toward the lower level. They were going against it. For an instant, Lin Mo felt as if he were doing a completed task backward.

Then he watched the caster cross an old wheel track. The arrow belonged to an earlier purpose; it didn't decide who should return. He needed no new arrow pointing up to justify leaving.

They couldn't turn directly at the corner. Yu Mei stopped, and Fang Ji indicated a protrusion in the wall. He Cen and Xiao Lu moved to a wider patch inside the turn, clearing room for the footplate. The seatkeeper waited against the wall behind.

Lin Mo's shoe brushed dusty plaster, leaving a pale line. Yu Mei stopped at once. He checked his foot. The shoe remained on the plate; the wall hadn't struck his knee. He explained, but she still reversed slightly and corrected the wheels before continuing.

Beyond the first bend, the bench vanished.

He knew the wall had hidden it. He tried joining the recent places in order: the wider patch, the flaking plaster, the metal strip pressed aside below. They weren't old autobiographical memories. They were already a sequence of things he'd experienced.

That reassured him briefly, then made him fear losing them the next time he closed his eyes. Yu Mei asked about his leg. He had to answer about the leg first. He couldn't check his entire self at once.

Damp air reached them on the second section. The streak beside the wall broadened. Yu Mei stopped on dry ground and lit its edge. A thin flow ran down from above, split around a rough projection, and soaked into the dust near their feet.

Fang Ji bent to inspect it rather than crouching. No sealed opening, no container, no view of its source. He thought it might be leakage from higher up. He couldn't tell how much it would rise.

He Cen asked whether they could catch it in an empty bottle, then added that he didn't mean drinking it yet. Nobody laughed. There wasn't even a steady dripping point. The water clung to dirty concrete; a bottle's mouth would have to scrape dust to reach it.

“Let's look farther up,” Yu Mei said. “Leave this for now.”

She didn't promise cleaner water above. Lin Mo looked from the divided wet streak to the bottles bulging inside Fang Ji's bag. An empty container could make being full seem only one action away.

They didn't move immediately. Yu Mei needed this relatively level transition for another rest. He Cen's legs were shaking too. The second bend remained above them. The gate was still unseen.

Xiao Lu leaned against the wall and looked at Lin Mo.

“Did you remember the route?”

“A little. The part we just did.”

“Then remember where it's dry here.”

He looked down. Pale dust beside the left wheel remained untouched by water, its edge ragged. He remembered the shape without treating it as a permanent marker. Going up required strength. So did staying here. Having traveled this route before couldn't spare them either cost.
