# Chapter 197: Eat While It's Hot

The light outside the laundry seemed brighter than Lin Mo remembered.

His knee couldn't tell him how long he'd spent inside. Pain rose from the footrest; his left shoulder felt hot beneath his clothes. He left his braced wrist alone and asked Zhou to park against the wall, out of everyone's way.

Xiao Lu crouched in front of him to check his shoes.

“It's not touching,” he said.

“I meant here.”

He followed her finger. His trouser hem had caught in a narrow gap. She freed it without pressing his knee or telling him he didn't even know what condition he was in.

A delivery driver sounded his horn. Sun Cheng flinched, and the medical worker asked him not to hurry. Xu Qin held his left hand, her own wrist untreated. Each insisted the other should be seen first, until someone brought her a second chair.

Sun still coughed between words. Lin Mo didn't answer the medical questions for him. The pulling force had stopped; the shoulder and neck it had strained weren't necessarily all right.

Qiu came out without the shallow basket.

“We're leaving it as it is. Someone's watching the place. Nobody's touching that intake.”

“Not reaching in for the jacket?”

“No. Sun can't tell us where it came from. I'm not sending a hand after an answer.”

He crouched beside Lin Mo rather than asking Zhou everything over the wheelchair. He asked permission to go through the sequence again before opening his notebook.

Xiao Lu placed the water bottle beside Lin Mo's left hand, cap already loosened. He drank, then began with the white paper beneath the table foot. The torn edges and faded blue line matched. It said refused and return through the original opening. They hadn't erased the later collected entry. He described stopping when the cuff caught, listening when Sun said it hurt.

“So returning it releases the person?”

“It released him this time. I didn't see who filled in those slips, or where the jacket finally went.”

Qiu crossed out a line and rewrote it.

Lin Mo watched without asking him to tear up the page. This wasn't the repeated collected beneath the counter. Someone had misheard, asked and corrected himself. Both things involving paper didn't make paper itself a command.

“Have you done this work long?”

Lin Mo's thumb rested against the bottle. Asked differently, that question could summon many answers other people had supplied: special forces, his sister, the structure holding people up inside the Theater. What he could describe himself was what he'd witnessed these past few days.

“I can't give you a reliable account of before. Today I came with Zhou. Please don't write that I can guarantee a solution.”

Qiu waited for him to finish, then nodded. Zhou sighed behind the chair without supplying a more impressive identity.

Someone approached to ask whether the laundry would reopen that afternoon. A customer wanted his washing. Xu Qin looked up at once: those were last week's sheets, nothing to do with this.

Then she stopped herself.

Qiu didn't send her inside to prove the sheets safe. He had someone explain the suspension and take the customer's contact details. Xu Qin finally stopped trying to stand.

The customer sounded angry about waiting. Xu Qin gripped her sleeve, as though enough apologies might make the man leave.

“Have your wrist seen to,” Lin Mo told her.

“He’s a regular.”

“That doesn't make your hand the price of keeping him happy right now.”

She didn't smile. Slowly she released her sleeve and let the medical worker inspect the hanging thread. He didn't wait for thanks, or make her admit he'd touched something painful.

As Sun was helped into the vehicle, he turned for his wife. She lifted her bag and said she was coming. It struck the doorframe; she looked down in alarm. The open bag showed tissues and half a packet of biscuits. No blue jacket showed.

Xiao Lu watched them leave before returning to the wheelchair.

“Can we get noodles now?”

Lin Mo almost said after the remaining questions. Then he noticed the water mark on her coat. She'd spent the visit bending, examining paper and moving aside. None of them had eaten. His hot shoulder couldn't reliably measure what strength remained either.

“Yes.”

Zhou dealt with the remaining arrangements and wheeled them away. A small hollow jolted a front caster. Lin Mo clenched his teeth. Zhou stopped, moved onto smoother ground and asked whether they should go straight back to hospital.

“Let me stop a moment.”

Not no. Not I'm fine. Xiao Lu stood beside the chair so a cyclist couldn't cut through the gap. She didn't grab his handlebars. He grumbled and rode around.

When the sharper knee pain passed, Lin Mo pointed to a noodle shop with a level entrance. Zhou first checked that the table near the door could be cleared. Only after a chair was moved did he bring them in.

The table shone where grease had been wiped away. A wall fan rattled. Xiao Lu looked at her hands, then hid them in her lap.

“Wash them if you want.”

She looked up.

“I don't mean dirty. You were gripping the paper edges.”

Water ran twice at the little sink. When she returned, Zhou had ordered three bowls, adding chili to nobody's.

Lin Mo checked the menu prices and asked for his phone. Xiao Lu took it from her inner pocket. It still had charge after being plugged in at the hospital; they hadn't kept the screen lit.

His fingers paused over the payment screen. It wasn't that he didn't recognize the numbers. The armrest obstructed his left arm, and leaning wasn't easy. She moved the phone closer and turned it toward him rather than completing the payment herself.

Only when the confirmation sounded did she shift the water cup in front of her inward.

“Xu Qin wanted to give you money,” Zhou said. “I told her to see a doctor first. What do you think?”

The noodles hadn't arrived. Lin Mo studied the uneven chopsticks in their holder.

“We never agreed a fee. Don't charge her for getting him out now.”

“I'm not saying ask her. But you can't go every time someone calls. Travel, meals, treatment—all cost money. And there's Xiao Lu.”

She looked up at her name.

No white slip could be returned to solve this. Refusing to name a rescue price at the door didn't mean their future time, bodies and living costs were free to spend.

“Future work gets discussed beforehand. Whether I come, what I'll do, what it costs. Emergencies go to the appropriate services first. Nobody waits for me.”

Zhou nodded and put his phone facedown.

“May I give people your number?”

“Ask me first. Hearing about today doesn't mean they can send everything here.”

Zhou's faint smile wasn't quite relief. It looked more like hearing that Lin Mo would leave an opening he could close himself.

The noodles arrived. Xiao Lu nudged a leaf from the rim into her soup, tasted a mouthful and frowned.

“Too salty?”

“Hot. Don't eat so fast either.”

He lowered his first mouthful. She made a brief sound of laughter, almost afraid a louder one might pull tight whatever had finally loosened.

He didn't demand an explanation. He waited for the steam to disperse. His awkward left-handed grip dropped the noodles back. She pushed the spoon closer: he could start with broth. She didn't have to feed every mouthful to him.

Halfway through, Zhou's phone lit up. He read without passing it around. Xu Qin said they'd reached the hospital and were being examined. No results yet.

“All right,” Lin Mo said.

Being examined wasn't recovered. He didn't call to ask after the jacket. Someone wanted a takeaway order; the fan kept rattling. The water delivery truck outside had gone.

Xiao Lu held a leaf beneath her spoon without eating. Asked whether it was still hot, she shook her head.

“Inside, once, I didn't hear you call.”

“I called again.”

“I know. If I don't hear, it doesn't mean I don't want to help.”

He laid his chopsticks down. Her quiet words couldn't simply be covered with you're overthinking. He'd seen her concealing something while she held the slips beside the machine. Seeing didn't entitle him to an explanation.

“You're allowed not to hear. You're also allowed to hear and not want to go in. Next time I ask. Being beside me doesn't count as agreeing.”

She finally looked up, as if checking whether those words could remain.

He couldn't supply every promise from a brother in somebody else's memory. Nor would he promise there'd never be danger again. The shop was only streets away. They were still trembling here.

“Was there something today you didn't want to do?”

After a long silence she said watching the bell. The longer she waited, the nearer its ringing seemed.

“Then I won't ask you to watch a bell next time.”

“I'm not saying we should try it again.”

“I know. Not to prove you aren't afraid.”

She ate the leaf slowly. Zhou hadn't interrupted. He turned the fan slightly so it stopped driving steam into her face.

Later Lin Mo checked his follow-up appointment and showed her the screen, giving her the actual time instead of merely saying there were things to do tomorrow. She read it aloud and asked whether she could sleep when they got back.

Yes. He and Zhou would sort the documents they needed. She wouldn't have to remind them of each item before resting.

Zhou took out a receipt, turned it over and suggested keeping a short record of today. Lin Mo asked him to note the address, who'd called, everyone present, that Sun and Xu Qin had gone for examinations, and that the laundry hadn't reopened.

“Should we give the case a name?”

Lin Mo shook his head.

“Get the people's names right first.”

Zhou checked the names he'd been given and tucked the receipt into his notebook. It wasn't an old diary. It recovered no piece of Lin Mo's past. Something had happened today, and someone was willing to record it accurately.

Xiao Lu gathered the last short strand of noodles and asked whether they could turn the phone's sound off for a while at home.

Lin Mo lowered the ringtone himself, keeping the screen where incoming calls could be seen. Hospital arrangements still mattered. They couldn't pretend nobody could reach them. But every sound didn't have to enter through her shoulders first.

“Let's rest awhile.”

She nodded and ate the last mouthful.

There was still warm broth in her bowl. His own noodles hadn't gone cold. He adjusted his left-handed grip to make it easier, leaving the injured wrist supported where it was instead of recruiting it to finish a simple meal.

This time the chopsticks held.
