# Chapter 201: The Words Did Not Bring Him Back

The gray line said maintenance annotations.

The screen rod's shadow hid the rest. Han Wei insisted his editing software had no such function. The ordinary console remained disconnected. He spoke faster, as though admitting he didn't recognize something might make all his previous work look deliberately concealed.

“Just tell us what you can see,” Lin Mo said.

Liang had the rod steadied while the light shifted. More words emerged: retrieval index, written characters, no receiving endpoint. They sat outside the narrow control strip, separated by darkness from the large invitation to correct the statement on somebody else's behalf.

No receiving endpoint tightened something beneath Lin Mo's ribs.

He understood the words. That didn't mean he remembered encountering them. His body had already started forward; pain in his left shoulder brought him back against the chair.

“Don't lean toward it,” Xiao Lu said.

He nodded. From where she already stood, could she see anything about a compatible, functioning access point?

She searched, then found it. Actual retrieval required. Her voice stayed steady, though her hands gripped the bottom of her jacket. She didn't approach the large white box to press anything for him.

Zhou brought the bag beside Lin Mo's left hand and asked whether to open it.

Lin Mo looked at Tang Jing first. Medical staff remained with her. Liang's crew were inspecting the mat beneath her chair; they hadn't suspended useful work to wait for his old words. Her eyes followed the left pedal while another continue squeezed through her throat.

“The sheet with the index first. Nobody has to wait while I look for my past.”

The cloth unfolded around a curled corner of the book. Zhou laid it flat without opening the sealed private fold. Xiao Lu pointed to the back of the outer fair copy. Two separate lines: Diary One and Current Addendum. They mustn't confuse them.

Knowing where the paper lay didn't mean she wanted to discuss its contents. Lin Mo didn't ask. He slowly turned it over. One string of marks ended with a small stroke that would be easy to misread.

Perhaps the access point was false. Perhaps familiar text would persuade him to hand over the whole book. Keeping the sheet toward himself, he asked whether the narrow strip required uploading the original or accepting the larger confirmation beside it.

“No. Only an index,” Han said.

“Then don't give it the book.”

Liang asked what retrieving it might accomplish.

“We need to distinguish something displaying other people's words from an access point that can retrieve text separately. That big correction box joins reading on someone's behalf to their personal confirmation. I won't use it as though they're the same. This paper has an index to text stored before. We can compare. Reading it correctly won't make every other operation trustworthy.”

Liang kept the crew working beside the chair. One person, already able to see the strip safely, could assist; everyone else didn't need to surround the book.

Lin Mo read the index a character at a time. Xiao Lu checked the paper, not the diary's prose. Han entered the characters into the small retrieval field and stopped at the final ambiguous stroke, asking for another check.

They didn't open the writing function. Tang Jing's name didn't enter this record.

The strip darkened, then several fine lines appeared.

DIARY ONE.

The opening described Lin Mo's account at the time of writing. Some details remained unverified. It wasn't a complete memory.

The name matched the one he knew. Below it, the text said his body had been changed against his will. When the record was made, he still understood himself as a man, his sister's brother.

His breathing became shallow. He looked once toward Xiao Lu, then back.

She didn't nod to certify it. He didn't ask whether it was true and make her become the page's witness.

The words continued. If his future self didn't share those feelings, he could stop. He needn't hurt his body to prove the paper true. His wrist, shoulder and right knee couldn't bear normal loads. Help from other people wasn't a debt that required repayment with himself.

He looked down at the fixed wrist. The pain hadn't diminished. No picture of the injury returned. He knew only that somebody had expected him to forget and had still written that he could stop.

That person had used his name.

Farther down lay a smaller caution.

POSSESSING THIS TEXT DOES NOT ENTITLE ANYONE TO MAKE MY DECISIONS FOR ME.

His left thumb pressed the back of the sheet. He wanted to carry the sentence over to the white box and declare everything there invalid. But these were words he had left behind. No door opened because of them. Tang Jing kept speaking.

He asked Han to remain on this page.

“This text doesn't ask somebody else to confirm it,” he told Liang. “The big box keeps asking Mr. Luo to confirm the person reading for him. It's stitching separate actions together.”

Liang looked at the old man. His right hand rose halfway while the left gripped the chair. Nothing about him expressed agreement.

“Then we leave his confirmation alone.”

He turned to Tang and asked whether she wanted to end only her own reading. She shut her eyes urgently, tears running down her cheeks. Liang waited until she could focus on him and asked again. He didn't ask Luo to answer for her.

Han withdrew his hand from the large box. He'd been searching for a corrected, finished film, as though Luo's eventual approval were the only way his work could end. Now he crossed waiting for personal confirmation from his notes and wrote that Tang wanted to stop.

Something in Lin Mo's shoulder eased as he watched the pen.

The crew reported the mat's position: a corner trapped beneath a chair leg. Dragging the whole chair wouldn't safely free it. Liang asked medical staff to support Tang while the others inspected how to relieve only the pressure trapping the fabric.

Lin Mo's old words hadn't moved anything for them.

The large white box flickered.

Beside Luo's still face, another line declared that the reader had agreed to continue. Tang shook her head, while her mouth kept repeating her willingness. Han glanced from her to the screen and started extending his notes, almost trying to explain her movement through the film again.

“Watch her,” Liang said.

He didn't first require proof that Lin Mo's diary was genuine. Her actual refusal mattered. Medical staff asked whether she wanted the chair work stopped. She didn't nod; her gaze remained on the caption. Asked separately whether she rejected its claim that she wanted to continue, she closed her eyes.

Liang acknowledged that he'd misunderstood. She wouldn't have to explain why she wasn't cooperating when the fault lay in the question. Han slowly brought the paper back against his chest.

Lin Mo understood the large display more clearly through that exchange than through seeing his own name. Different answers were being arranged into its preferred sequence. The diary couldn't decide for Tang. Her refusal couldn't be moved sideways to answer an old interview for Luo.

The gray box stayed. Someone outside asked if they'd found a solution; the person guarding the entrance moved them farther away. Lin Mo didn't promise soon.

His knee ached more heavily. He asked Zhou to adjust the outer edge of the cushion without touching the support. Zhou crouched where he could see the footrest and followed his directions. Pain remained; Lin Mo asked him to stop there. Pretending to sit comfortably now would leave his body to pay for the journey home.

Xiao Lu asked whether he needed to retreat toward the window. Liang was preparing the mat; none of Lin Mo's hands had to remain suspended over an operation. Once the frame was steadied, they could move back, keeping a view without crowding forward.

She didn't tell him his former self would have persevered, or decide he was finished for the day. They cleared the route behind his chair. A page slid toward the board's edge and Zhou caught it by a blank margin, first checking he hadn't pressed a folded insert.

The papers remained in place. Nobody opened the sealed fold to make them seem more important.

Lin Mo returned his attention to the fair copy. Its opening resembled the retrieved text, but the letters were small and the light oblique. He couldn't pretend to have checked every line. Turning the edge exposed a passage about an umbrella.

I took her umbrella before she'd agreed to lend it, then came back expecting her to put it away.

He read slowly.

No rain returned inside his head. No former hand overlaid his unfamiliar one. There was only the line, correcting an earlier account that had omitted too much. The writer had first placed the trouble in his sister's refusal to take the umbrella. Later he'd put his own unpermitted borrowing back into it.

Xiao Lu stood beside him without looking at the page.

He opened his mouth to ask whether she remembered, then stopped. Somebody behind them was still being forced to answer for another person's statement. A gentler voice wouldn't excuse putting his urgency onto his sister.

“I won't ask now.”

She looked at him. “You don't have to ask about that later, either.”

He nodded, without changing later into once you're ready, you must tell me.

Han asked about closing retrieval. Lin Mo requested a moment. Zhou could use his own ordinary notebook to copy the title, the opening they'd actually retrieved, and the caution about decisions. Not the whole book. Not Xiao Lu's answer.

“Does that prove it's your original?” Zhou asked.

“It records what we read here. We'll compare it line by line with the paper at home.”

Zhou wrote against the board without borrowing Tang's lap board. Lin Mo checked the sentence. He didn't add so I've come back.

Retrieval closed to an empty field. No new voice answered from the book or gray strip. The reading continued in the center. Its end control remained below, not overwritten by the diary.

Lin Mo asked Zhou to wrap the book. He didn't want its photograph dropped during the next movement, or his wish for another piece of the past to occupy a present somebody was struggling to end.

A sealed inner fold emerged as the book closed. Zhou stopped at its edge. Lin Mo told him it wasn't part of this diary. It went back unopened, yielding no miraculous set of answers.

Tang made a quiet choking sound.

The crew paused the chair work while medical staff helped her recover. Her left hand pressed her chest, then lowered. Her right foot stayed on the pedal; the board was gone from her knees.

She watched the left side. For the first time, her urgency pointed somewhere particular rather than toward a roomful of faces.

“She's looking at End,” Xiao Lu said.

Liang asked whether she wanted to try her left foot. Tang kept her eyes open. They waited instead of supplying the expected answer. She looked at the mat, then back at Liang: it wasn't clear yet.

“Mat first.”

Lin Mo felt how nearly willingness had become ability right now. His hand settled on the armrest. He wouldn't finish her answer.

Zhou closed his notebook inside his jacket. The copied words were silent, heavy enough that Lin Mo wouldn't readily forget them. He wanted to know what had happened to the umbrella. First the person before him needed to get out of a sentence without an ending.

He watched the small gap beside the chair and waited for Liang's fingers to withdraw safely.
