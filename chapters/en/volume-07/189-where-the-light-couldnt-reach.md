# Chapter 189: Where the Light Couldn't Reach

The empty seat's armrest turned toward them.

Only slightly, enough to deflect the white beam. Lin Mo's right shoe pulled outward against the footrest. He tried drawing his leg back. Pain stabbed through his knee, and a wheel made a small noise.

Yu Mei seized the handles and hauled the chair farther behind the wall. The brakes remained on, yet the tires dragged a pale mark across the brick.

“Don't brace with your legs.”

He relaxed them. Xiao Lu reached toward his footrest, frightened by the movement, but Yu Mei called her back before she touched it. Her scraped palm brushed her clothes as she withdrew. She hadn't caught some invisible thing and pulled him free.

The light left the wheel. The pull weakened.

Yu Mei waited until everything stopped before leaning against the wall. Sweat ran toward her eye. The knot in her torn sleeve had drawn tight.

“Was it like this before?” Lin Mo asked.

Fang Ji nodded. “People and chairs, toward the seats when the light catches them. We used canvas.”

A scrap still hung across the hall. Its earlier usefulness didn't make it wide enough now, or oblige them to retrieve it.

Wheel tracks broke off behind two rows of seats. Between them lay exposed floor. White light swept around the seats, paused, and returned. The distant corridor escaped some sweeps, but they couldn't reach it directly.

“Wait for it to turn, then hurry?” He Cen suggested, barely able to give hurry any force.

Yu Mei glanced at her shoulder.

Lin Mo counted breaths between sweeps. The next arrived sooner. They had neither a reliable interval nor people capable of sprinting.

“We can't use one pass as a timetable.”

The seatkeeper kept his injured leg close and avoided looking at the seats. He muttered that they might not all want the same kind of person. Nobody asked him to test one. His face lifted as if he expected the suggestion, then turned away.

Fang Ji indicated two old partitions behind the maintenance sign. One was narrow; the other had a split lower edge. Thin rigid screens, not canvas, and certainly not load-bearing structures.

“Some shade.”

“Can you carry one?” Yu Mei asked.

He examined the narrow panel. Taller than his shoulder, old nails along its edge. His right palm couldn't grip it and his left shoulder couldn't lift it. He nudged the bottom with his shoe, leaving its upper end against the wall.

A strip of floor appeared behind it. The shadow moved with the panel. Lin Mo thought of the light in the water with no visible source, then separated the observations. Something real stood between this shadow and the beam.

“What if it leaned instead?”

Two fixed feet behind a nearby seat could prevent the panel sliding inward. They couldn't support a person. Yu Mei proposed shifting it close enough to check for light leaking around it, not using it as a bridge.

Fang Ji tried steadying the upper edge, gasped, and withdrew. Xiao Lu offered her uninjured hand lower down. Yu Mei had her wait until the top was controlled.

They didn't crowd every injured hand around the nails. Yu Mei held a clear side, controlling the base with her foot. Xiao Lu gave a small push below, then withdrew when it settled between the fixed feet. Fang Ji watched without using his leg as a stop.

The beam came around.

Brightness showed above the panel. Below, the floor remained dark. Lin Mo's shoulders and wheels fit in its shadow, but Yu Mei's standing face would be exposed. Bending pulled at her injury.

“I can't push like that all the way.”

The shaded stretch ended at a wall recess where she could straighten. Lin Mo described it. She checked for herself before agreeing to try. They weren't covering the whole hall, only connecting the next short stretch.

Fang Ji crossed empty-handed first and checked the recess's floor. He Cen followed, stopping once and waiting for the light to pass. The seatkeeper didn't force past him. Xiao Lu watched the panel's base as she went, never reaching toward the sinking seat.

The chair moved last.

Lin Mo relaxed his right leg, keeping his shoe on the support rather than trying to resist the pull with it. The front wheels entered the shade without swerving. As the rear wheels followed, something tapped the panel.

“Sit here.”

The voice seemed pressed against its far side. Lin Mo didn't twist to look. Yu Mei didn't argue with it. A few steps brought the chair into the recess, where she braked it and finally straightened.

Fang Ji fetched the bag and set it on a raised edge, old cloth underneath. All six crowded into the narrow space. Light crossed the panel and marked the top of the wall. Nobody pronounced them safe.

The brick pressed into He Cen's side. He shifted; his glasses case clicked in his pocket. Beyond the shade, the seat sank farther.

“It's soft here.”

He stared at it. Yu Mei didn't grab him. She stood in his route and asked whether his legs were giving out.

“I know what it is,” he said hoarsely. “I just want to sit.”

The cushion seemed already shaped for his weight. Lin Mo, who at least had a chair, couldn't tell him to endure from behind an armrest. He asked whether the recess floor was dry enough. Yu Mei swept debris aside with her shoe, revealing intact flooring.

He Cen lowered himself along the wall. She supported his forearm while the chair stayed braked. His gray coat pressed against dirty masonry. He breathed out, buried his face in his arms, and ignored the offer repeating beyond them.

The seatkeeper stood at the far edge of the shade. “Someone used to ask like that. Just to sit.”

He didn't say who. Lin Mo didn't turn the moment into a confession they must all hear.

“If you remember something specific, tell someone who can investigate when we're out.”

“I might not remember all of it.”

“Then what you remember. Don't fix the rest.”

The man lowered his head. Xiao Lu yielded a little wall space without touching his leg. At his thanks, she only asked him not to step on the bag.

After resting, He Cen wanted to rise himself. Yu Mei waited for his feet to settle before offering an arm. The seat repeated its invitation. He spat toward it, scarcely producing moisture.

“Keep it.”

A half-fallen curtain blocked the next stretch. Its top remained on a rod; the lower edge lay in water. Yu Mei touched a dry edge with her shoe. Something submerged moved. She withdrew at once.

They couldn't pull the whole curtain. Lin Mo followed a taut strip around a seat foot and into the water. He wouldn't discover its far end by using He Cen's weight.

“Could we get behind it?” Xiao Lu asked.

“There was a route,” Fang Ji said. “Can't see it now.”

She bit her lip without turning was into is.

Between a seat back and the actual wall, dry ground connected the panel's shadow to the corridor. An extended footboard blocked the narrow part. A person could step over it; the wheelchair couldn't. It belonged to the seat frame, not an obstruction they could simply tear out.

Lin Mo asked for a brief look at its connection.

The clamp lamp revealed an exposed hinge and a broken wooden wedge underneath. Fang Ji moved the wedge with his shoe, keeping his hand clear. Yu Mei pressed the board gently. It folded halfway, then caught.

Lin Mo's left hand twitched. An old cord knot strained outside the hinge. No secret control cable: something had once tied the board up, then jammed when it slipped.

“Take your foot off first. Then see whether the knot moves.”

Fang Ji tried the loose end farthest from the hinge, but the water-stiffened knot resisted. His shoulder couldn't maintain the posture. Xiao Lu asked whether Yu Mei could steady her while she crouched. Yu Mei needed to return the chair to the recess before freeing her hands.

They moved back.

Xiao Lu lowered herself along the wall and picked at the knot with her good hand, scraped palm upturned. When her knees began shaking she stopped and rose. The knot was looser, not undone.

“So close,” she said angrily, breathing hard.

“It can stay a little looser,” Lin Mo said.

Fang Ji took the loosened end and finally pulled it through. The cord remained beside the seat, not a new rescue tool for their bag. Yu Mei folded the footboard underneath. Clearance opened.

Lin Mo remembered telling her to remove her foot, and watching her do it. He couldn't attach the habit to a training day. His attention had found the loaded part before he could name why. That didn't mean he still knew everything.

They crossed in stages. Panel shadow and seat backs blocked most of the light. Yu Mei waited for the brightest band to move before pushing through the last exposed stretch. It wasn't a passage to take with closed eyes. A wheel brushed a seat foot; pain numbed Lin Mo's knee.

But they reached the corridor.

Yu Mei pushed beyond the solid corner. A whole wall now stood between them and the white beam. Fang Ji retrieved the bag again. He Cen sat inside; the seatkeeper dragged his leg over the last dry brick. Xiao Lu came alongside the chair, inspecting her palm without asking whether they were outside yet.

Ahead lay a shallow gray-white surface.

Two rows of old screw holes enclosed it. A narrow iron edge crossed its base. Lin Mo couldn't remember entering, but the shape matched what Yu Mei had described. A separate ordinary iron door stood to its right.

He didn't reach to push the pale surface.

“It looked like that last time,” Fang Ji said.

Yu Mei stared, then quietly wondered whether it remained closed. Lin Mo was about to say he didn't know when a crooked dark line appeared at its lower corner. Something covered it, then moved aside.

A cough followed.

Not the voice that had invited him to sit. Difference alone didn't make it human. He watched a shoe stop beyond the dark line, ordinary cement dust along its edge.

“Is anyone still in there?”

Yu Mei lifted her head. Lin Mo didn't send everyone rushing forward. He asked her to stop the chair short of the iron strip and described their position.

“Six people. Injured. The wheelchair hasn't crossed.”

The shoe stayed outside. Dust shifted beneath its heel, leaving a mark on the cement beyond the narrow strip. The person asked whether they could hear clearly and told them to remain where they were visible. Lin Mo looked back at the other five, checking that each remained in the passage, before answering yes.
