# Chapter 186: Check the White Lines Again

The wet streak hadn't reached the pale dust.

Lin Mo watched before looking away. Its stillness didn't mean he could wait here indefinitely. Yu Mei straightened and asked whether He Cen could reach the bend above. After he'd tried a step against the wall, she returned to the handles.

Fang Ji lifted the bag. Empty plastic crackled instead of full bottles knocking. He adjusted the cloth so the notebook's pages wouldn't rub the opening. Xiao Lu waited, then moved aside, scraped palm upward while her other hand sought support.

The section before the second bend wasn't long. They still couldn't do it in one effort.

Halfway up, Yu Mei called a stop, her voice shorter than before. She secured the chair on rough, dry ground. Lin Mo stayed against the cushion and watched her engage the brakes without lowering a foot to help. Her sore shoulder rose with each breath. Eventually her rigid fingers loosened.

Ahead, He Cen had no strength left to ask the distance. The seatkeeper stopped below, keeping his leg clear of the wet streak. Fang Ji warned him about a projection beside his calf. He shifted without taking it as a demand to hurry.

Xiao Lu looked down.

“We can't see the bench anymore.”

“I know.”

She shook her head. She wasn't afraid it had vanished. She'd realized there might be nowhere to sit above either. Lin Mo didn't know what he would once have said to keep her moving. He promised only that they'd look for somewhere and wouldn't make her stand for someone else.

“Look after the place you're sitting first.”

She meant his right leg. Turning had drawn his knee outward again. He looked down and relaxed it instead of offering her a larger promise.

Near the second bend, Yu Mei asked He Cen to reach the transition first. She couldn't support him while controlling the chair. He followed the wall. At a real iron railing, he stopped his hand before a joint and returned to the brick.

“That catches your hand.”

Fang Ji waited for him, then showed Xiao Lu the rough spot. She went around rather than pressing her scraped palm to it. The seatkeeper passed last, shifting onto his sound leg, jaw tight at every step.

Lin Mo didn't confuse this railing with the projected one. It was real and had a part that could hurt them. Recognizing a real object didn't excuse ignoring its edges.

Beyond the bend, the bottom of the gate appeared.

Not the gray door. Wire-mesh edges cut across the light beside a dark hand slot. From the slope, Lin Mo couldn't read its whole label. Yu Mei pushed him onto the more level ground above and stopped for the others.

Nobody ran through the gate to wait.

A thin layer of damp dust covered parts of the floor, with dry patches remaining. Fang Ji inspected the ground before setting the bag between his shoes, clear of the wet wall. Lin Mo knew the pages were there without asking to see them again.

Yu Mei didn't proceed through the gate. She began reaching beside it, then changed position so the light could enter the round cover.

【Maintenance Bypass: Activate with Passage Clear】

A flat handle and white alignment marks lay beneath the words. One mark was visible; dust concealed most of the other. Before Lin Mo could ask whether it had shifted, Yu Mei said she couldn't see yet. That wasn't the same as seeing it loose.

Fang Ji secured the bag and approached clear of the black slot. With a brief, low movement of his left hand, he used a dry piece of sleeve to brush away the dust. Two white lines emerged, aligned.

The flat handle rested in the horizontal slot, not halfway along its vertical travel. Yu Mei checked the latch tongue from the side. It was in the white position. She didn't reset a correctly seated handle merely to repeat a familiar procedure.

“That's how it is now.”

She wasn't explaining every moment since they'd left. They could inspect the present, not use a remembered action to account for the unwatched interval.

The maintenance-passage indicator was lit. The gate held. Torn cloth remained on its protruding edge, close in color to Yu Mei's sleeve. She didn't retrieve it. She tucked her own knot nearer her arm to avoid another snag.

Xiao Lu looked at the dark hand slot.

【Male Position】

【Hand Support】

She lowered her scraped hand. Her other hand tightened, then opened. Nobody asked her to insert it and discover what would happen.

“Which way?”

Yu Mei indicated the bypass clearance. “Here. Hands and clothes away from that metal edge.”

Nobody's body was holding the gate while others slipped through. Lin Mo studied the physical connections between handle, latch, and gate. They had checked a route. They still needed to cross it individually.

Fang Ji went first empty-handed, leaving the bag on dry ground. He stopped inside and showed Yu Mei where to step. No new step blocked the way, but a wet line from farther above crossed where a rear wheel would land.

“Room to change the angle here.”

Yu Mei inspected it, then returned behind Lin Mo. Before releasing the brakes, she told him they would go slightly left. The chair turned slowly. The clearance beside the footplate made him hold his breath.

His left hand rose toward the frame for support. He saw its sharp, curled edge and withdrew his hand inside the armrest, telling Yu Mei he hadn't touched it.

“I saw.”

She didn't stop to praise him. She was steering the rear wheels clear of the slipperier patch. Only when both had crossed did she park to one side, brake, and lean near the handles to breathe.

Lin Mo looked back. The gate remained where it had been.

He Cen waited until Yu Mei could accompany him across the wet line. She supported his forearm for that short transfer while the wheelchair stood secured. Beyond the gate, he leaned against the wall. Damp darkened his gray cuff. He lifted it away rather than wiping his mouth with it.

Xiao Lu crossed by herself, gathering her clothes close before easing past the metal. Lin Mo watched her scraped palm stay clear until she reached him.

The seatkeeper was slowest. His shoe slipped slightly on the wet line. He shifted his weight back toward his supporting hand without falling. After a tight-lipped pause, he told the others to wait. Only when he could lift his foot again did he cross.

Nobody braced the gate with his back.

Fang Ji retrieved the bag. Lin Mo feared a page might fall into the gap, but the cloth held the notebook flat and nothing dropped. Fang Ji set it within sight, shook out his free left hand, then rubbed his fingers.

All six were through. Yu Mei checked the handle and latch again from the side. She didn't release the bypass and send everyone back to test reopening it. Neither did she promise it would remain open forever.

“How many more?” Lin Mo asked.

“The old load-bearing frame. Then the lamp-repair room.”

He nodded. The room wasn't reality. Hearing its name couldn't remove his splint or shoulder pain. But he had traveled here from the lower platform. That distance was behind him, not merely an old experience someone had described.

Resting beyond the gate, he asked whether the technical pages illustrated the locking pin. He didn't want his diary open. He did need to know where the second groove was.

Fang Ji waited for his left hand to recover before taking out the notebook. Yu Mei opened its back section. The blank separator remained between oily pages and the photograph at the front. She showed him one nearly complete sheet, keeping the other two underneath and the private inner fold closed.

The notebook rested flat on his lap. She held down a curling corner. He didn't rub oil away with his injured finger. An obscured patch stayed obscure. Tilting the page toward the lamp avoided glare and revealed the edge of the pin's socket.

The drawing looked cleaner than the real frame. Corrections and failed attempts marked its lines. Lin Mo could follow the diagram to the locking pin. He couldn't tell where the writer had stood, or assume its arrows governed another gate.

“I didn't draw this.”

“We think they're copies Old Chen left,” Yu Mei said. “Not his original notebook. Parts of the attribution still need checking.”

He studied the corrections. The name summoned no face sitting before him. Still, this wasn't a tool that had simply come into existence for them.

“What happened to him?”

Yu Mei didn't attempt the whole story during this pause. What they could confirm of his remaining voice was at the hospital in reality, not here guiding them. They still lacked his original name, his son's name, and the original reply. Finding pages didn't mean finding those answers.

“Even if the reply wasn't yes?”

“Even then.”

He nodded, retaining the question now without pretending an old promise had returned to memory. He'd located the second groove. Yu Mei closed the pages in their original order. They didn't become cloths for wiping water, or get abandoned to save a few steps. Fang Ji packed the notebook and rested his shoulder before lifting the bag.

Lin Mo looked again at the real metalwork. The drawing told him where to look. It couldn't confirm what he would find there now.

After resting, they followed the short corridor toward the frame. Fang Ji carried the bag lower, putting it down every few steps. Yu Mei didn't hurry him. Xiao Lu said her palm burned. The fine scrape looked redder but wasn't dripping blood.

“Don't wash it with this water,” Yu Mei said.

She kept her hand away from the wet line and asked whether there would be clean cloth upstairs. Yu Mei said they had nothing they could call clean now. They'd have to see what remained. Xiao Lu stopped asking, holding her palm upward like something she had nowhere to put.

Iron sounded at the corridor's other end. Lin Mo saw the frame ahead. Its shoulder supports stayed raised; the route labels glimmered on the floor. He couldn't see the pin's second groove, so he didn't count the absence of pressure as a completed inspection.

Yu Mei stopped outside the classification plate. Fang Ji indicated its old boundary beneath the dust with his shoe, without stepping on it.

“Here. Last time.”

He touched his left shoulder. Lin Mo understood that this wasn't an abstract boundary. He asked Yu Mei to wait until the lamp reached the pin.

Water sounded beyond the frame amid the room's hollower echoes. He Cen pressed a knuckle to his dry lips without asking whether there were full bottles again. Lin Mo didn't give the sound a drinkable name either.

He could see a corner of the lamp-repair room's rear doorway.

They stopped on the side that still required checking.
