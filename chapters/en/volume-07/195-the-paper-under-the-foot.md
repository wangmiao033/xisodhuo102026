# Chapter 195: The Paper Under the Foot

The clapper moved first.

No sound yet. A fine line beneath the brass slowly tightened. Xu Qin looked down as the black stitches sank into her wrist. She didn't cradle the blue work jacket again, only kept its shallow basket steady on the counter.

Sun Cheng drew a painful breath inside.

“Leave the jacket still,” Lin Mo said.

He meant the actual garment, not that the man should endure. Sun Cheng's right arm remained pulled behind him. The frosted-glass door remained open only at its existing narrow gap, but no rope showed that someone could cut. Old Zhou stood behind the chair; Xiao Lu waited left of the doorway. Nobody tried scissors against skin.

White paper lay beneath the ironing table's front-left foot. The exposed corner read 47. Half the sheet was folded under the metal base. Xiao Lu could see it, not reach it without squeezing between Sun Cheng and the table.

“Don't go in yet.”

“I haven't.”

Her shoes remained short of the threshold. Lin Mo withdrew the next command. His eyes saw danger; his mouth wanted to complete other people's movements for them.

Sun Cheng shifted his left cloth shoe. His right shoulder jerked backward. Xiao Lu asked him to stop, waited for his breathing, then asked whether he could restore his foot. He nodded and eased it back.

The paper revealed no more.

Xu Qin suddenly said the jacket hadn't arrived like this. It had been neatly folded when Sun Cheng found it in the disused drum the previous night. Nobody had handed it to her at the door. She'd only wanted him to keep an unclaimed garment separate from customers' clothes. She gripped the basket harder; stitches contracted. She released it at once.

“Don't treat it like something missing from our wardrobe,” Sun Cheng said. “I don't recognize it either.”

“I know.”

“You kept saying mine.”

She'd meant her husband. Beneath the counter, a blurred fragment repeated mine without an identifiable speaker. Xiao Lu didn't search the jacket's pockets for a loudspeaker or repeat a fuller sentence to it.

Lin Mo asked Xu Qin to face the open door. Sun Cheng could see her and interrupt. The counter's captured fragment needn't finish their meaning.

“Look at me, Sun Cheng. I'm not leaving you to it.”

His chin lifted. The stitches didn't loosen, but he stopped twisting toward the sound. The firefighters could inspect the table edge behind him.

One checked the inner room, then the counter's condition. He didn't treat Lin Mo's possibility as a proven procedure. He kept an exit clear and asked Sun Cheng what he could move and what hurt immediately.

“Left foot. Shoulder won't come forward.”

A flashlight examined the underside. Ordinary power had already been disconnected, plug visible. There was no normal reason for machinery to run, yet the bell's line remained taut. Nobody restored the breaker for another attempt.

“Can you illuminate what pins the paper?”

The table leg ended in a round leveling foot. Dust packed its threads; paper lay between the pad and a thin piece of wood underneath. Braces connected the frame. They couldn't simply lift the whole thing with one hand.

Lin Mo's finger moved on the armrest. He could recognize where a load rested without being able to crouch and turn the fitting. Reaching wouldn't remove his wrist support.

He indicated the threads. Relieving a little weight might avoid dragging the table away from Sun Cheng's shoulder. He didn't promise a single turn would rescue him.

From a reachable position, a firefighter placed a thin support beneath nearby solid metal. It came from the tools outside. He took the weight slowly while his colleague watched the tabletop and the man's shoulder.

Sun Cheng cried out at the first movement. They stopped and released that small pressure.

Xu Qin's stitches tightened too. She checked: the jacket hadn't slipped, the red clip stayed at its collar. She called that out. Not every pain came from the same movement. Holding a basket still couldn't make the rest irrelevant.

“What moved against you first?” Lin Mo asked.

“The table edge. My back.”

The support shifted away slightly. They abandoned lifting the entire table. Sun Cheng could separate his back only a fraction before his arm pulled tight. He stopped there. Not enough to free him, but enough to reveal no additional strip pinning the paper.

Someone outside suggested sawing off the table foot. The crew stopped that discussion. A slight lift had already affected a person's body; old equipment didn't mean faster dismantling was safer. Sun Cheng's breathing broke at saw. Xu Qin lifted her hands from the basket.

Lin Mo asked Zhou to explain rather than let her rush inside: they were finding how to unload the paper, not abandoning the rescue or dragging her husband out with the table.

Zhou relayed it. Returning, he offered to move the chair nearer for a better view. Lin Mo checked the distance to the threshold and declined. He would obstruct the crew's retreat. Blocking someone couldn't make his sight more useful.

Xiao Lu moved aside so her sleeve didn't interrupt the flashlight. She indicated a shadow's source without declaring the unseen area safe. Part remained unlit.

“Have you remembered?” Zhou asked quietly.

Lin Mo shook his head. “I can see where it presses.”

Zhou didn't demand the training story behind it. They needed to keep pressure away from Sun Cheng's back, not hear a convincing account of experience.

Xiao Lu didn't say he used to do this, or turn other people's expectations of competence into a demand for an answer.

The firefighter selected a lower support point, stabilizing the corner without raising the top. The leveling foot resisted the tool. He paused, cleared loose grit from the thread, and turned slowly.

A hairline gap opened above the paper.

“Don't lift more,” Sun Cheng said.

Everything stopped. The tabletop didn't press farther into him, but the white corner fluttered free. Xiao Lu asked whether she should retrieve it. The firefighter could reach from his existing position.

He drew gently on an untrapped edge. The middle caught. He stopped rather than tearing off the numbered corner and calling that success.

Lin Mo saw another folded edge behind the round pad. The sheet wasn't flat. He described it; light from the other side revealed curled fibers caught around the fitting.

A thin tool freed them. Slowly the whole sheet emerged, dusty and marked by a circle. An original slip, no new signature.

The bell rang.

Xu Qin covered her mouth. Stitches drew inward along her wrist. The basket shivered in place. The blue jacket's torn left cuff gaped, empty, while Sun Cheng coughed as though cloth smothered him.

Lin Mo started leaning forward. Pain checked his left shoulder. He settled against the cushion, where he could keep seeing instead of becoming another person needing support.

“The paper's out,” Xiao Lu told Sun Cheng. “We haven't finished. Don't rush forward.”

Her voice didn't take away the pain. It stopped his attempt to wrench free. The firefighter laid the original slip on a dry edge under the light. Beneath the circular impression they read:

REFUSED.

DO NOT KEEP IN STORE. RETURN TO ORIGINAL INTAKE.

Number 47 remained. An old perforated edge matched the missing part between the two slips at the collar. Lin Mo asked Xu Qin to compare the number from where she stood, not bring the garment across.

She stretched to see through tears. Forty-seven at the collar too. The two slips beneath the red clip were crumpled. Sun Cheng's later collected notation remained. Finding another sheet hadn't erased it.

“Does that mean he signed the wrong thing?” Xiao Lu asked.

“At least an original refusal slip was separated from this batch. The other two aren't the complete record.”

He hadn't seen who kicked it beneath the table. He wouldn't write a useful explanation into the past. Sun Cheng gasped that he'd seen a blank collected box and thought completing it for his wife would end the problem. He hadn't looked carefully. His voice dwindled.

Xu Qin nearly told him to stop talking, then asked him to breathe first. She kept a hand beside the basket, neither making the signature a reason he deserved everything nor erasing what he'd done.

Lin Mo asked Xiao Lu to bring the slip near the counter. She took a dry corner without reaching across Sun Cheng, following the cleared route. As the sheet passed the doorway, the bell's line still hung ready.

Possessing the paper hadn't freed anyone.

Xu Qin wanted to wipe its gray mark with a cloth. Xiao Lu asked whether she needed to. The writing remained legible; Lin Mo preferred it unchanged. If a stroke rubbed away, imagination couldn't restore what it originally said.

Xu Qin recognized a faded blue mark at the edge. She pointed from her position instead of touching the paper. A similar mark crossed the two attached slips near the clip. Together with the torn edge and number, it gave them something more than a common number to compare.

She asked whether she should sign the refusal herself. Lin Mo asked her to wait. There was already writing on the original; they needed to read that, not add her name because an empty space looked unfinished. She put down the pen she had reached for beneath the counter. It rolled toward the basket, and she caught it before it struck the rim.

Sun Cheng watched from the doorway. He didn't ask her to sign in his place. For the first time since the bell rang, she let both hands rest away from the garment.

They laid it flat beside the basket, away from moisture. Xu Qin pressed her free hand beside her wrist without picking at the stitches. Trembling fingers, a question: what did she need to do next? Lin Mo wouldn't assign lifting to someone already struggling with pain. He asked her to point out the machine’s marked original intake again.

She indicated the narrow opening beside the disused dryer, then the route Sun Cheng had taken from its drum to the counter. She hadn’t seen the jacket pass through that intake. She could only describe its journey after her husband found it.

The original intake was beside the machine; the delivery rail still extended above the counter. The old lettering beside the opening matched the refusal slip. Their earlier attempt to shift the basket had already hurt someone. The right direction alone wasn’t enough without this original sheet.

Lin Mo asked Zhou to roll him back until both openings were visible, then brake. Instead of making Xu Qin turn with the basket, he examined the stretch between counter and intake. The blue jacket remained flat. It hadn't grown a third sleeve or become someone he remembered.

“If we send it back while he's still inside?” Xu Qin asked.

Sun Cheng remained conscious in the doorway, breathing painfully. A real person couldn't become disposable contents because someone said return.

“Keep room for your husband first,” Lin Mo said. “The garment goes to its original opening. Not him.”

The rescue crew stayed beside the door, tools still in their hands.
