# Chapter 184: The Rail Didn't Reach the Wall

The curtain was thicker from behind than Lin Mo had thought. Its gray-black folds hung almost to the floor, occasionally pierced by a narrow brightness from the other side. He could see the real wall beside their feet, but not what stood beyond the cloth.

At his warning, Yu Mei stopped before the gap.

The clamp light came on, aimed low beside a caster. A piece of the path's edge had broken away. Light revealed no bottom to the darkness beneath it. Lin Mo kept his left foot on its plate. He couldn't explore with his toes, and didn't want his body measuring another depth he couldn't see.

“It was here before,” Yu Mei said. “Stay against the wall.”

Ahead, Fang Ji moved closer to the real wall, clearing the line the wheels needed. His shoulder tightened under the light bag's weight. He set it beside his shoe.

They moved the bag first, then the wheelchair.

The sequence seemed needlessly fussy until Yu Mei pushed and Lin Mo saw the narrow strip of gray between the front wheel and the broken edge. He stopped wanting to eliminate a step. His back remained against the cushion while his breathing grew shallower with the wheel's progress.

“Xiao Lu.”

“Behind you.”

She didn't squeeze alongside to prove it. He heard her shoes moving slowly, then the seatkeeper dragging his injured leg. He Cen waited against the wall ahead, adjusting his glasses. His fingers lingered beside his feverish temple.

Past the gap, Yu Mei set the brakes instead of hurrying. The knot in her torn sleeve brushed the wall as she stood beside the handle.

“How long do you need?”

“I don't know. Don't ask how many steps are left.”

He hadn't, but had meant to. He swallowed the question, his tongue dry. The curtain suddenly bulged, as if someone behind it had raised a hand, bending what appeared to be a straight silver railing.

“You can't hold that,” He Cen said.

Lin Mo followed the projected rail. It ended in a fold, not the wall. As the cloth moved, the screws' shadows twisted. Knowing it wasn't solid didn't immediately undo the impression that it was. He looked back at the brick beneath He Cen's hand, the real dust on his fingers.

Xiao Lu stopped behind the chair, palm against the wall. Unable to draw her moving foot back at once, she straightened her toes where they stood. The seatkeeper waited until she was steady before passing. He neither touched her nor told her to get out of the way.

“Brother.”

The voice came through the curtain. Quiet as it was, it seemed close to Lin Mo's ear. Xiao Lu looked up, pressing her palm harder against the wall.

The rail's image withdrew. An arm appeared in its place, reaching toward the other side of the path.

“Over here.”

Lin Mo didn't rush out. He sat in the chair, scarcely able to turn his head faster. But something pulled inside his chest. The edges and wheels he'd been watching seemed suddenly far away. He knew Xiao Lu was behind him and still wondered whether the voice beyond the curtain was another version of her who needed him to stop.

Yu Mei didn't tell him not to listen.

“Can we move now?”

She wasn't asking about the voice, or which sister was real. Her hands were on the handles. She needed to know whether his knee could bear another stretch.

He nodded, then said yes so she could hear.

The voice shifted with the chair. The arm didn't; it stayed on its original fold. A moment later, another “Brother” came from ahead, cut short at the same point, as though neighboring sections had borrowed the sound in turn.

He couldn't know how much came from old recordings or what was receiving it now. Repetition revealed a discrepancy. It didn't prove there was nothing inside.

“I heard it,” Xiao Lu said behind him.

He tried turning. A seam jarred his knee and interrupted his words. Yu Mei stopped briefly until he settled. Xiao Lu watched from the wall without asking for an answer that would prove he recognized her.

“Let's get past this bit. I don't want to stand here listening.”

He agreed. It wasn't a promise never to look elsewhere. Her legs were shaking, and this stretch wasn't over.

Fang Ji returned from checking the next bend without finding a new door. He set the bag beside He Cen's feet and warned him not to step on it. Dust marked the cloth around the notebook, but the pages hadn't slipped. Lin Mo recognized his belongings without needing them on his lap.

“We'll need to angle through.”

Fang Ji pointed to a bulge in the curtain. No hand was projected there. It simply occupied the space a wheel needed. Yu Mei reached to push it aside; her shoulder seized halfway up.

“Don't lift.” Lin Mo caught himself. “Yu Mei, back up first. Let's look underneath.”

She returned the chair to its last stopping place. Fang Ji didn't try lifting the whole curtain for her. Its lower edge was caught on an old screw protruding near the floor. The cloth formed a hollow pocket, obstructing only the little space the caster needed to turn.

Xiao Lu said she could reach it. She moved along the wall and began crouching, then straightened when her knees shook.

“Don't crouch,” Lin Mo said.

“I know.” She frowned. “You don't have to feel it again for me.”

He closed his mouth.

Standing against the wall, she gently lifted the caught fabric with her toe, keeping her fingers away from the screw. The first attempt failed. She lowered her foot, found her balance, and tried again. The frayed edge slipped free and fell heavily, flattening the pocket. Her supporting hand scraped the wall, leaving a red line across the heel of her palm.

“Broken skin?” Yu Mei asked.

Xiao Lu inspected the fine scrape. No blood dripped. She didn't rub it with her sleeve or ask anyone to surrender a dressing still in use. She lifted that hand away and found support with the other.

“Thank you,” Lin Mo said.

“Don't decide that means I can keep doing it.”

“I won't.” Too quick an answer. He looked at her slightly bent knees. “We'll still rest after the next bit.”

She didn't correct him. Fang Ji moved the bag past the corner, then Yu Mei brought the chair. Its wheels cleared the cloth. A stretched figure projected there returned to its proper proportions; its head turned toward their departing backs.

Lin Mo didn't announce that a real person had turned. He said there was still a shadow on the left. Yu Mei acknowledged him and watched the wheels. Laughter rose behind the curtain, then vanished beneath other noise.

At the next turn, something scraped softly under the chair. He mistook it for the seatkeeper's shoe until it continued when everyone else stopped walking. He asked Yu Mei to stop and pointed to the left caster.

A long strand from the loosened curtain had caught around the outside of its axle. The chair could still move. Fang Ji reached to pull it free, then stopped as his fingers touched it.

“It's tight.”

Lin Mo couldn't see behind the axle. Yu Mei lowered the lamp to illuminate the loop against the metal. Nobody wanted to discover what more forward movement would do. Three injured people couldn't simply lift the occupied chair.

Lin Mo asked her to reverse a little, watching the distance between footplate and wall. As the caster turned back, the strand slackened. Fang Ji worked the outermost loop free with his left fingertips, lowering his arm when his shoulder hurt rather than pulling harder.

A second small movement released the strand from the axle's end. He laid it by the wall without winding the length around his hand. Yu Mei tried moving forward. The wheel made its familiar old noises, but the fine scrape was gone.

Lin Mo unclenched his jaw. His bitten cheek touched a tooth and stung. He hadn't noticed himself clenching it.

“Keep going?” Yu Mei asked.

“To that wider part. Then stop.”

He didn't thank the wheel for consenting to turn. It was an old object that needed watching. A minor failure today promised no immunity tomorrow.

They rested a long time at the wider section. He Cen eased down against the wall, glasses on his lap until his hand stopped shaking. Fang Ji set down the bag, keeping his injured palm off the floor. The seatkeeper extended his sore leg clear of the chair.

Xiao Lu remained standing, examining her hand. Lin Mo asked whether she disliked sitting on the floor.

“Getting down is easy. Getting up might not be.”

He remembered her unfinished crouch and didn't urge her. Yu Mei touched the knot in her sleeve, then let her hand fall. Their progress hadn't proportionately reduced her pain.

Lin Mo almost asked how far remained. Instead, he asked what the next resting place looked like. He Cen described a wooden bench fixed at the platform's edge. Nobody would have to carry it. There was a deep scratch he'd felt with a fingernail.

“I'll see it when we arrive?”

“If it's still there.”

He Cen disliked those words. He looked toward the unseen destination. Lin Mo didn't reproach him for uncertainty. The scratch seemed more useful to search for than a promise of safety.

They moved again, keeping the clamp light low in dark sections and switching it off where they could see. Nobody took out a phone. With each jolt, Lin Mo felt his pocket against his leg. He knew what was inside without treating its screen as another route that would automatically disclose an exit.

At last, solid concrete ceiling appeared beyond the curtains above a wider floor. He Cen saw the bench first. He didn't lunge toward it. He pointed, letting Fang Ji check its legs and the ground.

Fang Ji stopped where the light reached, checked for freshly fallen debris, then stepped back for Yu Mei to push the chair onto the platform. Both rear wheels cleared the narrow path before she braked.

The deep scratch was there.

He Cen sat, touched it, and withdrew his hand, as though finishing something too small to celebrate. Xiao Lu waited for him to settle before sitting beside him. She rested her scraped palm upward. Her breathing was loud; arrival hadn't made her ashamed of it.

Fang Ji and the seatkeeper joined them. All six had reached the platform. The bag lay at Fang Ji's feet, the notebook still with them. Yu Mei switched off the light, caught her breath against the wall, then took a remaining stretch of bench.

Lin Mo looked to one side. The metal sheet still covered the old window, dim light at its edges. That said nothing about other nodes. The way up the gentler ramp remained, its first bend hiding everything beyond. He couldn't see the newer gate or the lamp-repair room.

“Is there water?” Xiao Lu asked.

Nobody offered the bench as an answer. Fang Ji shook his head and moved the bag farther away so its empty bottles wouldn't crackle beside her.

Lin Mo rested against his cushion and waited for the dryness in his mouth to ease. It didn't. Instead, a light metallic knock reached them from above.

Somewhere past the first bend.
