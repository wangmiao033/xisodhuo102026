# Chapter 183: First, the Doorway

When Lin Mo opened his eyes, Xiao Lu was still on the ledge, drawing back a foot with a short scrape. He Cen hadn't slept. A hand rested against his forehead; he looked up at the noise. Nobody announced how long Lin Mo's eyes had been closed. Yu Mei didn't take his waking as proof that he could travel.

He remembered the bottle cap.

The bag stood against the wall, the small bottle beside it, the cap nearby. That modest discovery let him hold back his first question. He didn't need to test everyone's name to establish that he still belonged here.

Yu Mei left the wall with one shoulder lower than the other. After inspecting the short corridor, she returned and told Fang Ji to pack the notebook.

“We're leaving?” Lin Mo asked.

“First we'll see if we can get through this door. There's no water here. Lying around until tomorrow won't produce any.”

She sounded impatient and pressed her lips together afterward. He didn't ask her to take it back. His own throat was impatient too. Its dry ache didn't slow because they were considerate of one another.

Fang Ji screwed the fallen cap back onto the small bottle so it wouldn't get lost. He reminded them it would need cleaning before they filled it. Four large empties and two small ones went in the light bag. Another large bottle remained beneath the gray door. Fang Ji glanced that way, but wasn't waiting for someone to retrieve it.

He unfolded a corner of the old cloth wrapped around the bottles, checked it for damp, and laid the notebook above the flatter empties. The cloth protected its cover. He kept his injured right palm close to his body; his left hand worked briefly, then rested. There wasn't enough cloth for a complete wrapping. Yu Mei didn't tear another strip from her damaged sleeve.

“Pages inward.”

Fang Ji turned the exposed edges toward the inside of the bag and lifted it a little to check for slipping. The photograph, three technical sheets, and unopened inner fold stayed in place. Lin Mo asked whether the bottom would rub against the wheel.

“I'm carrying it. Not hanging it.”

“But your hand—”

“I'll put it down to rest. I won't hold it the whole time.”

He demonstrated by setting it on the ledge. Lin Mo looked at his footplates. He could apparently judge how to angle a wheel across a seam. That knowledge couldn't lift his injured knee.

He wanted Yu Mei to move the chair back. What came out was simply, “Back up.”

Her hand stopped beside the handle.

“Who?”

Xiao Lu had begun to rise. Fang Ji, thinking he needed room, had turned and knocked his sore shoulder.

“My chair,” Lin Mo said. “I want to see if crossing at an angle would jolt it less.”

Yu Mei examined the threshold. She didn't reject the suggestion, but asked him to use names first.

“Nobody here has spare movement.”

He wasn't entirely incapable of working with people. The trouble was almost the opposite. He knew how to break a task into steps, how short commands could save time. But no remembered relationship softened the words. These people couldn't tell whether he was thinking aloud or had already decided.

“Yu Mei. Would you see if it would work?”

She started to crouch by the caster, stopped, and bent slightly instead. Grit filled the seam. Its metal edge rose on one side and lay flush on the other. She brushed loose sand aside with her toe, keeping her foot out from under the chair, then straightened with a slow breath.

“Everyone else first. No turning room here.”

He Cen shook his head. His vision would go dark if he stood now. He wasn't refusing to go; he needed longer. Yu Mei glanced down the passage, then returned and asked whether he wanted her to support his forearm or let him stand against the wall first.

“To sit first,” he said.

Nobody rephrased the question.

A drop struck the ledge beside him. He turned so quickly his glasses slipped. Fang Ji looked up too, the bag's handle taut in his left hand.

The damp mark lay near where the notebook had been. Lin Mo told them to move the bag. He Cen's lips moved.

“A pipe up there?”

Yu Mei turned the wheelchair's clamp light toward the ceiling and switched it on. A narrow black trail hung from a crack in the rough concrete, disappearing above a metal sheet. They could see neither a joint nor where it led. Another drop flattened some dust. The light revealed no container full of water.

He Cen withdrew his hand without touching the drop and looked at his empty bottle.

“I know we can't just drink it.”

Yu Mei didn't lecture him. She asked Fang Ji to move the bag, leaving the wet patch clear. It remained small enough not to prevent them sitting there. Still, Lin Mo could no longer imagine this room as something that stopped changing once they shut a door.

He Cen moved along the ledge with a hand against the wall. He fixed his glasses before bracing both hands again, leaving space for the bag and its handle.

Xiao Lu had started to stand. She slowly sat back, pressing her hands to her knees as though embarrassed that she, too, had leaned toward the wet patch.

“Was the water you found collected somewhere like this?”

“No,” Fang Ji said. “Sealed bottles.”

He didn't promise more. Hearing the drip, Lin Mo imagined the weight of a full bottle. That required no old memory. Like He Cen, he wanted it now.

Yu Mei switched off the lamp. Nobody pried at the overhead metal. Lin Mo didn't treat the leak as a signpost either. He asked Fang Ji to remember this end wasn't a place for paper if they used the room again.

While they waited, he tried loosening his collar. His splinted wrist couldn't reach, and his left index finger hurt against the fastening. He stopped, noticing Xiao Lu watching.

“It's folded under.”

“I know.”

She stayed where she was until he asked her to pull out the tucked corner without dragging the whole shoulder. Unsteady herself, she rested one hand on the side of the chair's back, freed the fabric, and withdrew.

The loosened collar felt cool for an instant.

He meant to thank her, but a breath escaped through his nose. Seeing her look at his mouth, he explained that he wasn't laughing at her.

“I wondered if you'd always been this tall.”

She looked at her feet, then his seated height.

“You're sitting down. You didn't used to see me from this angle.”

He felt foolish. One unfamiliar view, and he'd nearly decided her body had changed again. She didn't recount growing up or ask him to apologize for missing it. Removing her hand from the chair, she said the floor was uneven. She still needed to watch her feet.

He let her walk away without following her every movement. There was already enough to see in the seam ahead.

Eventually He Cen stood, leaning against the wall. He held his glasses until the dizziness passed, then put them back on. Yu Mei left the wheelchair and accompanied him through the doorway to a solid stretch of wall nearby. Only after he stopped did she return.

Fang Ji went ahead empty-handed to watch the corner. The bag remained on the ledge. The seatkeeper moved his injured leg aside; nobody asked him to brace a door. Xiao Lu waited farther out.

Yu Mei released the brakes, rolled the chair back a fraction, and angled it. Lin Mo knew to keep his back against the cushion rather than lunge forward to help. He did, although his stomach tightened when the caster met the seam, as if his body could intercept the pain.

“Stop.”

The chair stopped. Nobody asked whether he'd changed his mind about leaving.

He waited until the pain stopped climbing from his knee, then said they could continue. One caster crossed the metal, followed by the other. Taking the shocks separately spared his shoulder the sudden jolt. But the angle left them less room to turn outside.

The footplate nearly touched the opposite wall.

“Not forward yet.”

Yu Mei drew back a little instead of forcing it. The rear wheels crossed one at a time. Fang Ji called out the remaining space, keeping his injured hands off the rims. A low threshold took them a long time.

In the corridor, Yu Mei set the brakes and slowly opened her fingers.

“We can't do every seam like that.”

Lin Mo nodded. He'd nearly filed their method away as a solved problem. Beyond this passage were dim paths, slopes, and more turns. This small success couldn't be stretched into an entire safe route.

Fang Ji retrieved the bag, carrying it low with his left hand, and put it down ahead of the chair. Xiao Lu watched its bottom clear the doorway without taking it onto her own back.

“Could we use the lamp? I can't see the base of that wall.”

Yu Mei turned the beam toward the floor and switched it on briefly. No new door appeared, just the old wall they had to follow. Wheel tracks overlapped in the dust. Lin Mo couldn't identify his, but he could distinguish disturbed dust from deeper cracks.

“This way back?”

To the lower platform, Yu Mei said. Behind the curtains, not toward the gray door. She indicated the direction and waited until he'd seen before switching off the lamp. Distant light still showed the line of the wall; they would use it again when that wasn't enough.

The seatkeeper came last, easing his sore foot across the seam. He stopped against the wall without invoking familiarity with the place to hurry them.

Looking the other way, Lin Mo couldn't see the gray door beyond the bend. He knew facts about what had happened there, facts he could repeat. Losing sight of it didn't make him think the structure still running there had stopped with their departure.

“If we leave, does it—”

Yu Mei shook her head. “This corridor isn't its switch. The continuing structure transferred earlier is still running. We can't simply cut it off.”

He wanted to ask whether it hurt inside. She didn't preempt the question by declaring that nobody was there. He left it unfinished. Moving solved where six people stood; it didn't settle everything else.

He Cen wiped his mouth with the back of his hand and winced at his dry lips. Xiao Lu began saying she wanted to sit in the little room, then stopped.

“We can go back and rest,” Lin Mo said.

She shook her head. “I was wondering whether there's somewhere to sit when we get there.”

There had been a fixed wooden bench on the platform, Yu Mei told her. They'd have to reach it to know its condition now. Xiao Lu eased away from the wall and tried standing straighter without demanding a guarantee.

Fang Ji carried the bag a few steps, then waited. They formed no tidy procession: Yu Mei behind the chair, He Cen against the wall, Xiao Lu stopping and starting, the seatkeeper slower on his injured leg. Whenever someone stopped, someone ahead looked back.

Lin Mo saw the first dark gap behind the curtain. Instead of another clipped command, he called Yu Mei's name and told her where a piece of the right-hand edge was missing.

This time, she knew where he meant.
