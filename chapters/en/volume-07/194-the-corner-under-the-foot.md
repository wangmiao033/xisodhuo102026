# Chapter 194: The Corner Under the Foot

First Lin Mo asked Zhou to move his chair back.

Less light reached him through the gap, but the space on his right now admitted the rescuers. Xiao Lu stood left of the door, looking down at the white corner. Even from there she couldn't read it. The iron leg held the paper firmly. This wasn't a loose scrap to nudge closer with a stick.

Hearing them discuss it, Sun Cheng bent his left knee. His sole scraped the floor.

“Don't reach yet,” Lin Mo said.

“I'm right above it. I can't see it, but you're talking as though it has nothing to do with me.”

Silence followed the outburst. A short breath sounded through the door, as if he'd regretted spending the effort on anger. Lin Mo didn't tell him to calm down. The paper lay beneath the front-left leg, about a palm's length from his toe. Only Sun Cheng could explain how his back and shoulder were bearing force.

The foot slowly returned.

“My back's against the table. Right arm twisted behind. Left hand can reach the top, not underneath.”

“Your chest?”

“I can breathe. Don't move the jacket.”

Xu Qin answered from the counter. Seated, she steadied the basket left-handed against the smooth wood. Supports carried its weight; her right wrist rested on a towel. She told her husband they weren't touching it again.

Fresh pry marks showed beside the upper hinge. Qiu explained their first attempt: spreading the door had immediately produced a horizontal compression mark on the man's neck. Removing the tool eased his breathing. Nobody should mistake this for an old wooden door that merely required more strength.

Watching the shoe, Lin Mo asked whether Sun Cheng had leaned forward as the gap widened.

“Yes. Like someone hauling me out by the collar.”

“Did you want to come out?”

“Of course.”

“Not whether you wanted it. Did you take that step yourself?”

After a hesitation, no. Lin Mo repeated it for Qiu: moving the door outward brought the man toward it. They needed to relieve the pulling first, not enlarge the gap to accommodate tools.

An explanation wasn't a reason to repeat the dangerous action. Keeping the existing opening, Qiu sent his colleague for a narrow-handled inspection mirror. Its small face had a protective rim and could examine the floor without anyone inserting their head. He showed Sun Cheng the tool outside the gap: observation only, no lifting the table or touching him.

Xiao Lu stepped aside. Her heel caught a loose board beside the door. It toppled inward with a quiet noise, but Sun Cheng's shoulder snapped rigid. Lin Mo stopped everyone before asking Qiu to move the board where they could see it.

An ordinary pressing board, its edge wrapped in old cotton. It became nothing else. Xiao Lu apologized. Several breaths later, Sun Cheng said it was all right. Seeing her pale fingertips, Lin Mo didn't pile a lecture about carelessness onto her fright.

“It's stable now.”

She nodded and took a new position left of the door, clear of the stacked objects.

Qiu crouched, elbow braced against his own leg to steady the slender handle. First the mirror reflected glare. After a slight turn, Lin Mo could see a black edge beneath the table leg. Too distant to read. Qiu said the paper corner bore a number; they needed lower light.

Lin Mo didn't ask Xiao Lu to cross the doorway while holding a lamp. Zhou gave her another light. She crouched at a height she could sustain and rested it on an inverted empty box, controlling only its angle. The beam skimmed beneath the obstruction. Writing showed through the paper.

47.

Xu Qin stood, stool scraping. Xiao Lu turned to tell her to wait. Seeing the number wasn't recovering the sheet. Lin Mo could confirm the digits, but iron and wood concealed the rest. Nobody could read the full text.

“Is that it?” Xu Qin asked.

“At least the number matches.”

Some brightness left her eyes, but not all. She sat, hand still against the basket rim. The skin inside a loop of black thread was swollen. He couldn't promise that waiting a little longer would heal it.

Qiu moved the mirror less than a finger's width. A round leveling foot ended the iron leg, rust marking its threads. The shim lay between that foot and the tiles, with the white paper trapped alongside it. Pulling might simply tear off the exposed corner.

Lin Mo asked what was on the table.

An iron, folded clothes, and a water tank by the wall. Sun Cheng didn't know whether the unplugged iron was still warm. He didn't pretend to see what he couldn't. Xu Qin said the equipment had been cold all morning. Qiu still treated its temperature as unconfirmed, keeping hands clear rather than testing it by touch.

“Is the table heavy?” Zhou asked.

“Iron frame. Very solid. We put that shim under it last year because it rocked.”

Sun Cheng said he could try lifting it. His left hand was already on the edge. The toe of his shoe rose; Lin Mo stopped him. Moving the jacket had pulled his right shoulder. With the table against his back, blindly raising the whole thing could be worse than pulling the paper.

“I've been waiting all night.”

Lin Mo swallowed. He couldn't claim to know the length of that night. He retained plenty of ways to measure time, but no complete night from before the deletion that he could draw on to keep the man company.

“We can see the paper. Next we need less weight on that foot. Tell us which way you can move. I can't make the effort for you.”

No immediate answer. Then Sun Cheng lowered his left hand from the tabletop. Its back brushed the gap, exposing rough fingertips. Xu Qin called his name. The fingers curled without reaching toward her.

“Once you get it, what else will you make me do?”

“Read it first. I can't promise getting it out will end this.”

The hand retreated. Sun Cheng agreed, quieter now. At least he wasn't lifting the table alone.

Xu Qin opened the blank receipt book to its white copy. Could she write the number on a fresh one? Not to deceive anyone. The trapped sheet was so far away; this paper was in her hand. The pen trembled between her left fingers.

“We haven't read the original,” Lin Mo said.

“The boxes are the same.”

“What's inside may not be. Write the wrong thing, and who knows whose account it'll become.”

She put down the pen with a sound louder than her words. Two carelessly written words had put her husband behind the door. She stared at the empty boxes, then closed the book. Lin Mo didn't call his concern a proven rule or lecture her for desperation. The embedded stitches weren't in his wrist.

Zhou put the book farther away, glancing at Lin Mo's supported wrist as he returned. He didn't ask whether paper could cure that too. Lin Mo thought of the notebook in their room, the unopened fold, the continuing support. Helping someone else hadn't quietly converted those things into the past. They would remain after this.

He returned his attention to the gap. Here he could see a living person's shoe and hear where it hurt. Recovering the paper required work at floor level. His own blankness offered no shortcut.

Plastic clicked at the counter. The supporting hanger had turned outward, letting the cuff descend half an inch. Xu Qin couldn't steady its support while holding the main basket. Xiao Lu didn't rush to catch it. She sent Zhou first.

He stabilized the inverted outer basket, keeping the hanger crosswise beneath the cuff. The jacket's shoulders sagged as though someone inside had exhaled. Sun Cheng released a long breath at the same moment. He didn't call it relief, but stopped demanding the paper.

Lin Mo asked whether the main basket still aligned with the chalk. Yes. Only the sleeve was creeping outward. Supporting that didn't justify shifting the whole basket.

Should they use the white sheet? Xiao Lu unfolded the edge and showed Qiu. A lining might keep loose cloth from threading through the holes, but inserting it meant raising part of the garment. They'd just paid for a movement. Nobody agreed immediately.

Lin Mo studied the crease where the cuff met plastic. Raising the basket the first time had improved Sun Cheng's breathing. Trouble followed low placement or departure from the original position, not every small touch. He described the distinction to Qiu without calling it a guarantee.

Qiu asked Sun Cheng whether they could support the hanging sleeve more evenly—cloth only, basket unmoved. Would it hurt his wife? Xu Qin said she'd tell them, not endure it silently. Once both understood, Zhou lifted the hanger bar slightly.

She watched her wrist. No tightening. With another empty hanger, Xiao Lu worked the sheet between the basket holes and the sleeve, without putting her hand through the cuff. White fabric separated blue cloth from plastic. Zhou fed the hanging sleeve back into the basket and gradually withdrew its support.

When it lay flat, Sun Cheng said his shoulder was no worse. Neither was Xu Qin's hand. The sheet didn't seal the garment away; it provided a level surface. Yellow and pink paper remained visible at the collar with the red clip. Nobody covered the number and pretended it had gone.

Afterward Xiao Lu leaned beside the counter and briefly closed her eyes. Asked to sit, she shook her head, then reconsidered. Half a minute. She moved a small stool out of the passage and sat properly, palms open. Lin Mo waited while she drank instead of immediately calling her back to the door.

Behind him, Zhou quietly asked whether he could manage.

“Shoulder hurts. No dizzier than before.”

“Once we've read this paper, hand over whatever you can.”

Lin Mo agreed. Qiu was already explaining the table leg and adjustable foot to his colleague. They needed support and a slight reduction in load, not another door-spreading tool. People with practical capability were taking the physical work. Seeing the number first didn't oblige Lin Mo to command every subsequent movement.

His chair was braked a meter outside the door, the right-hand passage clear. After resting, Xiao Lu returned to its left. Sun Cheng asked whether she'd been there all along. She said she'd sat down and had come back. No promise of permanent presence required her to stand until her knees failed.

The white corner remained beneath the foot, its number visible in the mirror. The paper hadn't torn. Qiu placed the handle clear of the doorway and asked for packing blocks. Change an oversized block; don't force the door wider to admit it. Hearing this, Sun Cheng withdrew his toe half an inch to make room, then reported pulling in his right shoulder. Everyone waited until he'd settled before continuing with the equipment.

Lin Mo was about to ask which way the foot could move when the thread beneath the counter stirred. It rose through the brass guide, drawing the clapper toward the metal shell. They hadn't touched yet.

Xu Qin watched it without reaching for the jacket.

“It's going to ring again.”

Lin Mo looked toward Qiu's equipment. Sun Cheng's left shoe remained beneath the gap, its toe able to move. His right arm still stretched backward. Lin Mo called his name, letting him know they were watching the paper.

“I'm listening,” came the reply.
