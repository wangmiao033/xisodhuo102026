# Chapter 181: The One Who Woke

Lin Mo opened his eyes to a torn cuff.

Its dusty edge was close to his face. He tried moving away, struck the chair back with his head, and felt pain in his right knee. His body lagged behind the thought of waking. Only his eyes could pass the fabric and search for another way.

“Awake,” a woman said.

He didn't answer. Gray door, narrow passage, exhausted faces: none immediately became a person he recognized. A crushed bottle projected beneath the door. One glance told him not to reach for it. That judgment arrived quickly. Why he sat here did not.

Lin Mo. Twenty-five. Special Forces.

Three complete facts. He repeated them silently, seeking a barracks after Special Forces, or a face that had spoken his name. Nothing. Twenty-five supplied no birthday, no meal, not even someone waiting for a day to pass.

“Lin Mo. Can you hear me?”

“Yes.”

The voice came from his own throat, thin and hoarse. He reached toward it; the splinted right wrist stopped him. Pain had a clear location. His body had understood the command but couldn't perform it as imagined.

The woman lowered her sleeve, revealing her face.

“Yu Mei. I was stopping you sliding sideways. Nobody carried you elsewhere. You're still outside the gray door.”

A man withdrew a hand that hadn't been pressing on him. Fang Ji, he said; he'd supported the chair frame. Lin Mo tried asking how long ago, but breath ran out midway through the question.

“Not long,” Yu Mei said. “We're all still here. Didn't leave or sleep through a night.”

No precise time. He instinctively sought a window, looking up until he found only darker wall.

A girl moved ahead and to his left. She sat low against the wall, palm on the ground. Withdrawing it scraped the rough surface and tightened her brow. Dust coated her hand. Her sleeve slid upward, exposing skin that genuinely blocked the light behind her.

She was here. He didn't know why that came first.

“Do you know who I am?”

He hesitated. Then Xiao Lu surfaced like a sheet of paper placed on top: clear writing, nothing beneath.

“Xiao Lu.”

“Mm.” She drew her legs closer. “You're my brother.”

He looked at a dark strip of the metal arm behind her. Its surface reflected the wheelchair and an adult woman's face. He moved his mouth; the reflection followed. He knew it was his face, but found no past to receive the word brother.

No former face rose inside that dim reflection.

Neither how she'd once called him nor the voice with which he'd answered returned. The reflected eyes watched him. Exhaustion underneath them and cracked lips were concrete. Touching them would still hurt his wrist. The female face was here, present in the dull surface before his eyes. Being her brother was here too, a fact he could state without difficulty. But between that visible face and that certain fact, he could find none of the road he had actually traveled.

“I know I have a sister.”

Her fingers stopped on the floor.

“That hasn't gone. But I don't know how to remember you. Not the name.”

She lowered her eyes. “Don't try yet.”

He felt he should apologize, without knowing for what. Ready-made affection wouldn't settle onto his face. He refused to manufacture it so she'd believe he'd found something. Nor did the reflection tell him what to call himself now.

He looked away from the metal.

“What happened before I fell asleep?”

Unable to crouch again, Yu Mei sat beside the wall. He'd completed the clearing he'd chosen. Xiao Lu had emerged whole from her previous connection; the source had released. Then he'd lost consciousness. Yu Mei and Fang Ji could only stop him sliding, not drag him elsewhere.

“Cleared what?”

After a pause, she answered directly.

“Memories connected to your lived male past. Including the daily things you did as her brother. You chose to retain facts: name, age, Special Forces, having a sister. Your injuries remain too.”

He understood each word, not the size of what he'd lost. He wanted to ask why he'd agreed, then looked at Xiao Lu: dry lips, bloodshot eyes, a supporting hand that made her pant. She wasn't waiting for him to repeat some noble decision.

“To get you out?”

“Yes.”

“You're out?”

“Look at me.”

Anger sharpened it. Looking again, he heard the voice from that actual mouth, not the hole in the door. She lifted a hand toward him, then returned it halfway to her knee.

“I'm not asking you to check again. I'm just sitting here. Tired.”

He tried making room beside himself. The chair didn't become wider; only his left shoulder tightened. He stopped, face warming.

“Can't sit there,” Fang Ji said.

“I see.”

“You weren't looking.”

He didn't argue. Since waking, he'd assembled the scene from words. Trying to put someone in a nonexistent seat finally showed him why she needed the floor.

He Cen coughed against the wall. Yu Mei used his name and asked whether he'd hurt his head. No, he murmured, only a dry throat. The man beside him, one leg held stiffly, offered a bottle before realizing it was empty and withdrawing it.

Plastic knocked loudly against plastic.

Lin Mo was thirsty too. None of the necks protruding from the bag seemed to belong to a bottle with weight inside. Yu Mei explained the last water was gone, its bottle returned to the bag; the trapped one couldn't be retrieved. A water source? They'd have to look after leaving, Fang Ji said. None here.

“Don't throw away the empties,” Xiao Lu whispered.

“All here.” The seatkeeper gathered the bag's opening.

Lin Mo wanted to take one, as though an empty hand needed something useful. Instead he asked whether Xiao Lu wanted a drink. She seemed about to say more, but only answered yes.

With no water, the question stopped there. Helplessness couldn't rewrite her stated need into something she'd cease needing if she waited.

Could they leave the gray door first?

“Back toward that small room.” Yu Mei pointed. “Level ground here. You need to sit unaided first.”

He shifted forward to prove he could. Darkness swept down; the door seemed to topple toward him. His fingers sprang apart with pain as he reached for the armrest. The left shoulder supplied no second chance. Fang Ji shouted not to brace. Fabric tightened briefly beside his ribs, then loosened.

He hadn't fallen. Nor was he sitting straighter.

“Don't treat me like someone who can stand.”

Fang Ji's face tightened. “I'm not. You were getting up.”

Lin Mo stopped. In his mind lay a clean sequence: lean forward, plant feet, stand securely. Ordered movements; the shoulders, hands, and legs executing them each had objections.

“I know how. My body can't.”

“Then work with can't.”

No comforting wrapper. After a while, Lin Mo found it easier to accept than promises of getting better soon. He leaned back and waited for the darkness to retreat.

Xiao Lu tried changing position against the wall. Her knee weakened; she sat again, biting her lip without asking for support or naming the pain. He almost accused her of pushing too hard, then noticed his own grip on the armrest.

“I didn't manage to stand either.”

“Is that comforting?”

“Don't know. It did just happen.”

Her laugh lasted one breath. She rubbed her dusty palm. He didn't laugh along, waiting for pain to ease or for her to tell him not to stare. Eventually she asked that the footrest not sweep across her when the chair turned.

“Where will you sit?”

She pointed beside a low notch in the wall. He finally placed that space and the wheels' path in the same mental picture. He asked Yu Mei to wait while Xiao Lu edged there, then checked his right foot hadn't extended beyond its support.

A small sound came inside the door.

Turning, he saw a running glow. His back tightened somewhere shoulder damage couldn't explain, though it might only be fear. Yu Mei noticed and explained that the sustaining continuity remained in the layer. Completing delivery hadn't ended it.

“What's it like now?”

“We can only see it running. We don't know whether it feels anything.”

He waited. No easier second half followed. Would pushing his chair away break it? No. It wasn't a line tied to the chair. Their earlier returns to the small room hadn't dismantled the structure; the actual carrier remained within the layer, not something to terminate casually.

He didn't demand she close anything now.

Xiao Lu rested by the low notch, panting. She didn't cover the glow with a claim that everything was over. Nobody could carry that fact away. He still needed to move his injured body from the place capable of crushing a bottle.

“I want to go to the small room. Not too fast.”

Yu Mei stood, rubbing her shoulder. Fang Ji watched the near wheel. He Cen withdrew his feet; the seatkeeper moved the bag, clearing the chair's rear. Nobody suddenly gained strength.

Yu Mei explained she'd first reverse only far enough to turn, not back through the whole corridor. Lin Mo asked her to repeat left and right, afraid he'd believe he understood and move his foot the wrong way. She stopped using left and right. Look here, she said, pointing at the wall; then there, at the empty ground where the wheels would go.

This time he understood.

Fang Ji moved a small fragment from beside the wheel with his shoe, not asking anyone to retrieve it by hand. The seatkeeper lifted the bag. Bottle necks struck the wall. He Cen frowned and shifted, clearing shoulder room. None of the six could move quickly and steadily, but Lin Mo began to see how they could move together.

He repeated Yu Mei's and Fang Ji's names, not demanding proof of identity, only afraid of calling them incorrectly. She answered; he said he could hear. He Cen volunteered his name too, requiring no immediate recollection of everything they'd said along the way.

Xiao Lu watched them and warned the seatkeeper not to hit the trapped bottle with the bag. He adjusted his grip. She too was watching their actual route, not merely waiting to be collected. Lin Mo's chest loosened slightly.

The brake released. His heart leaped.

Xiao Lu called. He turned to find her still seated.

“This time I don't have to wait inside.”

He didn't know what answer wouldn't hurt her. The wheels slowly left their crack in the floor. At last he said only, “I'll watch you walk.”

“Watch your own feet first.”

He looked down. His right foot remained on its support; the shoe edge hadn't caught. Only after checking did he look back at her.

Yu Mei stopped at the short corridor's entrance while Xiao Lu shifted herself. She drew her legs in, pressed a hand against the wall, and paused halfway up, breathing too hard to speak. Nobody pulled her arm. When her knees stopped sagging, she slowly rose higher, keeping her side against the wall, and tried one step.

It was a short step. Lin Mo waited until she steadied before asking Yu Mei to continue slowly. Xiao Lu followed along the wall, her free hand held before her, not clutching the moving chair. All six headed toward the same short passage. Not because anyone had suddenly gained strength. This time she, too, was outside, making her way a little at a time.
