# Chapter 210: This One Is for Today

A button fell off Lin Mo's shirt the following morning. It rolled into the shadow beside his shoe. Bringing in her dry towel, Xiao Lu thought it was a tablet and stopped sharply. When he explained, she hung up the towel before retrieving it.

The old thread had broken in two. The neckline was awkward already: pulled during yesterday's transfer, caught on his wrist support today. Holding the button, he remembered Yu Mei wanting front-fastening clothes. He'd heard her need without recognizing his own.

“Can it be sewn back?”

“Yes. It'll still catch when you take this off.”

He put it in a small paper bag instead of immediately handing her the shirt. Repairing the bag hadn't signed her up for the next job.

Zhou was available that morning and knew a nearby shop. Lin Mo first tried looking online. Everything appeared suitable until he tried relating the measurements to his present body. He knew facts about his former build, knew his shoulders and hips had changed, yet kept imagining cloth around the larger old outline.

“We can look. Not for long.”

Xiao Lu wanted things too. She left the repaired bag on the table, still without the book inside. Lin Mo considered taking the diary, then brought only his phone and the medical documents kept at hand. Buying a shirt didn't require his entire past.

The shop had two low entrance steps. Walking past, Zhou had noticed the level street and forgotten those. Embarrassed, he started asking for a board.

“I can look from outside,” Lin Mo said. “Don't put some improvised plank under me.”

The owner brought garments to a low rack by the door. Seeing the wheelchair, she asked if he was buying for somebody at home. For himself, he said. She studied his shoulders and withdrew the largest.

“Too big. You won't fill it.”

An ordinary remark made him want it back. A larger shirt seemed to offer room for the person on paper. It wouldn't restore his bones or lost years. He knew that, and still watched the cloth until she asked about color.

“Let me see how it opens first.” He also asked her to leave the larger one within reach for comparison.

She unfolded one with large front buttons and wide cuffs. His left hand struggled with the lowest button. She reached to help; he asked for another moment and finally worked it through.

“I can. I'm slow.”

She suggested leaving some undone over a thin layer. He explained he couldn't freely raise his arms to try that. Zhou held it while she compared shoulder width and sleeve length without demanding he stand and turn.

Xiao Lu examined trousers, discussing fabric and shrinkage. She wasn't pretending to shop while waiting to advise him. Eventually he stopped calling her over to every shirt.

A reflective label on a shoe box showed half his face, tilted, shadows under the eyes. He didn't enjoy being caught by that small bright surface. He didn't need to cover it either. He wanted a better angle for seeing the buttons.

Zhou raised the garment. At an inside seam, a short phrase sounded in Lin Mo's mind: I'll do it myself. Lower than the voice coming from his throat today.

He didn't know whom he'd been addressing. No room or following action accompanied it, only an urgency to take something from someone else's hands. His fingers tightened, wrinkling the fabric.

“Don't like the material?” the owner asked.

“No. Give me a moment.”

Zhou lowered it without asking which memory had returned. Xiao Lu looked around; Lin Mo raised his left hand to show he knew she was there and didn't need her immediately beside him.

He asked for water. Zhou presented it at an angle he could hold. After two mouthfuls, Lin Mo could distinguish the impression in his mind from an actual person speaking in the shop. He didn't ask the owner to verify something she couldn't have heard.

Xiao Lu returned with her selected trousers.

“Still want to look at this? Why so large?”

Zhou still held the first garment Lin Mo had watched.

“I wanted to buy for the way I used to be.”

She didn't tell him that person was gone. Folding her trousers, she asked which shirt would hurt less to wear today. He didn't have to love the changes or prove they didn't matter. He pointed to the wide-cuffed one with a better shoulder fit. The answer was narrower than deciding who he was. It concerned a wrist that had to pass through cloth and a shoulder that couldn't lift freely. He could make that choice while still missing another body. He didn't need to announce to the people at the door how those feelings fitted together.

The owner offered another: a shaped waist, small decorative buttons and a thin tie at the neck. It would look smarter on his present figure, she said kindly.

His fingers recoiled from the tie. He couldn't separate concern about catching his support from anger at having somebody else choose what suited him. His refusal came out sharply.

Startled, she withdrew it. There were other styles.

“I'm not saying it's bad. I don't want the tie or these extra buttons. I want it easier to get on and off.”

She nodded without requiring him to establish which style belonged to his face. A plain alternative had side pockets he couldn't reach into easily. Were there front-pocket versions? Not in this batch.

That ordinary absence brought the choice back within reach. One garment couldn't satisfy everything; a missing pocket didn't require surrendering all preference. He chose the earlier shirt, wondering whether a phone in an outer pocket would pull it crooked. Zhou suggested keeping the phone in the existing accessible bag while seated.

“You buy clothes like storage boxes,” Xiao Lu said, paying for her own purchase.

He laughed. He could dislike a shirt's shortage of pockets without hating the body inside it. He didn't buy an extra garment to apologize for his sharpness. Waiting while the owner greeted another customer, he finally passed over his choice. She folded the sleeves inward with its label visible; he checked it before releasing the fabric. A shallow pressure mark remained on his fingertip.

She explained the exchange conditions: keep the tag, try it somewhere clean, return according to the shop's terms if it didn't fit. He kept the receipt. He could already imagine being ashamed to return, as though choosing wrong would expose something larger than a mistaken size. The small slip made the possibility ordinary: a garment that didn't work could be exchanged under stated conditions. It didn't have to become evidence against him. Making a decision didn't require throwing away the option to correct it.

Leaving, he didn't request another look in the reflective label. Xiao Lu carried her own bag, Zhou the shirt. Everything hadn't migrated onto her shoulder.

They stopped outside a breakfast shop. The hot soy milk was beyond his reach. He asked Zhou to move it closer and laughed.

“What?”

“That voice said I'd do it myself. Now I'm asking you for everything.”

“You can do the asking yourself.”

Xiao Lu almost choked on her drink. Lin Mo laughed too, then moderated it for his old cheek injury. The warmth wasn't entirely embarrassment now.

He told her there'd been a short phrase in his head, not necessarily a memory. Had it appeared in the diary? Those words could be said anywhere. The paper hadn't supplied their tone. He almost imitated it to make it more distinct, then left his voice alone.

Partway through the drink, Fang Ji messaged to ask whether he'd deciphered the passage. Lin Mo replied yes, and that he'd read the third account too. He didn't send the voice for identification. Permission to ask about particular writing hadn't made Fang Ji an on-call examiner of his former life.

Trying the shirt at home took time. Zhou helped him settle with proper support. Xiao Lu laid the garment on a dry part of the bed, then put away her own things.

Lin Mo undid the buttons he could reach and asked for help with the others. When his supported wrist couldn't pass an opening, they backed up instead of forcing it to prove the purchase correct. The wider new cuff still required a different angle. Once dressed, he noticed cloth trapped behind his waist and said so before another pull.

No wet-collar sensation followed this familiar tightness. No voice came. He was disappointed, and pleased that the new shirt didn't catch his support. Both could remain without one becoming the day's verdict. He had wanted the ordinary action to do two jobs: make dressing easier, and give him another piece of the person he'd been. It had accomplished one. The lack of the other didn't make the first purchase useless or the present comfort dishonest.

Xiao Lu thought the color looked darker than on the rack. Did that mean it looked bad? No, the room's light differed. She didn't add that it looked like his former clothes. He didn't ask her to.

The old button went into a box in its paper bag; the old shirt wasn't discarded. Mending it could wait until putting it on wasn't urgent. The receipt stayed apart from the medical papers. The diary acquired no date declaring his identity restored because he'd bought something to wear.

That afternoon, Zhou prepared to go out for a while. Xiao Lu ate leftover bread near the door. Lin Mo brought the three old accounts and the recent notes close enough to compare.

The correspondences were limited. He'd genuinely read how he'd taken an umbrella, lifted a teammate's shoe and moved his sister's clothes. Today's hand image, damp collar and low voice had acquired no independent witness by resembling those stories. He couldn't make that distinction less necessary simply because he was tired of writing that he didn't know.

Yet the paper no longer felt only like instructions from a stranger. Its writer had been frightened, wanted a satisfactory ending, and felt embarrassed admitting uncertainty. Lin Mo hadn't recovered all those moments. He could begin allowing that awkward person to remain near him, without accepting everything or throwing him out.

He closed the notebook and asked Xiao Lu to return it to the dry bag. Before she reached, he wanted a fresh sheet left outside: to note where the new shirt might rub.

“Not the voice?”

“I've recorded that. I have to wear this tonight.”

She pushed the paper across and put the pen on his left. While he formed the second character, Zhou's phone rang outside. He hadn't gone far. Through the doorway Lin Mo heard him say he didn't know, then saw him glance into the room.

Lin Mo didn't turn the glance into proof that the next danger had already arrived. He finished the stroke and waited for Zhou to speak.
