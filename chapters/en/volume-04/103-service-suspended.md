# Chapter 103: Service Suspended

The wheelchair's front wheels touched the counter base with a small sound.

Lin Mo told Zhao Yun to stop.

The surface was higher than he'd expected. His left hand barely reached its outer edge. Getting to the clip meant leaning forward. Above his eyes, the line drawn on the glass no longer aligned with the actual boundary. A different angle could hide the difference.

“Can you see my hand from behind?”

The maintenance man bent down. His face appeared beside the pale shape.

“Yes.”

“If I pass the scratch, tell me to stop. Don't wait for me to say it hurts.”

Zhao Yun applied the brakes and reached for his shoulder. He shook his head. His waist and back instead; keep clear of the left shoulder and splinted wrist. Her fingers were still blue, trembling under pressure. When she was steady, he gave her the bag, leaving nineteen on his knees.

“Don't touch the number.”

She held the bag against her chest. The water bottle tapped the phone. The call was still connected.

“Close enough?” the escort asked.

“Close enough to see.”

Whether he could do it remained to be discovered.

He asked the escort to check the passage behind him, then had Zhao Yun shift the chair sideways. If the sign turned, her father and the workman would need the same narrow route. His backrest couldn't block their exit. She looked toward her father, released the brakes again, moved half a wheel's turn, and secured them.

Slow work. The speaker hadn't hurried them yet. Taking nineteen had at least kept its attention on this number. It hadn't advanced to clearing the area. He didn't know how much time remained. He could only use what he'd obtained.

The thing behind the counter lowered itself.

Up close, there were no edges to its clothes. Pale matter extended from shoulder to countertop like soaked paper nobody had peeled away. Thin horizontal lines crowded its hollows. Looking at the third, Lin Mo recognized numbers pressed together.

Seventeen lay at the bottom.

Eighteen was being added above it.

He turned to the small clip on the right.

Raising his left hand sent pain from beside his collarbone to his elbow. His fingers faltered. Instead of reaching farther, he rested his forearm on the outer edge and breathed out.

Once he'd been able to grip a person's collar and haul them up. Now a few centimeters of height brought sweat across his back. He pressed against the counter. Two fingers. That was all he needed here.

The clip was bent from thin sheet metal, holding down the sign's pivot. A broken plastic cord hung from a small hole at its right end.

“Press the sign a little left. Not inward.”

The maintenance man shifted the base a fraction.

Lin Mo caught the cord.

Too short. Pinching it put his fingertips against the sharp metal. He hooked a nail beneath it and lifted. The clip stayed. The plastic slipped free and struck the frame with a click.

A gasp came from the old man's direction.

Zhao Yun turned, then immediately faced forward again. Her hand stayed at Lin Mo's back. He could feel it weakening.

“Some gauze.”

The workman pushed over the tray. Zhao Yun padded Lin Mo's fingers. Too thick for the gap. He asked her to unfold two layers, leaving only a narrow corner between skin and metal.

Nineteen flickered on the display.

“Number nineteen. Do not leave.”

The ticket fastened to his trousers. Its number burned through the cloth into his skin. He didn't reach down. If his only usable hand retreated now, he might not manage to raise it again.

“Hold it down harder.”

The maintenance man pressed both hands against the frame. The clip lifted slightly. The pivot sprang upward beneath it.

The pale thing raised its head.

It didn't reach for Lin Mo. The invisible boundary beneath the scratch moved toward him instead.

Not the pen mark. Dust just inside that mark abruptly vanished. A narrow, brilliantly clean strip appeared across the counter, advancing toward his fingertips.

“Stop!”

Lin Mo snatched his hand back.

A corner of gauze remained inside, severed as cleanly as by a blade. The outside of his left index finger split. Blood gathered and dripped from the counter.

Zhao Yun eased him backward. The wheelchair didn't move. Its brakes were still locked.

He braced his right elbow against the armrest without putting weight through his wrist. Breathing hard, he saw the clip still tilted. Half of it had lifted. It hadn't fallen fully back.

Almost.

The old stage's applause rose in his memory. Some people loved that last little distance. Loved raising the price when someone knew how much it would hurt and still had to finish with their own hand.

He closed his eyes once.

Enduring pain wouldn't buy anyone here another minute. Turning the sign might. Telling the people outside when to pull the chair might. Those were things he could still do.

“No more fingers,” the maintenance man said. “I've got needle-nose pliers. Can you take them?”

He turned to the tool bag behind him, took out a short pair, and brought them around the side of the frame to the outer edge, without crossing the advancing clean strip.

Their handles sat lower than the clip. Lin Mo could reach them.

Now that the clip's corner stood exposed, clear of the frame's lip, the jaws could grip it. The maintenance man asked whether someone should replace him.

Both Zhao Yun's hands were turning blue. The workman pressed his ankle, blood already through the first layer of gauze. Bringing in a guard meant trapping another uninjured person before they could get anyone out.

“Let me try once.”

He didn't say he was certain. The maintenance man looked at his arm, then the handles he'd passed across, and pressed the base down again.

The long tool had snapped farther inside. This one only needed to grasp the raised metal and pull it toward Lin Mo. He opened and closed the jaws once, checking that his cut finger wouldn't let the handles slide.

“When it turns, hold it. Don't let go.”

“Understood.”

Lin Mo looked at Zhao Yun.

“Bring your father behind my chair. When the sign turns, take him out. Someone's coming for me.”

“What about you?”

The escort answered through the phone. “I'm behind him. Help your dad.”

She settled the bag against Lin Mo's left leg, clear of nineteen on his right, then went to her father. The workman pulled himself upright against a chair back, keeping weight off his injured foot. Outside, the guards cleared the receiving space again. The nurse brought a trolley right up to the tape.

Nobody counted down.

As the pliers caught the clip, nineteen began to rise from Lin Mo's lap.

The paper folded, lifted as though by an invisible fine stem, and leaned toward the gap beneath the glass. He felt the pull through his trousers. His right knee rose involuntarily. Pain tore through the wound.

“Quick.”

The jaws slipped, then caught the little hole.

He pulled outward. Pain drove deep into his shoulder, a hand gripping bone and hauling down. The pliers jumped free with the clip, striking his chest. His left arm fell. He couldn't lift it again.

The maintenance man turned the pivot hard.

Service Open rotated away.

Service Suspended faced the hall.

The announcement broke in the middle of the number.

The ticket collapsed on his knees. The clean strip stopped. Behind the glass, the pale thing folded through its middle. The two hollows closed, one after the other.

The number disappeared from the display.

When service is suspended, temporarily leave the marked waiting area.

White letters.

“Out! Now!”

Zhao Yun helped her father across the tape. His heel caught, but his knee bent freely across. The nurse took him and helped him onto the trolley. The workman hopped beside them. A guard caught his arm and brought him clear.

Lin Mo heard their shoes landing outside. One sound after another.

His chair didn't move.

He looked for the brakes. His left hand wouldn't rise. His right wrist was splinted. In everything he'd prepared for, he'd forgotten the simplest thing.

“Brakes.”

Zhao Yun turned. The escort had already crossed the yellow line and reached the handles. He tried pulling backward. The locked chair jolted without rolling.

“Right brake first. Don't drag it!”

Behind the counter, the maintenance man swore.

The sign was turning back. Both his hands shook on the pivot. The clip lay on Lin Mo's knees. Nothing locked the sign in place anymore.

A red fragment flashed beneath the display.

At the end of this round…

The trolley was already moving away. The workman was outside. Lin Mo had nobody left to wait for.

“The left one too.”

The escort released the right brake, hurried around, and lifted the other lever. Lin Mo drew his injured leg inward. Pain darkened his sight. He didn't attempt to stand. He pressed his back into the cushion so he wouldn't sway out of the chair while crossing.

The escort stepped backward across the yellow line first, both hands tight on the handles.

Rear wheels over the tape.

Then the front wheels.

Last, the shadow beneath the footplate withdrew from the yellow rectangle. His sole caught momentarily, as though brushing a thin film of glue.

The escort kept moving, several backward steps, bringing him behind the barriers.

Nineteen slipped off his lap.

Paper again. Its number almost too faint to see.

Lin Mo looked back. Both of the escort's feet were outside the tape. Wheel dust marked his trousers; the back of his hand was scraped red. He hadn't been pinned as the workman had. Suspending service had really opened a way in and out. Nobody knew how long it would stay open.

“Everyone's clear!”

The maintenance man still held the sign. Another colleague found a wooden wedge and blocked the pivot from behind. Once it held, they lowered the shutter completely. Nobody knocked on the glass.

Metal met the floor with a dull sound.

The guards moved the barriers farther out, enclosing the dispenser too. The tall one briefed his relief: no taking tickets, no lifting the shutter, nobody crossing to retrieve anything. Looking at the broken tool, his colleague didn't ask whether an electrical fault had caused this. He wrote down each instruction.

Only then did Lin Mo let his head rest against the chair.

Elsewhere in the hall, examination numbers were still being called. At the sound of two, his entire body tightened.

“The regular system,” the escort said beside him. “Over there.”

He knew.

His body didn't.

The nurse crouched and asked what hurt.

“Left shoulder. Right knee. Finger.”

She checked the splinted wrist without pulling at his arm and asked a colleague to take him for further examination.

“The other three?”

“All out. The older gentleman's gone ahead. We'll examine the other two as well.”

“Will they—”

He stopped. Getting everyone out didn't mean the damage inside their bodies had vanished.

The nurse didn't promise they would be fine. She asked for his discharge papers.

He directed the escort to the outer pocket of the bag. That bundle only; leave the originals in the notebook. Gauze pressed against his finger. Blood was still seeping through.

Zhao Yun looked back from her father's trolley. She gave Lin Mo a small bow, then hurried after him. The workman occupied the second wheelchair, one shoe on, the other still in a guard's hand.

Lin Mo lowered his eyes before he could think of the slippers missing beside his sister's bed.

Behind the barriers, a guard gathered the fallen tickets and broken tool onto a tray to show whoever came next. Seventeen and eighteen were blank. Half a gray loop remained on nineteen.

“Don't turn the sign back either.”

The tall guard nodded and wrote it down.

Beneath the shutter, a thin black edge appeared.

As though someone inside were slowly feeding a sheet of paper through the gap.
