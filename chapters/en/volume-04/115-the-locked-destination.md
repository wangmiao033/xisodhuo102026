# Chapter 115: The Locked Destination

At the rear-room door, Lin Mo asked to stop.

He'd slept badly. A cold draft in the passage tightened his jaw. The care worker asked if it hurt. His shoulder always hurt, he said. This was cold. She spread a thin blanket over his legs, avoiding the dressing on his right knee, and waited for his nod before crossing the threshold.

The doctor had reassessed him before approving this short visit. His wounds still needed attention afterward. Sounding brighter on yesterday's calls didn't mean he could move freely now.

He heard the reminder through and remembered when to return.

An ordinary lamp lit the space behind the window. The main black sheet remained in the sign's original support. The rusted gap hadn't widened. The maintenance man had marked yesterday's camera position on the floor behind it, so feet and tools needn't advance again. Out front, the shutter remained down and the box walls lay pinned flat, panel by panel. The fallen black scrap was still caught beneath the shutter, separate from the main sheet here.

Zou laid last night's record on an empty table.

“He woke once more. Asked if it was night. We told him. Didn't ask about numbers.”

Lin Mo looked at the crack.

“Old Chen. I'm here.”

A thick needle dragging across a table answered first.

He asked the care worker to lift the notebook from his lap and find the newly inserted sheet. She held its corner behind and beside him, without offering it into the crack. Old payment-source account and yesterday's completed number lay together. Old Chen needn't read them again.

After a while, the paper spoke.

“Who are you calling?”

“You. It's Lin Mo.”

“Lin Mo… came down?”

“Yes. I'm here today.”

Gray gathered along the crack. He watched its outline, hearing the effort of finding a voice. Yesterday's words might not stay, yet “came down” seemed to have followed him from the phone.

“You're… looking for the girl.”

“I want to see where Xiao Lu's record leads.”

“She's waiting?”

His breath stopped briefly.

“I don't know if she can wait. I haven't spoken to her.”

A faint nasal sound came from the paper. Zou placed his recording phone without leaving the speaker on. The room quieted. A loose screw in the lamp stand still vibrated; the maintenance man pressed a finger against it. That sound vanished too.

“Yesterday you asked us to stop at that line,” Lin Mo said. “I'd like to continue today. Will it press on you? Hurt?”

“It's always… pressing.”

The gray withdrew, exposing a fine old crease. Remembering Old Chen's complaint last night, he finally saw how the new writing crossed the original mending mark. Words weighed nothing. What remained could no longer find a whole space for itself.

“Yours first, then.”

“I remembered.”

Lin Mo looked up.

“What I wanted written yesterday.”

The care worker opened a blank page and waited with her pen.

Old Chen spoke slowly. First he said child, then stopped, as if he'd made the matter too small again. When they found the missing answer, he wanted to hear the original words first. Don't make it into something good for him.

“He might… not have promised.”

“All right.”

“Might have said… he wouldn't look.”

Lin Mo nodded, then remembered the paper might not see.

“We'll keep whatever was actually said.”

“Don't comfort me first.”

The care worker wrote, then read it back quietly. The gray lingered at the first line and slowly murmured agreement. This hadn't restored his son's name. He'd preserved another place where, someday, nobody should answer for him.

Lin Mo felt able to wait a little.

The maintenance man opened yesterday's final photograph, showing Zou where the text folded back. The new record crossed the gap in the support; its lower line lay inside the rusty edge. Standing, he couldn't see everything. Crouching at the designated spot gave him a side view of the end.

He crouched without taking his knees beyond the rear mark.

“That's the character for 'go.' Another row below.”

Watching his back, Lin Mo felt the old urge to move closer. Leaning forward tightened his right knee beneath the blanket. He settled back and asked the care worker to turn the phone toward him.

Core appeared first at the photograph's edge.

His eyes stopped there.

He had never seen where his sister went after she disappeared. Her mouth had opened silently; his reaching hand had met a wall. Core had no shape or direction for him, not even a place name he could give a driver.

But it was printed beneath the storage-transfer destination.

“Next part?” Zou asked.

The maintenance man turned the phone sideways without reaching behind the support. The faint writing continued beneath an innermost sliver of black. He captured the left side of the first character in backup. Only the top of the last character showed.

“Backup,” Old Chen suddenly said.

Lin Mo turned to the crack.

“You can see it?”

“Beside… core. Then… lock.”

The voice stopped. Lin Mo wanted to supply locked. He'd heard so many locking announcements in the theater; he knew what usually followed. This time he couldn't bear to have his mouth settle something about his sister before his ears heard it.

Zou read the confirmed text through Core Backup, then waited.

Old Chen's voice sank lower.

“Lock… ed.”

The maintenance man answered from the side. From below, he could see the last two strokes. He showed Zou where to stand. Zou stooped to look for himself, refusing to mistake Lin Mo's urgency for a clear view.

The photograph finally captured the complete lower line. The support still hid one part above, but yesterday's wider photograph continued along the same fold. Together, they joined the text.

Forced preservation takeover.

Core Backup: Locked.

Reading both lines, Lin Mo suddenly hurt too much to draw a full breath.

The care worker asked if he needed to leave. He shook his head, then hurriedly asked for a moment. He could breathe. She stayed close, watching his face, steadying his supported left arm.

“She went there,” he said quietly.

She hadn't reached the outer door or followed the low cart. Beyond the last step he could see, she'd been transferred somewhere called Core Backup. The hospital record from a year ago remained in his notebook; nobody had withdrawn it. But this post-collapse transfer record finally connected to the Xiao Lu he'd seen himself.

She was more than an unfinished question.

“Old Chen.”

The gray moved faintly.

“I know where she was sent.”

“Where?”

“Core Backup.”

“So far?”

“I don't know how far.”

Silence. He had no route he'd traveled to offer. Old Chen didn't even know how many floors this hospital had. New words pressed against him; his old voice remained beside them. That was what he knew.

“Can your voice reach her there?” Lin Mo asked.

Zou looked up, still holding an ordinary phone. The recording waveform rose with the question. Distance here still had concrete parts: walls, microphones, connecting and ending calls. In one sentence, Lin Mo had crossed every place they hadn't found.

“Where?” Old Chen asked.

“The place you just read. Xiao Lu's destination.”

The crack fell silent. Eventually, the gray released a breath.

“You call her… I can't see.”

His mouth was open, her nickname on his tongue. He didn't call. He wouldn't make someone who'd struggled through a few words carry a message, or turn the blank paper's edge into another listening ear.

“Only these words in front of you?”

“Crowded.”

“All right. You don't need to find her.”

The gray drifted toward the old crease, as if it could finally rest a little. His throat tightened. He wanted to say she'd remember his chipped cup when she saw him. That she'd never thought him only a man who asked for names. He kept those words, afraid of making something already happen between two people who weren't together.

“Thank you for reading it to me.”

He didn't ask where the key was.

He asked whether the maintenance man could see the small section beyond. The text turned beside an old tear. Its fine returning line broke off at a missing edge. Beyond it, the paper wasn't covered by black. A piece was gone.

The maintenance man reduced the photograph to its original size, keeping his finger outside the gap.

“Ends here. Another angle won't help.”

Zou read the short line beside it.

Acknowledgment of receipt: separate segment.

Below, half a continuation mark remained. No door number. No instructions for entry. The maintenance man couldn't confirm whether the scrap fallen out front belonged here. The tears' directions didn't match in the photographs. He didn't hook it over from the floor to compare.

Looking at the broken line, Lin Mo finally saw what he needed next. Who received her, which section received her, what response remained after locking: all lay beyond this piece.

“Can Xiao Lu still hear anything?” the care worker asked softly.

“I don't know yet.”

He looked at the original crack, then spoke to the small gray patch.

“She wanted out. At the end, she wanted to come out.”

His voice didn't pass somewhere deeper. His sister didn't call him brother through the paper. Behind the counter remained this narrow room: ordinary light, two screw holes from the repair work, people carefully leaving one another space to stand.

Old Chen said, “Then… don't tell her to just wait.”

The tears finally fell.

“I won't.”

He couldn't tell her he'd be there soon. Two newly found words weren't a room whose door he could already open. But he needn't circle that wall at the corner, asking whether a destination existed at all.

The gray began to scatter.

The old needle sounds returned, covering half a breath. Old Chen tried to speak. His first words vanished beneath them; only “my own” remained. Lin Mo asked him to go slowly. He hadn't heard.

“My own… don't lose that either.”

“We wrote today's down.”

He asked the care worker to read the new note back: when they found the rest of the answer, preserve the original words first. Don't make it into a promise for him.

Before the last word, the gray retreated deep into the crack. The mending sequence advanced another two beats, stopping at the question about a name. No new answer came.

The care worker checked the time. They needed to go.

This time, he nodded first. Zou saved photographs and recording, checked the fastenings again in place, and didn't box the main sheet. The rear retreat remained open. Service Suspended still faced the front. No one opened the window an inch for the word Core.

At the doorway, Lin Mo asked to stop and told the crack where he was going.

“Upstairs. The paper's staying here. Nobody's taken it.”

He waited briefly before continuing out.

Back at his observation bed, transferring still required two people. He planted the foot that could bear weight. Someone supported his lower back; his right leg slowly followed. Sitting on the edge, he shook with exhaustion. Knowing a destination hadn't saved the steps back into bed.

The care worker settled his legs and asked where to put the notebook.

“The photograph first.”

She found the old picture of brother and sister. The photograph still showed him as a woman. Big sister remained on the back. His sister's unsteady hand was slightly blurred. Nothing had suddenly corrected itself downstairs.

He looked for a long time, then asked her to leave it faceup on the little table beside his pillow.

Lunch was still warm, condensation beneath the lid. She uncovered it, blew on a spoonful of porridge, and brought it to his mouth.

He swallowed.

“A little less on the next spoon.”

She took less.

Lin Mo swallowed slowly, his eyes staying on the photograph.
