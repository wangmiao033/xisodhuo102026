# Chapter 118: They Took Him Downstairs

As the transfer chair tilted backward, Lin Mo's left foot tried to find the ground.

“Don't reach down,” Yuan said. “Keep it on the footplate.”

He relaxed the pressure. They'd checked the straps across his middle and legs. His left arm was supported, his braced wrist clear of the armrest. The second transport worker stood a step below, watching the space ahead. Only when the equipment rested against the first stair did they begin descending.

It was the twenty-eighth day since the incident at the window.

Street sounds receded overhead. Somebody unloading goods pulled tape from a box in one long rip. Lin Mo watched the old handrail, recognizing the exposed nut at its fixing. He'd struck it with an empty plastic pipe, hoping somebody would look down.

This time, two people knew he was here.

A few steps down, they stopped to check the chair's position. Yuan asked whether anything was pressing on him. The outside of his left shoulder felt tight. Instead of asking him to endure it until the bottom, Yuan waited for his colleague to secure the equipment and adjusted the padding beside his arm.

Looking up at the boards moved against the wall, Lin Mo finally breathed more slowly.

He hadn't walked here from the hospital or stood to show Zhou how much better he was. Leaving home had required two people for the bed-to-chair transfer. Here, on level ground, he'd changed to the stair chair. His rented low-seat wheelchair had been folded and carried down ahead of him.

This short return journey cost more than an ordinary ride.

Reviewing the fees last night, he'd given up buying a larger lamp and kept the whole return service. He could borrow light in the basement. He couldn't reach the bottom and hope somebody happened to be free to bring him up.

“Level ground,” the lower worker said.

They settled the chair. The rusty iron door stood ahead and to his right, the little hole still in its bottom edge. Old Zhou held it open without joining the lift. He'd listened to the two workers divide their responsibilities. Another hand might not help.

“Recognize it now?” he asked.

“Yes.”

“You were sitting right there.”

The third step's corner was bloodless now, with less dust than Lin Mo remembered. Somebody had cleaned the stairs and cleared the exit to its full width. The people moving goods upstairs needed it too.

The workers unfolded his wheelchair on dry ground inside the iron door and checked its footrest and brakes before transferring him. Lin Mo clenched his teeth as his right leg moved across. Once seated, he asked them to stop a moment.

Zhou left the water he'd brought on a stool outside instead of immediately offering it to his mouth.

“Catch your breath,” Yuan said.

Lin Mo nodded. Damp concrete smelled around him. White scale crusted the old pipe joint overhead, still dripping. The high window looked narrower than it had from the floor. A shoe passed outside, briefly blocking half its light.

So this was the basement.

When he'd woken here, he'd thought opening the iron door might reveal Wang Hai and Sun Ning waiting on the stairs. Now it stood open on Zhou and two ordinary people he'd paid to bring him.

“Has anyone asked about me?”

Zhou shook his head.

“I'm not here every day. Asked next door. They haven't seen anyone looking.”

Lin Mo didn't demand to know whether they'd missed a woman carrying a bag. Sun Ning might never have come this way. Neither might Wang Hai. This street couldn't account for everyone lost beyond that outer door.

Yuan gave Zhou the flashlight and stayed behind the wheelchair.

The man responsible for the storage rooms came downstairs. Zhou had explained the visit. They could look in this original room, but leave the pipes alone and stay out of the sealed storage areas farther in. Lin Mo agreed and briefly repeated the strange events he'd described by phone.

The man frowned. Were they looking for props left after filming?

“An acknowledgment of receipt,” Lin Mo said. “Maybe thin paper, maybe on the back of something old. If you see writing, don't pick it up.”

“You don't even know?”

“I know I woke here. I'm checking because it's somewhere I've actually been.”

The man glanced at his wrist and stopped smiling. He tried a wall switch twice. The overhead light stayed dark. He left the old wiring alone and let Zhou keep using the flashlight.

Less than a meter inside, a broken plastic pipe blocked the right front caster.

Yuan stopped. Lin Mo pointed out that the other end was trapped beneath boxes and asked him not to force the chair over. The storage man removed two empty boxes and lifted the whole pipe against the wall rather than kicking aside its visible end.

A fallen rack still blocked the way deeper in. Before the man could summon the workers upstairs, Lin Mo stopped him.

“The wall by the iron door first.”

Zhou turned the light back.

Two rows of old screw holes enclosed a paler patch, half an arm's length inside the original doorframe. Lin Mo remembered its color. He couldn't remember whether he'd seen the break along its bottom edge.

Today, it was clear.

The pale surface stood slightly away from the wall, its edge thin and hard. The storage man tapped beside it with the flashlight handle. It sounded hollow.

“Something covered this. Probably left when they removed a cabinet.”

He didn't pry at it. Holding the light along the wall, he picked out a black edge beneath the cracked white paint. Its upper part lay behind the pale covering; below, it protruded like the trapped corner of a bag.

Lin Mo kept his left foot still.

“Light here. Don't put your hand in.”

Zhou gave the flashlight to the storage man and moved aside, leaving Yuan a route back to the iron door.

The black edge neither moved nor spoke. Enlarged on a phone screen, pale strokes appeared across it, densely packed like the writing behind the hospital framework. Below them, a bent line ended in a short outward angle.

Lin Mo asked Yuan to get his phone and open the hospital photograph, then enlarge its half-mark. Side by side, the short angles pointed toward the same edge. He didn't announce a discovery.

“That little shape isn't enough. Can we get the writing above it?”

Yuan checked the time. A few minutes remained. Lin Mo nodded and asked the storage man to change the light, keeping clear of the covering.

The first picture caught white glare. The second shifted it away, showing small words midway down the black edge.

Acknowledgment of receipt.

Zhou read them aloud, then fell silent.

Lin Mo's pulse quickened. He pressed against the chair back to stop himself leaning forward. Above, he could see After front-stage structural failure. Other lines continued in the same column: forced preservation, payment-source association.

He knew these words.

The storage man turned his phone sideways for another shot. Numbers in the dim area grew clearer. Lin Mo first checked the whole photograph to make sure the lines occupied the same strip, then asked Yuan for the record tucked in his notebook.

Yuan held it open on a lightweight tray beside the chair, facing Lin Mo and well away from the wall. Lin Mo looked between the image and the old page.

He didn't need Old Chen to read this for him.

Both payment-source associations matched from beginning to end. The forced-preservation record appeared in the same order. In the new photograph, the continuation line extended sideways, completing the shape missing from the hospital sheet. The two pieces hadn't joined through space. He could finally keep reading the same event.

“The same record,” he said.

“Your sister's?” Zhou asked.

“A receipt connected to her.”

He stopped himself from saying she was here. Before him was paper caught beneath an old covering. He didn't know why it had ended up here or what lay beyond it.

Lower on the black edge were more words.

Transferred into.

The pale covering hid the rest.

After staring, Lin Mo glanced down beside it. A grain of dust fell without reaching the wall's foot. It vanished inward. He waited, wondering if he'd misseen. Another grain dropped from the paint's edge.

There seemed to be a gap below.

“Light on the floor.”

The flashlight revealed a thin black line between wall and floor. It departed from the ordinary shadow and extended behind the pale covering. He couldn't see into its depth, let alone say whether another room lay there.

The storage man crouched to examine it. The impatience left his face.

“This should be blocked off.”

“You're sure?”

“It was sealed when I took over. I've never been behind it.”

He stood without pushing the flashlight into the gap.

Yuan touched the chair back. Time was up. His hand still held the notebook's corner steady, but Lin Mo's injured leg had begun to throb. He didn't know what they should remove next. The storage man still needed to establish whether the hollow pale layer was ordinary material.

“Leave it today,” Lin Mo said. “Please find out what that is and who can safely remove it.”

The man nodded, photographed the whole wall on his own phone, and told Zhou not to summon the people upstairs to pry it off.

They withdrew toward the iron door.

Trying to turn inside it, the wheelchair's right rear wheel met the wall. Yuan stopped. His colleague supported Lin Mo's right leg while they backed the chair half a turn of its wheel. Lin Mo watched the doorframe pass beside his footrest without lowering his left foot to help.

“How much room do we need?”

“Can't turn here. We'll back out.”

One worker moved outside to watch the rear wheels; the other drew the handles slowly toward him. With a chair back, footrests, and an injured man occupying it, the apparently generous space wasn't wide enough to turn.

Lin Mo listened to them call positions, the new black gap behind him. Left wheel clear. Right almost through. The door needed opening farther. Zhou wedged wood beneath it, checked it wouldn't swing back, and stepped away.

The wheelchair finally crossed the original threshold.

Lin Mo let out a breath. Next time, could Yuan bring a short bridging plate for this ordinary doorway? The worker checked the height on each side. A plate would serve this measured threshold; it couldn't guarantee passage over unknown ground.

“This one is enough,” Lin Mo said. “Let's fix the route we know.”

Behind them, the storage man locked the gate leading farther into the storerooms and told the people upstairs to stop bringing goods down for now. Zhou reminded him to leave the iron door open. That was their way back.

Near the empty boxes, Lin Mo noticed a dry patch close to where he'd curled up on waking. He didn't ask to stop or call Xiao Lu's name. They pushed past the space where the broken pipe had lain, toward the clear area for changing to the stair chair.

Going up was harder.

He rested his head securely and listened to the equipment negotiate each step. The truck had left. Outside, somebody wiped a small table, water dripping down its legs. Zhou waited by the exit until they reached level ground, then fetched the water he'd left on the stool.

Yuan unscrewed the cap and asked if he wanted some.

Lin Mo nodded. After the first mouthful, he realized his lips had been trembling all along.

“You found something down there?”

“It fits,” he said.

He stopped before his next sentence could put his sister at the foot of these stairs too.
