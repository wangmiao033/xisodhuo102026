# Chapter 211: The Photograph Was Still Moving

Zhou didn't put the telephone straight into Lin Mo's hand.

He came in and explained. Qin Yue, an old acquaintance, owned a nearby photography and printing shop. Nothing to do with the activity room. A machine continued feeding paper after its power was disconnected. A technician had seen photographs by phone, told him not to touch it, and arranged to come tomorrow.

“Anybody hurt?” Lin Mo asked.

“He says no.”

“What does he want from me?”

Zhou didn't quote a price or promise attendance. He told Qin that Lin Mo needed to hear what had happened, then raised the volume enough for them to listen.

“I know you helped the laundry,” Qin began rapidly. “I don't need you to dismantle it. Just see whether this is—”

“Describe how it moves first.”

Qin restarted. An ordinary printer connected to an added cutting assembly. A proof sheet had stopped between them. With all normal electrical supplies unplugged, the sheet still shifted occasionally. Only that sheet. Previously cut strips lay at the outlet. The screen was dark.

“Stored power, perhaps?”

“What did your technician say?”

“Don't test it with my fingers. He hasn't inspected it. He didn't decide.”

Was the shop operating? Anyone beside the equipment? Qin had closed that afternoon. He and the assistant were outside, taking no further work before the technician arrived. He'd wanted to fetch the photographs.

“Don't retrieve them to show me.”

The image he sent had been taken from the entrance. Lin Mo didn't enlarge every face. Paper lay inside a gray assembly, its right corner folded beneath a long narrow cover. A border and black line were visible, not the rear. A loose cord didn't establish that every other supply was disconnected.

“I can't tell you more from that.”

“Could you come?”

Lin Mo looked at his supported knee. The follow-up papers nearby hadn't permitted him to bend beside cramped machinery because a phone rang. Zhou thought this entrance lower than today's clothing shop, but they'd have to inspect access.

“I won't test the machine or operate unidentified controls. If somebody's in danger, call emergency services. Don't wait for me.”

Qin quickly agreed, then asked whether payment could depend on getting it fixed.

The room went quiet. Lin Mo understood fearing the cost of somebody who might only say he didn't know. He also feared that taking money would make him manufacture answers after every uncertainty.

“I can't guarantee a solution.”

He would observe what could actually be seen and explain it to whoever was responsible at the site. If uncertain, he'd stop. Repairs still required a technician and appropriate intervention. This visit couldn't purchase a certificate that the shop was safe to reopen.

Qin explained the photograph. A customer had come that day for an identification portrait, chosen a retake, then rejected the final crop. He tried explaining why the paper remained, starting with payment and tangling the sequence.

“Is she there?”

“No. She left after the sitting today.”

“Don't bring her back to demonstrate.”

“I wasn't going to.” A pause. “I was thinking of asking how she'd stood.”

He could preserve her original complaint without a reenactment, Lin Mo said. Any inquiry should disclose the equipment problem, not lure her back with a free reshoot. Paper rustled over the phone. Qin explained he was handling his receipt outside, not pulling the photograph from the machine.

His nervousness mattered. This wasn't a person guaranteed to follow every instruction. He feared damaged equipment, refunds, describing something so strange the shop might never reopen. A simple don't touch could still leave room for a convenient little experiment.

Lin Mo asked him to tell the technician what remained unknown, not merely send a picture suggesting a paper jam. Who had disconnected which supplies? When had movement followed? Had anyone been touched? Keep observations apart from guesses.

Repeating the account, Qin admitted he hadn't watched every shift. Twice he'd only heard movement from outside.

“Keep it at twice. Don't fill it in.”

Zhou didn't intervene with a discount for acquaintances. Xiao Lu remained across the room with the last of her bread, listening without answering for him.

They agreed on the following morning, if the site allowed it: one limited observation visit from a safe position with a short written account, two hundred yuan, necessary transport separately against actual receipts. If attendance became impossible, or danger prevented the observation from starting, it wasn't a completed visit. Further work would require a separate discussion. Saving somebody wouldn't determine whether these two hundred yuan had been earned.

Qin repeated the terms and said he wouldn't ask Lin Mo to come alone. After the call he sent the address and scope. Zhou read it aloud; Lin Mo confirmed only after checking it hadn't acquired a repair guarantee.

“You nearly promised to fix it,” Xiao Lu said, crumpling the empty bread bag.

“Could you tell?”

“You paused a long time.”

“I thought he'd say anybody could look.”

She could accompany them as far as the entrance tomorrow. She didn't want the machine photographs or responsibility for rereading every instruction. Her willingness to go wasn't an offer to perform every task. Lin Mo agreed and asked Zhou to establish transport, waiting and access. They would check the site's condition again in the morning.

Before dinner, Qin asked whether plugging the machine in would make a demonstration video clearer. Lin Mo immediately said not to restart it for a recording, and to confirm the technician's instruction. An unclear image could wait; an empty shop wasn't an invitation to experiment.

Qin replied that he hadn't connected anything. The shop was locked, he held the key, and a closed notice was outside. The assistant wouldn't begin work before the inspection.

Lin Mo set the phone down and found his palm damp. Nobody was calling for rescue, yet he wanted to rush over—as though delay might cost him his chance to be useful. He separated that urge from tomorrow's journey and ate first.

That night brought no complete old face. Sleep was shallow. On waking he recalled an indistinct collar, perhaps only something he'd thought about all day. He didn't manufacture a third proof of recovery. He recorded broken sleep. When he needed water before dawn, he called to the Xiao Lu who could respond now, not the sister on a page who was supposed always to understand him.

She handed him water, yawned and said she wanted to sleep too. He didn't use her wakefulness to recount every danger tomorrow might hold.

Before departure Zhou called Qin. The technician was on the way, the shop still closed; they could wait outside. Lin Mo brought necessary water, phone and medical information. The notebook stayed home. He wasn't searching for his diary. Its private index didn't belong at every abnormal site.

On the last street Qin called again.

He didn't begin with the machine.

“Zheng Lan can't lower her arm. I've called for help. Don't come straight inside!”

Zhou asked the driver to stop safely before asking where she was. Zheng was yesterday's customer, passing by to collect a refund for the unfinished photographs. Qin had arranged a refund by the doorway, not another sitting. She waited at the counter just inside while he found the receipt. As she lifted her own bag toward her shoulder, the paper moved inside. Her left forearm rose; her head bent forward and sideways. Qin hadn't established that lifting the bag caused it. That was when it happened.

“She didn't touch the machine. Didn't approach the workbench. I didn't ask her to try anything.”

Had emergency services received the address? Yes, rescue and medical help had been called, and the assistant was waiting at the corner. Lin Mo told Qin to follow the responders' directions and not abandon someone who needed watching to stay on the phone. Zhou would contact whoever arrived and explain that Lin Mo was an invited observer, not enter a restricted area unannounced.

Beside him, Xiao Lu gripped her bag's opening.

“Still going?” Zhou asked.

An electric bicycle passed with empty baskets lashed behind it. The street hadn't become the theater merely because someone couldn't lower an arm. Lin Mo could turn back. He could also approach the boundary and find out whether anything useful was possible. Yesterday's willingness didn't oblige everybody to continue today.

“Outside first. If the person in charge says no, we leave. Neither of you has to go in for me.”

Xiao Lu would wait where she could withdraw. Her grip loosened. He didn't translate that into fear disappearing.

An area had been cleared outside the shop by the time they arrived. Just inside, beside the counter, a woman in a dark coat sat on a low stool against the wall. Her left forearm was raised as though the elbow were being lifted by a photograph's edge. Her right hand gripped her clothing. Trying to sit upright, she found her head slowly inclining forward and left.

One responder steadied the stool. Another supported the raised arm with padding instead of forcing it down. Qin stood pale beside the doorway. Before he could call Lin Mo closer, the lead responder held up a hand. The wheelchair stopped outside the line.

Across the street, somebody raised a phone toward Zheng's arm. Seeing it, she tried covering her face with her right hand, then gasped as her shoulder pulled forward. Xiao Lu didn't charge across to seize the phone. She alerted the person maintaining the boundary, who asked for it to be put away. Spectators stepped back without blocking the rescue route.

Zheng's hand returned to her coat. Sweat shone on it. This strange posture wasn't painless simply because she could hold it. Lin Mo didn't demand her story to speed his understanding. The medical worker adjusted the arm support until she could breathe more comfortably.

Qin folded and unfolded a receipt, softening its edge. Now wasn't the time to make him prove whether the refund had been paid.

Lin Mo recognized Liang from the activity-room incident. Hearing Zhou's explanation, Liang didn't treat their previous meeting as permission to enter.

“Same as before. Tell me what you know before looking at the machine.”

Lin Mo separated last night's telephone account from the photograph, and both from events he hadn't witnessed that morning. He didn't transfer the laundry's solution to this place or promise they could return something and undo it.

Liang asked whether he could safely remain where he was. Lin Mo checked the space around his leg. Here, yes; not squeezed beside the workbench. He would observe only from permitted positions and touch no controls.

Zheng looked from his wheelchair to the narrow shop.

“Don't make me take another picture.”

Before he answered, a faint paper scrape came from inside. Her arm rose a little farther. The padding followed. A responder asked her to breathe slowly; another signaled that nobody should advance. Qin stared inward without telling her to endure it and smile.

Lin Mo watched the narrow bright edge beneath the gray cover.

This time the photograph was visibly still moving.
