# Chapter 213: Don't Straighten Her

The paper had stopped. Zheng Lan's hand had not come down.

Lin Mo waited for the whiteness in his vision to recede. The shop door stood behind his left shoulder. Outside, somebody wheeled an empty cart over an iron cover, producing a solid, ordinary clatter. He watched the actual step by the doorway and tried to breathe with that sound. He avoided the reflective surface beside the workbench.

“Outside?” Zhou asked, holding the wheelchair handles.

“A little farther back. Away from the shiny part.”

Zhou turned and braked the chair near the door. Lin Mo's right leg remained supported. The pain from his earlier startle had not passed. He released the armrest, leaving sweat on it, and heard Zheng Lan ask whether it had stopped.

He almost said yes. Then he looked at her wrist.

The responder supporting her forearm with padding changed knees. Support could ease its weight, not press her into a comfortable pose. Zheng Lan tried opening her fingers. Her index finger extended halfway and curled again, as though searching for a cup that wasn't there.

“The paper stopped,” Lin Mo said. “Tell them where you're still uncomfortable.”

“Here. And behind.”

Her free right hand indicated her neck. The medic asked her not to keep testing movement, checked her fingertips and adjusted a smaller pad. Across the bench, Qin Yue held the disconnected plug as though somebody needed to see it before believing he'd switched the machine off.

Lin Mo asked him to put it down. Qin set it beyond the bench, moving the cable clear of people's feet.

“Confirm it for me—which sheet is caught?”

“Three proofs together. Not cut apart.”

“Where is her picture?”

Qin pointed at the middle without touching. Lin Mo could see only a black border and part of a light sleeve beyond the roller. Qin had said they ran across. Lin Mo wanted that arrangement checked on something visible, rather than completing the hidden sheet in his imagination. Qin fetched unused sample paper and laid it on an empty surface away from the machine.

“Across. Hers in the center. She said her face was crooked. I tried printing the full frame again, but it came out like this.”

He indicated blank paper as though explaining a refund to an ordinary customer. His finger stopped in the middle, knuckle pale.

Eyes closed, Zheng Lan said she hadn't asked him to alter her arm.

“I know.” Too quickly.

“You said cropping it out would hide it.”

Qin opened his mouth, then fetched a clean towel. A responder stopped him before he draped it over Zheng Lan's shoulder. Give it to the medic first. He obeyed and retreated, muttering about his own meddling. Lin Mo didn't assure him he'd only wanted a better photograph.

Something rattled beneath the workbench.

A responder shone a torch into the rear. The contact sheet's right corner had folded between the rubber roller and the side. Only paper was caught there; no physical strip connected it to the woman. Yet her shoulder flinched after the rattle, and she paled.

“Did something move?” the medic asked.

“Like a finger pushing inside.”

Ordinarily, Qin said, he lifted the pressure roller and withdrew a jammed sheet. The responder didn't do it. What was the narrow metal part beside it?

“The cutter track. The guard normally covers it.”

Then Qin noticed the exposed blade.

The guard's edge was split. From his angle, Lin Mo couldn't judge the opening, but torchlight entered it and fell on the paper. Straightening that paper might release a blade that hadn't finished its journey.

“Don't loosen the jam yet.”

“We can't leave it forever,” Qin said.

“I know. I almost treated the jam as an ending. It still means only that the paper is not moving.”

Lin Mo didn't conceal the mistake. The kneeling responder settled himself more securely and asked Zheng Lan about sensation in her fingers. She began to nod, remembered the pull in her neck, and froze halfway through. Tears leaked from an eye she hadn't quite closed.

Everyone lowered their voices.

He'd nearly treated the stalled machine as a safe interval for conversation. Her muscles had been bearing the strain throughout it. Saying it was not over did not prevent him from having to remember that again a moment later.

Liang, the responder directing the work, sent a colleague for support that could stabilize the cutter carriage from outside. He asked Qin for its instructions, forbidding him to disturb the proofs while searching. Qin hurried to the front cabinet and twice missed the lock with his key.

“Left drawer,” he told himself, changing doors.

Zheng Lan was looking at the brace beyond Lin Mo's sleeve.

“You're hurt too?”

“Not here.”

“Then don't come over.”

Her arm rose fractionally as she spoke. He'd been about to ask Zhou to wheel him closer, where he would obstruct the medic changing position. He nodded and stayed put.

Xiao Lu stood near the entrance with his water. She asked about a little stool. The person guarding the door moved it out of the passage for her. She sat, leaving the bottle capped.

Someone outside raised a phone and asked what was happening. Told to stand back, the person kept it lifted. Zheng Lan heard and demanded they not photograph her face. Lin Mo saw the phone lower, but couldn't see whether it had recorded anything. He didn't promise deletion. He asked the guard to repeat her request, and the man blocked that slice of the doorway.

“I came for a work ID picture,” Zheng Lan said. “Not this.”

She'd hoped to submit it that day. The required dimensions had changed. Qin said a small crop would suffice. In the first version, the cut beside her left arm looked too severe, as though a piece were missing below her neck. She wanted the picture as it had been taken. She didn't understand why selecting the full frame had produced the altered pose again.

“I'm not saying he meant it,” she added hurriedly. “I'm saying it wasn't what I asked for.”

Nobody was demanding an accusation, but she seemed afraid she must explain the whole disaster. Qin stopped searching to explain a computer option. Her free hand rose.

“Tell me when I'm out.”

He returned to the cabinet.

Zheng Lan asked for her phone. Her family would be waiting to collect a child; she didn't want them unable to reach her. The guard brought her bag within view. She identified its fastening and directed him to the outer pocket. Holding the screen at her requested height, he let her operate it with her right hand. She missed once before finding the number.

She began by saying she'd be late. Then she looked at her raised arm and corrected herself. Something had happened. Medics were there. Don't bring the child.

After giving the address, she had the phone returned to her bag. The towel was crushed in her right hand. When the responder offered to dry her tears, she said she could manage the right side; please help with the left.

Danger hadn't erased the rest of her day. People still waited for her. Tomorrow she might still need the work ID. None of that required her to endure the present pain gracefully.

Qin finally produced dusty instructions and receipts. The first manual belonged to an earlier printer. Liang told him to check carefully, not try every illustrated action on the machine.

Lin Mo wanted to hurry him. Waiting for correct words seemed cruel with sweat running down Zheng Lan's face. But haste couldn't turn the wrong manual into the right one.

“Has that cutting unit been repaired?” he asked.

“A carriage spring was replaced. It's an attachment, not part of the printer.”

That helped distinguish its mechanism from the unplugged printer. It didn't explain its continued movement or why it pulled a living arm. Lin Mo didn't confuse a mechanical description with knowing the anomaly's source.

A service receipt yielded a telephone number. Liang called the technician on speaker. Asked first about power, then told somebody was trapped, the man went silent for a while. He understood the attachment, he said. Not the other things they were describing.

“Describe what you fitted,” Liang replied. “Which part moves the blade? Which holds the paper?”

The technician requested the number on the side. Then he warned them not to mistake the paper-return knob for the cutter return. The controls resembled each other but moved different parts, in different directions.

Qin's hand stopped near the closer black knob.

“Not this one?”

“That turns the roller. The short handle behind, on the right, returns the cutter. Check the carriage position first.”

Qin withdrew. Liang asked him to step aside and inspected the indicated area himself. The instructions lay on a dry clipboard, not on the trapped proof.

“Could I straighten my face?” Zheng Lan asked suddenly.

Everyone looked at her.

“It keeps pulling this way. If I follow it a little, might it loosen?”

In the narrow window, the small printed face tilted to one side. The real woman's head had been held askew too long. Lin Mo abruptly saw a white cross where he'd once been expected to stand before a mirror. Heat came with the impression. His shoulders seemed to shrink.

He looked away, at Zheng Lan's free hand holding the towel. She was here.

“Have you tried that?”

“I was afraid to. I don't know who to listen to.”

He couldn't promise that following the picture would release her. The medic asked her not to test another turn, but to maintain the position in which she could breathe. Her eyes reddened further.

“So I just wait?”

“We're finding where to stop it,” Liang said from behind the bench. “You don't have to make the pose right for us.”

Qin turned his face away.

The words didn't relieve Zheng Lan's pain. She said irritably that everybody said things like that. The responder kept supporting her arm. She could swear at him if she needed; he'd warn her before changing hands. She bit her lower lip, then released it without swearing.

Lin Mo requested his water and drank a little. Only when the bottle touched his teeth did he notice he was trembling. Xiao Lu watched without asking him to identify the image he'd seen.

Liang raised his head behind the machine. Torchlight found a gray track.

Zhou saw Lin Mo lean and smoothed the folded cloth behind his back. Could he see from there? Lin Mo told him to stop. Better to ask Liang for a description than drag his feet off their support to gain a few inches.

At the end of the track was a downward notch, beside a faded yellow label. The technician asked them to read it.

Qin bent.

“Cutter returned… parked position.”

For the moment, the handle remained untouched. Seeing the destination did not mean the carriage had reached it. Liang kept his hands clear. He adjusted the light so they could see how far the carriage remained from that notch.

On the pad, Zheng Lan's left hand twitched.
