# Chapter 231: The Missing Half of the Sentence

Lin Mo was reading about the man beneath the eaves wringing out his clothes when his phone vibrated.

Xiao Lu stayed seated. He finished the line and closed the book with his left hand. Qin Yue had sent a long promotional image, followed by a question: Had they agreed to another event?

A brightly lit doorway appeared above the heading.

[Finding Your Place: An Information Session]

People who had experienced abnormal events were invited to the old Minghui Screening Hall to reclaim their past selves. Farther down was a photograph of a document, cropped to straight, tidy edges.

Lin Mo recognized his report.

The first surviving sentence read:

[The paper was temporarily held in place.]

New red lettering beneath it claimed the earlier problems had been safely resolved. Participants could enter with confidence.

“Move the phone stand closer, please.”

Xiao Lu positioned it where he would not have to lean forward. Resisting the impulse to straighten up, he opened his old conversation with Qin Yue. The original file was still there. The sentence continued with Zheng Lan still unable to lower her left arm freely and the ordinary power supply disconnected. Below were his account of leaving the observation position and recommendations to preserve the material and keep the equipment out of use.

All of that lay beyond the clean white edge.

“Did they fade out ‘temporarily’ too?” Xiao Lu asked.

He enlarged the image. The word remained, pale but legible. Whoever had made this had not needed to remove it. The red lettering gave readers their conclusion first.

“I even had Old Zhou correct a word when we wrote it.”

Zhou had typed “already” where Lin Mo had meant “at the time.” They had caught it. Now he could identify every missing passage without being able to take the advertisement back from anyone who had read it.

He sent Qin Yue the original page.

“I haven't agreed to this event. They've cut off the rest of my account. Nothing in it says a new venue is safe to use.”

Qin Yue called immediately. Someone was greeting arrivals behind him; footsteps mingled with the rustle of bags. A former customer had forwarded the invitation, asking whether the shop's trouble was over. The sender called itself only the Seating Team.

“Where are you?”

“Minghui. In the lobby. I came to find whoever made this.”

Lin Mo checked the time. Qin Yue had already been there when he sent his first message.

“Don't go any farther in.”

“I haven't. Their table's beside the door. They said someone gave permission to use the document. Your name wasn't on the image, so I thought perhaps they'd approached you separately.”

Xiao Lu set her cup down harder than usual. Lin Mo told her his report was being used to draw people in. Qin Yue was at the venue, still in the lobby. He did not turn the phone away and leave her to ask about the danger.

“What do you want to do?” she asked.

“Stop them using that page to persuade people to enter. Find out whether anyone's hurt.”

Her gaze passed over his supported right leg. She pulled her chair closer instead of proposing to wheel him out. With Qin Yue's agreement, they switched to speakerphone. An amplified microphone crackled at the other end.

“Please leave the place beside you empty,” a man announced. “Don't take the seat of the self who hasn't arrived yet.”

Lin Mo's fingers stopped on the book cover.

The coaxing friendliness recalled other voices. He did not follow it. A copied report, a forwarded invitation, an unsettling name: none told him who stood beyond that doorway.

“Have you taken a ticket?”

“No. I asked for whoever's in charge. They told me to wait.”

“Don't sign anything for me.”

“I haven't. Want to see how they've displayed it?”

Qin Yue also asked whether they should send the invitation to Mr. Xu, the man who had proposed the interview. Could these be the same people?

Not yet, Lin Mo said. Mr. Xu had drafted material without their approval, but they had no evidence connecting him to this advertisement. Qin Yue would keep the image and the conversation in which he had received it. Lin Mo asked him not to forward people's faces to any group.

First, he wanted Qin Yue to ask the entrance staff to suspend admission.

Lin Mo heard Qin Yue explain the missing passage and ask them to take down the poster. A younger voice replied that publicity was someone else's responsibility. People had already registered; they could hardly throw everybody out because of a phone call.

“I'm asking you to stop admitting more people.”

“You can leave your contact details if you wish to complain.”

Qin Yue gave a humorless laugh. “You've used my document. Now you want something else from me?”

A woman interrupted to ask whether the old photography shop really was all right. She had come because of that report. Her husband had tried twice to discourage her, but she couldn't see the point of waiting any longer.

Qin Yue told her the equipment was still sealed off.

“Then why does the picture say it's safe?”

Even before hearing his own name, Lin Mo felt the weight of it. A passage could remain entirely true and, with enough removed, send someone over a threshold.

“Tell her it isn't a safety guarantee. You can show her the original page I just sent. Only that page.”

His old notebook lay at the other end of the table, its spine resting over a corner of his current notes. Xiao Lu followed his glance but did not reach for it. He would not put his entire life behind this one page to make it more convincing.

The woman thanked Qin Yue. Footsteps seemed to retreat, though Lin Mo could not see where she went. She asked why registration was still open if it wasn't safe. Someone muttered that admission was free; what could be wrong with that?

“Free doesn't guarantee you'll be able to leave,” Qin Yue said. “Wait outside while I find who's responsible.”

He asked new arrivals not to enter their names for now, then checked with Lin Mo.

“Stop adding people. Find out who's already registered. Don't just cross them out.”

Xiao Lu moved the bag holding her paper samples beside her chair, clearing the table. She wanted to stay and listen, she said, but she would say so if it became too much. Helping other people did not mean she could bear to watch anything.

Lin Mo agreed. She moved her chair so she need not face the screen.

Qin Yue requested a video call.

The picture opened on the ceiling before dropping toward the entrance. The glass outer door stood open. The narrow lobby held chairs along a wall and old film posters covered with white paper. A woman wearing a staff badge carried a box of tickets. She waved arrivals back, telling them the session hadn't started and they could wait.

Qin Yue aimed at the threshold beside his shoes. One leaf of the screening room's inner door was open. Beyond it were chair legs and the gray lower edge of a projection screen.

“This is where I am. The outside door's behind me.”

“I can only see this much. Don't go in to give me a better view.”

The phone rose slightly. A man in the nearest row stood, turned his gray-trousered legs between the chairs, then stopped. One hand rested against the seatback in front, its back pressed to a narrow ticket strip.

“Wait. I'm not trying to leave. Just get this off first.”

Qin Yue asked whether he was stuck to some glue. The man picked at the paper with his other hand. One corner lifted from the chair, but the trapped hand bent downward with it. He gasped and struck his knee against the raised seat.

Pain tightened through Lin Mo's right wrist. He nearly reached for the phone with his immobilized hand, catching himself halfway. Xiao Lu asked if he needed to stop. He told her his wrist hurt and asked her to move the book beside its support, without touching the brace.

Onscreen, the man was pulling backward.

“Don't pull!”

Qin Yue relayed it. The man took another half step anyway. A taut ridge rose beneath his skin as the back of his hand was drawn toward the black final digit of the seat number. The paper did not encircle his wrist. No cord showed between his fingers and the chair.

The woman with the badge put down her ticket box and hurried over. She stopped short of touching him.

“I didn't use glue when I put those up.”

The loudspeaker sounded again.

“Once seated, please remain in your assigned place. Everyone has an empty seat of their own.”

“He's in pain!” the woman shouted.

No answer came through the speaker. Words flickered along the bottom of the screen, too high for Lin Mo to read beyond a reference to seating.

Qin Yue said he was ending the call to contact the police.

The image vanished. Water running nearby and a neighbor closing a door became startlingly clear. Xiao Lu stood holding the unfinished story.

“Do you think it's the Theater?”

“It looks like it. What I actually saw was a hand being held. Tell the people coming to help that first.”

He asked her to message Zhou: something had happened at a venue, Lin Mo was still at home, and could he call when he saw this?

“Are you planning to go?” she asked.

“I want to find out what I can help with. I haven't worked out how to get there, or promised to come.”

She asked whether he needed to change position before Zhou returned. He checked the ache at the front of his shoulder and chose to stay as he was. She could not move him alone. They raised the ringtone so another call wouldn't be lost beneath the video.

“I'll tell him exactly that,” she said.

While she used her own phone, Lin Mo resettled his wrist. The white strip remained before his eyes. He had seen paper hold a person before. That did not make every method he had witnessed safe to throw across a screen. Someone else's hand would pay for a wrong instruction.

Qin Yue called back several minutes later. He had given the address and described an injured person unable to free his hand, requesting medical help too. The dispatcher had told him to keep the passage clear and prevent others approaching. The woman with the badge, Ms. Luo, was a temporary volunteer. She was asking people who had not entered to wait outside.

Another man's voice approached the phone.

“Show him this. He's the expert, isn't he? We followed the instructions.”

Qin Yue objected. A stiff display board entered the picture, fingers hiding part of its upper edge. The event schedule occupied the top half. Below were the names of invited presenters.

Lin Mo saw his own.

According to the next line, he would help participants reclaim their complete lives. His cropped report sat beside the name like a stamp of approval.

“Move your hand.”

The fingers shifted, revealing the admission status: still open.

Someone at the entrance asked what was happening inside. The board holder turned. Qin Yue's camera shifted slightly. Lin Mo heard the answer.

“Don't worry. It's a demonstration. We've connected with the presenter.”

Lin Mo told Qin Yue to turn up the volume.

“I did not authorize this event. Someone inside needs medical care. Stop telling people to enter.”

Footsteps continued outside.

The board bearing his name rose toward the entrance first.
