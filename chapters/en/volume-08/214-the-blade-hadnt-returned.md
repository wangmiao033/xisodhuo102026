# Chapter 214: The Blade Hadn't Returned

Liang lowered the torch.

The carriage's shadow retreated from the paper. The metal didn't. Lin Mo could see only a short portion of track before the casing concealed it. He asked which way the blade faced. Liang needn't put the torch somewhere dangerous just to show him.

“It travels across. Blade underneath, rail above. It's stopped left of center.”

On the phone, the technician asked Qin Yue to turn the instructions over. Glue from an old receipt obscured half the diagram. Afraid to tear it, Qin turned the paper until he found a matching note along the edge.

“Don't release the paper clamp while the cutter is extended.”

At cutter, Zheng Lan clenched her towel.

“You're moving a knife beside me?”

Liang came far enough into view for her to see his face. The blade was inside the machine, nowhere near her hand. They weren't taking it out. But he couldn't guarantee movement inside the machine wouldn't worsen her pain. They would explain each step.

“I don't want another test.”

“First we stabilize it so it can't move by itself.”

She considered him. Unable to nod, she said yes.

Another responder brought support to the bench, not thrusting it into gears. They steadied the base first, then supported the carriage's load-bearing crosspiece from outside the rail. Liang checked its bolts. A thin casing panel wouldn't bear the load. Lin Mo could see only the side nearest the door; Liang described the other. He didn't pretend to inspect what was hidden.

The bench sounded as the support settled. Zheng Lan cried out. No increased pain, she told the medic; the noise had frightened her. The man supporting her arm promised to announce even a change of footing. Every small sound needn't seem to come from the machine.

Lin Mo slowly loosened his left fingers.

The white cross still hovered at the edge of his thoughts, fading when pursued, returning when he looked away. It wasn't a clue pointing toward the correct control. A returning memory might only make him more frightened here.

“Could we cut the picture away?” Qin asked.

Liang asked where. Qin indicated the exposed white margin. Remove the person's image and perhaps the trouble would stop. His voice weakened on perhaps.

“The part being cropped is pulling her now,” Lin Mo said.

“I can't just watch.”

“Tell us exactly what you know.”

Qin returned the scissors to a drawer, leaving it open. After a moment he said the original photograph hadn't been intended for use.

“You took it while you asked me to get my bag,” Zheng Lan said. “I thought you weren't keeping that one.”

He'd pressed the shutter before she settled again, then kept the accidental shot beside the others for comparison. She saw her raised arm and requested a different picture. He was certain he'd selected another, but this one printed instead.

Lin Mo understood a distinction. The suspended hand wasn't performing an entirely invented movement. The image had caught a moment when she'd been leaving the pose and refused to release it. But why did her arm keep bending farther inward? It hadn't bent that far in the original.

He watched the narrow opening ahead of the clamp. A black line pressed across the pale sleeve. It had seemed a printed border. With the torch repositioned, he saw thickness: the line stood above the paper.

“Is that on the print, or over it?”

“The cutting guide,” Liang answered after inspecting it.

Adjustable for different dimensions, Qin explained. The full image went beneath; the blade followed the guide's straight edge. He didn't suggest simply moving it. One corner pressed against the folded paper.

“And the part marked full frame?”

“Underneath. The original image below, cropping edges above.”

The whole image hadn't changed whenever Zheng Lan's arm jerked. Lin Mo asked Qin to demonstrate on unused paper, away from the trapped sheet. Qin laid the sample sideways, covered its center with another paper's edge and raised his own arm to indicate reaching for a bag. He didn't ask Zheng Lan to copy him.

“For the ID dimensions, the hand shouldn't even be in the frame.”

“Was this the picture she wanted?”

Qin shook his head. Zheng Lan protested that she'd already told them. Lin Mo hurried to explain he wasn't asking her again, but establishing which image the machine was processing. She closed her eyes and stopped trying to defend her request in every conversation.

Liang's colleague found that the rear cover could lift outward. Was it separate? The technician told Qin to inspect the hinge, not the rod below connecting to the clamp. Qin identified the upper cover by torchlight. Don't lift the lower part.

The cover opened a crack.

The folded corner sprang a little flatter. A short white edge slipped into the opening.

Zheng Lan's arm jerked inward, pressing a hollow into its padding. Her cry to stop broke off. Liang arrested the cover where it was, rather than closing it and disturbing the paper again. His colleague steadied the support. The medic protected her neck. Nobody pulled against her arm.

Noise filled Lin Mo's ears.

This wasn't a familiar sound reminding him of an old mistake. Someone before him had been hurt while they believed they were moving only a cover.

“The paper was pressed against the casing,” Liang said slowly. “The casing moves, so does the paper. Leave it.”

Qin apologized twice. Zheng Lan told him to be quiet. He stopped.

Lin Mo resisted offering another method. Was it possible to continue? The medic checked before answering. They couldn't allow further traction. Sensation remained, but she couldn't be held like this indefinitely.

The towel had fallen beside Zheng Lan's legs. She couldn't bend for it. Xiao Lu rose from her stool and approached from the outer side indicated by the medic, returning it to her free hand without going behind the machine. Tears darkened one corner.

“I don't want to hear try again.”

Liang promised not to repeat the cover movement. He had its angle recorded and stabilized externally. On the phone, the technician stopped saying under normal circumstances. He could confirm the short handle returned the blade without actively rewinding paper. He couldn't guarantee this particular jam from a telephone.

Lin Mo saw that the cropping edge had advanced farther across the sleeve.

He wanted to raise his right hand and block it. Pain stabbed inside his brace. The movement stopped beside his leg without touching the bench. His body remembered rushing forward. It couldn't support even a single stumble afterward.

“Another image?” Zhou asked quietly.

“Here. Look at what's here first.”

Liang said he needn't force himself. Lin Mo could still speak clearly; if that changed, he'd stop. He didn't promise never to become frightened again.

Liang asked Qin to clear the passage behind the bench. Empty frames stood there. Plastic wrapping on the top frame hooked around a table foot. Qin tried lifting the stack; the bench wobbled.

“Put it down.”

Qin relaxed the pull without dropping anything. Crouching, he found the snag. A responder cut the wrapping outside the machine after checking it wasn't connected to a cable. Qin moved the frames individually. They couldn't block the door either. Zhou backed the wheelchair to leave enough room.

Lin Mo lost his view of the narrow opening. He twisted instinctively, stopped by his knee, then his shoulder. Asked whether he could still see, he admitted he couldn't. Qin offered to bring a stool closer. Lin Mo couldn't transfer to it.

“Blank paper, please. I need to know which side you're looking from.”

Zhou held a clipboard while he drew the bench and door left-handed. The lines wandered. He didn't redraw them, just marked the side facing him. Qin indicated the other end: his right had meant the view from behind the machine, the reverse of Lin Mo's.

A chill settled in Lin Mo's stomach.

If he trusted the complete machine he'd imagined, his next sentence might direct a hand toward the wrong end. He changed the labels to door side and wall side. Liang compared the untidy sketch with the actual apparatus: the short handle at the wall corner, the roller knob farther out. Liang would identify each himself. Lin Mo, unable to see everything, wouldn't direct a hand inside.

Xiao Lu picked up a fallen bottle cap and returned it to Zhou without entering the cleared passage. Seeing the old mark on her hand, Lin Mo realized he'd wanted her closer, ready to supply words when his failed. She was frightened here too. She didn't have to become part of him.

He nodded to show he was sitting securely. She returned to her stool, feet on the floor, watching Zheng Lan. Zhou passed the sketch to Liang and put the pen away. Lin Mo rested his aching left hand.

With the carriage supported, the line inside the opening finally stopped advancing. Following the technician's description, Liang traced the actual linkage from the short handle to the cutter. He didn't rely on a label promising return. Torchlight separated that connection from the roller's.

Qin's breathing slowed.

“Does the paper stay clamped when the blade returns?” Lin Mo asked.

“Normally. The clamp lifts separately.”

“Then don't lift it yet.”

Liang agreed. They would move the carriage a very small distance toward its parked position, with support adjusted beneath it, no longer relying on crumpled paper to arrest it. The sheet would remain where it was. So would the cover. If traction worsened, they would stop, not gamble by turning to the end.

Zheng Lan listened.

“Can I stop seeing the photograph?”

Qin picked up cloth, then hesitated. Draping anything over the apparatus risked disturbing the paper and guide. Liang requested a separate board, held between her sightline and the opening without touching the machine. It didn't cancel the anomaly; it concealed the distorted little face. The medic could still observe her from the side.

“Like that?”

“Lower. Don't hide the people too.”

The board descended. She could see the person supporting her and Xiao Lu by the door instead of staring at the machine behind Qin.

Her responder finally needed to change hands. He warned her first. A colleague supported the pad from beneath, waiting until she confirmed its position before the first man withdrew his damp glove and wiped it away from her face. She watched the replacement.

“You were at the door?”

“Yes. I heard where it needs supporting. I won't lift your elbow.”

She still requested a lower position, until the ache stopped spreading into her neck.

“Tell me,” she said to Liang. “Before you move it.”

He returned to the short handle. His colleague readied the support adjustment. Lin Mo handed his water to Zhou, leaving his left hand empty against his trouser seam. He avoided every reflection and waited for Liang to announce the beginning.

Lin Mo waited. He did not tell them she was ready on her behalf.
