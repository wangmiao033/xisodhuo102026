# Chapter 227: The Word Together

The outline remained beneath the book when lunch arrived.

Lin Mo hadn't read farther. Pouring noodles into bowls, Zhou found a small leak in the soup bag. Oil trailed over the table. He moved the paper; Lin Mo asked him not to put it with the diary. The empty chair by the door would do.

Halfway through eating, Xiao Lu noticed the discarded chopstick wrapper still caught beneath the box. She crumpled it, picked up a broken noodle and went on. Lin Mo wanted to ask whether she was thinking about the sentence too. Instead he asked whether the noodles were cold.

“They're fine.”

Someone knocked to borrow scissors. Zhou went to answer. Listening to his own spoon touch the bowl, Lin Mo thought the afternoon might pass easily if nobody retrieved that page. A standard opening, Xu had said. They weren't filming. Why concern themselves with it?

Except Xiao Lu had already told him it was wrong.

When Zhou returned, Lin Mo asked for the paper instead of reaching across hot soup. Xiao Lu asked whether he intended to contact Xu. Yes, about the inaccuracies, without surrendering every experience he hadn't decided how to describe.

“Read it all first.”

He agreed.

One page, many spaces. Reunion first; a joint decision next; then each participant was to name what they most wanted to thank the other for. A closing narration had already arranged for them to leave the past behind and embrace new life. No names at the bottom, only details to be added during interview.

Lin Mo almost laughed. He hadn't fully recovered the past, and someone was already organizing its disposal. Xiao Lu drew the page closer. She disliked the last question most.

“Why must it be gratitude?”

Two blank lines, enough for a brief good word apiece. Anger probably wouldn't go there. I don't know would deprive the page of its desired ending.

He tried crossing out jointly. His left hand swerved, scraping the space below and tearing the paper slightly. Xiao Lu didn't offer a fresh printout. The crooked line stayed. It did not turn the printed sentence into the truth, or make his objection less clear because his hand could no longer draw neatly. He had spent enough time trying to make words look certain. Here was an untidy mark over a certainty that had never belonged on the page.

“You don't have to replace it with separately agreed,” she said.

“I wasn't going to.”

“I know. I want to say it first.”

He understood. She might have heard too many attractive replacements, a slight adjustment turning never asked into tacit understanding, never given a chance to object into acceptance. He capped the pen, leaned against his support and let her finish.

She didn't know his every thought inside. She couldn't testify to thoughts she'd never heard. She had wanted to save him and return alive. Neither desire meant consenting to her brother forgetting himself. When that choice was made, she'd had no place to sit down and answer no.

His fingers pressed the cap until the pads whitened.

He wanted to say there hadn't been another way. A familiar sentence, rubbed smooth between many pages. The information he had offered no easy exit or exchange. Nor had their return made the core completely safe. But arranging those reasons now would require her to establish his choice as reasonable before she could keep her anger.

“I didn't give you a chance to decide,” he said.

She neither nodded nor said finally. Her eyes shone, as though the admission made speech harder.

“You'll still say it was for me.”

“I won't use that to answer you now.”

“And later?”

He didn't immediately promise. He didn't know whether fear would make him want to push her away again. She waited while Zhou accepted the returning scissors outside. Finally Lin Mo said that next time, if he could speak and had time, he should explain what he intended before doing it, rather than ask for understanding afterwards.

Her gaze returned to the paper.

“That can stay here. Don't send it to him.”

“All right.”

Xu needed to know the outline wasn't approved. He didn't need their argument. They owed him no deeper wound as proof of his mistake.

They prepared their messages. Lin Mo stated that he wouldn't participate and didn't authorize use of this outline, his name, voice or image. Xiao Lu pointed out that Xu might possess no pictures or recordings; don't describe a possibility as something established. Lin Mo removed a demand to delete existing material. Contact and a meeting must not be treated as consent. If the outline had already gone elsewhere, Xu should identify where.

She wrote her own response through her own contact, reading it aloud without typing into his phone. No reenactment; no presentation of a joint decision or settled peace as her words. She identified those inaccuracies without supplying the details of the actual choice.

Typing tired Lin Mo's shoulder. He paused for Zhou to adjust the phone support, saving corrections until the end rather than racing to send before his sister. Zhou glanced at the screen. Should they call it deception? Lin Mo shook his head. He knew Xu had written words they hadn't spoken. He couldn't establish motive.

After sending, Xiao Lu folded the outline instead of destroying it.

Xu called later. A misunderstanding: this was preparation, everything would be discussed during filming. Lin Mo answered on speaker, explaining that his sister was present. Xu's voice became slightly more contained.

“If you don't want to do it, don't. There's no need to make it so serious.”

Looking at the crooked line, Lin Mo said he needed confirmation that the outline wouldn't be treated as approved or used as their story. Of course, Xu replied. It certainly wouldn't be posted if unused.

“Has anyone else already received it?” Xiao Lu asked.

A pause. One colleague had seen it for discussion. No account had posted it yet.

Lin Mo couldn't verify that over the phone. He asked Xu to forward their refusal to that colleague and confirm in writing. Why all this fuss over a generic outline? Because it already used their relationship, Xiao Lu answered, and she was the person he'd arranged to meet.

Xu turned to Lin Mo. His sister was young, perhaps unfamiliar with the industry. Surely her brother understood the difference between preparing material and publication.

Lin Mo opened his mouth to explain their position again. Xiao Lu's hand stopped beside the phone. She looked at him without touching it. He heard the role he was about to assume: place her among those who didn't understand, and he could translate her refusal into something more acceptable.

“She explained clearly,” he said.

Xu hadn't meant anything by it. Xiao Lu asked directly whether he'd heard that she wasn't participating. Yes. Then her brother needn't approve that sentence for her.

Sweat gathered in Lin Mo's palm. He'd worried about her being dismissed as naïve. Sitting here, he could become another procedure required before her words counted. He withdrew his hand from the phone.

Someone in the corridor called another tenant's name. For an instant he felt the posture of waiting before an audience for judgment. His spine pressed the chair; the next sentence seemed about to bring a number deciding whose account sounded true.

No numbers appeared on the screen except the call duration. He looked at it, then at Xiao Lu in an ordinary chair, unhappy and waiting for an answer to her own question.

He didn't declare the dizziness a new anomaly. Zhou quietly asked whether he needed a pause. Lin Mo nodded and told Xu to wait while he drank. Xu kept talking. Lin Mo muted the microphone and took time for several mouthfuls.

Xiao Lu didn't finish everything for him. When he signaled readiness, she unmuted the microphone and repeated the question: would the colleague be told the outline wasn't approved?

Xu finally said yes. A sigh, then an offer to reconsider later.

“Our answer now doesn't need to wait for later to count,” Lin Mo said.

His sister didn't complete the sentence for him. He stopped without describing what he'd lost or proving his firmness. Written confirmation soon followed: Xu would tell his colleague not to use the outline. No filming would be arranged.

Lin Mo kept the message. It showed what Xu had now replied, not that a file had vanished from another phone. Xiao Lu understood. At least the refusal no longer existed only in their room.

Zhou brought warm water. Could the phone rest? His interrupted nap had left real irritation in his voice. Lin Mo noticed the heavy eyelids and put it down. Zhou should lie down. He'd be called if a transfer was needed; Lin Mo wouldn't attempt it alone.

Xiao Lu left the door partly closed.

Lin Mo asked whether she wanted to add a private sentence beside the outline. Her brow tightened. She'd already spoken. She didn't want to submit another reflection about it. He put the page in its own bag, not in her notebook.

The pen cap bore a shallow mark from his grip. On his current record he wrote the date, then stopped after back then. That would mingle today's admission with old recollection. He crossed it out and wrote: Today I acknowledged that I hadn't asked her first.

Xiao Lu passed, saw it and didn't bend to inspect the rest. She took her thin notebook to the window and opened it where the paper sample lay. He could see a colored corner. Whether she was writing about trial work or something else remained her own business.

A scrap of leaf had blown onto the sill. She picked it up and put it in the rubbish. Returning, she sat slightly farther from him. He didn't ask her to move closer, or make the distance mean she no longer wanted to stay. Her pen paused for long stretches as she looked outside. Constant conversation wasn't needed to establish their presence together.

Lin Mo drank slowly, leaving a damp ring on the table.

After a while she said she'd ask if tomorrow's task changed. Pay and two hours were already written down. Trial work couldn't become an indefinite obligation, and she wouldn't operate machinery she'd been told not to touch.

“Ask what you need to know,” he said.

She closed the notebook and gave him a small smile, no declaration that the argument was over. Today, she said, he hadn't added, I'll come and ask for you.

He smiled too, then stopped when his chest pulled and drank water.

His crooked writing remained. One admission hadn't reversed the old choice; his sister hadn't said she forgave him. For once, he wasn't rushing to fill the margin with reasons she must understand first.
