# Chapter 219: The Journeys Not Included

The next morning Zhou found a crumpled receipt in his coat.

A grease stain covered part of a number. He held it near the window, then moved it away; stronger light might make the faded printing harder to read. Eating porridge, Lin Mo assumed it was for medicine until he recognized the pickup location. Qin Yue was still waiting for their transport expenses.

“The trip to the shop is on my phone,” Zhou said. “The driver gave me this afterward.”

Afterward meant the hospital, then the rental. Don't send it yet. Lin Mo wanted to establish the journeys, not leave Zhou paying for whatever they couldn't identify.

Zhou propped his phone against a box on Lin Mo's left. The first trip showed its time, endpoints and actual payment. Going to the hospital had changed their return route. The receipt covered one journey; another payment had no destination attached.

Lin Mo tried flattening the paper with his left hand. His right wrist still hurt. The sleeve around the brace caught the table edge, so he settled that arm rather than leaning farther over a number.

“Remember how you paid?”

“I remember paying.” Zhou smiled. “Which record goes with which trip needs thought.”

He didn't insist merely because he'd handled it. Passing with a cup, Xiao Lu heard the locations. She'd held the bag outside the hospital and hadn't watched payment. She could confirm riding, not assign every sum to a road.

Lin Mo kept that distinction to himself instead of putting it in Qin's message.

He'd thought clear accounts meant owing nobody. The two hundred yuan had arrived, yet these scraps showed that money Zhou advanced didn't disappear because they were close. Were there other receipts? Even yesterday's water belonged among their own expenses. It needn't qualify for Qin's reimbursement to be mentioned.

“Even the water?”

“I'm not demanding a complete report. I'm worried you're only producing reimbursable costs and pretending you never spent the rest.”

Zhou watched him, then found another slip. Water and the biscuits Xiao Lu had chosen. Lin Mo remembered her opening one on the way to hospital. Busy fearing his fragment of memory would vanish, he'd scarcely considered who was paying to keep them fed and supplied with water on that journey.

Xiao Lu set down her cup.

“I have two biscuits left. Don't make it sound as though I ate somebody's emergency fund.”

He realized how grave his face had become.

“I didn't mean that.”

“Then count it normally. I ate it.”

She fetched her coat. She wanted a few things nearby. He asked what, then swallowed the next question. He needn't know every item before permitting her outside.

She answered anyway: a hair tie, since hers had broken, and perhaps a suitable notebook. She wasn't taking his old diary or buying memory-recording paper for him.

“Choose what you like.”

“I was going to.”

There was a little humor in it. He smiled, but called her back at the door. How long did she expect? If he needed something, he'd ask Zhou first. Half an hour or so; she'd message if she stayed longer. She wouldn't promise to check her phone every minute.

After the door closed, he watched it awhile.

Zhou didn't ask whether he missed her already. Outside, breakfast stalls were packing away, pans clanging against frames. They compared payment times with the receipt. One journey remained unclear. They could establish that a payment had happened without yet establishing which stretch of road it paid for. Going over the same scraps again wouldn't make the missing destination appear. Zhou called the driver's number.

Waiting for an answer, Lin Mo feared the man wouldn't remember.

The fear was disproportionate. As though an unmatched fare could remove part of yesterday, taking the hospital, hot wood and Xiao Lu's biscuit with it. He looked at his cup. Yesterday's journey didn't depend on the driver remembering today. Only the payment's correspondence remained unconfirmed.

The driver answered while waiting for a fare. Hearing the pickup point, he assumed they'd lost something. A black bag? No. Had he recorded yesterday's two journeys separately?

“With a girl in a wheelchair?”

Lin Mo's chest tightened.

Zhou confirmed the wheelchair passenger and said he was beside him, on speakerphone. The driver asked whether the hospital had been an unplanned diversion. Yes. Destination and arrival period matched. Could they read the payment reference?

Lin Mo heard his present voice.

“Wait. I'll read the last digits.”

He neither lowered it nor hurried to explain whether he was a girl. Right now he wanted to identify the money. The driver confirmed receipt. The paper covered the later trip from hospital to the rental, not both together. Nothing extra had been charged; he'd omitted the location.

Zhou thanked him. After hanging up, he paused.

“Just then…”

“I heard.”

Lin Mo knew what he meant. He didn't want every mistaken address to become an incident requiring everyone to wait for his recovery. Nor could he pretend it hadn't hurt. Looking at the paper under his cup, he said it had felt bad. He could continue now.

Zhou nodded and turned the receipt toward him without advising him to take it lightly.

They separated the actual trip to the photographer's from hospital travel. Lin Mo asked Zhou to conceal irrelevant payment details, retaining the time, locations and amount Qin needed to check. They didn't send the entire account screen or attach medical examinations.

Qin replied promptly: he'd pay that. Could they also delete a paragraph from yesterday's note?

Lin Mo's left hand stopped.

The paragraph explained that he'd left the workbench and hadn't witnessed the operation's final stage. Customers would think the observations useless, Qin said. Everyone had seen Zheng Lan leave eventually.

Lin Mo read it twice.

A short horn outside sounded like pressure to agree. He'd received two hundred yuan; Qin was willing to settle transport. Deleting one paragraph might end this comfortably.

He asked Zhou to bring the phone closer so he could type.

“That stays. Her coming out doesn't mean I watched everything inside.”

Then he added that the note wasn't meant to attract customers or guarantee safety. If Qin needed someone else's account, he should ask a witness, not edit away Lin Mo's absence.

Qin said he hadn't meant that. He only feared misunderstanding.

Lin Mo didn't debate his intention. Don't change the note and keep his name attached. After a while Qin agreed. He transferred the verified fare. Lin Mo checked and accepted it, preserving the corresponding message and evidence. The two hospital journeys hadn't slipped into it.

Finally done, Zhou said.

Lin Mo shook his head. He still owed Zhou his own expenses. They compared the confirmed advances; Lin Mo transferred repayment from his balance. Only after seeing it arrive did Zhou gather the slips. Small expenses without evidence or clear recollection could wait, not be replaced with a convenient rounded sum.

Zhou didn't immediately close the payment screen.

“Don't start counting my time here.”

Lin Mo looked up. His urgency to put things right faltered. He hadn't proposed that, but Zhou seemed to have heard another possibility in the itemized checking: fares today, helping move a leg or carrying water tomorrow.

“I meant money you've paid for me.”

“I know. Just saying it now.”

The phone slipped against the box. Catching it, Zhou pressed his palm onto the greasy receipt, then lifted the paper before it stuck. They were quiet over its fraying fibers.

Lin Mo had been about to say he remembered everything Zhou did. It sounded like a promise to repay each item eventually. He didn't know when he'd manage transfers independently or how long Zhou would stay. Turning present help into fair commerce through an uncertain future was another kind of avoidance.

“There are things I need you for. Repaying the fare hasn't changed that.”

Zhou darkened the screen and acknowledged him.

“But when you have things of your own, tell me. We'll work something out. Accepting repayment doesn't mean you have to stay forever.”

Zhou finally smiled. He'd meant to go out that afternoon for clean clothes, but these two exhausting days had made him hesitate to mention it. How long? Could he wait for Xiao Lu's return? Lin Mo wouldn't attempt an unsupported transfer. Zhou agreed; it wasn't far.

They brought lunch forward, sparing anyone a hurried, hungry departure. Lin Mo specified what he wanted rather than saying anything would do. Zhou chose his own meal, not an identical one because he happened to be beside him.

That took several minutes. The receipts remained on the table, their corners slightly flatter beneath the cup. One conversation about money and care hadn't taught them how to manage every future day. But Zhou's afternoon errand now had a place in this room's time, instead of being postponed until all Lin Mo's problems ended.

Less money remained than he'd imagined when the two hundred arrived.

He didn't regret accepting it. Nor did it mean he could support anybody yet. Water, biscuits, Xiao Lu's own hair ties and his next hospital journey would continue. He examined his balance seriously, then closed it, not wanting Zhou to think he was calculating whether caring for a person was worthwhile.

“I need to find work I can manage, gradually.”

“Not another trip today.”

“Not another place where something's gone wrong.”

Only after speaking did he understand how important the distinction was to him too. Every encounter with the old room's terror couldn't become an opportunity for recovery, with money earned alongside it. He might fail at ordinary work as well. At least he could ask what was required first.

Xiao Lu returned a little later than half an hour, carrying a paper bag.

Changing shoes, she said there'd been a queue; she'd messaged. He checked. She had. Busy answering Qin, he'd missed it. He acknowledged the message without asking why she hadn't called.

The bag held two differently colored hair ties, a thin notebook and a few oranges. The fruit was for everyone. The notebook was hers, and she didn't intend to show it yet.

“Where would be convenient?”

She chose a space beside the book and put it there herself. He saw the cover without guessing from the lifted page edges whom she would write about.

Peeling an orange, she gave Zhou the first two segments and asked whether the accounts were settled. Everything they could confirm, he said. Nobody else had been charged their hospital fares. She acknowledged that and peeled another segment, not requesting the entire story.

Lin Mo took the fruit she offered. The juice was cool, the first bite tart. His hand remained today's hand; no other throat had appeared during the call. But the sentence he hadn't deleted remained with Qin.

He hadn't required his former self to return and refuse for him.
