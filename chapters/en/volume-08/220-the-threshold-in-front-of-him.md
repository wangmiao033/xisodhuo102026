# Chapter 220: The Threshold in Front of Him

Zhou returned with clean clothes and an empty food container.

Wiping his soles at the door, he said he'd stopped to see someone. Lin Mo didn't ask whom. Xiao Lu put down her pen before offering to take the clothes. Zhou could manage. He showed them a piece of ginger left in the container: he'd eaten too quickly and mistaken it for meat.

She laughed aloud. Lin Mo laughed too, then stopped when his chest ached slightly, without requiring everyone to become solemn again.

The light outside was lovely.

From the bed, the eaves reduced the sky to a strip. He'd watched it awhile before Zhou asked whether he wanted to go out. Yes. Nowhere to inspect, no troubled shop. Just the courtyard.

Before they prepared to move, Zhou asked about his wrist. It was still painful, Lin Mo told him, though he had noticed no clear worsening since morning. He gave the answer as it was, rather than the answer most likely to get him outdoors. Wanting outside didn't prove comfort. They transferred with the usual supports, his right leg bearing no weight, his left shoulder not pushing his whole body upward. By the time he was settled, his forehead was damp.

Xiao Lu fetched a light coat. Should she come?

“If you want.”

“I meant do you need help carrying things?”

Looking at Zhou arranging the water, he said he'd like her to bring the book. She needn't read to him. She left her own notebook on the table. Nobody requested a lock; Lin Mo didn't look back at it.

The threshold looked higher from here.

Zhou knew where to stop and adjust. Today a wet cloth concealed part of the ground outside, only visible close up. He braked and asked Xiao Lu to remove it rather than let the wheels slide over it.

Someone coming upstairs caught sight of them and reached for the chair's front.

“I'll help lift.”

Lin Mo's leg tightened at lift. Unable to explain every injury in time, he said wait. The man was already bending. Xiao Lu also spoke, palm forward, without lunging into a struggle over the chair.

“Don't move it yet.”

The man let go, embarrassed. He was strong enough.

“It's not strength. My leg can't be jolted. We need to agree how we're crossing.”

Good intentions weren't a reason to endure it. Nor must he produce his whole history to make pain credible. Zhou explained they only needed room beside them. Xiao Lu put the cloth into the basin. Now they could see the route. The man stepped back. All right now? Lin Mo checked his support before agreeing.

They crossed slowly.

At the joint between surfaces he still inhaled sharply. Zhou stopped until he could continue. Behind them, the man held the door instead of grabbing again. In the courtyard Lin Mo thanked him for that, without adding that the earlier attempt hadn't mattered.

The courtyard was narrower than its sounds from inside suggested.

Two electric bikes, a hose along the wall, dispirited scallions in a plastic basin. Someone shook a dry sheet from the line, releasing a smell of detergent. For days the courtyard had been a layer of sounds outside his door. Now the hose occupied actual space to avoid. Actual shade spared his eyes.

Zhou parked clear of the route and checked the brakes. Xiao Lu chose a stool beside the steps and opened the book. Lin Mo watched a child retrieve fallen clothes pegs.

“Shall I read?”

“Wait. I want to sit awhile.”

She read to herself, turning pages without seeking permission. He watched a crack in the wall. It didn't become a rule. Its present stillness didn't guarantee no future anomaly. He was simply here, feet off the ground, water beside him.

The woman gathering sheets approached. Was he the young woman staying inside?

He looked up.

She wasn't preparing to discuss his body. Somebody had asked her to pass a message: yesterday's faulty electrical equipment would be checked that evening. Keep the area clear. She thought they might have missed the call downstairs.

“Thank you. My name's Lin Mo.”

Miss Lin, then. Please tell anyone inside.

His left fingers closed against the armrest.

“Just Lin Mo, please.”

She glanced at him, perhaps finding it rather solemn, then agreed and carried her sheets away. She didn't ask why or learn the part he hadn't said.

Xiao Lu didn't pursue her to add it.

Watching the gate, he said he'd wanted to say he was a man. Only his name had emerged. His identity wasn't a decision made today, yet a stranger and a casual form of address could obstruct the complete sentence.

“I heard you give your name,” Xiao Lu said.

“Does it seem as though I won't acknowledge it myself?”

Holding her place in the book, she said she couldn't determine what the woman had understood. Nor did she want to judge whether he'd done enough. He could speak another time. Not wanting to follow her today needn't mean punishing himself here.

The woman was discussing detergent elsewhere. Life had already left their exchange. He could follow, of course. But he didn't want to force a stranger into providing immediate proof of him.

“Not today.”

Xiao Lu reopened the book. That decision neither restored his throat nor deleted the unfinished words.

Sunlight reached his collar.

The coat was getting warm, but he said nothing. A broad men's shirt hung opposite, wind filling its empty sleeves. Seeing the shape beneath his own collar, he wanted to pull the coat closed despite his damp back.

Was he hot? Xiao Lu asked. He said he was fine, then admitted he was.

“Want it off?”

The words stopped him. Sleeping hadn't blunted yesterday's remembered refusal. But nobody was ordering him before a mirror. She meant a coat resting over him. Her hands remained on the book, not reaching to remove clothing.

“Loosen it first. Nothing underneath.”

Zhou helped clear the braced wrist, pausing at the shoulder. Lin Mo managed what his left hand allowed and explained what he couldn't reach. The coat came away from the supports. His front-opening shirt stayed on. Nobody hurried him for making it complicated. The empty shirt opposite kept fluttering.

Cool air touched his damp back. He looked down.

He hadn't suddenly come to like this body. Relief wasn't betrayal of the former one either. The shape he'd wanted hidden remained. Overheating wouldn't erase it. He asked Zhou to leave the coat visible for when he grew cold.

Xiao Lu placed the bottle within reach and rolled her own sleeves up. He wasn't the only person negotiating this patch of sunshine. He drank twice and replaced it securely. She remarked that today's oranges were rather sour.

“The later segment was better.”

“You choose next time.”

He agreed, then laughed: he might not choose sweeter ones. Two each, she suggested, not a whole large bag. They didn't immediately set a date. The courtyard breeze had given next time an ordinary destination.

Soon the man who'd held the door returned with a sheet of paper. Could anyone write clearly? The landlord needed a notice keeping the side entrance free for inspection staff. Deliveries kept blocking it. Halfway through writing, he wasn't sure where the sentences should break.

Zhou said Lin Mo's hand was injured too. The man hastily withdrew the page.

Lin Mo asked to see it, without promising help. Taking it left-handed, he found don't use the side entrance and don't block it combined into one instruction. Which did the landlord mean? He couldn't guess.

The man called from the gate. The landlord answered: keep that entrance available for maintenance workers; residents should use the other way. Don't seal it. Normal access would resume after inspection. No time could be written yet.

Lin Mo asked him to make those points clear. He'd only arrange them, not add that inspection was complete or the equipment safe. He read out a brief version of the notice, keeping the two routes distinct and leaving the uncertain finishing time uncertain. The man saved it on his phone but still disliked his handwriting. Could it be printed? Xiao Lu knew the nearby general store had a machine. She'd ask, once the landlord confirmed the wording.

After confirmation, the man gave her money for the printing. She took his phone and went.

Lin Mo didn't volunteer her for future printing trips or classify these two minutes as another consultancy. Sitting here, he'd noticed a sentence that might close the entrance they meant to leave open, and asked.

The man stayed briefly, apologizing for reversing the directions. Fortunately it wasn't posted yet, Lin Mo said. Looking at his hand, the man seemed about to ask how he'd been hurt. His lips moved. Instead he asked whether he needed deeper shade.

“Not yet. Thank you.”

Lin Mo didn't know whether this was tact or another thought interrupting. He didn't supply a moving explanation for the unasked question.

Xiao Lu returned with the printout and change, which she gave back first. The page contained no medical history or mention of yesterday's photographer. The man checked it. The landlord indicated where to post it without covering the existing contact number.

Someone at the entrance read the two sentences, then moved a box. That small usefulness earned no ringing verdict. Reaching for water, Lin Mo noticed his fingers had loosened since speaking to the stranger.

Xiao Lu offered the book again. Read now?

He read half a page. Wind lifted the corner; she held it down, hair blowing across her face. Brushing it aside freed the page again. He used the marker to steady it rather than leave her squinting for the sake of one line.

Zhou yawned beside them.

Should they return? His sleepiness didn't oblige them, Zhou said, though they'd been sitting awhile. A change of position would help. Only then did Lin Mo notice the dull discomfort around his leg support. He agreed before waiting for severe pain to justify it.

The helpful man wasn't at the threshold now. Zhou still stopped, inspected the ground and used their familiar method. Lin Mo kept wait close to his tongue, available when needed. It didn't depend on a special witness.

Inside, Xiao Lu replaced the book and washed her hands. Her own notebook remained face up on the table. Lin Mo asked Zhou for the sheet he'd begun the previous day. In a blank space he put today's date, then slowly wrote one thing that had happened.

Not that he'd finally been recognized. Not that everyone had addressed him correctly.

Today somebody wanted to lift the wheelchair. I said wait, and they stopped.

He didn't add that everyone would therefore stop in future. The page held this actual occasion. It was enough to revisit if tomorrow blurred the details.

This wasn't a supplementary page of recovered memory. The threshold had been in front of him. Today he'd crossed it himself.
