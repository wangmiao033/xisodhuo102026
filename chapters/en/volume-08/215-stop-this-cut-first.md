# Chapter 215: Stop This Cut First

“Moving now,” Liang said.

Zheng Lan gripped the towel against her thigh. Her lips moved, but Lin Mo couldn't hear an answer. Neither he nor Liang supplied one. They waited until she said through her teeth that she'd heard.

The short handle turned slightly.

The responder adjusted the support beneath the crosspiece, preventing a sudden drop. The paper didn't reverse. The cover remained at its fixed angle. Zheng Lan inhaled, then cried softly. Liang stopped with his hand steady on the handle.

“What do you feel?” the medic asked.

As though something had been pulled a little way out of her arm. An intense ache. She couldn't tell whether it was worse or looser. The medic didn't seize on looser as good news. Let the pad carry the arm; don't force it down yet.

Outside, a child laughed loudly.

The shop seemed narrower to Lin Mo. People beyond it could buy breakfast or wait for a bus. Time inside had compressed into a hand that couldn't be lowered. Qin Yue pressed toward the wall to give Liang a clearer view, touching no control.

Liang requested the position.

“Moved back. Not parked.”

The black guide hadn't moved back with the blade. Worried they might mistake the effect, Lin Mo asked whether the roller had been released. No. That distinction mattered: the paper hadn't simply returned to an earlier pose.

Zheng Lan moved her index finger.

Not the earlier half-extension and involuntary curl. She slowly straightened it, touched the pad's edge, then withdrew it herself. Her eyes widened at the medic.

“I did that.”

He asked her to rest it and describe numbness. A little, but mostly aching. Her voice no longer held only fear. The responder didn't take away the support because she could move a finger.

Liang asked about another small movement. Zheng Lan couldn't see the blade. Looking at him, she agreed, provided he didn't turn straight to the end. He announced the next attempt.

A fine scraping came from inside the casing.

Lin Mo's right palm grew hot.

A rough piece of wood lay across it, a screw projecting from a broken end. Splinters entered his skin. He saw no complete room, only a lamp-heated table edge and a hand losing its grip, then closing again. The hand was wider than today's. Pain at the wrist was terribly familiar.

He drew his right hand toward his chest. The brace struck his clothing; actual pain made him gasp.

Zhou asked what was wrong.

Liang's voice sounded distant. Had the woman moved again? Lin Mo wanted to explain that this wasn't about Zheng Lan. Two other words escaped first.

“Won't undress.”

The shop fell briefly quiet.

Liang had stopped. Zheng Lan hurried to say she hadn't spoken; her arm hadn't worsened. Lin Mo recognized Zhou before him and lowered his hand to his leg.

“Me. Not her.”

His voice shook badly. He didn't describe mirrors or voting, or say he was fine to hurry them onward. Zhou crouched where he could see his eyes. Outside?

“Yes. I can't watch the machine properly now.”

Xiao Lu put the bottle into the bag's side pocket instead of grabbing his injured arm. Zhou released the brakes and backed the wheelchair slowly. The door guard cleared an empty frame from the footrest's path. As they passed, Zheng Lan asked whether the machine had affected him too.

“I don't know what that was. I need a minute outside.”

Her danger wasn't a necessary stage in his recovery.

The threshold jolted the chair lightly. Pain in his knee made him close his eyes; the fragment of table faded. The air outside was cooler. Xiao Lu asked whether he wanted to face away from the door. Toward it, he said, but not the reflective place inside.

Zhou braked. Xiao Lu crouched, found it uncomfortable, and brought her stool outside instead. After several breaths, Lin Mo noticed he'd pinched his trouser seam into a fine crease.

“What did I say?”

“That you wouldn't undress. Then that it was you.”

“My voice…”

He didn't know how to finish. The voice in his ears had sounded deep, from a different chest. Xiao Lu and Zhou had heard only his current voice. She told him honestly: no sudden change of speaker.

He slowed his breathing against the backrest. The old palm's width seemed to remain beside his hand. He almost lifted the brace to inspect it, stopping at its edge. Xiao Lu reminded him the doctor had fixed it for a reason. It wasn't a tool for testing a memory.

“I didn't undo it.”

“I saw.”

Ordinary words, not those of a guard appointed to restrain him. A breakfast seller accepted money. Plastic bags snapped in the breeze. Inside, Liang remained behind the machine. Zheng Lan's right hand held the towel, her left supported. Nobody had stopped to wait for Lin Mo's return.

The breakfast seller approached with a folding chair, asking whether the young woman needed to lie down too. Lin Mo knew she meant him, but his throat caught. The broad hand in his mind had dragged him toward a place that no longer fitted.

Zhou thanked her; the wheelchair back was enough. She offered a sheltered patch beside her stall if they obstructed the door. Xiao Lu waited for Lin Mo to choose. Here for now. He wasn't angry at the offer. He wanted to see the doorway.

The woman carried her chair away without being made responsible for an argument about his identity. Lin Mo stared at his left hand.

“I really was broader before.”

Then he worried he'd made it sound as though he'd remembered everything. He already knew his body had changed. Today's face wasn't a discovery. But the weight of that other palm had briefly become something beneath his skin instead of a reported fact. It came with fear, refusing the order he wanted.

“With the wood?” Xiao Lu asked.

“Yes. Heat too. Nothing else clearly.”

“Do you want to tell it all now?”

“I don't know if telling it will grow more things.”

She first asked whether he'd struck his wrist. He checked the brace: clothing, not the table. The sudden movement had hurt. Her shoulders eased. Establish that first. The fragment of room needn't be completed immediately.

He wanted her to tell him how he'd once been, sparing him the unfamiliar body that also felt his. Seeing her tired eyes, he asked instead whether she'd listen to a little of it when they returned.

“A little. Not everything now.”

He let go of his trouser seam. She moved the stool to level ground and stretched her feet. They didn't hold hands. The space between them nevertheless felt quieter.

Liang announced another turn of the handle. Lin Mo heard clearly this time without adding instructions.

The scraping continued briefly, then stopped.

“At the notch.”

The technician asked whether the carriage had actually seated and the blade reentered its protective edge. A handle refusing to turn didn't prove completion. Liang adjusted the torch. The support concealed part of the carriage; he'd inspect from the side without removing it. They wouldn't extend the cutter again merely to see.

Qin crouched where Liang permitted, read the rest of the faded label and checked the repair record. The downward notch was the parked position. Only after inspecting from both sides did Liang confirm the metal was seated and the cutting edge enclosed.

The casing wasn't perfectly quiet.

A breeze tapped the loose cover. Everyone froze. The paper didn't advance; Zheng Lan's hand didn't rise. Liang kept the cover stabilized. One harmless movement didn't justify releasing it.

“Don't straighten yet,” the medic said. “Tell me what you want to move.”

Her fingers. She relaxed all five, letting the heel of her palm settle into the pad. Raising her head hurt. She stopped.

From outside, Lin Mo couldn't see every finger. He heard her say this felt like her own sore neck rather than something continuing to twist it. The medic supported her. The distinction wasn't an order to stand immediately.

“Can we pull the sheet now?” Qin asked.

“Leave it.”

Liang's firmness made Qin flinch. He put both hands on his knees, accepting that rescuing a customer didn't require restoring the shop to business in the same moment.

They helped Zheng Lan lower her arm. The pad moved first; then she relaxed into what her body could manage. Twice they stopped. Someone removed the little stool in front of her. Another watched her feet. Nobody hauled her up by the armpits. Trying to look toward the door, she stopped again at the ache in her neck.

With the positions inside changed, Lin Mo could see that her shoulder was no longer drawn so high. Sweat creased the pale fabric at her collar. No photograph needed to smooth it.

Her bag remained within view, phone in its pocket. Asked about standing, she wanted to sit a little longer. Both feet tingled. Liang prevented anyone from hurrying her or gathering at the door to inspect the outcome.

“Go home?” Xiao Lu asked Lin Mo.

He began to shake his head, then answered aloud. Wait until she was out. He wouldn't reenter.

“You don't have to see the ending for it to count as something you helped with.”

“I know. I want to know she can leave.”

She moved her stool clear of the route. Zhou draped a light coat over Lin Mo. He thought it was for the breeze until the collar touched the sweat soaking his back.

Zheng Lan finally came through the doorway supported, taking a short step, then resting. She didn't smile or thank everyone first. Her eyes searched for the vehicle. The medic pointed it out. She asked about her bag. Qin held it up behind her.

“Don't put it back on the counter.”

“It's coming with you.”

At the vehicle, they asked Qin to stop explaining and set the bag where directed, clear of the transfer. Zheng Lan gave her name and contact person herself. She corrected a digit. Being able to speak didn't erase her injury. She asked where they were going and listened before releasing the door edge.

Looking once toward the shop, she said she wasn't having that work photograph taken. Nobody corrected her about possibly needing one later. The door closed. Outside, there was finally no hand held perpetually in the air.

Lin Mo thought of his own two escaped words and tasted bitterness. He didn't take out the notebook in the street or ask Xiao Lu to explain what had happened to him before. Liang came outside to record what each person had observed. Could he wait a few minutes?

“Yes. There was a part I didn't see. I need to keep that separate.”

He adjusted the coat clear of his wrist and waited for Liang to begin with where the blade had stopped.

He waited for Liang’s next question.
