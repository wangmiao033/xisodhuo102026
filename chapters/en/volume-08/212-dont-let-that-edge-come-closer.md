# Chapter 212: Don't Let That Edge Come Closer

Liang first had the loose papers cleared from the counter.

Not the paper inside the machine. Old leaflets had been used beneath cups, leaving no room for medical supplies. At their rustling, Zheng Lan stiffened and caught the stool's edge with her right hand. The responder stopped, showed her which stack he was moving, then continued.

Two of her fingers slowly loosened.

The same sound now came from too many places. Qin Yue started to explain that nobody had touched the trapped sheet. Liang shook his head. Qin swallowed the explanation and removed a curling mat by the doorway so the medical case could sit flat.

“Can you see behind it?” Liang asked Lin Mo.

“Not from here.”

The workbench hid the lower section. Through the glass counter, even the gray cover had a doubled edge. He wouldn't turn a reflection into an object merely to sound useful. He could see the paper's end and one dark line. Someone behind the bench would have to check the rest.

Liang inspected the space before letting Zhou move the wheelchair. The near side of the entrance lay outside the medics' route. A little farther in was possible, but no closer than that. Asked whether he wanted to move, Lin Mo checked the way back before agreeing.

Zhou didn't yank the entire chair over the low threshold. Another person steadied its opposite side. They checked that the right footrest would clear the cabinet, then eased forward. A jolt sent pain up Lin Mo's right leg.

“Stop.”

They stopped just inside. Zhou waited while he breathed out, without saying they were almost there. His foot remained supported and his ankle hadn't twisted; neither fact proved nothing had happened. Liang asked whether he wanted to leave.

“This is enough. No farther.”

The doorway was behind him to the left. Street sounds still came through.

Xiao Lu stayed near it with his water. She didn't accept the extra photograph Qin offered. He drew back and put it on the empty counter, explaining that he only wanted to show what a normal proof looked like. He didn't insist she hold it.

Lin Mo left the unfamiliar face alone.

“Whose is the sheet caught now?”

“Zheng Lan's. The middle picture.”

Three portraits, Qin said, arranged horizontally. The other two belonged to the same printing batch. He began to say those people were certainly fine, then stopped. Had he contacted them? No. They weren't in the shop; neither had called.

“Then we don't know yet,” Liang said.

That didn't turn the danger into something happening across the entire street. Lin Mo wouldn't imagine two unseen people suffering elsewhere and use his own imagined emergency to hurry everyone here.

Zheng spoke softly.

“The middle one's me. But it isn't the picture I wanted to submit.”

She'd needed an identification portrait for work, Qin explained. There had been a reshoot, followed by a trial crop. She'd rejected it. When he tried to print the full composition, the narrow-edged version remained. Her breathing grew heavier. The medic asked him to pause.

“He took it while I was picking up my bag,” she said.

Her right hand moved slightly toward the bag by the counter, then stopped, as though repeating a gesture might cause another harm. The medic said she didn't need to demonstrate. She could explain. Her hand returned to her lap.

Her left arm had been lifting; her head wasn't straight. She'd said wait. Agreeing afterward to sit properly didn't mean the first exposure could be selected for her. Yesterday she'd left. Today she'd returned for a refund, not to prove which pose she could manage.

Lin Mo looked at her supported forearm. Similarity wasn't a complete correspondence. He couldn't see the printed fingers clearly enough to claim every bend was being copied. But her rejection of that photograph needed no invention.

“You don't have to lift the bag again.”

“I couldn't if I wanted to.”

The rest of his reassurance stopped. He didn't correct himself by saying he'd meant her right hand. She needed the trapped arm lowered, not his sentence made faultless.

Someone behind the bench described the sheet. Its right corner had folded beside a rear roller. A narrow strip at the front remained under the gray cover. That cover belonged to an added assembly in the output path, not the printer's main shell. Qin admitted he'd called everything the printer without distinguishing the attachment.

Liang had him indicate the ordinary power connections without reconnecting anything. Responders checked the accessible disconnected ends and moved cables away from feet. Lin Mo couldn't see every connection across the bench. He heard what had been checked. Nobody declared the paper incapable of moving again.

A customer called from outside, asking for developed photographs. Qin answered automatically, then looked at Zheng. Liang had the doorway attendant explain that the shop was closed. Business couldn't continue across the space she occupied. Qin asked about photographs already packed in the cabinet. He wasn't left to decide which package was certainly unrelated. For now, he could take the customer's contact details.

The customer complained about making two trips. The voice moved farther away without becoming magically understanding. Qin's receipt had damp marks where he'd squeezed it. He put it somewhere dry and used another sheet for the name. Ordinary demands hadn't disappeared, but they couldn't displace the rescue.

Zheng heard too. She told the medic she wasn't deliberately holding up the shop. He didn't ask her to sympathize with its owner. He asked whether loosening her collar would help. She started to nod, caught herself and indicated the zipper with her right hand. He lowered it a short distance without pulling the affected shoulder.

It was a very small opening. Nobody could point to it and claim to have solved the emergency. Yet her next breath no longer snagged in her throat. Lin Mo waited for it to settle before listening to the next description from behind the machine.

“Usually I just pull a jammed sheet out,” Qin said quietly.

Liang looked up.

“This time a person hurts when it moves. Don't pull.”

Qin stepped back with empty hands. He hadn't actually reached in. Perhaps he'd only wanted to finish a familiar task. Once an ordinary movement became unusable, even standing there seemed to leave him nothing useful to do.

“What about the picture she selected? If I bring that up and replace—”

“We're not restarting the screen to test it,” Liang said.

Lin Mo looked at the black display. There had been a different piece of paper at the laundry. That didn't mean this problem required the correct photograph inserted somewhere. Familiar shapes offered tempting shortcuts. Here there were three proofs and a cutting assembly that hadn't settled, not a worktop he'd already understood.

He said so. He didn't suggest making Zheng accept an original portrait.

At the assurance that she needn't pose again, her mouth moved without becoming a smile. The medic asked about numbness. Before, she'd only ached. Now it felt like needles being crowded inside her fingers. They adjusted support without pulling her wrist and prepared to change the person holding it.

Through the handover she watched both pairs of hands. Only when the new padding was secure did her eyes close briefly.

Lin Mo had held his breath too.

Xiao Lu said his name and indicated his shoulder. He'd been leaning for too long, lifting the left shoulder as though getting nearer could take weight from someone else's arm. Slowly he settled against the chair. The pain didn't disappear.

“Water?”

“Leave it for now.”

She didn't ask whether he'd worked it out. He was grateful for that, without turning her present quiet into a lifelong understanding he couldn't remember. Missing parts of their past couldn't be filled with what she happened to do well today.

Qin found a small flashlight beneath the counter. Two presses made it work. He handed it to Liang. Light crossed the side of the cover and struck a narrow mirror beside the workbench.

White brightness flashed toward Lin Mo's eyes.

Suddenly the face seemed too close.

He hadn't recognized an old face. Though seated, he felt his chest close to another cold, hard surface, his eyes compelled to remain open, unable to remove himself from the brightness first. The fear had neither beginning nor end. Not even a room he could identify.

His right hand jerked. The brace caught it immediately.

Pain brought someone's call to stop into focus. Zhou steadied the chair back without pressing the injured hand. After several disordered breaths, the whiteness separated into flashlight, mirror edge and water marks on the counter.

“Move the light,” Liang said.

The beam turned downward; the room wasn't plunged into darkness. Zheng still breathed against the support. Her emergency hadn't been replaced by his startled movement. Lin Mo fixed on tiny paper flecks against her free hand, things belonging to the room he could actually identify.

“Outside?” Zhou asked.

“Back a little first.”

The chair moved a short distance, still just inside along the edge. He didn't push on its arms. His supported leg moved with it and still hurt. He told Liang that the reflection had frightened him. He didn't know whether it had been a memory. It hadn't been a message in the machine.

“Then we don't use it to decide what to do.”

The plain response quieted an urgent feeling in his chest. He'd almost wanted another look, to discover whether the brightness might yield more. Zheng's sharp inhalation cut that desire short. Someone else's danger couldn't become a room in which he reenacted his own past.

He asked Zhou to stay where they were, without claiming he'd completely recovered.

Something clicked at the rear.

This time he saw the pale printed sleeve shift a tiny distance and stop. Zheng's left shoulder shuddered forward. The responder moved the padding with her rather than forcing her body to remain fixed. Liang immediately asked whether anyone had touched the back. Nobody had.

The flashlight found the folded right corner.

Paper pressed hard against the casing. The rear roller seemed intent on carrying it onward, though Lin Mo couldn't tell from a sound whether a motor was turning. He could see the crease deepening. Above the black edge, a short bright piece of metal appeared when the light changed and vanished beneath the cover's shadow when it changed again.

“Don't move it,” Liang said.

Qin's hand withdrew slowly from the bench's edge.

“Is it all right if it's jammed?” Zheng asked.

Lin Mo almost nodded. The paper had indeed stopped advancing. Her hand was still raised, and the medic was still asking what she could feel. The folded corner had given them time they couldn't waste, not permission to declare an ending.

“It's jammed,” he said. “It isn't all right yet.”

Tears fell again. She didn't ask another question.

Liang had someone watch the rear without disturbing the fold. Another responder went for something that could safely support the assembly. The proof remained where it was. Nobody pulled it out. The front display stayed black.

Lin Mo lowered his shoulder. Outside, wheels crossed an iron street cover so near it sounded beside the door.

The last spot of white brightness slowly faded from his eyes.
