# Chapter 216: There Were Things He Hadn't Seen

Liang didn't make Lin Mo return to the workbench to give his account.

He brought the clipboard outside, asked whether this spot was too cold, and kept the passage clear. Ordinary power remained off. Separate supports held the machine base, carriage and cover; the paper stayed inside. Zheng Lan leaving hadn't reopened the shop.

Lin Mo began with their arrival.

Describing the black cropping edge, he started with I saw, then corrected himself. Initially he'd seen a line. Only the changed torch angle revealed its thickness above the paper. Liang had described the far linkage from behind the machine. Lin Mo hadn't sat there or seen both sides of the carriage enter its parked position.

Liang paused.

“What you heard afterward goes separately.”

Lin Mo identified when he'd left. He described the fragment of the old room only as a powerful sense of familiarity that prevented him attending to the present. The wood, mirror and refusal to undress didn't belong in Zheng Lan's incident account. Explaining when he'd become unable to observe didn't require surrendering a private experience to a public pavement.

Had he worked with similar equipment?

“Similar anomalies. Not this machine.”

“Had you given Qin repair advice?”

“No. I came to observe. You and the technician established what the short handle connected to.”

Afraid of sounding evasive, he added his own suggestions: not releasing the jammed paper, and suspecting the cutting process still affected the woman. Liang recorded both. I don't understand wouldn't erase what Lin Mo had actually said.

Liang read the account back. Lin Mo changed the machine stopped to the paper wasn't advancing. A later reader shouldn't mistake that for his certifying a motionless machine harmless.

Qin Yue waited nearby. His phone lit several times. Eventually he stepped away to answer quietly. Lin Mo heard him repeat that he wouldn't restart it, but couldn't identify the caller. That didn't prove he'd warned every customer or establish what he'd do later.

Liang asked Qin to preserve the actual settings, rejected proofs and original files separately. Faces in the other photographs didn't establish other victims; lack of reports didn't establish their safety either. Qin promptly promised not to delete anything.

“I've deleted unwanted pictures before.”

“Don't delete these to make it look as though this didn't happen.”

Qin understood, but kept watching the bench. Would none of this have happened if he hadn't corrected that shoulder line?

Lin Mo couldn't confirm it.

“You knew she didn't want that picture. We haven't established why the anomaly followed.”

Qin seemed to find this both too severe and too slight. He wanted one definite mistake whose admission would guarantee no recurrence. Lin Mo recognized the desire from reading about the umbrella, wanting one new action to replace everything unknown.

“I wasn't trying to make her look unlike herself.”

“When she said it wasn't her, did you hear?”

After a long silence, Qin admitted he had. He'd thought she wasn't used to the adjustment and would appreciate it when she submitted the picture. He rubbed his phone's edge without requesting reassurance that every photographer did this.

Zhou had arranged transport. Was Lin Mo's hand still painful?

He moved the fingers of his left hand before answering, as though that small motion might help him find a more accurate description of the other one. The pain in his right wrist had eased since the sudden pull. Eased was not the same as gone, and he told Zhou that it still hurt. Hearing, Liang suggested he report the sudden strain to a medic. An existing brace wasn't a reason to ignore it. The medic remaining at the scene asked about impact and new numbness. Lin Mo answered precisely. He should have his treating team examine it; the medic wouldn't remove the brace or declare him fine on the pavement.

Xiao Lu loosened the outer sleeve without touching the support. Zhou said they could go to the hospital. Lin Mo first asked Qin whether the agreed observation note could follow later. Writing immediately might mix his disorientation into the account.

“You're still writing it?”

“What I saw, what I don't know. Not a guarantee the machine's safe.”

Wasn't Liang's record enough? The two records served different purposes, Lin Mo said. Liang had needed an account of the emergency from the people present. Qin had asked him to look at a particular problem and explain the limits of what he could observe. Those were not interchangeable just because both involved the same workbench. He couldn't pass off the rescue account as his promised work. Nor would his note collect customers' private photographs.

Qin opened a payment screen for the agreed two hundred yuan. Lin Mo asked him to wait for the short note. Transport would follow actual receipts, not invented journeys rounded into a whole day's charge. The rescue and medical care were separate. His fee wasn't the price of everything that had happened.

“I'm not refusing payment. I haven't delivered what I promised yet.”

Qin finally seemed to understand he wasn't turning it into a speech about money being inappropriate around a rescue. He closed the screen. Lin Mo asked him not to post that he'd hired somebody who could solve anomalies. Embarrassed, Qin admitted he'd thought about posting that the customer had been rescued.

“Only what you can confirm yourself. Not her photograph.”

Qin nodded, then said Zheng Lan had already made her own objection clear. He would follow it; he didn't ask Lin Mo to authorize an image for her.

The ride to the hospital was slow.

Transferring into the vehicle still required pauses. Zhou used their familiar method without pulling the braced wrist. Lin Mo wanted to shut his eyes, but saw hot wood beneath a lamp and opened them again. Outside were plastic crates from stalls packing away. Xiao Lu put water within reach.

She didn't ask whether he'd remembered her.

“Why haven't you asked?” he said.

“You said you'd tell a little when we got back.”

“I thought you'd want to know.”

“I do. I also want to reach the hospital. You keep grimacing.”

He relaxed his jaw, realizing he'd clenched it since leaving the doorway. She offered a wrapped biscuit. He preferred to wait until they stopped; swallowing felt difficult. She ate her own. His thinking about something important didn't postpone her hunger.

At the hospital, they waited.

The receptionist looked between his face and the registration. His fingers tightened against the wheelchair armrest. He had already begun preparing for a question about the mismatch she might have noticed, though she had not actually asked one. She only wanted to know which clinic had handled the previous review. He answered and breathed out.

They sat clear of the corridor door. A young man nearby stood and straightened his jacket across broad shoulders, revealing a damp collar. Lin Mo stared briefly, then looked away. The man had only stood to adjust his own clothes. Someone else's body wasn't a specimen placed in the corridor for reconstructing Lin Mo's past.

“Another episode?” Zhou asked.

“Not like before. I keep searching.”

For a width, a posture, a movement proving he'd lived. Knowing he shouldn't stare didn't immediately stop the impulse. Xiao Lu didn't summon the man or tell Lin Mo his present body was fine. She offered her phone: should they choose lunch?

He hesitated.

“Eating won't make that little bit disappear?”

He heard his own urgency. She didn't know and wouldn't guarantee a memory's survival. She only wanted lunch arranged, rather than leaving him hungry while he waited.

He chose something soft. The feeling of heated wood remained, neither more complete from being grasped nor erased by a meal selection. He returned the phone and waited for his name.

Before it was called, a nurse came to verify the date of his latest immobilization. She kept the form low and her voice quiet. Taking it, Lin Mo saw the familiar sex field. His hand stopped in midair.

That entry hadn't changed because he'd glimpsed an older hand.

Thinking he'd missed her question, the nurse repeated the date. He asked to consult his follow-up paperwork rather than calculate backward from memory. Zhou opened the medical folder and removed one relevant sheet, without spreading out the rest of the medical papers. The nurse compared dates and asked whether anything else needed adding.

Lin Mo wanted to say he was a man.

It wasn't a discovery made today, or a protest prompted by being called a young woman. But he hadn't worked out what he wanted changed in this particular exchange. A fragment of feeling couldn't turn his actual injuries into those of a previous body. Today's wrist really was this narrow. Treatment needed to address this wrist.

“Call me Lin Mo. Some of my older memories are incomplete. Use the date on that sheet.”

The nurse nodded and continued her work without asking what completeness meant. His palm remained hot. He felt he'd left something important unsaid. Not finishing it in a corridor didn't make it nonexistent.

Xiao Lu didn't finish the sentence for him. She closed the medical folder where it could be retrieved easily. Later he asked whether he'd taken too long. The nurse had been waiting for them to check, she said. Nobody had hurried him.

The doctor examined him and heard the actual movement he'd made. Lin Mo didn't remove his own brace. The new wrist pain required observation; his right leg remained non-weight-bearing and the shoulder restrictions stayed. Asked about the sudden fragment—dizziness, lost consciousness, changed vision—he answered what he could distinguish, not calling an earlier dream part of today's event.

He began asking whether repeated exposure to similar mirrors might restore memory faster. Halfway through, he stopped. The doctor advised against using dangerous sites as stimulation experiments. Worsening symptoms should prompt help as instructed. Rest first, with further assessment to be arranged. No exercise was promised to reconstruct all the missing past.

Xiao Lu waited by the door while he explained what he'd asked. Her face changed at mirrors, but she didn't scold him publicly. He said he wasn't planning to return to the shop. She nodded slightly.

By the time lunch reached the rental, it was late.

Zhou cleared a dry part of the table for food, without spreading out the rest of the medical papers. Lin Mo settled and managed several bites. His phone received Qin's message: Zheng Lan's family had reached her; she was still being examined. There was no diagnosis and no claim that she'd forgiven him.

Lin Mo acknowledged it and set the phone down. The two hundred yuan remained unpaid, the observation note unwritten, the machine in a closed shop. A morning ending didn't finish every matter within it.

Xiao Lu ate half her bowl and asked whether he still wanted to talk.

“Yes. Just the piece of wood.”

She moved her bowl aside without producing a pen. Zhou narrowed the window opening and took his own food farther over. They could still call him, but needn't explain every sentence to a third person.
