# Chapter 234: Who Keeps the Next Show Going?

"Back where?" Lin Mo asked.

The man's badge scraped the table edge. Instead of answering, he reached for the continuation strip beside the register. The officer blocked his hand and asked what it was for.

People attending the next session, the man said.

"Where are they?"

"Some are still inside."

"Have they agreed?"

He looked toward the auditorium. Another length of paper emerged, bearing a surname Lin Mo had just heard. Mr. Shao had risen from the bench and taken two steps with the nurse. Seeing the paper move, he stopped and tucked his injured hand inside his clothes.

Lin Mo didn't ask him to come back. He asked Qin Yue to read the tickets from outside the inner doorway so Shao could confirm them where he stood. Row Three, Seat Seven; then the matching serial numbers. The void ticket with the same seat number remained separate.

Xiao Lu studied the register. The columns showed current session, continuation, and a narrow return field. Shao's name ran into the next line. Still Seat Seven, but a diagonal connected it to a later time that day. There was no second signature, only a clerk's check mark.

"Same time as the back of the yellow slip," Qin Yue said.

Shao had received one ticket. At the entrance, someone had promised to help explain his frightening experience if he told them about it. He had begun, then wanted to stop. They told him to wait while others spoke, or nobody would acknowledge what he had said.

"You don't need to tell it again," Lin Mo said.

The staff member gave a short laugh. "You don't get in without telling something."

Ms. Luo turned over a leaflet. Three blank fields asked for the experience, the unfinished answer, and the moment the visitor most wanted to return to. She'd been recruited to collect tickets. The organizers called it bearing witness for one another: you listened to the others before receiving your own turn.

"You said two copies meant tickets wouldn't get lost. You never mentioned another session."

The man said arrangements had changed while she was busy helping people.

Luo pointed out two neighboring entries to the officer. Both people had already spoken, yet the screen was still calling them to listen. Lin Mo could read one of their names through the inner doorway. Beneath it was a title made from that person's experience.

Hearing a story registered you as its witness. Your own became an inducement for someone else to sit down. One register and one room were enough to turn his stomach. His cropped report sat at the top of the leaflet, guaranteeing an arrangement he had never approved.

Qin Yue explained how he had received the invitation and preserved the forwarded message for the police to check. He didn't use a phone number to accuse everyone he had dealt with.

"Who are you working for?" the officer asked.

The man first claimed not to know, then looked at Lin Mo.

"After it fell apart, how many people even knew what they had left? What's wrong with bringing it back?"

"The Theater?"

"Some people want it back. Not everybody." His voice quickened. "You've been there. You know people can change places. Of course you hate it when someone else always gets the high seats."

A pale old mark showed on his wrist. Lin Mo didn't know what had caused it. It explained nothing by itself.

Luo swore under her breath.

"Let the people out first."

The man shouted toward the hall. "Keep Row Three, Seat Seven! Don't leave it empty!"

A gray sleeve appeared through the doorway, carrying a narrow strip toward the replacement clip. Qin Yue turned to block the hand. It kept coming. The paper touched the clip's mouth.

Shao clutched his hand. "It's tightening again!"

"He didn't agree!" Luo put down the box and hurried to Qin Yue's side, blocking the clip.

An officer reached the inner door and ordered the person holding the strip to put it down and step back. The hand hesitated, then laid the paper on an empty stool. At the table, the other officer made the man face him and stop calling instructions inside.

Qin Yue didn't tear the strip. Guarding the clip, he asked whether the person beyond the doorway knew Shao had come out.

Yes, came the reply. But they'd been told to keep his seat anyway. Missing numbers would be filled from behind.

Shao took a step toward his old place. The nurse steadied him. His coat was still there, he said; he only wanted to fetch it.

"Someone can bring it," Xiao Lu said. "You told us you didn't want to go in."

He stopped. The coat hung over a nearby seatback. Qin Yue asked which one, lifted it from the visible outer side, and showed Shao the cuff before handing it to the nurse. Shao held it against him and stayed where he was.

Xiao Lu read the paper's latest line aloud: a continuation booking, with a replacement taking the seat.

The new strip carried the same serial. Someone had prepared another entry to fill the vacancy as soon as the yellow counterpart left the clip.

"Don't pull anything apart," Lin Mo said. "Look at the return field."

Luo came back to the table. Xiao Lu opened the neighboring page, leaving the stubs undisturbed. Several groups were marked withdrawn, their numbers intact and yellow counterparts folded back against their original fastening points. The little boxes beside them were unchecked.

Luo recognized one. That visitor had decided against attending before entering. The registrar had told her how to return the tickets. She had matched the pair herself, without tearing out the name.

Lin Mo asked her to start with the first step.

Pointing to the record rather than reenacting anything with a live ticket, she explained. The visitor had stated the decision; the registrar canceled the continuation entry. Luo returned the counterpart to its original fastening point. The current-session group was closed last. The original ticket was preserved and nothing went into the replacement clip.

"He's already sat down," the staff member said. "What good is a void ticket to you?"

"That's why nobody's moved his yet."

Lin Mo asked about the instruction the sticker had concealed on the yellow slip. The same position in the register carried the complete printed text. Luo lowered the lamp; Xiao Lu read it. Current-session withdrawal and continuation cancellation were recorded separately. A canceled continuation could not be converted into a replacement for the current session.

A short stroke in Shao's return field had been covered with red ink.

Luo stared at it. When he had asked to leave, she'd requested a withdrawal and been told it was recorded. It had been. Then the red diagonal had crossed out of that field and joined the later session.

"You put him back," she said.

"We only held his place."

"You held him. He's the one it hurt. You tricked me into putting that slip in and telling him he was all right."

Lin Mo addressed Shao. "Do you want the later booking canceled? Just your decision. You don't need to explain what happened to you."

"I don't want another session. I want out of this one too."

Xiao Lu didn't move the tickets. She asked Luo to identify the matching fastening point and Qin Yue to read the original number while she checked both entries. She mistook a zero for a six. Qin Yue stopped her and repeated the digit from the original.

Her face reddened. She read it again, including the digits on either side.

"They match."

The officer watched the table. Qin Yue guarded the clip, keeping the new strip away. Luo recorded Shao's decision in the existing cancellation field and carried the register to the outer side of the inner doorway. Qin Yue read the serial on the torn white piece at the chair edge. Luo returned its yellow counterpart to the original fastening point.

The paper advanced a little. Shao's name did not vanish immediately. Xiao Lu stayed beside Lin Mo to read the roll. He asked her to wait for a change rather than hurry Luo onward.

The continuation entry changed to returned.

Shao still cradled his hand. It had eased, he said, but not completely. Lin Mo asked the nurse to stay beside him where they were. They wouldn't test it by making him walk farther toward the exit.

Luo checked the current-session withdrawal, preserving both pieces of the white ticket. Qin Yue guarded the clip while she recorded his withdrawal in the page's existing closure field and separated the returned yellow counterpart from the outstanding tickets. Both white pieces remained where they were. They stopped and watched.

[Row Three, Seat Seven: Withdrawn from current session.]

A snapping sound came from a seatback inside.

Shao drew his hand out, moved his fingertips, then slowly opened it. The red marks remained, but it no longer angled toward the door. After examining him, the nurse helped him toward the ambulance. He paused beside Lin Mo, as though wanting to speak and afraid that stopping would trap him again.

"Get it checked first," Lin Mo said.

Shao nodded and went. Qin Yue returned the register to the reception table, keeping the verified group separate from the outstanding papers.

Someone in a back row called to leave, rose, caught against a chair edge, and sat again. Some entries lacked matching tickets; some people were afraid to answer their names.

Luo's arm shook after supporting Shao for so long. Qin Yue noticed and had her sit on an empty stool outside the doorway. She would identify only what she had actually handled. He moved where people could hear him, while Xiao Lu checked numbers at the table. They dealt with one entry at a time. Police kept the man away from the register and the exit clear, so those who could leave didn't have to crowd around the desk again.

At the third group, pain tightened from Lin Mo's shoulder into his neck. He started to brace against the wheelchair, then stopped his left arm.

Old Zhou bent down. "We need to adjust that."

"After this—"

Lin Mo heard himself. Xiao Lu was still pointing out a matched pair to Qin Yue, not coming over to finish his sentence.

"Now," he said.

Zhou asked for space and settled the support. Sweat ran behind Lin Mo's ear as he leaned back. Xiao Lu drank some of her own water before offering his cup. When he couldn't hold it securely, she put it within reach and resumed her work.

By the time the paper stopped feeding, it was dark.

[No further seating for this session.]

Several unresolved places remained below the notice. Luo said they weren't starting another round here. Those able to leave were with medical staff; the rest needed individual checks. Lin Mo couldn't see backstage or establish whether other registers existed elsewhere.

From the far side of the table, the man called his name.

"Is that enough for you?"

Turning tugged Lin Mo's shoulder. He didn't ask Zhou to move him closer.

"There's a way to remember everything you lost. You still have that notebook, don't you? Bring it. Let her cooperate once more, and there's a way."

Xiao Lu's cup touched the table softly.

For a moment, Lin Mo felt an absence in his chest. He wanted to tell his own past from the beginning, to place a gesture in its year and month, to know why he had held a particular hand. This man offered one promise. Without asking what the notebook contained, he had already made Xiao Lu part of the price.

"Words in a notebook aren't the lived memories that were deleted," Lin Mo said. "Tell me what you actually have."

The man glanced at the officer, then indicated the register's rear pocket.

The officer removed two sheets. One listed recent staff and ticket collectors; a name matched the man's badge, though his identity still needed checking. The other was folded in four. Next-session reception appeared outside, above a local district name. Lin Mo didn't ask them to unfold the address for his sake or send Qin Yue to investigate.

"That's where the next one is," the man said. "Go there if you want to find out whether you can remember."

Lin Mo watched the folded paper. Sweat had dried on his hand; his right wrist still ached inside its support. Someone outside called for medical help. Zhou moved an empty chair out of the passage.

Neither his sister nor the private notebook would be handed over for that promise. The unanswered question remained.

"First tell me what you claim you can bring back. Then tell me what you want me to pay."
