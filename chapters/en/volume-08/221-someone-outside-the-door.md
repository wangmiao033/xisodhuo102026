# Chapter 221: Someone Outside the Door

The electrician called from downstairs rather than knocking.

Lin Mo had just put away today's record. A ladder struck the corridor wall. Behind it, the landlord explained that a connection needed checking; residents should use the other entrance. The notice printed that afternoon was up, but someone still squeezed past, asking why the route was suddenly closed.

Zhou stood in the doorway with water. Should he half-close the door against the dust?

“Leave a gap.”

Xiao Lu came through and pulled it wide open.

She seemed not to have heard. Would they cut the power? The electrician couldn't say until he'd looked. Don't touch the switches over there. The landlord pointed at a cable and asked them to move the chair nearest the entrance.

Xiao Lu's coat lay on it. She picked it up without retreating into the room.

Lin Mo told Zhou he wasn't obstructing anything. Put the water on his left. Xiao Lu suddenly asked whether he should return to bed.

The dull pain from the courtyard transfer remained in his right leg. Moving again meant another effort. He wanted to sit until dinner, not fold away his evening because someone was outside.

“Not yet.”

She nodded, holding her coat.

Someone called the landlord about a locked door downstairs. He went for a key, leaving the electrician gathering cable. Xiao Lu asked how long he'd be there. Her words came quickly. It depended what was wrong, he said.

She folded the coat once; a sleeve fell free. Lin Mo called her to finish the oranges. In a moment. The electrician shouted downstairs, his voice bouncing off the narrow walls. She flinched and drew one foot inside.

“Need to let somebody past?” Zhou asked.

She shook her head.

Lin Mo's phone rang: the food delivery. It was charging outside. Xiao Lu reached it first and answered, explaining which staircase to use and where to leave the food. Afterwards she kept the phone.

“Pass it here. I need to check which order is mine.”

She turned the screen towards him without releasing it.

He could see half an order. His left finger reached and withdrew. The braced right wrist couldn't help him wrestle for it.

“Xiao Lu. I need to hold it.”

His voice had cooled. She put the phone beside the cup. Zhou moved the water before anything spilled. Nothing had happened, yet the room went quiet.

Tools continued outside.

Lin Mo checked the order and left the phone on the little table by his legs. Xiao Lu draped her coat over the chair. Saying never mind would let dinner proceed easily. But her hand around his phone had reminded him of people discussing his destination over his head outside the hospital.

“Why wouldn't you give it back?”

“I wasn't refusing.”

“I asked twice.”

She looked down at her empty hand.

He almost asked whether she feared a call from Qin Yue, then stopped. Offering his guess would leave her only agreement or denial. He waited until footsteps approached again.

The delivery. Zhou went to collect it. Xiao Lu followed at once. Warned about the cable, she caught the threshold with her toe and steadied herself against the wall. She didn't fall. Lin Mo told her not to crowd forward; somebody had the food.

She turned, and the tension in her face broke.

“Don't send me outside.”

The delivery man didn't hear. Zhou was putting the bags down. Lin Mo heard clearly.

He didn't summon her to his side or promise she'd never have to leave. He'd meant the cable, he said. He wasn't sending her away.

She watched him, then sat with her hands pressed against her knees.

The landlord found the key. The electrician began checking the connection. The light flickered and went out.

Lin Mo's chest contracted. His left hand followed the table edge to his phone, not the brace on his right. Zhou switched on a torch, illuminated the floor, then rested it securely.

“They said they're isolating this section. He's just outside.”

Lin Mo could hear coughing, screws dropping into a box. The noises didn't relax him immediately, but neither became theater instructions. Through the gap he saw a crouching shadow reach for a tool.

Xiao Lu stood between him and the door.

“You're blocking my view.”

She didn't move. He repeated that he wanted to see what the man was doing. She turned partly aside, asking whether he could look into the room instead. As though avoiding the doorway might prevent something there from calling him away.

His breathing hadn't settled before anger arrived.

“You can't decide where I'm allowed to look.”

She stepped aside; her chair knocked the wall. He regretted the sharpness, not the need to say it. Her hands disappeared into her sleeves. Outside the torchlight he couldn't tell whether she was about to cry.

The landlord returned, asking about a short screwdriver. Perhaps it had fallen by their door. Could the electrician look? Zhou asked Lin Mo before admitting him.

The food boxes were open. Lin Mo turned his phone face down. Only near the entrance, please, not by the bed.

The man's own light caught silver beside the chair. Xiao Lu bent to retrieve it. Lin Mo told her to wait until they knew what it was. She stopped. The electrician picked it up himself. Yes, that one. Thank you.

“No need to thank me. I hadn't picked it up.”

He smiled and withdrew. Lin Mo almost smiled too, then saw her expression. Her insistence resembled his over the transport receipts, though he couldn't simply label it a lesson she'd learned.

Zhou half-closed the door and went to wash his hands. Xiao Lu freed the delivery receipt from under a box. One corner was wet.

“Do you think I can't pick up a screwdriver?”

“I couldn't see what it was. I was afraid it might be something live.”

“It wasn't connected to anything.”

True. He'd seen a flash of metal and called out. He admitted that his urgency shouldn't have sounded like certain knowledge of danger.

“But you can call out,” she said.

“And you can ask why.”

She left the receipt on the table. Red marks remained on the palm that no longer needed to grip his phone. He'd spoken as though she were taking his entire person away. Here was somebody struggling to open her hand.

He apologized for snapping. He still needed to see outside and hold his phone.

She didn't immediately say it was all right. Instead she shifted her chair. Could he see now?

“Yes.”

“Then let's leave it there.”

Dinner didn't have to wait until someone conceded every point. Zhou returned and failed to find the opening in his chopsticks wrapper. Finally he borrowed scissors, cut it, and put the other pairs down without arranging everybody's first mouthful. Small things could still happen without an argument.

Zhou went to ask how much longer the work would take.

“I'm staying here,” he added from the doorway. “Not going downstairs.”

Xiao Lu nodded. Lin Mo noticed. Until now he'd thought only of being obstructed. Each time she looked up, she seemed to be counting how many people remained in the room.

Had anything else happened that afternoon?

“No.”

“What about now?”

She didn't answer.

He moved his food slightly aside, leaving space without patting it for her to approach. He still intended to eat. His wrist hurt. He wasn't going out tonight. Tonight was all he could offer honestly.

Her gaze dropped towards his legs.

“You used to say everything was fine first.”

“Which time?”

She shook her head. She wasn't asking him to retrieve a matching memory. When Zhou had turned away and the electrician had turned his back, she'd suddenly felt that if she went to collect dinner, the room would be empty when she returned.

After empty, she stopped for a long time.

Someone tested a switch. The light returned. She narrowed her eyes without taking back what she'd said.

“I know this isn't that place. I just couldn't bear to move away.”

Lin Mo remembered wanting to hide his food during last night's blackout. Being told it was merely a power cut might not have steadied his spoon. Yet hiding his own dinner and holding somebody else's phone involved an important difference: another person.

“You can stay seated. I still need my phone. And to see outside.”

She nodded without smiling. Would the chair work to one side, where she could see the door too? She tried the position herself, keeping its back clear of the wall.

Zhou reported that the connection had been restored. The electrician was continuing downstairs. Keep the equipment area clear tonight. Lin Mo didn't take working lights as a permanent guarantee. Leave the torch within reach, please.

Lifting the lid he'd rested back over his dinner, he discovered his spoon beneath the paper bag.

Xiao Lu reached, paused, and asked whether he wanted it.

“Yes.”

She handed it over without putting it farther away because they'd argued. His fingers still trembled. The soup was no longer hot. It took two attempts to bring the first spoonful to his mouth.

Zhou complained about the pitiful portion of greens. Xiao Lu shared hers. Lin Mo reminded her not to give them all away. Her wary glance made him add that he'd only noticed she hadn't eaten much; he wasn't assigning her a portion.

“I know.” She took a bite herself.

Dinner went slowly. The ladder scraped the corridor wall as it was carried past. Her shoulders tightened again. This time she didn't step in front of him. She looked towards the entrance and put down her chopsticks.

“I'm still scared.”

He swallowed before answering. He'd heard her.

He didn't require her to demonstrate understanding by walking outside immediately. She didn't take his phone again. The footsteps descended. He turned his head and saw an empty stretch of corridor through the gap.

After a while she asked, quietly, “What if I'm angry with you?”

He looked back. She watched the food, avoiding his face as though an answer there might forbid her to continue.

He set the spoon down securely.

“Angry about what?”

While she hesitated, he moved his phone onto the table. No delivery call remained to answer. Nobody was insisting they finish dinner now. He asked Zhou to keep his portion for reheating later, without clearing hers just because he'd stopped. Zhou took his own food outside, leaving the door open and himself within hearing.

She no longer needed to force the words out before the dishes disappeared. Lin Mo watched her gathering them instead of suggesting a kinder question. He wanted to know what she meant. He was also afraid of knowing, and couldn't make that fear a reason for her to stop. The open doorway held the ordinary sounds of Zhou eating, close enough for either of them to call.

Xiao Lu finally looked up.

“You didn't wait for me.”
