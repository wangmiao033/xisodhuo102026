# Chapter 233: Keep the Way Out Clear

Xiao Lu checked her phone.

"He replied. He's on his way back."

Before Lin Mo could answer, Qin Yue's voice broke up. He was saying the doorway was clear, but the picture still showed a hand on the handle. Lin Mo waited for the image to move, then asked whether the door had actually closed. It hadn't. Qin Yue had persuaded the newcomers to wait outside and was taking a call from the emergency dispatcher.

Xiao Lu moved the storybook to the side of the table. Shoes crossed the video; Qin Yue settled the phone again. The dispatcher was checking the entrance. Medical help had been contacted. They were to keep the passage clear.

"Has anyone arrived?" Lin Mo asked.

"Not yet. I'm in the foyer. I can get out."

A key sounded in the outer room. Old Zhou came in with the bag holding his tea tin. Seeing both of them intent on the phone, he set it down and came where Lin Mo could see him.

Lin Mo explained the false invitation, the ticket pulling at a man's hand, and the grip that had just eased. He kept to what he knew now. Not everyone registered had come out; the camera showed only one corner.

"I'd like to go."

Xiao Lu looked up.

"Only as far as a place outside where someone can meet us," he continued. "We need to establish where I could stay, how to get there, and who brings me back if the pain gets worse. Nothing's arranged."

"Why do you need to be there?" Zhou asked.

The question stopped his first answer. Hearing someone cry for help didn't automatically make his presence useful. The low camera missed hands passing tickets, and the picture had lagged behind the sound. An instruction based on an old image could stop the wrong action. Qin Yue also had a man to support. None of that meant rescue had to wait for Lin Mo.

"They don't necessarily need me. But someone behind that door keeps trying to take the separated tickets back. From outside, I could follow what actually happens without asking them to act on a frozen picture. If I can only see by entering the hall, I won't take that position."

Zhou asked about his shoulder. Sitting made it ache; the morning's pain remained. There was no new numbness in his wrist. Lin Mo gave him that much instead of saying he could endure it.

Zhou examined the support. He could accompany them, but without someone waiting to receive them, he wouldn't push Lin Mo into a crowd.

Xiao Lu set down her phone.

"I'll come. I can help check the papers. I can't lift you."

"I know."

"I don't want to get there and hear you say there's just one more thing."

A car door slammed outside. Lin Mo's right hand curled. He waited for the involuntary tension to pass, then said he would discuss any change of position before making it. She could challenge him; she wouldn't be responsible for bringing him out of danger alone.

"That doesn't mean you'll listen."

"If you name a risk, I have to answer it. I can't pretend you haven't spoken."

She didn't smile or demand a promise that he would never be afraid. After a while, she began looking for transport that could take him in his wheelchair.

When Qin Yue called again, a police car had reached the street. One officer was questioning people at the entrance, another keeping newcomers outside. He handed over the phone.

Lin Mo explained that his name had been used without permission. He had helped with one limited intervention elsewhere. He didn't turn that experience into a title the officer was obliged to trust.

Asked what he could do, he said he could compare available tickets and records. He wouldn't touch unfamiliar equipment or enter the auditorium. After a pause, with Qin Yue explaining the steps at the entrance, the officer said there was level ground beside the outer doors. They could come to the reception point and follow directions there.

Xiao Lu called the transport provider. She confirmed that Lin Mo could remain in his chair: his injured leg couldn't bear weight, and they weren't asking the driver to improvise by carrying him.

Before she booked, Qin Yue sent another update. A nurse was in the foyer. The two people already outside had help; Shao remained near the exit, without renewed pulling at his hand. Police had stopped admissions, Ms. Luo was protecting the separated tickets, and someone had relieved Qin Yue at the doorway. They still didn't know about the back rows, but one person no longer had to support an arm and guard the entrance at once.

Lin Mo said he still wanted to go. Xiao Lu booked the vehicle.

Getting ready took longer than he expected. Zhou secured the wheelchair and helped him transfer and settle using their familiar supports. Xiao Lu passed cushions and kept the space around his feet clear. Lin Mo did not push himself up with his left arm. A change in his right leg's position sent pain deep through his knee. He called a halt.

He nearly said to leave it, meaning they shouldn't waste time over a little pain.

"Lower," he said instead. "It's pressing there."

Zhou adjusted it and asked before continuing. Xiao Lu picked up the bag containing the old notebook, then hesitated. Lin Mo said they wouldn't need it. His report was on the phone. Private diaries weren't required for inspection.

She left the bag and brought water for them both.

The driver got out to check the ramp. Once aboard, Lin Mo heard the restraints click into place. Zhou checked that the footrest wasn't pressing against anything before sitting beside him. Xiao Lu boarded from the other side.

As the vehicle pulled away, trees slid backward past the window. The road jolted his shoulder. He settled against the backrest rather than bracing with his left arm.

Qin Yue sent a voice message during the journey. People could pass through the outer doors normally now. The person who had tried to close them had stopped. The seats inside were another matter. Shao's hand hadn't been pulled again; Qin Yue was staying with him while they waited for examination.

Lin Mo told him not to leave someone he was helping just to send updates.

By the time they reached Minghui, sunlight lay at an angle beneath the sign.

A table had been moved beside the outer entrance. An officer indicated where they should stop, with a stretch of foyer between them and the auditorium. The route to the street remained clear. A middle-aged woman supported a man who had just emerged while a passerby shifted advertising stands out of their way. Nobody made him tell his story before finding him a seat.

Zhou braked the wheelchair and stayed beside it. Lin Mo sat at an angle to the outer doors. Turning toward the hall tugged at his left shoulder. He asked Zhou to rotate the chair a little, not push it closer.

Xiao Lu stood across the table, where the chair back didn't obstruct her view.

Qin Yue approached with sweat covering his face. Shao had reached a foyer bench with the nurse, who was examining his reddened hand. The torn white piece remained on the chair edge inside, Qin Yue said. Its other half was still attached; the yellow counterpart lay beside it. Luo hadn't let anyone collect them.

Shao asked whether he could leave. The nurse said she would check sensation in his fingers, then tell him how to get to the ambulance. Lin Mo didn't answer over her.

A faint scraping came from the inner doorway. A narrow box was feeding paper into a shallow tray. Beyond it, the doorframe cut across the illuminated screen.

Lin Mo asked Xiao Lu to read what she could from where she stood, without approaching the box.

The latest line still called for absentees to be filled. The screen changed; its visible words matched what she had read. Before he could ask about the preceding line, more paper emerged, turning an old loop over in the tray.

"Should we unplug it?" someone asked.

"Don't touch it yet," Qin Yue said, looking at Lin Mo.

Lin Mo asked whether anyone had seen what it connected to or had moved it. The answers conflicted. Luo detached the replacement clip from the box and left it on the original stool outside the inner door. She approached with the transparent box, containing only the separated void and unused tickets. The printer had been running when she arrived; she hadn't touched its cables. Shao's pair remained in place. She asked Qin Yue to watch from the doorway so nobody slipped it back into the clip.

A man wearing a staff badge on a black cord stood behind her. Lin Mo hadn't seen his face clearly on the call. He couldn't establish whether this was the man who had held up the sign.

The man called it an ordinary seating printer. The machine was old, the wiring messy; they should leave it alone.

"Whose seat is it assigning now?" Lin Mo asked.

The man surveyed his wheelchair. "You got here quickly."

"You know who I am?"

"Your name's on the invitation."

Qin Yue laid the unauthorized notice on the table and indicated where the report had been cut off. The man didn't look. Publicity was someone else's job; he only handled registration. What would he know about the photography shop?

Asked for the register, he said the list needed sorting first.

Luo looked up sharply. "You told me to use the replacement clip. Put the yellow slips in, you said, and nobody would be missing."

He reached for her box. She stepped back and asked for the original register. His hand hung in the air, then pointed beneath the reception table. The officer told him to take it out, put it on the table, and leave it there.

As he bent down, someone arrived with a freshly printed invitation. Qin Yue intercepted him and showed him the full report. The newcomer had taken half a day off. When was the next session?

Qin Yue pointed to Shao's injured hand. At last, the man lowered his phone and went back to the street.

Even keeping one person outside required someone to finish the sentence that had been cut short.

The register had curled blue covers and a brownish water stain. The man opened it. Names stood beside two columns of seats; narrow counterparts rested along the inner edge. Lin Mo found Row Three, Seat Seven and a smaller serial. The seat number alone wasn't enough to identify Shao's entry.

He asked Xiao Lu to turn the desk lamp toward the page instead of bringing the register onto his lap.

"This page is void," the man said.

He pinched it beside the stitching and pulled. Paper cracked.

"Don't tear it."

His hand kept moving. The officer pressed down the register's edge and told him to let go. Xiao Lu pointed beneath the lifted page and read Shao's surname, then indicated the bench. Lin Mo recognized the character too. She didn't wrestle for the paper.

Lin Mo asked Qin Yue to preserve the torn white piece.

"The man's outside. His name's still here. Match them first. If you tear this out, how do we find which entry is keeping him inside?"

The man released it, leaving a short split beside the binding. More paper scraped into the tray. A continuation strip lay under his thumb, not yet submitted. Lin Mo had been listening for the interval between that sound and the movement of a hand.

The officer ordered the man away from the table. His gaze moved from Luo to Xiao Lu, then settled on Lin Mo.

"You think getting everybody outside finishes it?"

A stretcher wheel bumped over a paving joint. Zhou stayed at the wheelchair's handles, keeping the passage clear.

The man's mouth tightened.

"Without an audience, none of you can go back either."
