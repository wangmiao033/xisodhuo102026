# Chapter 1: The Man in the Mirror

The man in the mirror took his hand off the door before Lin Mo did.

Mo kept his fingers on the handle.

Across the narrow room, his reflection stood with its arms at its sides. Same wet jacket. Same cut below the left eyebrow. The cut had come from a cupboard door that morning, when he was looking for the receipt his sister swore she'd left beside the rice cooker.

He let go.

The man in the mirror raised his hand to the door.

Mo backed away. His heel struck a chair, scraping it across the floor. The reflection's chair stayed still.

There was one door, no window, and a dressing table beneath the mirror. Six bare bulbs ran along its frame. The third bulb on the left flickered; the others gave off enough heat that he could smell dust burning.

He checked his pockets. Keys. Wallet. A folded hospital receipt, damp along one edge.

No phone.

He had been holding it when the lift stopped. He remembered the number eight above the doors, the buzz of the ceiling panel, his sister's message open on the screen.

*Get something to eat before you come.*

He had typed *Did you eat?*

He couldn't remember sending it.

A speaker crackled overhead.

“Good evening, performer.”

Mo looked up. A black grille sat above the door, beside a camera no larger than his thumb.

“Where's my phone?”

“Please take your position.”

He tried the handle again. It turned freely, engaging nothing. He pushed, then pulled. The door didn't move in its frame.

“Your position is marked on the floor.”

A strip of white tape made an X in front of the dressing table. One end had curled up. Someone had walked dirt into the adhesive.

Mo stood beside it.

“What is this place?”

The mirror went dark.

For a second he saw himself properly: pale, breathing too fast, left hand still clenched around his keys.

White letters appeared over his face.

> ABYSS THEATER
>
> DRESSING ROOM 04
>
> FIRST APPEARANCE

A number in the top corner read 0. It changed to 19, then 63.

Below it, a line of small gray text slid upward.

*another one wearing a jacket*

Mo leaned closer. More lines arrived.

*turn his mic up*

*he can read us lol*

*anyone know the starting odds?*

He glanced at the camera.

“I'm not participating.”

The number reached 208.

“All performers must complete wardrobe inspection before entering the corridor,” the speaker said. A woman's voice, pleasant and slightly muffled, like an announcement in a shopping center. “Please remove your clothing and place it on the chair.”

Mo put his keys back in his pocket.

“Open the door.”

“Please remove your clothing.”

“No.”

His refusal appeared in the mirror a moment later.

*No.*

It had quotation marks around it. Then a small heart appeared beside it, followed by another.

The speaker clicked off.

Mo pulled the chair toward the dressing table and stood on it. The camera was fixed into the wall with two screws. He caught its housing in both hands and twisted.

It was warm. Too warm for something so small.

A sharp pain ran through his little finger.

He let go, thinking he'd cut it on the casing. There was no blood. His finger had bent inward at the middle joint.

He watched it straighten.

The bone moved under the skin with a faint, dry click.

Mo stepped down badly, landing on the side of his boot. He caught the table. His little finger lay against the others, visibly shorter than it had been.

He flexed it once. It worked.

“Wardrobe inspection has been declined,” the woman said. “An alternative fitting will now begin.”

Text opened in the center of the mirror.

> AUDIENCE SELECTION
>
> A. BONE RESTRUCTURING
>
> B. VOCAL ADJUSTMENT
>
> C. SKIN REPLACEMENT
>
> Voting closes in 00:20.

Mo looked for a wire, a projector, anything that might explain what was happening to his hand.

His right wrist began to ache.

“You said twenty seconds.”

The ache stopped.

The timer showed nineteen.

Mo held still. The gray messages continued to climb.

*A first obviously*

*C is wasted on beginners*

*does he have military training? look at the haircut*

He hadn't been in uniform for eleven months. The barber below his flat charged fifteen yuan and knew one cut.

He seized the chair by its back and drove a leg into the mirror.

The impact jarred his shoulders. The chair bounced away, leaving a smear of white paint on the glass.

The timer kept running.

He struck it again. A joint in the chair gave. One rear leg fell to the floor.

*keep that bit for later*

*he's going to try stabbing someone*

Mo picked up the broken leg. A screw protruded from the end.

Nine seconds.

He pressed the screw against his palm. It left a dent. He pressed harder until it hurt.

His hand was real. So was the room. Whatever else was happening, he needed to get through the door.

Three seconds.

He shoved the receipt deeper into his pocket so it wouldn't fall out.

> A. BONE RESTRUCTURING — 81%

He sat on the floor before his knees could give way.

The first pressure came in his wrists, as if someone had strapped a cuff around each and was tightening them one notch at a time. He gripped the chair leg. His right hand slipped; he closed it again, harder.

Then his jaw hurt.

He tried to keep his teeth apart. In training he'd seen a man bite through his tongue during a seizure. The memory gave him something practical to do, though he couldn't tell whether it mattered here.

The bulbs hummed. He counted them from left to right and lost his place at four.

Something shifted high in his cheeks.

He made a sound he hadn't meant to make.

The comments sped up.

Mo lowered his head until his forehead touched his knees. The pain in his wrists eased. He waited for it to return. Sweat dripped off his nose onto the floor, darkening a seam between the boards.

“Initial fitting complete.”

He stayed there.

“Performer, please check your appearance.”

Mo wiped his mouth on his sleeve. His jaw still ached, but nothing felt loose. He pushed himself up using the table.

The man looking back at him could have been a younger relative.

The bridge of his nose was the same. So was the small cut. But his jaw had narrowed, and his cheekbones sat differently beneath the skin. He turned sideways, searching for an angle that would put his face back.

His collar rubbed against his neck. The jacket had room across the shoulders that hadn't been there before.

*much better*

He put his fist against the glass, over the message.

“Put it back.”

“Your appearance will develop according to audience preference.”

“Put my hand back.”

The speaker hissed softly.

He spread both hands on the table. His wedding-finger knuckle had an old scar from a tin lid. The scar was still there. He traced it with his thumb, feeling its raised edge.

The door latch clicked.

Mo looked up.

> FIRST APPEARANCE COMPLETE
>
> Proceed to the Mirror Corridor.

Below the instruction, smaller lines appeared.

> Identity: Lin Mo
>
> Age: 25
>
> Physical revision: 6%
>
> Cognitive overwrite: 0%
>
> Beginner protection: ACTIVE

He read the last two lines twice.

“What does overwrite mean?”

“Please proceed.”

He pulled out his wallet. His ID card was tucked behind an expired discount voucher. The photograph showed his own face, square-jawed, annoyed at the photographer.

He held it beside the mirror.

The comments paused.

Not slowed. Stopped. The last message remained halfway up the glass.

“Personal documents are not part of your wardrobe,” the woman said.

Her voice had lost its muffled quality.

Mo slid the card into his jacket's inside pocket, separate from the wallet.

“They weren't on the chair.”

For several seconds the speaker made no sound.

Then the comments jumped, filling the space they'd left empty.

*that got her attention*

*who paid for this one's protection?*

The second message vanished before it reached the top.

Mo leaned forward.

“Wait.”

A red notice replaced the chat.

> Disclosure restricted.

He waited for another message. None came.

Outside the door, something scraped along the wall.

Mo took the chair leg and approached from the hinge side. He opened the door a few centimeters, keeping his body behind it.

A corridor extended in both directions. Mirrors lined the walls between numbered doors. The carpet was green, flattened down the middle by years of use. Somewhere water dripped into a metal container.

No one stood outside.

On the opposite wall, a mirror showed the back of his head.

He moved his free hand. In the glass, the hand stayed still.

Mo looked away.

A card had been nailed to the inside of the door. The corners were greasy from being touched.

> MIRROR CORRIDOR
>
> 1. Your destination is Room 09.
> 2. Keep your assigned name until arrival.
> 3. If a reflection calls to you, do not answer.
> 4. At the bell, face the nearest mirror.

Under the printed rules, someone had scratched a fifth line into the card with a pin.

*Don't give them a name you want to keep.*

Mo touched the scratches. The card had been punctured in several places. Whoever wrote it had been shaking or in a hurry.

A bell rang.

He looked at the mirror opposite him.

His reflection had turned around. It stood in the corridor, holding the broken chair leg in its left hand.

Mo held his in his right.

The reflection wore his old face.

“Mo?” it said.

He bit the inside of his cheek.

The voice belonged to his sister. Hoarse from the oxygen mask, impatient with him for arriving late.

“You didn't answer my message.”

His fingers tightened around the wood.

The reflection reached into its jacket and brought out a phone. There was a crack across the screen, through the time display. His phone. He'd dropped it outside the hospital pharmacy three weeks ago.

“I was asking if you ate.”

That was wrong. She'd told him to eat. He was the one who'd asked.

He drew a breath through his nose and kept his mouth shut.

The bell stopped.

Behind the reflection, a second figure rose into view, too far back for him to make out a face.

“Please,” his sister's voice said.

Mo stepped into the corridor.

The dressing-room door shut against his heel. He lifted his foot clear and heard the latch catch.

In the mirror, the phone lit up.

*Don't tell them I'm here.*

Mo stared until the screen went black.
