# Chapter 294: A Little Farther In

On Sunday morning, going out for groceries, I stopped at the building entrance.

No new problem with the door. It stood open, admitting daylight. Somebody had put an ordinary sheet on the wall. Passing it, I'd noticed only a bold line. Two steps farther on, I turned back.

A temporary diversion affected a nearby road today. Beneath the words stood a small diagram. I wasn't going that way, but studied it briefly. Its heavy line ran opposite my usual route. I'd automatically treated the paper as though it faced the same direction I did.

A neighbor came in carrying things. I stepped aside. Seeing me examine the notice, he asked whether I needed the diversion. Just looking; I wasn't going there now. Nodding, he continued upstairs without organizing a walk for me.

Outside, I looked back once more. The notice occupied the wall near the entrance. Someone searching for keys might easily pass it. That didn't prove every missed notice involved nerves, or automatically explain the man in my story. Hands in my pockets, I continued shopping.

The shop had a handwritten sign directing customers farther inside for one kind of produce. Following the arrow, I found an empty basket. I almost asked whether everything had sold. Several more steps revealed the actual shelf. The arrow hadn't declared the basket my destination. I'd merely stopped at the first object resembling one.

After choosing what I wanted, I queued. Someone held a bag toward the counter and asked to put it down. Making room, I realized she was already paying farther along the counter, not trying to push ahead of me. She thanked me. No problem. Understanding in time didn't make me especially pleased with myself.

Walking home, I changed hands. An old shop sign's shadow touched the edge of a new one, the two names positioned almost as though they belonged together. Tom and I had discussed changing signs nearby. This one didn't require me to write it down before it existed.

I didn't fetch my note. Only after putting food away and washing my hands at home did I take out the folder containing The Last Chair.

The original, earlier clean copy, and Friday's reader copy remained inside. I placed the third on the table and left the other two in the folder. The explanation about finally understanding was already crossed out on the old clean copy. No need to delete it again today.

I read the new copy's opening.

The man approached the last chair; the woman moved her bag; he retreated toward the wall. A door appeared. Not until the later inner did readers receive the position already lodged in my head. Opening the notebook's sketch made me smile. I'd drawn myself a route while giving someone else a doorframe that couldn't be completed until afterward.

Yet I didn't want to begin with a tour of the room. My clumsy lines wouldn't necessarily grow clever inside prose. On a separate sheet, I copied the opening and added farther inside the room when the door first appeared.

Only a few words, yet heavier aloud than expected.

I tried farther in. Reaching the seated woman, I found it sounded like an instruction to keep walking rather than a door's position. Crossing it out, I tried that door at the far side of the room. Then I removed an unnecessary that, leaving the door at the far side of the room.

I stood to fetch water without asking Lumi to choose. The channel wasn't open. Returning, I found the first sentence still began with the chair, not the notice. Tuesday's experimental sentences remained in the yellow notebook. Today's small alteration didn't make all of them worthless.

I read the entire piece, not only those words. The woman's bag still traveled from the neighboring chair to her lap. The man still misunderstood. The notice still said today's counter had moved upstairs. The arrow beside the entrance hadn't migrated onto the inner door. Both people still left, with an empty chair behind them.

Those things hadn't been wrong. I could bring the door's position forward today without announcing that no reader would ever take the wrong turn again.

Just before marking it, I discovered my trial sheet covered the story's end. Moving it revealed the last sentence about the empty chair. I read it at an ordinary pace and suddenly remembered I'd never specified the chair's color. Once, I might have added one while revising, as proof I could see more clearly.

Today I tried gray. Aloud, it merely sounded like gray occupying space. Removing it didn't make the chair disappear. I left those letters on the trial sheet without requiring a useful place for them in the prose.

Someone carried something along the corridor. Footsteps paused briefly outside. Looking up, I waited. Not for me; they continued upward. When I lowered my eyes, my hand still rested at the paper's edge. The brief interruption made the added door position feel less intrusive. Even so, I read the earlier wording once more before choosing the new sentence.

At the pen's first touch, ink gathered in a small dot beside the existing words. I waited before continuing, rather than dragging a hand across and smudging everything. Few changes; hurrying to put the sheet away could supply another problem.

I marked Friday's reader copy lightly, clipped the trial sheet behind it, and identified this as the small opening adjustment. The original and older clean copy remained. No third full recopying. They weren't failures requiring secret concealment, or versions that must become identical to prove I remembered my own work.

At noon, I cooked what I'd bought. One dish contained more liquid than usual. I'd thought a small plate could hold it, then changed to a deeper one. While transferring it, I laughed. Containers kept correcting me today. Tom hadn't mentioned dishes, however, and the notice hadn't taught cooking. They needn't assemble a single lesson together.

Only after eating and clearing up did I open the channel. Lumi said I sounded like someone who'd completed a very small task and resisted admitting how small. Laughing, I asked what she'd heard.

“A huge breath before you called me.”

“Changed a few words. Perhaps afraid they wouldn't sound worth mentioning.”

“Mention a few words, then.”

I explained that the story's first reference to the door now placed it at the room's far side. Not a changed ending. Was that what Tom had said? He'd described initially confusing the doors. I'd tried the particular wording myself today.

“Went out today?”

I paused, remembering I hadn't mentioned groceries. Yes. A diagram in the building corridor; an arrow in the shop taking me beyond my imagined destination. I kept the account short, adding that lunch had required a deeper dish, without declaring a new understanding of space.

Was the empty shop basket joining the story too? Laughing, I said the waiting room was crowded enough. Poor basket, Lumi said, expelled before it entered.

I wanted to read her the changed opening and lifted the paper before asking. Today she didn't want another route through the room. Why had I selected a dish too small for lunch?

“I haven't mentioned the dish.”

A pause. “You just said you changed to a deeper one.”

Thinking back, I remembered saying it with the shopping account. Amusement slowly rose. Preoccupied with my handful of added words, I'd forgotten my own recent speech. Lumi heard more than the portions I considered most important.

“The little plate has a reader too.”

“Only one for now.”

I put the manuscript down. The plate, then. She needn't hear the whole story today to demonstrate a lack of jealousy toward Tom. I didn't label her refusal a fresh outbreak of that lingering dislike. A flatter rim wouldn't help, she remarked. Advice for next time, I said. This meal had already happened.

We laughed. Later, I put the paper away and sat at the other side of the table with the channel still open. Did I like reading the altered version? The door was easier to find, I said, but I still didn't know whether the story was particularly good.

“I didn't ask particularly good.”

“I like it a little.”

“A short sentence at last.”

Her voice smiled. So did I, without asking whether she could now accept their leaving together. She hadn't heard the altered sentence and couldn't judge the whole from my description. Clearing up one door didn't settle our different tastes either.

Taking rubbish out toward evening, I saw the notice lift at one corner in the breeze. I didn't tear somebody else's paper down. Its fixing still held, so I passed. Returning, I knew which way my stairs lay without consulting the small diagram again.

That night, I opened the gray-covered comedy booklet. One character insisted he'd entered the correct door; the room had been put in the wrong place. I laughed aloud. The channel was closed. Lumi hadn't heard. A clean scrap marked the page for some later opportunity to read it to her. Tonight didn't have to become another occasion that counted only after sharing.
