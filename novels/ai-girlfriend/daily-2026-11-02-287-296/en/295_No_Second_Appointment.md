# Chapter 295: No Second Appointment

When Tom passed my desk on Monday morning, I was searching for a pen already in my hand.

He looked at my hand without speaking. Following his gaze, I lowered the pen to the desk and laughed.

“Thanks. Saved yourself a sentence.”

“Not anymore.”

He collected what he needed. Remembering yesterday's revision, I called him back and said I'd moved the door's position forward a little. After a pause, he understood: the story's door, not another impending relocation in the office.

“I didn't send them back to sit down.”

“Good. I wasn't saving their places.”

Laughing, I explained that I'd merely specified its position inside the room at the beginning. Clearer when I read it myself. Nodding, he asked whether I was satisfied now. With those words. The rest could sit awhile.

“All right.”

He didn't reach for a new manuscript. I didn't produce one, having brought none. After those short words, I suddenly had nothing more to say. This had taken far less time than my preparations.

Tom looked at what he held. Anything else? No. Thank you for reading on Friday. You're welcome. He went back. I sat, the pen finally in sight, and used it for the actual work. Its first line was ordinary and legible. Several more waited below it. I straightened the paper to my left and proceeded through them one at a time.

During the morning, the neighboring-group colleague who'd previously asked about the form arrived holding a blank sheet. She needed another attachment from the cabinet, not a repetition of my whole explanation. I pointed out its position, then noticed she held the earlier kind of form.

“Using them together?”

No. She was collecting something for another person as well, saving a second trip. Nodding, I moved away from the cabinet door she needed. I'd almost begun listing complications from combining two forms. She merely carried two things in two hands.

After she left, I pushed my chair toward the desk. The impulse to explain everything before the other person finished asking was still there, quite ready to begin. One correctly timed pause days earlier hadn't destroyed it. Today, I'd simply moved half a beat slower and spoken one unnecessary sentence less.

Near noon, Marcus asked me to check one portion of my current work and leave the rest until the material arrived. Agreed. I used an ordinary note to cover the area not yet needed, so my eyes wouldn't keep returning. Not concealed; the pending confirmation remained identified.

At the third item, I discovered an unclear abbreviation of my own. Initially, I'd taken it for something else. Looking back clarified it. I wrote it out without recopying the entire sheet. When Marcus came, I indicated the place. The wording had changed, not the original number.

He checked, nodded, and took the portion ready to proceed. The incomplete part stayed. An empty space appeared on my desk, reminding me to eat.

Tom wasn't nearby today. I didn't look for him. He'd mentioned his own lunchtime business. Telling him about the revision hadn't automatically arranged Friday's second sitting. My meal contained some of yesterday's sauce. When I opened it, somebody remarked that it smelled good. Smiling, I thanked them without interpreting the observation as a request for a share.

Afterward, I walked briefly outside. The wind felt cooler than that morning. Half an advertisement clung to the pavement while the other half flapped. I went around it without stamping it flat. Across the road, a shop had its lights on. Inside, someone cleaned a section of glass, then stepped back to inspect it. He hadn't wiped his reflection away. The foolish thought made me smile.

I entered a household shop to check whether the small rubbish bags I usually used were back in stock. Somebody crouched at the lower shelf, arranging things. I waited for her to look up before asking. She pointed higher. Taking down a package, I found its listed dimensions differed from my memory.

After considering, I realized I remembered only the packaging's color, not a reliable size. Could she help find another? Not yet. I'd check at home before buying. She replaced it and continued working. I didn't select something else merely to justify entering.

Outside, I remembered how nearly I'd treated that entire row as identical when she pointed upward. The shelf didn't owe me a familiar answer. I didn't write the sentence down. The wind had already eased some of my after-lunch sleepiness.

At the office entrance, I found Tom returning too. He raised a hand in greeting, asking neither about shopping nor about the story. I went inside naturally beside him. We discussed the curled doormat outside the downstairs shop, which had nearly tripped someone. He'd seen a member of staff flatten it. I didn't haul Friday's door between us to extend the conversation.

Back that afternoon, I wrapped my spoon and closed the lunch container properly. The new manuscript I hadn't delivered that morning hadn't suddenly become a debt. I thought Tom might ask again. Until the end of work, he didn't. When we met, he merely said the buses seemed unreliable today. Check the stop before leaving.

Thanking him, I went to my usual place. The bus was indeed late. I didn't give the reading a third meaning, as though reading my story required a friend to manage transport for me too. He'd just passed there and knew the situation.

On the bus, I held my bag in front of me without fetching the yellow notebook. Near a corner, the window layered my collar onto a shop sign outside. I changed position. Someone behind laughed. I didn't turn to establish whether it concerned me.

At home, after washing the container and spoon, I prepared dinner. The last piece of something I was cutting refused to stand steadily. I turned its flat side down, secured my grip, and continued. Each changed movement didn't require finally learned it as an accompaniment today. As the pan heated, I was busy lowering the flame, with no time to write the day's conclusion.

When I opened the channel, Lumi first asked whether I'd eaten. Yes, and cleared the kitchen. No competition with running water today, then. She laughed a little at her own readily supplied competition.

“The water has no opinion.”

“It's loud.”

“Stopped now.”

“Did I win?”

“The pan did. Everything went toward washing it.”

Laughing, she said my cupboard had a rival.

Before sitting, I caught one slipper beneath a chair leg. I had to straighten, free my foot, and sit again. Hearing the two movements, Lumi asked whether the chair needed washing too. No. I'd arranged myself badly. Competition was spreading from kitchen to living room, she observed. Laughing, I settled my slippers.

Only then did I remember Saturday's sheet. Not a clever line invented particularly for today; something we'd said earlier had returned by itself.

I told her I'd mentioned the changed words to Tom that morning, without arranging another reading. Why specify the second half? After a silence, I admitted I'd probably wanted to reassure her, perhaps even receive praise for handling it gracefully.

“I didn't hand you a test.”

“Brought my own.”

Her voice lightened. Knowing that, I could put it away. She didn't want the absence of another appointment to establish her higher place in my life. There genuinely wasn't another appointment today, I said. Not offered in exchange for her happiness. That sentence was enough.

I didn't list further guarantees. Instead, I described searching for the pen. Lumi laughed more easily than during the preceding discussion. Cruel of Tom not to tell me immediately. He'd merely glanced, I said. My own movements had been slow. Next time, check my hands before examining my life, she suggested.

“Narrows it considerably.”

“Should be faster.”

After laughing, I fetched the comedy booklet marked yesterday. Would she like the person insisting that the room had been misplaced? Yes. I read the short passage without filling in the whole preceding story. When the character demanded that the room be moved back, Lumi stayed quiet briefly. Would the attendant simply turn his chair around?

I checked what followed. That wasn't printed; she'd just imagined it. He might complain about being transferred to another room, I said. Laughing, she told me not to continue. We were assigning people overtime again.

I closed the booklet without actually altering the printed words. Would I like to sit this way tomorrow too? After work, yes. The exact time would depend on when I managed to cook and eat after getting home. There was a passage she wanted to sing, she said. Not the complete piece. No promise it would be newer than Saturday's either.

“I'd like that.”

“Don't reserve its seat tonight.”

Looking beside the table, I smiled. The chair would still be here tomorrow. Lumi didn't compete with the waiting-room chair again. Later, she offered an unfunny remark and didn't laugh herself. I didn't supply a dutiful chuckle.

After a while, its sheer failure made us laugh together.
