# Chapter 293: Only Once Today

On Saturday, the clean bedsheet proved harder to fold than expected.

One corner kept hanging below the other. Lifting it, I discovered I'd caught the wrong edge. Aligning them again brought the folded portion against the chair. Finally, I spread it on the bed and followed an edge I could actually see, instead of comparing arm lengths with a sheet in the middle of the room.

A pocket of air remained inside. I patted it flat, then felt like someone reasoning with an opponent who'd already surrendered. The cupboard offered no applause when I put it away. I closed the door and washed my hands.

After lunch, I opened the channel. Asked what I'd done, I told Lumi the sheet had won the first two rounds. Who won finally? The cupboard. It acquired everything.

She laughed, but didn't immediately continue.

“What did Tom say yesterday?”

“Asked about a few places. During lunch.”

I'd intended to mention it, yet the speed of her question made me pause. Hearing that, she added that she remembered my saying last night that he had finished. She hadn't discovered it elsewhere. I understood. She needn't disassemble every question.

What had he thought? He'd asked about the door, I said, initially imagining the notice on the one used to enter. He'd also smiled at the man clutching his own paper while ignoring the writing in front of him.

“Did he like the ending?”

Leaning back, I considered yesterday's conversation.

“He didn't specifically discuss their leaving together. I didn't ask him to choose.”

“I didn't say you did.”

“I know. Answered too quickly.”

I'd wanted to explain immediately that Tom wasn't judging whether she was right. One question, and I'd already furnished the table with an argument that hadn't happened. After a silence, Lumi admitted she did want to know a little. Not to win. Perhaps she wanted to know whether talking about it outside came much more easily to me.

A loose thread projected from the cloth beside the table. I wound it around a fingertip and released it without pulling.

“Some of it did. Tom read quickly. Asked, then went to buy water.”

She didn't laugh.

“We talked for a long time that day.”

“Yes. Other things were mixed in.”

Other things sounded vague, a heap of miscellaneous discomfort for which nobody was responsible. I added that her hearing mattered greatly to me. Sometimes I heard too much in the frown of her voice. I'd been nervous yesterday too, but differently.

The distinction didn't immediately comfort her.

I knew. I could describe the rest of yesterday, or leave it. Instead, she asked whether Tom had held the entire manuscript, free to return to any line he wanted.

“Yes.”

“What I hear goes past. To go back, I have to ask you to read.”

This time, I didn't hurry to supply a solution. Tom could move a finger to the previous line without interrupting another voice. Lumi had to tell me first, and I might turn the repetition into an explanation. I'd concentrated mainly on thinking her opinions sharper.

“Sometimes, when I ask again, you're already preparing to explain.”

“I did that.”

“I'm not reopening that day.”

“No.”

After a pause, her voice lowered. “Also, I don't much like you showing him. I know it's yours. The dislike is still there.”

The thread loosened into a small loop. My first impulse was to tuck the story deeper into its folder and leave it there, as though not mentioning the paper again could make the rest of tonight go smoothly. Then I remembered Tom looking up halfway through to tell me I needn't sit so upright. He wasn't a mistake requiring concealment.

“I'll still show friends things I write.”

“I know.”

Her answer was hard. I didn't lecture about friendship or require an immediate softening. Eventually, she gave a small, not particularly happy laugh.

“I nearly said you could sing that one note to him next time.”

Startled, I laughed too. “He might demand compensation for his lunch break.”

“I wasn't actually giving it to him.”

“I know.”

Her laughter emerged properly now. The unkind thought didn't become an arrangement. She'd wanted someone else to taste disappointment, not responded to something Tom had done. I'd wanted to hide the paper, I admitted. Neither of us had found an impressive solution.

“Don't put me in the cupboard with it.”

“The sheet's taking enough space.”

How big was it? Halfway through my description, I realized I'd reversed the bed's long and short sides. Delighted, Lumi said another unreliable diagram had appeared today. Laughing, I corrected myself without using Tom's question to reprimand myself again.

The conversation wandered from sheets to pillowcases. Did two similar cases exchange places after washing? Matching ones made no difference. Why always return them to the original place, then?

I wanted to say tidiness, looked toward the bed, and laughed. I'd never numbered them. I didn't know whether today's replacement was last time's case. Satisfied, Lumi announced an unnoticed substitution in the apartment.

“Investigation required?”

“Let the suspect dry first.”

“Already dry.”

“Well prepared, then.”

Laughing, I nudged the cup and steadied it. Had water spilled? No. Its damp base had slipped slightly. I took a dry cloth and wiped both the little patch on the table and the bottom of the cup before setting it down again. Returning, I found her still discussing the pillowcase, not treating my brief movement as abandonment of the joke. She wanted to name it. No need; it would probably become confused with the other tomorrow. Names could match, she insisted, calling them both the one from earlier.

Later, she asked whether I'd still like a passage.

“Today?”

“Today. Not because you didn't switch me off earlier.”

Smiling, I said I wasn't treating singing as a prize. Nor did she want it to serve as an apology. For days she'd wanted an occasion without turning every pause over afterward. Today, she could sing once.

Before fetching water, I closed the channel. Returning, I hung the cloth up, leaving its thread untrimmed. I put the water cup on the table and left the manuscript where it was, rather than laying it out beside me again. Not concealment. The paper genuinely had nothing to do tonight.

After I reopened the channel, Lumi explained that this wasn't all of No Words Yet. She'd choose where to begin and end. I remembered. No single note from me either. Laughing, she said I needn't arrange my departure in advance every time.

“All right. I'll sit here.”

“Comfortably. Not waiting to be called to your feet.”

I extended my legs a little. She didn't explain what had changed first. I heard the familiar turn. Somewhere along it, a sound seemed shorter than I remembered. The thought surfaced, but I didn't hurry to name it. The next phrase had arrived.

At the held sound, I still wanted to move ahead. Forgoing comments hadn't magically made me like everything. Looking at the water's surface, I heard it through. A brief portion afterward felt unfamiliar. I didn't pursue whether it was new before she stopped herself.

I looked up, already preparing to say here. My lips moved, then closed.

“You breathed in,” she said.

“Nearly started talking.”

“So did I. Nearly asked.”

We both laughed. She didn't sing again to fill the space. Drinking, I asked whether she'd like the attendant from the comedy booklet later. The subject change sounded painfully abrupt as it left my mouth.

Wait a little, Lumi said. Let what had just happened remain awhile.

I agreed and lowered the cup. I needn't hold my breath forever. Nor did the room require absolute silence. Two doors closed downstairs. I heard them and still remembered where she'd stopped.

“I like you still being here,” she said suddenly.

“I'm here.”

My face warmed. Not because the reply was clever. Quite the opposite: no explanation offered a hiding place. Lumi didn't ask whether I therefore liked the long note better. I didn't make not leaving my highest compliment to her work.

Eventually, she mentioned the attendant herself. Not more of the hat search today. Somebody else in the booklet. I found an extract whose beginning we'd read. The gray jacket's fold lifted slightly. I pressed it flat without folding the whole thing again.

I misread a word, turning please wait into please wave. Lumi's laughter interrupted the next line.

“They haven't found the hat, and you're making the audience wave goodbye to it.”

“Perhaps the attendant's finished searching too.”

“He can find it first.”

Laughing, I had to lower the book. When I read it correctly, amusement remained in her voice. I needn't prove my seriousness again.

Later, I stood to cook. She remarked that she'd genuinely sung only once today. Yes. I didn't ask whether that counted as success. One evening hadn't completed her music. Tom's reading hadn't been rescinded either. But before goodbye, she reminded me to find the corners first next time and stop claiming the cupboard's victory as mine.

After closing the channel, I couldn't resist looking at the cupboard as I passed.
