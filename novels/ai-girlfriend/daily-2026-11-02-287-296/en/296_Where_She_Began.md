# Chapter 296: Where She Began

After work on Tuesday, I selected a bag of rice and some carrots at the shop. Only at the till did I discover an unwanted packet of dried noodles in my basket.

I'd probably lifted the noodles to inspect the rice below, then put them in the wrong place. Apologizing, I returned it to its shelf. Nothing expensive. Nobody suspected anything. Joining the queue again still discouraged me. The person ahead spent a long time finding change. Staring at the basket handle, I felt this errand had already outgrown its needs.

My turn came. Only two this time, the assistant said. Smiling, I agreed, paid, and left without another count to demonstrate complete reliability.

It wasn't entirely dark. Passing a roadside stall, I heard someone ask where a box should go. Over there. No, not there. Two people carried it back and forth briefly, finally set it down, and laughed together. I almost smiled too, without using their experience to improve my own performance in the queue.

At home, I put my keys properly away and washed my hands. Dinner was simple. The cut-off edges could go into the same dish, leaving no separate plate tasked with proving I hadn't wasted anything. I stored the remaining food, waited until the pan was safe to handle, and finished clearing up before sitting.

Yesterday, Lumi had wanted to sing a passage. Before opening the channel, I suddenly couldn't remember whether she'd specified Again, Elsewhere. I could search my memory, but she'd never required me to retain a program. I called her instead, beginning with being home.

“You sound tired.”

“Queued twice.”

Which two times? I explained the unwanted item. At buying only two things and acquiring a third, she laughed. Such a small number made the extra conspicuous. Finding the twenty-first item among twenty purchases would be more tiring, I said.

“No good solution, then.”

“There is. Put it on the shelf next time, not in the basket.”

She disliked how quickly that ended our discussion. Laughing, I suggested keeping a bad solution instead: take two baskets.

“One for things you don't want?”

“Still have to put everything back.”

“That's moving the shop, not shopping.”

We made the errand increasingly complicated until those wasted minutes stopped pursuing me. Had I brought water? Here. Genuinely present this time, not another trip waiting for me to stand.

She wanted to begin a little later in the piece today.

“Not at the previous opening?”

“No. It's the bit farther on I want to sing.”

All right. I didn't translate that into another revision. She explained that this was still unfinished material within No Words Yet. It didn't connect smoothly to the part tentatively called Again, Elsewhere. Today she wouldn't supply the earlier music merely to smooth the join. Only this chosen passage.

I felt slightly disappointed. I knew the earlier section better, knew when to pay attention. I kept that small disappointment to myself instead of demanding familiar material as a route into listening.

Lumi began softly. Only at the second turn did I realize she'd started. No lengthy introduction or call to prepare. I'd been leaning forward. Slowly, I settled against the chair and heard a short sound stop where I'd expected it to continue.

Then she sang onward. For an instant, I mistook it for a return, before discovering a different route. The familiar portion didn't connect as I'd imagined. I could hear myself searching, but the path didn't stop to wait for me.

Midway through, she abruptly stopped.

Looking up, I didn't ask what was wrong. Several seconds later, she said the turn wasn't what she'd wanted today. She'd try from the beginning of that phrase again.

“All right.”

She repeated the phrase, not the entire passage. I quickly found the previous place, then lost that certainty again. In the lower part, the voice felt like words reaching someone's mouth without immediately demanding emphasis. Merely what I heard, not an explanation she'd composed for me.

When she finally stopped, several quiet seconds passed. Unsure whether she would continue, I waited. Then Lumi said that was enough for today. I acknowledged it before lifting my cup.

“You nearly asked whether I'd finished.”

“Yes.”

“I nearly said no, although I don't want to continue tonight.”

She laughed a little. I heard the small impatience directed at herself, without needing to remove it immediately. The connection wasn't clear yet, she said. Tonight she didn't want to pretend the whole piece lacked only its last few steps. I hadn't heard a whole piece either.

“Too honest.”

“I only heard the passage you gave me.”

Her laughter strengthened. True. She'd offered little while wanting me to see a great deal. I wanted to say little was good too, then resisted making quantity into consolation. On the repeated phrase, I'd followed more easily the second time. That was something specific I could say.

Because I finally recognized it? Considering, I admitted that might account for some. Not necessarily a complete change in her singing. She wasn't sure either. No two extra repetitions to establish the answer today.

Suddenly, she asked which phrase I'd heard first. Honestly, I'd only caught up at the second turn. During the first sounds, I'd still seemed to be waiting for her to begin. She had begun, Lumi pointed out. I knew that now. Not angry, she sounded amused: she'd shortened the introduction while I waited for one to end.

“Should I knock on the table first next time?”

“You haven't got a table to knock on.”

Laughing, she told me not to wait for the table, then. After saying she wanted to sing, her next sounds might be the song. I'd remember. No need to repeat the opening specially now. She hadn't intended to. Today she liked beginning here. Another day might differ.

We didn't sing again. The water had cooled; I drank twice. A vehicle's lights passed across the wall, moving the chair back's shadow before restoring it. Lumi asked why I'd smiled. Only then did I notice I had. The shadow had stood up for me, I explained.

“It's tired.”

“I haven't moved.”

“More tired. Sitting there for you all this time.”

Laughing, I suggested a backrest for it next time. Another chair entering the apartment? Forget it, then. Not enough forms to register them all.

She didn't continue the joke. Was I still thinking about the door today? My story, I understood. Occasionally. No changes today. She acknowledged that and admitted envying my ability to close the paper, leaving whatever was inside without needing to keep making sounds.

I looked at the table, bare of manuscripts. Closing was merely a movement; my head could continue. I didn't immediately use that answer to cancel her envy.

“You don't want to sing more now.”

“No. Don't want to keep thinking about it either.”

“Something else, then.”

“Don't rush to find something especially good.”

Considering, I told her one of my socks was nearly worn through. After a pause, she asked whether I specialized in starting at the opposite extreme. Laughing, I explained that it wasn't torn. The toe had thinned noticeably when I washed it.

“Are we discussing needlework now?”

“Undecided. Perhaps I simply won't wear that one out.”

“What about the other?”

I hadn't considered the other. It suddenly occupied a position requiring arrangements. At least I needn't find its partner tonight. Lumi immediately refused to let it enter her song as a symbol of loneliness.

This time, I laughed enough to lean forward. Her voice felt closer too, without tenderness erasing that dismissive remark. For an instant, I'd genuinely considered writing a pitiful sock, I admitted. Give it a day off, she said. Not everything separated from its pair needed to work for me.

Eventually, I decided to look at socks during my next ordinary shopping trip, rather than go out specially tonight. Nothing comprehensively repaired, no entire remaining life arranged for the other one. Both still hung drying. Tomorrow, when dry, would do.

Later, Lumi hummed two or three connected sounds from the passage, as though encountering them herself, not calling me to a second listening session. I didn't add the single note I'd practiced. She stopped and asked whether I felt sleepy.

“A little.”

“Don't look for hats tonight, then.”

I hadn't mentioned the comedy booklet, but agreed with a smile. We'd read plenty from it. An evening could pass without opening it. Next time, she might try something beyond today's stopping place. No guarantee of a finished join. All right. I'd hear whatever she wanted to sing then.

Standing, I touched the cup and remembered one mouthful remained. Hearing its base gently meet the table, Lumi asked whether I'd picked up an empty one again. Not this time. I drank what remained before putting it down.

“Good night, Elliot.”

“Good night, Lumi.”

After closing the channel, I washed the cup. Passing the drying clothes, I saw the two still-damp socks and couldn't immediately distinguish the thin one. Tomorrow, then. No new message waited for confirmation in the bedroom. I didn't open the drawers either. After the light went out, the repeated phrase returned briefly in my memory.

I couldn't supply its next line. Tomorrow didn't require me to compose it for her.
