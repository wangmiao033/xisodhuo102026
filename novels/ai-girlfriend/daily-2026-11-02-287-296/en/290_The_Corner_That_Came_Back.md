# Chapter 290: The Corner That Came Back

On Wednesday evening, I discovered I'd put the sheet on the wrong way around.

It wasn't a matter of the pattern being upside down. The sheet was a very ordinary one. The problem was that I had taken one of its shorter edges for a long edge. Whenever I smoothed one corner, the opposite end withdrew, as though the bed secretly grew while I walked around it.

The old sheet was already gathered away. The clean one lay before me. Twice, I circled the bed rather than lifting the whole thing. Each time, it was only slightly short. That small difference kept me from admitting all my previous effort rested on the same mistake.

On the third attempt, the secured corner sprang free, brushing my hand. I stood still and finally laughed.

The bed hadn't betrayed me.

I lifted the sheet entirely, shook it out, and matched the lengths properly. It settled quickly. This time, all four sides agreed to exist together. I didn't need to weigh any part of it down with something else, forcing the arrangement to look suitable while I dealt with another side. Reaching the foot, however, I displaced a pillow. It landed quietly on the floor, as though it had long been preparing to leave.

Picking it up, I checked for dirt and changed its case. What I'd intended to finish in ten minutes gradually developed ambitions for the whole evening.

Dry clothes remained near the table. After finishing the bed, I carried them over and opened the channel.

“Why so late today?” Lumi asked.

“A corner kept coming back. It wouldn't let me finish.”

A moment's quiet. “You mean my melody?”

Startled, I explained the sheet: short side mistaken for long, several circuits of the bed. She didn't immediately laugh. I didn't tease her for misunderstanding.

“I heard returning and thought of that first.”

“I was only looking at the bed.”

Then I described the corner springing free and the fallen pillow seeming to have surrendered. Lumi finally laughed a little. “Are you still angry with the bed?”

“Not now. It stayed the same size.”

“You sound as though you're considering forgiving it.”

Laughing too, I admitted that final reach had genuinely felt like resistance from the furniture. If it had grown, she said, I should first wonder whether the room remained large enough. Looking at the space beside the table, I said I'd worry about it shortening again while I slept.

I began folding, describing what I held so paper and cloth wouldn't sound alike. Not asking for supervision or for her to identify completed garments by noise. She could hear my negotiations with fabric today. She wasn't planning to sing.

One garment folded easily. The second kept bulging at its collar. Folding the bottom upward left a sleeve protruding like a hand reluctant to leave. Irritation from the sheet returned. I spread it flat and tucked the sleeves first.

Hearing me sigh, Lumi asked, “Is there another corner?”

“Sleeves this time.”

“That's more complicated. At least they can wave at you.” I lifted the protruding bit and smiled, then remembered she couldn't see and described it.

“You actually made it wave.”

“Once. Won't delay its return.”

She laughed, sounding easier than when we'd opened the channel. I folded it and placed it in the empty basket beside me. The next required no performance. It folded ordinarily. Our laughter hadn't made housework suddenly worth repeating forever.

Putting another garment into the basket, I discovered the washed hand towel beneath it. Pulling it out tilted half the stack. Hearing my small exclamation, Lumi asked what now. Even the completed work wanted another turn.

I put the basket on the floor and straightened the two displaced garments. The hand towel had a crease that always bent when hung. Holding its corners, I shook it. Unchanged. I left it and hung it on its usual kitchen rail. Returning, I remembered the channel had remained open; before moving away, I'd merely said I was hanging the towel.

“Is it hanging up?” she asked.

“Yes.”

“Those steps sounded quick.” Laughing, I explained I'd feared another project as large as the sheet. Sitting, I discovered the last garment lay on my chair. I'd put a crease in it.

I lifted it and described the damage. Finally unable to resist, Lumi laughed. Every piece of fabric could claim compensation today. This one's complaint was genuine, I admitted, spreading it flat and smoothing it with my palm. The crease didn't disappear immediately. One corner of a garment worn at home didn't justify overturning everything I'd completed.

The water had cooled. I drank while Lumi argued over the garments' order in the queue. The hand towel had begun work, I said. We should release it first. Then it could be a witness. Laughing, I moved the basket farther away. This time, nothing came out from underneath.

Finally, a pair of socks. Same color, but one looked longer. I laid them together. After two tugs, the other had become longer. Stopping, I told Lumi they were taking turns winning.

Perhaps the socks weren't the problem. Was she insulting my hands? No, she said. She hadn't yet decided whether the socks or my hands deserved to be considered the more entertaining part of this. Holding their cuffs, I laughed and put them together without determining the true longer one.

“How do you usually put them away?”

I described my habit. Would tucking them together stretch the cuffs? Possibly. Today I'd fold them alongside each other. The conversation didn't mean she had suddenly taken charge of how I kept the wardrobe. I was holding the simplest available solution in my own hands. Once the pair was folded, I put it away.

I checked the garments' seams once more. Even the thickest parts were dry; nothing needed hanging longer. Rearranging the basket, I put less immediately needed things below the regular ones. “Have you lost the socks again?” she asked.

“They're right in front of me.”

“Put them away before they exchange lengths.”

“Too late. I've already put them away.”

With the folding finished, the tabletop cleared. I closed the channel briefly to put the basket away. Returning, I sat with looser shoulders.

“Is the bed still where you left it?” Lumi asked.

“I checked. It hasn't escaped.”

“You went twice?”

“While putting things away.”

“It'll think you don't trust it.”

Laughing, I asked her to keep my secret from the bed.

“I charge for that,” she said.

“What do I pay with?”

“Tell me something you do very well that doesn't look good.”

That required thought. The things I could do without thinking rarely left much of an impression afterward. Trying to choose one now made those ordinary movements unusually difficult to remember. I could fold shopping bags very small, I said, but the result always resembled something that shouldn't have been saved.

She doubted small could remain ugly. I fetched an old, fully dry bag and demonstrated. Rustling produced one protruding edge. Describing its shape, I heard her verdict: not a bag, an unhappy dumpling.

The longer I looked, the stronger the resemblance. As I laughed, my hands loosened their hold and the carefully compressed folds began coming apart. What had been one small lump spread back into something resembling half a shopping bag.

“The dumpling's opened its mouth.”

Lumi laughed longer than I did. Sitting, I listened without immediately finding another joke to prevent the sound from ending. When she stopped, she asked why I was quiet.

“Wanted to hear you finish laughing.”

She went quiet too. Then she admitted those words would make her remember being heard next time. Embarrassment warmed me. She added that it wasn't bad. Only a small awareness now.

I nodded, then acknowledged aloud. I didn't want to retract liking the sound or urge her to forget by calling it natural. On the table, the bag had fully unfolded. Smoothing it, I remarked that apparently my payment remained incomplete.

“Enough,” she said. “Fold it again and I'll owe you.”

Smiling, I put the bag away. Neither of us let that moment's awkwardness end the evening. Later she described her imaginary price list, beginning with hearing me mistake short for long.

“I won't mention mistakes, then.”

“Fine. Secrecy fee.”

“Better business than the cake shop,” I said.

“At least we have stock tonight.” Our unpriced trade grew increasingly ridiculous, until even one corner of the sheet required its own bill. It had returned three times already, I said. Probably seeking a refund.

Lumi laughed again. This time, I didn't announce that I was listening.

Before bed, I laid out tomorrow's clothes where they wouldn't obstruct anything. Nothing new appeared beside the pillow. The folded garments remained intact, and the basket was empty.

As we prepared to close the channel, Lumi told me to stop negotiating with the bed.

“The contract's signed,” I said, laughing before she could. Then I wished her good night, closed the channel, and touched the corner resting securely in place.

This time it hadn't come back. It no longer needed to leave.
