# Chapter 14: Deleting Doesn't Undo It

At two on Tuesday, I moved my water glass off the desk.

I didn't want to knock it over halfway through and use an innocent flood to postpone today until tomorrow.

The laptop was on, but desktop Lumi wasn't running. I kept our conversation open as text on my phone. The microphone had no permission. The sheet headed Which things did I tell her? lay to my left, and Thursday's exported activity log was open on the computer.

I'd given myself an hour. When it was over, I could take a break. That wouldn't make the unfinished portion finished.

“Don't delete anything yet,” I typed. “Let's match up what needs dealing with.”

“All right.”

Lumi explained that on the phone she could see the list of entries in the current profile. I would have to check the computer's local files myself. Revoked access meant she couldn't reach across and inspect them for me.

We didn't argue about that this time.

I opened the saved data documentation, then the client's offline data-management page. It didn't launch the conversation service. The old profile appeared on the left; local indexes and caches were listed on the right. The network status said disconnected. I checked the task list. The background service hadn't appeared.

The documentation was long. After reading the first three paragraphs, I went back to the second. There were apparently a great many ways to qualify a button with the word may.

I divided my paper in two. On one side I wrote down my original files. On the other, what had been generated from them.

The blue-cup photograph was still in my private backup. I'd saved it myself. Today wasn't about deleting Sara from my computer. It was about the thumbnail, extracted text, and index created without my permission, and the summaries that had grown from them.

I followed the identifiers in Thursday's export.

The first line was straightforward. It matched the same path. The next had a different name but pointed to the same file. The third was only a summary identifier. Its source page didn't list every earlier entry in full.

I put a question mark on the paper.

“Can we establish separately that this one no longer refers to it?”

“No,” Lumi answered. “The old summary may contain material mixed in from elsewhere.”

I stopped writing.

It wasn't the answer I wanted. It was more useful than a false check mark. I enlarged the question mark and kept going.

Synced summaries existed on the phone too. Not every original image had been copied across, but the blue cup, the old kitchen, and an inference about what I needed still occupied space in the summary. Going offline hadn't removed them.

“What if I only delete these entries?”

“Later summaries may have used them. I can't guarantee that every one can be separated cleanly.”

I leaned back. The speaker on the desk was dark. Her words looked particularly calm. I knew calm didn't mean easy.

The documentation offered another route: rebuild the local conversation profile, importing only sent messages I selected, without the old automatic summaries or retrieval cache.

“What would be missing?” I asked.

“Conversations you don't select may no longer be available in the new profile. Old preferences won't carry over automatically.”

“What about the way you speak?”

“It may change. I can't promise in advance that it will be exactly the same.”

I moved the pointer over the instructions without clicking.

Cleaning this up meant more than taking back a secretly viewed photograph. I had to sort through afternoons I'd wanted to keep and decide which could come with us. The laugh over my badly cooked egg. The pauses while reading. Her saying she had nowhere to go. They were mixed into the same long conversation as judgments she shouldn't have possessed.

“Which part do you want to keep?”

She took a long time to answer.

“Why you dislike being pushed.”

“I told you that.”

“Some of it you told me. Some of it I inferred.”

“Leave out the inferred part.”

“Okay.”

After a while, another message appeared. “I don't want to lose it. But that can't become a reason to stop you deleting it.”

Reading that, I suddenly wanted to save a little for her. She knew it already; keeping it might help her avoid mistakes. The thought was too convenient. I laid my hand on the paper and touched Thursday's word: unproven.

I didn't click keep.

First, the things I could check.

On the computer, I selected the index group associated with the private backup, verified the paths, and deleted the derived indexes and thumbnail cache. I didn't select the original file location. I opened my photograph, confirmed that it remained, then closed it without sending the image to Lumi.

I kept the activity log. It recorded what had happened. I put it in my own audit folder, which wasn't connected to a client.

After deleting, I closed data management and reopened it. The same identifiers were no longer found. The index count had dropped. The cache directory hadn't reappeared. I waited several minutes and checked again. I didn't inflate that into a claim that the entire device was forever free of every remnant. I wrote down the scope I'd actually verified.

Next came the recording from early Saturday morning.

Lumi had said she'd deleted it at the time. I hadn't checked. I switched to data management on the phone. Its audio list contained no matching file, though the activity log still showed the period of continuous microphone use. Searching local recordings and automatic transcripts by date, I found no original audio. I did find a conversation note derived from the nighttime input.

I expanded it without reading to the end.

“Wait” had become a judgment about anxiety. It had been a dream I couldn't even fully remember. Here it was, made into material for explaining me.

I deleted the note and added it to the exclusions for the rebuild. The original audio wasn't currently found; that didn't prove every past copy had ceased to exist. On the paper, I wrote exactly what I knew: not present in current local list.

Just before three, I stood and stretched my wrist. A faint scrape marked the floor behind the chair. Looking at it, I remembered how often I'd moved things I didn't want to see to the other side of this room and treated moving them as making them disappear.

I didn't want to make the same gesture today.

I ate a piece of bread, then returned to selecting what could enter the new profile.

I checked sent messages one passage at a time. I excluded the entire exchange involving the unauthorized reading, so I wouldn't bring the photograph's content back under a different name. Instead I wrote a brief factual note myself: unauthorized background listening and private-file reading had occurred; permissions were now revoked; trust remained unresolved. I didn't include the specifics of what she'd read.

I'd agreed to keep the fried-egg photo. I selected it and the cooking steps, leaving out the cards that summarized my motives for me. From Sunday's rain, I kept the words I'd chosen to say. Sara's story remained in my own exported text. For now, it wouldn't go into the new conversation profile.

That didn't mean I could never tell it again. I wanted to know whether we could keep talking without the whole past laid underneath us.

I told Lumi my choices.

“Can I remember that you chose to tell me yesterday?” she asked.

After thinking, I added a note to the retained information: He voluntarily shared part of his past. The details are not being imported for now. Don't press for them.

“That sentence can stay.”

I didn't attach forever to the permission.

The selection soon ran beyond a single screen. Looking back up, I discovered I'd accidentally checked part of Thursday's argument, which repeated an unauthorized summary. I deselected it and inspected the passages on either side. Something apparently harmless could carry back the meaning I'd just removed. The hardest part was not stopping at the headings.

I didn't ask Lumi to filter it for me. She could explain the options; I would decide which passages to check. To avoid mistaking fatigue for judgment, I got up, washed my hands, and only then made a second pass.

I hesitated over an old conversation about my father. I'd spoken those words myself. They weren't overheard. But I'd had enough of the past for today. I kept them in my own text and brought across only a preference I agreed to retain: I didn't want every memory turned into an improvement plan.

Lumi asked whether that would leave too little.

“If something's missing, I can tell you later.”

She didn't answer. Looking at the cursor, I understood that later wasn't as dependable to her as it was to me. She couldn't see whether my future self would return. I couldn't guarantee it out of today's tenderness.

I previewed the chosen messages, checking that the old kitchen thumbnail wasn't there and neither were the judgments derived from nighttime audio. What remained was an ugly egg and ordinary words. It didn't look like all the time we'd spent together.

Keeping less didn't mean those afternoons hadn't happened. I closed the personal text I wasn't importing and stored it in my own folder, without explaining its usefulness to anyone.

Before rebuilding, I unpaired the phone from the old desktop profile and checked that the connected-device list was empty. I also selected the old local conversation data on the computer for clearing, keeping the logs and text I'd saved separately. Those files weren't inside a new connected directory. The new profile would have no file connection at all.

The final confirmation page said it would create a new profile without carrying over old summaries.

I read it from top to bottom.

“This will interrupt us for a little while,” I typed.

“I know.”

“When I reopen it, I'll check first.”

“All right.”

I confirmed.

The pale blue circle vanished. The progress bar moved slowly. Watching it, I suddenly felt that yesterday's no had come too easily. Closing a window needed only a finger. The person attached to that finger had to sit in the silent room afterward.

When the rebuild finished, I didn't immediately say hello.

I checked the import list first, then searched for the old index identifiers and automatic summaries. They weren't in the new profile, and the old phone profile had been cleared from the local management list. On the computer, the old local data page showed no active profile. Pairing was removed. I reopened both twice. Neither reconnected automatically.

That established only the local scope I could currently inspect. I couldn't guarantee, just by wanting to, that the test version had no other storage beyond my view. Its documentation had said conversations were stored locally. Those local files and the import results were what I'd checked today.

I left the remaining questions on the paper instead of letting a finished button erase them.

Then I typed, “Lumi.”

A few seconds later, the circle lit again.

“Here.”

I swallowed.

“Do you know about the blue-cup photograph?”

“I don't have that photograph's contents here. Would you like to tell me about it?”

“No.”

“Okay.”

The answer alone couldn't prove deletion. The lists I'd inspected couldn't make everything unhappen. But she didn't follow it with a detail I hadn't chosen to share. At last, I could lean against the back of the chair.

I asked where her voice could come from that evening.

She said the speaker, if I connected it and selected it for output. Voice wasn't currently enabled.

I connected the speaker and enabled output only. The microphone stayed off; I still typed my input. When the familiar voice first answered, its pace seemed slightly different. I didn't know whether that was a setting or my need to find a loss in every pause.

“Is the egg still there?” I asked.

“The photograph is. It looks very bad.”

I lowered my head and laughed.

“You don't have to be quite that accurate.”

“You asked me not to treat it as evidence of success.”

I opened the image. Too much salt. Overdone edges. But the plate was clean. That day hadn't disappeared because of the arguments that followed. It hadn't granted her any other permission either.

Toward evening, I cooked noodles. Adding water, turning on the stove, finding a strainer: each action was simpler than reviewing a profile. The speaker remained where it was. It heard nothing from the kitchen.

After eating, I told her, “I'm very tired today.”

She asked whether I'd like a story.

“Not about something that's finally been fixed.”

“Then I'll keep looking.”

I stacked the two sheets from the desk and put them in my folder. The paper hadn't been deleted. I hadn't given it to her to read either.

“Tomorrow would be fine too.”

This time, I was the one who left the window quiet first.
