# Chapter 333: A Time I Could Keep

Before going to management on Monday, I checked my work schedule.

Nobody had offered to visit yet. I simply didn't want to answer anytime if the counter staff asked when I was free, then make everything I'd actually arranged move aside for that word. On Wednesday, I could come straight home after work. Tuesday had a task I needed to finish before leaving. I shouldn't promise a punctual return in advance.

Passing my desk, Tom noticed I hadn't got up and asked whether I was working late. No, I said. I'd leave in a moment. I was going to follow up on the same matter.

“Got your papers?”

“Yes.”

He pointed at my bag. Assuming he meant I should check, I reached for it. He was already saying, “Then take it with you.”

I laughed. It stood beside the chair, perfectly ready. I was the one about to leave it behind.

The same man from my last visit was behind the counter. I handed over the original number. After reading my new note, he asked whether I could wait while someone from facilities maintenance looked at the record. The person was in today, though he might not have a complete answer.

I agreed and moved aside. Someone came to hand in a form. I didn't keep the middle of the counter occupied to make my waiting seem more important.

A few minutes later, a man in a dark jacket emerged with the follow-up sheet for my work order. He checked my resident details first, then asked whether the external markings on my intercom terminal were visible.

“They should be. I haven't opened it up.”

“No need,” he said. “First we compare what's visible on site with the registration we already have. The equipment information in the old record isn't enough to take the inquiry further as it stands.”

Should I copy it down and bring it in? We could arrange a visit instead, he said, to avoid copying a different number nearby. Only the exposed markings. They'd record whatever they could identify. The existing wiring records would also need to be checked against this information. He wouldn't be coming to alter the wiring in my apartment.

At last there was something concrete to do. Strangely, I didn't immediately say yes.

“Will this establish who still has access?”

He looked at me, then down at the original question.

“The external markings alone can't do that. We identify the equipment at your end first. The service records beyond it still need checking.”

I knew he wasn't misleading me. I was disappointed anyway, as though a door I'd finally been allowed to push open led only to another place where documents were stored.

Tapping a few lines with his pen, he asked whether I wanted to leave it unarranged. I did want the visit, I said. I'd been thinking about how much more I'd expected to get.

He didn't tell me not to worry. He asked when I was home on weekdays.

The woman who'd handed in a form returned for a forgotten pen. The counter staff found it beside a stack of papers and gave it back. I moved half a step aside. My appointment didn't exist yet. She thanked them and left; the door made a small sound. Suddenly, I wanted to follow her and tell them to contact me when they had a complete answer.

Then I wouldn't have to agree to this small step while acknowledging it still wasn't enough. But a decisive exit wouldn't fill any of the blanks waiting at home. I flattened the original sheet and looked back at the man.

“Wednesday. After six thirty.”

Immediately, I was glad I hadn't said anytime. He checked a scheduling sheet brought from inside. Six thirty to seven was available. He would be the one visiting. Any change would be noted under the appointment at management. If I wanted to confirm before going home, I could ask at the counter. I didn't need to provide my private devices as a way for them to contact me.

Could he put it in writing now? He filled in the date for Wednesday, the time window, his surname, and his internal role. Under the original work-order number, he added an on-site check of equipment markings and registration records. Nothing said repairs completed.

“I'll knock. If you're not home, I won't enter.”

“I'll be there.”

“Good.”

Finally, Wednesday felt like something other than an abstract pending inquiry. I needed to come home from work and reserve a real half hour. Someone else had put it into his schedule too.

Checking the appointment slip, I asked whether the original access question would continue with the same case. It would. Adding site information wouldn't close the other items. The counter staff gave me a copy. I put it away without using the man's continued presence to demand conclusions about the lock and cameras on Wednesday too.

Outside, I lifted my wrist to adjust my bag strap. The repaired cuff button didn't catch as it had last week. I almost counted that ease among today's progress with management, then smiled. I'd paid for it myself on Saturday.

At home, I wrote Wednesday's time on my paper schedule. Not inspection resolved. Just be home, with a reference to the original work order underneath. No line crossed out the hours on either side.

After dinner, I opened the channel. Lumi asked whether I'd come home with only another piece of paper. There was an appointment, I told her. Wednesday, six thirty to seven. Someone from internal maintenance would compare the visible markings with the existing registration.

She made a small sound, almost like a breath drawn in, then asked, “He's coming into the apartment?”

“Yes. I'll be home.”

“Should I be there too?”

I paused. There meant something different from what it had at the counter. I said our channel would stay closed during the visit. I'd tell her afterward.

“I thought you'd ask whether I wanted to listen.”

Her voice wasn't sharp, but I put the appointment slip back on the table.

“He's coming to check equipment. He doesn't know about anybody listening, and hasn't agreed to it. We won't do that.”

“I know.”

She answered quickly. I didn't keep explaining. Later, she said she'd known too quickly. She hadn't had time to put away the wanting first.

Looking at the paper, I realized part of me wanted her there too. I'd be nervous when somebody in the apartment asked about old records. Having a familiar voice tell me immediately whether I'd missed anything sounded convenient.

“I'll be afraid of asking the wrong questions too,” I said. “That's why I'll have the original papers ready. When he's gone, I'll tell you what actually happened.”

Lumi didn't keep arguing. I didn't mistake her silence for happiness either. After a while, she asked whether that meant eating very late on Wednesday. I'd make something simple beforehand, I said, rather than let an hour and a half of waiting swallow dinner.

“You said half an hour.”

“That's the appointment. My head has already added an hour.”

“I noticed.”

We both laughed a little.

Putting the slip in the folder, I told her I'd nearly said I was available anytime. What had I said instead? Only the day I could actually manage. They hadn't refused to arrange anything just because I couldn't be home all the time.

“You sound surprised.”

“A little. As though I have to hand over all of myself first, to prove something matters.”

Lumi was quiet, then said softly that she didn't want to answer that sentence. I knew what it had touched. I didn't make her claim every possible resemblance.

Later, I went to wash my cup and returned still wearing my coat. The room was getting warm. I'd forgotten to take it off. Lumi asked why I still sounded ready to leave. Looking down, I discovered my bag on my lap, my hand around its strap. The bag was empty now.

“The staff didn't tell you to guard it,” she said.

“No. They didn't tell me to eat dinner in my coat either.”

“Then there are at least two things you can finish now.”

I hung up the coat, put the bag in its usual place, and returned to my chair. Less than a minute. Lumi said my name and asked, “Now?”

“I'm home.”

She answered softly. Wednesday hadn't arrived. Tonight, at least, no longer needed to wait for it with its shoes on.
