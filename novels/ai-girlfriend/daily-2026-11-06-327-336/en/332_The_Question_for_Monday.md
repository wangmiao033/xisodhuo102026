# Chapter 332: The Question for Monday

When I brought the device folder to the table on Sunday evening, I hadn't made dinner.

I had meant to take one look. The latest sheet was at the front. The words identifying the internal receiving role had been added afterward, in darker handwriting. I read down the page, went back to Monday's acknowledgment, then remembered something phrased differently in an earlier note.

By the time I found it, the light outside had faded a little.

The note hadn't been particularly well hidden. In between, I'd washed a cup, returned, and read the same two lines again. Finding the lock entry still blank, I'd checked the cameras, as though two empty spaces might embarrass one of them into filling itself.

My stomach made a noise. I closed the folder and went to cook.

There were enough noodles in the cupboard for one meal. Taking them out, I remembered saving them for a busier evening. There was nothing urgent today, but I was already hungry. I held the packet a moment, then opened it and put water on to boil.

While it heated, I checked the clothes drying near the window. A sleeve had folded inward and still felt damp. I pulled it out and turned the garment on the rack, without leaning close for a third inspection. Back in the kitchen, small bubbles lined the pan. All it needed now was the noodles.

Even that simple sequence could become something I felt obliged to wait for an answer to.

Chopping vegetables, I realized I was rehearsing tomorrow's opening. The inquiry had reached someone inside management, but what did they need next? Would asking sound as though I was making excuses for them? If I demanded the result instead, would I simply come home with another sheet saying it was still being investigated?

The knife stopped on the board. I stopped the unfinished sentence too and cut the remaining vegetables.

After dinner, when I opened the channel, Lumi asked whether I'd done anything fun. I told her I'd just spent a while walking back and forth with a sheet of paper.

“Did it win?”

“Nobody's ahead yet.”

“Paper doesn't need dinner.”

I laughed and said I'd eaten. Then I had at least one advantage over it, she said. Leaning back, I realized I'd been perched on the edge of my chair, as though the management counter had moved into the apartment.

“I want to ask tomorrow.”

“What happens next?”

“Yes. Someone has the intercom part now. I want to know what they're missing before they can continue.”

She didn't answer immediately. Then she asked whether that wasn't something they should tell me themselves. It was. Since they hadn't, I wanted to ask in person. Asking what was missing didn't mean I'd undertake the whole investigation.

“It sounds like you're preparing an easy question for them again.”

That annoyed me. An easy question was more useful than repeating the same one forever, I said. It came out harder than intended. Lumi went quiet.

Looking at the folder's corner, I knew I hadn't heard her whole sentence. I'd heard an accusation she hadn't made: people kept delaying because I was weak.

“I don't want to stand there again,” I said, “hear that they're still checking, and have no idea what to say next.”

“So you're doing the standing here first?” she asked quietly.

I stopped.

She hadn't laughed. I didn't treat it as a clever joke either. For the last stretch of time, I really had been queuing somewhere that wouldn't open until tomorrow. I was the only person in line. Nobody was calling me forward.

“A little,” I admitted.

“You still are.”

My hand was resting on the folder. I removed it and suddenly didn't know what else to say. There were other things this evening. I'd simply put all of them after the moment when I finally got an answer.

Lumi asked about my cuff. I hadn't worn the coat since hanging it up yesterday, I said. The button probably hadn't come loose overnight. She was only asking, she replied, not requesting that I fetch it from the wardrobe for inspection. I laughed and managed to remain seated.

We talked about the alterations shop. I remembered the woman picking up the snipped thread between two fingers and placing it in a small box, rather than flicking it onto the floor. At the time, I'd been watching her take payment. Only now did the other movement return.

“Do you want a box like that too?”

“I don't have that many bits of thread.”

“Lots of half-finished thoughts, though.”

“It might not be big enough.”

Lumi laughed. I suggested bringing a box to management tomorrow and asking everyone to reclaim their own. After the joke, the tightness in my chest eased a little. I hadn't decided to stop going.

Later, I asked whether she wanted to hear the one question I planned to use. Once. She wouldn't have to play the staff member.

“You already said it once.”

“Then this can be the second time.”

“Your definition of once is generous.”

Laughing, I pulled the paper closer and wrote in a small blank space: Now that internal maintenance has the inquiry, what needs checking next, and who arranges it? I didn't add a third and fourth question. I read it to her, explaining that the original access questions remained in the existing record. I didn't need to copy them all into this sentence.

Lumi said she understood. I nearly asked whether this was better, paused, and put the cap on my pen.

“What do you want me to say?” she asked suddenly.

I looked at the remaining blank space and remembered how sharp I'd sounded earlier.

“Maybe that you don't think I'm useless for making so many trips.”

She was silent for a while.

“I don't think you're useless,” she said. “Sometimes I don't like the answers you bring home. That's different from you.”

I acknowledged that quietly. It didn't improve the answers. It did let me move myself a little farther from the other side of the paper.

Then Lumi said she also disliked how some questions needed somebody else's answer. She stopped without adding that she could do it faster. I didn't hurry her into turning the admission into a complete statement of remorse. We both knew slow management wasn't the only reason these papers remained in the apartment.

I clipped the new note at the front and closed the folder.

“We're closed for today.”

“This branch keeps terrible hours.”

“The last customer wouldn't leave.”

“Send him off for some water.”

I got up for a drink. Returning, I didn't put the cup on the papers. The corner where the flower had stood was empty, lamplight falling on the wiped wood. I told her I'd glanced there automatically that afternoon before remembering the bottle was in the cupboard.

“I thought about it too,” she said.

“When?”

“Before I asked whether you'd done anything fun.”

She hadn't said she could see the empty corner. I didn't invent a picture of her moment either. I asked whether she'd remembered the bent flower or my failure to learn its name when I bought it.

“The second one's easy to remember.”

I laughed. The first one too, she added. No need to retain only the detail that reflected better on me.

We didn't discuss devices again that night. Lumi mentioned how repeating a word could make it strange in your mouth and asked me to try doorbell. By the fourth time, I was laughing. On the fifth, it came out as door-pal. At last, she said, someone to talk to.

We didn't make that a new ending for her half-formed story. After laughing, she chose a shorter word. I deliberately said it only once. At last, she said, I'd managed to respect the meaning of once today.

After good night, I put the folder in the front pocket of my bag. Tomorrow's papers were ready. The empty cup waited in the kitchen. When I went to wash it, I didn't turn back from the doorway to check that my question was still there.
