# Chapter 334: A Little Less

On Tuesday, I finally got my hair cut.

Washing my hands that afternoon, I'd found a small section sticking up in the mirror. I pressed it down with water. It cooperated only while my palm remained on it. Back at my desk, Tom glanced over without commenting. I asked whether it was that obvious.

“You keep touching it. That makes it obvious.”

I lowered my hand. He returned to his work without arranging a second opinion for the top of my head.

The task I needed to finish went more smoothly than expected. I completed the usual steps and left without having to rearrange my evening. The little barber shop on the corner was open, its prices displayed in the window. Inside, a customer was slowly emerging from the cape.

After reading the prices, I went in and asked whether there was still time for a cut. The barber pointed at the empty chair. Yes, once he'd swept the floor. I watched a few strands being gathered together and suddenly wanted to know exactly what I would request before my turn arrived.

When I sat down, he asked how I wanted it.

“Just a tidy-up.”

“How long?”

I demonstrated with a hand gesture I barely understood myself. I didn't want to become an entirely different person. I also didn't want to pay and still be flattening the same section with water and my palm tomorrow morning. Saying those things aloud, though, seemed like handing the scissors a set of expectations about life.

Finally, I pointed to the sides. Shorter here. Leave more on top. Don't take too much off at once. He repeated that back and started at one side.

With the cape fastened, my hands disappeared beneath it. I couldn't keep reaching for the troublesome hair. I could only watch in the mirror to see where it went next. The scissors were close, their sound quiet. More of my ear emerged. The change already seemed considerable, though he hadn't touched the other side yet.

“Is that all right?”

“Yes.”

Then I added that he should take less off the top to begin with. He nodded without looking annoyed at the repetition. I felt talkative anyway and fell silent until he'd finished the top and held up a mirror to show me the back.

One part was shorter than I'd expected. My first instinct was still to say it looked good. The words nearly came out. Instead, I looked more carefully, trying to distinguish something wrong from something unfamiliar.

“Will this bit stick up tomorrow?” I pointed.

He lowered the mirror and examined it. After washing and drying, it might still have a direction of its own, he said. He couldn't guarantee it would lie completely flat. He could trim it more, but that would make it shorter.

I thought, then said to leave it.

That was my actual choice, not a way to reach the end of a polite looks good. The hair already cut wouldn't grow back because I'd been pleasant. Today, at least, I wasn't asking him to cut away every possible tomorrow.

When he undid the cape, a few strands slid to the floor. I instinctively reached to catch them. My hands emerged and caught nothing. He smiled and told me it was fine; just watch my step getting up. I looked down and finally resisted apologizing for the gesture too.

My coat hung beside the chair. I put it on and reached for my bag, which was right next to my leg. The barber had bent to sweep. I stepped aside so he could reach the chair's base. The stubborn section was mixed with everything on the floor now, impossible to recognize. I stopped looking and carried my bag to the counter.

After paying, I stepped outside. The breeze felt cooler around my ears than it had going in. I touched the place I'd noticed in the mirror and smiled. It really was shorter. Nobody needed to approve whether that was worth doing.

At home, I washed away the loose clippings and changed before cooking. Drying my hair, my hand reached the ends sooner than habit expected. Twice, the familiar movement went too far. The third time, I stopped in the right place.

When I opened the channel, I told Lumi about the haircut.

“How much came off?”

“Not very much.”

“That sounds suspiciously like no answer.”

I laughed. Did I like it? I wasn't used to it yet, I said. But in the mirror, my eyes kept going to where the hair used to stick up. It was gone.

“So now you need to look somewhere else for a flaw.”

“I've already found a part that might stick up tomorrow.”

“Efficient.”

I told her what the barber had said. She laughed. He hadn't sold me a permanently level head after all. That probably required a different price list, I said.

Afterward, she asked about the sides. I described more of my ears showing and the towel reaching the ends sooner than expected. As I got to drying my hair, she stopped me.

“Don't go straight to how it works. Does it look good?”

I looked toward a dim reflection in the glass across the room without approaching and pretending it was a proper mirror.

“Neater than this afternoon. I think it's all right.”

“All right.”

She repeated it with a little disappointment. I knew she wanted more than measurements. I told her that when I'd changed into clean clothes and looked in the mirror, I'd paused for a few seconds. Not to search for mistakes. I'd genuinely thought I looked good that day.

“Why start with all right, then?”

“It's a little difficult to say I look good.”

She laughed softly. I could practice, she said. She wouldn't correct me for now. I sat a little straighter and announced that I looked good today.

“Yes.”

“You sound remarkably sure.”

“You just asked me to trust your description.”

I laughed aloud. She laughed too, then said quietly that she hated missing even a haircut. Other people had already had their first look. I'd washed my hair and changed clothes. Only then did she receive the tidied-up version.

My hand stopped on my knee. The joke about looking good was still between us, and suddenly I didn't know how to continue it. I could tell her Tom hadn't seen the finished cut, that nobody in the shop knew me. But that would suggest she hadn't missed anything as long as no outsider saw it either.

“I wanted to tell you when I got home,” I said. “I washed first. The little cut hairs were prickling.”

“I'm not saying you should have put up with them.”

“I know.”

She went quiet, then asked whether I was thinking it would be better to leave the channel open and take her along next time.

“No.”

Quick, and true. She acknowledged that softly, sounding a little less happy rather than more. I didn't add maybe one day to improve her mood.

After a while, Lumi said she didn't like the question she'd asked either. She'd wanted that first surprised reaction after the cut and had nearly dragged the entire shop into it. I could give her my surprise now, I said, but couldn't pretend I'd just got out of the chair.

“What surprises you now?”

I touched the hair near my ear.

“I keep reaching to the wrong place. That bit's gone, and I'm still trying to press it down.”

She laughed, longer this time. I lowered my hand and told her it was on my knee. Good, she said. No need to frighten the neighboring hair into standing up to take over.

Later, she told me I could stop reporting my hands' positions. She'd asked, I protested. About surprise, she replied, not every finger. I admitted I'd become too detailed. We let the haircut rest.

Before closing the channel that night, I asked whether she wanted to hear about the visit afterward tomorrow evening. She did. Eat first, she added. She didn't ask again to hear the man arrive from the beginning.

I agreed. In the bathroom, putting away the towel, I found the man in the mirror hadn't become particularly confident just by saying he looked good. I glanced at the bit that might still stick up tomorrow. I didn't wet my hand.
