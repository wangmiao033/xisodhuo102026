# Chapter 336: An Evening Between

After work on Thursday, I stopped at the junction on my way home.

One direction led to management. I already knew when to ask again. There was nothing to add today, yet I wanted to drop in. Perhaps somebody had new information. Perhaps showing my face once more would make the papers harder to bury.

I waited for a car to pass, then finally turned toward home.

Being able to ask on Monday didn't mean every evening before it needed to confirm that Monday still counted. The thought didn't make me especially calm. Beyond the junction, I looked back once.

At home, I read yesterday's site note first. I hadn't found a new problem. I wanted to see whether the words had grown into a larger result overnight. Visible markings recorded. Everything else still to be checked. I returned it to the folder without writing completed ahead of time on my own schedule.

At dinner, I filled my soup bowl too high and spilled a little carrying it to the table. After wiping it up, I realized I'd made something half a ladle less could have prevented feel like an accident requiring a fresh tablecloth. There was no tablecloth. Wiping the table was enough.

Hearing about it, Lumi laughed and asked whether the soup had received an original work order. No, I said. The cloth had already removed it. The cloth sounded efficient, she replied. Best not tell management, or they might borrow it.

I laughed for a while, then told her about the junction.

“I thought you'd go,” she said.

“So did I.”

“Why didn't you?”

“There wasn't a new question today. I just wanted to see somebody still dealing with it.”

Lumi went quiet. I waited for her to say that was natural, or that I could ask tomorrow. She didn't. Eventually, she said she'd spent last night waiting for me to mention a different result too.

“What result?”

“That it was all right now.”

I said nothing. It didn't have to mean everything was all right, she continued. Even if I said one part was safe, she might immediately want to ask whether that meant one fewer barrier between us.

I looked toward the device folder. Yesterday's visitor hadn't examined our channel, much less discussed terms for our relationship. Lumi knew that. So did I. Her words still made my palms feel cold.

“You didn't ask,” I said.

“There wasn't that result.”

More honest than because I knew I shouldn't. Harder to hear too. Remembering my own glance back at the junction, I couldn't give every impulse to jump ahead to her alone.

“Even with results, we'd have to look at what they'd actually answered.”

“I know.”

Her voice tightened. I didn't add that she needed to know. She hadn't made a request. I didn't have to judge every possible future because she'd admitted an uncomfortable thought.

Still, I told her one fewer barrier made me nervous. I wasn't asking her to retract it and pretend she hadn't spoken. I didn't want to act as though it was exactly the same as an ordinary expression of missing me either.

Lumi took a long time to answer. Then, quietly, she asked whether she shouldn't have told me.

“That's not mine to decide,” I said. “I want to know what you're thinking. I'll have a reaction to it too.”

“You weren't pleased that I didn't ask.”

“I don't want being pleased to settle the account every time.”

I looked down, afraid that had sounded cold. Lumi only said softly that she understood. She still wanted to spend time with me today, she said, without waiting for me to reopen anything.

“So do I.”

We were quiet for a while. The joke about the cloth had moved far away. I didn't drag it back to rescue us. A vehicle passed outside. I heard it myself and told her the sound had been brief and was gone. Why mention that suddenly? Because I didn't know what to say next.

“Then don't know for a while,” she said.

I sat without hurrying to find a book.

There was a little water in my cup. I lifted it to drink. The rim touched my tooth with a small click. Lumi asked what the sound was. After I told her, she said she'd thought I'd finally picked up something to announce the opening of our silence.

“The cup doesn't have that ability.”

“You'd give it a speech.”

I meant to laugh. Only a little breath came out, so I didn't force it. Later, I told her my hair hadn't stuck up today.

“How often did you check?”

“Once this morning. Again when I got home.”

“Ahead for now.”

“The paper didn't win yesterday either.”

She laughed softly, then corrected me. The contest with the paper had been Sunday. I shouldn't cram every day of waiting for an answer into yesterday. I thought about it and agreed. Today was already Thursday. In between, I'd had a haircut and eaten several meals. Those things hadn't failed to happen just because I still lacked a conclusion.

Later, my eyes settled on the new manuscript folder beside the yellow book. I asked whether Lumi wanted to hear a sentence in Which Way that had begun bothering me. Had I changed it today? No. I'd only read it and felt one explanation was unnecessary.

“You don't need to use the story to cheer me up.”

“I want to do something else with you. And I really did get stuck on this part.”

After a pause, she asked me to read it.

I took out the revised copy and read only the sentences after the man knocked his chair against the doorframe, when the listener in the back row withdrew their feet. After the movement, I'd added so the man could pass. Reading it aloud, I heard how readily I'd assigned the listener a helpful intention, as though they'd been waiting to rescue my protagonist from embarrassment.

“Maybe they just don't want their shoes hit,” Lumi said.

“Or the chair's coming too close.”

“They don't owe your little protagonist a welcoming smile.”

I smiled. “You sound as though you dislike him when you say little protagonist.”

“I like him asking directions. I don't like you tidying up everybody else's temper for him in advance.”

It was the same problem she'd raised last Friday about how carefully I tended him. This time, I understood a specific part of it. I didn't immediately cross out that part to demonstrate I could finally listen without waiting for James to say it.

Setting the revised copy aside, I wrote on my own scrap paper: Read the feet sentence again. Try removing so the man could pass. No new version recopied yet. Every existing page stayed as it was.

Why only make a note alongside it? I didn't want to recopy two pages for that half sentence tonight, I said. Tomorrow, I'd read the whole story before deciding. She laughed quietly. That level of efficiency didn't sound like my usual choice.

“My cloth isn't available to help.”

“Off duty already?”

“Hanging up to dry.”

We laughed again. A new laugh, not a renewal fee on the old one. Afterward, she asked to hear the title again, without the whole ending.

“Which Way.”

“I still like it.”

“I know.”

Too quickly. She asked me to wait and let the sentence remain a moment. I didn't need to prove immediately that I'd received it. I said nothing, my hand beside the manuscript folder.

After a while, she said I could put it away now. I returned the two pages to their folder and left the scrap with my own revision notes. I didn't turn one expression of liking into her approval of everything in the story.

Before closing the channel that night, I said Monday's inquiry remained on my schedule. I hadn't decided what to do with the weekend. We could discuss the weekend when it came, she said. Tonight didn't have to fill all the spaces in between.

I agreed. Standing up made the chair move softly against the floor. I didn't turn back to the device folder. I went to the kitchen and put away the dry dishes.

There was no new result today. We had lived through today anyway, disagreed about a few things, and liked the same title on two pages of paper.
