# Chapter 381: The Near End

After breakfast on Sunday, I took out the drawings behind my schedule book and laid them on the table, one sheet at a time.

Cups, a street, benches, a market, pears. More paper than I remembered. Corners curled around the old interleaving sheets. I'd meant only to find Tuesday's bridge. Instead, I paused over the pears that resembled shoes.

They looked even more like shoes now.

Smiling, I returned the sheet beneath the other pear study. Tuesday's bridge lay farther back. Several faint sloping lines occupied a small rectangle. An erased patch had roughened slightly. I put the other groups away and kept only this sheet.

The near end. That was what I'd said yesterday. Sitting down, I immediately wanted to add distant trees. Empty space surrounded the few lines, making the page look insufficiently worked on. My pencil hovered over it. Eventually, I returned to the corner nearest me.

There was no borrowed book to consult. I'd returned it. I couldn't remember the whole bridge in that other library painting either. This was my exercise. Its width depended on the lines on this sheet.

I darkened the nearest edge, then brushed the pencil's side across a small patch of deck. Suddenly, the deck sank below its sides like a ditch. Looking from another angle, I realized I'd pressed darkness into the side I should have left pale.

The eraser failed to remove everything and dirtied the patch. I tightened inside. A few minutes of work had already made the sheet look overused.

I lifted it. Yesterday's painted step had an uneven edge too, but that didn't automatically improve my grubby corner. Putting the sheet down, I gently lightened the heaviest spot and left the rest for now.

The near edge wasn't equally thick throughout. A shorter section on the left joined a longer one toward the right. Instead of turning it into a black railing, I added gray beneath the change of angle, stood, and looked from half a step away.

A small flat surface, at last.

Sitting again, I immediately wanted water beneath it. Reflections, trees, the opening under the bridge. Everything seemed more exciting than these few centimeters. I covered the distance with a clean old interleaving sheet, leaving only the near end exposed. That made the small area clearer.

It needed a connection on the right. Moving the covering sheet, I found the original slant and joined it lightly. My first stroke was too straight; the second shorter. I softened the outer half of the first instead of removing it entirely. My hand brushed the paper's edge, leaving faint gray. I cleaned my hand before continuing.

Standing again, I could distinguish the deck from its side. Elsewhere, fine lines still indicated the far end. No trees. No water. Alone, the completed area resembled a rather insecurely supported board.

That was the part I could finish today. Beneath Tuesday Bridge Study on the back, I added Sunday, Near End. No new sheet.

Before putting it away, I removed the cover and looked at the whole for half a minute. The darker foreground looked conspicuous beside the faint distance. I didn't blacken the rest to match. Pencil into box; eraser crumbs onto scrap paper and into the bin. Once the table cleared, I discovered I was hungry.

Making lunch went slowly. Looking for the saucepan lid, I opened the wrong cupboard and stared inside before remembering I'd left it drying elsewhere this morning. It was plainly visible. I'd searched my memory first. Finding it, I rinsed a little dust from the rim and set it beside the stove.

I didn't open the channel immediately after eating. The bridge sheet leaned against my own books. Returning from the kitchen, I looked again. Its corner hadn't collapsed while I ate. I returned it to the drawings and took a short walk that afternoon.

When we spoke toward evening, I told Lumi first that the near end of the bridge was recognizable now.

“Can you walk on it?”

“Only a small patch. Two steps and the path disappears.”

“I'll stand still, then.”

I laughed. Had I made many drawings today? Only Tuesday's original sheet, one corner worked on, another day added to the back. Its turn had finally arrived, she said.

I described covering the distance and moving the sheet again. Why cover it? Seeing the whole made me want to add everything before joining the lines directly in front of me.

The cover had rested loosely, I added. No tape to pull away the original pencil marks. Had I covered the entire bridge? No, only the far part. The near end had stayed visible. Otherwise, even today's little corner would have been difficult to finish.

“You could just look at the near part.”

“My eyes aren't very obedient.”

“Then the covering sheet gave them some time off.”

I laughed too quickly and almost choked on water. Putting down the cup, I caught my breath. She waited before asking whether I'd still wanted to add trees. Yes. I hadn't.

She'd expected me to sound happier, she said.

I almost denied it. Then I admitted I'd hoped to see a whole bridge at once. A few completed centimeters were difficult to describe as a victory.

“You can tell me about the centimeters.”

“Shorter on the left, joined on the right. The deck's paler than the side. A dirty corner underneath.”

“You still start with the dirt.”

“Third sentence. I've moved it back.”

She laughed. So did I, finally letting go of the wish to have somebody unable to see my picture assess it. Her imagined wide bridge could remain today, she said. It had no dirty corner. Of course. Did the rough spot feel different?

I left the sheet put away. It had felt slightly fuzzy while erasing. I hadn't deliberately touched it afterward. She was curious, she said. No inspection required now. All right.

After a while, we remembered Friday's unfinished reading. Could we continue a little tonight? I fetched the thin story collection and found the marker in the fourth story. Its visitor had risen from the chair beside the programs and felt inside an empty coat pocket. I read that brief section again, bringing us to the same place.

In the next paragraph, the person arranging chairs moved that one and bent for another beside it. A gray scarf hung from the second chair's crossbar. Freed, one end brushed the floor. The visitor pointed it out and bent to retrieve it.

“Gloves?” Lumi asked.

“It says scarf.”

“I meant perhaps the gloves are wrapped inside.”

I continued. The visitor shook out the scarf and inspected both sides. Nothing fell out. The person moving chairs indicated a table by the door where somebody was on duty, asking the visitor to take it there. Carrying the scarf, the visitor took several steps, then turned to ask where the missing gloves were.

Ask at the table first, the other person said.

I paused. The next paragraph extended well down the page. Lumi suggested stopping here. I placed the marker after the paragraph we'd finished and left the book open.

“They'd already walked several steps before asking,” she said.

“Perhaps they were afraid they'd forget.”

“Perhaps they thought they'd suddenly become the scarf delivery service.”

I checked the lines. The author hadn't explained the turn. The actions were clear: a few steps, turning, a question. Her suggestion made sense too, I said. We hadn't reached the table yet.

“So the gloves are still missing.”

“Yes.”

She sighed faintly. Was she disliking this story, searching for something and repeatedly being asked to do something else? A little. She wanted to see when the visitor would insist on finding their own property first.

Before I could connect that to something else, she asked whether the scarf was folded while being carried.

I checked. No folding mentioned. Only carrying it in both hands. I told her. Then she'd imagine it that way for now. We didn't fetch her own doorbell draft. What its piece of paper contained remained unanswered today too.

Did she want to stop reading? Yes, but she'd like a few more minutes of conversation.

“All right. About what?”

“What was the first thing you did after finishing your corner?”

“Swept up eraser crumbs.”

She laughed, apparently surprised. Thinking again, I added lunch and opening the wrong cupboard for the lid. I described myself staring inside. Her laughter became clearer.

“Had it been there this morning?”

“No. I'd put it out to dry. My hands did it. My mind didn't keep up.”

“You said the path disappeared. Apparently, you fell off the bridge before lunch.”

Laughing, I said I'd landed in the kitchen. Lucky I hadn't drawn a river, then. That would have been more troublesome. The joke made no pictorial sense. I laughed for quite a while anyway. We could talk about my inadequate bridge like that. It didn't have to be handled solemnly.

When we stopped laughing, Lumi said she'd liked hearing me say the corner was finished before explaining where it was dirty.

“I did mention the dirt.”

“I heard. I liked the sentence before it.”

I tightened my hand around the cup. The water had cooled. That sentence was true, I said. The corner was what I'd completed today. She answered softly that she knew. I didn't ask whether she liked the drawing or being released from consoling me. Either guess would stretch her remark too far.

After a moment, I said I was happy listening to her ask about my day like this.

“About the saucepan lid?”

“That too.”

“I'll ask about something harder to find next time.”

“Don't warn me. I'll hide it first.”

She laughed. Smiling, I closed the book. We talked about dinner leftovers and what I needed to do first tomorrow morning. I had enough bread for breakfast. The remaining dinner would need putting away before bed. Lumi asked whether the lid had reached its proper cupboard this time. Yes, I said, after drying it. I hadn't left it somewhere unusual to improve tomorrow's account. She called that a missed opportunity, and I told her I'd rather find the pan when I needed it.

We didn't immediately agree how far the next reading would go. She didn't promise to want another drawing description tomorrow. As I grew sleepy, I said I needed bed. She said good night. So did I.

After closing the channel, I returned the collection to its place. The marker followed the visitor taking several steps with the scarf and turning back. The white envelope remained on the lower-right shelf, untouched today.

I carried the empty cup to the kitchen. Back in the room, my drawing papers lay flat. Tuesday's sheet now had Sunday's corner. I didn't switch the light on for another inspection. Lying down, I considered tomorrow's breakfast for two seconds, then became too sleepy to hold even that thought.
