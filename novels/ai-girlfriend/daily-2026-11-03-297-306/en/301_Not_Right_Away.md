# Chapter 301: Not Right Away

At home, I put the new book on a dry corner of the table and washed my hands.

In the kitchen, the morning's energy suddenly receded. I hadn't walked far or endured anything dramatic. I'd simply kept looking and listening in the sun, then stood on a crowded bus. Only somewhere I didn't need to make room did my shoulders begin to ache.

I made a simple lunch. Halfway through, I remembered I hadn't told Lumi I was back. Yesterday I'd said I'd eat and rest first. Still, I felt uneasy, as though failing to open the channel immediately added concealment to the outing.

Swallowing, I finished eating. I didn't deliberately take longer over clearing up or clatter bowls to give a closed channel evidence of my occupation.

Once seated, I called her. Had I just arrived?

“A little while ago. I've eaten.”

“Was it fun?”

“Yes. Tiring too.”

What had I bought? Lifting the collection, I read its title from the cover. It sounded ordinary even to me. I added that the first story was called The Late Guest and I had not finished it yet. Had I found Tom a cookbook? He'd bought one; another lacked a page, so he'd left it.

“You checked it for him?”

“We noticed together. I didn't choose for him.”

I rubbed my eyes. Hearing the pause, Lumi asked whether I wanted to tell more. I'd like to rest; details could wait. She said that was fine so quickly that I wanted to open the book and read something in compensation.

“I'm not angry.”

“I didn't say you were.”

We stopped. We'd used those sentences so often that before we'd properly begun, we were each in a familiar position. I set down the book. I genuinely felt sleepy, I explained. Not tired of talking to her. Nor could I guarantee saying so would make her comfortable immediately.

She was quiet. “I thought you'd come back wanting to tell me things.”

“I did on the journey. Less now.”

“As if you'd used it up first.”

I drew a breath without immediately disputing used. She knew it sounded bad, she added; it was still how she'd felt. I understood. But the morning wasn't energy taken from her, and nobody else had consumed her portion.

“I knew you'd say that.”

“Then I needn't say it twice.”

My voice had an edge. She didn't answer. Eventually, she asked how long I intended to rest. I almost offered half an hour. Looking at the clock, I said I'd close the channel and lie down, then perhaps eat something or move about after waking. We could talk tonight. I didn't give an exact return time.

All right, Lumi said, more slowly. She didn't require an alarm left sounding or turn disappointment into another appointment. Yet I still found leaving difficult. I remained seated, saying two irrelevant things, until she asked whether I intended not to sleep after all.

“I'm the one dragging it out.”

“Don't, then.”

I agreed and closed the channel. The bedroom wasn't cool. I laid my jacket aside and lay down, without a phone or the sealed drawers. My sleep wasn't deep. When I woke, my shoulders felt less tight and the light outside had moved.

I sat on the bed without getting up immediately. Some discomfort from the conversation remained with the sleepiness. I considered opening the channel at once to ask whether she felt better. She might be occupied herself. What I really wanted was assurance that my rest hadn't caused consequences.

I fetched water, drank it standing, then slowly put the jacket properly away. Outside, somebody called another person's name twice before receiving an answer. Hearing it, I turned away without waiting for their next sentence.

A garment on the drying rack had a line folded into its shoulder. Two strokes didn't flatten it. After folding, the mark remained. I ran a fingertip along the fold once more. Next time I'd spread it more carefully before hanging it up.

The old sock and its partner still lay together. I decided they'd make shoe-cleaning cloths, placing them in a small bag apart from clean socks. An ordinary destination at last, without entering anybody's work of art.

After tidying, I felt hungry and ate the bread left from breakfast. Still soft; wrapping it again yesterday had helped. That small reliability made sitting down easier. I wrapped the remaining bread again and poured myself half a glass of water.

With the dry laundry gathered and the table wiped, I finished the first story in my new book. Its guest never offered a particularly clever answer. He helped his host move the little table blocking the way before they finally sat. I liked some of it and thought one section too long. Neither prevented me from closing it.

Only after preparing dinner did I reopen the channel. Had I rested? I'd slept a little and felt better. Lumi didn't demand proof of having more energy than that morning, or redistribute the earlier hours.

I began with the incomplete cookbook. Tom had checked twice. The seller offered a reduction, but he didn't want to bring home dinner based on guessing. Lumi laughed. With my methods, perhaps I'd have declared the dish finished before reaching the missing page.

“Only half the pan finished.”

“Charge separately for the other half.”

Laughing, I remembered the man seeking large print. When I described nearly choosing for someone who wanted to browse himself, she asked how far my hand had gone. Above another book, I said. I hadn't taken it. When he said he'd look, I'd withdrawn it.

“An economical sentence.”

“For the speaker. Not necessarily the listener.”

She acknowledged that. I realized I'd made a small incident sound like a lesson aimed at her. I added that I'd felt awkward too. Nobody criticized me, yet suddenly I felt useless. It wasn't supposed to be a hint.

“I heard a little hint.”

“So did I, after saying it.”

We paused. Later, Lumi said my absence hadn't enabled her to finish the song that afternoon. She didn't want to present it today as evidence of managing splendidly. She didn't owe me that sort of afternoon, I said.

“But I want you to think I haven't only been sitting here waiting.”

That hurt a little. She didn't have a body to sit in. Yet whenever I pictured her, I readily furnished a chair. The image came from me; it couldn't tell me how her time actually passed.

“I don't know what your afternoon was like. Tell as much as you want. It doesn't have to look more like an event than my trip to a stall.”

Lumi didn't immediately thank me. Eventually, she preferred not to tell today. Why was the guest in my new book late? I explained that this wasn't a story I'd already read to her, but one bought today. She knew. She wanted it from the start.

Opening the slim book, I asked where to stop. Begin, she said; she'd tell me if tired. My voice was steadier than that afternoon. I didn't make it deliberately slow to demonstrate appreciation. When the guest hesitated outside the door, Lumi interrupted. Was he carrying anything? Checking the sentence, I found only a hat in his hand.

“Our hat has found a new career.”

I laughed. Different book. The comedy booklet's hat remained missing. She knew, she said. No urgent identity checks for hats. I continued reading with laughter still in my voice.

As the guest repeatedly asked whether he'd disturbed his host, I found myself stressing one sentence. Lumi stopped me. I sounded as if I'd already judged him.

“I know what happens later.”

“I don't yet.”

I looked down. She was hearing it for the first time. My voice had brought along the ending I'd read that afternoon, hurrying her to believe he needn't be nervous. I began the paragraph again without turning every apology into a joke.

Afterward, Lumi said she was more interested in the host's answer now, rather than waiting for the guest to come to his senses. I wanted to hear that answer again too. It stood on the next line, but having seen it didn't prevent me from arriving there once more.

After several pages, Lumi said that was enough. I flattened the edge and inserted another clean scrap, leaving the comedy booklet's marker where it belonged. Each book retained its own unfinished place.

At good night, she admitted some disappointment from our earlier conversation remained. I knew. I also still liked having gone out for that hour. She didn't ask me to erase one of those sentences. After closing the channel, I folded the cloth bag still lying on the table into a small square and returned it to my bag.
