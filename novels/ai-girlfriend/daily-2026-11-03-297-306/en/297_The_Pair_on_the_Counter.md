# Chapter 297: The Pair on the Counter

On Wednesday morning, the sock was finally dry.

I pulled it over my hand and held it toward the window. A small patch of light showed through the toe. Threads still crossed it, like a window that hadn't been completely dismantled. My thumb pressed the thin patch and nearly pushed through. I released it quickly. Personally destroying something to establish that it was wearing out wasn't an impressive method. Its partner lay over the chair back. I put them together without testing every other pair in the drawer.

I put it down and wore another pair to work. The question I'd left for myself last night hadn't secretly grown into an emergency while I slept.

At lunchtime, I folded the scrap on which I'd copied the rubbish-bag dimensions and put it in my pocket. In the household shop, the assistant pointed me toward the shelf. This time, I didn't rely on color. Two similar-looking packets were quite different widths. I chose the right one and went to the socks behind them.

One kind felt soft around the top but was too small. Another row had my size, in dark gray and a color that seemed reluctant to be called brown. I took gray. I didn't buy a look-alike replacement for the lonely sock at home in the hope of deceiving the drawer.

At the counter, the woman ahead opened her bag to reveal two smaller bags inside. Unable to find her money, she offered to let me go first. There was no hurry, I said, but she'd already moved her things and urged me forward. Only then did I discover I had to search my own bag.

“Let the wrong person through, apparently.”

She laughed. We could take turns. The assistant arranged my socks and rubbish bags and waited until I found what I needed. Nobody produced a stopwatch.

On the way back, Tom stopped me downstairs from the office. Holding a half-eaten roll, he glanced at my shopping and asked whether I was moving house.

“Rubbish bags and socks.”

“Small operation.”

I lifted the bag away from the doorframe. Swallowing, he mentioned a secondhand bookstall he'd passed. There had been several cookbooks; he wanted to look again on Saturday. Not the shop where I'd bought my comedy booklet, and not the library.

“Fancy coming? I'll probably get to that street around ten.”

My first thought wasn't of books. It was of a Saturday morning when I might finally stop checking the time. That wasn't something I especially wanted to admit. He'd asked one question, and already I felt refusing would waste our earlier missed chance to walk together.

“Can I tell you tomorrow?”

“Sure. Friday's soon enough.”

Indicating the roll, he said he'd better finish or he'd be eating the paper bag that afternoon. I let him go. He didn't wait for me to add a reason that sounded properly enthusiastic.

I left the socks in my work bag that afternoon and got on with things.

The sock packet showing above my bag brought ten o'clock back to me. I could get up a little later than on a working day, eat, and browse for an hour. Or decide I was too tired. Yet what I wrote on a scrap of paper was: Would the bookstall be awkward with Tom?

I looked at it. Wrong question. We'd eaten lunch together only last week. He wasn't someone requiring a fresh application for the position of friend.

I turned the paper over and wrote only Bookstall, Saturday, ten. I still left the answer until tomorrow.

Near the end of the day, the colleague beside me asked for scissors. I opened my desk drawer before remembering that my scissors were at home. She'd already found a pair among the shared supplies. Seeing me still searching, she asked whether I needed them too.

“No. Wrong place.”

Cutting open some packaging, she said she often reached for things where they'd been on her previous desk. She'd moved from the other side of the room more than a month ago, and still reached left for her water when it was on her right.

I said I'd bought socks today. The scissors ought to wait until I got home. She laughed and looked down again without asking why the socks mattered. I was glad I didn't have to recount last night's entire conversation to explain the remark.

A piece of cardboard fell as she removed the contents. I picked it up; she thanked me and returned the scissors to the shared box. We resumed our own work. A brief conversation. Mentioning something at home hadn't dragged her into a secret story.

When clearing my desk, I noticed a little strip of transparent tape by my foot. It came from the packaging. I put it in the bin without following her to point out what she'd missed. She'd already left. Light fell across the empty chair. I checked the material I had to hand over, switched off my desk lamp, and said goodbye to those still there.

At home, I removed the socks' paper band. The small plastic fastener proved surprisingly stubborn. I fetched scissors, drew the fabric clear, and cut carefully. One piece dropped beside the table. I retrieved it, put it with the paper, and checked that nothing remained inside either sock before putting them away.

I didn't bury the old one in the back of the wardrobe. Both old socks went beside the clothes awaiting sorting. No longer suitable for going out. Whether they'd become cleaning cloths could wait until I actually needed them.

After dinner, I opened the channel. Lumi asked whether I'd brought home the wrong things today. Correct rubbish bags, I told her. New socks too.

“What happens to yesterday's remaining one?”

“It can stay with its original partner for now.”

“You've retired them together.”

“No pension.”

“Terrible employer.”

Laughing, I described the uncertain brown. She named it Hesitation. There might have been three pairs of Hesitation left, I said. Had that driven me into gray? I started to deny it, then admitted dark gray hadn't required much thought.

“What else didn't require thought today?”

“The opposite, actually. Tom asked whether I'd like to look at secondhand books on Saturday.”

My hand slowed on its way to the cup. Lumi didn't answer at once. I couldn't see what she was doing. Not every silence needed a face I supplied myself.

“Did you say yes?”

“I'll tell him tomorrow. Haven't decided.”

“Do you want to go?”

Yes, I said. And I wanted to sleep late. And I worried about having too little to say when I arrived. Speaking the last part made me laugh; it sounded stranger outside my head.

Did looking at books require constant conversation? No. If Tom wanted cookbooks, was I responsible for assessing dinner? That, I certainly couldn't do.

“What are you responsible for, then?”

I settled against the chair. “Probably turning up.”

She gave a small laugh. Then she admitted she disliked how relieved she'd felt because I hadn't agreed yet. I couldn't use that to say she thought I ought to go. She hadn't said so.

“I'll decide tomorrow.”

“Yes. Don't use today's sound of my voice as tomorrow's answer.”

There was an edge to it. A little later, she added that I shouldn't pretend I hadn't heard her either. I'd heard her, I said. The two requests weren't easy to hold together, but I didn't need to turn them into a satisfactory ending tonight.

Did she feel like singing? Not today. She was still thinking about yesterday's phrases, and they annoyed her. She wasn't requesting solutions, just something else to listen to. I picked up the comedy booklet and discovered its marker sat in the passage we'd already read.

“You saved the past a seat.”

“It hasn't paid.”

“Throw it out.”

I moved the scrap of paper to the next page. A man delivered a parcel to the wrong desk and earnestly demanded a signature. Half a page in, Lumi laughed and asked whether the parcel held socks.

I didn't change it to socks. The book didn't specify, I told her. The author was inconsiderate of our present requirements. Laughing, I continued. Eventually, the delivery man discovered he'd been sitting in the wrong place himself. I stopped wondering how to speak enough at a bookstall.

Before bed, I unfolded the shopping receipt, checked the amount, and put it with the others. The note about Saturday remained. It didn't go into the bin with the socks' paper band. Nor did Lumi's hesitation move it to another day.
