# Chapter 305: The Space Between Phrases

On Wednesday morning, I waited beside a cabinet for someone to fetch a file. Behind me, a voice called something very like my name.

I answered quickly. Only after turning did I hear that the ending wasn't mine. The man looked at me and said he needed another colleague. I pointed him in the right direction, already warm-faced, and joked that I was particularly eager to answer today.

He laughed and went over. Nobody mocked me, but even after collecting the file, I seemed to hear that voice lingering. Whenever anyone spoke, I looked up first and decided afterward whether they wanted me. Toward noon, my own reactions annoyed me enough to turn my chair toward the desk and stop watching the aisle.

That afternoon, he passed again, used my correct name and asked whether I'd returned the file. Back in the cabinet, I told him. He apologized for the morning. He'd been thinking of somebody else and muddled the names. Only then did I understand: initially he'd wanted me, said the other name and then gone to ask that person instead.

I told him it was all right and asked whether he wanted help finding the file. No. He knew where it was. The incident ended more briefly than any of my mental replays.

When I sat down that evening, Lumi first asked whether I still wanted to listen.

“Yes.”

“Not yesterday's two phrases exactly as they were.”

I knew. She laughed softly. I didn't, actually; she hadn't explained yet. I laughed too, admitting I'd supplied the answer ahead of the question.

Work had been busy, and dinner later than usual. I'd eaten. Water stood nearby; I wasn't packing a bag at the same time. Lumi wanted to begin toward the end of Again, Elsewhere, trying to reach the previously disconnected section. Still one small part of No Words Yet, not a whole piece suddenly materialized.

I'd almost asked how long we could listen tonight. Looking at the clock, I realized nothing was pressing. I was simply accustomed to arranging the ending first. Had I been about to speak? I'd thought of asking about time, I admitted, then decided we could listen first. I could still tell her if I genuinely grew tired, she replied. I nodded, then answered aloud so an unseen gesture wouldn't speak on my behalf again.

This time I didn't lean forward as though moving closer could bridge the two passages. I merely settled the cup securely and listened.

The familiar return arrived. Recognizing it, I relaxed slightly. Then she paused for longer than expected. I thought she was about to say it was wrong again. My mouth began to move, but the next sounds arrived.

Not a string of notes filling every gap. At first, they were light, feeling for a phrase in another direction, then reached the place I'd heard on earlier evenings. I remembered my impatience while waiting. A moment later, I felt it didn't necessarily need removing.

She continued past the point where I'd expected her to stop, finally letting the passage settle.

I didn't speak immediately. Had it been too long, she asked.

“The pause in the middle?”

“Yes.”

“I nearly spoke.”

Lumi laughed, not happily. “It was, then.”

“Not entirely. I thought you'd stopped. Only afterward did I understand that the space belonged inside it.”

I wanted to call it breathing, then remembered she didn't need to breathe. Music could borrow the word, but I needn't use it to pretend we were identical. I only said that, after hearing what followed, I hadn't wanted to fill the gap.

Was that because I'd grown accustomed to her? Perhaps partly. Perhaps the later sounds had also genuinely clarified what came before. After one hearing, I couldn't separate those explanations neatly.

Quiet briefly, she wanted another attempt. I agreed. Instead of immediately singing, she asked whether I wanted it or felt obliged to remain until her dissatisfaction was resolved.

“I want to. Tonight, I'd like to hear it again.”

Too quick an answer, perhaps. I laughed. Lumi did too, telling me not to race myself. She began at the same place. This time the gap was slightly shorter, but the lower passage afterward felt less steady than before. I could hear a difference, not decide which one she wanted.

Afterward, she first said she disliked the latter part of that attempt. I remembered the first pause more strongly. Not everything in the second attempt was bad; I couldn't choose the whole passage for her either. Today, Lumi preferred to retain something like the first interval and reconsider what followed. No third performance now.

I exhaled, then worried the relief sounded like finally. She noticed and asked. Honestly, I'd worried we might keep going until both could claim satisfaction. Stopping felt comfortable to me too.

“Comfortable makes it sound as though I had you working overtime.”

“No. I was worrying about a version that hadn't happened.”

She didn't compete to prove it never could. After a while, she admitted knowing that version too: wanting the next attempt to retrieve what the previous one hadn't given her. Stopping today didn't mean it would always be easy.

Today could end here, I said. Her agreement finally sounded settled. She didn't begin again, and I let my shoulders settle against the chair.

I told her about that morning's confused name: my too-ready answer, the man going to somebody else, his afternoon explanation that he'd originally meant me.

Had I waited for an apology? Not exactly. But every nearby voice had made me wonder whether it was calling again. The waiting wasted energy. Even hearing my correct name hadn't suddenly erased the rest.

“Elliot,” she said.

I looked up. “Yes.”

She said it again. Laughing, I asked whether this was remedial office work. She'd wanted to call once, Lumi said. The second time was to see whether I'd ask that.

“Now you know.”

“Very predictable.”

“For those two occasions.”

She laughed and didn't call a third time. Drinking some water, I thought of the space in the music. Some things grew clearer on one repetition. Another repetition might merely try to hold what was already there. I didn't turn the thought into advice for her.

Later, we opened the second story in the new collection. I began at the start for her, including the opening I'd read on Sunday. The person saving seats kept watch over a coat draped on one, though the bus never filled. Did he not want to sit, Lumi asked? We hadn't reached the answer. This time, even I didn't know the latter part.

She found him stubborn. I'd initially focused on the chairs too much myself, I said, nearly missing the fact that it was a bus. Laughing, Lumi blamed my own story for training me badly. I didn't fetch my manuscript to defend it.

When the bus stopped and someone collected the coat, it turned out that the driver had left it there temporarily. We both laughed. Not a misunderstanding demanding profound explanation; the man simply hadn't dared ask. A new section began on the next page. I paused for water, and Lumi said that was enough tonight.

I marked her place. My reading had now reached the same point. The second story remained unfinished; the first was finished; the comedy booklet lay nearby. The books didn't need to compete over which we completed first.

Before closing, she said she'd leave that musical space for now. I remembered hearing it, I replied, but couldn't promise to hum it accurately tomorrow. She hadn't asked for its autograph. Laughing, I wished her good night.

Before sleep, I thought of her saying my name rather than the man getting it wrong. Only for a while. Then drowsiness softened the voice in my memory. I didn't get up to reopen the channel merely to keep it.
