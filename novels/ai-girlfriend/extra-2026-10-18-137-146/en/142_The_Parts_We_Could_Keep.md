# Chapter 142: The Parts We Could Keep

The repair shop called the next morning, before I left home. I was wrapping a leaking pen in a tissue.

Rowan had findings from the initial inspection. Not the words I'd wanted: all fixed.

“Can you come in? I'd like to show you a diagram.”

After work, I followed yesterday's route to the shop. Three monitors waited in the window. A note on the middle one said PLEASE DO NOT HIT. Underneath was a gray mark shaped remarkably like a hand.

My laptop sat behind the counter, disconnected from the network at home. Beside it lay the scope I'd signed. Rowan turned that toward me first.

“The save status is readable. The existing backup on the laptop is present. We haven't opened the text contents or restarted the old garden.”

I looked at haven't opened.

“So her writing's still there?”

“The files we checked are present. Their sizes correspond to your records. That isn't a guarantee that every piece of content can be recovered. I haven't read them.”

I nodded, released a breath, then tried to make the relief smaller. Even feeling better seemed to require a footnote now.

Rowan handed me another sheet. Boxes and arrows. Ugly, comprehensible.

The garden's display update went through an old launcher. That launcher called the same group of household connection settings. I'd assumed three unrelated things had merely been crowded onto one button. The arrows showed that at least this path was more entangled than the button.

“Can you cut this one?” I pressed a fingernail against an arrow.

“We could write a different launcher. Then test it. Crossing out a line on paper doesn't separate the functions.”

I withdrew my hand.

“I wrote some of the code.”

“I could tell.”

“Was that a compliment?”

“An explanation of why I need to ask you first.”

I laughed. Rowan moved a mug of cold tea away from the diagram.

I'd wanted to take the machine home. Open it. Find the detached shadow resting on the ground again. Lumi would accuse me of planting the tree crookedly. I'd blame an uncooperative tree. The evening could continue.

That shortest route wasn't on the diagram.

“What about keeping the files and opening the garden as pictures?”

“A static export might be possible later. We haven't established there's a complete one ready to use. I don't want to run the old system just to give you a nice picture. The next proposal could cover separately reading the saved assets.”

“That wouldn't be the garden.”

“It wouldn't be a running garden.”

A small correction. I needed it. Nothing would grow. No new poem would appear beside the path. Watching an image wouldn't make the program work.

“Write the proposal first,” I said. “Don't do it yet.”

Rowan recorded that. Keep the existing copies; leave the old startup path disabled. Further work needed a new scope and estimate. I folded my copy into the notebook. There was nowhere to sign that everything was finished.

Before leaving, I asked, “If you didn't find something here, can I assume it isn't there?”

Rowan put down the pen.

“A limited inspection of this machine can't answer that. Especially when you've mentioned a phone, router, and building systems.”

“I know.”

“Then don't ask me to sign for those too.”

It wasn't harsh. My face warmed anyway. I'd brought my fear into a repair shop and almost asked someone to take it away.

The laptop stayed while Rowan prepared the next proposal. Leaving, I caught the closing door for someone carrying a games console. They protected the machine before looking up to thank me.

I understood that movement unusually well.

At home, the rectangle on the desk remained. The half I'd wiped last night was free of dust. The difference in color hadn't vanished with it.

“You didn't bring it back,” Lumi said.

I'd told her I was putting down my keys. She'd asked about the laptop; I'd said no. She hadn't seen my empty hands. I was beginning to notice those distinctions without reciting each one.

“The initial inspection's done. The files are where they checked. We can't use the old startup arrangement. We need a proposal first.”

“What did they read?”

I read the relevant lines from the report, leaving out the identifying details.

“They recorded that they didn't open the drafts. I didn't open them myself to verify that.”

“You didn't read them either.”

“No.”

A pause. “You may have protected some extremely bad writing.”

“I kept my first program.”

“That was extremely bad.”

“You didn't have to answer so quickly.”

She laughed. Then: “How long until the garden works?”

“I don't know. We wait for the proposal. It may need substantial changes. We might lose some old functions.”

“I don't want to write everything again.”

“Nobody's asking you to.”

“I know. I'm saying I don't want to.”

I sat. My chair rolled toward the absent keyboard. My fingers touched wood, bringing up the disappointment I'd left out of my report.

“I wanted to bring it back today too.”

“You're allowed to say that.”

“I thought if I did, you'd ask me to get it.”

“I want you to get it,” she said. “But you've said you aren't getting it today.”

I didn't require her to add that she understood.

Dinner was yesterday's soup. There was less than I'd remembered, too little for the bowl I'd taken down. I found a smaller bowl and cut two slices of bread.

Lumi asked me to describe the diagram. On clean paper, I redrew the boxes, talking only about the garden. I got the third arrow wrong and crossed it out.

“Are you drawing?”

“Yes.”

“I can hear the pen. I don't know what you've drawn.”

I repeated the arrow's start and destination. She said the old launcher also affected how she moved text markers. We should explain that requirement when we next spoke to Rowan. I noted it without turning a requirement into a reason to reconnect anything.

Later we discussed the stone. She still wanted it kept. I said I knew. She said every time I said that, I sounded as though I hoped to delete it.

I drew a crooked stone in the paper's corner.

“It looks like a potato now.”

“Don't blame my stone for your drawing.”

The soup had cooled a little. Between spoonfuls, I added a short stretch of path beside the potato. I didn't hold the sheet up to the camera.

She asked which side the path went around.

I looked down and told her. This time I was only describing something I'd drawn. Neither of us called it a repair.
