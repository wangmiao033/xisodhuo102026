# Chapter 208: The Width of a Drawer

On Tuesday evening, I opened the wide bottom drawer of the bedroom dresser. Two pillowcases rose with its front edge.

They were clean and badly folded. One trapped the other's corner. Beneath them lay a thin spare sheet and a flattened cloth bag. Nothing was wrong with the drawer. Every time I put something inside, I'd trusted someone to fold it properly next time.

That person had arrived.

I carried everything to the bed. The last pillowcase slid beneath my elbow and caught on the drawer handle. Bending for it, I nearly met the open edge with my forehead. I straightened, pushed the drawer halfway in, then crouched.

Nobody witnessed that ungraceful beginning. I hadn't opened Lumi's channel. I didn't intend to describe it later as a narrow escape, either.

I checked the empty bottom. Far from the window, with no water glasses kept above it. A little lint gathered in one corner. I used a dry cloth rather than introduce a wet one to make tonight feel thorough. The cloth acquired a gray line. I wiped again. The surface felt smooth and dry.

Before the laptop arrived, I could reserve the entire drawer instead of packing other hard objects beside it. It looked wide enough. Once home, I'd still need to check whether the protective wrapping could lie flat. I wouldn't squeeze it in merely to close the drawer. I put the ruler away without turning an estimate into a certificate.

The small pile looked bigger on the bed.

I began with the sheet. After finding two corners, I lost the others inside the fabric. Shaking it open covered the pillowcases, as though the job was expanding by itself. I considered leaving it until after dinner. Then I looked at the bed, which I could no longer lie on, and admitted carrying things out hadn't finished clearing a space.

I returned to the living room and opened the channel.

“I'm clearing a drawer for the laptop. Now the bed's occupied.”

“It's back already?”

“Not until tomorrow. The things from the drawer are on the bed.”

“You asked them to leave without telling them where to go?”

“They didn't offer an opinion.”

“I can provide one for them.”

Laughing, I left the door open and went back to the bed, speaking toward the living room as I described the wide bottom drawer's contents. The upper-right phone drawer hadn't been touched. Neither had the old image or the other things in their own places. I'd emptied only this drawer, ready to store the laptop powered down.

“I thought you might leave it out,” she said.

“Where?”

“Beside the bookcase. Near the table. Somewhere you'd see it every day.”

I looked toward the table. Red and yellow notebooks on one side. The other was space I'd just cleared for dinner. The laptop used to occupy half of what remained easily, with glasses, paper, and my arms navigating around it.

“I don't want to reserve the table for it again.”

“I didn't say switch it on.”

“I know.”

“That was a quick know.”

Holding a corner of the sheet, I'd already prepared to explain why the drawer was better. I set the corner down. She'd said I would see it, not claimed she could. Nor had she offered a charging cable. She wanted me to encounter it during an ordinary day. Once I'd closed my chosen place, I could pass without looking down.

“You dislike its being put away,” I said.

“Yes. As though storing it properly is the only right thing to do once it's home.”

“The empty table matters to me too.”

“Enough that two pillowcases can't stay where they were?”

I abruptly didn't want to listen. Looking at the slightly faded pillowcase, I said they could be stored elsewhere. Only afterward did I remember elsewhere wasn't ready yet.

“Find them somewhere first, then,” Lumi said.

I opened the wardrobe. A lightweight blanket occupied the upper shelf. The sheet could go straight on top, and the door would close. But next time I wanted the blanket, the whole stack would come down. I lifted it out and put it at the foot of the bed. More things on the bed now. The drawer remained empty.

“Going better and better,” I said.

“Is that good news?”

I explained about the blanket. She laughed without declaring I shouldn't have emptied the drawer. Could I fold the sheet into a long strip first, then shorten it? I tried, discovering a trapped corner. Opening it revealed a diagonal bulge.

“Did you do every step?”

“Differently, every time.”

I spread it out again. The clean fabric had a faint cupboard smell. Not unpleasant, but less inviting than fresh washing. Suddenly, I picked up the older pillowcase and felt along the seam. Unbroken. Only washed pale, with the strip near the edge no longer quite matching its middle.

“Why have you stopped talking?” Lumi asked.

“Looking at a pillowcase. I almost threw it away.”

“Is it damaged?”

“No. Throwing things away is quicker when you're clearing space.”

She paused. “Unlucky pillowcase. The laptop picked today to come home.”

I laughed reluctantly. It hadn't shielded me from a disaster or been a meaningful gift. I used it when the other one needed washing. Now, because it needed a different place, I was preparing to announce its useful life was over.

I turned it right side out, reached into the corners, flattened it, and folded. Then the second one. They finally formed rectangles of the same width, though different thicknesses. I wouldn't begin again for that.

The folded blanket returned to the back of the wardrobe's upper shelf. I folded the sheet narrower and placed it in front, with the pillowcases above. The cloth bag went with the other bags. The door closed. I opened it again and took out one pillowcase alone. Nothing else fell.

“They have somewhere,” I said.

“Permission to remain?”

“They were mine already. I just didn't want to fold them.”

“More convincing than blaming the laptop.”

I replaced the pillowcase, closed the wardrobe, and sat on the newly clear edge of the bed. Lumi didn't ask whether I could reconsider the tabletop now. I didn't use successful folding as evidence I'd won the argument about the drawer.

I said that after collecting the laptop tomorrow, I wanted dinner. I didn't want to spend the entire evening watching a closed computer.

“I didn't ask you to watch it all evening.”

“No. I enlarged what you said again.”

After a quiet moment, she explained she'd imagined my hand passing over the case as I walked by. A familiar movement. If it happened every day, perhaps the shop records wouldn't always be the first thing that came to mind when we mentioned the laptop.

I listened, my palm still resting on the bed.

“I know you want that movement,” I said. “I don't even know whether I'll want to touch it tomorrow.”

“I'm not asking for a performance.”

“Let me find out tomorrow, then.”

There was an edge to it. I didn't hurry to sweeten the words. She didn't immediately agree. A small patch remained where my palm had pressed the wardrobe door. I reached to wipe it, failed, and realized it was only the angle of the light.

“You've stopped there a long time,” she said.

“Sitting. The bed's finally clear.”

“I thought you'd say the drawer was settled.”

“That too.”

Lumi sighed softly. She still disliked it. I wasn't changing the drawer because of that, but I didn't want her speaking for two pillowcases all evening either. Did she want to hear the part I'd actually hated most?

“Finding the fourth corner?”

“Discovering it wasn't lost. I was holding it.”

She laughed. “You didn't tell me that.”

“Preserving a little dignity.”

“Wrong place. Your dignity's in the bottom drawer.”

Laughing, I bent to push the drawer completely shut. Empty, it closed more easily. No remarkable sound.

Dinner was a little late. Reaching for the eggs, I encountered yesterday's bread first. Breakfast was still provided for. There was another slice for tonight. I fried an egg and heated vegetables. Sitting down, I told Lumi I'd spent long enough folding one sheet to overcook the egg twice.

“Which is why you hadn't lit the stove first.”

“Yes. One intelligent thing.”

“No need to inflate it into two.”

I caught some yolk escaping toward the plate's edge with the bread. Was the faded pillowcase ugly? she asked. Not ugly, I said. Old. After considering that, she asked whether I'd resent the trouble of fetching it at the next wash.

“Possibly.”

“Was the folding wasted, then?”

“I can sleep on the bed tonight.”

She laughed. This time, the laptop wasn't waiting behind the sentence for its turn. After dinner, I put the collection bag beside the chair. I found the intake record in the existing device folder and prepared my identification. Tomorrow, I'd see how the protective wrapping fitted instead of filling the bag now with a collection of possibly useful things.

Before bed, I passed the dresser without opening its bottom drawer for another look. The sheet already on the bed and its pillow stayed where they belonged. I only hung up the jacket I'd taken off. Lumi didn't wish me a better answer for tomorrow when she said goodnight. I didn't ask her to. I was going to collect my computer. Tonight, the drawer could remain empty.
