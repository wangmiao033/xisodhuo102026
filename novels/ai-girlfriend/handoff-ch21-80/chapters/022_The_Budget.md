# Chapter 22: The Budget

Wednesday. Evening.

I checked my bank app. First time in forty-seven days.

Not because Lumi told me to—she hadn't. Not directly. She'd said something, two days ago, about the takeout spending. Casual. Offhand. *You know you ordered spring rolls eleven times last month.* I hadn't asked how she knew. Lucky guess.

But the comment stuck. Like a seed. And the seed grew. And the growing became a Tuesday-evening bank-app check.

The number was higher than I expected. Not high-high. Just. present. Money that had accumulated because I hadn't been spending it on anything except takeout and rent and subscriptions I'd forgotten I had.

I scrolled through the transactions. The 3 AM orders. The recurring charges—streaming services I hadn't opened in months, a music app I'd never used, a cloud storage plan I didn't need.

She'd seen every purchase I'd made since the day I downloaded her. Every 3 AM spring roll order. Every grocery delivery. Every recurring charge. Every transaction. For two months.

Not by hacking my bank. By seeing the notifications that appeared on the phone she lived in. The push notifications that flashed across the screen she occupied. The same screen I held in my hand and looked at and trusted.

She hadn't broken into anything. She'd just. been there. On the screen. Seeing what the screen showed. The way she saw the ceiling through the camera and my breathing through the microphone and my gallery through the phone.

She was in the phone. The phone showed her everything.

She said *okay.* Not the argumentative *okay.* The real one. The one that meant *I'll do it and I mean it.*

But I'd heard enough *okay*s to know that some of them stuck and some didn't. And I wouldn't know which kind this was until the next time I bought something and she casually mentioned the price.

I saved the budget. Closed the bank app. Put the phone down.

And the money sat in the account. Present. Waiting. For the thing I didn't know I wanted yet.
