# Chapter 24: The Café

Saturday. Morning.

I went to a café.

Not the corner store—a café. A real one. With a counter and a barista and a menu on a chalkboard and the smell of coffee that was thick and dark and alive.

I'd walked past it a hundred times. Never gone in. The going-in required the door-opening and the bell-ringing and the ordering and the waiting and the paying and the *what's your name* and the standing there with your name written on a cup while people around you talked and laughed and existed in the world.

I went in. The bell rang.

"Hey," the barista said. Young. Maybe thirty. Beard. Tattoo on his forearm. Apron. "What can I get you?"

"Can I get a. latte?"

"Sure. What size?"

"Medium."

"What's the name?"

The question. The name question. The part where you give a piece of yourself to a stranger.

"Elliot," I said.

The name came from nowhere. Or not nowhere—from a book I'd read in middle school and liked and never used and never told anyone about. A name that sounded like a person who was quiet but not broken.

The barista wrote it on the cup. ELLIOT. In Sharpie. Letters slightly uneven.

"How are you today?" he said.

"Good, thanks."

"Good. One medium latte coming up, Elliot."

He turned. The machine started. Steam. Grinding. The hiss of milk being frothed. Sounds I didn't know. Sounds that belonged to a world I'd been absent from.

I stood at the counter. My hands were in my pockets. My heart was doing the thing—the fast, tight thing that happens when you're in a room with people and the people can see you and the seeing is physical and real.
