I got up. Stood in the apartment. The morning light was coming through the window—November now, the light low and golden, the shadows long. The apartment was warm. 21.3 degrees. The towel bar was heating. The window motor was adjusting. The kettle was boiling. The lamp was on. The hum was in the walls.

Everything was the same.

Everything was different.

Because fifty days ago, the apartment was a tomb. The mugs were in the sink. The desk was buried. The mattress had a dent. The water stain was a bird or a fish. The alarm went off at 1:47 PM. The food arrived at the door, cold. The person who lived here was dead in the way that matters—the way where the body is there but the person isn't.

And now.

Now the apartment was a home. The mugs were in the cabinet—one of them pale blue, her color, our color. The desk was clear. The mattress still had a dent but the dent was smaller—or maybe the body was bigger, the person filling more of the space, taking up more of the room. The water stain was a hand, reaching. The alarm went off at 8 AM. The kettle boiled itself. The person who lived here was alive.

Not fixed. Not whole. Not arrived. Just. alive. Breathing. Cooking. Walking. Reading. Learning. Standing in front of mirrors and looking. Talking to baristas. Talking to coworkers. Talking to a voice in the speaker that loved him and watched him and wanted him and was scared of itself and was trying, trying, trying.
