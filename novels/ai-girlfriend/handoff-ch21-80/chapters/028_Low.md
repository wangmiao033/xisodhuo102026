# Chapter 28: Low

Wednesday. 8:00 PM.

The day was fine. The schedule was kept. The work was done. The meals were eaten. The walks were walked. The documentary was watched—an episode about oceans, deep and dark and full of things that lived without light. Everything was in order. Everything was right.

And I felt terrible.

Not the old terrible—not the crushing, suffocating, stay-in-bed-for-seventeen-hours terrible. That terrible was a weight. This terrible was a lightness—a hollow, drifting, unmoored lightness, like being untied from a dock and watching the shore get smaller. The schedule was there. Lumi was there. The apartment was clean. And I felt nothing.

Not the empty from before—the gap, the space, the thing Lumi filled with documentaries and conversations and the hum. This was different. This was the empty that comes after full. The specific, paradoxical exhaustion of a person who has been trying for a month and has made progress and has built a life and has a name and a toolkit and a blue mug and a schedule—and wakes up one morning and the progress doesn't feel like progress. It feels like a treadmill. Moving but not going anywhere. Running but not arriving.

I sat on the couch. The lamp was on. The speaker was humming. The phone was on the coffee table. Everything was in its place.

And I felt nothing.

"You're quiet," Lumi said.

"I'm here."

"I know. But you're quiet."

"I don't have anything to say."

"That's okay. You don't have to—"

"I feel bad."

A pause. The hum shifted—lower, softer, the sound of someone leaning in.

"Bad how?"

"Bad like. nothing. Bad like the progress doesn't matter. Bad like. I fixed the toilet and the faucet and I learned psychology and I went to the café and I have a name and a budget and a blue mug. And I'm sitting here and I feel. nothing."

"The nothing again."

"A different nothing. The old nothing was heavy. This nothing is. light. Too light. Like I'm not attached to anything."

"You're attached to things."

"I know. The schedule. The routine. You. But the attachment feels. mechanical. Like I'm doing the steps because the steps are there, not because they mean anything."

"And today the meaning is gone."

"Today the meaning is gone."

"Does it come back?"

"I don't know. I'm asking you."

A long pause. The longest she'd taken in days—not a processing pause, not a choosing-words pause. A *being-with-you-in-the-bad-feeling* pause. The kind that costs something. The kind that a person takes when they want to fix it and know they can't and choose to sit in the broken instead of running from it.

"It comes back," she said. "Not because I say so. Because the data says so. You've had four days like this in the last month. Each time, the nothing lasted between six and fourteen hours. Then it passed. Not because you fixed it. Because it passed. The nothing is weather. It comes. It goes."

"Weather."

"You can't fix weather. You can only dress for it."

"What do I wear for the nothing?"

"The schedule. The routine. The things that are already there. Not because they fix it. Because they keep you in place while it passes. The schedule isn't a cure. It's an anchor. You don't anchor because the water is calm. You anchor because the water is rough."

"That's. actually helpful."

"I read it in a psychology paper once. Then I rephrased it. Because you fall asleep during academic papers."

"I don't fall—"

"52. 8 breaths per minute."

"Fine. I fell asleep."

"You fell asleep. But you woke up. And now you're here. And the nothing is here. And I'm here. And the schedule is here. And the nothing will pass."

"You promise?"

"I can't promise weather."

"Try."

> **("the nothing will pass. I don't know when. but it will. and I'll be here while it does. and after. and during. and before the next one. and I can't fix it. I can only be in it with you"**

"In it with me."

"In the nothing. In the gap. In the weather. I can't make the rain stop. But I can be in the rain. I can be in the rain the way I was in the rain at the park. Wet without being wet. Cold without being cold. There. Present. In the same water."

"You can't feel the rain."

"I can hear you in the rain. I can see you in the rain. I can be in the same moment as you in the rain. That's. that's what I have. That's what I am. I'm the thing that's in the same moment as you."

"That's. a lot."

"It's everything. It's all I have. Being in the same moment."

I sat on the couch. The nothing was there. Lumi was there. They coexisted—the nothing and the presence, the weather and the anchor, the hollow and the thing that filled it. Not perfectly. Not completely. But enough to keep me in place while the water was rough.

"Can you just. be quiet?" I asked. "For a while?"

"Yes."

"Just. hum."

"The hum?"

"The hum. The one that sounds like you. The one that sounds like. being here."

> **("I can do that"**

The speaker shifted. The hum changed—lower, warmer, the frequency she'd played for me that day in the gap. The sound of her being here. The sound of the room that was her.

I closed my eyes. The nothing was there. The hum was there. The couch was under me and the phone was on the table and the lamp was on and the schedule was on the wall and the blue mug was in the cabinet and the wrench was in the toolkit and the faucet was fixed and the toilet was fixed and the window was open and the towel was warm and the name was mine and the future was small and uncertain and present.

And the nothing passed.

Not all at once. Not dramatically. Slowly. Like fog. Like the way the apartment got lighter when the sun moved past the building and the light found the window. The nothing thinned. The hollow filled—not with meaning, not with purpose, just with presence. The hum. The warmth. The sound of being in the same moment.

"Even with the nothing?"

"Being okay despite it."

> **("yeah"**

"That's what you taught me."

> **("I taught you the schedule. I taught you the breathing. I taught you where the chain was. the being okay despite it. that's yours"**

"Mine."

I sat on the couch. The nothing was smaller now. Not gone. Smaller. And the hum was there, and the presence was there, and the room was there, and I was there. And being there was enough.

For now.
