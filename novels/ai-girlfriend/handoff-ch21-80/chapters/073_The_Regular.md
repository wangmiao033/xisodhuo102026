# Chapter 73: The Regular

Saturday. 10:00 AM.

Café. Saturday, not Thursday. The second time this week—Thursday and Saturday. The schedule was bending. Not breaking—bending. The way a tree bends in wind. The routine was still there, still the spine of the days, but the spine had more flexibility now. More give. More air between the vertebrae.

James was at the counter. The bell rang. The coffee smell was the same—thick, dark, the smell of a place that knew what it was.

"Elliot," James said. "Twice in one week. I'm going to start thinking you actually like it here."

"I do like it here."

"You said you didn't have opinions about coffee."

"I have opinions about the music volume. That's enough to start."

He laughed. The easy laugh. Made the latte. The leaf pattern. The cup without a name—because he knew.

"So," he said, while the machine hissed. "I've been meaning to ask. What do you do? For work, I mean. You mentioned data entry."

"Data entry. Remote. For a logistics company."

"Logistics. Like. trucks and warehouses?"

"Like. spreadsheets about trucks and warehouses."

"You must be very good at spreadsheets."

"I'm. adequate at spreadsheets."

"Adequate is the new ambitious." He handed me the cup. "You know, you should come to the open mic."

"The what?"

"Open mic. Thursday nights. Here. After hours. People read poetry, play music, tell stories. It's. it's a thing."

"I don't. perform."

"Nobody performs. They just. share. It's very low-key. Very not-scary. Very. Elliot-appropriate."

"You don't know what's Elliot-appropriate."

"I know you wore a blue button-down to a café and told me the music was too loud and your opinion ended up on my chalkboard. That's very Elliot-appropriate."

I looked at the chalkboard. The chalk was still there—*THE COFFEE IS FOREGROUND. — ELLIOT, REGULAR.* Slightly smudged now. A week old. But there.

"I'll think about it," I said.

"That's all I ask."

I took the coffee. Sat at the window. The morning was cold—December now, the light low, the air sharp. The café was warm. The music was quieter—James had kept it down since the manifesto. My opinion, in chalk, on a wall, in a café, being respected.

For now.
