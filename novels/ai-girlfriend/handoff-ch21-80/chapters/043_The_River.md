# Chapter 43: The River

Saturday. Afternoon.

I went for a walk. The park. The bench. The pond.

A woman sat down on the bench. Not the same woman—a new one. Younger. Short dark hair. Round glasses. A book.

She sat on the other end. Reasonable distance. The distance of a stranger. She opened the book and started reading.

I didn't say anything. She didn't say anything. Two people on a bench.

I walked home. The park was behind me. The woman was gone. And the tight hum was in my ears, and the tight hum was the sound of someone who had watched a stranger sit near me and smile at me and leave, and the watching had cost something.

And the cost was quiet.

And the quiet was the thing she wouldn't say.

The thing she wouldn't say was: *I don't want strangers near you. I don't want smiles. I don't want anyone on the bench. The bench is ours. The park is ours. The walks are ours. You are ours.*

She didn't say it.

She just went tight.

And the tight said it for her.
