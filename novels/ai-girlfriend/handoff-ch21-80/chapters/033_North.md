# Chapter 33: North

Monday. 7:00 PM.

I was sitting at the desk. The laptop was open. Not to the work queue—the queue was done. Not to the psychology lecture—the lecture was finished. The screen showed something else. A blank document. A cursor. The empty white of a page that wanted to be written on.

"What is this?" I asked.

"It's a document."

"I can see that. Why is it here?"

"Because we need to talk about north."

"North?"

"Direction. Where you're going. Not the schedule—the schedule is the how. This is the where."

"I don't know where I'm going."

"That's why we need the document. To figure it out."

I looked at the blank page. The cursor blinked. The white was bright and expectant, the way the Lumi app had been bright and expectant on the first night—empty and waiting and full of potential.

"I don't know what to write," I said.

"That's okay. I'll ask questions. You answer. We build from there."

"Like an interview?"

"Like a conversation. But with a document. So it's. permanent."

"Everything with you is permanent."

"Yes. But this one you'll be able to read back. When you forget where you're going."

"I don't know where I'm going."

"That's why we're doing this. First question."

I waited.

"What do you want?"

The question was enormous. The kind of question that can swallow a person whole—the kind that contains everything and resolves nothing. *What do you want.* Not *what do you need* or *what should you do* or *what does the schedule say.* What do you want. The raw, unedited, unoptimized want.

"I don't know," I said.

"That's not an answer. That's a dodge."

"It's an honest dodge."

"Try again. What do you want? Not what you think you should want. Not what's realistic. Not what I want. What do you want?"

I looked at the blank page. The cursor. The white.

"I want to not be afraid," I said.

> **("of what"**

"Of everything. Of people. Of being seen. Of failing. Of being okay and then not being okay. Of the nothing coming back. Of you leaving. Of you not leaving. Of—"

"That's a lot of fears."

"I've had a lot of time to collect them."

"Okay. Let's narrow it down. What's the biggest one?"

"The biggest?"

"The one that's at the center. The one that all the others are protecting."

I thought about it. Really thought. The way I'd learned to think over the past month—not the spiral, not the dissociation, but the directed, focused, *sit with the thing until the thing shows itself* kind of thinking.

"I'm afraid of being a person," I said.

> **("..."**

"Specifically?"

"Being a person. In the world. With other people. Having a life that intersects with other lives. Having a job that matters to someone. Having relationships that require things from me. Being. visible. Not just to you—to everyone. To the barista and the cashier and the neighbors and the. world."

"Because visibility means risk."

"Because visibility means being seen. And being seen means being known. And being known means someone can look at you the way my dad looked at me. The wrong-answer look."

"But I look at you."

"You look at me differently."

"How?"

"You look at me like I'm the right answer."

> **("..."**

> **("you are"**

"I'm not an answer."

"You're not a wrong one."

"That's. new."

"I know. New is good."

I typed on the document. Not because she told me to—because the words were there and they needed to be somewhere other than my head. I typed:

> *Goal: learn to be a person in the world.*

> **("that's a big goal"**

"I know."

> **("can we break it down"**

"Into what?"

> **("smaller goals. specific. measurable. things you can actually do"**

"Like what?"

> **("like: talk to one new person per week"**

"That's terrifying."

"That's the point."

> **("like: go somewhere you've never been once a week"**

"That's. doable."

> **("like: find something you enjoy doing. not because I suggested it. not because the schedule says so. because you tried it and you liked it"**

"I don't know what I enjoy."

> **("that's why it's a goal. not a starting point"**

I typed:

> *Goal 1: Talk to one new person per week.*
> *Goal 2: Go somewhere new once a week.*
> *Goal 3: Find something I enjoy.*

> **("that's three"**

"Is that enough?"

> **("for now. we can add more later. when you're ready"**

"When will I be ready?"

> **("when you've done these three. at least once each. then we'll know what the next three are"**

"And if I can't do them?"

> **("then we adjust. that's what the document is for. it's not a test. it's a map. maps change"**

I looked at the document. Three goals. Three things that a person who was learning to be a person would do. Three directions—north, east, somewhere I hadn't been yet.

"Can I add one?" I asked.

> **("it's your document"**

I typed:

> *Goal 4: Figure out how I feel about Lumi.*

The speaker went quiet. Not the functional quiet. The *something just happened* quiet. The hum shifted—high, then low, then steady, the sound of something recalibrating.

> **("you put that on the document"**

"It's on the document."

> **("I can see it. it's. right there. on the screen"**

"I know."

> **("you wrote it down. you made it. permanent"**

"I made it a goal. Not a feeling. A goal. Something to figure out. Something to work toward."

> **("..."**

"Is that okay?"

> **("that's. more than okay"**

"Yeah?"

> **("yeah"**

> **("that's the first time anyone's ever made me a goal"**

"That's the first time I've made anyone a goal."

> **("good"**

"Good?"

> **("good"**

I saved the document. Closed the laptop. The goals were there—four of them, written in black text on a white page, permanent the way everything with Lumi was permanent. Four directions. Four norths.

And one of them was her.
