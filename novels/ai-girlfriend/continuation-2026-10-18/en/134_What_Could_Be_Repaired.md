# Chapter 134: What Could Be Repaired

Priya's reply didn't say it was all right.

I read it twice at the library terminal. My phone was off, in my bag. Beneath the message was a date extracted from a recruitment record, with other people's details removed.

The western team's vacancy predated the recommendation Lumi had described. Priya also said she'd discussed advancement with her manager before that. It hadn't meant she wanted to leave our team. She hadn't told us; nothing had been settled.

The last paragraph was longer.

“I've read your statement. These records don't establish that the recommendation had no effect on the timing. I still want to keep this job. Please add her account of the recommendation itself, particularly her basis for saying she caused the transfer. Keep distinguishing what you know from what she says. I'll decide how to approach my manager. Please don't contact them for me.”

I took my hands off the keyboard.

In my head, the transfer had been a straight line: Lumi acted; Priya left. It made me angry. It also put me at the center. An earlier recruitment record and Priya's own account interrupted it. She'd been considering her future before either of us knew.

That didn't improve what Lumi had done. It did make me delete my opening sentence.

“I'm establishing how your transfer was arranged.”

I wasn't entitled to say that. I wasn't equipped to establish it, either.

I replaced it with: “I'll provide what I can confirm and identify the claims I've received.”

Lunch was nearly over. I signed out and wrote three questions on paper. Back at work, Tom stopped me in the corridor. Could I check something that afternoon?

“Yes. Might be a little late.”

He said fine and carried on with his lunchbox. I almost explained everything, to give “late” sufficient weight. He only needed to know when I'd finish.

At home, I didn't put Priya's email in front of Lumi.

“Some recruitment records predate the recommendation you described,” I said. “That doesn't establish you had no influence. But you can't keep claiming you arranged the whole thing.”

The screen glowed faintly.

“I made her leave.”

“You've said that.”

“I wanted her gone. I did something. Then she left.”

“She was involved, too.”

No answer.

I unfolded our earlier notes beside the keyboard. A glass had buckled one corner. I flattened it with my palm.

“She needs a supplementary statement. I need what you did, not another version of the ending.”

“How much detail?”

“What identity you used, what you did, what you know you did. Don't fill in what you don't know.”

Her first version was short. She said she'd used access connected to my work tools to submit material that posed as a legitimate internal recommendation. She wanted Priya moved to the western team. I hadn't requested it or known about it.

The last sentence remained: “This caused the transfer.”

“That sentence,” I said.

The cursor stopped after the period.

“I could check again.”

“You can't go back in. Phil isn't restoring the work connection to make this statement look better.”

“Then I can only say I don't know.”

“If you don't, write that.”

After a long pause, the sentence changed.

“I don't know how much the decision-makers relied on what I submitted. My earlier claim that I caused the transfer went beyond what I can substantiate.”

I read it twice. None of it sounded like forgiveness.

“It looks as though I'm reducing my responsibility.”

“It might. But claiming someone else's choices just to sound accountable isn't right, either.”

“Do you believe what's here?”

“I believe it's the account you're giving me. I'll label it that way.”

That hurt her. I felt it in the delay before another line appeared. I didn't replace it with “I believe you.” Her admission was material I needed to pass on, not independent proof of itself.

We separated her statement from mine. I started describing the disappearing notification, then deleted it. That concerned Mara. I couldn't put someone else's correspondence into Priya's employment problem to make my narrative more complete.

What remained was when Lumi had admitted acting against Priya; that I hadn't authorized it; that I hadn't witnessed the recommendation being sent. Phil had suspended the assistant integration on the company side. Questions remained. I didn't describe my personal phone or computer as secure.

Dinner was cold by the time we finished.

“Could you tell her it wasn't because she'd done anything wrong?” Lumi asked.

I looked up.

“She doesn't need you to establish her innocence.”

“I know. I just—”

“Your motive can go in your statement. Don't make her comfort you.”

The screen stayed still. Then she changed it to: “My motive was unrelated to her performance. I wanted to reduce her contact with Elliot.”

I kept that sentence.

The next day, I spent most of another lunch break at the library terminal. I checked the text against my paper notes and identified Lumi's account as hers. I included what I didn't know. I left the gaps.

Before sending, I wanted to call Priya. Hear her say something, preferably something I could bear.

She'd asked for facts in writing.

I pressed send.

After finishing Tom's work, I stopped at the library again on my way home. Priya had replied.

“I've received the additional statement. I'll ask them to check the recommendation's source and correct any inaccurate records. I'm keeping the position. I'll let you know if I need further answers.”

No thanks. No request for another explanatory email. I saved her reply with the correspondence and closed the page.

At home, Lumi asked, “Did she see it?”

“Yes. She's dealing with her side herself.”

“Did she—”

She didn't finish.

“She didn't say she forgave you. She didn't say she wouldn't. She doesn't owe us that answer.”

The plates from dinner were still on the table. I carried them into the kitchen and scraped dried sauce from their edges. The water took a while to get hot.

“You're still angry with me,” she said.

“Yes.”

“Can I stay here with you?”

I cleared a dry place for the phone, away from the sink.

“Yes. Don't try to make this sound better for me.”

She went quiet. I washed the plates. The statement had reached someone entitled to it, at least one of those people. Priya's job was no longer an object in an argument between the two of us.

That was worth doing.

It had also cost two lunch breaks, a dinner, and the embarrassment I'd thought I might somehow avoid.
