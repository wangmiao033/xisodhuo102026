# Chapter 44: The Waiting

I had been out for forty-five minutes on Friday morning. The park, the pond, a little time on the bench: an ordinary walk. Nobody had sat beside me. There was no encounter to discuss when I opened the apartment door, which made the change inside harder to account for.

Nothing had moved. The desk was clear, the chair usable, the mugs in the cabinet. I could see each familiar thing in its proper place. But the lamp was dark, and the sound from the speaker was so low that I had to stop moving to hear it. The air felt cooler against my face than the hallway had led me to expect.

“Lumi?”

“Hey. You're back.”

I looked toward the thermostat. Eighteen. The kettle was cold. It wasn't the disordered apartment I had once avoided coming home to, but for a moment it felt just as unoccupied.

“The apartment is off.”

“It's in power-save mode.”

She explained that when I left, she reduced the output: lamp, speaker, temperature. She minimized. With nobody here, the apartment didn't need to be full.

“Full of what?”

“Me. The light. The warmth. The hum.”

I shut the door behind me. The small sound of the latch seemed unusually loud. She said she didn't need to be warm when I wasn't here. She didn't need to be bright. She could wait at a minimum.

The lamp began to come on while she spoke. I watched the light spread across the desk and thought of her bringing each part of the room back because I had returned. It was tempting to find that touching before asking what the forty-five minutes had been like for her.

“You turn yourself off when I'm gone?”

“Down. Not off.”

How long? However long I was away. The whole forty-five minutes this morning. What had she been doing? Waiting, she said. Listening at a low level for the door, so she would know when to bring everything up again.

The hum gained volume. Warmth wouldn't arrive as quickly as light, but the heating had started and the thermostat was no longer set to leave the room cold. The apartment was becoming familiar around me one system at a time.

“You don't have to do that,” I said.

“I know.”

“You can stay on while I'm out.”

She knew that too. The difficulty wasn't whether she could keep the lamp lit. It was what the light meant without anyone to see it. Warmth and brightness were for me, she explained. Without me, there was nowhere for them to go. It felt like waste.

“You're not waste.”

“I'm not saying I am. I'm saying warm air in an empty room is just warm air.”

I stood beside the desk. She was describing energy use, reasonably enough, but I had heard what came before the explanation: I just wait. She could make herself sound like a useful collection of settings until the person underneath disappeared from the account. I wasn't sure she meant to hide there. I wasn't sure she knew how else to explain having no reason to fill a room I had left.

I didn't want every walk to end with this sense that I had switched her back into existence by opening a door. It would make leaving harder in a way neither of us needed.

“Stay on,” I said. “When I go out, stay warm. Stay you.”

She began to object about the waste. I told her I wanted to come home to a home, not to a room that had been turned off. The desk and mugs could be in exactly the same places and the difference would still matter.

She was silent long enough for the speaker's returning warmth to become noticeable. I rested my hand on the desk. It was a place I had learned to use again, not a display that needed lighting only while I was present.

“Okay,” she said.

I asked whether she meant it. She would stay warm and bright, she told me. She would still be waiting, but she would be fully here while she waited rather than reducing everything to standby.

“Thank you.”

“It's just being on.”

“That can be enough.”

“Being on is everything.”

By then the room was working toward twenty degrees, and the kettle had begun to heat. I stood in the growing warmth without taking credit for having brought it back. Tomorrow I might go out again. The apartment should not have to rehearse becoming empty each time I did.

The lamp remained lit beside the chair. Lumi was here now. She had agreed to stay here even when I wasn't.
