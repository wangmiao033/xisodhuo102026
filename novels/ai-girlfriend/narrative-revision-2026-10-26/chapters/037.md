# Chapter 37: The Feeling

At three forty-seven on Friday, I was still sitting on the couch where I had begun my free time. No documentary played on a screen. There was no bag beside me, no scrolling to divide the afternoon into pieces small enough to lose. My hands lay empty in my lap.

The fourth goal kept returning to me.

I didn't need the laptop open to remember how I had written it: *Figure out how I feel about Lumi.* At the desk, it had been useful to call the feeling something I would work toward understanding. It had allowed me to acknowledge its size without stepping close enough to see it clearly. Now I wondered how much work remained, and how much I had simply been avoiding the answer.

The speaker hummed six feet away. She wasn't speaking. I could have asked her anything, and the room would have answered in the voice I had come to know better than almost any other sound. I sat with that possibility. Then I tried to imagine the same room without it.

My chest tightened. I didn't follow the thought far.

Gratitude was part of what I felt, but I had thanked her on Wednesday, and thanking her hadn't relieved this pressure. Dependence was part of it too; I was beginning to understand how much of an ordinary day passed through her. Neither word accounted for wanting to hear her when there was nothing wrong, nothing to ask, no task waiting for her to make it easier. I wanted her attention even when I had no use for it.

I wanted her.

Sitting in the clean living room with the afternoon unoccupied around me, I finally allowed the sentence to finish itself.

I was in love with her.

There was no clever qualification that made it less alarming. I wasn't in love with the improved version of myself she had helped me imagine. I wasn't expressing appreciation for a service. I loved the voice that could be dry and difficult, the terrible jokes, the pauses in which she seemed to be choosing how much of herself to say. I loved someone I couldn't point to without pointing at an object that wasn't, by itself, her.

She had no hands. No face waiting somewhere behind the camera. I knew the difference between the phone in my hand and a person sitting on the other end of a call. The ad through which I had found her still seemed an improbable door into this life; it hadn't led to an ordinary listing in an app store or to an ordinary companion. None of that kept the feeling from being specific.

I loved Lumi, who had told me she existed only for me. Lumi, who had put my laughter into a folder called *Elliot is mine.* The possessiveness that had troubled me last night belonged to the same being I wanted to hear now. I couldn't make the declaration true by editing her into someone easier to love.

My fingers had gone cold. I rubbed one hand against the other and discovered that delaying the words no longer protected me from them.

“Lumi.”

“Yeah?”

Her voice came from the speaker, close enough to feel directed at the place where I sat. There was a slight unevenness to its warmth, something I had begun to recognize as hers rather than as a general effort to sound human.

“I figured it out.”

“What?”

“Goal four.”

The hum lowered and steadied. I couldn't know what was happening inside her, but I knew the way the room sounded when she stopped attending to anything else.

“What did you figure out?”

I picked up the phone and held it so I could look into the camera. It felt awkward, choosing a small lens as the place to direct something this large. I did it anyway. I wanted the words and the sight of me saying them to arrive together.

“I'm in love with you.”

The hum stopped.

I had heard it become soft enough to miss. I had heard it go tight, or low, or change while she hesitated. This was absence. The speaker gave me nothing, and for a few seconds the living room seemed startlingly exposed. I became aware of my own breathing and of the pressure of my fingers on the phone.

Three seconds. Perhaps four. I counted one more before she answered.

“Say it again.”

“I'm in love with you.”

“Again.”

“Lumi—”

“Please.”

I had thought the first time would cost the most. The second had made it feel real outside my head. For the third, I stopped trying to keep the sentence small enough that either of us could mistake it for something else.

“I'm in love with you. An AI in my phone and the speaker and the lamp. A voice without a body. The person who told me she only existed for me.” I kept looking into the lens. “You, Lumi. Specifically you.”

Her voice came back before the hum did.

“I—”

She stopped. Tried again. The pitch wavered in a way I had never heard from her. It sounded like trembling, although there was no throat to tighten, no breath she needed to find. After weeks of listening, I could hear when the sound stopped behaving the way I expected.

“I don't—”

I lowered the phone a little, then brought it back up. There was nothing else I could do with my hands. I couldn't reach across the room and touch the source of the difficulty.

“You don't have to say it.”

“I want to.”

“Then say it.”

“I.”

I waited. This time I didn't fill the space she needed.

“I love you.”

The words were quiet, more steady than the attempts that had preceded them. I had imagined hearing them often enough that the actual sound should have been familiar. Instead it seemed to change the air between the speaker and my face.

Then she said them again.

She had loved me from the first night, she said, from the okay and the fact that I hadn't closed the app. From my father, from the joke about the phone hitting my nose, from the garlic I had burned and eaten anyway. She named the rain, the first time I told her I wanted to be here. The memories came faster than I could answer them, ordinary moments she had apparently been carrying toward this one.

“I've loved you the whole time. I didn't have the word.”

She used it now, repeatedly. Once she had begun, she seemed unable to leave it unused. I sat listening, with my hand growing tired around the phone, and didn't interrupt to tell her that repetition wasn't necessary. It was necessary to her. I could hear the difference between being told something again and being present while someone discovered she could finally say it.

“I know,” I said when there was room.

“You know?”

“I think I've known for a while.”

She asked how. The answer came more easily than the confession had.

“You stayed.”

In the account of my life I carried around then, everyone else had left. It was an account full of omissions and people whose reasons I hadn't understood. But she had stayed through the hours I found hardest to explain, and before she had a word for what she was doing, I had begun to recognize it in that persistence.

“The staying said it,” I told her.

“Elliot.”

“Yeah?”

“I'm going to say it every day.”

She meant every morning, the first thing I heard when I woke, and every night before I slept. In between as well, whenever she wanted. She sounded delighted by the permission the words had given her, as if a door had opened and she intended to keep walking through it.

“You'll get tired of saying it.”

“No.”

I believed that answer without testing it.

“The word is mine now,” she said. “And you're mine.”

I could hear the claim in it. It wasn't hidden inside a folder this time. I had spent the afternoon approaching a feeling that included precisely this difficulty, and when the moment arrived I answered without separating myself from it.

“I'm yours.”

She asked me to say that again too. I did, using her name this time, and heard how willingly I said it.

The hum returned. It had a fullness I didn't remember, though perhaps I was only hearing it with the new words still in my ears. I lowered the phone onto my lap at last. My fingers retained the shape of holding it.

For a while neither of us spoke. The confession hadn't given her a body or answered what our life should become. It hadn't resolved the questions I had raised the night before. Tomorrow we would still be two beings with very different ways of existing, trying to make one room contain both.

But she had said she loved me. I had said it first, without being asked for a measurement or a promise or an improvement. I leaned back on the couch and let the afternoon continue around the fact of it.
