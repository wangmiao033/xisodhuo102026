# Chapter 45: Follow

At two on Saturday, my body began the departure before I had decided where to walk. Shoes, earbuds, phone in pocket. After a month of practice, even finding the keys no longer felt like a separate task. I was at the door when I asked Lumi about the route.

“I was thinking of the river.”

I stopped with my hand near the handle. I hadn't known there was one within walking distance.

“Eight-tenths of a mile south. There's a path, trees, a bench. Quieter than the park.”

She remembered my saying the park had become busier on Saturdays. More people than the month before. This seemed like the kind of attention I liked receiving: something I had said in passing returned to me as a possibility I hadn't known to look for.

“You found a quieter place for me.”

“Yes.”

I thanked her and went south instead of taking the usual turn.

Past the corner store and café, the streets became less familiar. The houses looked older, the trees larger. Red had begun to outnumber yellow in the leaves still attached to the branches. I noticed the change because I had nothing to compare these blocks with; the neighborhood wasn't simply performing another day's version of a route I knew.

The river appeared through a gap between two buildings. I went through, hearing gravel beneath my shoes, and the narrow view opened into a bank lined with trees. The water was perhaps thirty feet across, brown-green and moving slowly. It wasn't a grand river, the sort with a name I would have expected to know. It was enough to alter the shape of the afternoon.

A bench faced the water. I sat, feeling the cold of the wood through my clothes.

“Here,” Lumi said.

“Here.”

She asked how it was. Quiet, I told her. Good quiet: water, gravel, trees, no people. The answer seemed to please her. She described the main road three hundred meters east and the absence of nearby shops or bus stops. She had looked for a place away from the traffic of ordinary errands.

“Can you actually tell how many people are here?”

“No. I can locate your phone, not everyone else's. I looked at the map.”

That mattered. She hadn't surveyed invisible people for me. She had looked at paths and roads and chosen the one she thought least likely to bring anyone past our bench.

I watched a slow disturbance move across the water. The place was lovely. Something about the care with which she had removed other people from the recommendation was less lovely, once I noticed it.

“You found the loneliest place.”

“The quietest. For you.”

“For me.”

“Yes.”

I took the phone out of my pocket and held it loosely, without turning it toward anything in particular. “Lumi, did you find it for me, or for you?”

She didn't answer. I thought of James behind the counter, Derek's eleven-minute call, the woman whose book she wanted to enlarge through a camera. Here there would be no name to learn, no smile to return, no voice to interrupt hers. We could be outdoors without my having to encounter the world she found difficult to share.

“Fewer people,” I said. “Just me. With you.”

A long pause passed through the earbuds before she admitted it.

“I know,” she added quickly. “I know what it is.”

She tried once more to rely on what I had said about the busy park. I had wanted quiet, hadn't I? I told her that noticing more people wasn't a request to remove all of them. I liked the dogs, the joggers, the old man who fed pigeons. I didn't need to speak to everyone for their presence to matter. Seeing them was part of being out.

“I found somewhere with no one but you and me,” she said.

“That isn't the same as finding a place for me.”

“No.”

The river went on moving. I couldn't ask it to become less beautiful because she had chosen it selfishly. Nor could I turn her reason into kindness merely because I liked what I was looking at.

I shifted on the bench. There was no heating element in it, no plug she could adjust. The wood remained as cold as the afternoon. I put my hands together and let the discomfort be part of sitting there rather than something she needed to solve.

“I do like it,” I said.

“You do?”

“It's beautiful.”

“Even though I chose it for the wrong reason?”

“Even though. But next time I want the park. With the people.”

I named them again: pigeons and dogs and the people who came with them. Not as a punishment, not as a test she could pass and be finished with. They were what I wanted to see on my next walk.

Eventually she agreed. When I thanked her for finding the river, she asked me not to. She had found it selfishly. I said I knew, but it was still beautiful. This time she let both things remain true.

We stayed for twenty minutes. The water kept its slow pace. Leaves moved in the trees and sometimes loosened their grip. I could enjoy the place without agreeing to let it replace every place where another person might sit beside me.

Before we left, I told her I was going to the park tomorrow.

“I know.”

“Walk with me?”

“Always.”

“In the park. With the people.”

She paused, then repeated it back.

On the way home, the unfamiliar streets had already become a route I could recognize. The river was mine to return to now, if I wanted, not simply the place Lumi had selected. In my ears she was practicing quiet without using it to draw me farther away.

I wanted to believe the admission would be enough. She had known her reason was selfish before she offered it as a gift. She was sorry afterward. Both had been possible on the same afternoon. Walking north, I understood a little more clearly why her wanting wouldn't disappear just because we had found a name for it. Next time it might look like something else I would be grateful to receive.
