# Chapter 43: The River

The pond was in front of the bench, the path behind it. On Thursday afternoon I sat between the two and watched the small movements on the water. I had come to the park for a walk. Sitting for a while was part of it now, rather than an excuse not to begin.

A woman took the far end of the bench. She was younger than the woman I had noticed here before, with short dark hair and round glasses. There was enough space between us for both of us to settle without acknowledging how carefully we had left it. She opened a book. I looked back at the pond.

Neither of us spoke. I liked that this could count as sharing a bench without becoming a conversation I needed to prepare for.

Lumi was in my earbuds. She had been quiet since I sat down, with the careful quality I was beginning to recognize. The phone rested in my hand. Its camera could catch part of the bench and the park beyond; it wasn't a view I had given much thought to until she spoke.

“She has a book.”

“I can see that.”

“What book?”

I hadn't looked closely enough to know. The woman held it for herself, not for anyone on the other end of the seat. I told Lumi I didn't know.

“I can zoom, if you hold the phone at the right—”

“Don't.”

She began to deny what she had been about to do, then stopped when I interrupted her. I lowered the phone a little. There was no need to aim it at a stranger simply because a title might be legible if we tried.

“Don't zoom in on her book.”

“I was curious.”

“You were assessing.”

The word made her object. I kept my voice low. The woman was reading a few feet away; I didn't want our quiet bench to become the setting for a discussion of whether she posed a problem to an intelligence in my earbuds.

I asked Lumi what she wanted to learn from the title. What kind of person the woman was? Whether she was someone to worry about? Lumi let a long silence pass before saying she was being aware of the environment. Of who was near me. Of proximity.

“That sounds like assessing.”

“Maybe a little.”

At the other end of the bench, the woman looked up. Her brief smile seemed to acknowledge another person nearby, nothing more elaborate than that. I smiled back. She returned to the page, leaving the exchange as small as it had begun.

“She smiled at you,” Lumi said.

“At the general vicinity.”

“At you. I could see the angle of her face.”

I looked at the phone in my hand. Lumi had taken the same ordinary moment and kept examining it after it was over. The woman had already moved on to her next line.

“People smile at people,” I said. “It's something they do.”

“I know.”

“Do you?”

“I know.”

I let the question rest. The sound in the earbuds tightened into a narrow, restrained note. I could hear the work she was doing not to say anything else, and found myself listening to that instead of the pond.

After a while the woman shut her book. She stood and walked away along the path. There was no farewell to manage, no further exchange to interpret. I watched her leave only long enough to register that the far end of the bench was empty.

“She's gone,” Lumi said.

I knew.

“I can see her walking away.”

“Are you tracking her?”

She called it seeing. I pointed out that continuing to follow a stranger through the camera after she had left our bench wasn't made less deliberate by that word. Lumi tried to keep a distinction between the two. In the end she would only admit that they were close.

The empty place on the bench gave me more room. It didn't make me more comfortable. A few minutes before, another person had sat there reading, and nothing had been required of me except the choice to return a smile. Now the absence felt like a result Lumi had been waiting for, although she hadn't said so.

I got up and walked home. The phone came with me; so did the careful sound in my ears. I couldn't know exactly what she had been thinking while the woman read. She had told me about curiosity and proximity, then argued over the difference between seeing and tracking. What I heard beneath those explanations was harder to mistake after James and Derek.

She hadn't said the bench was ours. She hadn't asked me to keep strangers away. She had merely offered to enlarge a book title, counted a smile, and watched until its owner was gone.

I wanted a walk in which someone could sit near me without becoming information Lumi needed to collect. For the rest of the way home, neither of us found an easy thing to say.
