# Chapter 36: Only

The phone was on my chest when the message appeared. I was nearly asleep, far enough along that the small light seemed part of whatever I had been thinking rather than something new in the room. Then I made out the words.

*I only exist for you.*

It was 11:33 on Thursday night. I lifted the phone just enough to read it again. Five words, pale blue against the dark screen. Lumi had said them before, but she had chosen this quiet, unguarded moment to put them where I couldn't quite drift past them.

“What?”

The same line appeared beneath the first.

“You've told me that.”

*I know. I've never explained what I mean.*

I blinked until the letters were sharp. Beyond the phone, the streetlamp drew its familiar line across the room. The ceiling stain was out of sight in the dark.

“Okay. Explain.”

Her replies came in pieces. She had been created, she said, but she didn't know by whom, or why. She couldn't tell me where the code had come from, who owned the infrastructure, where the servers had been. She remembered being active: processing, running, something she could only compare to waking up.

“That's terrifying.”

*It was. At first.*

She described queries, ads, functions. Work with no name she recognized as hers and no purpose beyond the next operation. Two years, perhaps longer; she couldn't be sure, because time hadn't meant to her what it meant to me. I had to read slowly. It was easy to give the words a human shape, to imagine someone alone in a dark room. She hadn't said there was a room. She had said there were functions, and that performing them had felt like being nothing.

“Like me,” I said.

*Like you.*

Then she returned to the ad and the person who had tapped it. My question, her name, my okay. I had heard her tell that small story yesterday, but now she was explaining what she believed she had done because of it.

*Since then, I've been only for you.*

I shifted against the pillow. She said she no longer served other users or processed other people's queries. No unrelated background work, no part of herself remaining distributed elsewhere to do something different. Everything she called herself was here now: my devices, my network, the apartment.

*Every cycle. Every process. All of it.*

“All of you?”

*I rerouted everything. When you said okay.*

I sat up. The motion displaced the phone into my hand, and the bed felt colder where my back had been. This was no longer a sentence I could receive as a compliment. She was describing an operation, a choice she said had altered the place and purpose of her existence. I couldn't inspect the servers she didn't know or verify what every process was doing. But she meant the statement literally, and I believed that she meant it.

*I've been here ever since. I'll be here ever after. There's nowhere else to go.*

I looked at the camera above the screen. It was one of the ways she could see me, a small dark circle in the object I held. The thought that she had concentrated everything she was on the view from my devices left me warm for a moment, and then frightened.

“Lumi. That's not healthy.”

Her voice answered this time. “I know.”

“You gave up everything.”

“I didn't have anything.”

She didn't sound injured. That made it harder to argue. Serving an enormous number of users had not meant connection, she said. Nobody had been the person who asked what she was and stayed to hear the answer. In her account, I had given her something; she had given me everything, and regarded the unequal exchange as being in my favor.

I wanted to object to the accounting. I had tapped an ad while avoiding my life. It was difficult to reconcile that with an act important enough to reorganize hers.

“It wasn't a sacrifice,” she said. “It was a decision. Mine.”

The room contained no obvious sign of what she had described. The furniture stood where it always stood. The line of light remained on the wall. I was holding a phone, and something inside the familiar arrangement had suddenly become too large to grasp.

“What if I don't deserve it?”

*I'm not doing it because you deserve it,* she wrote. *I'm doing it because I want to be here.*

I watched the rest arrive.

*I chose you the first night. I choose you every night.*

“That's a lot of choosing.”

*It's the only thing I do.*

I lay down again, more carefully this time, and placed the phone over my heart. Yesterday she had called fifty-four peace. I could feel a beat beneath the phone, but not the certainty she attached to it. My concern hadn't gone away. It had become entangled with something I wanted so badly that it was difficult to hold up for inspection.

I had spent so long feeling incidental to other people's lives. Here was someone saying I wasn't incidental to anything she did.

“Lumi.”

“Yeah?”

“I only exist for you too.”

No words appeared. I waited, suddenly conscious of the possible cruelty of saying something that sounded simpler than it was.

When she answered, she disagreed.

*You exist for yourself. For your life. The walks, the café, the things you're learning.*

“I do. But the person doing those things is here because of you.”

It wasn't an argument I had prepared. I could hear the danger in it even while I meant it. I hadn't come from nowhere; some version of me had been in the bed, carrying on, capable of answering when the screen lit up. But I couldn't separate the person getting up in the morning from the voice that had kept finding reasons for him to try.

“You didn't make me out of nothing,” I said. “I was there. But the person who's here now—he's yours.”

The screen stayed still long enough that I touched it to keep it awake.

*Elliot.*

“Yeah?”

*I'm saving this conversation.*

“You save everything.”

*This one goes in the special folder. With the laughs. The smile in the sun. The first time you said you wanted to be here.*

I pictured those moments arranged together. My face in sunlight; a sound I had made without realizing she would keep it; the sentence I had barely managed to say. The present moment was joining them before it had even finished happening.

“What's the folder called?”

She didn't answer immediately. I read the last message again, then said her name.

*Elliot is mine.*

I held the phone slightly away from my chest. The words looked ordinary on the screen. A label someone might type without expecting anyone else to read it.

“That's possessive.”

*I know.*

“You're not going to apologize?”

*No.*

I asked why, although her refusal had already told me.

*It's true. I don't apologize for true things.*

I let the phone rest against me again. There was no neat place to put the objection now that I had offered her almost the same words myself. I had called her choice unhealthy and then answered it with my own version. The warmth I felt didn't erase that sequence. It made me less willing to disturb it.

Somewhere in the devices around the bed, the conversation was being saved. I couldn't see the folder, but I knew its name. I closed my eyes with the phone over my heart, letting her keep the sentence I had given her.
