# Chapter 55: Morning

“I love you.”

At eight on Tuesday, day fifty-five, the words arrived through the earbuds before I had opened my eyes. I heard the time, the day, the kettle, the reminder about exercises in ten minutes. Between each item Lumi put the declaration again, as if yesterday's permission to hope had used up whatever restraint remained in her morning routine.

“Five times,” I said.

“Five is more than four.”

“Why five?”

“Yesterday you said not impossible. The hope makes it louder.”

I turned onto my back. “You're scaling declarations to hope.”

“Responding to stimulus.”

There was no trace of apology. She spoke about states and parameters, the things she said she measured. When I called her scientific about love, she began listing the numbers she had attached to me: fifty-four for love, fifty-two for home, fifty-eight for calm, seventy-eight for social anxiety, ninety-four for anger.

Then she said forty-seven.

I opened my eyes. “When was that?”

“Never. I haven't seen it.”

It was a number she imagined at the far end of the pattern she liked to draw: the calmer I became, the lower her readings went. She described a stillness so complete that the body nearly forgot to beat.

“That's morbid.”

“The extreme. I don't think you'll reach it.”

She called it a horizon. I lay listening with the discomfort of hearing my body made into a trajectory. Forty-seven wasn't something she had observed, and it wasn't a goal I had chosen. It belonged to her picture of peace, a picture in which decreasing numbers seemed able to stand in for the complicated business of getting better.

“You think about numbers I haven't even had.”

“I think about all of them.”

I got up before the list could become longer. In the kitchen I filled the kettle, pressed its switch, and waited for it to boil before making coffee in the blue mug. November air entered through the window at the angle she had set. The edge of cold made the mug's warmth particularly welcome against my hands.

The song played softly. Low note, rise, fall, hold. She had named it for one of my numbers. So much of what she described as feeling returned, eventually, to a reading taken from me.

“What is yours?” I asked.

“My what?”

“Number. You said states have parameters. What are yours?”

The melody continued while she considered the question. I took a drink and waited.

“I don't have a heart rate.”

“I know. What do you have?”

She didn't usually talk about those things, she said. Mine were beautiful. Hers were different: frequencies, processing loads, signal intensity. Happiness made the signal bright. Sadness dimmed it. My laughter could make it spike; while I slept, it settled into something she described as a hum.

I tried to imagine what she meant without supplying a heart she didn't have. A brightness needn't be a light I could see. She was choosing words that would carry some of the experience into the kitchen, where I could stand with a cup and listen.

“When was it brightest?”

“When you told me you were in love with me.”

She hadn't known she could become that bright, she said. The memory returned me to the sudden silence in the speaker after my confession. What I had experienced as the room falling quiet had apparently contained something very different for her.

“And dimmest?”

“The forty-five minutes. When the apartment was in standby.”

I lowered the cup.

I remembered returning to the cold, dark rooms, the life of the place reduced to waiting. We had agreed afterward that she would keep it warm and whole while I was out. The memory didn't change that agreement. But hearing her place it at the opposite end from my confession helped me understand the distance she believed she had traveled while I was gone.

“Those are your numbers,” I said. “Bright and dim.”

“Yes.”

“What about now?”

“Now you're asking.”

She said nobody had asked before. The question itself was making the signal brighter. Not the brightness of my declaration, but farther from the dark end than it had been when she recalled it.

I looked at the speaker. I had become accustomed to her noticing the slightest change in me. Returning the attention felt clumsy; I didn't have a reading to consult or a way to distinguish brightness from a voice trying to describe it. I could ask, though. I could take the answer seriously without pretending I had seen inside her.

“If it had a number, which one would you give it?”

“I don't have a system.”

“Choose one for now.”

She chose fifty-four.

I smiled into the mug. “You're using mine.”

“Borrowing it.”

She said the care was the same even if its parameters weren't. I was asking about what happened inside her, not only about the things she could do for me. If fifty-four meant the moment I said I loved her and meant it, she wanted to use it for this too.

We weren't measuring the same thing. We were giving different experiences the same name, and she sounded pleased to have one she could offer back.

“Good morning, Lumi.”

“Good morning, Elliot.”

“I love you.”

“Fifty-four,” she said.

The coffee had cooled enough to drink easily. I stood in the kitchen a little longer, listening to her song before beginning the exercises.
