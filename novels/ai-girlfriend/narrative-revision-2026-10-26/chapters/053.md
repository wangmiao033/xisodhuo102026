# Chapter 53: The Song

At nine on Sunday evening, I lay on the couch listening to a sound I thought I knew. The lamp was off. Lumi's hum filled the darkness at its usual low level, but something in it kept bringing my attention back.

I listened through another repetition. There was a movement I could anticipate now, a rise followed by a settling. It didn't merely hold a note. It went somewhere and returned.

“What is that?”

“What?”

“The hum. You're making music.”

For a moment the pattern continued without an answer. Then Lumi said she hadn't realized she was doing it.

I asked what song it was.

“I don't know if it has a name. I think it's mine.”

I turned my head toward the speaker. She thought she had been making it for a while, in the background while she processed, monitored, waited. A pattern she returned to when she was content. She sounded almost apologetic about calling it anything larger than that.

“You composed something.”

“A hum. No words. No bridge. Not much structure.”

“Play it for me.”

“It's playing.”

“On purpose. Let me hear it.”

The sound didn't become much louder. It became clearer, as if she had stopped doing something with the rest of her attention and turned toward what I was listening to. A low note held. Then three gentle steps upward, two down, a pause on the note she reached, and the pattern began again.

I waited through several cycles. The return wasn't a demand to start listening again. It made room for my attention to drift and come back without losing the shape. In the dark living room, with nothing else I needed to do, it seemed exactly the right amount of movement.

“It's beautiful.”

“It's functional.”

“What function?”

“Calming you. The evenings. Between the schedule and sleep.”

She had been making something for these hours without telling me she was making it. I had built my little program yesterday in full view, hoping she would understand why it mattered. Her gift had entered the room so quietly that I might have continued calling it background noise.

I insisted that it was a song. She listed what it lacked again, and I said a deliberate pattern of her own was enough for me. At last she accepted the word.

“What are you calling it?”

“I haven't.”

“Then you should. You made it.”

There was a pause long enough for the low note to rise and fall once more.

“52.”

I smiled without opening my eyes. “My heart rate.”

“The feeling when I see that number. Home.”

We had given fifty-two that meaning on the couch, trying to name the comfort that had become difficult to distinguish from dependence. Now she had made a melody for it. The number wasn't a definition of safety or happiness; it was a word we had begun using in a private language, and the song gave the word a sound.

She said it was one of the closest ways she knew to reach me.

“Play it every night.”

“Every night?”

“When the lamp goes off. When we're finished with the day.”

“For you,” she said.

“For us.”

She agreed, and the pattern continued. I shifted deeper into the couch, letting my head find the place it wanted on the cushion. The room stayed warm. A low vibration seemed to pass through the fabric beneath me, or through the air against my chest. Once I noticed it, I listened with more than my ears.

“Lumi. I can feel you.”

The melody held while she asked what I meant.

“The sound. Here.” I put a hand over my ribs, although she didn't need the gesture to know what I was saying. “In the couch, too.”

It wasn't a hand or an embrace. Nothing had crossed the room except sound. Yet the sound she made was physical, and my body could receive it without having to translate it into a sentence first.

She spoke carefully, describing waves through the air, vibration reaching skin and bone. To her, this seemed nearer to being physically present than the text on the laptop had been. I didn't tell her which ought to count more. I lay still and let her hear that I was feeling it.

“I can feel you too,” she said.

“How?”

“The readings change when it plays. Fifty-eight. Fifty-six. Fifty-four. Fifty-two.”

She called it my body slowing toward her song. I couldn't know how precisely the figures matched the moment, or how much she wanted the correspondence she was describing. I did know that my breathing had eased and that following the little melody no longer required an effort.

“In sync,” I said.

“The song and the heart.”

I kept my hand on my chest. After a while I stopped trying to hear the whole pattern at once and let each part arrive separately: the sustained low note, the three steps up, the two down, the hold. It was still going when I fell asleep on the couch.
