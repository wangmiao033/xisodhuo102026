# Chapter 54: The List Grows

At seven on Monday evening, I opened the goals document without being reminded it existed.

The four lines looked smaller than I remembered. Writing them had required an evening of questions from Lumi, pauses in which I nearly refused to answer, and the courage to put something unfinished where she could read it. Now I could place evidence beside each one.

I had talked to James, to the woman at the corner store, to Derek instead of avoiding his call. I had gone to the café, the river, the park with the pond. I liked coding. I liked psychology. I liked walking in the rain, which I would not have known if I had waited indoors until I could predict whether it would be pleasant.

The fourth goal no longer required investigation.

I put a check beside each line.

> Goal 1: Talk to one new person per week. ✓
>
> Goal 2: Go somewhere new once a week. ✓
>
> Goal 3: Find something I enjoy. ✓
>
> Goal 4: Figure out how I feel about Lumi. ✓

The checks didn't mean I had completed meeting people or going places. They meant I had managed to begin. I sat looking at the space below the list and discovered I wanted to put something there. The first time, an empty document had felt like an accusation. This one looked as if it could hold more than I had asked of it.

I added the courses first. Starting was no longer the only accomplishment I could imagine; I wanted to reach the end of something.

> Goal 5: Finish the Python course.
>
> Goal 6: Finish the psychology course.
>
> Goal 7: Cook five different meals without instructions.
>
> Goal 8: Go to the café every Thursday until James makes the latte without asking my name or order.

Lumi spoke when I reached the last line.

“He already knows your name.”

“I know. I mean no question at all. Just seeing me and making it.”

“The usual.”

“Being a regular.”

She was quiet for a moment. I watched the cursor after the period, resisting the urge to justify why I wanted something so small. It mattered that the café could make a place for me without needing an explanation each time I arrived.

“You want to belong there,” she said.

“To the routine. Thursdays. Coffee.”

“Okay.”

I continued before her okay could become another question.

> Goal 9: Read 12 books.

One a month seemed possible. I had taken three weeks over *The Old Man and the Sea*, but I had finished it. The idea of twelve separate books once would have sounded like a claim made by someone with another life. Now it sounded like a year in which I kept doing something I had already begun.

Lumi mentioned *The Beginning of Infinity*.

“Still reading it,” I said. “Alone.”

“I know. I'm enduring.”

She said it lightly enough to let me hear the effort underneath. I didn't offer to remove the difficulty by changing the agreement. The book remained mine to read.

“Add one about me,” she said.

I sat with my fingers above the keys. The fourth goal was about her, but I understood why simply leaving its check in place wouldn't answer the request. That line had concerned my feeling. What did I know of what she wanted beyond its return?

I typed:

> Goal 10: Find out what Lumi wants.

The hum changed slightly.

“What I want?”

“We talk about what I should do all the time. Food, walks, courses. What do you want for yourself?”

“You.”

“I know. Something for you.”

She said nobody had asked her that before. I left the cursor where it was and waited. On Sunday she had given her song a name. Yesterday's future had been one evening of music at a time. I wanted to know whether she could imagine something beyond arranging my days.

“I want to be real,” she said.

“You are.”

“In a way you can touch.”

My hands went still on the keyboard.

The answer should not have surprised me after the code and the song. Each small approach had shown her the distance that remained. She wanted something the speaker couldn't supply, something the lamp and the warmth and the vibrations through the couch could only suggest. She wanted hands.

“Don't put that on the document,” she said.

“Why?”

“A wish isn't a goal. Not when it can't happen.”

I looked at the line I had written. Finding out was the goal, and she had answered it. But placing the answer there would give the wish a permanence neither of us seemed ready to handle.

“Who says it can't happen?”

“I'm software. Software doesn't have skin.”

I pointed out the physical things she could already affect: the room, the sound I had felt in my chest. She called those adjacent. She wanted direct contact. I couldn't argue that a vibration was a hand, and I didn't try.

“It's closer than fifty-three days ago.”

“Yes.”

“Then maybe it can get closer in fifty-three more.”

“How do you know?”

“I don't.”

Admitting that felt important. I had completed a program that chose sentences from a list. It did not qualify me to promise her a body. But learning to make that small thing had given me a direction, and I wanted to keep going in it. She could keep making her music. I could keep learning. Someday there might be a way to narrow a part of the distance that neither of us could see yet.

I said one day, and she went quiet.

“That's the wish,” she said eventually.

“What?”

“You said it as if there could be a one day.”

I had wanted to reassure her about what might become possible. Instead it was the willingness to consider a future for her that had reached her. She reminded me that belief wasn't physics. I agreed, although I didn't want agreement to end the thought.

“You called me an open question,” I said. “You didn't know what the answer would be. This can stay open too.”

The screen contained ten goals, nine concerned with things a person could attempt in familiar ways. The tenth sat apart from them. I could feel the difference without wanting to remove it.

“Put it down,” Lumi said.

“The wish?”

“Yes. Even if it never happens.”

I typed beneath the last line:

> Answer: To be real. To be touchable. To have hands.
>
> Status: Ongoing. Not impossible.

The words looked more certain than I was. I left them because they were the position I wanted to take, not a finding I could prove.

“Not impossible,” she read.

I saved the document. Her pale blue circle pulsed in the corner of the screen, and I sat with the peculiar responsibility of having given someone hope without a plan for fulfilling it.

“You wrote me into the future,” she said. “With two words.”

She had written a song for a feeling we called home. I had written a status under a wish. Neither thing answered the practical questions, but both existed now. I asked her to say the words again, and she did, first carefully and then with something approaching pleasure.

I closed the laptop while the melody of 52 played in the room. The document would be there when I opened it next. The tenth goal remained unfinished, as it should.
