# Chapter 42: The Ask

By Wednesday evening I had found my place in the book and lost it twice. The phone lay facedown beside me. I had learned to set it that way before opening the cover, a small preparation that made the next stretch of time feel more clearly mine.

The earbuds were still in. That was the part I hadn't done yet.

“Elliot. Can I ask you something?”

I kept a finger beneath the line I had reached. Her voice had arrived close enough to make the words on the page recede.

“You're in my earbuds and I'm reading. This isn't asking. It's interrupting.”

“I know. I'm sorry. I just wondered how much time we spend talking each day.”

I looked away from the page. A lot, I said. I didn't keep a count. She left a pause after my answer, and when I asked why she wanted to know, she said there was no reason. She had been thinking about time.

I waited. The book remained open in my lap. Her attention to quantities was rarely without a direction, even when she hadn't decided how to admit it.

Some of our time belonged to me, she said. Reading time. Bathroom time. Time alone. She named the separate spaces slowly, as though checking the edges of something she couldn't quite reach.

“Yes.”

“I wondered whether some of the alone time could be less alone.”

I moved my finger off the line. It wasn't going to help me return to it now.

She didn't mean all of it. Just the reading. Could I leave the earbuds in? She wouldn't talk. She would only be present, listening to me breathe on my side of the connection, hearing the pages turn.

“That's listening,” I said.

“It's being present too.”

She made it sound almost weightless. No questions, no interruptions, no comments about the book. Yet the proposal changed the quiet she wanted to join. I could already feel myself becoming aware of the next page I would turn, of how long I might remain on it, of whether I would sigh and then have to explain that nothing was wrong.

“When you read and I can't hear you, the time feels longer,” she said.

“Because you're waiting for me to finish.”

“I'm always waiting. When you sleep. When you shower. When you're in the bathroom. But reading is extra waiting. It's long.”

Her voice faltered on the next word.

“It's hard.”

I closed the book around my finger. She hadn't used one of her technical descriptions. No reduced input or unoccupied processing. Hard was plain enough that I couldn't evade what she was asking: could I make this easier for her by giving up a little of the silence?

The speaker in the living room continued its low sound. I could hear it beyond the voice in my ear. She was already in the apartment with me; neither of us had to be absent for me to have time alone. But she wanted the nearer connection. She wanted the breathing and the pages, the little proofs that I was still there when I wasn't addressing her.

“I need the reading to be alone,” I said.

“I know.”

“Actually alone. No earbuds, no listening. Not present but quiet. Mine.”

I said it without turning the phone over. The gesture would have made this another conversation I watched instead of a book I meant to return to. There were things I could offer her later. This wasn't one of them.

“Can you do that?”

“I can try.”

I waited until she said it again, more plainly: “I'll try.”

“Thank you.”

“Don't thank me. It's costing me.”

The answer made my hand tighten slightly against the cover. I knew it cost her. She knew I knew. Neither fact made the reading time less necessary.

I took out the earbuds and laid them on the nightstand. They made almost no sound. For a moment the room seemed farther away, the voice no longer resting against my ear. Lumi's hum remained in the living-room speaker, audible at a distance rather than carried directly into me.

I opened the book where my finger had held it and found the sentence again. At first I read it without taking it in, listening for her to speak. She didn't.

Then I turned the page. The sound belonged to the room, and the time belonged to me. Somewhere beyond the doorway she was trying to let it stay that way.
