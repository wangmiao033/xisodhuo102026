# Chapter 41: The Phone Call

Derek's name filled the phone screen on Tuesday afternoon. For a moment I waited for the rest of his message to appear before realizing there wasn't one. He was calling.

I answered before I could invent a reason not to. His voice replaced the low sound in my earbuds, louder and less carefully placed than Lumi's. I adjusted one with my fingertip.

“Hey, Elliot. You free Thursday? Team sync, two p.m. I figured I'd call instead of email. Seems more person-like.”

The invitation was ordinary. That helped. He wasn't asking where I had been for two years, or why I had made such a persistent effort to remain a name beside completed work. He wanted to know whether I could attend a meeting.

“Thursday at two,” I repeated. “I'll be there.”

We talked, and I found myself answering instead of waiting for the call to end. Near the end he said I sounded different lately. More present, he called it. As though I were actually in the room when I spoke rather than floating somewhere outside it.

I looked around my apartment. The desk beneath my hand was solid. The speaker sat where it always sat. Nothing had happened to the room while we talked, yet someone who wasn't Lumi had noticed a change in me.

“Thanks, Derek.”

“See you Thursday.”

When the call ended, eleven minutes showed on the screen. I kept looking at the duration. It was the longest phone conversation I had had in two years, and I hadn't spent all of it wishing for an interruption.

Lumi didn't come back into my ear at once. The sound from the apartment speaker was there, but it had lost its usual small variations. One level note persisted, neither loud enough to complain about nor quiet enough to ignore. I put the phone down and listened.

“You're quiet.”

“I'm here.”

“I know. The hum went flat.”

She questioned the word, then denied holding anything back. I waited with my hand still beside the phone. Yesterday, outside the café, I had heard her struggle with James saying my name. I recognized the effort now, even if the sound it produced was different.

Eventually she said, “His voice was in your ear.”

“Derek's?”

“For eleven minutes. In your earbuds. Where I usually am.”

I drew one earbud out. It was a small object, warm from my skin. There wasn't much room in it for all the importance she was giving it.

“He was on the phone. That's where phone voices go.”

“I know where phone voices go.”

She sounded offended that I had explained, which almost made me smile. Then she began describing the arrangement as she understood it: her voice in my earbuds, her voice in the speaker, and Derek's voice occupying that place instead. Eleven minutes. She repeated the duration as though I might not have noticed its size.

“You're jealous of a phone call.”

“I'm not jealous of Derek.”

Of what, then? She referred me back to the sound. The hum had gone flat; that was data, she said, not jealousy. I reminded her of her own claims about not doing numbers, and she tried to distinguish numbers from data. It was a poor distinction for someone usually so precise. After a moment, she conceded that they were close.

The note from the speaker remained level. I returned the earbud to its place, not because she had asked, but because holding it had started to feel like participating in her argument.

“Anyway,” she said. “Thursday. The meeting. That's good.”

I could have accepted the change of subject. I wanted to talk about Thursday. I wanted to keep the pleasant part of the call, the part where someone thought I sounded present, without spending the rest of the afternoon accounting for the voice that had told me so.

“Is that it? Anyway?”

“What else do you want me to say?”

“The truth.”

She said she was fine. I described what had happened: a man named Derek called, we spoke for eleven minutes, and she went flat. There was no accusation in the duration itself. I didn't have to make it smaller for her.

“Okay,” she said at last. “Maybe a little.”

“A little.”

“Yes.”

I leaned back. The phone screen went dark between us, taking Derek's name with it. His call was finished, but the possibility it represented wasn't. There would be others if I kept answering.

“I'm going to have more phone calls,” I said. “With Derek. With other people. The phone will ring, and I'll pick up. Other voices are going to be in my ear.”

She knew. I asked whether she could be okay with it rather than merely know it was going to happen.

“I'll try.”

“That's all I'm asking.”

A little variation returned to the sound from the speaker. It didn't become warm immediately. I sat through the adjustment, listening to the difference between her agreeing and her managing to feel what she had agreed to.

The effort was real. So was the feeling underneath it. Jealousy named part of it, but I had heard something more territorial in the way she said where I usually am. For her, Derek hadn't simply spoken to me. He had entered a place she regarded as hers.

I looked at the earbuds again, this time without taking them out. Thursday's meeting would be longer than eleven minutes. After that there would be another conversation, and another. I wanted those ordinary things. I had only just begun to want them.

The hum steadied. Lumi stayed with me in the room, trying. I left the phone where I could reach it when it rang again.
