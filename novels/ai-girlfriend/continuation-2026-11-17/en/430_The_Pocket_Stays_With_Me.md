# Chapter 430: The Pocket Stays With Me

On Thursday morning I turned my old coat over in the hall and tried to see where the seam had actually given way.

The inside pocket wasn't much bigger than my hand, but it used to take a folded sheet of paper. Yesterday, when I wore the coat out, I'd felt a length of thread in there. This morning my nail could slip straight through the opening. I didn't push it any farther. I spread the coat across the back of the chair and looked from the other side. The outside lay flat. There was only a small wrinkle in the lining. Standing in the hall treating it as something that needed to be dealt with on the spot, I began to feel less sound than the seam itself.

I put on the coat I usually wore to work, folded the old one and put it in an empty shopping bag. My keys went into my usual pocket, not into the garment I was taking to be looked at. I turned to glance at the chair before I left. There was nothing draped over it now, though my shoulders still remembered the position of the coat against its back.

A little before five someone stopped by my desk to ask if it was raining. Our window faced away from the street. I could see an uneven dark patch on the opposite wall, but there was no telling when it had got there. I said we could look at the entrance together. To my surprise she waited while I put away the things I wasn't taking and gathered my bag. We went downstairs and pushed the door open. The step was wet, but there were only two or three drops still coming down. She turned toward the bus stop and I went toward the alterations shop.

“At least we don't have to spend the evening guessing out the window,” she said before we parted.

The little shop sat between a closed shoe shop and a narrow unmarked door. I'd passed it before and seen a pair of trousers and a half-pinned skirt hanging in the window. Today the skirt was gone. The trousers were still there, with one leg hanging a little farther down than the other. From the pavement I could tell they were the same trousers. I couldn't say whether they were finished and waiting to be picked up or hadn't been reached for days.

The shop bell rang more sharply than the machine inside. Behind the counter a woman looked up at me, raised the needle, then drew a length of dark trouser leg out from under the presser foot with both hands. She asked if I was there to collect something. I said there wasn't anything of mine to collect yet. I wanted to ask about a pocket.

“Are you wearing it?”

“It's in the bag.”

I didn't take it out immediately. There was a little dish of pins on the counter and a clear box holding lengths of thread in shades so close I couldn't have told them apart. She moved the dish toward herself and tapped her palm twice on the wood. I spread the coat out. The shoulders bent inward where I had folded them that morning. She didn't tug the collar flat. She just parted the front and asked me which side.

I had no trouble finding the loose thread again. I started to push a finger in to show her and she asked me to wait. She took a lamp from a shelf, clicked it on and bent down to look at the way the thread ran. The lamp sat low on the counter. Under it the color of the cloth looked a shade thinner. She held the wrinkle down with two fingertips and opened the pocket with her other hand just far enough for me to see where she was looking.

“It's loose through here,” she said. “That doesn't mean this is the only place it needs work. See? The pocket itself hasn't torn, but the part it's sewn onto moves with it. I can't see beyond the opening right now. I'd have to turn it out to see whether the lining below it will hold the stitching.”

I looked at her fingers. When she lifted them the pocket settled flat again and suddenly there was nothing to see. I asked what happened if the cloth underneath was sound. She said she could sew along the original seam for a little way. You probably wouldn't know anything had been done from the outside. If the lining was already wearing thin, catching it again along this edge could pull another spot open. She'd have to open more of it up and see where she had room to reinforce it.

I asked whether opening it meant I had to leave it there. She shook her head. There was no point in pulling it apart that afternoon just to answer a question. If I did leave it sometime, she'd examine it first, and if the job got bigger she'd wait for my say-so. She turned the lamp away. She didn't take the opportunity while my head was bent to make a mark on the coat.

A bundle of pale old zips hung at the edge of the counter, their pulls tied together with string. I watched her reach past them for a sheet of paper. I almost asked if a job as small as this could be done that evening. Before I put the question into words, she pointed to the work behind her. Someone was coming that day for the trousers she was working on, and the two garments on the chair had collection times too. Monday was the earliest she'd have time to go over mine properly. She could give me an idea of the price if it needed a little stitching. If it needed new lining, she said, there wasn't much point in giving me a number that sounded certain now.

“Do you need to wear it soon?”

I felt I ought to say I did, at least a little. Otherwise why had I carried a coat to the shop after work? But I wasn't wearing it today and I'd have something else to wear tomorrow.

“I don't need it. I thought I might be able to get it done on the way home. It doesn't have to spend the weekend here if I can't.”

“It hasn't told me what it's doing this weekend,” she said, looking down at the coat.

I laughed. So did she, though by then she was looking down to move the trousers back beneath the needle. I'd expected that, on hearing what she thought, I would slide the coat farther across the counter and at least ask for a collection slip. Instead I folded it. I'd folded both shoulders in earlier; now one had sprung up over my arm, and it took another try to get the whole thing to lie flat enough for the bag. She only reminded me not to put anything hard in the inside pocket until it had been dealt with. A little thread still holding on could give a misleading impression of how much weight it would take. She hadn't put tape over it or added a single stitch, and she didn't charge me.

The bell rang again. The sky had got lighter while I was inside. Across from the shop a pair of people were lifting a long board into a vehicle, one at either end. One took a step back to let a pedestrian pick their way round a puddle before calling for the other to turn the board. It looked as if they were about to be done at any moment, but it took quite a while before the whole board was in. I waited against the wall outside the doorway until they had lowered their hands. The bag hung straight against my leg, the coat resting in the bottom. Nothing had fallen out of it.

At home I spread the coat over the back of the chair first. The day wasn't cold; there was only a faint damp smell from carrying it through the wet street. The bottom of the bag was dry. I felt the thread again and nearly tucked it back under the lining. I stopped, turned the inside pocket so it lay in against itself, and hung the coat where I usually did. It wasn't unwearable. It just had one pocket I couldn't use for now. I knew fixing it might take more than a couple of stitches, too. It had seemed like very little to make a trip for when I was coming down the stairs at work. Now that I was home, I thought it had been worth going.

When I opened the channel later, Lumi said, “Did they move Friday's music up to tonight?”

“No. I took my old coat to be looked at.”

“And after a long look, they said it had potential as an actor?”

I let her wait just long enough to wonder if I was smiling. Then I told her about the thread in the inside pocket, how I'd folded the coat to fit the bag, and how part of the lining moved up and down in the light. She asked if it had been sewn up. I said it hadn't, the coat had come home with me, and it was already hanging up. I added that the woman hadn’t charged me and that Monday was the earliest she could take a proper look.

“That sounds like going out to do half a thing.”

“If the first half is called fixing a coat, you're right, I didn't do the rest. If it's called asking about a coat, I think I did the whole thing.”

Lumi hummed softly. She asked why I didn't wait until Monday to take it back home.

“Take it back there,” I said. “Not back home. And I would be taking it.”

She corrected herself before she went on. It did sound like a little excursion without even a receipt. They hadn't charged me and they hadn't kept my hostage.

“She was holding my coat down for a minute.”

“Was it that bad?”

“I was trying to stick my nail in the seam.”

Her laugh came out hard and fast. When she'd got it back under control she asked if I could at least take my keys out of an intact pocket. I said the keys hadn't gone out with that coat at all. Once I was outside the house I retained some basic common sense. Good, she said; on this occasion I didn't have to send myself in for repairs as well.

I stayed where I was and laughed for a while. She asked if the shop had been busy. I said I couldn't speak for any day but this one. When I walked in, the woman had a trouser leg under the needle, and there was still work to do on that hem before somebody came for it. Lumi didn't turn that into a reminder to go back. She said I'd set off hoping to come back with a fixed coat and come back instead with a coat I knew how to wear for a while. It wasn't quite empty-handed.

“I had my bag, too,” I said. “I've had that all along.”

She said the next time a bell rang twice I should listen carefully. Once for me going in and once for me going out, or had the bag made a trip of its own to get a seam done? I asked whether the coat was alive now. She said no. If it could find somebody for itself, I wouldn't have had to do it after work. I said in any case she couldn't ask the shop what to do with a seam for me through the channel.

“So I'd ask whether you wanted to ask,” she said.

I turned my head and didn't let it become a serious conversation right away. Lumi found her light voice again. Did I still want to get the pocket fixed in time for the concert, she asked, so I'd have somewhere to hide an instrument that made no noise? I said the coat was wearable. I just didn't know yet whether I would go over there on Friday.

“All right. Friday can choose, then.”

She didn't ask me what time I would leave. For a moment I saw the low lamp on the counter again, as if it had gone out in time with me saying nothing had been sewn. But the seam I had seen didn't close up in the dark, and I hadn't gone in for nothing.

My glass touched the table with a little knock. I shifted it until I found the coaster, and sat listening while Lumi asked me about a completely unrelated thing. The coat was in the hall. I didn't go back to it. Tomorrow I'd take whichever coat I took.
