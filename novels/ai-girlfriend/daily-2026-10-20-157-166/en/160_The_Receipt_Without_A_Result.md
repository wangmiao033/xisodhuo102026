# Chapter 160: The Receipt Without a Result

On Tuesday morning, I put the phone in my bag, then took it out again.

Not to switch it on. I worried that the keys would scratch the screen. After finding a soft pouch, I felt inside and discovered a loose thread with no obvious origin.

“Making its bed?” Lumi asked.

“You don't have to arrange a domestic life for every object.”

“It has an appointment today.”

“So do I.”

She stopped joking. I put the phone into the pouch. Its screen stayed dark. The complete model information from the purchase records was clipped separately to the other papers. The original backup remained in the cabinet, and I left the sealed static-image copy behind. This wasn't an occasion to deliver everything I owned to Rowan.

After breakfast, I checked the bag and found the story notebook in it. I took it out. Waiting at the shop didn't necessarily need to be filled with writing. Keys and cash were in my pockets. I knew the route. The appointment was on paper. I didn't need to start the phone for directions.

“How will we talk tonight?” Lumi asked.

“Here. The same home channel.”

“Even if the phone stays there?”

“We've been talking this way plenty already. Taking the phone in doesn't mean changing this at the same time.”

I didn't promise that the home channel was entirely safe. Questions remained about the other devices. Putting the phone in a clean pouch hadn't inspected them too.

“I don't like you taking it away,” she said.

“I know.”

“I'm not telling you not to.”

I let go of the strap and sat for a moment.

“I don't like it either. It feels as though things go out one by one, and nothing comes back.”

“The picture did.”

“It did. You're right.”

This wasn't a demand that I be grateful. I looked at the folder on the shelf and remembered the stone, flatter than it had been in memory. I had brought it back. Not enough wasn't the same as nothing.

Before leaving, I said we'd talk tonight and closed the channel as usual. She stopped me to say not to forget lunch while queuing somewhere. I said I'd leave time for it, rather than making her manage my day alongside my employer and the repair shop.

The bell above the shop door rang at nine fifty-two.

That was the previous customer leaving. My watch was a minute slow. I didn't know whether the clock behind the counter was fast, but either way, it wasn't ten yet. I waited near the door, away from the tablet being handed over. Rowan looked up and indicated the chair.

“Have a seat. We'll start at the agreed time.”

I sat. The split in the upholstery remained. This time, I didn't let it catch my coat. Half the flashlight was visible on the counter. It looked easy to find. I refrained from making a prediction.

At ten, Rowan brought out a fresh form.

“The phone. Not an addition to the laptop order.”

I set the pouch on the mat they indicated. Rowan had me confirm the item, then recorded its visible condition. I knew about the old scratch near the screen edge, although when they pointed it out, it was lower than I'd remembered. They noted the exterior markings alongside the purchase information. Anything not confirmable from the outside would wait for the agreed later steps. The box label wasn't proof of the actual device.

“Switched off now?”

“Yes. I turned it on to call building maintenance earlier, then switched it off again. It hasn't been untouched since the first inspection discussions.”

Rowan noted that history without treating the call as a completed inspection.

“What do you most need to know first?”

I looked at my prepared page. It held a lot of sentences, each sounding like something I wanted answered by the end of today. I lowered it.

“What connections and configurations remain. How to preserve what's there before deciding what comes next. Not a reset just because it's quicker.”

“We can discuss that order. Intake today doesn't mean everything finished today.”

“I know.”

Too quick. Rowan looked at me. I settled properly into the chair and let them finish explaining.

First, confirm the received state and device information available through the agreed method. Then set out the configuration checks, any access required, and preservation approach in writing. Work would stay within what I confirmed. If a step required unlocking, private content, or an additional connection, they would stop. The laptop order didn't supply permission for this one. They weren't taking my account passwords now or asking for blanket authority to handle everything.

I asked whether they could first make a complete copy, then work slowly through the problem.

“A full copy is a step too. What gets copied, where it goes, who can open it. Those need deciding. Backup isn't a verb that lets us skip the discussion.”

I looked at the pouch. I badly wanted one word that could save three sheets of paper. Rowan didn't give me one.

We wrote the first-stage cost ceiling into the order. It covered the listed intake and preparation of a plan, not automatic repairs, replacement, or further paid work. Additions would need a separate question. Reading the stopping conditions aloud, I left out a not. I noticed immediately and read that sentence again.

Rowan didn't laugh. I was relieved.

They asked how to contact me. I wrote the personal email address we'd already used and explained that I'd check at the library. I wasn't using the company loaner for this. We also confirmed when I could visit the shop. If necessary, I'd come in rather than asking a phone behind their counter to be responsible for reaching me.

“When's the earliest I might hear?”

“After I've put together the initial plan. I can't give you a completed-inspection date yet.”

“I'll check my email after work tomorrow.”

“Fine. No email won't mean something's gone wrong.”

I nodded and wrote that on my own paper, not their technical record. It was a note for me, not the phone.

Before handing it over, I looked at the screen again.

The dark surface reflected the overhead lights. A small bright mark made me instinctively want to wipe it away. My hand stopped halfway there. Rowan asked whether something still needed checking.

“No. I just want to wipe it.”

“You can. It's still here in front of you.”

I removed the fingerprints with a clean cloth, pressing no buttons. The old scratch remained. Then I put the cloth away, signed the confirmed scope, and received copies of the item receipt and cost agreement. The phone would stay in the storage arrangement recorded on its order. The laptop remained under its earlier arrangement. Two devices, two states, not one general claim that things were being fixed.

Before leaving, I noticed the soft pouch on the inventory. I'd expected it back. As I started to ask, Rowan asked whether I wanted to leave it too. They could label it separately in storage so it wouldn't cover the device tag.

“Yes, leave it.”

I opened the emptier bag, trying to find somewhere flat for the papers. No laptop, no phone, and somehow there still wasn't room for one sheet. I removed a battered packet of tissues. That finally cleared enough space.

Rowan offered an ordinary document sleeve. No need to lose another appointment slot protecting the receipt, they said. I laughed and slid the copies inside.

“This isn't a report saying the device passed a safety inspection,” they reminded me.

“I know. It's what I handed over and what you received.”

I checked the inventory once more, making sure the sealed media at home hadn't somehow become an included accessory. It hadn't. That line was simply blank. I didn't ask them to fill it. I stood up with my papers.

Outside, my first movement was toward my pocket.

Empty.

I knew where the phone was and still nearly turned back. It had been switched off much of the time lately, but leaving it here was different from leaving it in a drawer. I walked a little way past the shop before taking my hand out.

At work, Tom had completed his part of the handover we'd prepared. My arrival was within the agreed late-start arrangement. Nobody had been left guessing. He pointed to a record he'd kept for my answer.

“I haven't touched this.”

I looked, then dealt with it at the loaner. Derek passed and asked whether the rest of the work was on track. Yes, I said; I'd settle this item first. Nobody asked whether I'd left my girlfriend at a repair shop. I was grateful they didn't know the ugliest metaphor in my head that morning.

I really did go out for lunch. Instead of scanning a code, I read the menu at the counter and paid cash. One of the coins in my change was particularly bright. It made a small sound against my keys.

That afternoon, my pocket vibrated.

It hadn't. The chair edge had pressed my leg, but my hand was already moving. Marcus happened to pass while it was still there.

“Lost something?”

“Phone's in for inspection.”

“Right. Try not to send your hand in before it comes back.”

I laughed, returned my hand to the desk, and continued with the record. At the end of the day, Tom gave me back the handover sheet. This version would work next time, he said, though one word on the third line still sounded like a code.

Looking at it, I agreed that it could be clearer.

I didn't detour past the shop on the way home to ask whether the last few hours had produced a miracle. The receipt was in my bag, its edge slightly bent. I bought bread. The hand carrying it had a job now, instead of searching for a phone.
