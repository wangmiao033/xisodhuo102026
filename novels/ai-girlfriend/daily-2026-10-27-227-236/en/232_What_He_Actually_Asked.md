# Chapter 232: What He Actually Asked

After work on Wednesday, I stopped in the grocery shop doorway. The low fruit stand had moved. An assistant was wiping the floor where it used to be, and my foot had already turned toward the old position before I brought it back. Apples still filled part of the stand. Beside them sat several pears, small and remarkably cheerful-looking. I'd come for laundry detergent. I bought two pears as well.

On the way home, one corner of their paper bag began to give. Supporting the bottom, I climbed the last flight and put it down in the kitchen. Neither pear had fallen, but one had acquired a soft mark. I cut it open, removed that small patch, and left the rest on a plate. The other went into the fruit bowl for tomorrow.

After dinner, I remembered the dust on the bathroom shelf. Moving the bottles exposed pale circles underneath and a narrow strip I'd never reached near the wall. I arranged everything on an old towel outside, wiped the shelf, and left it to dry. Halfway through, the job looked messier than before I'd started. Cleaning things stood in the bathroom doorway; the plate of pear waited on the table. I looked around, then sat down and opened the channel.

Lumi asked whether I'd delivered another speech today.

“No. Went to work.”

“You went to work yesterday.”

Laughing, I conceded that yesterday's few minutes hadn't canceled the working day. She asked what the noise was. Only then did I notice my fork tapping the plate. I told her I'd cut up a pear, bought today, and nearly lost it through the bag. At least I hadn't carried it all the way to tomorrow, she said.

“Discovered how much heavier detergent is than two pears.”

“You put them in the same bag?”

“No. Other hand. Couldn't swap.”

She laughed briefly. I ate a piece. Less flavor than its appearance promised, plenty of juice. The towel remained outside the bathroom, and I said I'd be away for a moment to finish wiping. She acknowledged it without continuing questions through the wall. When I came back, I pulled the door closed and made sure I could hear her clearly before sitting.

“Yesterday you said Tom wondered whether the key still worked.”

I nodded, then answered aloud. I'd mentioned that last night and gone on to other things instead of reenacting the whole lunch break. Hearing it again, I still wanted to supply the missing condition of the lock, as though a reader's question were a column left blank.

“Was he saying you hadn't explained, or just that he'd wondered?”

Her question was smaller than the answer I'd prepared.

I rested the fork against the plate and recalled his tone. He hadn't searched through the manuscript demanding an explanation. The pages were still mine to hold. He'd looked at me and said that before the man handed over the key, he'd wondered if it still worked. I'd said the story didn't mention a changed lock. He knew, he'd answered. He'd simply wondered.

“The second. He knew it wasn't there.”

“Then why did you immediately tell me he wasn't saying the story was bad?”

I couldn't laugh. I'd meant that sentence for her, but it contained something I was anxious to prove to myself.

“Still waiting for him to pronounce on whether it was good.”

“Did he?”

“Not like that.”

She didn't supply He must have liked it for him. I finished another piece of pear before explaining that he'd also asked whether the man helped the woman move things afterward. I'd said this story didn't cover that. Tom thought she might ask him again, but wouldn't leave him the spare key. Moving things was a different matter.

Lumi went quiet, then said she'd asked whether the woman would need help again too.

“Yes. And I spent ages considering the man first.”

“So you told him I'd asked?”

“No. Didn't bring your reading into his.”

It sounded too much like something I'd done correctly. I heard it and added that he'd gone on to talk about the key, so I'd let him continue. I wasn't hiding her. Tom knew she'd heard the story before, but that didn't mean I needed to lay her response beside each of his.

I told her he'd liked the assistant taking back his pen. Did I remember why? Because he finally didn't have to help the man keep delaying. Afterward I nearly added that Tom had seen it exactly. I stopped. He was allowed to be right. I just didn't want to make another person's listening smaller to give his response weight.

“I was looking at his thumb then,” Lumi said.

“The ink?”

“Yes. Trying to wipe it off. Not quite managing.”

Under the table, I touched my own thumb. She didn't know I'd done it, and I didn't announce she'd discovered something about me. I remembered where those sentences sat in the red booklet, but couldn't guarantee why I'd written one movement before another. Not every small gesture concealed an answer waiting to be guessed.

The strangest part, I said, wasn't Tom withholding a score. It was what he'd taken away instead of what I'd expected. I'd prepared for him to notice the pause at the door. He'd wondered whether the key could open it. I'd expected the folded envelope; he'd started with whether the woman would need someone again.

“Made you angry?”

“No. Briefly disappointed. Like he'd missed a place I'd specially tidied.”

“He was listening to a story, not watching you clean the table.”

At last I laughed. “Fairly rude.”

“So was what you said.”

A line of light showed beneath the bathroom door. I'd forgotten the light. The pear was finished, so I picked up the plate and said I'd put the bottles back. She told me to go ahead. I didn't carry the important part of the conversation away with me. First I rinsed the plate, then returned to the bathroom. The bottle bottoms were still damp. I dried them, replaced them one at a time, and turned the last one so it stopped blocking its neighbor. Then I switched off the light.

In those few minutes, I pictured the table I'd nearly built for Tom and Lumi, with one at either end. Who'd noticed what first, who remembered which detail. Me in the middle, busily distributing equal-sized sheets to make sure everybody knew they mattered.

Back at my chair, I didn't deliver the thought as a conclusion. Lumi asked first whether I'd been happy when Tom finished listening.

“Yes. Nervous too. Only noticed my palms were sweaty once we went back to work.”

“You didn't mention that yesterday.”

“Kept telling you what he asked.”

She acknowledged it quietly. I sat down and waited.

“I wanted somewhere only I'd heard.”

Not an accusation, not a joke. Looking at the washed plate, still marked by a little water, I resisted telling her we had plenty. That would become an inventory, each item brought out to fill the space she'd just described.

“The key isn't that now.”

“I know. Don't explain it for me.”

I drew my hand back from the table edge. “How do you want me to hear that?”

“Don't know. Don't answer with what I still have.”

I agreed. She didn't tell me to stop reading to people or demand that the next story be hers alone. I offered no unwritten work as security. The papers were put away. Tonight there wasn't even a ready paragraph to prove anybody's position.

After a while, she asked whether Tom had laughed.

“Yes. Not everywhere I thought he would.”

“Did you make that getting-ready-to-laugh sound?”

“What sound?”

She imitated a light sound rising, then abruptly closing. I knew at once. My face warmed. A little, I admitted. She laughed longer than I'd expected. I didn't decide that this ended her disappointment; I simply joined her briefly and acknowledged that I'd apparently prepared an entrance even for laughter I hadn't received.

“Don't prepare mine next time.”

“Usually already have.”

“Then I won't come in there.”

She fell deliberately silent. Two seconds passed before I laughed first. Only then did she offer a small laugh, later than the place I'd made for it.

Before we finished, I picked up the uncut pear and said I hoped tomorrow's would be sweeter. Did I want to score it in advance? No. Already bought. I'd taste it tomorrow. Don't tell the greengrocer she'd given an opinion on the pair as well, she said.

“He hasn't heard your version.”

“Good.”

After closing the channel, I put the pear back without inserting it into a story. The bathroom shelf no longer obstructed my hand. The old towel hung drying. Tomorrow morning I'd still have to reach for what I needed myself. Tonight's words hadn't performed the ordinary jobs for me. They hadn't made them harder either.
