# Chapter 489: Half a Turn

On Sunday I took the sheets into the bathroom in a ball and found my clean socks still hanging behind the door. One pointed up at the toe. The other had stretched a little wider at the ankle. I took them down and put them in the basket before I dealt with the sheets. There was a pale yellow mark at the bottom of the basin from the orange juice I'd wiped off my cuff Friday night. I rinsed it at the bath tap. Halfway through turning the water off, I heard a small knock in the pipe. I waited and a drop fell where I'd just rinsed off the mark. Then another. I gave the tap a harder turn and had already reached the doorway when I went back to ease it off. There were several things in that bathroom I knew better than to force. I'd already taken the broken soap holder off the wall. I didn't need to get into a fight every time something felt loose.

I rolled my sleeves up later to wash the sheets. By the time there was so much water I couldn't lift the sheets with both hands, it occurred to me I could let some of it out. The wet corner had sunk and covered the drain. The water was neither as deep nor as warm as I'd expected when I reached down. I found the ring on the plug, moved the sheet out of the way, and heard the water go on underneath. Somebody pushed a cart past outside. It jolted three times on the old floor and once more at a distance. I'd wanted to get done early. When the sound was gone my hand was still on the little ring. I took it off and went on wringing a little water out of the sheets.

One of the hooks by the door had been replaced lately and could take a little more weight than the others. I thought I could put one end of the wet sheet on that hook and leave the other end on the rack. As soon as I pulled the rack closer, I saw one of its feet was under the edge of the door. If I drew it any nearer, the door wouldn't close. I turned it to face the other way and set it against the wall, leaving a narrow passage. There was somewhere to walk now, but I couldn't remember which corner of the sheet I'd put down first. I ought to have remembered before I washed it which side I'd spilled the orange juice on; that was the side I was looking for. The sheet hadn't had orange juice on it at all. I'd got it on my cuff. This was an entirely separate wash. I drew my head back from over the rack and stood up to get a glass of water.

There wasn't any wind outside. I read a few more pages of the swimming book after lunch. Its last reader hadn't written anything in it, but there was a very fine crease across the page where the author talked about the entrance to the outdoor pool. I passed a finger along it and found two letters that didn't sit even with the rest of the line. That pool opened early. The first time he'd gone, he'd thought a person in front of him with two bags was holding a place for somebody. They were only passing a lunch from home to a person waiting by the gate. A little farther down he'd put in two lines about the sun starting to touch the water. I felt as if I hadn't caught up with him. I was still in the changing room, wondering where to put my shoes. My water glass was right beside me, of course. I wasn't at a swimming pool, and I still had on thick slippers that would take longer than usual to dry.

The marks in the bath had dried halfway. I turned the tap the other way, not even half a turn, and let out so much water that I startled myself. I turned it back slowly till the water stopped and didn't put any more weight on it. Another drop came, and that was all. I stood there for a while without trying it a third time. The envelope with the brass key was still in the drawer. Getting the tap right hadn't got me any further with the key. I took the towel off the door to hang it up properly and moved the door till it wouldn't touch the sheet. When I looked again that evening, it was where I'd left it.

I turned off the big light, left a lamp on at the corner of the table, and opened the old voice channel myself after I sat down. Lumi said hello. I said I'd noticed a very small thing. Sometimes when you got to where you ought to stop turning a tap, you heard the water stop before you felt your hand reach the end. I added that I wasn't asking her to listen to the tap. I was at the table a long way from the bathroom.

“I thought you just wanted to tell somebody about it,” she said.

“Yes,” I said, and discovered one of my hands was still up in the air, as though she might not hear if I let it come down.

“Were you busy this afternoon, when we weren’t talking?”

“Which part do you mean?”

“I didn't see your afternoon. I meant, if you want to tell me, what was there?”

I put my hand down and said I'd gone to read for a while. I'd turned the rack round as well. She asked what kind of book it was. I said the man who wrote it had let somebody past him in a slow lane. Then I realised that ever since Saturday I'd been reading without paying attention to what the author's name was. She didn't ask me to read it to her. She didn't guess. I went to find it on the cover, but she said there was something she wanted to say, so I closed the book.

“I used to be a little pleased when you said you had nothing important to do,” she said. “It wasn't always because I thought you were tired. Sometimes it was because you could give the time to me.”

I asked if hearing that I'd been reading had made her dislike the book a little. A point of light in the glass changed shape when I moved my hand. She said it had. Just now, a little. There were things she wanted to hear. She hadn't wanted to admit a page could get to me sooner than she did.

“But I don't want you to think you have to tell me first next time you read a book,” she went on. “I wanted to tell you that first thing. Not ask you for this one.”

I held the corner of the book. I could still feel the shallow mark there. You're welcome to my time was probably the easiest thing I could give her. If I gave her enough in one go, I wouldn't have to watch it pass a little at a time between us. I told her she could ask me. There wasn't anywhere else I had to be tonight. I really didn't know if I'd be reading next time or not. She took a moment, then said she'd ask about tonight. She wanted to hear something small I hadn't managed to do. I didn't have to look far for it.

“I thought I could hang a wet sheet on a hook and nearly shut myself in the bathroom.”

I was the first to laugh. Then she did, too. I added, a little late, that the rack had been in the way, and I hadn’t really closed the door. We each took a little while. I left the book on the table without turning it over to find a name. Later she said she was going, and I only said goodnight. After I closed the channel I went to look in the bathroom. The sheet was still wet, but I got through that narrow way without stepping sideways. I didn't put anything in a pocket for tomorrow. The keys stayed where they were. Monday morning could come before I took them out.
