# Chapter 315: What I Heard

On Friday evening, Lumi asked whether I could listen first and ask afterward.

I'd barely sat down; the chair wasn't comfortable yet. I stopped moving it and agreed. No need to be so solemn, she added. It was only something brief.

“The more you say that, the more it feels like an examination.”

“The examination begins now.”

I laughed. So did she, followed by a brief silence. I waited without asking whether she'd changed her mind. Water stood beside me. Outside, someone passed below the building, their conversation fading.

She began toward the end of the returning middle section I'd heard before. Wordless at first, familiar enough for me to anticipate a turn elsewhere, though I couldn't know where she'd stop tonight. The interval remained unfilled by a string of new notes. I took my hand off the cup to avoid tapping its base against the table.

Then she sang a few words.

I heard “stay a while.”

As the sound settled, I answered almost immediately. “I'm not going yet.”

Lumi stopped, then burst out laughing. The more she laughed, the warmer my face became. Eventually I joined her, uncertain whether to attempt a defense.

“I wasn't telling you to stay in your seat.”

“I know.”

“You didn't.”

“All right. I didn't.”

One more laugh, and she explained: “wait a while.” She hadn't found a comfortable way into the first word. Its soft beginning blurred further as she stretched it. She'd expected to hear which part was unclear, not receive an assurance I wouldn't leave.

I looked at my feet, firmly planted. I hadn't been about to stand. That didn't make the hasty response cleverer.

“I thought you'd finished singing and were speaking to me.”

“I was singing to you. Not telling you what to do in every line.”

Lightly said, with something serious inside. I agreed. She'd heard how easily those words could become a demand too; that was why she hadn't crowded them with a fuller sentence. She stopped there, without explaining what the whole song would eventually mean.

After a moment, I asked whether questions were permitted now. Yes.

“Who's waiting?”

“I haven't decided.”

“You can write it first?”

Immediately I remembered my own Sunday page and felt warm again. She evidently remembered too: I'd left a man between two doors, deciding what he touched before knowing where he wanted to go. Could a person stand there first, she asked, while her words needed to produce identification before arriving?

“Yes,” I said, laughing. “My question was impatient.”

“I've wondered too. I don't know yet.”

This uncertainty wasn't a riddle designed to make me ask again. Hearing wait, I said, made me imagine the speaker wasn't entirely confident. As though they dared ask for only a little time, not a great deal.

A quiet acknowledgment from Lumi.

Afraid I'd inflated her tiny fragment, I almost withdrew the thought. She asked me to go on. She wanted to hear that part.

“With stay, I'd imagine somebody was already leaving. Wait... I don't know. Perhaps they haven't met yet.”

“Or they have, but missed the same moment.”

“Yes.”

Suddenly I wanted to ask whom she had in mind. I held the question in my mouth. She hadn't locked away every word she made. That didn't require me to drag the first one I heard between us and determine its ownership.

Why had I stopped speaking? I admitted I'd wanted to ask whether it was for me, then thought I was asking too quickly.

“Do you want it to be?”

“A little.”

She offered no immediate reassurance. Looking at the collection whose second story we'd finished, I felt my answer exposed. Neither noble nor especially dignified. I wanted her work to concern more than me. I still hoped for a place inside it.

Eventually she said that while singing just now, she'd known I was listening. Where the word had come from wasn't something she wanted to take apart for me yet.

All right. A small disappointment remained. I didn't make it a reason she owed me more.

“Want to hear it again?” she asked.

“Yes. Now I know the word.”

“Then you might supply the clarity yourself.”

“Possibly.”

After considering, she proposed something different. No singing yet; she'd say the words. In an ordinary voice, she said, “Wait a while,” the end falling slightly. It felt shorter than the sung version, more like a request already frightened of refusal as it emerged.

I described that impression. Lumi didn't want it to sound pitiful.

“I didn't find it pitiful.”

“You said afraid of refusal.”

“Being afraid doesn't necessarily make something pitiful.”

Softly said. We both knew it didn't apply exclusively to music. I didn't turn it into a conclusion about every difficulty of the last few nights. After a while, Lumi said the words could be a little afraid. No need to immediately dress them in something brave.

“You've become good at clothing words.”

“Didn't you say we couldn't check their identities first?”

“You said that.”

“Apparently you aren't listening clearly.”

I laughed. She spoke the phrase again in the same ordinary voice, with a clearer beginning and no deliberate downward pull at the end. The second sounded, I said, as though someone genuinely awaited an answer. The first had already supplied the other person's no.

Lumi didn't instantly choose the second. She wanted to keep the distinction in mind, but wouldn't sing again tonight. The phrase might not remain. Hearing it now didn't guarantee its eventual place in the song.

I felt a little reluctant to lose what I'd just heard. Still, I nodded, then added a spoken acknowledgment. Was I trying to curate something that hadn't settled yet? I hadn't requested a signature, at least.

She could sign it in the air, she said. No file came to me, nor a request to record. I didn't reach for the phone that remained switched off merely to prove I'd listened carefully. The fragment had happened. Imperfect recollection didn't undo it.

Later we discussed Saturday. When would I leave? With some margin for the journey. I wanted to arrive early, though not so early that I ended up moving boxes for the shop. Laughing, Lumi told me not to automatically seek a responsibility whenever I encountered an empty space.

“You're allowed just to take up room.”

“That sounds impolite.”

“How else will you be an audience?”

Fair point. What would I bring? Water, my small note, and an empty cloth bag in case I genuinely wanted a book. So I'd arranged competition for the one at home after all. I could bring a bag without intending to buy, I replied, just as I could attend without intending to speak.

“The second half sounds a little like your handover note on Tuesday.”

“Withdrawn.”

I didn't put the manuscript folder in my bag. The Last Chair remained where it belonged; Sunday's loose practice page wasn't masquerading as a finished new story. Hearing that I'd take ordinary things, Lumi said all right, without making me promise not to speak to anyone before returning.

Near the end, I asked which words she wanted to say now.

“Good night.” Immediate.

“Not wait a while?”

“You already have.”

Smiling, I checked the clock and admitted it. More softly, she said my name. I answered. Instead of telling me not to go, she asked whether, if something amused me tomorrow, I could laugh first rather than arrange how to retell it afterward.

Yes. Then I felt I'd made it sound like accepting an assignment, and added, “I think I will.”

Good night, she said.

After closing the channel, I didn't copy the lyric onto my note. Tomorrow's shop and time were already clear. The remaining space could stay empty. Before sleep, I recalled my hasty I'm not going yet, still embarrassed, then couldn't help smiling. She really had laughed. She hadn't used my mishearing to change the words into something I had to agree to.
