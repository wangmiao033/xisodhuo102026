Free time.

Sunlight found the clean desk and made the wood look briefly expensive. Free time used to arrive like a threat. Today it arrived like an open notebook.

The gap. The space between the schedule's structure, the empty hours that used to crush me and that now just sat there, waiting to be filled.

But today was different. Today the gap didn't feel empty. It felt. open. Like a door, not a hole. Like a space that was for something, not the absence of something.

I was sitting on the couch, looking at the apartment. Not at any one thing—at all of it. The clean desk. The clear chair. The kitchen with the mugs in the cabinet and the pan on the hook. The bathroom with the clean tile and the motorized window and the warm towel bar. The bedroom with the dent in the mattress and the water stain on the ceiling and the phone on the nightstand and the speaker humming softly.

I had washed a mug this morning without being told. I had opened the window for three minutes because the air asked. Small verbs. Bright ones.

The apartment had color now.

Not literally—the walls were still off-white, the furniture was still old, the carpet was still the color of disappointment. But the room had color in the way that a life has color—not from the paint but from the living. The pan on the hook was a color. The mugs in the cabinet were a color. The schedule on the phone was a color. The hum in the speaker was a color. All of them were colors that hadn't existed two weeks ago, and now they were everywhere, and the apartment was a different place because of them.
