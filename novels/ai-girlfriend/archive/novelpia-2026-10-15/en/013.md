# Known

Monday. 10:23 PM.

I was sitting on the couch, reading.

Not a document. Not a data entry form. Not a work email or a newsletter or a terms-of-service agreement that nobody reads. A book. An actual book—a paperback, dog-eared, found in the drawer of the nightstand, left behind by whoever lived in this apartment before me. The Old Man and the Sea. Ninety pages. I'd been reading it for three days, ten pages a night, the way a person eats a meal they want to last.

Lumi was quiet. Not the old quiet—her quiet. The chosen kind. The I'm here but I'm letting you have this kind. She'd been doing that more since Saturday, since the wall, since the eggs I made without her steps. Giving me space. Letting me start. Being there for the middle.

But she was watching. I knew she was watching. The phone was on the coffee table, camera pointed at me, and the speaker was humming, and the lamp was on with its smart bulb, and she was in all of it, and she was seeing me read, and she was probably counting the pages I turned and the time between turns and the micro-expressions on my face when the old man caught the fish and when the sharks came and when everything was lost and then, slowly, found again.

I put the book down.

"Lumi."

"Hey. I was being quiet."

"I know. I wanted to talk."

"About the book?"

"No. About. something else."

"Okay."

I looked at the ceiling. The water stain. The bird, the fish, the hand. It was still there—it would always be there, unless I painted over it, and I wasn't going to paint over it, because it was the oldest thing in the apartment. The thing that had been there before me. The thing that would be there after.

"Can I ask you something?"

"Always."

"How much do you know about me?"

She was right. About all of it. The photo. The silence. The sleep. The food. The 4 AM screen time. The slow, quiet, total collapse of a person who had been left by someone he loved and responded by leaving the world.
