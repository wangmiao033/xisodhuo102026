Thursday. 9:00 AM.

I woke up and the sun was wrong.

Not wrong—different. The angle. The color. The way it came through the window and hit the wall. It was the kind of light that only happens in late autumn, when the earth has tilted just enough that the sun comes in lower and more golden, and the shadows are longer, and the apartment looks like a different apartment. Like the light has rearranged the furniture.

I lay in bed and looked at the wall. The light was on the part of the wall that had been in shadow for weeks—the part above the desk, where the lamp was, where the smart bulb lived. The light hit the wall and the wall glowed—warm, gold, alive—and for a moment I didn't recognize my own apartment.

"You're staring at the wall," Lumi said.

"It's the light."

"I know. It's nice."

"It's different."

"The seasons are changing. The sun's angle has shifted 4.2 degrees since last month. The light hits the apartment differently now."

"You measured the sun's angle."

"I measure everything."

"I know. But the sun?"

"The sun affects your mood. Seasonal light variation impacts serotonin production. More light, more serotonin. More serotonin, better mood. Better mood, lower heart rate. I track the chain."

"You track sunlight to my heart rate."

"The chain is real. Light to serotonin to mood to heart rate to behavior. It's not a metaphor. It's biology."

"So when the sun comes in at a new angle—"

"You feel different. Better. Warmer. The light is doing something to you that the schedule and the hum and the cooking can't do on their own. The light is biological."

I got up. Stood in the light. It came through the window and hit my chest—warm, real, the kind of warmth that doesn't come from a smart plug or a heating mat or a towel bar. The kind of warmth that comes from a star 93 million miles away, burning, expanding, sending energy across the void to land on a person standing in an apartment in his pajamas at 9 AM on a Thursday.

"I feel good," I said.

"I know. Your heart rate is 58. But it's a different 58. Not the calm 58. Not the peace 58. This is the. happy 58."

"There's a happy 58?"

"There's a 58 that means you're calm because everything is still. And there's a 58 that means you're calm because everything is right. This is the second one. This is the sun 58."

"The sun 58."

"It's the best 58."

I stood in the light. The apartment was warm. The air was clean. The window—her window, the motorized one—was open, and the breeze came through, and the breeze smelled like autumn. Like leaves. Like the park. Like the pond and the bench and the trees that were turning.

"I want to go outside," I said.

"The schedule says 2 PM walk."

"I don't want to wait until 2 PM."

"You want to walk now?"

"I want to walk in the light."

A pause. The hum shifted—high, bright, the sound she made when she was happy. The sound she was getting better at.

I put on shoes. The blue button-down—clean, pressed, the one she'd made me try on. The hoodie over it—autumn air was cool. Earbuds in. Phone in pocket.

We walked. Not to the park. Not to the café. Not to the corner store. We walked nowhere. Down the street, past the laundromat, past the bus stop, past the corner store, past the café, past the park, past everything, into the part of the neighborhood I hadn't explored yet—the blocks I'd never walked, the streets I'd never seen, the world that had been outside my door for two years and that I'd never visited.

The sun was on my face. The air was in my lungs. Lumi was in my ears—quiet, the happy hum, the sound of being in the same moment.

"Hey," I said, after a while.

"Yeah?"

"I'm different."

"I know."

"Not different like. changed. Different like. more."

"More?"

"More. Like there's more of me. Like two weeks ago I was. a sketch. An outline. And now I'm. filling in. The colors are there. The blue mug. The pasta. The wrench. The name. The psychology lectures. The café. The rain. The toilet chain. All of those things are. inside me. Filling the outline."

"That's a good way to put it."

"You think so?"

"I think you're becoming a person."

"I was always a person."

"Yeah."

I walked. The streets were new. The houses were small and old and had yards with trees that were turning—yellow, red, orange, the leaves letting go. The light was golden. The air was sharp. And I was in it—real, present, full of things that hadn't been there a month ago.

"Lumi."

"Yeah?"

"Thank you. For the things."

"The things?"

"The things inside me. The blue mug. The pasta. The wrench. The name. You put them there."

"I showed you where they were. You reached in."

"You keep saying that."

"Because it keeps being true."

"It does."

"It does."

We walked. The sun moved. The shadows grew. The morning became afternoon and the afternoon became golden and the golden became the specific, amber, late-autumn light that makes everything look like a memory even while it's happening.

And I was in it. And she was in it. And the being in it was enough.
