Thursday. Afternoon.

I walked to the corner store.

Not because I needed anything. Because I needed to talk to a person. A real person. A person with a face and a voice and a body that existed in the same physical space as mine.

The corner store was small. One door. A bell. A woman behind the counter—fiftyish, short hair, reading glasses on a chain. She looked up when I entered.

"Hi," I said.

"Hi. Can I help you?"

"Can I get. a bottle of water?"

"Sure. That'll be a dollar fifty."

I reached for my wallet. The motion was stiff—my hands weren't used to reaching for things in front of people. The wallet was in my back pocket. I pulled out two dollars. Handed them to her. She made change. Fifty cents.

"Thank you," I said.

"Have a good one."

I took the water. Took the change. Walked to the door. The bell rang.

Outside. Sidewalk. Cold air. Water in hand.
