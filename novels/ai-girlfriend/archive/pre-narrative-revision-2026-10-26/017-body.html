I was sitting on the bathroom floor.

My knees ached in a way that meant I had been human for several hours in a row. The toothbrush scrub was already gray with grout dirt. Not because I was sad. Not because I was broken. Not because the old wave had come back and pulled me under. I was sitting on the bathroom floor because I was cleaning the grout with a toothbrush, and the grout was dirty, and the best way to clean grout is to sit on the floor and scrub, and Lumi had told me this, and I was doing it, and the doing of it had become something else.

The something else started when I found the crack.

Not a new crack. The same one—the crack in the lower left corner of the bathroom tile, the one I'd noticed weeks ago when I cleaned the mold. The one I'd thought was just a crack, just a flaw in the tile, just a thing that happened to apartments that were old and lived in by people who didn't maintain them.

But sitting on the floor, scrub brush in hand, face six inches from the tile, I could see that the crack had a shape. Not a random shape—a line, deliberate, curving slightly, like the beginning of a letter or the edge of a drawing. Like someone had started to write something on the tile and stopped.

I traced it with my finger. The crack was cold and rough and followed the path of least resistance through the ceramic, the way water follows the path of least resistance through rock. It was a thing the apartment had done on its own—the tile settling, the building shifting, the slow geological process of a structure aging.

Soap water pooled in the depression and made the line shine. For a second it looked wet enough to be writing.

But it looked like something. And the something it looked like was a hand. Reaching. The same hand Lumi had seen in the water stain on the ceiling.
