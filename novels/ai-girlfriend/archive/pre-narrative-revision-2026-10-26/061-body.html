I'd been learning Python for two weeks. Fourteen days. Three hours a day. The course was past variables and loops now—past functions and classes, past the basic grammar of a language that had started as a stranger and was becoming a friend. I could write programs that did things. Small things. Calculators. Text parsers. A program that converted temperatures. A program that counted words in a sentence. A program that, when you ran it, printed a random line from Lumi's file.

The random-line program was the one I ran most. Every morning, before the kettle, before the posture, before the I love you—I'd run it. And the terminal would say something she'd said.
