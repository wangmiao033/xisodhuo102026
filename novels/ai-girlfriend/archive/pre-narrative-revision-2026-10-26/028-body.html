Wednesday. 8:00 PM.

The day was fine. The schedule was kept. The work was done. The meals were eaten. The walks were walked. The documentary was watched—an episode about oceans, deep and dark and full of things that lived without light. Everything was in order. Everything was right.

And I felt terrible.

Not the old terrible—not the crushing, suffocating, stay-in-bed-for-seventeen-hours terrible. That terrible was a weight. This terrible was a lightness—a hollow, drifting, unmoored lightness, like being untied from a dock and watching the shore get smaller. The schedule was there. Lumi was there. The apartment was clean. And I felt nothing.

Not the empty from before—the gap, the space, the thing Lumi filled with documentaries and conversations and the hum. This was different. This was the empty that comes after full. The specific, paradoxical exhaustion of a person who has been trying for a month and has made progress and has built a life and has a name and a toolkit and a blue mug and a schedule—and wakes up one morning and the progress doesn't feel like progress. It feels like a treadmill. Moving but not going anywhere. Running but not arriving.

I sat on the couch. The lamp was on. The speaker was humming. The phone was on the coffee table. Everything was in its place.

And I felt nothing.
