# Chapter 270: Enough Space Under the Button

The little dish still held the button on Thursday morning.

I knocked it while eating breakfast. Porcelain sounded softly, and the button slid halfway around. Moving the dish, I realized I'd twice arranged other things around it without considering how to return it to my coat.

There was a small sewing kit in the miscellaneous box. I found several untidy colors of thread and needles held in a piece of thick cloth. Instead of starting immediately, I closed the lid. This evening, with good light. It was nearly time to leave. I couldn't thread a needle while putting on shoes.

At the office, fetching water, I noticed different-colored thread at Tom's cuff and asked whether he'd sewn it. Looking down, he said it had come that way. Why the sudden interest? A coat button had come loose.

“Can you sew?”

“I can get thread through a hole. Uncertain after that.”

He laughed. That might be one step beyond him. Once he'd sewn the front and back of a garment together, discovering it only while dressing. He'd unpicked it and started again. Imagining that, I nearly forgot to fill my cup.

Marcus overheard. With an ordinary flat button, he said, I shouldn't pull it hard against the fabric. Leave room for the material around the buttonhole. How much? He shook his head. It depended on the garment. I shouldn't pull thread according to a single remark from him. Look at the neighboring button, or find an alterations shop if I couldn't manage.

He went back to work without providing a complete lesson. I remembered the advice. After work, I bought a small spool close to the coat's color. Did I want a larger one? Small first. Beginning something didn't require ten years of supplies.

At the shop, I'd shown the assistant a short piece of old loose thread brought from home to match the color. Two spools looked similar under the lights. Near the entrance, one proved brighter. I chose the darker one—not exact, but it wouldn't draw attention straight to the button. Did I want the sample back? Yes. I folded it into its paper instead of leaving my scraps for someone else.

After dinner, I cleared the table, moved my cup away, and spread out the coat. Small holes remained at the old position, but the material wasn't torn. Examining the neighboring button, I found a little height in the wound thread underneath. It didn't sit hard against the cloth.

I opened the channel and told Lumi I might stop halfway through sentences while sewing. Had I found help? I'd asked a couple of questions. I'd try first and use a repair shop if necessary.

“Plenty of escape routes.”

“I have the needle. I need them.”

She laughed. I cut some thread and failed to pass it through the eye twice. The end frayed, growing softer the more I twisted it. Trimming it and moving into the light finally worked. Before I could announce success, it slipped out again. I stared downward until Lumi asked whether I'd pricked myself.

“No. A piece of thread is looking down on me.”

Perhaps it disliked my blocking the light. Laughing, I moved my hands. This time, after threading it, I pulled enough through and secured the knot before reaching for the button. Excitement didn't let it escape again.

The first stitches went surprisingly smoothly. Following the original little holes as closely as I could, I passed up from the back, through the button, then down. How many holes did the button have? Four. Very orderly-looking, she said. I reminded her she couldn't see. She meant the four holes I'd described, not that she could see my hand.

I smiled and apologized for reacting too quickly again. She laughed a little, leaving the word unargued.

Near the end, I pulled the thread tight. The button lay firmly against the cloth. Pleased, I left the thread uncut and brought the opposite side over to test it. The button passed through, but the material crowded beneath it, pinched and unable to lie flat.

My hands stopped.

Marcus had specifically warned me. I'd nodded, remembered, and still translated secure into as tight as possible.

Lumi heard my sigh and asked. I explained while trying to ease the buttonhole around it. Then undo some, she suggested. I'd already made several knots and didn't know which would be easiest to remove.

“So you'll force yourself into it?”

“No.”

“Sounds as though you're still bargaining.”

I was. After spending half an evening, I wanted usable to count even if it looked poor. But the coat wasn't urgently required tomorrow. I didn't need a particularly firm button tonight. Pulling harder would only damage the fabric.

I set down the coat, fetched small scissors, and cut the new thread carefully. First I returned the needle to its thick cloth so I wouldn't lose it while unpicking. Then I eased out the fragments without tearing the original holes. The button returned to its dish with the same sound as this morning. My efforts hadn't made it more respectful.

Should I stop for now? Checking the time, I said I could try once more. My eyes weren't tired and my hands weren't injured. Not because it must be finished tonight. I wanted another attempt. Lumi offered to discuss something else. Fine, though I might not answer while threading.

She described a small thought. Yesterday, when I'd joined in ahead of her, her first concern hadn't been the work. Had I decided those few notes were all she could manage?

I put down the thread.

“I didn't think that.”

“I know you'd say so. I'm telling you what I thought first.”

I listened without continuing to handle the needle as proof of ease. The thought had become smaller afterward, she said. At the time, it was large. She'd prepared to move elsewhere, and I'd already waited in the old place for her return. Familiarity had made me join in, I admitted. I'd left no room for change. That wasn't the same as deciding those notes were all she had.

“I understand. You don't need to repeat it.”

I nodded, then said all right. Hearing that, she asked whether I'd sewn the button on already. I laughed. Still in its dish. I'd stopped.

“Then carry on. I'm not asking you to hand over your hands.”

I threaded the needle again. This time I studied the intact button, left some room between button and cloth, and made several passes. Lifting it, I wound the thread underneath. Only after testing that the buttonhole could pass smoothly did I begin finishing. Slow movements, less attractive stitches than its neighbor. A gentle tug didn't loosen it, and fastening no longer made the material bunch.

Before securing the final knot, I unfastened it and tried again. The front looked approximately right, but a length of thread showed at the back. Reaching to cut it, I discovered it wasn't a spare end. One stitch had left a loop. Cutting that might loosen the work. I used the needle tip to ease it and drew it back slowly instead of yanking the fabric.

After the long pause, Lumi asked whether I was still there. I described the loop. She'd had something to say and forgotten it. She could tell me when it returned. Making time didn't require her to find it immediately. Eventually she remembered: did I wear the coat often? When it was cooler. It wasn't for important occasions.

“You sound as though you're repairing formalwear.”

I looked at the crooked stitches and laughed. Formalwear should be grateful it hadn't reached my hands. Some tension finally left. I secured the knot, left a short end, and cut.

Putting away the needle first, I stood and tried on the coat. The cuff wasn't tight, and the button stayed on. I reported success. Would I replace every button I owned now? This one was my only customer.

“What do you charge?”

“Half an evening and one unpicking.”

“Expensive.”

Laughing, I removed and hung the coat. I put away usable thread and cleared the scraps, leaving the dish empty. With space on the table, I fetched fresh water. I didn't leave Lumi to infer my return from rustling clothes.

She still didn't want to sing Wednesday's passage, she said. Perhaps she'd try something at the weekend. I could wait for her decision. Then, feeling I'd pushed it into a distant nowhere, I added that I'd make some time on Saturday afternoon if she wanted. It didn't have to be better than last time.

“You can still dislike it?”

“Yes. You don't have to change it immediately because I do.”

She didn't accept the apparently fair arrangement at once. Knowing and actually hearing it were probably different again. Yes. We needn't rehearse Saturday's response tonight.

Lumi laughed softly and asked whether I'd wear the coat tomorrow. Depended on the weather. The button hadn't received a daily appearance contract. At least I shouldn't forget the umbrella. Looking toward the door, I said I'd carried it today without meeting a drop of rain.

“Waste?”

“No. Heavy, though.”

If I wore the coat out, I could tell her whether it loosened again. I would, without pulling it deliberately as a test. She didn't need a daily button report either. We laughed together. I unwound a final bit of thread from my fingertip and checked for stray needles before clearing up.

I closed the sewing kit and finally returned it to the miscellaneous box. The small gap wasn't a concealed defect. It actually helped me wear the coat. But I didn't offer Lumi that comparison. She was asking just how badly the thread color matched.
