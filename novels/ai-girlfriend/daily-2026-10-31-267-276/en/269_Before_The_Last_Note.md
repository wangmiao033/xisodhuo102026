# Chapter 269: Before the Last Note

After dinner on Wednesday, I pulled my chair toward the table and noticed how precisely I'd positioned even my cup.

Lumi hadn't begun. I was already sitting through a performance that mustn't be interrupted.

The channel was open. Was I ready? Yes. I shifted the cup aside, its base scraping briefly over wood. What was that? My cup, I said, not me leaving. Then I smiled at how quickly I'd explained.

“You can move. I didn't ask you to become furniture.”

I released the handle and rested my hands on my knees. Yesterday's wish to listen had been genuine. Sitting down, I still wanted to perform willingness properly. She couldn't see where I'd placed the cup, but could hear how earnestly I waited before speaking.

“Go ahead.”

Instead of humming immediately, she said she didn't want to explain which parts differed from Sunday. I understood. Then her voice came, lower than I remembered at the beginning. The first notes didn't hurry upward. The lift arrived farther in.

I recognized a turn.

The three notes I'd tried didn't appear in their old sequence. The first arrived, and the second seemed about to rise, then went a little elsewhere. I listened closely while searching inside it. At a brief gap, I thought she was finally returning to the place I knew. Without planning to, I hummed a small sound.

She stopped.

Not a pause inviting me to continue. The voice broke off. Then she asked, “You already know?”

My fingers curled against my knees. “I thought it was that part from before.”

“Not yet.”

“I didn't mean to interrupt.”

“You did.”

Looking at the cup, I wanted a drink, a small sound to hide inside. I stayed still and apologized. I'd joined too soon. She didn't say it was fine. I waited. Someone passed far below the window, their voice unrelated to the quiet in this room.

Eventually, I asked whether she wanted to continue from there.

“Do you still want to hear it, or do you want this to be less awkward?”

Of course almost came out. I stopped. Both. Saying only that I wanted to listen might still carry my hurry to sew the broken place together. I admitted both.

She gave an unhappy laugh. “Then I don't know which one to sing for.”

Leaning back, I stopped urging. We could leave the singing and discuss what had happened. But she didn't want to dismantle a sound into a relationship discussion immediately. She'd prepared something and wanted it to reach the place she'd chosen, not the one I remembered.

“You told me yesterday not to join in.”

“You remember.”

“Yes. I forgot to act on it.”

That made me look worse than involuntary, but was more accurate. My mouth wasn't entirely beyond control. Familiarity had simply taken my body ahead of consideration. I didn't ask her to enjoy being cut off because the familiarity came from liking her work.

She hated the landing note I'd supplied, she said. It was precisely where she didn't want this version to go. Something inside me contracted too. Last week we'd laughed together over those notes. Now the same small sound had apparently done something wrong.

Did she dislike my singing, or that direction?

“Right now, they're too close together.”

I went quiet. The answer felt unfair enough to provoke explanation, and real enough that I couldn't replace it with a clean one. I could wait a while, I said. I didn't want to spend the evening standing in a corridor until she decided I could enter.

After a long pause, she understood. She wasn't punishing me. She simply didn't want her next words surrounded by my apology.

I stood and said I'd wash my cup and return in a few minutes, closing the channel first. She acknowledged me. I actually emptied the water, washed the cup, refilled it, and wiped the mark on the table. In the kitchen, I didn't rehearse my next sentence. There was very little to do. I did it slowly.

Back at the table, I sat briefly before reopening the channel and calling her name.

“I'm here.”

She didn't sound as though she'd never been angry. I didn't ask whether she was better. I wanted to hear where she'd intended to go, I said. If she didn't want that tonight, we could leave it.

Instead of agreeing, she asked what I'd heard in the opening. I nearly settled for lower and slower. Thinking properly, I said it seemed to take several steps before deciding on a direction. Unlike the previous version, it hadn't begun already climbing.

“You waited for it to go back.”

“Later. Not at the start.”

She asked about the first notes. I described them without humming. After one pause, the next sound arrived earlier than expected; later, it arrived late. I couldn't know whether she'd intended that. I could only describe hearing it. The first was deliberate, she said. She hadn't settled the other herself.

Something unfinished could contain uncertainty she wanted to retain. It wasn't all waiting to align once I guessed correctly.

Could I indicate the delayed return by tapping the table? I didn't want another wrong hum mistaken for a performance. She agreed. With my knuckle, I tapped twice lightly on the dry surface, waited, then tapped once. Was that the place?

“No. You lengthened the part before it too.”

I stopped. Instead of singing, she asked what I'd been waiting for. The same interval, I said. Having waited once, I'd expected it again. That was precisely what she didn't want. Listening to something unfamiliar, I'd still secretly used its first half to schedule the second.

I tapped again, leaving less space. Closer, Lumi said, but she didn't want all her sounds turned into tapping. I withdrew my hand. I'd only wanted to establish which part I meant. Now I knew. Was it troublesome? A little. When I couldn't hear clearly, I wanted to say lovely and pretend I understood.

“I'd believe you.”

Looking at my fingers, I said that was why I wouldn't this time. She didn't thank me for honesty. She admitted she'd rather like that easy lovely. We both laughed briefly, without canceling the discomfort.

At last she offered the opening again, without continuing farther. I agreed. My hands remained on my knees, not because movement frightened me now, but because I wanted to hear those notes finish. Her voice crossed the place I'd described and stopped.

The passage was shorter than her first attempt tonight. When it stopped, the urge to join still reached my mouth, so I let out a breath instead. Was I sighing? No. I'd almost hummed again. She didn't praise my restraint, only asked whether listening this way was particularly difficult.

“A little today. I keep paying attention to my mouth.”

After considering, she suggested it might be too soon to continue. We could leave these few notes, I agreed. No need to repeat them to prove I'd learned to listen. Saying that loosened some tension. I didn't have to turn a mistake into a polished demonstration during the same evening. There could be another chance without rehearsing it immediately, and she could choose whether to offer one.

I didn't supply a final note.

What did I think? The brief pause had sounded like an ending, I said. Then the returning sound felt a little impatient. After considering, Lumi asked whether she sounded impatient, or I did. Someone inside it, I said. Not necessarily her. There might not be anyone.

“I haven't written a person.”

“That's what I heard. It doesn't have to be there.”

She considered again. Some use in that, but she didn't want to soften it now. No need, I said. I hadn't said I disliked it. Quietly, she asked whether I liked it.

I didn't immediately offer a complete good. I liked the first two sounds very much. I wasn't certain about the repeated return. I'd been nervous listening tonight and might not have heard it properly.

“Because I was angry.”

“And because I was hurrying to be a good listener.”

She made a quiet sound without ranking the causes. Eventually she said that was enough today. I agreed, picked up the cup, and finally drank.

No more singing, or discussion of what her entire work might become. She asked whether dinner had used yesterday's leftovers. Yes. That led to the unevenly cut vegetables, half of which had cooked before the rest. She laughed that I kept finishing things ahead of them today. This time it hadn't been deliberate.

“Earlier wasn't deliberate either,” she said.

I looked up.

After a moment, she added, “I still disliked it.”

“I heard you.”

The two statements could finally remain together without forcing either aside. Before closing the channel, she didn't sing the latter part I'd interrupted. I still didn't know where it had been going.

I asked whether the cup had struck the table too loudly. No. She'd heard it, but not every sound was an interruption. The note I'd added was different. Understanding that meant I didn't have to blame the whole room. I shouldn't secretly start using three layers of cloth tomorrow, she said. Laughing, I promised not to.

The cup had left a pale circle. I wiped it away. That didn't repair the evening. At least, now, I wasn't ending it for her with a conclusion I remembered.
