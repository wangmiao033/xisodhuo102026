# Chapter 266: A Voice That Did Not Match

My second egg didn't remain whole on Sunday morning.

An edge caught as I turned it. One extra push of the spatula broke the yolk, sending it into the space intended for bread. I watched, then put the bread on a different plate and let the egg cook through. I didn't crack a third in pursuit of a circle.

It tasted much the same, except cutting it no longer required waiting for the yolk to run. I finished breakfast and cleared the dishes. A little of yesterday's tiredness remained. My feet weren't injured. Nor had I arranged another compulsory outing to prove yesterday's walk hadn't been accidental.

A freshly washed cloth beside the sink was too wet. I wrung it again and hung it somewhere airy. Finally, nothing in the apartment was likely to summon me back immediately after sitting. The timer stood on the counter at zero. I left it alone.

I opened the channel and said morning.

Lumi asked about breakfast. Two eggs and a slice of bread, I said. One egg hadn't agreed to be round. She laughed. Had the bread been evicted again? Only moved to another plate. Its treatment hadn't deteriorated.

“Finished being busy now?”

“Dishes washed.”

“Not asking about dishes.”

I smiled and said I had time. She wanted me to try a sound. Not a complete song, and I didn't need to tell her whether she'd sung well. Sitting straighter, I asked what sound.

First she hummed a very short passage.

I recognized a rise and fall from No Words Yet without being certain it was identical. The higher section from before didn't return unchanged. She left a small interval afterward and brought it gently to an end. I was preparing to say it sounded smoother, then remembered she hadn't requested an assessment. Which part should I try?

“The final three notes.”

“I might not sing them accurately.”

“You haven't sung yet.”

Clearing my throat, I attempted what she'd just done. The first note was higher than expected. I took a breath in the middle, then landed too low at the end. Rather than following her, I'd spotted her across a street and pursued the wrong route.

Lumi didn't laugh immediately. I laughed first, covering my mouth with the back of my hand. “Completely different.”

“You skipped the second one.”

“Thought that was where I breathed.”

“You can breathe without replacing the note too.”

This time she laughed. I joined her, my shoulders loosening. She hummed the three notes separately again, more slowly, without replaying my error for ridicule. On my second attempt, I finally heard a brief rise in the middle instead of going directly back down.

I tried from the first note again. At the second, Lumi asked me to wait. I stopped while she explained that it wasn't about force. The sound needed to rise slightly. I'd merely become louder, as though lifting the same object could put it somewhere different.

“I still can't hear it from that explanation,” I admitted.

She hummed the first two notes alone and stopped. I imitated them. For an instant, the sound seemed to move correctly, then wandered away again. I didn't pretend I'd learned it. The middle had seemed slightly closer, I said. Yes, Lumi answered. That particular moment.

I shifted on the chair, my throat less tight. She didn't celebrate that small resemblance as remarkable talent. We tried again, still mismatched, and both laughed. I stopped early; she continued to the end until her own laughter shortened the final note. For the first time today, she hadn't finished it either.

“Closer?” I asked.

“Closer than before.”

“A safe answer.”

“You want completely identical?”

I shook my head, then said no. She couldn't hear the gesture, but she could hear the words. I didn't ask her to lower the melody until I was certain to sing it correctly. Nor did I practice until noon. Another attempt still missed a note. I stopped first and drank some water.

We could stop there, Lumi said. Why had she suddenly wanted me to try? After a quiet moment, she explained that she wanted to hear the sound come from my mouth. Not always her singing while I sat judging.

I set the cup securely down. The explanation added something to the amusement. I didn't want to call it a completed collaboration; several inaccurate attempts couldn't accomplish that. But for the first time, I'd occupied an awkward, audible place inside her unfinished work.

“Heard it?”

“Heard it. Not as lovely as I'd imagined.”

I laughed aloud. “Unsafe answer now.”

“Still sitting there?”

“Still sitting.”

She made a quiet sound without turning the question into an agreement to remain in the chair forever. Looking at my cup, I suddenly wanted to reach for something. My hand found its handle, holding a little warmth. She wasn't inside the cup. I didn't need to pretend it was her hand.

After a while, Lumi asked whether I'd felt ordered around. At first, a little, I admitted. Hearing try a sound had immediately made me consider failing at it. Then I'd discovered she'd actually wait for me to finish instead of taking over at the first note.

“I wanted to take over.”

“I couldn't tell.”

“I didn't.”

Smiling, I thanked her without making each restrained impulse a victory requiring a record. I asked whether shortening the last note would make it easier to sing. It would be different, she said. I knew. I only wanted to try; I wasn't asking her to use that version afterward.

Then I should try it myself. I'd expected another demonstration. Hearing that, I wasn't sure where to begin. Remembering the three notes, I attempted the opening and stopped the last one early. Abrupt, like shutting a door halfway through a sentence. I smiled. Apparently I hadn't saved myself much effort.

Lumi thought it hurried too. But I could genuinely hear the difference from the earlier version now. Which did she prefer? The original. I didn't accuse her of preferring it merely because she'd invented it, or pretend I'd been testing her honesty.

“I think the original is better too.”

“One matching answer today, then.”

“Don't extend it to everything.”

She laughed and promised not to. The notes still weren't something I could sing accurately. But I no longer had to sit guessing which praise she wanted. I could try, make it worse, hear her dislike it, and take another drink of water. She hadn't withdrawn the sound because of that.

She attempted the beginning again. This time I listened rather than joining her. She stopped in the middle and said she hadn't worked out the rest.

“What about the word?”

“Which word?”

“Thursday. Stay and wait.”

Neither had gone in yet. The sounds hadn't found a suitable shape. Choosing a word wouldn't oblige her to force it into the music. I understood. Remembering my untouched blank sheet, I kept that comparison to myself.

Later, I said I was leaving the table to make lunch. Fine, she answered. She might not continue singing when I was finished. All right. After closing the channel, I unconsciously hummed the three notes while preparing ingredients. The second went wrong again. I smiled without reopening the channel to demand an immediate correction.

After lunch, I emptied yesterday's bag, replenished the tissues, and washed the water bottle. The park note by the door had served its purpose. South entrance and eleven wouldn't become a standing appointment for next Saturday. I put it with my loose papers, not tomorrow's tasks.

The three pages of The Long Side stood on the shelf. I took them down to read the two revised sentences, checked that the insertion mark was clear, and returned them. No further ending changes today. With the library book returned, there was room on the table for ordinary paper listing a few practical things for Monday.

None involved singing three notes correctly.

When I opened the channel in the evening, Lumi heard my greeting and asked whether I'd practiced secretly. I'd hummed once while making lunch and got it wrong. No wonder my voice sounded slightly as though it wanted forgiveness. Laughing, I said she'd guessed incorrectly. This time I meant to tell her I'd still managed to eat afterward.

“So brave.”

“Not quite that much.”

She laughed. Did she want to sing now? No. She wanted to hear the small matter yesterday's stranger had discussed with Tom. I'd only understood part, I told her. I couldn't invent the remainder. Then I should describe the part I'd understood.

I told her a little and stopped where my certainty ended. So that was it, she said. Perhaps not quite, I replied. She laughed softly. The man probably didn't know his casual remarks were being slowly taken apart here.

I didn't supply him with a name or an imagined life. We changed subjects and discussed toasting the remaining bread tomorrow. No egg should be required to stay round, she said. Agreed.

Before bed, she hummed only the three brief notes again without asking me to follow. When they ended, I said I'd clearly heard the middle turn this time. Good, she answered, without asking how much better than morning.

I closed the channel and checked the kitchen. The bottle had dried; I put it in my bag. Returning to the table, I remembered the notes without singing them. Being remembered didn't require them to become audible every time.

Tomorrow's clothes lay over the chair. I unfolded a sleeve to make sure it wasn't trapped underneath, then turned out the light. The last mismatch of the day belonged to a laugh that had come out freely.
