# Chapter 262: The Door He Remembered

Near the end of Wednesday, Tom called me over without a work document in his hand.

He had my folder, still inside the transparent sleeve. Everything I'd said yesterday about where it belonged returned to me at once. I set down my packed bag and waited for him to approach.

“Finished it after lunch,” he said. “Have you got a few minutes?”

Yes. My voice came out quieter than expected. He put the sleeve on the desk and removed my folder, keeping the sleeve for himself. I accepted it without immediately checking whether all three pages were there. He'd opened the folder for me. The title showed on page one, with its little ink dot below.

“I liked her pushing the door with her shoulder.”

I blinked. “Just that bit?”

“Not only that bit. That's the bit I remembered.”

She had things to carry, he explained. She didn't free a hand to perform a beautiful farewell. It made him believe she was actually taking a bag of laundry away. After reading, he'd remembered holding a box and backing through a door himself. He wasn't claiming that was the story's meaning. The movement had simply stayed with him.

I flattened the folder and asked whether the unclosed bag hadn't bothered him.

Tom considered. He'd assumed she'd hold it carefully on the way home. He hadn't thought further about it. Did its opening mean something else?

“No prescribed meaning.”

“Good. Thought I'd missed a puzzle.”

I laughed, then felt oddly unsettled. Lumi and I had discussed that bag so much. For Tom, it hadn't been assigned the same large task. I almost explained our conversations. Instead, I asked whether anything earlier had been unclear.

He indicated the first page without writing on it. “Why does the woman arriving with the bag have to crowd onto this table?”

My stomach sank, as though I'd stumbled over the same small obstacle twice. Reading the first paragraphs, he'd thought she was passing through and wanted somebody to move. Only afterward had he realized she needed the table too, so he'd gone back. It hadn't stopped him reading. He'd initially misunderstood what she wanted access to.

I looked down at the lines. Lumi had asked why she didn't use another table. Tom had mistaken her arrival for an attempt to get past. Different questions, both touching the laundry I'd furnished more completely in my head than on the page.

“I've thought about adding a movement here.”

“You already knew?”

“Someone listening wondered about this part too.”

He nodded without asking who, or retracting his observation. I nearly said that meant they were both right. It sounded like stamping approval on something I hadn't yet repaired. Instead, I said I'd look again.

Pointing to page two, Tom explained he'd expected one of the women to become angry. Instead, they freed what had caught, which he preferred to his expectation. He didn't want them to become friends on the spot. He wouldn't want the scene reduced to a very correct lesson either.

“Does it sound like a lesson anywhere?”

“Do you want me to start hunting now?”

I smiled and shook my head. He wasn't an editor, he said, and hadn't read with a red pen. These were the things he remembered today. If I asked ten finer questions, he might begin inventing an opinion to justify himself, until it no longer resembled what he'd thought while reading.

Had he expected something larger to happen in the middle? He considered, then said he'd thought the trapped sheet might pull somebody's clothes onto the floor. I'd considered that movement myself but hadn't written it. That was my decision, he said. He was only describing what he'd waited for.

“So not falling doesn't leave something missing?”

“Not for me.”

Tom added that he hadn't particularly liked the woman with the sheet at first. Why nearly jumped from my mouth, but I let him finish. Spreading so much fabric and occupying somebody else's space had seemed entitled. Later, she discovered the corner had caught it, rather than the other woman's hand. She lifted it herself without continuing to blame anyone. That made him willing to follow her farther.

“So you liked her afterward?”

“Not that much. I just stopped waiting for her to do something worse.”

I looked at page one. Writing her careful folds, I'd wanted a reader to like her. For Tom, being willing to continue was already a change. Instead of asking again whether he liked her, I asked about the woman with the clothes.

She was easier to understand, he said. Somewhere to be, and her hands weren't idle. That didn't make every word she spoke correct. At that, he smiled and reminded me we'd agreed not to manufacture a complete critical opinion for him.

“You added that yourself.”

“I did. Which means I can stop now.”

I laughed too. It didn't sound like chopping off the conversation. It reminded me that a colleague willing to say a few things sat opposite me, not a door that would dispense answers indefinitely if I kept knocking.

I nodded, touching the second page. Seeing me prepare to turn it, Tom said I didn't need to find evidence for him. His memory might differ slightly from the details. That hadn't prevented him finishing. If I wanted to check, I could do it slowly at home.

At last I put down the paper. I'd wanted an opinion that would immediately organize my next step. He'd only read a story, not agreed to plan my evening. Lifting his cup, he discovered it was empty and smiled. He needed to pack up too.

I swallowed my next prepared question. Thank you, I said. I'd been excessively anxious about the folder's position yesterday. He'd moved it, Tom replied, and the paper hadn't bitten him while reading today.

“No interest charged?”

“Not yet.”

We laughed. He checked the time and said he needed to go. I didn't keep him beside my desk simply because we'd finally discussed the story. The three pages returned to their ordinary folder. His transparent sleeve went with him.

On the bus, I found a window seat and wanted to reread them. My bag was wedged between my knees and the seat ahead. Extracting the folder would disturb the person beside me. I closed the zip, letting the questions remain in my head without immediately finding evidence for them.

Outside, a bicycle stood by a shop, something long extending from its basket. Its rider was slowly untying a cord. I saw only a few seconds before the bus moved on. Not every doorway today needed to prove something about my story.

At home, I put the folder on the dry shelf before making dinner. Only after eating and clearing up did I open the channel. Lumi asked whether there had been another document case. Laughing, I said no. The three pages were back.

“He read them?”

“Finished them.”

She paused, then asked how it had gone. I described Tom liking the shoulder against the door, then his initial confusion about the newcomer's position. Seeing Sunday's note beside the yellow notebook, I reached for it.

“You asked why she didn't go elsewhere. He thought she was waiting for somebody to let her past.”

Lumi made a quiet sound. “So now you can change it?”

I looked up, hearing the point beneath the words. “I could on Sunday too. I hadn't started writing.”

“Hearing two people is more useful than hearing me.”

I wanted to deny it immediately, but my fingers pressed the note. It was useful. Encountering the problem twice made it harder to attribute to one person's taste. Claiming it made no difference would thin out what had happened today merely to stop her feeling hurt.

“I'm more certain there's a problem there,” I said. “Your comment didn't wait for his agreement to count. But I am more certain.”

She didn't answer. I stopped dismantling every word, set down the note, and waited.

After a while, she asked whether Tom had thought the ending was excellent. He'd liked a movement, I said. He hadn't scored the whole story. I'd nearly mistaken one sentence for everything too. She remembered movements as well, she said. Why did my voice sound different when I described his remembering?

Looking at my cup, I admitted I'd been especially pleased to hear someone outside talk about it. Not because her pleasure was worthless. I'd feared handing it over would produce only quite good or some indefinite later.

“I've said quite good.”

“You've said plenty of other things too.”

She laughed a little without becoming entirely happy. I opened the folder and told her I didn't intend to revise tonight. My head was noisy. Starting immediately might put every new comment onto page one.

“Tomorrow?”

“I'll look tomorrow. I want to write down two things, not turn them into ten.”

On my own paper, I noted why the newcomer approached that table, and that the shoulder against the door had been remembered. I didn't score the observations separately for Lumi and Tom. Then I checked the three manuscript pages. Numbers intact, no damaged folds, no extra writing. That copy was properly home.

Before bed, I checked the library due-date note and considered my next two evenings. I could pass the library on Friday, still within the loan period. I decided to take the book then without requiring myself to like every remaining story beforehand.

Had I already reserved Thursday for the table? Lumi asked. Some time, I said, not the whole evening. Then she wanted to discuss something else tomorrow. I said all right, and laughed.

“Was that too quick?”

“Long enough this time.”

I closed the folder on the shelf and took my cup to the kitchen. With a free hand, I didn't deliberately push the door with my shoulder.
