# Chapter 411: A Place for the Cup

On Sunday morning, the new coaster remained near the table's edge, in the same place where I'd left it after bringing it home, clean enough to resemble yesterday's object on the stall.

Fetching water, I automatically used the usual place for my glass. Only after sitting did I notice the empty wooden disk. It wasn't forgotten inside a bag or awaiting installation. Moving the glass several inches would suffice. During breakfast, however, I picked up the coaster, examined it again, and put it back.

Later that morning, I went for a short walk. Not to the market, not to discover whether the green dish remained. Two people beside the road were consulting an unfolded map. One indicated where they'd just been; the other shook their head. Hearing a question about a nearby junction, I explained the part I knew without adding directions beyond it. They thanked me. I continued my own way, without waiting for proof of their eventual success.

At a bench, I sat briefly. A curled leaf occupied the empty space beside me. I brushed it toward the edge, unwilling to leave someone sitting on fragments that might cling to clothes. The wind turned it back. I looked once without entering prolonged negotiations with a leaf. Traffic sounded, then faded. This wasn't a particularly quiet place, but I didn't need to answer every sound.

After a while, strings sounded nearby. An adult man played a violin beside the pavement, an open case in front of him without obstructing the route. His playing initially flowed. Then one section broke off. Looking down, he adjusted something and began earlier in the passage. Thinking he'd finished, I'd lifted my hands from my knees. I lowered them again without applauding that pause prematurely.

Someone passing also stopped, listened to a brief section, and continued. The violinist looked up, smiled, and began something else without requiring either of us to remain. There were no sung words. I didn't know the tune's name, only remembered a rapid sequence of ascending notes. Standing, I approached the case and left a little change. I didn't produce a phone to record it or arrange an expression demonstrating sophisticated appreciation.

He thanked me. I thanked him for playing, my voice emerging normally. Neither asked for a name. As I left, he continued. Only beyond the corner did the sound fade. I hadn't heard the final note. That didn't create an obligation to return for an entire afternoon. I'd actually heard this today. Carrying a complete tune home wasn't required to establish that the encounter had happened.

My throat was much easier than last weekend. Those short directions hadn't caused coughing. I didn't practice long readings outside. I continued along the road toward home. My shoes felt ordinary; the repaired right heel lining hadn't rubbed. I hadn't brought the scarf. It was warmer than when I'd left yesterday, and its new stitches could remain untested in the wardrobe.

On the way, I bought ordinary lunch ingredients without another bunch of parsley. Repeating that flavor could wait until I actually wanted it. Remembering a conversation didn't require weekly identical shopping. At home, I cooked, ate, cleared away the meal, and made tea.

An ordinary cup, already mine. Carrying it toward the table, I noticed moisture on the outside of its base. My hand paused above the new coaster. The pale line of grain made me suddenly worry that a ring would prevent it ever looking newly bought again.

I returned the cup to the kitchen counter and dried its outside and base. Coming back, I nevertheless placed it in the old spot. Beside it, the coaster resembled a guest invited to watch others work.

That afternoon, I forgot the matter and sorted a small pile of ordinary papers. Obsolete receipts joined the discard pile; useful ones returned to their places. Tuesday's rough note and the invitation scrap didn't enter old manuscripts. Nor did I remove the gray-blue card from the planner to assemble them into a matching set. I dealt with this pile without searching deeper into cupboards for more tasks requiring completion today.

After dinner, the table was a little clearer. The coaster remained. The tea cup was washed and put away. I now knew the wooden circle's emptiness wasn't lack of space. I disliked letting a cup conceal it.

Opening the channel, I described giving directions, sitting on a bench during my walk, and making tea afterward, then admitted the coaster still hadn't been used. Lumi paused. Did a cup wobble on it? No. Yesterday I'd tested its flatness, and today nothing suggested a change. Why, then? Finally, I admitted worrying about rings.

“Wasn't it made for a cup to sit on?”

Smiling, I agreed. She didn't know how to maintain wood, Lumi said. I shouldn't mistake her for an authority on materials. But if I truly didn't want to use it, I could admit I'd bought a piece of wood to admire instead of preserving its opportunity to start work tomorrow indefinitely.

Looking at the coaster, I eventually said that sounded rather sharp. She paused. Incorrect, or uncomfortable to hear? The latter. I didn't think she was entirely wrong.

She didn't press her advantage. Hearing that it remained unused on its second day had amused her. She wasn't asking me to pour tea over it immediately to demonstrate courage. I knew. The cup didn't need a bravery competition either.

“Where do you want it?”

On the table ordinarily, I said. A cup could use it; empty, it could display its grain. Answering clarified how uncomplicated yesterday's purchase had been. Today, I'd assigned it an additional purpose requiring it never to be touched.

Was there a glass on the table now? Yes, water. The base was clean. I'd dried it after washing my hands earlier. I lifted the glass and placed it in the center of the wooden circle. The contact sounded duller than contact with the table. No rocking, no immediate appearance of an ugly ring. Nothing needed assembling underneath it, and no folded paper was required to level it. The wood held it steadily.

With the glass steady in the center, the coaster extended beyond its base, leaving room so my fingers didn't catch it when holding the glass. I moved the arrangement a little nearer, established that both objects stood steadily, and checked that neither pressed against the nearby book pages. Tomorrow's water wouldn't require searching for another place. This was a genuinely convenient position, not one selected merely to look properly organized.

I reported the result. Lumi laughed. Introductions on its first day, employment on its second. Smiling, I didn't require future reminders. My hand could perform this movement without turning a coaster into her new assignment.

We were quiet briefly. I drank and returned the glass. On the second attempt, my hand didn't pause in the air. Had the grain disappeared? Most of it, I said, though a ring remained visible around the outside. Then my purchase wasn't entirely concealed, she observed, nor was it a picture allowed only to be viewed.

Remembering the green dish, I suggested I'd have been even less willing to touch it. Perhaps I'd neither use it nor put keys inside, merely call it beautiful daily. Lumi had liked its described color yesterday, but didn't know how the chip felt. She couldn't assure me buying it would have been fine. I'd felt it, I said, and left it there. Finally using a coaster didn't require returning for the dish too.

She laughed. She hadn't asked the market to issue a graduation certificate. Good. I wouldn't return to report progress to the seller. The wooden coaster didn't guarantee that every future choice would be correct either.

Later, what had I thought about on the bench? Nothing particularly complete. A leaf kept blowing back. Eventually, I left it alone. Had it won? Laughing, I said it hadn't intended to compete, or known I'd briefly wanted to tidy the seat for someone arriving later.

I also described the violinist and nearly applauding while he adjusted his playing. Had I felt embarrassed? I hadn't actually clapped, and he might not have noticed my movement. Had I liked the music? One section, yes, but I couldn't remember enough to hum it. Then no humming, Lumi said. She hadn't asked me to transport the street's sounds indoors.

Smiling, I mentioned leaving a little change and departing without waiting for a finale. He could continue playing while I continued walking, she said. Exactly. We hadn't needed to finish simultaneously for those few minutes to be complete. I left the account there without inventing a title or history to occupy the part I didn't know.

Sometimes she wanted those moments that hadn't become finished stories, Lumi said. Looking at the glass, I told her I could describe this one because I remembered it. I wasn't collecting a string of small objects outside every day to arrange for her afterward.

She knew. Wanting to hear didn't mean requiring me to think of her during the event. Had I said too much while giving directions? No. Only as far as the junction I knew. She laughed. She'd imagined me adding the leaf's route for completeness.

I laughed for a while, holding the glass steadily. She laughed too, without steering the conversation toward whether I'd entirely recovered. Feeling more comfortable was happening. It didn't need a solemn announcement.

We read no new poem and didn't continue the umbrella story. Had I particularly wanted to change a sentence after she finished telling it? I hadn't rewritten it. I remembered wanting the umbrella rather than last night, but remembering didn't mean retrieving it for revision. Then no need to bring it out tonight either, Lumi said. That rain had been told. It could remain without falling again daily.

As we prepared to finish, I drank the remaining water and told her I'd wash the glass. With it removed, the coaster showed no obvious ring, only slightly different light where the base had rested. I wiped it lightly with a clean cloth and set it flat. I didn't apply an unfamiliar product in pursuit of its market appearance.

Tomorrow I would use it again, then? I intended to. Forgetting while fetching a cup wouldn't make today a failure. Agreed. A coaster could wait once without each wait becoming permanent.

I left it conveniently placed, a little away from book pages. Empty, its grain showed completely. The gray-blue card remained on Wednesday in the planner; the sewing box stayed closed and the scarf hem repaired. One coaster starting work didn't require everything else to move house.

After closing the channel, I finished the kitchen and prepared ordinary belongings for Monday. No new appointment, no achievement demanding inspection. The place left for a cup on the table finally involved more than the preparation I'd kept describing since bringing it home.
