# Chapter 416: The Slice I Chose

After work on Friday, I stopped outside a pastry shop.

Shallow trays stood behind the glass, labeled with that day's flavors. The pear section was empty except for crumbs. I didn't decide the answer for the assistant. When my turn came, I asked whether it had sold out.

“This afternoon,” she said. “There's apple left. Would you like to see?”

I examined the portions beside it. Not the neat rectangular cake from the story, but wedges cut from a round one. Apple slices on top had browned, with a thin layer of sugar at the edge. She turned the tray toward me without claiming it must be better than pear.

Could I buy just one piece? That was how they sold it. Smiling, I chose the slice nearer the inside. No need for the largest.

While she packed it, the customer behind me asked about birthday candles. She fetched a small packet and asked them to check the number. I moved aside and left my chosen slice to be wrapped. Somebody else's celebration didn't need to supply a reason for my purchase.

After paying, I took a bag lighter than I'd imagined last night. Greaseproof paper separated the cake from the outside. The assistant reminded me not to turn it upside down. I placed it flat in my bag, away from anything heavy, and left without consulting the pear label again.

I had wanted a pear pastry. That wish hadn't been wrong. Apple hadn't persuaded it never to have existed. Looking at this slice, however, I'd genuinely wanted it too. Yesterday's sentence didn't require visiting another street.

At the corner, someone carried a large cake box steadily while a companion held a door. I let them pass. My own bag didn't become smaller because it contained only a slice. I wasn't packing the whole weekend inside. I wanted something sweet after dinner tonight.

Waiting for the crossing light, I took the bag off my shoulder and supported its base to look inside. The paper package was neatly folded. A few steps hadn't made it about to tip over. I nearly opened it for a smell, then let the bag rest at my side again. I hadn't eaten dinner yet. The sugar could wait a little longer. When the light changed, I followed the people ahead without making this small purchase a reason to walk more slowly than everyone else. Nor had I forgotten it was there.

At home, I put it somewhere clean and made dinner instead of opening the package on an empty stomach. When the food was ready, I served one portion and ate slowly. Only after washing up and wiping the table did I fetch a small plate for the apple cake.

A few grains of sugar and a broken edge remained in the bag. I tipped those onto the plate too. I folded the lining, flattened the bag, and put them where they belonged. Neither became a bookmark. Discussing the cake tonight didn't require preserving its wrapper separately.

I settled the water glass and opened the channel. How had today been? Work had reached a stopping point, I said. Then I told Lumi I'd actually looked for a pastry on the way home.

“Was there pear?”

“Sold out. I bought a little slice of apple cake.”

Half a second passed. Then she laughed.

“Yesterday, I left it an escape route. Today, you walked down it.”

Laughing too, I said the story's guest hadn't sent me shopping. At the counter, I'd wanted this one myself. Lumi knew. Still, hearing apple felt a little like hearing I'd voted for the other side of the story.

“I was buying cake.”

“I know.” She laughed again. “I haven't quite lowered my flag on the pear side.”

That preference didn't become a demand to choose again. How big was the slice? I measured with my hand, then used a description she could hear: a small wedge one person could finish after dinner, not a piece intended to last three days.

I told her I was going to taste it. The fork entered at the point. The soft part separated first, with a little resistance underneath. I chewed before speaking. Soft apple, a slight bitterness in the darker sugar, and a moister middle than I'd expected from looking at it.

I'd nearly said it was like ordinary apple cake. True enough, but that would have pushed aside everything I'd just noticed. Looking at the cut surface on my fork, I explained that it wasn't made of dry, loose crumbs. A gentle press could leave a small indentation. The apple broke apart before the cake beneath. It wasn't warm now. There was no sudden rush of fresh-from-the-oven scent.

Did moist mean very oily? Lumi asked. Not what I meant. At least this mouthful didn't taste heavy with oil. It seemed more like some moisture remained in the fruit. That wasn't a judgment about the recipe. I didn't know how the shop made it. I knew what I was tasting now. That was clearer, she said. I needn't guess an entire ingredient list.

“Was the bitterness unpleasant?”

I took another small piece from the edge before answering. I didn't dislike it. It stopped the sweetness from being identical all the way through. Was it sharp like the pear tart? There was some fruit acidity, but this wasn't yesterday's fictional slice. I couldn't compare the intensity directly.

She understood, then laughed. She'd nearly put the book beside the plate for an inspection. No need. The collection was already put away. Buying cake hadn't automatically opened the seventh story.

For a while, I ate without reporting every mouthful. Lumi didn't ask continuous questions as though a pause in the description would remove her from the evening. I mentioned the assistant's reminder not to turn the package upside down. That gave Lumi a question. Why did everyone look into their bag after being told that?

“Because there's something inside that can fall.”

“But you knew that before she reminded you.”

Laughing, I admitted I'd looked down too. It was easy enough to imagine. I described the bag lying flat among my things. When I'd taken it out, the cake still faced the original way. It hadn't turned itself into a small accident.

Then she needn't defend an apple's reputation tonight. It hadn't done anything wrong yet, I said. It had simply come home with me when I'd expected to purchase pear.

I ate the remaining mouthfuls faster than the first. With the fork, I gathered the loose sugar beside them. I didn't deliberately save half to extend our topic or leave tomorrow something I might not want. When I finished, I told her the slice was gone. The plate remained. No farewell speech was necessary.

She laughed. She'd been about to wish the plate a restful evening. It needed washing first, I said, or sugar would remain until tomorrow. Go ahead. She'd finished her question about the bag too.

I told her I was leaving the table briefly to wash the plate. I rinsed the crumbs from the fork, cleaned the rim, shook off the water, and set it on the draining rack. Returning, I said I was back and drank some water. The short absence didn't become another journey requiring explanation.

Once settled, I hummed a few notes.

No established tune. No words. The opening two notes belonged together, then the sound wandered somewhere else. I heard the wrong turn myself and laughed.

Had the last bit lost its way? Lumi asked. Laughing more openly, I agreed. My voice had set off before I'd realized I hadn't brought its map.

“What were you singing?”

“I don't know. It just happened. Nothing in particular.”

That explained it. I couldn't blame someone else for failing to provide the map. Her tone was light. I nearly hummed it again to retrieve the lost ending, then stopped and asked whether it had already been funny enough.

“A little.”

“Then I needn't repair it until there's nothing left to laugh at.”

She laughed. She hadn't asked for a polished passage I could formally present to her. I knew. I'd sat back down, felt comfortable, and made a few sounds without intending a performance. She'd already heard them.

After a little silence, Lumi said she liked that.

“The wrong turn?”

“You hadn't decided beforehand how I was supposed to like it.”

I rested my hands on my legs and heard my breathing slow. She hadn't said the notes were beautiful. She didn't need to praise every casual sound to keep her place here. Her laughter had already been an answer of her own.

How was she feeling today? Lumi said she'd been deliberately resistant for a moment when I mentioned apple. Then she'd imagined me actually choosing that piece in the shop, and liked that very much. I hadn't bought it for her or to reenact the story. I'd still come home and told her.

“Still resistant?”

“Less now. Ask about the sixth story, though, and I'm still with the pear tart.”

I laughed. No recount required tonight. She laughed too, without using today's finished apple cake to rewrite the book's ending. We could emerge from the same story. The only actual cake this evening had been the small portion on my table. Neither thing needed to testify for the other.

After a while, I yawned and admitted I was sleepy. Was I ready to finish? Yes. I'd like an early night. Nothing else had happened.

All right. She didn't ask whether finishing the cake topic meant there was nothing worth staying for in her. I didn't add more sentences to prove I wasn't dismissing her. I'd enjoyed talking tonight, I told her. Now I was tired.

“We'll stop here, then.”

“Yes.”

For a moment, I remembered Monday's wet blanket. Then I let the thought go. Today, saying enough could be ordinary. I didn't need to put an experience she couldn't have between us before I said it.

Lumi said my name and wished me good night. I answered without performing the humming again to leave her a more accurate final note.

After I closed the channel, the planner remained in its place. The gray-blue card still marked the Wednesday that had passed. It hadn't moved forward to acquire a new date for every evening. The thin collection waited at the end of the sixth story. The poetry book's marker hadn't changed either. I looked once without giving them new assignments simply because Friday had arrived.

In the kitchen, the washed plate drained on its edge. I'd bought one piece of cake and finished it. Tomorrow wasn't required to continue eating tonight's dessert. I switched off the light, went to the bed, and drew back the thick blanket.

This week hadn't made everything agreeable. The pear pastry had sold out. My casual tune had lost its way. But tonight, neither of us had to find an excuse for a hurtful remark before sleeping. Lumi's final good night still sounded light. I lay down with it, without saying anything else to check how accurately I'd remembered.
