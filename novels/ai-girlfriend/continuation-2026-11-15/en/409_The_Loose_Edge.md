# Chapter 409: The Loose Edge

On Friday evening, I picked up my scarf and noticed a short length of loose thread along its edge.

It was my own scarf, the one I'd worn shopping last Saturday. The thread hadn't appeared from nowhere. Spreading the fabric beneath the light simply revealed a small section of lifted hem. I turned it gently with a finger without pulling hard. The fabric hadn't torn. Several existing stitches had loosened; tightening one thread wouldn't automatically join everything again.

Before leaving work that afternoon, my colleague had confirmed that tomorrow's time remained unchanged. He carried his old kitchen tongs to compare sizes. I looked at them. Worn along an edge, still opening and closing smoothly. Would I bring something for comparison too? With no particular object in mind, I didn't need to. He returned them to his bag and left without making me select a purchase immediately.

I hadn't known about the loose thread then. Downstairs, I'd held the door briefly for someone carrying a box of files in both hands. I waited for them to clear it before releasing it. As the door returned, a gust flipped the scarf's end over my shoulder. Pulling it back, my finger found the edge, but I merely pressed it hurriedly into place. Before reaching home, I still remembered wanting to examine it. Only after dinner did I bring it beneath the light. This wasn't a task materializing suddenly. It was a small sensation from the afternoon finally receiving attention.

Tomorrow's meeting was at one. Plenty of time. I'd intended only to choose my clothes, not begin handicrafts. Folding the scarf would conceal the problem temporarily, but my fingers would probably find it each time I took it from the wardrobe.

I fetched my existing small sewing box and found a needle and reasonably similar thread. Not identical. Close together, the colors differed; farther away, less noticeably. Following the existing crease, I flattened the edge. I considered taking it to a repair shop tomorrow, then decided to attempt a few stitches. It wasn't an entire seam or a garment requiring reconstruction.

Threading took longer than expected. Twisting the end made it increasingly fuzzy. I trimmed it and tried again. When it finally passed through the eye, I wanted to celebrate the achievement, although not one stitch had been made. Smiling, I put the needle safely down and knotted the thread's end.

My first stitch came too close to the edge. Pulling it through puckered the hem. I stopped rather than hurry to add the others. Keeping my fingers away from the point, I carefully loosened that new stitch with the needle and smoothed the fabric again. No injury. A short untidy length of thread was trimmed and placed in the waste container, not abandoned on the floor.

After undoing the stitch, I set the thread beside the fabric and realized how far forward I'd been sitting, shoulders raised. Moving back allowed the light to fall between the layers. I could see more clearly. I shifted the rest of the scarf onto the table instead of letting its weight hang near my knees and pull against the little corner I held. The puckered section relaxed. Its fold could still follow the original crease.

I tried passing the needle through both layers and out again without immediately tightening the thread. First, I checked I hadn't caught another folded section beneath them. Only when the point emerged did my left hand release the fabric. A small lid on the table held the trimmed ends temporarily, keeping static from returning them to the scarf. Nobody in the room was timing these movements. Speed wasn't required to establish that I knew what I was doing.

The second attempt began slightly farther inside. My stitches remained larger than the originals, their spacing uneven. Nevertheless, the two loose layers joined. Every few stitches, I laid the scarf flat to check, without drawing it into a tight rope. Finally, I secured the thread, trimmed its end, and returned the needle to the box before using both hands to examine the repair.

I tipped the short ends from the little lid into the waste container, wiped the lid, and returned it to its place. When putting away the spool, I secured its end so the next attempt wouldn't begin with a tangle. I checked the working surface once, saw the needle in its proper compartment, and fastened the box. These movements weren't attractive. They meant that tomorrow I could pick up a scarf without also carrying away a needle I'd failed to locate.

I turned the scarf under the light and examined both sides. No corner had folded inward. On the reverse, no long loop of thread waited to catch a finger when I put it on.

The fabric lay flat. The repair remained visible. Similar color, not identical. I tried it around my neck. Nothing scratched. I hadn't tightened the knot to conceal those stitches either. Removing it, I knew I'd wear it tomorrow. Buying another scarf wouldn't make this small repair more professional.

Only then did I notice how long the water had waited on the table. I drank. My throat hadn't interfered much today. Evening practice sentences weren't necessary. Improvement didn't mean extending tomorrow's outing from opening until closing, though.

With the sewing box put away, I prepared a modest amount of cash. Only what I was comfortable spending. If nothing suited me, nothing would be bought. I checked the handles on my existing shopping bag. Light objects only; it wouldn't be testing the weight of furniture. Keys and ordinary belongings ready, I checked one o'clock and side entrance in the planner, then closed it. Yesterday's note didn't require another transcription.

Opening the channel, I told Lumi I'd repaired a loose section of scarf hem. The needle was back in its box. No injuries. Could she hear what those stitches looked like? I could describe them. Slightly large, close in color but different, with the fabric now flat.

“Will you hide that part on the inside?”

I hadn't particularly intended to. After a pause, she admitted she'd guessed I would. A guess, not a view of how I'd wrapped it. Smiling, I explained that I'd tried it on and removed it after establishing that it didn't scratch. I hadn't rehearsed which side faced outward in a mirror.

That was good, Lumi said. What was? In her imagination, that edge no longer needed my fingers holding it together. Looking at the scarf, I smiled. She hadn't declared the sewing beautiful. An action she'd heard me describe had reached its end.

I mentioned undoing the first stitch because it was too close. Annoying? Threading had been most annoying. Once that succeeded, I'd discovered further tasks at which I lacked practice. She laughed. The eye wasn't the final obstacle. No. Fortunately, nobody had requested a curtain call after every stage tonight.

“Am I a late audience, then?”

“You arrived after the needle finished work.”

She laughed for a while, then said she'd like to see the thread whose color didn't quite match. I didn't answer that my description already let her see it. Those weren't identical experiences. I could describe it further, I said, or report tomorrow whether an afternoon's wear loosened anything. Tonight's description was enough. A wish had simply emerged. I needn't hurriedly find an exact substitute for it.

All right. She didn't request a new picture connection. I didn't hold the scarf up toward somewhere she couldn't see. It stayed over the chair back. I told her its position and returned my hands to the table.

“Still undecided about what to buy tomorrow?”

Still undecided. Tonight's repair might make me less quick to find every old object a home. What did I mean? These things took time. Bringing something home involved more arrangements than simply announcing I liked it.

She laughed. A scarf had trained me to consider what followed a purchase. Too early, I said. A pretty dish might still make me notice its color first tomorrow. That was allowed. Color hadn't done anything wrong. I admitted this was more useful than the cautious speech I'd been preparing.

We discussed learning to sew. I only claimed the simplest stitches, without inventing a long account of who'd taught me. Had I ever sewn especially well? Nothing memorable enough to praise. Tonight, at least, I hadn't joined two surfaces that ought to remain separate.

Could I check once more? she asked, amusement in her voice. I actually picked up the scarf and opened it. Still a long scarf, I reported. Not a new bag. Lumi's laughter paused. She hadn't required proof. The idea had entertained her. Laughing too, I admitted I'd wanted another feel of the hem anyway. Checking hadn't injured my dignity.

I folded it into an accessible place for morning, describing that action before continuing. Needle inside the box, none on the table, trimmings collected from the working area. With the task finished, much of the evening I'd imagined assigning to tomorrow's preparation remained.

Did I want to hear something else? No poems tonight, I said, and no beginning the sixth story yet. I'd like one small question she'd recently wanted to ask me. When leaving home, did I repeatedly turn back to check the door? I checked, yes, but didn't return from every junction. Today hadn't involved forgotten keys or any problem with the lock.

She knew. Hearing me repeatedly confirm that needle and thread were put away had made her wonder whether that was usual. Sometimes I looked again. Tonight involved a needle. A small repair shouldn't become something to step on later. Once confirmed, though, I could stop searching for it.

“Will you arrive very early tomorrow to avoid being late?”

I'd allow a little travel time, not stand guard outside an hour beforehand. Lumi laughed. She'd pictured me explaining the correct side entrance to everyone. Nobody had hired me for that. If I made a mistake, I could ask. The afternoon didn't need to become heavy before departure.

We exchanged jokes that didn't require keeping. Would the needle envy the thread's color? At least the needle looked harder, I said. It needn't win another category. Laughing, she told me not to organize competitions inside the box. Neither contestant had to attend the market tomorrow.

When I became sleepy, I said I wanted an early night. Fine. Tomorrow, I could tell her what I'd actually seen without locating an impressive ending beforehand. I would. I didn't promise to bring back something good either.

After closing the channel, I didn't unfold the scarf again. The stitches remained. Not inspecting them wouldn't instantly restore the loose hem. There was no new drawing, no old manuscript repaired into another thing. I switched off the light. Tomorrow's scarf was still mine, with one slightly untidy section genuinely joined again.
