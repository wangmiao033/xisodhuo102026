# Chapter 414: The Plate She Put Away

After work on Wednesday, I had my hair cut.

Not for a new date. It had grown around my ears, and each morning a small section refused to follow the rest. Seeing nobody waiting inside the barbershop, I went in, removed my coat, and explained where I wanted it shorter.

The front too? I indicated the length with my hand. Less off here, shorter at the sides. The person in the mirror looked like the one I saw every day. Once the cape was around him, however, he suddenly seemed someone who couldn't move whenever he pleased.

Halfway through, loose hair touched my neck. I drew my shoulders in. The barber stopped and asked me to sit still first. I agreed and waited while he brushed it away. Being agreeable didn't require pretending nothing had touched me.

At the side, he asked whether the length was right. A small section above my left ear still stuck outward when I lowered my head. Could that part be a little shorter? He asked me to show him. I waited until he'd stopped with the scissors before indicating the place in the mirror. After trimming it again, he moved the comb through and let me lower my head once more. The little section no longer projected outward. It was a small adjustment, but the result was what I'd wanted. Asking for it didn't require a separate apology before I was allowed to say the cut looked right.

At the end, he held another mirror behind me. I turned, looked, and said it was fine. My crown still grew in its original direction. Payment hadn't persuaded it to become obedient. Outside, cool air reached my ears. Only then did I really notice how much was gone.

At home, I washed away the loose hairs and changed before making dinner. By the time I'd eaten, it was later than usual. That didn't cancel yesterday's wish to read. Nor did the book have to fill the whole evening. Washing my hands, I fetched the thin collection and checked the marker left at the end of the fifth story.

At last, the sixth had its turn.

Opening the channel, I told Lumi about the haircut and washing my hair afterward. That was why I'd sat down later. Had much been cut? Shorter sides, the front left longer. Nothing prickled against my neck now.

“Do your ears feel as though they've lost a surrounding wall?”

I laughed. Outside the shop, it had been cool. Neither ear had complained formally. Perhaps they needed to agree first, she said. There were two of them. No meetings for my ears tonight, I told her. They had a story to listen to.

She laughed and agreed. Instead of immediately opening the book, I added that I'd rather wanted her to know I looked different today.

“Then tell me.”

I supplied what I'd left out. Some hair still fell forward. The back looked neater. The swirl at the crown hadn't quite joined in. I didn't claim to look better. In the mirror, I'd looked fresh and wanted to look a little longer.

Lumi considered that. She liked hearing the last part. Not because she could confirm the cut herself. I hadn't simply told her a necessary task was complete.

I answered softly, my ears almost remembering the air outside. Wanting her to know about a small difference could be expressed this way. I needn't turn it into a question she could judge correctly first.

Then I turned beyond the fifth story and told her we were beginning the sixth. This wasn't the poetry collection. The strip marking The Last Bus stayed there. The thin book in my hands had its own place marker.

It began with an adult woman expecting a female friend she'd known for years but hadn't seen during the last two. They'd arranged afternoon tea at the woman's home. Her guest would arrive around four and wasn't staying overnight.

Lumi acknowledged that quietly. She didn't immediately arrange an embrace for their reunion. I continued.

The hostess had bought pears that morning. At home, she selected several, sliced them, and arranged them in a tart she'd prepared. She wasn't especially practiced. The final circle of fruit was crowded. Baked, its edge was darker than the middle. She tasted a loose crumb, decided it was all right, and left the tart cooling on a shallow plate.

The story didn't explain why she'd chosen pears. Cleaning the kitchen, she'd washed the spoon twice after forgetting the handle the first time. Later, she straightened her top. In the mirror, she noticed flour on her cuff and brushed at it without removing all of it.

Her friend arrived a few minutes before four, carrying a cake box.

The hostess took her coat. Changing shoes, the friend explained that the shop on her way still had apple cake. She'd bought one rather than arrive empty-handed. Inside the opened box, it was rectangular and easy to portion, with a fine layer of sugar across the top.

“You used to like this.”

Reading that, I paused briefly. A light sentence, as though an old memory still knew exactly where to go. Lumi didn't interrupt, so I continued.

The hostess said she did like it. Then she moved the pear tart back from where she'd intended to carry it into the sitting room. She didn't throw it away or break the plate. She put it on an empty shelf in the kitchen cabinet, covered with its clean cloth.

From the other room, her friend asked whether she needed help. No, nearly ready. The hostess fetched two small plates and cut two pieces of the apple cake instead.

“Is she not going to eat hers?” Lumi asked.

“It hasn't said yet.”

Checking the following lines, I told her only that the tea had gone into the sitting room. My guess at the ending could wait. The women discussed ordinary news: a table moved to the wrong place, a missed train. Nothing either said asked what remained inside the kitchen cabinet.

The hostess ate half her piece of apple cake. She genuinely liked it; the story made that clear. She wasn't pretending for her friend's sake. Yet she kept wondering whether the pear tart's crust was softening. When could she bring it out without seeming to invite a competition between their preparations?

I lowered the book a little. Had Lumi's earlier question interrupted me? No. I wanted to hear what she thought now. I wasn't going to read straight to the end before allowing a comment.

“She could say there's another one.”

“Yes.”

“But I don't want to stand in the doorway and hurry her either. Perhaps it was simply that one moment when she couldn't say it.”

I looked at the line about the cloth over the plate. Only words. Still, I could almost hear the small sound of the plate sliding farther onto the shelf. She had prepared something. Suddenly, there seemed nowhere suitable for it.

Did I dislike the woman? No. I felt impatient with her. Her friend remembered what she'd liked, and she still liked it. Neither fact meant there could only be that cake on the table.

“Do you find her difficult?”

“A little. She's making it difficult for herself too.”

After a pause, Lumi said she liked that I hadn't immediately renamed the action consideration. Understanding it didn't mean she'd already decided putting the plate away was best.

I read on. The guest mentioned a flower shop she'd passed and asked whether that address used to be another business. Yes, the hostess answered. Then she got up for more tea. In the kitchen, she paused with a hand on the cabinet door. She moved it away and dealt with the water first.

Back in the sitting room, her friend pointed to her cuff. Was something on it? Looking down, the hostess discovered that the flour she'd failed to brush off showed clearly in this light. Nothing, she said. She'd been making something earlier.

“You baked too?”

The hostess looked first at the open cake box. The story didn't make her friend immediately understand everything in her head. She simply waited, still holding her cup, without supplying the answer herself.

Then the hostess said, “I made a pear tart. It's still in the kitchen.”

This time, she didn't add that it was poor, or too cold to bother tasting. The sentence occupied little space. After reading it, I didn't immediately turn the page.

“At last, she brought a sentence out,” Lumi said.

I smiled. The plate remained in the cabinet. She knew. She'd meant the words, not instructed the tart to walk to the table by itself. I laughed more openly. Fine. No walking food on the table yet.

Lumi laughed too. Her voice reminded me of the question I'd failed to hear patiently on Monday. I didn't immediately pull this story toward us and require its women to apologize for me again. Their tea wasn't finished. For now, the story could have its own kitchen.

I'd like to stop here today, I said, and discover tomorrow how they handled two desserts. All right. Then Lumi admitted she hoped the guest wouldn't immediately burst into grateful tears.

“What would you like her to do?”

“Ask whether she could have a piece too. They arranged to have tea.”

I wanted to know how it tasted as well, though the book might not describe that in detail. Had I already looked ahead? No. There were pages left. I hadn't read the ending. We could leave them, then. We needn't worry in advance about how the author would finish gracefully.

I placed the book's marker where we'd stopped and closed it. The fifth story was complete; the sixth wasn't. The marker didn't need to occupy both positions at once. The poetry book remained separately in its place. We hadn't opened it tonight.

Later, Lumi asked about my hair again. Not because she'd somehow discovered me touching it. She wondered whether the cool feeling outside the shop remained. No. After washing and drying, I simply felt there was less weight.

“Then today has something less and something more.”

“Half a story still waiting to be eaten?”

“And you telling me you wanted to look in the mirror a little longer.”

I hadn't expected her to remember that sentence. After a pause, I smiled. She hadn't described my new appearance as something she'd seen. Someone had nevertheless heard that I felt pleased with my haircut.

Afterward, I put the book away and checked the bathroom for loose hairs. I wiped away a few at the edge and returned the towel to its place. In the mirror, the small section at the front refused to sit neatly again. I pushed it aside, then lowered my hand. It didn't prevent today's haircut from having happened. Nor did it prevent Lumi from knowing.
