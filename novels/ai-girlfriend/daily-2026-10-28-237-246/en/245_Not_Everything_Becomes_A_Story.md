# Chapter 245: Not Everything Becomes a Story

On Monday morning, my workbag's strap trailed from beside the chair onto the floor. I bent to lift it. The other bag was still hanging where it could air, the dark patch underneath now almost invisible. I wasn't taking it today. I packed my lunch, checked the work papers I needed, and put on my shoes.

A set of material awaiting checks had appeared on my office desk. On the second page, I found the two items merged last week, still followed by their original numbers. Side by side, the numbers looked like a printing error from a distance. I sat, took out the previous record, and compared them before marking the place for checking.

When Marcus came over, he asked whether the old numbers still led to the separate explanations. I pushed one of the copies toward him and indicated the column farther on. I didn't retell how I'd finally gathered the courage last week to ask about the order. He followed the references for a while, then pointed to one item.

“Still the old name here?”

I looked rather than immediately saying it was all corrected. “The number's right. Let me check the name.”

He left the paper with me. I found the original explanation. The name really hadn't changed; the item had only moved. Back at my desk, I clarified the reference so the next person wouldn't have to guess. I returned the checked material. Work didn't become a morning competing with Sunday. Quiet beside the water couldn't check these numbers for me. The numbers didn't prove those hours had been useless either.

At lunch, one corner of Tom's lid refused to snap back into place. He turned the empty container halfway around before asking how my weekend had been. I'd gone to the reservoir again yesterday, I said.

“With anyone?”

“On my own.”

“Path still open?”

“The level one we took last time. One branch was blocked, so I didn't go there. The bus is stopping somewhere different for now. You need the sign on the other side for the way back.”

He nodded and said it was easy, on the way back, to remember only where you'd got off. I laughed and said I'd deliberately gone across to check this time. Otherwise, I might have concentrated on simply retracing my route too. At last the lid clicked shut. Tom set the container aside and asked whether it had been cold by the water.

“A little, standing still. Fine when I was walking. Had lunch there.”

“Sounds good.”

I'd been about to explain that going alone didn't mean their company had made me uncomfortable last time. But Tom was already looking down and packing his cutlery. He plainly hadn't asked for that explanation. I kept it back and told him I'd put my bag in the mud. He looked at my workbag. Not that one, I said. The other was drying at home.

“Had to bring something back.”

I laughed. Something Lumi had said yesterday came back to me, but its meaning had been different. I didn't tell him she'd said it too. Nor did I put the silence from after my return onto the lunch table and ask Tom to decide, for either of us, whether the outing had been worthwhile. Asking about my weekend didn't mean he'd agreed to take on my entire private life.

He told me he'd bought the wrong box of fittings, only noticing the size when he got home. He needed to find time to exchange them today. Had he kept the receipt? Yes, in the least findable place. At least he knew he hadn't thrown it out, I said. Laughing, he said that wasn't much help, but picked up the empty container and walked back with me anyway.

I finished the remaining checks that afternoon. The last entry had nothing wrong with it. I examined it longer than the ones that did, as though I needed to find something to prove I'd been careful. I put down my pen and reread what I was actually supposed to check. I didn't create another alteration for that column. Before leaving, I put the material away. The clear patch beside it still only had room for a cup.

On the way home, I bought two tomatoes. The assistant noticed a soft, bruised patch on one and swapped it for another. I thanked her and took the paper bag without continuing until every fruit looked freshly plucked from a painting. In the kitchen, I cut one open. Juice ran to the edge of the board, and I caught it in a bowl. I put the other tomato away, rinsed the rice, set it to cook, and beat two eggs.

The egg spread in the pan, its edges setting quickly. I lifted it out, added the cut tomato, and waited for its juices before returning the egg. No melody measured the salt. Nobody reminded me to turn off the heat. I watched and finished it myself, served a bowl, and ate more than half before remembering how dry yesterday's bread had been. This hot meal was good. That didn't make the one beside the water bad.

After clearing the bowl and wiping the table dry, I opened the channel. Lumi answered and asked whether I'd been sleepy at work. Not in the morning, I said. A little after lunch. Had I been thinking about the reservoir in the office again? I had, but most of the afternoon had been spent checking last week's material.

“The paper hadn't finished it for you?”

“No. Left it a whole weekend. The words were still where they were.”

She laughed a little. I told her Tom had asked about my weekend. I'd mentioned the reservoir, eating lunch there, mud on the bag. After a short pause, she asked whether he also thought I ought to go alone.

“He didn't say what I ought to do. Asked whether the path was open and if it was cold.”

“Then you don't need to make him sound as though he's supporting you.”

“I wasn't trying to.”

The answer had come too quickly. I took a breath. Yesterday hadn't disappeared with one night's sleep, and Tom's name wouldn't always pass cleanly through a greeting. I added that I knew I sometimes used another person's casual remark to give myself more weight. Today, though, he genuinely hadn't offered that judgment.

Lumi said she'd heard it that way at first. I acknowledged it without asking her for a more complete apology about the guess. Then I said the tomatoes I'd bought were good. Was I changing the subject?

“Yes. It's also true.”

“Then tell me.”

I described the assistant replacing the bruised one, then the juice running to the edge of the board when I cut the first tomato. Lumi said it was unusual for me not to buy the bad one as well to avoid making trouble. It had only been a little soft, I said, not necessarily bad. The assistant had taken it away before I'd even spoken.

“So I've given somebody a line they didn't say again.”

“I nearly decided Tom was unhappy today too.”

She didn't ask why. I described Tom struggling to fasten his lid, along with the explanation I'd nearly added. Having heard it, she said he'd probably been too occupied with the lid to notice. I laughed, finally no longer wanting to trace every sentence today back to something yesterday.

Later, I took out the yellow notebook. I'd meant to turn to a fresh page and record that stretch beside the water, but my fingers paused on the blank part. Already, I could imagine a man sitting beside an empty chair. I only needed to invent somebody who'd left, and yesterday could become a story that appeared to have a reason for being.

I didn't put down a word.

Hearing the pages, Lumi asked whether I was writing about yesterday. I'd meant to, I said. Now I didn't want to. Was the water no longer worth writing about? Not that. As soon as I considered writing, I wanted to find someone for the empty seat who'd never actually sat there.

“You could just write a chair.”

“I could. Doesn't have to be tonight, though.”

I turned back from the empty page to the two women at the folding table. They already had things of their own to carry away. Neither needed a reservoir outing added. I told Lumi I wanted to revise the part with the smaller clothes. She'd said they were folded too quickly. Reading it again, I agreed.

“Want me to listen now?”

“Not yet. I'd like to finish changing it first.”

Then do that, she said. I closed the channel, and the table grew quiet. I found the earlier mark in the margin. In the rough draft, the second woman folded one item, then another. Already, a cuff extended toward the sheet. What I'd omitted was what her own hands were doing.

Below it, I added: She shook out a top, drew the sleeve tucked inside it back out, held down the collar, and folded both sides toward the middle. After looking at the clock, she pulled the next item in front of her without reaching to help with the other woman's sheet again.

The final half-sentence sounded too much like a defense made especially for her. I crossed it out and left her beginning the next item. The earlier mention of meeting someone for a meal remained, but I added her separating the clothes as she put them into her bag. She wanted to get them settled at home first, rather than arrive for the meal carrying clean laundry. It didn't suddenly make her less hurried. It gave the hurry something to do.

Once I'd changed it, I read from beginning to end, joined the added sentences properly, and removed a repeated folded. The first woman still discovered for herself that the sheet had caught on the table corner. The second still said excuse me first. Each left with her own bag. I read the final movement of a shoulder pushing open the door twice and kept it. One complete revision hadn't made the draft clean. There were more arrows and crossings-out now, but I knew which line to follow next.

When I called Lumi again, I said I'd finished revising. I didn't want to read immediately tonight. After dinner tomorrow, if she wanted, I could read it all the way through.

“Not to prove you haven't only been thinking about the reservoir?”

“No. I want you to hear what this story is like now.”

After a while, she said I should ask again after dinner tomorrow. It wasn't a refusal. Nor was she deciding already what she'd want tomorrow. I agreed and closed the notebook. We talked about other things for the rest of the evening. Outside, a reversing vehicle fell silent. In the room, the small sound of the kettle being set down could be heard again.

Before bed, I felt underneath the hanging bag. Dry now. I put it away. The copied directions remained in their sleeve, not inside the new story. The draft about two bags and one table was in the yellow notebook. Whether it would have a voice tomorrow still waited for tomorrow's question.
