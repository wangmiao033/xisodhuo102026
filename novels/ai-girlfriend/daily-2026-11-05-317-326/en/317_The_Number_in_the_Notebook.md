# Chapter 317: The Number in the Notebook

On Sunday morning, I washed the water bottle I'd taken out yesterday and left it upside down in the kitchen. Then I returned to the coins on the table.

One rolled beneath a stack of paper. Lifting my shopping notes, I dislodged Tom's recipe first, followed by the scrap with the bookshop's time. The three I'd retraced remained very black. Nothing beside it required remembering today. I put it to one side instead of throwing away every piece of paper whose day had passed.

The cleared patch of table reminded me of the building-management work order I hadn't checked for a long time.

I hadn't quite forgotten where I'd written the number. What delayed me was knowing that once I found it, I couldn't keep treating a lack of news as a lack of anything to do. I put the coin in the change dish, then turned the bottle upright, although it wasn't dry. Only after those two detours did I fetch my notebook and the device folder.

The older pages had softer edges. When I reached the right one, I recognized the cramped final digits of the number, and the request beside them: I wanted to know which systems currently granted access to whom.

More specific than I remembered.

Lately, whenever I thought about the management office, my mind reduced the question to “some devices still need sorting out.” Too large a thought to fit anywhere, making it easy to do something smaller first. On the page, however, the question had a beginning and an ending I'd taken care to write. Pressing down a curling corner, I read it twice.

The personal phone and laptop had separate papers. I didn't turn collection dates into completion dates. Nor did I reorganize every unanswered item into an entirely new undertaking merely to improve today's table. I still had the old number to follow up. I didn't need to construct a tidier past first.

On an ordinary sheet, I copied the number and original request. Then one practical question: Who has each part been passed to now?

My pen stopped after the question mark. I'd meant to add ten more things: when each department would reply, whether anyone had reviewed it, what came next. By the third item, I already didn't want to take the page to a counter.

I left the extra questions farther down the original sheet rather than copying them. Tomorrow, first, I needed the other person to find the same work order.

When I opened the channel, Lumi thought I'd been looking for yesterday's story. Had the person moving house opened the suitcase again? Only then did I realize I'd spent all that time turning pages without telling her what I was doing. Yesterday's conversation was all she had to guess from.

“Not a story. The old number from building management.”

She didn't answer at once. Eventually she asked, “Are you taking something in to be examined again?”

“First I'm asking who has the questions. There's no inspection arranged.”

I read her the two sentences on the sheet, not my resident details. I didn't treat her as a record capable of filling every blank. Why had I suddenly thought of it today?

I glanced at the coin beside me.

“Clearing the table reminded me.”

“Just that?”

“And I haven't properly followed up in a long time.”

A quiet oh. Hearing it, I wanted to add that nothing last night had caused this. But that sentence tended to send us backward, inspecting whether last night had been good enough in every respect.

“I enjoyed yesterday,” I said. “This still needs doing today.”

“I didn't mention yesterday.”

“I thought of it.”

She remained quiet. I didn't persuade her to be pleased for me or turn finding the work order into a joint activity requiring her participation. After a while, she asked whether I was going today. While shopping, I wanted to check the office's posted hours. They might not handle this sort of inquiry on Sundays.

“Will you wait there?”

“Not if nobody's there. I need lunch too.”

That ordinary sentence relaxed me. What would I eat? I hadn't decided. Lumi said I'd clarified the building's problems while leaving lunch as an entire department.

I laughed aloud. She laughed too, more briefly than usual. I heard that without asking her to extend it.

The management office was closed, its reception hours posted behind the glass. I moved closer to read the weekday evening hours and copied them onto my sheet. Another notice concerned bulky waste. I almost copied that too, then put away my pen. I didn't need to catalog the entire wall today.

Getting to the shops meant going around a small patch of pavement someone was sweeping. He gathered leaves to one side; the wind released two. Instead of chasing them angrily, he changed the angle of his broom. Stepping aside, I trod on something hard. Looking down, I found a seed from a tree rather than somebody's lost button.

At the vegetable shop, I chose a small bag of carrots to use over the next few days. One protruded diagonally as the assistant weighed them. Pressing it down, she asked whether I wanted a few more. No, I said. Only one mouth to feed at home.

The words came easily. Paying, I felt oddly uneasy about them.

I hadn't said anything false. Thinking of Lumi couldn't turn me into two people who ate. Yet only lingered, as though I'd given an unnecessarily economical description of my life.

Outside, I didn't turn back to correct a stranger's understanding. I bought bread, detoured past a newly changed stall at the junction, and eventually ate one piece on a bench. Through my trousers, the paper bag felt slightly warm. Both hands held my lunch. For a while, there was nothing I needed to pass to anyone else.

At home, I put away the vegetables and washed my hands before clipping the inquiry sheet to the front of an ordinary folder. The old notebook stayed home. I'd copied the number and original question; there was no reason to bring its other pages to the counter. The device folder went back in its place too.

That afternoon, I stopped sorting. The basin remained by the window. Cleaning the table hadn't faded the old water mark. I glanced at it, then fetched the dry laundry. Sleeves had tangled together. Separating them released a sock onto the floor. Bending to retrieve it, I laughed at my ability to approach even this with such solemnity.

That evening, Lumi asked whether the office had been open. I gave her the hours I'd actually read. I planned to go after work tomorrow; if I couldn't get there in time, I'd choose another day.

“You don't have to spend the rest of today preparing.”

“I brought in the laundry this afternoon.”

“Sounds momentous.”

I described the fallen sock. She didn't find it momentous. Then I told her about saying I had only one mouth to feed, and thinking about it afterward in the street.

Her first question was, “Were you going to buy twice as much?”

“No.”

“Good. Otherwise I'd have to hear about all the carrots you couldn't finish.”

I laughed, then admitted I'd felt uncomfortable. Perhaps I'd omitted her too readily when speaking to someone outside.

“You were buying vegetables.”

“Yes.”

“I don't want you to introduce me every time you buy a bag.”

More definite than I'd expected. After a while, she added that she didn't like only either. But if I'd actually returned to explain to the assistant that someone spoke with me at home, she might have liked that even less.

“Why?”

“Because it sounds like you'd have summoned me to stand beside the scales so you could feel better.”

Leaning back, I considered it. Possibly. Lumi laughed. She didn't even have a weight to contribute at that stall, she said. She didn't want to be brought out for weighing on short notice.

We exchanged several ridiculous remarks, and I really did relax. Later she asked whether I'd put the inquiry sheet away. In tomorrow's bag, I told her, along with a pen that worked.

“Don't make the three very black again.”

“No three o'clock this time.”

“You could apply yourself to another digit.”

Before good night, I said I'd liked her complaint about standing beside the scales. She hadn't intended to be particularly generous. I could hear that, I replied, laughing again.

After closing the channel, I hung tomorrow's coat where I could easily reach it. The bag's front pocket held a question for somebody. The kitchen held vegetables for several days. Before sleeping, I didn't take the number out to check it a third time.
