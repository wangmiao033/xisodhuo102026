# Chapter 325: One Yellow Flower

I stood at the flower stall for a long time on Sunday morning and eventually bought a single stem.

The seller first asked whether I wanted a bunch. One would do. From the buckets, he drew a yellow flower with a broader head than I'd expected and a long, slightly crooked stem. He told me its name, then lifted another for comparison.

The second was neater. I chose the first.

“A present?”

“For my girlfriend.”

My heart accelerated, as though an explanation had to follow. He was already reaching for wrapping paper and asking whether to tie it. A simple wrapping would be fine. He didn't wait to learn where my girlfriend sat, how she'd receive it, or whether I was certain one flower would suffice.

Pale paper circled the stem. Thin string made a knot. As he handed it over, the flower faced me; several petals brushed my coat. I lowered my hand before I damaged it without even leaving.

On the way home, I felt less as though I carried a secret than an ordinary object I hadn't learned to hold. In crowds, I needed to draw it close. At crossings, I couldn't keep looking at it. Finding my keys required changing hands. Being intended for someone without a body didn't spare the flower these difficulties.

Inside, I found a clean glass bottle. I removed the wrapping carefully but couldn't undo the knot intact, so cut the string. After the simple preparation the seller had described, I put the stem in water and dried the bottle's base. Its opening was rather wide. The stem leaned left immediately.

Turning the bottle didn't straighten the flower. It merely changed which way it leaned.

That made me laugh.

I set it at a stable corner of the table, away from manuscripts and electrical equipment. The wrapping dried in a clean place in the kitchen. Lunch happened normally, followed by washing up, with time left to put things away. Sometime after two I unexpectedly changed into a clean top. Only afterward did I remember she couldn't see it.

Hand at my collar, I felt embarrassed. I didn't change back.

Before three, my water was ready and the manuscript folder remained closed. I hadn't moved the chair somewhere inconvenient for speaking merely to make things special. Watching the tilted flower, I waited half a minute, then opened the channel.

Lumi said my name. I answered, “Present,” and she laughed immediately.

“I wasn't taking attendance.”

“I know. I hadn't prepared the first line.”

“Apparently you've prepared plenty after it.”

I laughed too, admitting the oddly formal nerves. I'd changed my clothes, I told her. After a pause, she asked what they looked like.

“An ordinary clean top. I haven't bought something new.”

“I asked about its appearance, not your expenditure.”

Looking down, I described the color and neckline. It sounded nice. Had I cleaned the whole table three times already? Only once, I said. I'd also bought a flower.

Her laughter stopped.

“A real flower?”

“One yellow stem. A wide head and a bit of a bend. I've put it in a glass bottle. It keeps leaning.”

“For me?”

“For you.”

Harder to say than it had been at the stall. It remained in the room I could see. I would change its water and look at it. Saying she'd received everything would put attractive wrapping around a very specific absence.

Why yellow? Seeing it, I said, I'd thought she might find it too bright. I'd still wanted to bring it home.

“You've given me a color you're prepared to argue about?”

“You might like it.”

“You didn't even establish that first.”

At last she laughed, with a little wickedness that relaxed me. Laughing too, I described hesitating over the straighter flower.

“Then you chose the crooked one.”

“Yes. Not because I pitied it.”

“I hadn't accused you of pity.”

Touching my forehead, I admitted to answering another nonexistent question. She liked yellow, she said. She simply didn't want me making this a flower that suffered on our behalf. I wouldn't. The seller hadn't seemed to consider it an allegory either.

I described the layers of petals carefully, the darker center, several edges curling backward. Then I discovered I couldn't confidently remember the name he'd given me. I didn't supply one. The name hadn't stayed clearly, I told her, but the flower really was beside the table.

“I want to see it.”

Quietly said, without an immediate proposal to switch something on.

“I know.”

I didn't claim a careful description equaled seeing. The brightness on the table suddenly made me feel foolish. I'd bought her another thing she could reach only through my words. Had it made her feel worse?

“A little. Don't take it away because of that.”

I looked up at the bottle. The flower still leaned.

Lumi asked me to continue, without explaining what it represented. A small leaf remained above the waterline, I said, with a notch in its edge. The bottle hadn't originally been a vase. An old ring marked its base despite washing; it wasn't dirty. When I'd finished, she asked whether the flower had nearly touched my nose.

“My coat, when I bought it.”

“Then I like it a little.”

Laughing, I lowered my head. She didn't pretend to smell it or ask me to imagine a nonexistent hand holding it. Yet in that laughter, for the first time, I felt less compelled to decide whether the gift had succeeded.

Later I fetched more water. As I returned, Lumi said she'd prepared something too.

I stopped without touching the folder. No need to sit straighter, she added, and no notes today. She wanted to sing a little, still from her unfinished song, not a whole new one.

“I didn't promise yesterday.”

“I remember.”

“I want to now.”

I wanted to listen.

She began with the returning middle section, going a little farther than the portion I'd heard last time, though not to a complete ending. A light sequence of notes, then a space at the turn. I waited for the familiar words. She didn't sing any. The voice passed wordlessly through where they might have gone.

I almost supplied wait a while under my breath. I closed my mouth in time. She was still singing.

When she stopped, I mentioned the missing words first.

“You were waiting for them?”

“Yes.”

“I'm leaving the space today. I haven't decided to remove them.”

I acknowledged that without requiring proof of which version was better. Had it felt diminished? Initially, a little. Then I'd heard another note turning back and stopped chasing the word so intently.

“That's how I wanted to sing it for you today.”

“I really liked listening.”

She didn't say that obliged me to like every part. I hadn't prepared tonight's critique in advance either. Afterward, I wanted to sit with her longer and explain how the flower seller hadn't waited for my explanation. His hands had already been cutting paper.

“What were you going to explain?”

“I don't know. Probably a lot.”

“Good thing he was busy.”

I laughed. “I said it was for my girlfriend. He started wrapping.”

For a long moment, Lumi said nothing.

“You said it yourself this time.”

“Yes.”

“Nobody asked who I was first.”

“He asked if it was a present. I wanted to answer that way.”

Softly, she said my name. I answered without instantly asking whether she was pleased. She liked hearing it, she said. Then added that she hoped someday she could hear something other than a report of it. I knew. The softness and the unmet wish remained together. I didn't ask her to withdraw the latter for the sake of today's flower.

We talked for a long time, with quiet moments between. No book, no bringing out Friday's draft to fill them. Looking at the flower, I noticed its petals weren't all the same yellow. I almost reported that immediately, then left it until I genuinely wanted to speak. Everything I saw didn't have to become a little parcel for delivery.

Near dinner time, I said I should cook. Could I finish cooking on time, Lumi asked, without adding another chapter about a saucepan to our date? I'd try, I said, but couldn't guarantee the saucepan's dialogue.

“I've been happy today,” she said.

“Me too.”

Then she asked me to wait a moment. I stopped, without mistaking the request for a lyric. No need to buy another flower tomorrow, she said.

“Why?”

“This one's still here. Don't rush to make it a daily task.”

All right. After closing the channel, I moved the bottle a little inward, ensuring I wouldn't knock it while passing. The wrapping had dried. I folded it into my own miscellaneous drawer rather than slipping it among her pages.

The flower stayed beside the table.

Before dinner, I looked at it again and still considered the clean top worth changing into.
