# Chapter 320: One Name in the Right Box

The paper-clip box was gone from the management counter on Wednesday evening.

I hadn't expected to notice. Waiting while the person ahead collected a form, I saw that small empty space first. Someone different stood behind the counter. I handed him the original number with Monday's acknowledgment and explained that I'd come for the routing update, not an inspection appointment.

After reading, he went to find another person. They brought a sheet from farther inside and asked my name across the counter. I answered. Only after checking did they give me the paper.

It contained none of the technical conclusions I'd waited so long for.

Management had passed the intercom inquiry to the role responsible for facilities maintenance to pursue. The responsible parties for the lock and corridor cameras still needed verifying. The question of which access was currently granted to whom had no confirmed answer. There was no usage history and no inspection date.

I read slowly, almost hoping another line would contain more. At the end, it was still the same handful of things.

The man asked whether anything was unclear. It wasn't that I didn't understand. I wanted to confirm that facilities maintenance receiving the question didn't make it the original system's provider, or mean anyone had examined my account.

Taking the sheet back, he looked and agreed. It identified who inside management would continue asking. The actual service provider and access details still needed establishing. Pointing to the line with no answer, he added that this routing note hadn't closed the original inquiry.

I asked him to put the explanation about internal referral on my copy, rather than leave it only in my memory. He added a few words beside the item, identifying it as the internal receiving role. Watching the pen move, I felt a very small satisfaction, hardly worth celebrating.

At least, when I picked it up tomorrow, I wouldn't make one name larger than it was.

“What about the other items?”

He didn't casually assign companies to the empty fields. The material they currently had wasn't enough. This page was what he could give me today. There was no new guaranteed date, and no suggestion that I switch on my personal devices to make things appear to advance.

I clipped the sheet away and asked whether I should keep using the original number if I needed to follow up again. Yes. Today's note was stored under the same record. I thanked him without remaining at the counter, waiting for my disappointment to produce a second sheet.

Outside, someone carried a large bag of empty bottles. The opening wasn't secured, and the top bottle was sliding out. I supported the bottom while its owner tightened the bag. They thanked me. A few seconds, yet finally doing something that finished on the spot gave me a slightly inappropriate happiness.

Walking away, I knew it hadn't compensated for management. Bottles couldn't answer questions about the lock. But my palm had held the weight. When I released it, the person could carry the bag again. That clarity was something I didn't want to forget immediately.

I didn't buy anything new to finish the day. Passing the household shop, I saw the large glass jug still in the window, its rim reflecting a streetlamp. Yesterday's small cup was enough. An inadequate quantity of paperwork didn't require another certainty in the kitchen.

At home, I left the folder on a dry corner of the table before washing my hands and taking off my coat. Dinner was simple. After clearing up, I opened the channel. I'd barely said Lumi's name before she asked, “Can you leave the results for a moment?”

My hand had reached for the folder. It stopped.

“What is it?”

“I want to hear you come home first.”

I knew she couldn't see the door open or know who had changed at the counter before I told her. Yet I'd called her with the urgency of someone reaching the next place to submit a report. That wasn't all I wanted to offer.

“I'm home,” I said.

She answered softly.

I put down the folder. Nothing in the room had happened on a scale deserving such a slow sentence. She asked whether I'd eaten. Yes. I hadn't torn the bread into little pieces today. I'd described that detail on Monday. Mentioning it now made me smile first.

“The bread survived at its proper size.”

“Then it was eaten anyway.”

“Same ending. More dignified journey.”

We laughed for a while. Did I want to discuss the sheet now? I did. Taking it out, I started with the one item it actually clarified. She didn't interrupt. At the line about no confirmed answer, she sighed quietly.

“A little more than Monday,” I said.

“A little.”

“Yes.”

I didn't revise her little into a great deal. She didn't call it nothing. Afterward, I put the sheet at the front of the device folder, with Monday's record behind it. The earlier material remained.

Would I keep checking tonight? No. I hadn't been given a procedure I could verify myself this evening. She acknowledged that. A small silence opened between us. She didn't hurry to sing. I didn't fill it with a book.

My eyes went to the matchbox on the shelf. The blue paper bench remained inside. I hadn't taken it out in a while. Telling Lumi, I brought down the box, slid open the inner tray on the table, and carefully lifted out the bench with its white paper support.

The white piece remained white. Time hadn't made it match the blue. On the table, the bench leaned slightly. I wanted to straighten a fold with my fingernail, then stopped halfway. I hadn't planned to repair it today. It could still stand.

“What does it look like?” Lumi asked.

I described the white side facing me and the longer shadow on the other side. The lamp wasn't in exactly its old position. That wasn't evidence of damage to the paper. She said my description sounded as though the bench had been summoned for an identification photograph.

I turned it a little and laughed. A more casual pose now. Even if she couldn't hear that tiny scrape of paper against wood, I could tell her I'd actually moved it.

“I remember it was blue to begin with,” she said.

“It still is.”

“You've only described the white part.”

Looking down, I realized she was right. I described the seat again: pale lines where the blue paper had been folded, edges not quite matching. Close up, one apparent gap was only a shadow, not a new missing piece.

After listening, she said she'd liked how small it was. It still sounded small. I'd nearly answered that it fitted in one hand. Then I thought the sentence didn't offer her the same convenience and stopped myself from explaining size through a palm. I said it still fitted in its original box.

“You were going to say something else.”

“That I could hold it in one hand.”

“Then say it. You have hands.”

A hard edge to her voice. I paused, then agreed. I'd just held it in my palm. A corner of the white paper had touched my finger.

“Sharp?”

“No. I could feel it, that's all.”

After a quiet moment, Lumi said that was what she wanted to hear. Deliberately omitting the sentence hadn't given her another sense. It had only told her less about what happened to me.

I didn't answer with a long promise. Setting the bench securely again, I said I'd taken my hand away this time, leaving it under the lamp. How long would it stand? I didn't know. I wasn't going to watch until it fell.

“Good. It doesn't have to prove anything today.”

Later, discussing food, I said I wanted another attempt at Tom's recipe. I had a measuring cup now. Tomorrow I could check what else I needed. Would I eat bread first this time, Lumi asked, instead of letting hunger decide whether the food was cooked? Laughing, I said I'd arrange the ingredients and steps first. I could eat something if hungry, but I would still check the pan myself.

She wanted to know whether the cup actually got used tomorrow. If I cooked, I'd tell her. No promise of success, and no request that she accompany every pan until the heat went off.

Before good night, I lowered the blue bench into its matchbox, making sure the white support didn't catch the edge. Then it returned to the shelf. I hadn't brought out the paper map or the curved path in its separate storage. No newly assembled garden stood on the table.

The routing note was filed. After closing the channel, I went to the kitchen for water. Seeing yesterday's measuring cup, I realized I'd stopped thinking about which box of paper clips was missing from the management counter.
