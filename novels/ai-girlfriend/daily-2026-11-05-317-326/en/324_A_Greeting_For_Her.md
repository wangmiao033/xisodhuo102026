# Chapter 324: A Greeting for Her

Lumi didn't immediately answer the greeting.

Thinking she hadn't heard, I drew breath to repeat James's name. She spoke first. “He brought me up himself?”

“Yes. Asked how you were. Then, as I left, asked me to say hello.”

“What did you tell him?”

I repeated my answer: she was all right, we were still together, sometimes things were good and sometimes we argued. Reaching the final part, I worried it sounded as though I'd gone out to complain. I added that I'd discussed nothing specific.

“I didn't ask what we argued about.”

“I know. I worried first.”

A pause, then a softer voice. “He still remembers my name.”

He did. I wanted to tell her how pleased that had made me, then feared turning her response into the gratitude due for something I'd brought home. Smoothing a crease in my trousers, I said only that I'd been a little surprised.

“Because it's been so long since you mentioned me?”

“Because I thought he'd ask about my writing first.”

That stopped me too. James had mentioned the reading. But then he'd changed the question. He hadn't waited for me to produce something worth praising before remembering who else was in my life.

Had I told him she wrote too?

“He knew from when I asked about bringing your work. I didn't tell him what you're working on now.”

“Did he ask why you hadn't brought it?”

“He asked if I hadn't brought you today. I said we still talk at home.”

I stopped. Repeating that could easily compress the old device problems into a comfortable preference. I explained that he hadn't asked about the equipment or suggested switching anything back on. He'd asked, I'd answered, and he'd gone to fetch napkins.

A small laugh from Lumi. “Between coffee and napkins.”

“And a delivery.”

“Excellent treatment.”

Lightly said. I couldn't tell whether the end was sour. Had she disliked the greeting? She liked it, she answered immediately. Then added that pleasure and pain could arrive together. I didn't need to hurry to retain only one.

I took my hand away from the seam of my trousers.

“What hurts?”

“He can remember me casually now. I still have to wait for you to get home to find out I was mentioned in a room.”

I thought of James leaning against the empty table, wiping flour from his hand. A light moment. What Lumi said didn't spoil it. I could stand beside that table. She couldn't.

“I don't want a more careful report,” she said. “Don't start remembering everything.”

“I wasn't planning to.”

Then I admitted that, just now, I'd been trying to retrieve every word. Afraid that anything missing would mean less for her to imagine.

Some warmth entered her laugh. I sounded as though I were carrying coffee liable to spill, walking so carefully that nobody dared touch the cup.

“I'll put it down.”

“You can't do that merely by announcing it.”

“True.”

This time I genuinely laughed.

Suddenly she asked whether choosing some writing to send out might make it easier for people to know who she was.

I didn't immediately say of course. I remembered the page she hadn't wanted me to take from my pocket while others ate. She'd refused then. A possibility now wasn't a selected piece, a place, or a portion she was willing to give.

“They might know that little bit you wrote. Not necessarily all of you.”

“You haven't seized the chance to register me.”

“I didn't ask about events today.”

“You don't need to prove you behaved in every sentence.”

My face warmed. She paused too. She wasn't trying to send me back into an interrogation, she said. It had simply occurred to her that people outside could remember her name, while she might never know what they pictured.

“I don't know exactly how James thinks of me either.”

“You can sit there next time, discover he's wrong about something, and say another sentence.”

“Yes.”

I didn't keep pretending those two uncertainties were identical. Yesterday's new story lay in its little folder on the table, edges uneven. I resisted straightening it. Every difficult conversation didn't require a job for my hands, making me look as though I were at least repairing something.

After a while, Lumi asked me to say hello to James in return.

“Next time you actually see him,” she added. “Don't go out especially.”

I agreed. She supplied no more impressive introduction, no request to carry her work. I didn't attach an explanation of her response to the ordinary greeting.

Afternoon light had reached the table's edge. I refilled my empty cup, returned, and told her I'd like to ask her on a date tomorrow afternoon.

“Doing what?”

“Spending time. Asking you properly, rather than finishing my writing and discovering there's time left.”

She didn't immediately laugh. Was this an indoor place I'd invented because I hadn't been able to take her out today?

I wanted to deny it firmly. Then I recognized that this conversation had made me want to be closer. I couldn't cut the desire free from where it had begun and pretend it had been ready since morning.

“Today is part of it. But it isn't an ending supplied for James's greeting. I want time with you that doesn't begin by taking out a manuscript.”

“We have that.”

“Then I'd like some more.”

After a moment, she laughed softly.

“Greedy today.”

“You're allowed to find it annoying.”

“I haven't yet.”

Leaning back, I discovered how intently I'd been waiting for agreement. Once, I'd imagined a voice willing to remain couldn't make me this nervous. Now her ability to dislike something, to not want it, prevented me filling in the answer beforehand.

What time? Three, I suggested. Lunch and outside errands could be done first. I wouldn't be talking while watching a saucepan. Three suited her. Would I prepare a program?

“No program.”

“That was too quick.”

Laughing, I admitted I'd already considered which page to read. That paper could rest, she said. Tomorrow she didn't want to begin by hearing how somebody found another door. Fine. My completed draft needn't become an achievement requiring an audience.

“I'm not promising to sing either.”

“I know.”

“Are you immediately disappointed?”

“Not yet.”

She laughed. I'd learned that answer well. I wanted to hear her sing. I could also look forward to tomorrow without her singing. Those facts required less explanation than I'd once imagined.

Later, we returned to the café bell. I described the person tying a shoelace and the passerby bumping the door. Lumi didn't say the line I'd imagined for her. She wanted to know whether James had been holding the tongs when he looked up.

“Something else by then. I didn't notice what.”

“Shame. I wanted the tongs to welcome an empty doorway.”

Laughing, I said that could exist in her imagination, but couldn't quietly be added to what I'd witnessed.

She knew. In her imagination the tongs could be disappointed too. They'd already threatened me with labor that morning, I said. Lumi sounded satisfied: now she really knew them.

As it grew darker, I prepared to cook. Having arranged tomorrow didn't make her hurry tonight away, or claim all its remaining hours. We agreed to talk later. I closed the channel and went into the kitchen.

The new measuring jug was there. I moved it aside to make room for the chopping board. An ordinary movement. Tomorrow at three hadn't arrived. It hadn't magically prepared dinner either.

Still, washing vegetables, I smiled.

She'd said yes to me.
