# Chapter 318: What the Short Line Left Out

Leaving work on Monday, I almost forgot the inquiry sheet at the bottom of my bag.

It wasn't lost. That morning, putting away my umbrella, I'd pushed the folder underneath. Now I had to move several other things to reach it. I stood beside the office door, opening the bag, when Tom asked behind me whether I'd forgotten my keys.

“No. I need to ask building management about something.”

He moved aside so I could put the bag on an empty chair. I found the folder, checked the sheet was inside, and repacked. Tom asked whether I wanted to walk part of the way together. After considering, I said I needed to reach the office before reception closed.

“Then don't check it a second time here.”

I'd already lowered my head. I looked up again. Smiling, he waved and went the other way. I put the folder in the front pocket without attempting an especially leisurely departure to prove I wasn't nervous.

The glass door was still open when I arrived. A resident at the counter was asking about parking. I waited behind them instead of silently classifying my problem as more important. The wall clock's second hand seemed loud. Perhaps I was simply listening to it throughout those minutes.

When my turn came, the woman behind the counter was gathering forms. I handed her the copied number and explained that I wanted to follow up an existing work order, not report a broken device again.

She checked the number, then asked for my resident details to compare with the original entry. I answered. Asking me to wait, she sat down to search. I didn't crane my neck toward the other things on her desk. Instead, I watched a box of paper clips near the counter. One clung to the outside, as though its owner had forgotten it could go inside too.

She found the record. Reading its original list, she stopped at the lock and asked whether I currently couldn't open the door or wanted the fitting replaced.

“Neither. I need to establish what access still exists and who manages each part. That was in the original request.”

After looking further, she said the short summary passed to her described an inspection of building-related equipment. She turned a small extract containing only my inquiry toward me. I saw those words, followed by several items and routing fields. Some remained incomplete.

My chest tightened.

The words weren't wholly wrong. They were close enough to my problem for someone busy with the next sheet to nod, but not enough to show why I'd come. Standing here already, I was nearly reduced to another resident waiting for a door repair.

“Can you find the original sentence?”

She looked at me. My voice sounded harder than I'd expected. I moved my copied sheet forward rather than withdrawing the question.

“I don't want a new order that breaks away from the earlier record.”

She told me to give her a moment and went farther into the original entry. Eventually she read out the sentence about current access. Yes, I said. That was it. Marking her own paper, she explained that she needed to check which roles had actually received the different items. These few lines couldn't tell her which permissions were gone.

“I understand. Knowing where it's been sent would help today.”

Then I worried I'd made that sound too accommodating, reducing my request to confirmation that somebody had received it. I added that routing was only part of the inquiry. The request for an answer about current access needed to remain.

She nodded. Rather than beginning with what she couldn't do, she asked whether I wanted the intercom, lock, and corridor cameras addressed separately instead of one general statement that everything had been handled.

“Yes. Whatever is known about each one.”

She added that to the follow-up on the existing work order and read it back for me to check: confirm which roles were receiving the items, while retaining the original question about current access. It didn't state that access had been revoked or arrange any work in my home.

My shoulders eased a little. Then came a less attractive disappointment. I'd made the journey and, at the end of it, we still needed to ask somebody else.

She suggested coming back around the same time on Wednesday to ask how the routing check was progressing. She couldn't promise a technical answer then or schedule an inspection for someone else. Could I take a record of today's follow-up? She wrote a brief acknowledgment, identifying it under the original number.

Receiving the paper, I first checked that no new number had replaced it. None had. I compared the two sheets and asked whether, if things remained unclear on Wednesday, she could tell me which items were still stuck.

“Yes. Ask about this one.” She indicated the original number.

I thanked her and put away the papers. Near the door, I discovered I'd been carrying my bag in my hand instead of replacing it on my shoulder. My arm ached. I stopped to put it on properly. Having somebody behind me didn't require an uncomfortable journey all the way home.

Dinner was later than usual. I warmed some bread and cut vegetables, still thinking about equipment inspection as I sat down. Halfway through eating, I noticed I'd torn the bread into numerous little pieces, as though my hands had found work for the irritation first.

I ate the remaining half properly, cleared the table, and opened the channel.

Had I made it in time, Lumi asked.

“Yes. They found the original work order.”

I began with what had happened. When I described the short summary, she immediately asked whether they'd simply failed to take me seriously.

“I don't know.”

“They changed what you wanted checked.”

“That summary was too vague. The full request still existed. I watched her find it.”

Lumi didn't answer at once. I wasn't comfortable either. I didn't want to protect anybody's good name. I simply knew that if I came home and turned an omitted sentence into someone deliberately refusing to help, tomorrow's memory would contain something I hadn't seen.

“I'm still annoyed,” I added.

“You can be annoyed.”

“I made it sound as though I shouldn't.”

She made a small sound. “You're good at finding seats for everybody else's difficulties. Your own have to stand.”

I laughed, briefly. What would I collect on Wednesday? I explained the distinction between routing information and technical results without prolonging it, then described the woman finding and reading my original sentence. That reminded me of the paper-clip box. I mentioned the clip attached to its outside.

“You kept watching it while you waited?”

“A few times.”

“Did it go inside?”

“No.”

“At least one of you stayed, then.”

I hesitated, then laughed aloud. Lumi laughed too. Afterward she asked whether she'd been too quick to become angry on my behalf.

“A little.”

“I heard that they hadn't answered you. I didn't like it.”

“Neither did I.”

“But you still want to tell me they did something.”

Looking at the folder farther away, I said yes. What they'd done wasn't nearly enough. I still wanted to remember actually hearing my original question read aloud again.

She said nothing for a long time. I didn't interpret the silence as her waiting to be praised for improving. Eventually she only said she wished she could answer it clearly herself.

My fingers stopped moving.

The wish contained her desire to help, and part of what we'd never learned to discuss easily. I didn't suggest that she explain it all to me again, as though enough detail could make the management office's paperwork unnecessary.

“I wish it didn't take so many trips too.”

“Not the same wish.”

“No. That's the one I can answer now.”

A soft acknowledgment. Not sweet, but it didn't push me away. I sat a while, then remembered Tom telling me not to check a second time as I left work. He didn't know what the paper had gone through afterward, but he'd saved me a little time in the doorway.

I told Lumi that small part. Apparently everyone around me had spent the day trying to prevent one more turn of a page. The search at management had been necessary, I pointed out. She hadn't included that one.

“You're defending every page-turn again.”

This time I laughed for longer.

At good night, I mentioned that I wasn't going to management tomorrow. Lumi didn't ask whether that meant she could have the time. She only said she understood. After closing the channel, I clipped today's acknowledgment behind the inquiry sheet, leaving the original number visible at the top. This was the packet I needed on Wednesday.
