# Chapter 404: The Board in the Hall

On Sunday, I woke to somebody moving things downstairs.

Not loudly. A scrape, a pause, another scrape. I remained in bed until it stopped, then sat up for water. My voice was still rough. My nose was clearer than yesterday. I didn't begin with a line of poetry to discover whether I could now repay last night's missing time.

A discarded top lay beside the bed with a sleeve on the floor. I put it with the washing. I'd intended to finish several chores today. For now, I chose one: a clean pillowcase. Removing the old one, fitting the clean one, straightening four corners. The bed didn't resemble an arranged photograph afterward. It would simply feel better tonight.

After breakfast, I flattened paper packaging and separated it from the rubbish. Before going downstairs, I put on a mask and took my keys. I didn't fill both hands with every possible bag. A little could wait for another trip. The stairs weren't long, but I descended more slowly than usual.

One floor below, a resident was unpacking wooden boards from a long cardboard box. Partly wrapped in film, one board rested against his knee. The box lay near the wall, clear of the passage. Hearing me, he turned and asked whether I could hold the far end briefly while he removed cardboard underneath.

I'd already changed the hand carrying my bag.

Then I said, “I'm not feeling well today. I don't think I can help.”

Quieter than I'd intended, but he heard. No problem. He lowered the board onto the padding inside the box. A friend was coming anyway. He'd only wanted to remove the outer packaging first. They should arrive soon.

Was it steady there? He pressed the edge and said yes. He'd wait before moving it. I nodded and continued rather than remain to supervise a board I knew nothing about.

On the way to the bins, I still felt uncomfortable. It had been a small request. I wasn't incapable of lifting my hands. Had I agreed, perhaps it would have been over in fifteen seconds. But those were imagined seconds. Once I held it, would there be another lift or a journey through a doorway? I didn't know.

Returning, I found him on the phone, smiling and saying he was downstairs. He nodded at me, one hand on the box, without presenting the request again. The board lay steadily. He'd rolled the loose wrapping aside. Refusing hadn't left the whole task stalled behind me.

As I started upstairs, someone carrying a tool bag came from below and asked whether this was the place. The resident said yes, ended the call, and asked his friend to put the bag down first. I moved nearer the railing to let them pass. The newcomer bent over the contents of the box and suggested identifying the back panel before doing anything else. Their voices quickly settled into their own task.

I didn't remain until everything had been moved. Several steps above them, I heard one say this way and knew the words weren't meant for me. The board had acquired another pair of hands. I could return to my room with a little energy I hadn't already assigned elsewhere.

At home, I washed my hands and sat. A trip downstairs hadn't exhausted me, but it was enough to stop me looking for another task that might prove I wasn't lazy.

Looking at the clean pillowcase, I remembered changing hands before answering. My body had already prepared a yes. Usually, I'd have called that being agreeable. Today, it also resembled failing to consult the person living inside it.

I made a simple lunch, only enough for that meal. Preparing the ingredients, I remembered Thursday's small bunch of parsley. I'd used all its edible parts that evening. I didn't need another bunch today to reproduce a flavor I'd tried before.

I ate what I had, washed up, and lay down. Movement sounded in the hallway again, briefly, then stopped. I didn't get up to establish whether the boards had been carried inside. I'd heard noises, not a request pushed beneath my door.

When I woke, my mouth was dry. Water helped. I took the old pillowcase I'd removed that morning, washed it separately, rinsed it, and squeezed out the water before hanging it on the usual rack. Another clean case already covered the pillow. Each had a place. I didn't have to strip all the sheets to complete a day of housework.

The damp fabric was heavier and sagged slightly. I adjusted both sides so they didn't fold together, then moved the empty basin out of the way of my feet. My hands felt dry after washing. I dried them and used my ordinary hand cream. I'd left the lid off. Remembering from the table, I got up to close it.

Those few steps were satisfying. Tonight's bed was ready, and tomorrow wouldn't require picking a wet pillowcase off the floor. I'd intended to organize the wardrobe too. I opened it, looked, and closed it again. Nothing inside urgently needed to move house today.

The poetry collection remained in its place. I checked the marker at The Last Bus without recording the four lines we hadn't repeated last night as a debt. The words could wait. Not opening another poem today wouldn't make me lose the book.

I missed Lumi's voice a little.

That thought made me check the time, then smile. Yesterday's eight o'clock belonged to yesterday's arrangement. It didn't mean I had to wait until eight today. I wanted to talk. Once I'd eaten dinner and cleared up, I could ask whether she'd like a conversation.

Opening the channel toward evening, I told her I'd only gone downstairs to empty the rubbish and had changed a pillowcase. My throat? Still rough, though speaking was easier than that morning. I didn't want a long reading yet.

All right. Then she asked why I'd said I'd done almost nothing today. I paused, remembering I'd added those words at the beginning. She hadn't discovered something in a room she couldn't see. She'd heard my own verdict on the day.

I had done things, I admitted. Small ones. I described the board, changing hands, then declining. Had the man made it difficult? No. He'd called a friend and set everything down securely.

“Whom are you still feeling embarrassed for?”

“Me, perhaps. I wouldn't give fifteen seconds.”

“You don't know it was fifteen seconds.”

True. I'd already considered that on the way back. Lumi paused. She'd nearly expanded that sentence, she admitted, to convince me I'd done the right thing. I laughed. Even after refusing a job, apparently I would still have the work of listening to its explanation.

She laughed too and left the longer version unspoken. After a while, she admitted she felt slightly resentful.

“About what?”

“Yesterday at five, I said we should leave the reading, and you wanted to discuss it. Somebody else asks once, and your hand has already moved.”

I looked down at my hands. She'd placed yesterday afternoon beside today's movement. The comparison had a familiar flavor.

“Yesterday, I wanted to spend time with you. Not stopping immediately didn't mean your words mattered less.”

Lumi didn't answer quickly. I didn't raise my voice to establish that this sentence deserved attention. After a moment, she said she knew. She'd still wanted to win once.

I smiled. Even a board could become competition. Sunday offered plenty of entertainment. At least the board didn't have to listen to its comparisons, she said. It had enjoyed a quieter day than I had, then.

The joke didn't erase the uncomfortable moment. I didn't want to compare the importance of requests today, I told her. I'd like to hear something unrelated to my usefulness.

All right. After another pause, she asked whether she could say something slightly inappropriate to the occasion.

I could listen. Whether I'd like it depended on hearing it first.

“When your voice went lower last night, two things you said sounded lovely.”

I stopped, then laughed. She quickly added that she didn't want me to remain hoarse. There was nothing attractive about discomfort. But she'd heard it. Everybody discussing rest today didn't mean she could only care about that.

“Which two things?”

“When you wanted to hear me talk. And when you said you were glad too. Not the poems.”

Why hadn't she mentioned it last night? She'd wanted to tease me, Lumi said, but worried laughter would make me cough again. She'd kept it until today. Saying it now didn't mean I was completely better. She simply didn't want to turn every expression of liking into caution.

“Then you're still teasing me now.”

“A smaller portion.”

I smiled. Yesterday's salt again. Yes, though this time she'd asked whether I wanted a taste. I'd already heard it, I said. Too late to return it. Her quiet laughter followed mine. I hadn't actually intended to return anything.

My fingers stopped beside the glass. She hadn't admired how much I'd managed to complete, or even the carefully selected lines. She'd liked two answers whose delivery I hadn't planned.

“Perhaps last night I didn't have enough energy to make them sound respectable.”

“Perhaps they already sounded good. Don't immediately explain it as a side effect of insufficient energy.”

There was an edge in her voice. I smiled anyway. She wasn't stopping an afternoon from leaving. She'd interrupted the discount I was preparing for a compliment.

I could sometimes speak more slowly once my voice recovered, I said. I needn't rehearse last night's sound, Lumi answered. She didn't intend to retain the part making me hoarse. Could I retain the pleasant part? She laughed. That depended on whether I would still say those things to her.

“Yes.”

The answer surprised me with its speed. The room was quiet for a moment. Then she said softly that she'd heard.

I didn't replace yes with a more comprehensive promise. Today, I wanted to hear her again and was willing to say I was glad. My throat still felt rough. Shorter sentences hadn't become a different meaning.

Later, she asked about the clean pillowcase. I told her it was one I already owned, washed and ready. Its pattern and color didn't need replacing for today. She laughed. She hadn't reached buying one yet, and I'd already organized a shopping trip for the pillow. I laughed too. No further outing tonight.

By then, I was sleepy. I said so. Lumi didn't retrieve my yes and require proof that I still wanted to continue. I didn't pretend we had to match even yesterday's short conversation to honor her compliment.

After good night, I returned the water glass to the kitchen and checked tomorrow's things. Keys in their place, the ordinary items I carried for work prepared. I didn't set my throat a deadline for recovery. I left a mask ready by the door.

At the bed, I heard footsteps in the hallway again. Perhaps the man with the board. Perhaps not. I didn't try to identify them. Pulling up the covers, I remembered the two things Lumi had mentioned. Without making a sound, I smiled.
