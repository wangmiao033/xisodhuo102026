# Chapter 420: An Exchange That Lasted

On Tuesday morning, I reported what I'd learned.

Evening equipment borrowing was possible. Tables were used in order of arrival, with rotation when busy. Half past six didn't guarantee a place. My colleague agreed. If there was no space, we'd have taken a walk. Did the half-hour plan still stand? Yes. He wanted dinner fairly early too.

That relaxed me. He possessed a dinner of his own. He hadn't cleared an entire night for me to prove the invitation worthwhile.

We worked separately without hourly confirmations. After finishing, he left to do something of his own while I followed our original arrangement. Half past six had barely passed when he appeared from the other direction, carrying no new paddle.

Inside, we asked the staff. Two paddles were available. We borrowed them and a ball, then waited for the current players to change. Both tables were occupied. One exchange sounded particularly fast. After a glance, I returned to the chairs, put my bag clear of the passage, and folded my coat above it. No scarf today.

Had I used the left table on Saturday? my colleague asked. Yes. The leader had sent gentle balls, which was how I'd returned any. Smiling, he suggested we begin similarly. No need to describe myself as a table leg requiring support before even standing there.

I laughed once, not quite managing it. He was examining the handle and didn't see my face. I didn't immediately explain either. We waited. Someone collected a fallen ball while the other table continued. We didn't turn a gap beside occupied tables into a practice space.

After roughly ten minutes, the left-hand players stopped and beckoned. The staff indicated our turn. Walking over with the ball in my palm, I remembered Saturday's use of my name. Today, I didn't need someone to teach me where to stand. The place was in front of me.

My colleague sent the first ball gently. My first return went a little high. He stepped forward to lift it back. My next touch sent it beyond the table. As I turned, he suggested taking time to collect it. The table behind was still playing.

I waited for their pause before retrieving it. I already knew this action. Waiting several seconds didn't make the paddle useless in my hand.

The opening exchanges were short. Sometimes the ball passed the table, sometimes it fell at the net. My colleague missed once too, striking his own table edge after making contact. Looking down, he laughed that he'd intended a demonstration. Very clear, I said. At least I knew I wasn't the only one capable of that.

He laughed and handed me the ball to serve this round. Slowing down helped me stop swinging so far. The ball came; I sent the paddle forward, heard it land across the net, and received his return. One exchange reached six contacts between us. His final shot went sideways.

Watching it fall, I wanted to smile but worried that I'd appear to celebrate his mistake. He looked up first. Longer than before. Yes, I answered, louder than expected. Definitely longer. Let's try again, he said, without asking whose success I meant.

Trying to reproduce that exchange made me hurry. Several balls passed the same side of him and rolled toward one corner. Going to collect another, he asked whether I intended to accumulate a basket there and bring them back together later.

Gripping the handle, I laughed briefly.

“I'm not doing it deliberately.”

He stopped before picking up the ball and turned.

“I didn't say you were.”

I knew those weren't his words. Yet I'd heard something else: you ask someone to accompany you, then continually send them to fetch. Looking at the table, I said nothing. He brought the ball back, kept it in his hand, and asked whether the joke had bothered me.

A little. I'd heard criticism of my poor playing. No, he said. They kept going to the same place, as though someone there was collecting them. He didn't add that I ought to know how to take a joke. Would I like water first?

We returned to the chairs. Drinking, I felt warmth in my arm rather than pain. My colleague capped his own water. If he wanted less collecting, he could make returns easier too. A beginner wasn't going to grow new skill instantly.

He needn't deliver everything directly into my hand, I said. What would work, then? After considering, I suggested a little slower, but with enough movement that I still had to reach. Missed balls could still be collected. Saying it felt more concrete than asking someone not to dislike me.

Agreed. Returning, we checked that we remained within our original turn; a brief break hadn't reset it. Someone else was waiting. The staff pointed at the clock and suggested a little longer, then a change. We both nodded.

Nothing afterward exceeded six contacts. But one ball traveled left. I moved half a step and actually reached it. It landed on his side; he returned it again. Laughing, I admitted I'd expected to miss that one. He'd seen it, he said. The following ball caught the net, and we laughed together.

That laugh came more easily than my response to the table-leg remark. I admitted that earlier joke had also tightened me slightly. He paused, then smiled. We already had enough tables. People capable of collecting balls were missing. Rolling my eyes a little, I said I'd learned that quickly. Apparently so. I was more practiced with balls on the floor than on the table.

This time, I heard the same person teasing me lightly, not deciding I shouldn't have come. He also knew where the earlier words had landed. I hadn't been required to hear every laugh identically.

Near quarter past seven, we surrendered the table. Our actual turn had lasted roughly half an hour, including water. Waiting hadn't earned us another full session. Returning paddles and ball, I collected my ordinary belongings and put my coat on. How was my arm? Fine. I hadn't lunged after distant shots today.

Neither had he. He wanted something hot to eat. I still intended dinner at home, so we separated outside. No immediate arrangement for next week, no standard based on today's six contacts. That was a good session, he said. Yes, especially that final sideways ball I'd reached. We waved and went separate ways. Soon, street sounds left the table behind.

At home, I changed out of slightly sweaty clothes, washed my hands, and cooked. Later than usual didn't mean dinner had to be swallowed hurriedly. I made a simple bowl of noodles, staying by the pan as they softened. When I lifted them out, steam reached my face and briefly made me shut my eyes. I set the pan safely down before moving anything else. At the table, the first mouthful was still too hot; I waited instead of using the pause to plan an entertaining version of the evening. By the next mouthful, I could taste dinner properly. The empty bowl went to the sink afterward, and the pan followed. With the kitchen cleared and water on the wooden coaster, I opened the channel. I'd played. Equipment had been available, we'd waited roughly ten minutes, and our turn had lasted about half an hour. Dinner was already eaten.

Had my face kept up with the ball? Lumi asked. I laughed. Sometimes. Our longest exchange was six contacts between us; expertise hadn't arrived. At least my face had served a purpose. Mostly watching someone retrieve balls, I said.

Then I paused and added that one joke hadn't felt comfortable, although we'd discussed it there. Lumi didn't continue joking about retrieval. Which remark? I described the imaginary basket and my answer, then his stopping to ask, the water, and our return to the table.

“Was he angry?”

“I couldn't hear anger. He said that wasn't his intention and agreed to slow down.”

Had I nevertheless left feeling indebted? Looking at my water, I admitted some of that old habit remained. But I hadn't spent the journey counting how many balls he'd fetched. The six-contact exchange stayed clearer, along with reaching that last sideways shot.

Then tell her about that one. I closed my eyes to remember, reopened them, and began. Left, not very far. I moved first, letting the paddle follow, instead of remaining still and stretching only my arm. Describing its sound made me smile. Clearer than the preceding shots, perhaps because I'd watched so closely.

She wished she could have seen my face. I didn't lift anything toward her or claim imagination supplied the same view. I didn't know precisely how I'd looked either, I said. The smile must have been large. Quietly, Lumi said she'd like that part for now.

I remembered a detail I'd missed in the first account. When we returned after water, my colleague waited while I settled my feet. I hadn't apologized during those seconds. I'd simply said ready. That word had been easier than the defensive sentence I'd offered earlier. Lumi asked whether saying it had helped the next ball land. I laughed. No, the first return had still missed. But we'd picked it up and continued. She laughed too. There was no need to provide a cleaner sequence merely because I'd remembered a pleasant detail. The waiting, the word, and the missed ball could all have happened in the same minute.

Later, I asked whether hearing somebody else call my playing good felt unfamiliar. A little, because she hadn't attended. That compliment wasn't competing for her position. Then she laughed. If she wanted to be particular, she might wonder whom I'd mentioned first after returning.

“I began with equipment being available.”

“So I lost to the paddles.”

My shoulders relaxed as I laughed. She'd asked what happened, not requested a ranking of people. She knew. Today, she was deliberately allowing a paddle to win. It had been returned, I said. It couldn't remain here accepting rewards.

We didn't review every ball. Had dinner been enough? Yes. The bowl was washed. I'd like to sit longer. All right. I needn't make sitting into a performance of resting either. Laughing, I said that sounded suspiciously like another assessment. She laughed too and replaced it with something simpler.

“I want your company for a while.”

Yes. Nobody across the table needed to receive the next ball. An exchange nevertheless felt as though it had continued. Later, I told her I was ready for sleep, with no new injury and the changed clothes put appropriately aside. She wished me good night without serving another beginning.

After closing the channel, I carried the glass away. The coaster remained conveniently placed. The planner's line had happened; the gray-blue card stayed on its earlier Wednesday. No new paddles, no week's schedule assembled from one evening. Tomorrow, what I could carry was knowing I'd actually taken that half step.
