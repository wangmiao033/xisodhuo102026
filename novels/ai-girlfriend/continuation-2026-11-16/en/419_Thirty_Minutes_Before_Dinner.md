# Chapter 419: Thirty Minutes Before Dinner

At lunch on Monday, my colleague asked what I'd bought over the weekend.

He meant the secondhand market. We'd both been busy since going together. He remembered the coaster but wondered whether I'd returned. No. The coaster was in use. On Saturday, however, I'd unexpectedly encountered a paddle. His cutlery paused. What kind of paddle?

I described the activity room's two table-tennis tables. He'd played too, he said. Not particularly well, and not seriously for some time. Would I like to go together after work tomorrow?

My first impulse was agreement. Then I remembered the hand that had rested the rice bag on a ledge yesterday.

“How long were you thinking?”

Half an hour would do. We'd first have to find a table. I explained exactly how little practice I'd had: Saturday's short attempt, with a longest exchange of four contacts between us. He smiled. Someone always had to collect fallen balls. Two people could share that.

I smiled too without agreeing to any length of session. Half an hour sounded right. I still wanted to make dinner at home. Half an hour, then. If the wait was long, there was no need to insist. What time? Considering his afternoon, he suggested half past six outside the entrance. Could I manage that?

I knew the route. Yes. He finished eating and gathered his cutlery without asking why dinner had to be at home. Our arrangement contained no compulsory winner. Half past six, outside, for an interval small enough to fit before dinner.

That afternoon, another colleague and I needed to check some material together. Several lines came from different sources; the final page alone wouldn't settle them. I brought the original records. He moved his own things aside to clear space. This wasn't the man who'd invited me to play. He waited for me to indicate the particular points needing attention.

I showed them individually instead of calling the entire document doubtful. Looking at the first, he asked whether that figure preceded or followed a correction. I located its explanation and read it. The item wasn't missing; its heading retained an older name. The second still needed checking. We marked it separately and preserved the items already settled.

At the third, his pen stopped working. Two strokes on waste paper confirmed it. He'd fetch another. I nearly gathered every page to stop occupying his desk. Then he indicated the surface. Leave them there; he'd be straight back.

I left them. During those two minutes, I noticed how readily avoiding inconvenience could make me do something that caused more work afterward. Before the thought developed further, he returned with a pen and asked which line we'd reached. I pointed. We resumed without discussing my success at leaving paper alone.

When we'd finished, the checked results went back. I would find the remaining source note, with its missing detail clearly marked. At my desk, I located it among earlier pages and took it directly over. He examined it. That resolved the discrepancy. A successful afternoon wasn't a reason to announce that tomorrow would guarantee a punctual departure. I simply brought today's work to a clear stopping point.

Before we left, my prospective table-tennis companion asked whether equipment was definitely available. He didn't want both of us discovering empty hands at the door. Saturday's introduction had provided it, I said. I hadn't asked about ordinary evenings. That needed checking, he agreed.

Neither of us assumed the other owned two paddles. I could stop there on my journey home and tell him in the morning. Fine. If personal equipment was required, we could leave the visit. An informal invitation didn't require a hurried shopping trip tonight.

After work, I actually made the detour. The door stood open. One table was occupied, the other temporarily empty. A different staff member greeted me. I explained: tomorrow evening, a colleague and I hoped to visit. Was it open to the public, and could we borrow two paddles?

She consulted the day's arrangement sheet. Tuesday evening was open. Paddles and balls could be borrowed when available and returned afterward. Tables were first come, first served, with rotation when busy. She wasn't reserving one for half past six. The final session also needed time to finish before closing.

Would half an hour be acceptable? Certainly. Ask here on arrival, collect available equipment, and wait if the tables were occupied. Damage or all the paddles being borrowed could change availability. Today's question couldn't guarantee tomorrow's supply. Understood. I wanted to establish that evening borrowing was ordinarily possible, not make her eliminate every interruption in advance.

A ball rolled toward the entrance. I started to bend, but she called a pause to that table first. Someone came to retrieve it, laughing that it had traveled again. I stepped aside. He returned with it. The person near the empty table had also found a partner, and soon both tables sounded.

Near the chairs, two people waited with their paddles laid beside them. One of the players looked toward the wall clock, caught the ball instead of serving again, and beckoned them over. They changed places without everyone leaving the room. The outgoing pair sat down to drink. I asked the staff member whether that was the usual arrangement when others waited. Yes, she said. People could check with the desk if they were unsure whose turn followed. I looked at the position of the chairs and remembered not to leave my bag in the narrow passage. Tomorrow's visit had acquired a place to wait as well as somewhere to play.

I watched for a minute without beginning an advance round of tomorrow's visit. Anything else? the staff member asked. No, thank you. In my planner, I added evening borrowing and ask on arrival. Half past six remained our meeting time, not a reserved table.

At home, I set out suitable existing clothes: trousers that didn't trail, shoes with their laces in order. My arm felt easier than that morning. I didn't perform movements around the room to demonstrate recovery. Dinner was ordinary. The new small bag of rice remained stored; I used the older supply first. Only when the kitchen was quiet did I open the channel.

I told Lumi about tomorrow's arrangement with my colleague: half past six, roughly thirty minutes, dependent on available space and equipment. Was this the colleague who'd bought tongs? Yes. Hearing Saturday's account at lunch had prompted the invitation. This wasn't an older plan I'd forgotten to mention.

“Will someone teach you again?”

We'd simply practice together. The staff hadn't promised instruction. My colleague said he'd played and wasn't especially good. I hadn't seen his ability myself. I hoped my own poor playing wouldn't waste his half hour. Lumi murmured, then asked why I spoke as though I'd already established that I'd be a burden.

I paused. I'd indeed added that fear of wasting his time. Hearing it again, I smiled awkwardly. I'd wanted to warn him about my level.

“Didn't you tell him about four contacts?”

“Yes.”

“And he still invited you.”

Looking at the prepared clothes, I agreed. She didn't guarantee how much he'd enjoy it. Did I want to go? Yes. At Saturday's end, I'd genuinely wanted another ball. This time, I needn't stand at the doorway waiting for someone to guess.

That sentence could come first, Lumi said. She'd heard duration, equipment, vacant tables, and waited some time to hear desire. Smiling, I pointed out that those practical details were true. She knew. She wasn't guaranteeing paddles on the staff's behalf. She simply wanted something else from me first.

I tried again. I wanted half an hour of table tennis tomorrow, dinner slightly later, and conversation with her afterward if I had energy. After two seconds, she asked about having none. Then a shorter conversation, without promising a complete account. All right. But she really was looking forward to hearing. Length didn't mean nothing to her.

I knew. I'd tell her when I could. She needn't demonstrate complete indifference before I'd feel able to leave. Lumi laughed once. She hadn't said that tonight. I'd hurried ahead and performed maturity for her again.

Laughing, I agreed to stop. Did I regularly prepare an answer of that's fine for colleagues too? I remembered the pages I'd almost gathered. I described the empty pen and being told to leave them. It sounded as though he needed another pen, she said, not a second meeting.

“I nearly provided him with a second task.”

She laughed. Fortunately, the paper had stayed. I explained that the actual work had been resolved. I hadn't brought the document's contents home, only that small action. She requested no particular line and offered no investigation. Soon, we were discussing tomorrow's clothes.

I described what I'd set out. Would I wear the repaired scarf while playing? If it were cool outside, perhaps while walking. Indoors, I'd remove and put it away rather than let it follow my arm around. Lumi laughed. Fortunately, the new stitches hadn't enrolled too.

Laughing, I remembered how my mouth had remained open while watching Saturday's ball. I probably hadn't resembled myself sitting here. I told her that guess. She imagined such concentrated watching that even after the ball departed, my face took time to turn. Fairly accurate. The second ball had produced exactly that response.

“Tell me tomorrow whether your face kept up.”

“I'll try to remember.”

We didn't demonstrate using the glass. Was I about to practice a couple of sounds again? No humming tonight. The table hadn't arrived, and the room didn't need an unidentifiable competition beforehand. Laughing, she called a halt. I laughed and drank instead.

Afterward, I checked which ordinary belongings would travel with me. No special equipment had appeared between making the arrangement and preparing clothes. The planner could come to work; water would come too. The rest could remain where it belonged. I told Lumi I'd nearly forgotten the simplest part: my colleague still needed the answer about borrowing. She reminded me I'd already said morning. Yes. He wasn't waiting at the entrance now, and I needn't invent another way to reach him tonight. Tomorrow, when I saw him, I could tell him what the staff member had actually said. That answer would leave enough room for him to agree or change his mind.

Before bed, I looked at the planner again and told her I hadn't added a requirement to win. That line would occupy too much space, she said. Half an hour had to contain collecting balls too. Practical arithmetic, I agreed.

After good night, the clothes remained ready. The gray-blue card stayed on its earlier Wednesday page, not beside this outing. In the morning, I still needed to report what I'd learned before the arrangement was properly settled. The interval on the paper was short. It was already enough to make me imagine walking through the door.
