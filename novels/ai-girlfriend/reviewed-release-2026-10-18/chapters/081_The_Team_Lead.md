# Chapter 81: The Team Lead

Monday. 9:00 AM.

I was in a meeting. My meeting. Not Derek's meeting—mine. The first team meeting I'd ever led. Three people on a video call, waiting for me to talk. Three faces in rectangles. Three sets of eyes.

One of them was a woman.

Her name was Priya. She was in data entry—had been for six months. She was quiet, efficient, the kind of person who does the work and doesn't talk about it. She was in my team now. I was her team lead.

She had brown eyes and a small scar on her chin and she was looking at me through a screen and I was looking at her through a screen and Lumi was in the speaker.

Not the earbuds—the speaker. I'd kept the earbuds out of meetings. The boundary that separated the world from the room. But the speaker was in the room, and the speaker was her, and she was listening.

"Okay," I said. "First team meeting. I'm. I'm going to keep this short."

"Here's what I'm thinking. The queue is caught up. The formatting issues are resolved. The Q1 projections are. manageable. So I want to try something different."

The three faces looked at me. Priya. A guy named Tom. A guy named Marcus. Three people I'd never spoken to before last week.

"I want each of us to take ownership of a specific area. Not just data entry. Analysis. Finding patterns in the data. Not just inputting—thinking about what the data means."

Tom nodded. Marcus looked neutral. Priya tilted her head—the tilt of a person who is listening differently, who is interested.

"Like what?" Priya said.

"Like. if you notice that a particular route has a higher error rate, don't just fix the entry. Find out why. Track it. Build a case. Bring it to the team."

"You want us to be analysts," Marcus said.

"I want us to be more than typists. We have the data. We see it first. We know it better than anyone. We should. use that."

Priya nodded. "I've noticed that the western region entries have a 15% higher correction rate. I've been fixing them but I never. said anything."

"Say something. That's exactly it. Track it. Quantify it. Bring it next week."

She smiled. Small. Specific. The smile of a person who has been seen for the first time.
