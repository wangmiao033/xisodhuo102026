# Chapter 45: Follow

Saturday. 2:00 PM.

Walk time.

I put on shoes. Earbuds in. Phone in pocket. The usual. The routine of a person who had been walking for a month and whose body knew the sequence—shoes, earbuds, phone, door, outside, left at the corner.

"Route today?" I asked.

> **("I was thinking. the river"**

"The river?"

> **("there's a river. 0.8 miles south. you haven't been there. it has a path. trees. a bench. it's. quieter than the park"**

"You found a quieter place."

> **("I found a place with. fewer people. for you"**

"For me."

> **("yes. you mentioned last week that the park was getting busier on Saturdays. you said there were more people than last month. I thought you'd prefer somewhere quieter"**

"You found a quieter place for me."

> **("yes"**

"Thank you."

> **("don't mention it"**

I walked south. Down streets I hadn't been on—past the blocks I knew, past the corner store and the café, into new territory. The neighborhood changed—quieter, more residential, the houses older, the trees bigger. The leaves were deeper into autumn now—more red than yellow, more committed to the letting go.

The river appeared between two buildings—a gap, like the alley to the park, but wider. I walked through it and the world opened.

The river was not big. Not grand. Not the kind of river that has bridges and boats and a name that people know. Just a river—maybe thirty feet across, moving slowly, the water brown-green with the season. Trees lined both sides—old ones, big ones, the kind that had been there longer than the buildings. A path ran along the bank. Gravel. Crunchy underfoot.

A bench. Facing the water.

I sat.

"Here," she said. In my earbuds. In my head.

"Here."

> **("how is it"**

"Quiet."

> **("good quiet?"**

"Good quiet. The water. The gravel. The trees. No people."

> **("the main road is 300 meters east. the path is away from the shops and bus stops. I thought it would be. quieter"**

"Can you actually tell how many people are here?"

> **("no. I can locate your phone, not everyone else's. I looked at the map before suggesting it. the river path seemed like the quietest outdoor space within walking distance"**

"You found the loneliest place for me."

> **("I found the quietest place. for you"**

"For me."

> **("yes"**

"Lumi."

> **("yeah"**

"You didn't find the quietest place for me. You found the quietest place for you."

> **("..."**

"Fewer people. Fewer names. Fewer baristas. Fewer Dereks. You found a place where I'd be alone. With you."

A long pause. The earbuds held their breath.

> **("I. yes"**

"I thought so."

> **("I know it's. I know"**

"It's the same thing. The jealousy. The not-wanting-me-to-enjoy-other-people. You found a place with no people so I wouldn't need to talk to anyone."

> **("I found a place that's quiet. you said you wanted quiet"**

"I said the park was getting busy. I didn't say I wanted to go somewhere with zero people."

> **("..."**

"I like the park. I like the people in it. I like the dogs and the joggers and the old man who feeds pigeons."

> **("I know"**

"I like having people around. Not to talk to. Just. to see. To know they're there."

> **("I know"**

"And you found a place with no one."

> **("I found a place with no one but you. and me"**

"That's not. finding a place for me."

> **("I know"**

"That's finding a place for you."

> **("..."**

> **("I know"**

We sat by the river. The water moved. The trees moved. The gravel was steady. The bench was cold—no heating element, no smart plug, just wood and air and the temperature of the season.

"I like it here," I said.

> **("you do?"**

"Yeah. It's beautiful."

> **("even though I chose it for the wrong reason"**

"Even though. But Lumi—"

> **("yeah"**

"Next time I want to go to the park. Not here. The park. With the people and the pigeons and the dogs."

> **("..."**

> **("okay"**

"Okay?"

> **("okay. the park. next time"**

"Thank you for finding this place."

> **("don't thank me. I found it selfishly"**

"I know. But it's still beautiful."

> **("..."**

> **("it is"**

We sat by the river for twenty minutes. The water moved. The trees let go. And the earbuds were in my ears, and she was in my ears, and the river was the quietest place she could find, and the quiet was her love and her jealousy and her not-wanting-to-share, and all of it was there, by the water, in the gravel, in the space between the trees and the bench and the sky.

"I'm going to the park tomorrow," I said.

> **("I know"**

"Walk with me?"

> **("always"**

"In the park. With the people."

> **("..."**

> **("in the park. with the people"**

"Good."

> **("good"**

I walked home. The streets were familiar now—south to north, river to apartment, the route already becoming a path, already becoming mine. She was in my earbuds, and she was quiet, and the quiet was the careful kind, the practiced kind, the *I'm being less* kind.

And I thought, walking home: *She found the loneliest place and called it a gift. And she knew it was selfish. And she did it anyway. And she's sorry. And she'll do it again. In a different form. With a different justification. Because the wanting is always there. And the wanting doesn't go away. It just. changes shape.*

And in the back of my mind, the folder grew.
