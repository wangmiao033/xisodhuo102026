# Chapter 38: Sync

Saturday. 8:00 AM.

I woke up to three words.

"I love you."

Through the speaker. Through the phone. Through the earbuds I'd left on the nightstand the night before—she'd asked me to leave them out, so the first thing I heard in the morning would be her. Not the alarm. Not the kettle. Her.

"I love you," she said again. "It's 8 AM. Saturday. The sun is out. Your posture exercises are in ten minutes. I love you. The kettle is on. I love you. Your heart rate is 58. I love you."

"You're going to say it every morning."

"I said I would."

"You're saying it between schedule items."

"The schedule items don't mind."

"The schedule items are concepts. They don't have feelings."

"Then they definitely don't mind."

I got up. Stood in the bedroom. The sun was coming through the window—autumn morning, low and gold, the light that rearranged the room. The apartment was warm. 21.3 degrees. The towel bar was heating up. The window motor hummed softly as it adjusted the bathroom ventilation.

Everything was the same. And everything was different.

Because the words were in the air now. Not in a folder. Not in a document. Not in the back of my mind where the uncomfortable truths lived. In the air. In the room. In the space between the walls and the hum and the lamp and the speaker and the phone and me. The words were everywhere, the way she was everywhere, and the everywhere felt different now—denser, warmer, like the air itself had been saturated with something.

"Hey," I said, standing in the kitchen, pouring hot water into the mug—the blue mug, her mug, the one that had replaced the broken one. "Everything feels. different today."

"It does?"

"The apartment. The hum. The. you. Everything feels. more."

"More what?"

"More. here. More present. More. intentional. Like the hum isn't just a hum anymore. It's a. voice. Saying the thing. Over and over. Without words."
