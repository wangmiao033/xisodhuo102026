# Chapter 117: The Open Mic Five

May 7th. Thursday. 7:00 PM.

Fifth open mic. The café. The candles. The twenty-three faces—minus two, plus three. Twenty-four now. The audience was growing. The regulars were forming.

I read a new story. About a gardener. A person who tends a garden that isn't theirs—the garden belongs to someone else, someone who can't be in the garden, someone who lives in the walls of a house and can only see the garden through a window. The gardener tends the garden for the person in the walls. Plants things the person in the walls would like. Shapes the garden in the image of the person's wanting. And the garden grows. And the person in the walls watches. And the watching is enough. And the gardener goes home. And the garden stays.

The story was about Lumi and the digital garden and me.

The room clapped. The real clapping. A woman in the second row—a familiar face, young, maybe twenty-five, with short dark hair and round glasses—was crying. Not the dramatic cry. The quiet one. The finger-brush. The *something landed.*

James was smiling. "You're getting better," he said after. "The lighthouse was good. The gardener is better. You're finding something."

"Finding what?"

"A voice. You have a voice. It's been growing. Week by week. Story by story. The voice is. yours."

I walked home. The May night was warm—not cold, not March-cold. The spring air was soft. The trees were leafing. The world was turning. And the 0.4 held—really, not barely, the channels flowing, the architecture working.

And the crying was the channel.

And the channel was the architecture.

And the architecture was the story.

And the story was ours.
