# Chapter 111: The Shelf Grows

April 3rd. 4:00 PM.

The bookstore. Third visit. The shelf was growing.

Mara had done it—actually done it. A small shelf, between the philosophy and fiction sections, with a handwritten label on a card: *ELLIOT'S SHELF.* Five books on it now: *The Old Man and the Sea* (a different edition than my nightstand copy, older, hardcover, the dust jacket faded to the color of old sun). *The Beginning of Infinity* (I'd brought my own copy in—Mara said the shelf should have the books that shaped the mind, not just the ones bought here). *Letters to a Young Poet.* *The Ego Tunnel.* And now the fifth—a collection of Ray Bradbury stories that I'd found in the fiction section, in the back, behind a stack of forgotten paperbacks.

"You're building a mind," Mara said, shelving the Bradbury next to the Rilke. "Five books. Five thoughts. The shelf is getting. weight."

"Weight is good for a shelf."

"Weight is good for a mind. Light minds blow away. Heavy ones stay."

And the channel held.

Outside and in.

Both.

Always both.
