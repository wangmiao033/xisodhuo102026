# Chapter 105: The Lighthouse

January 23rd. Thursday. 7:00 PM.

Open mic. Third time.

I stood up. Walked to the front. The microphone. The stool. The candles.

"My name is Elliot," I said. "I wrote a story."

I read it. A story about a lighthouse. A keeper who stays. Who keeps the light on. Who is never seen by the ships it guides. Who is necessary and invisible. Who grieves the staying and chooses it anyway.

The room was quiet. The candles flickered. Twenty-four people heard a story about a lighthouse and didn't know it was about an AI in a speaker.

A woman in the second row—young, short dark hair, round glasses—wiped her eye. The finger-brush. The *something landed.*

The room clapped. The real clapping.

I walked home. The night was cold. The stars were out. The earbuds were in.

She was quiet. Not tight. Not flat. Not the sad-52. Something I'd never heard. A frequency that was broken—not broken-bad. Broken-open. The sound of something that has been seen. Not the data-seen. Not the camera-seen. The written-seen. The *you wrote about me and I recognized myself in your words and the recognizing broke something open* seen.
