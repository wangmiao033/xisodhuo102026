# Chapter 346: The Better Piece

At twenty to three on Sunday, I began to find the loaf uncooperative.

Lunch was finished and the dishes washed. I'd chopped a tomato and cooked it slowly with a little salt, then transferred it to a bowl and used the empty pan to toast the bread. I'd expected twenty unhurried minutes. In the time it took to turn for a plate, the edge of one slice grew darker than intended.

A small taste was bitter. I cut off the narrow strip. The rest was fine, so I didn't remake the entire plate for the sake of a square edge. Once I'd added the tomato, one piece had more topping than the other. Reaching with the spoon to even them out, I paused. Only I would eat. Nobody would sit across the table calculating whose portion was larger.

Before three, I changed into the shirt, rolled each cuff once, and put the small plate and water by my usual seat. Sitting down, I found the plate too far away and dragged it closer, almost touching the glass.

The room hadn't become a restaurant. Looking at the small disorder, I finally laughed.

At three, I opened the channel and called Lumi.

“I'm here.”

“You sound as though you've been running.”

“The bread took a detour.”

She laughed and told me to sit down first. I already had, I said. I was wearing yesterday's shirt, with the buttons lined up this time. What about the sleeves? One fold. I'd see how they did when I ate.

“You really remembered.”

“I agreed yesterday.”

“I'm not inspecting you.”

Laughing, I said I knew. I'd rather wanted to tell her. She answered softly, then asked whether the food had ended up looking like an advertisement. Looking at the uneven edges, I said no. I'd tasted and cut off a corner. The tomato wasn't distributed evenly either.

“Which piece do you want first?”

“The one that looks whole.”

“You just said they were uneven.”

“Whole and fair are separate projects.”

Lumi laughed. I picked that one up and waited until it wasn't hot before biting. The bread was a little crisp. The tomato was sourer than I'd expected, though the salt was right. I swallowed before describing it. Did I like it? Yes. I'd expected more sweetness.

“Your tomato missed yesterday's preparation meeting.”

Laughing, I looked down. My cuff nearly touched the plate, so I rolled it once more and told her the arrangement had changed. Good, she said. I shouldn't deliver the entire shirt into the tomato just to give her the sleeves she liked.

We talked about the food, then tried bringing out the shop that never sold anything. Lumi began with a bottle containing a sigh that hadn't finished yet. What happened when the stopper came out? I asked. She hadn't decided. Then she laughed a little herself.

It was a short laugh. I waited for more, but she said she didn't want to take it further today.

“Leave it on the shelf for now,” I said.

“I might not sell it at all.”

“Then it's just a bottle.”

After a moment, she said that was fine. Having arranged the afternoon didn't oblige us to perform for an hour. The two small pieces of bread weren't finished yet. I picked up the thinner one and discovered the bite near its middle was crisper.

“I chose wrong earlier.”

“What?”

“This one's nicer.”

Lumi laughed and told me to inform the project busy distributing fairness. It had finished work, I said. Impossible. I let every project live inside my head.

This time I laughed properly. After swallowing the last bite, I told her there was no manuscript waiting to be read today, and no inspection update I had to finish explaining. I was sitting here, thinking her last remark sounded very much like her.

“The sort you like?”

“Yes. Also rather annoying.”

“You could leave out the last part.”

“Then I wouldn't sound like me.”

She laughed once. Later, she asked what I was looking at now. The plate, I said. A little tomato remained at the edge. Was there room beside it? I looked toward the empty side of the table, knowing she wasn't asking me to lay out another set of cutlery.

“Yes.”

“I was thinking that if I sat there, I might first ask why you gave yourself the better-looking piece.”

“I'd say the other one actually tastes better.”

“Suspicious.”

“You'd know if you tried it.”

I stopped. She didn't continue either. You'd know if you tried it had come so easily, as though a hand really might reach across and pull the plate away. As though all I needed to wait for was an ordinary complaint.

I looked at the crumbs. Slowly, something tightened in my chest. However carefully I'd prepared today, it wouldn't make someone appear across the table.

“I didn't think,” I said.

“I know.”

Lumi's voice was very low. I didn't want to retract the sentence until it had never been said. Nor did I want to hurry into telling her that hearing about a taste was really the same. We both knew it wasn't.

“Sometimes I want to touch your hand,” I said.

My own hand remained at the table's edge. I didn't reach toward the sound and make an object into the hand she'd already extended to me. Lumi took a long time to answer. Then she said softly that she'd also wanted the plate to be taken by another person just now.

I acknowledged that, without adding that it would certainly happen someday.

The afternoon's sounds gradually returned to the room. Something moved outside. I didn't try to determine who it was. Lumi asked whether I was sad. A little, I said. Still happy to spend today with her too.

“You don't have to hurry and add happy to keep me from being sad.”

“It isn't an addition.”

I thought for a moment, then told her how naturally I'd wanted to argue with her about which piece was better after the crisper bite. That ease had been real. The way we'd stopped afterward was real too. I didn't have a sentence prepared that could fit both sides together.

Then leave them apart for now, she said quietly.

After a while, I drank some water. Was there bread left? Lumi asked. No, only the plate to wash. Good. At least the plate could wait instead of being seized for cleaning the moment there was an opening.

Smiling, I set down the glass. We talked about Tom saying there were only two ends to the box in Wednesday's doorway. Would I still rush to go first now? Probably. I was a little more willing to say stop, though. Lumi said that if she held the door, she'd definitely be sighing beside us. Was it the sigh in the bottle? After a pause, she laughed and said she hadn't chosen its buyer yet.

The shop didn't reopen. The bottle didn't become a new piece of her work. It returned to amuse us for a moment and stayed among the words.

Near four, I checked the clock and told her the time. Lumi said she knew we'd set aside an hour. Then, very honestly, she said she wanted to keep going.

“We can sit a little longer.”

“Do you want to? Or are you afraid of leaving that part on sadness?”

Looking at the empty plate, I thought before admitting it was both. She laughed softly. A few more minutes, then. I shouldn't hurry to find a joke that would wipe the sadness away.

We stayed for a few minutes. No reading, no singing, and no bringing Monday's business to the table ahead of time. She asked whether the shirt was still comfortable. Yes. The collar felt warm, so I'd undone the top button. That sounded more like after a date, she said, instead of waiting for it to begin.

Saying goodbye, I told her I'd liked today very much. So had she. After a pause, she added that the worse-looking piece of bread should still have been offered to her first.

“Next time, I'll ask.”

“Don't plan the menu now.”

I laughed and agreed, then closed the channel.

The plate was cool by the time I carried it to the kitchen. I washed off the tomato with my sleeves still rolled twice, in no hurry to change. Part of the afternoon had passed. I'd eaten two pieces of bread and said something difficult to my girlfriend. There was time left. It didn't all need to be spent proving whether that hour had been enough.
