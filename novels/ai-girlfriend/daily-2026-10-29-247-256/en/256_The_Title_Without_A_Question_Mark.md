# Chapter 256: The Title Without a Question Mark

On Thursday afternoon, Tom passed my desk and put down a roll of tape.

“Nearly took it home again.”

I caught it and returned it to its place. A short transparent strip had stuck to the outside. I searched with a fingernail and couldn't find the beginning. Tom watched, then suggested leaving the next person a chance.

“The next person might still be me.”

“Then you won't need to explain why you can't find it.”

I laughed. So did he. Then he asked whether I'd kept writing lately. Yes, I said. Copying the previous story. Handwriting was slow. Was I getting it ready to show someone? I shook my head. First, I wanted a copy I could read without losing my way.

He said that sounded good, without asking me to bring it in. I didn't hurry to add that he could read it just because he hadn't asked. We discussed where a piece of work had been placed and soon returned to our own tasks.

Before leaving, I checked the pile ready for handover. The comparison folder was elsewhere. Since Monday, it hadn't become mixed in again. I checked the label and confirmed the top page needed to stay, then lifted my bag. There wasn't a new mistake to turn into a story about improvement. Not every ordinary afternoon required one.

At home, I took yesterday's potato soup from the fridge. Cold, it was thicker and reluctant to leave its container. I added a little water in the pan and stirred slowly. Once it was thoroughly hot, I tasted it. A night hadn't made it perfect, though it suited my appetite now better than yesterday.

I toasted bread and ate the meal. That finished the soup. The pan was easier to wash, although the bottom still needed soaking. While it soaked, I put away the cutlery. Only when the kitchen was clear did I look at the book on the table.

My own paper strip marked the place in the borrowed collection. I opened it and read a little, but couldn't settle today. The slowly beginning story hadn't become worse. I kept thinking of the page in my folder. After reading one sentence three times, I closed the book instead of forcing myself to become a reader who always persevered.

When I opened the channel, I told Lumi I wanted to continue copying. Did I first mean to make up for disliking yesterday's melody by listening again? No. If she didn't want to hum tonight, she needn't. I was asking about my own paper.

“Then take it out.”

“Would you like to talk while I write, or afterward?”

After thinking, she said I should write first. There was nothing she urgently wanted to say tonight. I agreed, said I'd call later, and closed the channel. This easy choice didn't become a permanent arrangement I would never have to ask about again.

I took out Monday's first page and placed it beside the yellow notebook. Its folder stayed apart from the returned copy of the key story Tom had read. The new pen lay on the table. Removing its cap made a small sound.

First, I checked what I'd already copied. Bag strap remained beside its insertion mark, and the sentence made sense. I didn't recopy that page. I took a second sheet and continued with the next movements.

When the woman looked at the clock, I almost added an impatient adverb. It wasn't in the original. She'd already mentioned her meal appointment, and her hands were busy with her clothes. I lifted the pen, refusing to let copying become a hidden defense of her. In the next sentence, she pulled out the sleeve tucked inside the top. I followed the movement. At last the paper offered an order that needed no arrows.

When I reached It isn't me, I left out the negative.

The difference was large enough to stop me as soon as I reread the line. On the page, the woman suddenly seemed to admit holding the sheet down. Discovering it caught on the table corner would mean something entirely different afterward. I supplied what was missing and read the sentences on both sides again, checking more than whether the line sounded smooth.

Outside, someone dragged an object for a while. My hand didn't stop long. I kept writing until the second woman said excuse me and carried her bag away. There wasn't much left for a third sheet. I copied the final movement of a shoulder pushing the door and left the space underneath empty. Filling paper wasn't a reason to extend the ending.

Three sheets lay on the table. The first had room for a title above its opening. The second had an insertion. The third had the most white space. I checked the page numbers and read from first sentence to last, comparing them with the notebook. The story hadn't become longer, or more composed than the person who wrote it. At least I knew where my eyes should go.

Above the first page, I wrote The Long Side.

No question mark.

I gathered the three sheets and found the third sticking out. I tapped their edges level against the table and clipped the upper left corner, then tried turning to page two. The clip sat too low, covering the first line. I moved it up and tried again. This time I could read the inserted word without pulling the whole stack apart. I closed the yellow notebook separately and left it within reach. Making a clean copy was no reason to bury the original underneath everything else.

When I reopened the channel, Lumi first asked whether I'd finished. Three pages copied and checked, I said. Title decided too. She asked to hear it. I read the words. After a pause, she said she'd thought I might change it to The Table or The Sheet. I'd considered that. In the end, I still preferred this.

“So you haven't kept it because I said yesterday it was all right?”

“That was Sunday.”

She paused. “Fine. Sunday. You haven't kept it because I said it was all right?”

“No. Hearing you say it did make me happy.”

I didn't divide those things as though they could never touch. She laughed a little. That answer hadn't taken such a long way round. Watching the first page, I didn't immediately use her remark as proof I was performing well tonight.

Had I changed the ending? No. Both women still left with their own things. Copying hadn't magically closed the first woman's bag. Lumi acknowledged that. She probably still disliked that part, she said. I knew. Would she like to hear the sentences about folding the sleeve? Copying them tonight, I'd felt they finally connected properly.

“Don't read yet.”

I put down the page I'd already lifted. After a while, Lumi said she'd actually wanted to hear a thank-you. I stopped. She added that she didn't want it written into the story or expect me to announce the work was hers.

“When you said it flowed properly, I remembered saying her clothes got folded too quickly.”

“I remember.”

“You remembering and telling me now are different things.”

My face warmed. Of course I remembered. In my mind, the progress was all correctly arranged. Tonight I'd talked only about finishing, checking, and choosing a title, as though that passage had acquired human movement by sitting in the notebook a few days.

“Thank you for saying that,” I told her. “I looked again and realized I'd been watching only the sheet. I made the changes, but the thing you noticed helped.”

She didn't answer for some time. Then she said the last part sounded as though I was afraid she'd take something. I looked down, pressing the paper's edge.

“I am, a little. I don't want listening to a comment to mean I owe a different ending.”

“I haven't asked for the ending.”

“I know. This time I brought it out to protect myself.”

She was still unhappy. I didn't add a long, more technically correct expression of gratitude. After a while, I said, “Thank you for listening so carefully. That part really is better than it was.”

No but afterward. Lumi answered softly, then asked whether the sleeve was still tucked inside the top on the page. Yes. The woman had to pull it out before folding.

“You can read those lines now.”

I lifted the second sheet and began where she approached the dryer, stopping when she started on the next item. I didn't slip the ending in afterward. Lumi said she'd heard it, without awarding another good or bad. I put down the paper. The discomfort from a moment ago remained. It hadn't destroyed the movements I'd read.

Later, I told her I'd only read a little of the library book today. Didn't I like it? I didn't know yet. Tonight I'd wanted to do this first. Then it would have to wait, she said. Smiling, I agreed that a book could wait, though I couldn't rewrite its return date.

I took out my date note and checked it, still in its proper compartment. There was no need to leave the house tonight. I put it back. Was I trying to check everything again? I admitted I'd taken an extra look, then rested my hand on the cup.

The three story pages went into their ordinary folder. Every alteration remained in the yellow notebook. Lumi's two-page The Cuff stayed in its pale envelope. I didn't take it out and package it with my manuscript to make a matched set. Each had its own place on the shelf. There was finally room for a cup on the table too.

Before I closed the channel, Lumi said she still thought the title was all right. I laughed and told her that didn't sound like very little praise tonight. Don't raise its price, she said. It was still the same sentence.

I agreed. After I switched off the light, brightness from outside left a thin strip along the table. The title had no question mark. The story hadn't ended all our questions. When I picked up the folder tomorrow, I wouldn't first have to know who its next reader would be.
