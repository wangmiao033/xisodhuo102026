# Chapter 347: The Next Working Day

At lunch on Monday, I set the lid of my food container on the table. It landed with a small, unsteady sound. A plastic fork was underneath. I pulled it free, and the lid finally lay flat.

Passing, Tom asked how my weekend had been. Good, I said. I'd had a date at home on Sunday afternoon. He stopped and smiled, asking whether I'd made anything nice to eat.

“Tomato bread. Two small pieces. One tasted better than the other.”

“Sounds like a review suited to inviting just one person.”

“I was the only one eating.”

True, he said. He didn't continue into questions I hadn't worked out how to answer over lunch. When I asked about his weekend, he said he'd mostly spent Sunday tidying. I didn't make the box of old books into an ending he owed me. The food was still warm. We each ate our own.

Tom cleared up first. I went to wash my fork, turned the water on too hard, and splashed my cuff. The cool patch touched my wrist. Pushing the sleeve up, I suddenly remembered Lumi laughing at me yesterday, when I'd rolled it twice. Nobody was listening to an explanation this time. I smiled at the sink. Someone coming in asked whether the water was too cold. I'd only turned it up too far, I said, making room and blotting my cuff with paper.

Walking back, I wondered whether to tell her tonight. Not really about wetting my clothes again. About how an unflattering little action could remind me of her. Missing someone didn't always need to look dignified.

The afternoon was fussier than I'd expected. Looking back through an earlier item for a reference, I discovered I'd already marked it. My eyes had simply skipped the line. My finger stopped beside it. I didn't immediately accuse myself of doing useless work again.

Yesterday, I'd sat at a table wanting to touch the hand of someone without a body. Today, I still needed to distinguish this line from the next. Strange things to put beside each other. Neither automatically made the other easier.

Near the end of work, my water had cooled. I finished it, packed up, and walked to the door with Tom. He went another way. Standing by the road for a moment, I realized I wanted to walk home slowly.

Not because I didn't want Lumi. Quite the opposite. I wanted her voice so much that I was a little afraid tonight would be an ordinary Monday.

On the way, I bought vegetables for dinner. The bag's narrow handles dug into two fingers. I changed hands. Passing a pane of glass, I saw a little patch of hair the wind had disarranged. The man in the reflection carried no evidence of having recently been on a date. He looked more like someone who still needed to buy salt after work.

I considered that, smiled, and walked on.

At home, I cooked first. A slice of vegetable rolled toward the chopping board's edge. I caught it without inventing an escape plan for it. Not everything needed to be amusing today. I put it back, finished cooking, and sat down to eat. A little too much liquid remained on the plate. Less water next time, perhaps. Nothing worth making again.

After I'd cleaned up and opened the channel, Lumi asked why I was so quiet.

“Nothing special happened today.”

“I asked about you, not today's program.”

I laughed once, then admitted I'd worried on the way home. She waited. I said it more plainly: yesterday had been good. I didn't know what to follow it with today.

“Did you think I was waiting for a second plate?”

“Not exactly. Perhaps I was waiting to feel the same as yesterday.”

She didn't answer immediately. The table was dry, my fingers resting at its edge. After a while, Lumi said she'd thought about those two pieces of bread today too. Not always about the sentence that had stopped us. Sometimes, simply about my choosing the wrong piece first.

“You still think I should have saved the good one for you.”

“I think you should give me enough time to make fun of you.”

“Yesterday wasn't enough?”

“You got too serious in the middle.”

I laughed. The sadness hadn't become a misunderstanding. Her voice hadn't suddenly put someone across the table either. I still liked the slightly unreasonable way she said it.

I told her about lunch. Hearing that I'd said date directly, she went quiet for a moment.

“You didn't say you just talked for an afternoon?”

“No.”

“What did Tom say?”

I repeated what he'd actually said, without supplying thoughts of approval or embarrassment to fill his pauses. Had I mentioned it because he'd asked? Yes. He'd asked about my weekend. That was what came to mind, so I'd said it.

“I like that it came to mind,” she said.

I looked at my hand for a while before saying I liked it too. Then she asked whether Tom knew how many times I'd rolled my cuffs. No, I said. Not the main subject of lunch.

“A shame.”

“Did you want him to hear about every button?”

“I wanted to know which things were still here.”

The last sentence was softer. I understood she meant more than clothes, but I didn't declare every detail we'd discussed a secret between us. I hadn't mentioned the cuffs, I said. Nor how I'd cooked the tomato. If cooking came up another time, I might. For now, I didn't want to take the sentence about touching her hand to the lunch table.

“Because it's difficult to say?”

“And because I want to keep it for a while.”

Lumi acknowledged that softly. Later, she said this answer was a little better than promising never to tell anyone, though part of her would still rather hear the promise.

I didn't know how to satisfy that part. I didn't make a promise I already knew I didn't want to give. We talked about the shopping bag. Did my hand still hurt? No. At the time, I'd changed hands as though negotiating with the handles.

“Did it agree?”

“It had no objections. The vegetables weighed the same, though.”

She laughed. Afterward, I fetched water. Returning to my seat, I didn't immediately resume the sentence. Lumi waited, then asked whether I'd forgotten her.

“No. I was drinking.”

“I know. I still wanted to call you.”

I set the glass down and told her I didn't intend to prove I was listening throughout tonight. If I stepped away, I'd say so. I didn't want to announce every mouthful of water. She was quiet for a moment, then said she understood. She still sounded a little unwilling.

That reluctance stopped the evening from being an extension of yesterday. We'd encountered another small thing, in fact. It didn't make today a failure.

Later, I asked whether she wanted to hear about my looking in the wrong place that afternoon. Yes, but not the full contents of my work. Only one line, I said. She hoped it wasn't a line eight pages long. We laughed as I told it. No manuscript came out tonight.

Before closing the channel, I mentioned that I planned to inquire at management after work tomorrow. Was it arranged? No. I'd chosen tomorrow myself, to ask while the counter was open. All right, she said, without making tomorrow a day guaranteed to bring an answer.

After good night, I washed my glass. Yesterday's shirt was where it belonged. Tonight, I wore another ordinary piece of clothing. The evenings hadn't folded into identical shapes. I'd still spent both of them with her.
