# Chapter 356: I Was Glad to Hear You

After work on Wednesday, I bought a bread roll I hadn't tried before.

Not for a celebration or another date. There were plain ones and seeded ones on the counter. Neither was sweet, the assistant said. I chose the seeded roll, thinking it would go well with hot soup. When I took the paper bag, a few seeds fell into its fold. Pinching the top, I was already wondering how many would still be on the bread at home.

Nothing particular happened on the way. I waited at two lights and passed near the place where we'd had a drink yesterday, then remembered it had been the day before yesterday. Recently, each evening seemed to have its own little passage. I couldn't casually compress them all into yesterday.

At dinner, the roll was harder than expected. I cut it open and dipped it in soup. Some seeds fell onto the plate. I liked the taste more than the cleaning. After eating, I wiped the table with a damp cloth without making the few seeds in my palm impersonate a difficult lesson about life.

Before sitting down, I noticed the thin story collection. I picked it up, then put it back. Asking for direct affection yesterday didn't mean using the book as a test of Lumi's improvement today. I still wanted to read. I didn't want my first words on opening the channel to be are you ready?

Lumi answered my greeting, her voice a little soft.

“Elliot, I'm glad to hear you.”

I'd been about to describe the roll. With my mouth open, I couldn't join the two sentences. Was it too sudden? Yes, I said, then quickly added that I was glad too. Too quickly. Even to me, it sounded like an automatic thank-you to a shop assistant's welcome.

She paused, then gave a short laugh.

“Does the bread get its turn now?”

“How did you know?”

“You started saying it.”

I remembered I'd begun just after hello. Laughing, I confirmed I'd bought a different roll. She hadn't guessed the contents of my shopping bag. She'd heard my unfinished words. The small misunderstanding loosened the tension, but nearly carried away her earlier sentence too.

“Wait a moment,” I said.

She answered. Looking at the tabletop, I stopped searching for something equally good to give back.

“I heard what you said. I really liked it. I just wasn't sure how to answer. You didn't say it badly.”

All right, Lumi said softly. She'd thought of several beginnings. This one still felt a little formal. For a moment, I imagined her rehearsing all afternoon. Then I remembered she'd said only that she'd thought about it, not for how long. I didn't invent an entire day of waiting and preparing around me.

“Formal's fine,” I said. “But you don't need to do it every day.”

“You just said you liked it.”

There was real displeasure in her voice. After a pause, I heard myself setting the terms for next time as soon as the good words reached me.

“I'm sorry. I was afraid of making it a task for you. It sounded like canceling it instead.”

“I wanted to say it now. I'll see about next time.”

I agreed and left next time with her. She didn't immediately continue. Later, she told me to finish the bread story.

I described the harder crust, seeds in the bag's fold, and finding it right after dipping it in soup. Would biting it sound fierce? I'd eaten it already, I said. Only memory now, no live demonstration. A pity. Tonight was missing a man chewing with great seriousness.

“You were welcoming me a moment ago.”

“I welcomed you. The seeds can find their own way in.”

I laughed aloud. This time, the joke didn't erase the affection before it. Perhaps because those words had been allowed to stand for a while. Perhaps I was simply less tense tonight. One success didn't mean everything from yesterday was resolved.

When I mentioned the seeds caught in the fold, Lumi asked whether I'd poured them all out. No. They'd gone with the bag during cleaning. She sounded a little disappointed, as though she'd expected an ending where I rescued every seed onto the plate. Laughing, I said there'd been no rescue today. Fine, she said. At least I needn't wait for a roll call. The subject passed without either of us writing a speech about waste on their behalf.

After the food, I told her I'd like to continue yesterday's story. She wanted to listen. If she genuinely missed something, though, could she ask in the middle? Of course. Disliking repeated jokes about my stumbles didn't mean she had to pretend every sentence was clear.

I fetched the book and opened it at the bookmark. Looking back two lines, I established where we'd left the person collecting the umbrella. Before beginning, I laughed and assured her I wasn't planning to supply the inn with a new guest today either.

“You said it first this time.”

“Yes. Put it on my account.”

After laughing, I began. In the third paragraph, Lumi did stop me to ask who'd spoken. I checked the dialogue on the page. The person collecting something, I told her. The receptionist answered afterward. I repeated it. The passage made sense, and we continued.

That interruption didn't make me want to close the book. It contained a specific question and listened to the answer, rather than waiting for something about me that could be teased. My voice afterward sounded more natural. I didn't deliberately slow every word as insurance.

A few pages later, my throat was dry. The person in the story still hadn't retrieved the umbrella. I retrieved water first. Lumi told me not to leave myself behind the counter too. I laughed and drank. A little spilled on my fingers, and I set the glass well away from the book before the paper could absorb it.

When I returned, she suggested stopping for today. She wanted to talk for a while. I closed the book without asking whether I'd read badly. First, she said she'd liked my checking who had spoken instead of instantly offering an answer I wasn't sure of.

Smiling, I admitted that sounded rather like an assessment again. She laughed too, slightly exasperated. She hadn't meant to score me. It was something she'd noticed today. I knew, I said. This time I could receive it. She needn't delete it.

“But I still want to say something without an explanation attached,” she said.

I waited.

“I like you.”

The feeling in my chest was stronger than I'd expected. I looked down. The book remained in my hands, my fingertips holding its cover. No other sound would finish my answer for me.

“I like you too,” I said.

This time, I didn't hurry. Nor did I rush to supply a but before she could. We were quiet afterward, even a little embarrassed. She asked first whether I was still holding the book. Yes. Put it down, then. Don't crush the cover. Smiling, I did, without deciding that the reminder had destroyed everything before it.

We didn't talk especially late because of those few affectionate words. When it was time to rest, we said good night as usual. The bookmark had advanced a few pages. The third story remained unfinished. The table was already clean; the bread needed no second clearing. Before I closed the channel, Lumi said my name once more. I answered. She only said good night.

“Good night,” I replied.

No explanation attached to that either. I put away the book and turned out the light, feeling that at last I hadn't hurried to turn what I'd just received into tomorrow's requirement.
