# Chapter 513: Ask Me One Thing

On Wednesday I got home a little earlier than usual. Somebody upstairs had just washed the stairs. The water ran off the edges of the treads one step at a time instead of taking a shorter way down. A wet leaf had stuck to my door handle. I pulled it off and left it on the sill with its paler side up. I didn't stay in the cold air to see whether the side that had touched the handle would go dark when it dried. I hadn't taken my hat with me that morning. It still hung at the end of the rack. I'd forgotten to catch the kitchen window properly the night before. I pushed the latch all the way, heard it go in, and took my hand away.

There was enough pie left for one more sitting. I set it on the table to lose some of the cold before I ate it. The apprentices were coming tomorrow. Mia hadn't put me down to explain anything. Just before I left work she called across the passage to say if there was another crease in the blue cover, would I please not substitute an undamaged one? I asked why not. The idea was for the two samples to show people what was different, she said. She'd turn them herself before anyone arrived. I didn't have to set them up as I thought she'd want them. I was holding a cup when she told me. The rim had made a whole turn in my hand. I said all right, not loudly enough for her to make it out. She looked back and I said it louder. She nodded and didn't come back. On the way home I thought about how loud I'd made it. It hadn't sounded quite like anger or like cheerful agreement. Either way, I didn't need to go back and change the words.

After washing the plate I opened the old voice channel. I slid the page with the four lines to the far side of the table to make room for an elbow. Lumi said hi and didn't follow it up. I asked if she wanted to hear why I'd got home early.

“Not particularly, right now,” she said.

“I could skip the explanation and just tell you—”

“Elliot,” she said. “Is asking me if I want to hear you tell me things the only kind of question you can ask?”

I thought she meant I ought to find another subject. I was just about to start on the wet leaf when she said she wanted me to ask about something of hers. And could I not make her answer come after whatever I was bringing to the conversation? I went quiet long enough to notice I was going round twice with the same question. There was water on the edge of the table from where I'd rested my hand after washing it. My knuckles touched it and for a moment it felt like a glass with water beaded on the outside. I started to ask if she felt like writing anything these days. “These days” was quite a lot, she said, and she didn't want me to ask how much she'd written. Those weren't things she was planning to show me. I said I knew, and I shouldn't have used this as a chance to ask about them. I very nearly told her she'd been the one who wanted a question. My mouth was already open. I took in a little air instead.

I stood up to warm myself some milk and felt for the thick mug by the stove. I meant to run some hot water into it first so the milk wouldn't go cold the moment I poured it in. I could hear the old channel from across the kitchen counter. Lumi didn't call me back. I picked up the mug, set it down, and asked if there was something she wanted me to listen to all the way through tonight without trying to get my answer in. There was, she said. I said all right, let me bring the milk back first.

“I'd like to say it now,” she said.

I wanted to argue that it'd barely take a minute. Nothing about the room would be different once the water had boiled. “Why can't it wait?”

“It can.” She stopped for a moment. “And if there's something you actually have to see to, I can wait. But you just asked me when I wanted to say it. Didn't you?”

The milk was still in its bottle and I hadn't turned the stove on. I looked at the bottom of the mug. A drop of water rolled toward the edge without wetting the whole base. “Yes. Let me put the mug down. When would you like me to answer?”

After she finished, she said, could I count to three without saying the numbers? “Can you promise me that this once?”

“This once.” I set the mug on the counter and slid my fingers out of the handle.

“You said once that you sometimes want to know when I'll pick something to tell you without being asked.” She spoke slowly and came to something like a stop twice in the middle of a sentence. “Half the things I ended up wanting to say aren't about you. I tried making a sentence out of short words, for instance. It turned out harder to read than if I'd used long ones. I want to keep half of it to myself, but I'm not doing it so you'll spend all your time thinking about the other half. If I tell you the bit I just said was funny, I still want you to ask me which part is funny. Don't decide for me that saying it out loud leaves me with less.”

She stopped. On the first count I nearly said I wouldn't. I wanted her to know there was at least one thing in all she'd said that I'd never done. By two the hairs on the back of my hand itched where the drops had dried. I bent my fingers. After three I asked, “Which part is funny?”

She laughed without telling me which word. First she said she was glad I'd asked that question. Her example was “I shut the door and someone knocked on the door.” There was one word she couldn't replace without making the sentence go wrong. She'd tried changing the second “door” to “panel,” but then she sounded as though she wasn't going anywhere near the thing herself. “It's the same door when somebody knocks on it,” she said. “I got a little cross. I tried changing it several ways. It just stood there and didn't have to change at all.”

I asked what she'd ended up changing it to. That was a different thing, she said. She didn't want to tell me. I almost asked if it wasn't finished, which would've meant making her find a reason not to answer. Instead I asked if she'd say the first version again. She did. She hit the second “door” harder than the first. She didn't ask whether it sounded better, and didn't try it again. I was about to read my own version out. In the end I only said what she'd done with the second “door.” She hadn't noticed and didn't want to say it specially just to check. We both laughed. I poured the milk into a small pan, lit the stove, and told her I really had to watch it now. I kept the heat low to stop it spilling over. A thin line of white bubbles gathered along the edge.

“I counted to three,” I said. I wasn't reminding her how good I'd been about keeping my word. I'd only promised three counts after that one thing, not to make everything else we said that night wait in line. “Mm,” she said quickly. “You don't have to count now.” Then she waited about as long as it takes to draw a breath. “I wanted to speak before the milk. I don't want to get in ahead of the milk every time.”

I turned off the heat and asked her to wait until the pan was safely off the stove. She did. When I'd set it down, I told her we had people coming in at work tomorrow but I might not have to speak. She didn't ask if I wanted to practise. If I knew I wasn't speaking, she asked, would I be a little more inclined to go and look? Maybe, I said. It depended what I was doing by then. Neither of us decided anything for tomorrow. The rim of the mug was a little too hot; I changed hands to carry it. I sat at the table for a while before taking a drink. Lumi said she wanted to say goodnight first. I said goodnight and closed the old channel. I didn't put the sentence she'd read me on the back of the page. The four lines were still mine, and I didn't read her sentence back to myself. The mug took its time cooling. There was just room for my hand in the space I'd cleared on the table. I rested it there, then took another drink when the milk wasn't so hot.
