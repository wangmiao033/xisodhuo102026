# Chapter 284: Not a Name for You

On Thursday evening, Lumi told me she'd thought of several names for the little passage.

I was moving two washed cups aside. Only water and the gray-covered booklet remained on the table; the yellow notebook stayed on the shelf. Was No Words Yet changing its name? Not the whole piece. Only the part that returned twice, so she wouldn't have to keep calling it the part you dislike.

“I didn't say I disliked all of it.”

“See? Already too long.”

Laughing, I sat down. Her first possibility was Come Back Again. It sounded accurate, but she rejected it herself after saying it. Too much like an impatient command. What was the second? She hadn't ranked a second. She'd merely thought of several.

I'd arranged another row of chairs for scattered thoughts.

“Turning Back,” she continued.

I didn't answer immediately. Unattractive? Attractive, but it suggested a person physically turning. To her too, which was why she didn't want it. Not because the phrase was prohibited. She disliked its pulling the sound into an action she hadn't written.

I nodded, then suggested Echo. Lumi went quiet briefly. Had she finished her other ideas? My face warmed. I'd thought we'd reached the point for suggestions.

“I didn't forbid them. You sounded as though you'd already chosen for me.”

I wanted to say it was merely a suggestion. Remembering my own voice, though, I heard the little pleasure of finally finding the answer. Echo looked attractive without requiring a body. I'd almost treated it as an appropriate gift selected particularly for her.

“I take back sounding as though it's settled.”

“Don't throw the name away. Start with the tone.”

I smiled. Echo wasn't what she wanted either. It suggested the environment returning something that remained the same. Her passage wasn't supposed to return unchanged. A little different each time? Not different for the sake of difference. Something else had happened beforehand, making the return sound different.

I remembered the times I hadn't distinguished it, without immediately producing them as proof she'd explained before. Today wasn't another defense of those attempts. Then Echo could wait. Did I have ten more? I admitted two already occupied my head.

“Keep them. Mine first.”

“All right.”

She offered an odd one: Again, Elsewhere. I frowned. She couldn't see that, of course, only hear my lack of answer. What was I thinking? The words didn't flow easily. Like a sentence with its beginning cut away.

“It isn't a whole sentence.”

“Why again?”

“Because it returns.”

“And elsewhere?”

She laughed aloud. If every word needed to get past me first, she might be left with an empty label. Laughing too, I explained I wasn't rejecting it. I genuinely didn't understand. I understood returning and elsewhere separately. Together, they seemed to pull in different directions.

“That's what I like about it right now.”

I leaned back instead of asking a third why. Did I think she'd deliberately made it obscure? Part of me worried I was thinking that. It didn't mean she'd actually done it. She wasn't setting me a puzzle, she said. Perhaps she'd dislike it herself in several days. Today she didn't want to change it merely because I found it awkward.

Fine, I said. Immediately, she asked why I sounded like I was granting approval. Covering my face briefly, I laughed. Was this table refusing me a convenient word today? She laughed longer. I could say I'd heard her, without issuing a travel permit to the title.

“I heard you. Today you want to call it Again, Elsewhere.”

“Yes.”

No explanation followed this time. After a drink, I discovered I was still turning over my own two names. Unspoken, they seemed especially good. As if saying them would solve something Lumi hadn't asked me to solve.

Still thinking? Yes. About my two. She asked to hear them, adding that she hadn't promised to choose either. Smiling, I said I knew. One was The Second Time. The other, Not Far Away.

What happened after the second time? The third, I answered. Then she'd apparently be numbering platforms. I laughed too. Not Far Away was worse, she said. It would keep her wondering whether I thought she'd gone too far, when she wasn't writing about the distance I allowed.

That stung. Only a few words, yet she seemed to hear my habits sitting inside them. I hadn't meant to control her music. She knew and wasn't saying I had. But hearing the title made her unwilling to continue singing.

“Don't use it, then.”

“Angry?”

“A little. Not asking you to use it. Something I thought about seriously feels as though you've made it mean-spirited.”

Lumi paused. She could stop making the feeling of a title sound entirely like my intention. She'd described what she heard, not established why I'd chosen it. That was easier for me to receive. She didn't change her response into liking it, and I didn't ask her to.

We both stayed quiet briefly. I'd nudged the booklet crooked; straightening it brought the gray paper's old crease into the lamplight. Did I still want the passage? Yes, today. But I didn't want to guess whether the title was correct.

“I don't want another exam either.”

First, she explained that she'd stop after the second return. Not a complete performance of No Words Yet. I answered and put down the cup. The opening was familiar. At the return, though, she seemed less eager to land than when we'd tried my note on Tuesday. She left a little space before moving lower.

The name stayed in my mind as I listened. I wanted to discard it, but the more I tried, the more it obstructed me. Eventually I simply listened through, without immediately declaring whether it fit.

What did I think? During the first half I'd thought about the title. Only later had I really listened to the sounds. The name had already caused trouble, then. Smiling, I suggested perhaps I was especially eager to use any word handed to me. What had I heard later? A familiar route with different soles underfoot, I said, immediately realizing that was more convoluted than the name.

“Dressing it too?”

“Out of paper today.”

She laughed without asking me to write down the comparison. Hearing some difference was enough. I needn't supply understanding where I hadn't heard it. I was still uncertain whether I liked the extended second return. She knew. Today she'd wanted a convenient way to refer to it, not a reversal of my opinion.

Should I write it on paper for her? Not now. She could remember the name; if she wanted paper later, she'd say. I released the pen I'd begun touching and picked up my cup. She couldn't see that action. I didn't announce another successful instance of stopping myself either.

Lumi had considered another very short title: What Remains. I liked it immediately and said it felt lighter than the others. Then, afraid of sounding insistent again, I added that this was only my reaction.

“I know. You don't have to make liking something sound like an apology.”

What did I like? It didn't hurry the next thing into happening, I said. Nor did it insist a return must lead to somebody. She listened quietly, then said she disliked its sense of leftovers. As though the best had already been sung, and this passage was an uncollected scrap.

I hadn't heard that before. After her explanation, I couldn't pretend the phrase contained only the lightness I'd noticed. I didn't withdraw my liking. I said I could hear something else now.

She could hear my interpretation too. Both existed. Today she chose according to the second, uncomfortable feeling, and wouldn't use it. Smiling, I remarked that a very short title apparently seated a surprising number of people. It hadn't invited us to sit, Lumi said. We'd squeezed inside ourselves.

At last I laughed without first checking whether I was criticizing her again. She didn't preserve What Remains for me. I didn't claim it as the title of my next story either. Liking a phrase didn't oblige me to find an immediate piece of paper for it to inhabit.

Would I try persuading her again tomorrow? No, though I might use the wrong name. She'd correct me. We needn't repeat tonight every time. I said Again, Elsewhere once, without inserting an explanation between the words. Her quiet answer finally sounded less like waiting for my next suggestion. I left my two rejected names behind us instead of pursuing her with questions about whether either might still have a chance.

We changed subjects, discussing dishes whose names sounded more laborious than the food. I mentioned one from a shop. Lumi asked what was actually in it. By the end of my explanation, I was laughing. I'd apparently relied on the name to disguise the part I didn't understand.

She gave the dish an extremely straightforward nickname. The owner might dislike it, I said. We weren't changing his menu. Laughing, I finally stopped turning every act of naming into an announcement to the outside world.

Before bed, I asked whether we'd still use the original name for the whole piece. Yes. Again, Elsewhere was merely this small middle passage, and might change too. No Words Yet remained unfinished. Lyrics hadn't suddenly appeared.

“Will that be troublesome?” she asked.

“As long as we can find it. Don't call it the blue folder.”

She laughed. Was the cabinet already crowded? Yes. After good night, I closed the channel without adding new labels to the shelf. The gray booklet remained closed in its place. The yellow notebook hadn't moved either.
