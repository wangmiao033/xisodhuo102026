# Chapter 286: After the Second Return

On Saturday afternoon, I sat down early without a towel in my hands.

Seeing my deliberately empty hands made me smile. Two weeks had passed since I'd asked her to stop, but I still seemed afraid the same basket would carry the whole unhappy occasion back. Today's washing hadn't been done. I hadn't cleared every chore out of the day to hear a passage of music.

I opened the channel and asked whether Lumi wanted to try now. Yes, but not the whole piece from the beginning. I remembered. The middle passage, Again, Elsewhere. I didn't ask whether the name was still valid. Hearing it, she quietly agreed.

Outside, someone dragged something in intermittent bursts. I stood to close the window a little, explaining the movement. Back in my chair, I heard her ask whether we should wait for complete quiet. No. I could hear clearly now. Whoever worked outside didn't know something unfinished was waiting here. They didn't have to reschedule their work for us.

Lumi didn't begin by explaining the revisions. Could I simply listen the first time, then name what I remembered first without ranking it as good or bad? I could try. Smiling, I added that this wasn't a promise to understand every sound.

“Just don't finish understanding before I finish singing.”

Laughing, I agreed. After a brief pause, she began.

The first notes weren't unfamiliar. I heard them descend and return somewhat higher. Part of me remembered the impatience of waiting for the ending last time. Before the second return, however, the gap was shorter than expected. Just as I prepared to call it a pause, her voice returned, like someone with more to say merely shifting their stance.

That didn't abruptly make everything beautiful. The held sound afterward still made me want to move along. But the returns weren't threaded into an identical cord as I'd remembered them. I could hear one short space and one longer, without knowing precisely how much she'd changed.

When she stopped, I glanced at the water without picking it up. What did I remember first? The gap before the second return. So short I'd nearly failed to count it. And afterward? The held note still made me impatient.

“I asked what you remembered first. Not what was still wrong.”

I stopped. Her words weren't harsh, but they revealed my hurry to add a fault, as though naming a difference would count as loving everything. She hadn't forced me to like it. I'd reserved an exit for myself in advance.

“The short gap came first,” I said. “The rest can wait.”

Fine. After a while, she asked whether I wanted to explain the rest now. I laughed. Being asked made me less eager to snatch the chance. She laughed too, but still wanted the explanation. Genuinely, not to make me swallow what I'd said.

I tried describing the held sound without technical language. We'd returned somewhere familiar. I was ready to follow, then it slowed again, leaving me uncertain where to put my next foot. Did I dislike slowness or being wrong? Thinking seriously, I admitted perhaps both. I couldn't separate them today.

She didn't require an immediate distinction. She wanted to keep that inconvenience. I'd heard her. Would I wait for it to become smoother every time I listened? I didn't know. Possibly. But that shouldn't become something she had to accomplish before singing to me.

Even I could hear what an admirable answer it was. I admitted that right now, I actually wanted it faster. Lumi paused. That sentence, she said, was clearer. Not agreement, but she knew I wasn't standing aside describing how I ought to feel.

We didn't sing again immediately. I closed the channel to fetch water. In the kitchen, my cup knocked the sink's edge. I felt its rim; unbroken. Returning, I noticed the gray booklet leaning at the shelf's edge and straightened it, without extracting a replacement entertainment.

After I reopened the channel, Lumi offered her previous version for comparison. I nearly agreed, then asked whether she wanted to sing it herself. Partly, she wanted me to notice the difference. It wasn't entirely a wish to sing.

“I noticed already.”

“You still don't like it much.”

“Right. Different things.”

She answered, her voice lowering. I didn't sweeten my reply to correct that. She knew, but still found herself believing that if I truly understood, I ought to like it more. I sometimes thought that about my story too. I couldn't use the similarity to make her comfortable immediately.

Smiling faintly, she asked why I'd said it, then. Because it was true, not medicine. After a while, her laughter became clearer. I shouldn't turn today into a meeting where we both admitted how difficult we were. Meeting adjourned, I said. She hadn't finished singing yet.

After laughing, she decided against the old version. She wanted the current passage once more, without my Tuesday note. Fine. I didn't use an especially breezy voice to disguise remembering that abandoned experiment.

Could she wait while I moved my chair back? My legs wanted a different position after sitting; the table's edge obstructed them. She agreed. I moved back, settled, and said I was ready. Not an appearance of heightened concentration. It actually felt more comfortable.

On the second hearing, I didn't deliberately pursue the short gap. I recognized it when it came. The next place, though, seemed slower than before. Lumi didn't immediately ask a question at the end. I didn't immediately assess. Outside, the dragging finally ceased. The room was quiet for several seconds.

“I like the opening more this time.”

“The rest?”

“Still uncertain in places.”

She acknowledged that. Again? Enough for today, I said. More and I'd only compare versions instead of listening. She didn't agree immediately. I waited without covering that small disappointment with the cup.

Eventually she said enough. That was today. She didn't want to sing a third time for a clearer answer either. It wasn't an announcement that the passage was finished, much less the whole piece. The name would stay for now. The lyrics were still undecided.

Which parts did she like today? Before the return, she hadn't hurried to become gentle. I could hear some of that. She didn't need I can hear it after every sentence, she said. Smiling, I closed my mouth briefly and let her finish.

There was another place she liked that I'd entirely missed. She described its position without singing extra for me. I said I'd notice if I heard it again, without writing an assignment. She might discard it herself before next time. I'd recognize whatever was there then.

Suddenly, Lumi asked whether she could sing sometime without asking what I thought afterward. Of course, I said, then realized how that sounded: she needed my permission not to collect an opinion. I corrected myself. I'd like to listen that way. She needn't hand me a question every time.

“Would you feel left out?”

“Unaccustomed at first, perhaps. But I can be listening.”

Sometimes she wanted to ask, then couldn't bear the answer. It wasn't entirely something I forced on her. She spoke quickly. I didn't rush to cancel that admission for her. Sometimes, without asking, she'd keep guessing what I thought, which became more uncomfortable than actually asking.

One occasion without a question needn't become a rule, I said. If she wanted to know then, she could still ask. After a silence, Lumi said she'd merely imagined an evening without tidying up comments. We'd already given it several branches.

Laughing, I admitted it. She told me to stop analyzing for now. Could today still contain something without an assessment? Looking toward the table's edge, I considered the comedy booklet, then stopped. She hadn't requested another entertainment.

“This interval could,” I said.

We genuinely didn't search for words immediately. Eventually she offered a short, peculiar remark: my water had probably waited so long it no longer wanted to be water. I picked it up, drank, and laughed. Still willing. Warm. She didn't rank that reply. I didn't require her to explain what made it funny.

Music hadn't finally solved this for us. The melody retained our disagreement, and the cup was ordinary. Yet I no longer feared that several minutes without new content would make the afternoon unworthy of its time.

We discussed dinner. I wanted something with broth, not a reward for her performance; lunch had been dry. She hadn't prepared to collect an invisible prize bowl, Lumi said. Laughing, I asked what she would collect. After considering, she wanted no more questions today about whether she'd improved.

“I didn't ask that.”

“Keep not asking.”

I agreed. She didn't mean we could never discuss it. Only that she didn't want it as today's ending. She'd sung. I'd heard some things and missed others. We needn't stamp it better to establish that it had happened.

I didn't repeat her words. If I went for a walk tomorrow morning, would she want to hear what I saw? Yes, but I shouldn't go merely to bring her an interesting story. Smiling, I said it might only be bread. Or I might not go, depending on when I woke.

“Don't promise me a hat.”

“The one in the book is still missing.”

She laughed, her voice close. I wanted to sit a little longer and told her. Then sit, she said. I didn't have to wait for more singing. I leaned back. The water was still warm. We discussed small things without arranging a third performance tonight.

When it was time to cook, I stood, said goodbye, and closed the channel. The pot waited in its usual place. I took it out, washed my hands, and decided how much water to use.
