# Chapter 282: One Note When I Ask

On Tuesday morning, I couldn't find the blue folder.

Yesterday it had been on the cabinet's second shelf. Today that shelf was neat, even its habitually raised piece of cardboard pressed flat. I searched my desk, then opened an empty drawer despite knowing I hadn't brought it back there.

Passing, Marcus asked what I needed. The blue folder containing blank handover forms. He pointed upward. Someone had tidied yesterday. Looking up, I found a narrow blue edge between two thick binders.

“How did I miss that?”

“You kept looking for the second shelf.”

I smiled and took it down. It held the wrong things. The small label on the cover said spare sleeves. Without reading it, I'd already completed finding, taking, and starting work in my head.

Marcus hadn't gone far. I called him back and showed him. There must be another, then; I shouldn't exchange their contents. Understood. I replaced it and drew the front folders slightly outward on the second shelf to inspect the labels.

Tom returned with water and asked whether the cabinet owed me money. A blue folder, I said. Looking around, he pointed to a thin brown folder on the neighboring desk. Someone had collected blank forms earlier. The blue one might be underneath.

Instead of lifting everything on the desk, I asked the woman sitting there whether I could look. She moved her own papers aside and agreed. Beneath the brown folder lay the blue one, containing the version I needed. I took several forms and straightened the rest.

“Where should it go afterward?”

Back in the cabinet. She'd expected to replace it shortly, then been called away. I nodded without stretching shortly into an argument about responsibility. Returning it, I noticed a corner of its label had folded inward, hiding several words.

I flattened it with a fingertip, read the label, and put the folder on the second shelf.

When another colleague needed it that afternoon, he looked upward first too. Second shelf, on the left, I told him. Finding it, he asked how I knew so precisely. We'd met several times today. He laughed and took a sheet without asking whether I'd permanently accepted management of the cabinet.

Before leaving, I returned the completed forms where they belonged. No stack remained on my desk for tomorrow's guessing. I took no work contents home. At the door, Tom asked whether I wanted lunch downstairs tomorrow. Yes, I said, then added that if something came up, he needn't come looking for me. He could go ahead. He looked at me. He'd only asked about lunch.

“Right.” I laughed. “Brought an extra set of instructions.”

After dinner, with the channel open, Lumi asked whether I could make a very short sound.

I looked up. “What sound?”

“Not yet. Wait until I say.”

She sounded slightly tense, as though expecting my mouth to open already. I'd actually drawn half a breath. I stopped. She added that this wasn't a test of my restraint. She wanted to try the return in No Words Yet, and hear what happened with one sound resting there.

Was I singing with her? Not together tonight. One after the other would do. She hummed a note, not particularly high. After she stopped, I tried it and landed too low. Laughing, Lumi said I'd sent it downstairs.

“I thought that was close.”

“Same building.”

My laughter dispersed the breath. I didn't force the remainder to sustain anything. She hummed again. I waited and supplied a short sound. This time was fine. That one. It wasn't beautiful, but didn't need to become beautiful enough for me to impersonate somebody else.

“When?”

“After I say your name. Once, briefly.”

I nodded, remembered she couldn't see, and answered. Instead of beginning, she said that if she forgot to call, I wouldn't have a part tonight. Laughing, I asked whether this counted as an easy job. Certainly, provided I didn't hurry her along from the sidelines.

When the melody began, I attended entirely to when she might call me. I missed the first two turns. Like someone waiting beside a doorway for his name, I heard everything happening inside without retaining it.

She stopped.

“Are you only waiting?”

“A little.”

“Forget it, then.”

My heart sank. Forget my note, or the whole attempt tonight? The note for now. She didn't want to add a nervous person to her melody. I nearly denied being nervous. My hands were pressing my knees down as though they might escape without permission.

“I turned it into waiting for my number,” I said.

She smiled a little in her voice. Identifying it didn't make her immediately restore the arrangement. I didn't demand another attempt to prove my nerves were gone. We discussed what I'd actually heard. Only the opening was clear to me. Afterward, I admitted, I'd been preparing.

Was it particularly unlike playing together? Rather like a new employee afraid of missing his first assignment, I said. She laughed more plainly and asked who was paying me.

“Nobody. Haven't found the blue folder yet.”

She didn't understand. I briefly described the morning's mistaken folder, mentioning colors and positions, not the forms' contents. At two blue folders in the same building, she laughed that even inside a cabinet I was waiting for the correct note. At least those had labels. I hadn't looked first.

“I don't want to hang one on you.”

“No convenient clip.”

After laughing, she considered in silence. Then she wanted to try differently. First, I could simply listen to a passage, without expecting my name. Afterward, she'd ask for the note I'd learned. It wasn't an official addition to the melody. She wanted to hear whether placing them consecutively changed the return she imagined.

“I can listen that way.”

“Try first. Don't promise.”

All right. This time she sang only a brief passage, leaving a gap before the return. Its last sound didn't land where I'd expected. I heard it suspended without immediately supplying my own voice to fill the space.

Lumi stopped properly and called me.

I made the short sound and ended it. Not because I'd locked myself down. It was genuinely the only note I knew.

Wrong, she said. I looked up, preparing to try again, but she explained that I hadn't sung it incorrectly. Coming afterward, it sounded flatter than she'd imagined. She'd expected something left unclosed. Instead, it felt as though someone had pushed a door firmly shut for her.

The note had sounded steady to me. Exactly. Too steady. She disliked it after that passage. I felt disappointed. Even when trying a single sound, I'd secretly hoped mine would prove precisely useful.

“You don't have to keep it.”

“I know. You sound as though you don't.”

Smiling a little, I admitted I needed to get used to it. I wasn't a person being deleted, only a sound she'd tried. Saying that didn't immediately remove the disappointment. Lumi didn't replace her reaction with praise to ease my exit either.

There was nothing wrong with my note itself. She still didn't want to attach it. I understood. We didn't attempt another round or call tonight a newly completed collaboration.

Lumi asked whether I still wanted another attempt. Yes, though not in that position. Only after saying it did I realize I was already finding another gap that might accommodate me. Instead of asking where, she asked whether I had actually heard the ending she left when I supplied my note.

Thinking, I said I had. Once my own voice began, however, I thought only about getting it right. That small question of pitch became larger than the melody before it. I hadn't heard the two together as clearly as she had.

“Where else do you want to put it, then?”

“I don't know yet.”

Then I could leave it unknown for now. Looking at the cup, I suddenly wanted to laugh. I had no score and didn't know how to arrange two voices. Successfully following a single note had made me look for my seat inside an unfinished piece.

I told her. Lumi refused to let me describe myself as entirely useless. She really had asked me to try and wanted to hear it. She hadn't included me out of kindness. After trying, she disliked the result. Neither person needed to retreat until it was as though they had never come.

“So I can still be a little disappointed.”

“Yes. Don't use it to change my answer.”

I wouldn't. If I started, she could remind me. That didn't make her responsible for defending herself against every sentence before I spoke. She acknowledged that, then described a small distinction: my note began with a breath. She'd actually liked that, though the rest didn't fit.

Could breath count as sound? Laughing, she asked what else I'd been waiting to release. I laughed too, without demanding that liking the beginning require her to choose the entire passage again.

Later, I closed the channel briefly to refill my water. Returning, I heard her ask whether I still remembered the note. About to demonstrate, I laughed first. Was she asking me to sing, or only asking whether I remembered? Only asking, she said, laughing too.

“Probably. Might send it downstairs again tomorrow.”

“We'll find it then.”

I didn't make that an appointment for tomorrow evening. Lumi wanted to consider her ending again, so I would read a few pages on my own. Before I closed the channel, she suddenly said my name. I looked up instinctively, but made no sound.

“Good night,” she said.

Smiling, I answered. Her final call tonight hadn't been a request for a note.
