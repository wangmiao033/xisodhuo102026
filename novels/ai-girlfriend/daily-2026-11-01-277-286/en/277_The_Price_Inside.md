# Chapter 277: The Price Inside

On Thursday, a handwritten notice stopped me outside the secondhand bookshop.

The books in the front rack had been reduced. I'd intended to walk past and buy something for tomorrow's breakfast. Then I recognized a cover resembling my collection of radio comedies. The missing hat was still at home, but I already wanted to know what somebody else had lost.

I picked up a booklet with no price on its cover. A sticker on the back had lost its numbers. The bookseller was finding change. I waited until his customer collected her bag before putting the book down and asking the price.

“Inside,” he said, indicating the cover.

A number occupied the upper corner of the first page. Higher than the notice outside. Was it reduced too? He turned several pages, examined it, and said this one didn't belong in that stack.

I pointed to where I'd found it. Frowning, he went over and moved a few books. Somebody had probably put it back incorrectly. The discounted ones belonged on the shelf below.

“So this is still the price inside?”

“Yes.”

I'd brought it to the counter. Now putting it back seemed to require explaining that I could afford it. I hadn't even read a page properly. Beneath my thumb, the damaged sticker had turned gray with handling. I released it.

“Can I have a look first?”

Of course. He left it with me and served the next customer.

I moved aside and read two pages. Another misunderstanding, but this one didn't make me want to continue. Someone mistook a visitor for a deliveryman; the visitor thought the other man had come to collect money. They kept asking the same question. Several pages remained. My first thought was how far I'd need to read before it justified the price.

Not a promising beginning.

Returning it to the counter, I said I wouldn't buy it today. The bookseller took it. Wrong price? Partly, but I didn't especially want the contents either. He nodded and put it behind the counter, without turning my hesitation into further bargaining.

A small old atlas stood among the reduced books. Inside, someone had circled a place. The street names were unfamiliar and the publication date old. I only wanted to see how the tiny print fitted on a page. I didn't actually need it to guide me anywhere.

A customer reached for a lower volume. The stack slid forward slightly. I caught it with my palm. He apologized and helped straighten the leaning books. He held a woodworking manual; my atlas had become trapped between us.

“Taking that one?”

“No. Just looking.”

“Me too. Though it looks remarkably like buying.”

I laughed. He found a page with diagrams and continued reading beside the rack. I closed the atlas and returned it without crushing another spine. When I left, my hands were empty.

The bakery remained open. After buying what I'd originally wanted, I stepped aside at the till and remembered that not buying a second book hadn't increased the first one's value. There were simply pages at home I still wanted to read. Tonight, I could read them.

At home, yesterday's tired throat barely bothered me. I didn't test a gruff voice to check. I changed, put the bread away, and cooked with the channel closed. After dinner, I fetched the booklet without looking for the yellow notebook first.

I continued from the bookmark. At last, the attendant opened a lower compartment. The visitor said his hat didn't have such a broad brim. Whatever lay inside wasn't his. They argued a little longer before the attendant shut the door and asked whether he'd tried next door.

Apparently, there was another small lost-property cabinet at a different reception desk.

The visitor became furious. Why hadn't he said so earlier? The attendant pointed out that he'd asked about this cabinet from the beginning. Naturally, that was the cabinet he'd answered about. Eventually, they went out together to ask next door. The dialogue stopped with a voice beyond the door: Now who's looking after this place?

Nobody returned carrying a hat.

The next page began a different piece. I checked the previous page again, making sure nothing was missing. The hat wasn't on somebody's head. No suddenly talkative cabinet resolved the difficulty. It might simply be somewhere else, and these two excessively serious people had finally agreed to ask.

I was disappointed. After laughing, I still wanted to know whether they'd found it.

When I opened the channel, Lumi asked whether I'd been practicing voices again. No. I'd finished reading the hat scene to myself. Immediately, she asked where it was.

“Still don't know.”

“You said you'd finished.”

I read her the final lines, then explained that a different piece followed. I wasn't deliberately withholding the hat. After a pause, she asked whether I'd turned the wrong page. I'd gone back to check too.

“Then my advance disappointment yesterday was entirely justified.”

Laughing, I said she could be officially disappointed now. She didn't need a certificate from me. Had the author forgotten the hat? Perhaps getting both people out of the room had seemed enough, I suggested. I couldn't explain the author's intentions with certainty.

“Did you like it?”

“The earlier part. I wanted more from the ending.”

“Then you didn't like the ending.”

Looking at the page, I realized I was protecting the feelings of a small purchased story. “Yes. I disliked the ending a little.”

She wanted to see the hat too, Lumi said, then laughed and changed that to hearing it had been found. The correction wasn't solemn. I didn't immediately reassure her that I knew what she'd meant. She could say see without reminding herself of the paper she couldn't see in front of me.

Wouldn't putting the hat on his head have been too easy? Yes, she said, but now it hadn't even received easy. I laughed and half closed the booklet, catching the bookmark with my thumb before losing the place.

“What if the attendant had worn it away?” I suggested.

Lumi objected immediately. They'd been facing one another throughout. Unless the hat had changed, its owner should have noticed. Perhaps he'd kept it behind him. Then he'd hidden it, not worn it. Trying to preserve the idea, I began supplying the attendant with an additional coat and laughed at myself. One ending already needed another passage to support it.

The hat could finish work by itself, she suggested. In my ordinary voice, I asked where it had gone. It had listened all day, she said. Surely it had the right to leave first. Hearing right, I deliberately borrowed the attendant's manner to ask whether it had left a handover note.

“Only: Don't follow me.”

Laughing, I almost lowered my voice further. Remembering last night, I kept my natural pitch and said the hat was cleverer than either person. That ending wouldn't work, Lumi decided. A clever hat would make her stop liking the original two fools. Hadn't it been her suggestion? She had to say it before discovering it was poor.

I moved the booklet aside instead of putting it between us and what we'd invented. Then I tried the version in which the people next door were discussing another cabinet. By the second attendant's entrance, I'd forgotten what the original visitor wanted.

“Now we're looking for you,” Lumi said.

I abandoned the unfinished line and laughed for a while. No restart, and none of our inventions written onto the printed pages. Each made the joke longer without improving it. Eventually, I gave up. At least we hadn't paid for these particular versions.

“Did you buy something else today?”

“No. Nearly.”

I described the book with its price inside. Had I initially wanted to buy it to prove I wasn't complaining about the cost? A little. Then the shop should hide every price, she said. Each customer could demonstrate their character at the counter.

Laughing, I said I'd run away quickly. I'd already stood there a considerable time, she pointed out.

“You weren't there.”

As the words escaped, I heard the unnecessary sharpness. Lumi stopped. I apologized. She meant the sequence I'd just described, not that she'd witnessed it. I'd felt accurately described and wanted somewhere to hide.

“I know you're hiding.”

“Yes.”

How much dearer was the book? I gave both prices. The difference hadn't put it beyond my means. I simply hadn't wanted it. After listening, she said she didn't have to make everything I wanted sound worthwhile in order to be on my side.

I laid the booklet flat. Her words brought the unfinished melody to mind, but I didn't drag the conversation toward it. I still wanted to keep this book despite the missing hat. She wasn't trying to make me return it, she said.

“Fortunately. Going back to look for the hat would take some explaining.”

“Ask about the cabinet first.”

I laughed. We didn't continue playing the characters this time. She asked about dinner. I answered briefly, then listened to the bad ending she most wanted to retain. Not to turn it into a work of her own. She simply still found the hat finishing work funnier than the attendant doing so.

Before bed, I moved the paper bookmark to the end of the piece without folding another corner. The yellow notebook remained in its place. I hadn't fetched it to compensate for somebody else's conclusion. Breakfast was put away, the shelf hadn't gained another book, and tonight I didn't owe anybody a hat that must be recovered.
