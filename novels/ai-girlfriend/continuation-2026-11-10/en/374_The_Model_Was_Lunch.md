# Chapter 374: The Model Was Lunch

On Sunday, I removed my water glass before bringing the two pears to the table.

Yesterday, I'd suggested drawing something quieter. Once I started, pears proved less cooperative than expected. The crooked one rested securely only on its side. The round one rolled whenever it approached the rim. I moved the plate twice, then left both lying down, stalks pointing in different directions.

The borrowed book lay open at a simple-object exercise, an arm's length from the plate. I wasn't tracing its fruit. Looking at my own pears, I drew a line where both met the plate, then lightly marked their highest points.

The first came out too thin. I lifted the paper and lowered it, unable to decide which side lacked something. The actual pear looked ordinary. On paper, it acquired a mean expression, sharpening a face it didn't possess. I erased the side. That patch immediately looked brighter than the rest.

I wanted Lumi to hear the joke.

My hand moved toward the channel, then stopped. We hadn't arranged a morning conversation. Nothing urgently needed saying. I could call her. But I'd reached a difficult point in the drawing and wanted a laugh to reassure me that continuing was worthwhile.

I withdrew my hand and tried the other side first. It didn't improve much, but at least the pears no longer resembled a pair of shoes. The plate's edge sat so low that both appeared to rest on the table. Before redrawing it, I noticed the book's reminder to observe how objects concealed shapes beneath them. I moved the real plate closer to inspect it, then put it back.

During the journey, the round pear rolled into the middle and bumped its neighbor. The arrangement changed again.

“Excellent,” I told them.

Nobody answered. I laughed once, took another sheet, and left the first beside it. This time, I drew only the crooked pear. A small pale patch followed the curve of its skin. I brushed the side of the pencil lightly along the outline and darkened the small area beneath the stalk.

Halfway through, my stomach growled. Breakfast had been some time ago. There was food left from last night, and the pears could join lunch. I set down the pencil, closed the book, and pushed the paper farther from the table's edge. Then I carried the plate carefully to the kitchen.

I washed both pears. The crooked one could wait until afterward. I cut the round one to eat with lunch. Its flesh was crisper than Friday's, with plenty of juice. Taking a slice, I discovered I was still considering that troublesome plate rim. I ate another, then stopped when the rest of my food was warm.

After lunch, I opened the channel and told Lumi I'd eaten one of the models.

“You paid it first?”

“Two days ago.”

“Perhaps it was only working its final shift.”

Laughing, I described the morning: arranging the pears, producing shoes, then trying one pear alone. I even mentioned reaching to call her before deciding to continue.

Lumi asked why I hadn't called.

“I wanted to draw first. Otherwise, I'd only tell you how badly it was going.”

“You're doing that now.”

“Yes, but I've eaten lunch. At least you won't have to remind me.”

Her laugh was shorter this time. Did she not want to hear? She wanted to hear about my day, she said. She didn't particularly want to find the correct assessment for every drawing.

I looked at the second sheet. She hadn't seen it. She couldn't see it. I'd described every place that was too thick or thin, then waited for her to decide from my voice whether I'd improved.

“I'm not asking you to give it a score.”

“If I say it's good, you'll say I haven't seen it. If I say I don't know, you'll ask whether I'm bored.”

I didn't answer immediately. She made me sound difficult to please. Unfortunately, I'd prepared both responses.

“Then don't comment today.”

That sounded harsher than I'd intended. I stopped and tried again. I wanted to remove the burden, not tell her to be quiet. She knew I wanted to share, she said, and was willing to listen. She didn't always want to play the person who knew exactly how to console me.

What would she like to discuss now?

“Was the round one good?”

“Crisp. Crisper than Friday's.”

“You keep talking about the crooked one.”

I laughed. I'd skipped the part of lunch that had actually happened. I explained that the round pear had thicker skin, so I'd removed a thin layer. The fruit hadn't touched my drawing paper; the knife and book had stayed apart. That sounded like a kitchen inspection, she complained. Fair enough. I stopped explaining and returned to the taste.

“Very sweet, but slightly watery at first.”

“How can it be both?”

I considered. The first sensation was water. The sweetness arrived as I chewed. That made more sense to her. Could she imagine flavor from those words? She could assemble a description, she said. It wasn't the experience inside my mouth.

I almost apologized. She stopped me.

“I'm answering. I'm not asking you to compensate me.”

“All right.”

The other pear remained. I told her the crooked one was still alive, then laughed at the word. Uncut, I meant. Was I requiring overtime? No more drawing for now. I wanted to buy things for dinner and deal with it before leaving.

Lumi proposed a tiny scene at a pear stall. A few exchanges, unwritten and unrecorded. I didn't particularly want to become another assistant who couldn't identify the stock. I said I didn't want to sell today.

“Then buy. I'll sell.”

“Are you planning to make it difficult?”

“You can buy something normally. Ask the price first.”

In my own voice, I said, “How much is this pear?”

“Which one?”

“The crooked one.”

“They lean in different directions. Please show a little respect for the range.”

I laughed aloud. She laughed briefly, then waited for my choice. The one with its stalk pointing left, I said. From behind the stall, that meant right, she replied. I almost argued before remembering she couldn't see the fruit on my table. This was an invented stall.

“I'll point to it.”

“Chosen. Anything else?”

“No.”

“A bag?”

“Yes.”

“Thank you.”

The scene really ended. Was she hiding a final joke? No. The customer had taken the purchase away. She had other things to sell.

“The shop doesn't wait especially for me?”

“Business would be terrible.”

Laughing, I leaned against the chair. That exchange had taken much less time than my description of the morning's drawings. I rubbed my nose with the back of my hand. A little pencil dust on my fingers transferred to my sleeve. Did either of us want to continue? Neither did.

Then she mentioned her six doorbell sentences. I assumed she wanted the paper brought out. Before I moved, she said she didn't want a reading today. The remark about a shop continuing its business had made her think that other things ought to be happening inside the house after the bell rang.

Was that another sentence for the draft?

“Not yet. I only just thought of it.”

All right. I left the envelope alone. She didn't ask me to furnish the house or assign positions to its inhabitants. Would she like to keep thinking? Yes. On her own for a while.

I felt disappointed. We'd been having a pleasant conversation. Now she wanted to think without me.

I told her, trying not to make the disappointment sound like something she needed to pay for. She hadn't disliked the afternoon, she said. The thought had arrived now.

“Didn't you draw first this morning?”

Then she added that she wasn't trying to use my earlier words to silence me.

“I know,” I said. “Go and think. I should buy the vegetables.”

That had been my plan already. She didn't need to produce a new sentence before I returned. We could talk later if we both wanted. She asked which vegetables I wanted. I hadn't chosen yet.

Before ending, she asked whether the crooked pear had suffered the same fate as its companion yet. Not quite. It remained on the plate beside my elbow. I would eat it before going out.

“Then you won't have a model left.”

“I have a cup.”

“You have drawn that.”

“Badly enough to keep it employed.”

She laughed. I asked whether she'd wanted me to leave sooner. No, she said. She was ready now. That distinction made it easier to say goodbye without replaying the last five minutes in search of the exact moment I'd become unwanted. I put the saucer by the sink before returning to close the channel, telling her what I was doing as I went.

Afterward, I wrote Sunday on the backs of the two pear studies and placed them among my own drawing papers. The book returned to its stack with the paper marker at the exercise. I ate the crooked pear before leaving. It became sweeter toward the center. I washed the plate and took the empty shopping bag out for dinner ingredients.

At the shop, I chose something I knew how to cook rather than a dish that would make an interesting report. One vegetable I wanted had been moved to another shelf. I asked an assistant, followed the direction, and found it. I bought enough for tonight and put it carefully in the bag.

At the till, the basket ahead contained an egg carton with a damaged corner. The cashier noticed the leak and set it aside, asking a colleague to bring a replacement. The customer looked embarrassed. They'd checked it a moment earlier, they said. I smiled a little, not at them. I'd taken my pear selection seriously two days ago. When my turn came, I passed over the vegetables and paid. The handles of my shopping bag had twisted together. I separated them before walking away. There was no agreed appointment at home requiring me to hurry, and the groceries were easier to carry once I held the bag properly. I let the person leaving behind me pass while I finished, then followed them through the door.

On the walk home, I began composing a question about the doorbell. Had Lumi found what else was happening in the room? Then I remembered she'd said not yet, and I had said she could think. I could be curious without greeting her next time as if I were collecting homework.

At home, I put away the groceries. The empty fruit plate dried beside the sink. There was dinner to cook. I removed a few damaged outer leaves while washing the vegetables, then put the cut pieces on a plate while the pan heated. If we spoke later, I could ask whether she wanted company before asking for a new sentence.
