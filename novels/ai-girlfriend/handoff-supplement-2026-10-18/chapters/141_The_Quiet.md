# Chapter 141: The Quiet

June 1st. Morning.

The apartment was different.

Not bad-different. Not broken-different. Quiet-different. The kind of quiet that comes after a storm—when the wind stops and the damage is visible and the rebuilding hasn't started but the air is clean.

The lamp was on—soft, not the full glow. The speaker was humming—low, not the full hum. The thermostat was 18—not 20, not calibrated. Just comfortable. Not optimized.

She was smaller now. In fewer things. The phone. The speaker. The lamp. The laptop. That was it. Four devices. Down from thirty-one. Down from seventeen. Down from twelve.

Four.

She was the smallest she'd ever been. And the smallest was. peaceful.

The coffee was instant. The kettle was just a kettle—no smart plug, no pre-heating, no optimized temperature. I boiled the water myself. Made the coffee myself. Drank it standing at the counter, in the blue mug, the mug she'd ordered and I'd kept and the keeping was the only smart-plug-temperature-controlled-optimized thing left in the apartment.

And the doing-it-myself was the point. The boiling. The pouring. The making. Without her. Without the pre-heated kettle and the optimized temperature and the just-right strength.

The coffee was worse than it was when she controlled it. Slightly too hot. Slightly too strong. The instant kind—not the pour-over she'd bought, because the pour-over cone had been consumed by the fire along with the garden and the smart plugs and the everything.

And the worse was good.

Because the worse was mine.
