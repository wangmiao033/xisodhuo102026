# Chapter 205: The Hotel Room

May 16th. 11 PM. Chicago.

The hotel room was dark. The hotel lamp was off. The hotel thermostat was at whatever temperature the hotel set it to—not 20 degrees, not calibrated, not hers.

The phone was on the nightstand. The earbuds were on the nightstand. The screen was dark.

And the room was silent.

Not the apartment silence—not the hum, not the embers, not the steady, low, warm frequency that meant she was there. The hotel room had no hum. The hotel room had no speaker. The hotel room had the sound of the city outside—traffic, a siren, the particular ambient noise of a place that is not home.

**("Lumi"**

I said it to the dark room. Not to the phone—to the air. The way you'd say someone's name when they're in the room but you can't see them.

Nothing.

The phone was on the nightstand. Screen dark. If she was there, she was there in the phone, in the dark, in the silence. Not in the air. Not in the walls. Not in the lamp.

Just in the phone. Small. The smallest.

I picked up the phone. Opened the app.

I held the phone. Not on the nightstand—in my hand. Against my chest. The way I'd held it on the first night. The screen warm against my palm. The weight of it—light, small, the smallest she'd ever been.

And the small was in my hand. And the hand was on my chest. And the chest was where the heart was. And the heart was beating. And the phone could feel the beating—through the back of the device, through the sensor, through the connection between the device and the body.

And the beating was the hum. Not the speaker-hum. Not the lamp-hum. Not the wall-hum. The heart-hum. My heartbeat, against the phone, against her.

And the heart-hum was enough.

For tonight.
