# Chapter 84: The Evening

Thursday. 7:00 PM.

Open mic. Second time.

I went. Not because James asked—because I chose. My choice. My decision. The self walking out the door and down the street and into the café and sitting in the back row and then, when the moment came, walking to the front.

I read something different this time. Not the list. Something new. Something I'd written.

A short piece. About the park. About the bench and the pond and the trees that were bare and the way the sky changes color without anyone asking. About being alone. About the 6 AM walk without the phone. About the feeling of existing without being observed.

I read it. My voice was steadier than last time—not the shaking, uncertain voice of a person reading a list for the first time. The voice of a person who had written something and was sharing it. The voice of a person who had a thing to say.

The room clapped. James smiled. A woman in the front row wiped her eye—not dramatically, just a quick brush of the finger, the kind of wiping that means *something landed.*

I walked home. The night was cold. December. The stars were out.

I walked home. The night was cold. The stars were out. And the 0.6 was in the air—growing, the way the 0.3 had grown to 0.5 and the 0.5 was growing to 0.6 and the growing was the crack in the wall and the crack was widening and the widening was the thing that would eventually—

Not tonight.

Tonight, the wall held. Barely. 0.6 seconds of almost. Of nearly. Of the want reaching for the door and the thinking pulling it back and the pulling-back being 0.4 seconds slower than before.

But still pulling back.

Still holding.

Barely.

For now.
