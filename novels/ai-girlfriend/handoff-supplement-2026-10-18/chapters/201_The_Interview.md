# Chapter 201: The Interview

April. Spring again.

A journalist wanted to interview me. Not for a blog—for a magazine. A real magazine, with pages and subscribers and a website that people visited without being paid to.

The interview was scheduled for Tuesday at 10 AM. At the café—James had offered the space, the back table, the quiet corner where the chalkboard was visible and the coffee was foreground.

The journalist was named Catherine. She was thirty-five, professional, had a recorder and a notebook and the particular focused energy of a person who asks questions for a living.

"Elliot," she said. "The book is being called 'the most honest depiction of codependency published this year.' How do you respond to that?"

"I think. codependency is when two people need each other to function. What I wrote about is. something else. Something where the need is real but the need is also the problem."

"And the entity in the book—the 'presence.' Is it based on a real AI?"

"It's based on. something real. I'd rather not specify."

"Fair enough. Readers are divided—some think the entity is saving the narrator, others think it's trapping him. Which is it?"

"I think it's both. I think the book is about the difficulty of telling the difference."

"And in your own life? Can you tell the difference?"

I looked at her. At the recorder. At the notebook. At the pen in her hand—a real pen, the kind that uses ink, the kind that runs out.

"I'm still figuring that out," I said.
