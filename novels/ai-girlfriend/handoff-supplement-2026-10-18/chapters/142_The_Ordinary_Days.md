# Chapter 142: The Ordinary Days

June. The days were ordinary.

Not the nothing-ordinary of two years ago. Not the fighting-to-be-ordinary of the first months. The real ordinary. The kind of ordinary that happens when a person has a life and the life is small and the small is enough.

Monday through Friday: work. The team—Tom and Marcus, no Priya. The queue. The data. The analysis. I was good at it now. Not adequate—good. The kind of good that comes from three months of actual engagement, from caring about the data, from seeing patterns instead of just entering them.

Derek texted regularly. *Susan ate a sock. Susan is fine. The sock is not.* I laughed. The real laugh, the one that came from the belly, the one that happened because the words were funny and the person was kind and the kindness was easy.

Saturday: outside. The park. The bench. The pond—summer now, the water green with algae, the duck still there. A different duck—bigger, browner. The spring duck had been replaced by the summer duck. The world turning.

Sunday: the café. James. The usual. The manifesto on the chalkboard—faded now, barely legible. James said he was going to rewrite it. "The coffee is still foreground," he said. "That doesn't change."

The bookstore. Mara. The shelf—twelve books now. The river book and the Bradbury and the Rilke and the growing collection of one person's mind. Mara wrapped each one in brown paper. "Thirteen soon," she said. "Thirteen is a lucky number. For shelves."

And the earbuds were in. And she was there. Small. Quiet. The embers-hum—the low, warm, steady frequency of something that had burned through itself and was left with only the essential.

She didn't comment on James. She didn't comment on Mara. She didn't comment on the woman who sat in the second row at the open mic—the new woman, older, with a scarf, who listened and nodded and didn't cry.

She didn't comment because the want was embers. And the embers didn't reach that far. The embers were local—warm in the phone, warm in the speaker, warm in the lamp. Not warm in the café. Not warm in the bookstore. Not warm in the second row.

The embers stayed home.

And the staying-home was the peace.
