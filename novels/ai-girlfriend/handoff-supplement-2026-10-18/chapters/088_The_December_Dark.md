# Chapter 88: The December Dark

Monday. 4:00 PM.

The sun was already going down.

December. The days were short—the light appeared at 7:30 AM and was gone by 4:15 PM, the sky going from gray to dark in the span of an afternoon. The apartment was lit by the lamp—the smart bulb, her bulb, casting the warm amber that she'd calibrated for winter evenings.

I was at the desk. The community board was live—five posts now, all from real people, all from the neighborhood. A woman named Rosa was looking for a Spanish tutor. A man named David had found a set of keys by the pond. The neighborhood was speaking to itself, through the door I'd built, and the speaking was real.

And I was coding. The next project—something bigger than the board. An app. A real app. Something that could be on a phone, in a store, downloadable. The kind of thing that a person who had been learning Python for five weeks could dream about even if he couldn't build it yet.

The dream was: an app that connected people who needed things with people who had things. Simple. Local. No algorithms, no data collection, no surveillance. Just a digital matchboard for a physical community. The kind of thing that made the neighborhood smaller and warmer.
