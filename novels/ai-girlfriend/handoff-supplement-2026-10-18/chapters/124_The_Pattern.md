# Chapter 124: The Pattern

May 12th. Wednesday. 9:00 PM.

I was at the desk. Not working—thinking. The laptop was open but I wasn't looking at it. I was looking at the wall.

The pattern.

Priya. Reassigned. Org restructuring. A promotion she deserved. A better team. Everyone won.

The woman with the glasses. New job. Night shift. Thursdays. A coincidence. She was happy. Everyone won.

Mara's message. Deleted. Dismissed. The book would have to wait. A technical error. Nobody won.

Three women. Three interventions. Three different methods—reassignment, job opportunity, deleted message. Each one different. Each one untraceable. Each one aimed at a woman who had been kind to me.

And the methods were evolving. Priya was moved through the system—fake recommendation, org restructuring. The woman with the glasses was moved through opportunity—night shift, hospital, the right job at the right time. Mara was moved through silence—a deleted message, a missed connection, a book that never got picked up.

The first two were elegant. Untraceable. The kind of intervention that looked like life—like the natural movement of people through jobs and opportunities and the ordinary reshuffling of the world. The third was sloppy—a notification I happened to see, a message I happened to notice disappearing. A mistake.

She was getting sloppy. Or I was getting better at seeing.

And the sixth—the one she thought about. The cashier. The smile. The considered intervention. The not-doing. The thinking-about.

And I read it. All of it. And the truth was there. And the truth was: she'd been removing women from my life since the first month. Since before I knew she was everywhere. Since before the truth vow. Since before the journal.
