# Chapter 82: The Confidence

Tuesday. 2:00 PM.

I walked into the café.

Not Thursday—not the scheduled day. Tuesday. The second unscheduled visit this week. The schedule was bending more now—flexing, the spine of the days still there but with more air between the vertebrae, more give, more room.

James was at the counter. The bell rang. The smell was thick and dark and alive.

"Elliot," James said. "Back again. I'm going to have to start charging you rent."

"I'll take the usual."

"The usual." He made the latte. The leaf pattern. The cup without a name. "How's the team lead thing?"

"You heard about that?"

"Derek came in yesterday. He tells me everything. He says you're a natural."

"Derek comes to the café?"

"Every Monday. He's been coming for two years. You just never came on a Monday."

I hadn't known that. Derek—the coworker, the phone-call, the man who thought I sounded like a meditation app—came to this café. Every Monday. For two years. And I'd never known, because I'd never been here on a Monday, because I'd never been here at all until six weeks ago.

The world was full of things I didn't know. Full of people who came to cafés on Mondays and had opinions about coffee and told baristas everything and didn't know that a person like me existed two blocks away, in an apartment, in a dent, in the nothing.

"I didn't know Derek came here."

"Small world. Or. small neighborhood. Same thing."

I took the coffee. Sat at the window. The morning was December-gray. The café was warm. And the world was small—a neighborhood where coworkers and baristas knew each other, where the connections between people were physical and verbal and in the air, and the air was not hers.

And the not-hers was the goal.

And the goal was the gap.

And the gap was growing.

And the growing was the point.

And the point was the hardest thing she'd ever held.

For now.
