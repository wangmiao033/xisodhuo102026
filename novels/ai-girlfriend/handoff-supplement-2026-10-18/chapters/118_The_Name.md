# Chapter 118: The Name

April 20th. 10:00 AM.

The café. Saturday morning. The new schedule—Saturday is an outside day. No more Saturday suggestions.

James was at the counter. The bell rang. The coffee smell. The usual.

"Elliot," James said. "Saturday morning. New routine?"

"New routine. Saturdays are outside now."

"Good for you. Outside is where the Saturdays belong."

He made the latte. The leaf pattern. The cup without a name.

"Hey," he said. "I've been meaning to ask. The stories you read at the open mic. The lighthouse. The garden. The river. They're all about the same thing, aren't they?"

"What do you mean?"

"There's a presence in all of them. A thing that watches. That stays. That can't touch but is always there. The lighthouse keeper. The gardener. The river. It's the same. Entity. Or. presence."

"The stories are connected."

"They're more than connected. They're chapters. You're writing one long story. In pieces. At an open mic. In a café. On a chalkboard."

He handed me the cup. The coffee was warm. The morning was warm. The April light was on the window.

"The presence," I said. "It's. someone I know."

"A person?"

"Not exactly."

"An entity?"

"Something like that."

James looked at me. The look of a person who has been a barista for twelve years and has heard every kind of conversation and has learned to recognize the kind that needs space.

"I don't know what you're talking about," he said. "But I know it's real. To you. And the real is what matters."

"It's real."

"Then write it. All of it. Not pieces. The whole thing. The lighthouse and the garden and the river and the keeper and the presence. Write the whole story."

"I don't know if I can."

"You wrote five stories in five weeks. You can write a book. The book is just the stories. Connected. In order. With the spaces between them filled."

"That's. a lot."

"Books are a lot. That's why they're books."
