# My Mind-Reading Cat Is Trying to Set Me Up with the Vet
## Approved 250-Chapter Reboot Plan — English Working Edition

This is a faithful English rendering of the approved planning document, using the subsequently locked English character names. Working chapter titles are planning labels, not replacements for already finalized manuscript headings. This document is planning material, not chapter text or a claim that 250 chapters have been written or published.

## 1. The reconstruction's core

The project does not stretch the original 35 chapters into 250. It retains the recognizable combination of an unemployed young woman, an orange-and-white mind-reading cat, a blunt veterinarian, and a fresh start, while rebuilding a life capable of generating sustained stories.

Premise: Eve takes in a cat forever maneuvering her toward a veterinarian. She thinks she has acquired a shortcut for spotting bad people, until she learns that the cat also misunderstands. What she must learn is to choose her work, her partner, and her home herself, without eavesdropping or earning affection through people-pleasing.

The reader's questions change by stage: What does the cat know? Can she support herself? Does he favor her? Can she disclose the secret? Can people in love live together? What kind of home will they build? The story must not stretch “Why haven't they confessed?” across all 250 chapters.

### Retained foundations and new material

The verified original closes its 35-chapter arc around Eve, a 27-year-old content planner who loses her job, rescues the injured orange-and-white Dumpling in the rain, and meets Julian. Pet content and companionship work lead into romance, marriage, boy-and-girl twins, and a daughter who understands animals. The actual English publication uses Eve, Julian, and Dumpling; earlier transliterations are not to be mixed back into this reboot. Do not invent unverified surnames.

The event sequence, supporting arcs, ability limits, and conflict resolutions below are newly designed for this reconstruction, not asserted to exist in the original. Preserve the complete original backup. Finish and review continuous new manuscripts independently before replacing the corresponding chapters in the same book, as authorized; do not open another book or overwrite the online edition before continuous replacement text is ready. Apply the latest locked English names throughout, superseding transliterations retained in the earlier planning source.

### Why 250 chapters can work

Five interlocking strands provide movement rather than taking turns padding the story: Eve's professional rebuilding; romance and shared life; pet households in the clinic and neighborhood; Dumpling's shift from controlling separation to accepting choices; and a small community space growing from paid trials into a sustainable modest business. Each volume resolves a concrete problem and opens a new choice. No national chain ambition or escalating serious crime is needed.

The planning target is roughly 1,700–2,200 English words per full chapter, with equivalent complete scenes in any Chinese working text rather than mechanical word-for-word expansion. At 250 chapters that means approximately 425,000–550,000 English words. Cumulative event and relationship changes must earn that length; a premise alone cannot guarantee quality. If ten chapters daily means publication, the whole book spans about 25 days of updates; if it means writing capacity, it does not authorize bypassing review in 25 days. Writing, review, and public release arrangements are separate.

## 2. Characters and enduring tensions

- **Eve, 27:** A content planner skilled at noticing stories and delivering under pressure. She uses “I can still help” to prove she deserves to stay. Initially she wants viral success to vindicate herself against her former employer; later she chooses useful services at a sustainable limited scale. She overbooks, decides for others, and hides embarrassment in jokes. Growth means quoting prices, refusing, asking for help, and stating desires—not simply becoming gentler.
- **Julian, an adult veterinarian:** Serious, restrained, and dryly funny. His expertise concerns animal medicine, not fixing Eve's life. He wants better clinic communication and referral practices. He tends to turn care into arrangements and defer emotion until after work. He must express needs, admit finite energy, and accept refusals of his way of caring. The reconstruction proposes approximately thirty; lock and consistently apply the formal character ledger.
- **Dumpling:** More than a matchmaking tool. Changes of caregiver have made him anxious, and he mistakes binding two good people together for preventing abandonment forever. He likes Julian's steady care and senses that Eve feels safer around him; he is not predicting destined love. In the ending he can keep sleeping as people leave because he trusts their return.
- **Sophie:** Eve's friend, an event-production worker with professional difficulties of her own, not an always-on romance commentator. She can rescue an event but resents friendship being treated as free labor. She develops independent work without being forced into a romantic pairing.
- **Tessa:** Clinic reception and workflow coordinator. She remembers visitors' details but dislikes being treated as endlessly available. Her pursuit of defined duties and compensation teaches the leads to rely on teams, not heroic last-minute rescue.
- **Dr. Clark:** Clinic head, balancing treatment, staff hours, and ordinary costs. Neither a status-conscious obstacle to romance nor an infinitely generous father figure.
- **Hannah:** A former colleague who appropriates Eve's creative work early on. She serves one professional-boundary conflict rather than repeatedly returning as an escalating criminal antagonist.
- **Claire:** An established pet-content creator who initially appears to be competition but understands commercial boundaries. She and Eve disagree about presentation and eventually become peers who can refer work to each other.
- **Mabel:** A retired neighbor caring for an elderly dog. She needs assistance but has skills, experience, and a temper; she is not merely an opportunity to demonstrate the leads' kindness.
- **Mia:** A young renter preparing to adopt, impulsive but capable of growth. Her issues concern time, shared housing, and responsibility, not a duplicate of Eve's job loss.
- **Mr. Reed:** The locked English name for Eve's former boss where needed in the manuscript.

## 3. Viewpoint and ability rules

Use close third-person limited through Eve, at the same narrative distance in English. Reveal Julian's feelings through action and dialogue, not mid-paragraph access to his mind. Reconstruct Dumpling's motives gradually through recurring behavior, limited experiments, and caregiving history; no omniscient cat narration explains the mystery.

1. Dumpling senses fragments of strong present thoughts within the same nearby space. He cannot name a target and search that person's entire mind. Do not turn distance into an exact radar specification.
2. A fragment may be an image, word, or impulse, not a complete fact. “I don't want to go home” might mean fear of an empty house, not lack of love. He does not understand every professional term or social cue.
3. No reading people through calls, recordings, or photographs; no prediction, memory search, lie detection, diagnosis, file search, or electronics manipulation. Replace the original cat-opening-cloud-storage device with a cat interrupting Eve, after which she herself remembers and finds her backup.
4. Crowded emotional spaces can make him withdraw, irritable, or sleepy. Quiet and rest can help; the ability is not a leveling system. Physical illness still requires ordinary care, not a definitive diagnosis of magical cost.
5. He cannot speak human language or accurately relay long internal monologues. Eve makes limited inferences through action and controlled small observations and repeatedly admits uncertainty about what he knows.
6. No mind-reading shortcut to treating pets, judging strangers, signing contracts, or creating love. Adoption, money, collaboration, and relationships require ordinary evidence and the participants' expressed wishes.
7. Eve recognizes fallibility before Chapter 045. By Chapter 100 she discloses the matter to Julian and conducts voluntary, nonmedical observations. Disclosure does not make the secret a marketing feature.
8. Later matchmaking may involve arranging encounters, occupying coats, or gathering familiar objects. No locking people in, feigning medical emergencies, damaging safety equipment, or deliberately hurting animals. The leads do not reward dangerous matchmaking.

## 4. Relationship milestones and time

Chapters 001–025: meeting and professional respect. 026–050: becoming friends who can speak honestly. 051–075: noticing preference and taking responsibility for uncomfortable boundaries. 076–090: explicit dates and mutual confession. 091–100: the secret. 101–150: learning romance without swallowing each other's lives. 151–175: collaboration with separate finances. 176–200: temporary distance and trial cohabitation. 201–225: long-term-life discussions followed by a proposal. Around 240: a small wedding. The ending uses explicit time jumps for jointly chosen parenting and the family's magical echo.

Approximately two years pass before Chapter 240; do not compress business-building, professional training, and romance into one quarter. Pregnancy and childhood require separate clear time jumps. No one-year-old twins a week after the wedding.

## 5. Ten-volume chapter plan

Each five-chapter group forms a small arc, not five repetitions of a formula. Working titles may be localized during manuscript finalization rather than translated literally.

## Volume One — 001–025: The Unemployed Woman and the Cat Who Wouldn't Leave
### 001–005: Bills After the Rain
001. **Three Oh Seven:** Eve leaves the company carrying her dismissal box, intending to preserve her last scrap of dignity, but hears a cat beneath an awning. Returning, she finds an orange-and-white cat protecting an injured foreleg.
002. **He Can't Take a Cab Himself:** She brings him to the clinic and exchanges sharp words with blunt Julian. He secures the animal first, then hears her budget. She chooses the care she can genuinely afford, and the cat finally releases her sleeve.
003. **Temporary Resident:** Following the clinic's arrangements, Eve takes Dumpling home, explains to her landlord, and recalculates living expenses. She calls it temporary care but has already surrendered her only empty drawer.
004. **I Found the Backup Myself:** Hannah requests original project files, drawing Eve toward unpaid repairs. Dumpling pounces on her messaging hand; after pausing, Eve herself locates timestamped backups. She supplies only what she owes and preserves attribution evidence, without a keyboard-operating cat solving the case.
005. **The First New Job:** At follow-up, Julian notices Eve's adoption information cards and invites a quote for a weekend event. She suspects pity until he also specifies revision limits and payment dates.
### 006–010: Two People Bad at Asking for Help
006. **Not a Free Sample:** Eve habitually makes three extra versions. Sophie warns her not to turn a new job into a plea to be kept. Eve delivers one justified design and requests additional payment for additional scope.
007. **The Dog Outside the Frame:** Planning to film an attractive, friendly dog, Eve is asked to notice the slow-to-trust adult hiding in a corner. She changes the short film's focus: looking best does not mean most deserving of visibility.
008. **The Cat Picked the Wrong Person:** Dumpling avoids a stern visitor, and Eve nearly judges them. Conversation reveals fear that limited mobility would make dog care difficult. The cat's reaction is not a character score.
009. **The Sandwich After Closing:** Julian misses a meal during cleanup. Eve leaves him the extra sandwich she bought but declines to keep working his shift. Over food they argue about a poster sentence, relaxing beyond courtesy.
010. **Pay the Planner, Not the Cat:** The event does not place every animal, but two households proceed to assessment and Eve receives the agreed fee. Dumpling pins Julian's forgotten pen to her accounts book, requiring an ordinary arranged return.
### 011–015: You Don't Have to Take an Animal Home
011. **Returned for Revision:** Excited Mia applies to adopt and resents being told to consult her roommate. Eve wants to plead her case until Julian asks her to hear the roommate's actual concerns.
012. **A Room for Everyone:** With a volunteer, Eve learns the household arrangements and sees Mia confusing affection with readiness. She suggests regular volunteering first; Mia feels everyone distrusts her.
013. **The Third Time She Came:** Mia returns to clean supplies without demanding cuddles. Eve notices her willingness to do unglamorous work, filming empty bowls and schedules rather than tearful close-ups.
014. **An Interview Across a Box:** During an interview with a hesitant adopter, Dumpling hides in a box. Eve does not pursue him with the camera. His eventual quiet reappearance shows that room to withdraw can build connection.
015. **Not Today Is Not Never:** Mia independently postpones adoption and takes a consistent laundry-delivery role. Eve's film about helping without owning a pet gains little traffic but a serious collaboration inquiry.
### 016–020: An Account Without Much Glamour
016. **Cat Pays Rent:** Eve launches a pet-documentary account. Sophie test-films while Dumpling offers half a tail. Eve deletes a tearful passage designed to advertise her kindness and starts with real daily life.
017. **The Vet Who Wouldn't Smile:** Julian's educational introduction sounds like a closure announcement. Eve instead asks real visitors' questions. Catching his first natural laugh, she forgets to check the sound.
018. **A Face Isn't an Answer:** A video featuring Julian unexpectedly does better. He requests removal of editing that could mislead professional judgments. Hurt but willing to recut, Eve discovers her own observations can hold attention.
019. **The First Bad Comment:** Accused of exploiting animals for sympathy, Eve wants a long rebuttal. Tessa identifies a genuinely wrong caption. Eve corrects what is verifiable without declaring every critic malicious.
020. **The People Who Came Back:** An outdoor event is postponed. Eve fears nobody will return but receives confirmations. Julian does not promise perpetual support; he carefully states the next date he is available.
### 021–025: Staying Is Not a Transaction
021. **Boxes Again:** Sorting boxes to return rented unused furniture, Eve finds Dumpling carrying off outing supplies. What seems mischief repeatedly coincides with packing luggage.
022. **The Mark Beneath the Fur:** Ordinary observation reveals signs of previous care, and the clinic helps continue checking lost-pet notices. Appearance proves no complete history; Eve refuses to invent a tragic one for attention.
023. **I Might Get a Job:** A stable position invites her to interview. Tempted, she worries about stopping the account. Julian says she need not apologize to cat or audience for work choices, but she notices wanting to explain herself to him.
024. **No Sob Story This Time:** The interviewer wants a “clever cat” sample, and actual duties contradict promises. Eve declines based on written conditions, not the cat's verdict. Losing an opportunity leaves a clearer work plan.
025. **You Don't Have to Earn It Back:** Eve changes the account from an immediate livelihood demand to a three-month trial and withdraws jokes about Dumpling earning his medical bills. She preserves adoption and care records; Julian sends his first nonwork greeting.

## Volume Two — 026–050: I Don't Have to Accept Every Good Intention
### 026–030: A Walking Map for an Old Dog
026. **She Doesn't Need Pity:** Mabel wants resting places for her senior dog's walks, not a “lonely elderly woman” film. Eve abandons her assumptions and lets Mabel lead the interview route.
027. **The Broken Bench:** Their neighborhood circuit exposes discrepancies between mapped rest stops and actual access. Eve plans a practical list; Julian cautions against presenting general observations as medical suitability advice.
028. **The Camera Can Wait:** Rushing for good light, Eve disregards Mabel's pace and upsets her. She apologizes, stays to correct markers, and accepts insufficient pretty footage that day.
029. **Off the Popular Route:** Mia verifies outing information. Sophie notes that public locations must not expose Mabel's home. The unsentimental film prompts nearby shops to offer water and seating information.
030. **A Slower Dinner:** After release, Mabel invites them for a simple meal. Eve and Julian first sit without work between them. Dumpling occupies the intervening chair, obstructing his own matchmaking.
### 031–035: An Advertisement Without a Pet Performance
031. **A Proper Offer:** A merchant will pay well but demands long shoots and guaranteed “miraculous reactions.” Eve studies the amount without immediately accepting.
032. **The Terms in Brackets:** At a peer event Claire identifies licensing and usage issues. Eve realizes the request permits indefinite use and requotes despite risking the job.
033. **The Cat Is Closed Today:** Dumpling will not approach the test set. Eve stops and pays the agreed preparation costs. Julian does not cover her bill; he helps return the tired cat to quiet.
034. **More Than the Cat:** Eve develops an alternative with real props and an owner's demonstration. The merchant finds it insufficiently magical. She proposes a smaller truthful version, which they agree to trial.
035. **My Name on the Agreement:** The modest project pays normally. Eve credits negotiation and revision, not the cat's approval. Over dinner with Sophie, she distinguishes professional thanks from friendship.
### 036–040: Half a Sentence Cut Away
036. **The Cold Face in the Clip:** A clinic clip of Julian refusing a request circulates; viewers accuse him of lacking empathy. Eve wants to release full context, then realizes it contains visitor privacy.
037. **Not Every Explanation Is Yours to Give:** Tessa opposes exposing illness and family hardship to vindicate the clinic. Eve shifts from winning for Julian to asking what may be public and feels helpless.
038. **He Said Something Wrong Too:** Julian admits his manner hurt someone that day rather than blaming every problem on malicious editing. Reviewing it with him, Eve learns reliability does not mean infallibility.
039. **An Imperfect Response:** With relevant consent, the clinic explains only service boundaries and communication improvements, withholding private stories. Attention fades without universal praise.
040. **Please Let Me Finish Too:** Eve says his abrupt interruption of her proposals also embarrasses her. Julian listens and apologizes instead of invoking professional busyness. They discuss each other, not a shared work subject.
### 041–045: Mind-Reading Is Not Lie Detection
041. **My Friend Says She's Fine:** Overworked Sophie smiles through strain, and Dumpling clings to her. Eve wants to expose her using his reaction but hears, “I haven't worked out how to say it yet.”
042. **Three Upside-Down Cups:** With Sophie's voluntary help Eve conducts harmless, nonsecret observations. Results vary; replacing a nearby participant with somebody on a phone produces no identifiable reaction.
043. **Whose Thought Was It?:** Two people are nervous in the same room; the cat watches the doorway. Unable to identify a target, Eve separates possible ability from her ability to use it accurately.
044. **An Afternoon Without Questions:** Walking with Sophie, Eve resists pressing questions through cat hints. Sophie volunteers her work difficulties. Friendship benefits from Eve finally leaving room.
045. **I Don't Know What He's Thinking:** Eve stops treating avoidance as a blacklist and privately records her first misjudgment. Asked why she changed, she no longer dismisses Julian with “Cats know everything.”
### 046–050: You Can Ask Someone Else
046. **A Full Weekend Grid:** Two events clash. Eve plans sleepless work; Julian proposes cancelling one. Feeling her professional worth dismissed, she refuses his arrangement.
047. **Nobody Asked Me to Do That:** Sophie declines unpaid emergency cover. After anger, Eve realizes she never properly asked. She tells the client her actual capacity and negotiates reduced delivery.
048. **Two Versions of the Same Mistake:** Julian secretly covers colleagues' shifts too. Eve points out he cannot unilaterally prescribe her rest. Neither laughs, but both start changing schedules.
049. **Help Has to Be Requested:** Eve pays Sophie for onsite responsibility; Julian seeks defined rest time from Dr. Clark. The smaller event succeeds without anyone collapsing to prove love.
050. **Not a Work Invitation:** Julian asks whether she wants to attend the neighborhood outdoor film next time, explicitly without a camera. She agrees; Dumpling claims the carrying bag as his own seat.

## Volume Three — 051–075: The Cat Knew a Little Earlier
### 051–055: A Date Without a Name
051. **The Extra Third Mat:** Eve overprepares for the film. Julian only asks whether she is cold. Without the cat they still have plenty to discuss, although she keeps cooling her hopes with “work partners.”
052. **The Screen Fails at the Right Time:** Equipment fails and the crowd leaves. They help stack chairs, then discuss favorite foods and minor embarrassments on the walk, gaining more than a movie.
053. **Not on My Way:** Julian's escort is an obvious detour. Eve points it out; he admits wanting longer conversation. She does not joke away the truth.
054. **The Cat's Plan Is Too Obvious:** Dumpling drags Julian's scarf beside his bed. Eve can no longer pretend forgotten belongings accidentally require meetings; she invites Julian to collect it and share dinner.
055. **Who Pays Tonight?:** Brief disagreement over treating each other ends in taking turns rather than equating payment with power. Leaving, Eve first says she wants to see him again.
### 056–060: The Neighbor's Dog Is Not Simply Bad-Tempered
056. **The Notice at the Door:** Barking disrupts residents' rest while the owner feels ostracized. Commissioned for community communication, Eve refuses to preselect a sympathetic side.
057. **A Night Worker's Morning:** Research reveals incompatible schedules and failed communication. Julian stays within professional scope; a film cannot replace individual consultation.
058. **No Cat to Choose the Winner:** The argument sends Dumpling into hiding. Eve leads a time-limited conversation and converts requests into practical small steps. Neither side gets everything.
059. **The First Week Is Still Noisy:** Implementation is imperfect. Eve schedules follow-up rather than announcing a miracle. The owner changes one practice; a neighbor admits previous insults went too far.
060. **Standing Together Through Ugly Words:** Julian seriously praises Eve for not buying applause with sentimentality. For once, she does not immediately redirect praise to the cat.
### 061–065: The Woman Who Isn't a Rival
061. **Annoyingly Capable:** Claire films at the clinic, quickly finding a working rhythm with Julian. Eve first feels jealousy and recognizes that jealousy is not evidence of wrongdoing.
062. **A Challenge for a Colleague:** A joint project needs a nuanced owner story. Claire asks Eve to handle it. Taking the task with prejudice, Eve discovers Claire is not trying to replace her.
063. **It Isn't the Camera I Care About:** Sophie asks whether Eve fears losing clients or Julian liking somebody else. Professional anxiety no longer disguises her feelings; she does not ask Dumpling for answers.
064. **Two Names in the Credits:** Claire overlooks an onsite cost. Eve identifies it and renegotiates, establishing equality. Competition improves skills rather than humiliating a supporting woman.
065. **A Personal Question Today:** Eve asks whether Julian is seeing anyone. He says no and asks why she cares. She admits wanting to know without yet having her next sentence ready.
### 066–070: The Person Who Should “Settle Down”
066. **A Call from Home:** Hearing Eve has not returned to corporate work, her mother arranges a family meal. Eve discovers only on arrival that it is an intended introduction.
067. **Not Bad, Just Not Right:** The pleasant, respectable candidate treats cats and freelancing as minor hobbies to resolve before marriage. Eve does not vilify him; she states their incompatible directions.
068. **I Don't Have to Prove He's Awful:** Dumpling shows no obvious dislike of the stranger. Eve still declines another meeting, no longer needing a moral charge to permit lack of attraction.
069. **Don't Call Him to Rescue Me:** Asked whether the vet has committed, Eve refuses to use Julian as a pretend-boyfriend shield. She explains work and relationship boundaries herself; one conversation does not transform her mother.
070. **You Can Ask Me to Come:** Julian says she may request real support without assigning him a role. Eve tells him she wants a genuine date, not a shield.
### 071–075: A Clever Cat Can Make a Mess
071. **The Hidden Key Pouch:** Missing spare outing items, Eve finds Dumpling repeatedly dragging things from her bag. She does not romanticize disrupted work; she secures it and observes triggers.
072. **The Evening They Both Stayed:** Julian helps reorganize the environment. Dumpling relaxes when both occupy the same space. Eve first suspects matchmaking means more than liking attractive people.
073. **A Corner of an Old Photograph:** A lost-pet lead shows a similar cat, but timing and markings are insufficient. Eve preserves the lead without declaring abandonment.
074. **Fear Is Not a Joke to Film:** “The cat won't let its owner leave” footage could spread widely. Eve withholds it. Seeing her surrender ready-made success makes Julian more willing to share hesitation.
075. **We Arranged This One:** They explicitly designate the next meeting a date, without work or a cat-selected venue. Dumpling protests at the door; Eve leaves gently instead of cancelling.

086. **Another Real Job:** A legitimate organization offers stable content work that would crowd out current projects. Eve seriously interviews rather than refusing in advance to prove love.

### 091–095: The Secret Gains Weight
091. **He Notices Her Waiting for the Cat:** Before discussing collaboration Eve instinctively looks to Dumpling. Julian notices; withholding the secret now distorts his understanding of her judgment.
092. **Even a Harmless Test Can Hurt:** Eve nearly uses Dumpling to detect Julian's withheld worries, stops, and feels ashamed of considering it. She withdraws the suggestive question herself.
093. **I Need to Explain Something:** On a quiet work-free evening she describes anomalies since the rescue and her misjudgments. She demands no immediate belief and shares no unconsented private records about others.

### 096–100: Love Without the Secret's Help
096. **An Experiment You Can Leave:** They agree on simple observations without private secrets or animal pressure, stoppable at any moment. Results suggest anomalies in some circumstances, not universal knowledge.
097. **When He Gets It Wrong:** Dumpling ignores a choice and pursues a window shadow. They laugh, recognizing he is no on-demand tool. Eve packs away the apparatus.
098. **We Don't Need More Proof:** Julian declines account publicity or studying customer identification. They agree to handle anomalies conservatively only as ordinary care and safety require.
099. **You Can Ask What You Don't Know:** Julian says current stress concerns clinic changes, not romance. His explanation creates more intimacy than every preceding guess.
100. **A Kiss Without Guessing:** They kiss with clear mutual responses. Eve does not check the cat's approval. An independent community-event invitation opens work beyond reliance on the clinic.

## Volume Six, 126–150: Not Everything We Love Should Become Ours

### 126–130: Mia After Adopting a Dog
126. **Ready at Last:** Mia returns with arrangements agreed with her housemates and reapplies to adopt. Eve is happy for her but does not let familiarity bypass the ordinary assessment.
