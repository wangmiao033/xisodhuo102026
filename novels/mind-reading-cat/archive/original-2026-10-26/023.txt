


Day One of Dating. Everybody Wants Updates.

Once you make it official, a lot of ordinary little things suddenly stop being ordinary.

Walking side by side.

Handing over water.

Saying wait for me.

Or, after a kiss, walking back into the shop to face a cat sitting behind the glass like he'd just watched a season finale and had notes.

The glass still held a faint smear from someone's earlier nose. Dumpling sat behind it anyway, round, orange-and-white, extremely present. Shop light caught on his whiskers. He did not blink first.

Eve stood in the doorway, ears still hot, mouth a little numb, and looked down into Dumpling's round eyes.

The night air on her neck felt colder than it should have. Her lips still remembered the pressure. Not dramatic. Just there. Like a stamp she had not authorized and would not be returning.

She felt, weirdly, guilty. Like she had been caught eating the last dumpling in a shared takeaway. The metaphor was too on-the-nose. She still felt it.

"When did you get back?"

He flicked his tail, slow.

The look was clear.

The minute before you two finally stopped stalling and kissed. Also: there is cat grass. I have already inspected it. You may resume being embarrassing.

Julian looked at woman and cat facing off. There was still a leftover smile on his mouth. He did not hide it. He had used up his hiding for the night.

His keys made a small sound when he set them down. The shop smelled like wood cleaner and leftover mixer treats. Ordinary. None of it felt ordinary.

"He seems pleased."

"Of course he's pleased," she muttered. "He's been managing us harder than a neighborhood committee auntie. I should put him on payroll. I won't. He would unionize."

Dumpling heard that, turned, very noble, and gave her his ass.

The tail stayed up. A flag. A signature.

"See? He admitted it. That's a signature."

Back inside, that delicate, overheated air still hadn't left.

The desk was the same desk. The sofa was the same sofa. Even the unfinished signup box was the same box. Eve still felt like nothing was the same, which was annoying, because she liked objects to stay objects. Objects that started meaning things became rent.

She touched the desk edge with two fingers. Cool. Solid. It did not care that she had a boyfriend. She cared enough for both of them.

Before, sitting here, she thought about whether bookings were full, whether an event had worked, whether they could break even by month-end.

Now she sat here and a sentence popped up, uninvited, like a pop-up ad with good copy.

I have a boyfriend.

And the boyfriend is Julian.

The thought was so specifically sweet that just thinking it made her want to bury her face in a pillow and scream without sound. She did not have a pillow. She had a sofa. The sofa was a witness. She sat on it anyway, carefully, like the sofa might report her to Tang.

He was steadier. At least on the surface.

He sorted the sheets like always, turned off one of the little outdoor lights like always, checked Dumpling's food like always.

The kibble made that cheap plastic rattle. Dumpling trotted over, inspected, ate three bites like a person signing for a delivery they had already decided was late.

Doing all of it, the tips of Julian's ears stayed faintly red.

She wanted to laugh more and more. The laugh sat in her throat, waiting for a professional moment that was not going to arrive.

"Julian."

"Mm?"

"You're my boyfriend now."

His hands paused. A sheet corner bent. He looked up. A beat. Like he had heard the title and was checking it against the chart.

The shop fridge hummed. Somewhere outside, a scooter went by. Eve heard both, and also the stupid thud of her own pulse.

"Mm."

"Why are you so calm about it?"

"I'm not."

"Where aren't you?"

He looked at her for two seconds, then walked over, bent, and kissed her forehead, very lightly. No warning. No meeting minutes.

His mouth was warm. The kiss lasted maybe a second. It still rearranged the furniture in her head.

"Here."

She sat on the sofa and cooked, instantly. Face, ears, the back of her neck. She opened her mouth. Nothing useful came out. She closed it. She looked at Dumpling, which was a mistake, because Dumpling looked back like she had failed a practical exam.

He stood at the top of the cat tree, looking down, feelings complicated.

Adults in love were, in fact, worse than a cat doing 3 a.m. parkour.

Parkour at least had a route.

This was just two people standing in a shop, calling each other new names, and then going quiet about the food bowl. He jumped down, ate, and did not offer commentary. Commentary would have been wasted. They were not listening. They were busy being a later that had finally shown up.

Eve watched him eat and thought, absurdly, that she should thank the cat. She did not. He would have taken it as a raise.

***

The next morning, of course, it exploded.

Su Li first.

A full sixty-second voice note. The thesis was one sentence.

How far did you two get last night.

Eve, brain softened by dating, sent four words.

We're together.

She stared at the two words after they left, like they might bounce back and demand a revision. They did not. They sat in the chat looking extremely true.

The call came immediately.

Su Li's scream nearly took out an eardrum. Eve held the phone away, then closer, because she was a bad person and wanted to hear it again.

The blankets smelled like detergent. Morning light hit the curtain. Eve's face was already hot before she said anything.

"AAAAA Eve you're finally not a single dog!"

"Calm down."

"I cannot calm down! Do you know I've been waiting for this like a web novel update! I had a betting pool in my head! I was the only participant! I still won!"

Eve laughed until she couldn't, rolling in the blankets with the phone. The blankets were hot. She was hot. She told Su Li nothing about the plant stand, then told her about the plant stand, then regretted it, then didn't.

"Three pots?" Su Li said, already delighted.

"Don't start."

Su Li asked if they kissed. Eve said yes. Su Li asked how it was. Eve said light, and then, because she was a traitor to her own dignity, said she had held his shirt. Su Li screamed again. The neighbor's dog barked, on theme.

Eve put her face in the pillow and made a sound that was not language. Dumpling was not here to judge her. She still felt judged.

On the other side, the clinic people had not been idle.

The second she and Julian walked into the shop, Tang was at the desk with a coffee, eyes like two hundred-watt bulbs. The coffee was a prop. The eyes were the product.

The door chime did its usual cheap ding. Eve wanted to unplug it.

"Morning, Eve."

"Morning."

"Morning, Julian."

"Morning."

"Last night, did you two..."

"No."

"I didn't finish."

"Still no."

Tang, wronged, set the coffee down like evidence. "I only wanted to ask if closing went smoothly."

Eve looked at her. Then at the coffee. Then at Dumpling, who was already on the counter, unbothered, a cat with no concept of HR.

Dumpling licked a paw. Slow. Like he had watched the whole thing and filed it under: humans, loud, again.

She did not answer. Answering would feed it. Not answering also fed it. This was a losing game. She had played it before, with Director Chen, and lost.

Director Chen passed behind them, delighted, thermos swinging like a pendulum of fate.

The thermos lid clicked. He did not need to speak yet. The click was already gossip.

"You don't need to ask if it went smoothly. Look at their faces. One glowing, one extremely pleased with the weather. The weather is ordinary. That one is not."

She wanted to argue.

Then she looked up and saw Julian putting breakfast by her seat, the motion so natural it was criminal.

Rice ball, soy milk, a little custard bun.

The paper bag made a soft rustle. Warm smell. Sweet.

Director Chen's eyes got even more meaningful. He did not say daughter-in-law. He thought it loud enough.

"See? What is there to ask. The bun is a statement. I have been married. I know a statement bun."

Her ears went hot. She turned to glare at Julian.

He was very calm. The ears, faintly red. The mouth, not helping.

"Don't you like this bun?"

"I do, but when you do it like this we look like..."

"Like what?"

"Like we just got together."

He looked at her. A shallow smile in his eyes. Not a big one. The kind that meant he had heard the complaint and was going to agree with the facts.

"We did."

She shut up.

Fine.

This man, once he was dating, could also just hit the ball straight.

Extremely rude.

Extremely effective.

She picked up the bun, because the bun had done nothing wrong, and because if she didn't occupy her mouth she was going to say something worse, like thank you, or come here, or don't look at me like that in front of the thermos. The bun was good. Of course it was good. He would not have bought a bad bun and then said we did. She ate it anyway, like a person who had a boyfriend and also a shop, and was going to need both kinds of energy today.

The custard was still a little warm. She hated how that felt like a detail she would remember later.

Dumpling leaned over and sniffed the soy milk, then sat back, disappointed.

Human breakfast.

No fish.

He had walked home last night, unsupervised, through a door that was supposed to be closed, to watch two people become a couple.

And this was the reward.

He closed his eyes on the counter, tail hanging off the edge, and decided the shop could run itself for ten minutes.

If they needed him, they could book a session.

Chief of Hospitality was off the clock.
