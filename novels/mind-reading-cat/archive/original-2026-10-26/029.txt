





Eve didn't say it out loud, but she'd had a fuzzy feeling for a while.

Julian had been off lately.

Not distant-off, or weird-off.

The kind where he was clearly hiding something, and trying very hard to look like he wasn't. Which, on him, was almost funny. He was a bad liar in a quiet way. He didn't add extra words. He subtracted them, and the empty space was loud.

Like the way he'd started taking calls with his back turned.

Not really hiding.

Just stepping outside, voice down, two sentences, then back. Once she heard "Saturday" and "don't." Once she heard him say "the cat too," which made no sense unless the cat was on a guest list.

Or, a couple days ago, out of nowhere:

"Do you like really loud occasions?"

She'd been measuring a corgi's waist, didn't look up. The tape slipped. The corgi looked proud of itself.

"Depends. Banquets yes. Lines no."

He went quiet long enough that she did look up. His face had the blank of a man whose survey question had failed.

"Why are you suddenly asking?"

"No reason."

She narrowed her eyes. "Are you planning something?"

"No."

"When you say no, your eyes go down and left."

Two seconds of silence. He changed the subject, very steady.

"Did you finish the corgi's waist?"

She was more suspicious. The corgi's waist was not an emergency.

The thing about her: her brain was usually fast. When the big thing was about her, she went a little dull. Maybe he was just busy. Maybe a new project at the shop. Maybe Director Chen had put him on some clinic thing and he didn't want to dump it on her.

Then Su Li showed up, mysterious. Nice clothes. The kind of "casual" that had been chosen in front of a mirror.

"Are you free Saturday night?"

"Yeah, why?"

"Eat with me."

"Since when do you invite me to dinner this formally?"

"Just say if you're going."

Eve stared at her for three seconds, and the lightbulb went on. Not a cute lightbulb. The ugly fluorescent one in the hallway of her brain.

"No."

"No what?"

"You usually invite me like you're collecting a debt." She narrowed her eyes, slow. "Today you look like you're covering for someone."

Su Li almost choked on her own spit.

"Why do you sound more and more like Dumpling?"

"So there is something."

Su Li, stubborn: "No. I just suddenly wanted to take you somewhere nice."

Eve looked at that obviously guilty face, and the fuzzy feeling snapped into focus.

She wanted to laugh, and also a little bit cry, which was rude of her own body. She was not going to cry in the shop over a maybe.

"Su Li."

"Mm?"

"Your mouth is about to reach your temple."

Su Li covered her face and surrendered.

"Fine. I don't know anything."

Eve laughed.

Alright.

Now she was sure.

Sure or not, Saturday still wrecked her.

She stood in front of the closet for half an hour, more serious than her first interview. Tried on a shirt, took it off, put it back, hated herself. White dress: too award ceremony. Red dress: too wedding. She did not need to walk into a maybe-proposal looking like she'd pre-booked the role.

She went with a simple mist-blue one, hair loosely up, a little pearl clip by her ear. Lipstick, then wiped half of it off because her hand was shaking and she looked like she was going to a karaoke bar.

Su Li picked her up, looked at her, and clicked her tongue.

"Good."

"What's good?"

"Good for getting proposed to."

Eve stared at her. "You're still pretending?"

"I'm not pretending." Dead serious. "I'm stating an upcoming objective fact."

The car stopped at a little restaurant with a terrace by the river.

The second she got out, her pulse started misbehaving. Not movie-misbehaving. The annoying kind, in her throat, like she'd run up stairs.

Not because the place was that romantic.

Because she saw people she knew.

Tang was squatting behind a planter by the road, peeking like a criminal. Phone out. Absolutely not undercover.

Director Chen stood not far off, pretending to study a money tree. The money tree had done nothing to deserve this.

Worse: Dumpling was there.

A light blue bow at his neck, held by one of the front-desk girls, radiating I was forced to attend an important event, and I still have presence. He looked at Eve like she'd personally signed the bow contract.

Eve stood there and fully laughed. The nervous thing in her throat loosened, a little.

"This setup. Is it a proposal or a variety show?"

Tang got caught, gave up hiding, and trotted over, grinning.

"Eve, this is the bride's side."

"Who's your bride."

"You." Director Chen, thermos in hand, benevolent. "Julian doesn't talk much. At the key moment, we still have to hold the field for him."

The nerves actually eased, being roasted by this group. Of course they were here. Of course Dumpling had a bow. Of course the money tree was an extra.

She breathed in, lifted her skirt a little, and walked toward the terrace.

River wind. Night lights on the water, broken into a handful of gentle stars. She told herself not to describe it later like a caption. It was still pretty. Fine. She could admit pretty.

The terrace wasn't big. The setup wasn't extra.

No carpet of roses. No big screen looping a love montage.

Just a few warm lamps, a few plants, a long table by the water.

On it: mint, cat grass, and a pothos that had been kept very much alive.

She saw it and laughed.

Very Julian.

Not loud. Not showy. Every detail still hit. He'd picked plants that wouldn't die in a week. Of course he had.

And he was standing by the table in a simple white shirt and dark pants, a little ring box in his hands.

Her jokes lined up and then left. She walked closer anyway, because standing at the edge looking at him was worse.

He looked at her. The nerves in his eyes were obvious too. That helped. If he'd been perfectly smooth she might have walked into the river.

"You came."

"Mm."

"Cold?"

"No."

"Nervous?"

"A little."

"Me too."

That one made her laugh.

"You get nervous?"

"I do." He looked at her. "Because it's you."

Her eyes filled. Great. It hadn't even started, and she was already being soothed into crying. She blinked hard. The pearl clip pinched. Good. Pain. Stay here.

He let out a small breath, set the box on the table. Not loud. As serious as always.

"Eve, I had a lot of lines prepared."

"Then I thought about it. The important ones, I've already said a lot of times."

"I like you. I want to take care of you. I want to run the shop with you. I want those long days after this, with you."

Wind lifted a bit of hair by her ear. She tucked it back, missed, tucked it again.

He looked at her. Didn't dress it up.

"I used to think a lot of things in life should go in order. Work, plans, the future. Think it through, then walk."

"After I was with you, I found some decisions don't get clearer the more you calculate."

"It's you, every day, next to me. So I keep getting more sure."

He opened the box.

A simple diamond. Clean lines. Quiet in exactly the right amount. Not a billboard. A ring you could actually wear while washing a cat bowl.

The tears went immediately. She hated that. She also couldn't stop it. Her mascara was going to be a crime.

"Julian."

"Mm."

"Don't make me cry before you start."

He smiled, helpless.

"Then I'll go faster."

He went down on one knee.

Her heart went completely off-script. She thought, very clearly, don't lock your knees. Then she thought, that's a stupid thought. Then she thought, if I say no as a joke I will die.

Tang, not far off, had both hands over her mouth. Director Chen was rarely quiet, thermos in his arms, looking pleased. Even Dumpling had been set on a little stool by the table, head up, serious as a board member in attendance. The bow had slipped a little. He did not fix it.

"Eve." He looked up at her, every word clear. "Will you marry me?"

"Not because we're a certain age, not because the shop is stable, not because it happens to fit."

"Because I want the person standing next to me, in every ordinary loud day after this, to be you."

This time she actually cried, no dignity left.

Crying and laughing, nodding like a chicken pecking rice. She could hear herself. She sounded unhinged. Fine.

"Yes."

"I will."

The tightness he'd been holding finally loosened. He stood, and put the ring on her finger.

The size was exact. Of course it was. He'd probably measured while she was asleep, like a criminal.

She looked down at it, hadn't even started feeling feelings, when Dumpling meowed.

Not loud.

Extremely present.

Everybody paused, then laughed.

Tang immediately: "Professor Dumpling says the match is approved!"

Director Chen clapped. "Excellent. The clinic cat-ethics committee formally signs off."

Eve was laughing and crying, the whole of her almost unscrewed. She had snot. She had a ring. She had a cat in a bow. This was her life.

Julian wiped her face, and kissed her forehead.

"Don't cry."

"You proposed in a way that's illegal."

"Then is it a yes?"

She held up the ring. It caught the lamp. She wanted to hide it and also show everyone.

"It's already on. A little late to ask, no?"

He finally laughed out loud.

The river wind that night was good. The lights were good. Everybody was laughing. Tang took too many photos. Director Chen poured tea like it was a toast. Dumpling tried to bite the bow, got stopped, sat in offended silence.

Later, in the car, she looked at her hand on her knee and thought: okay. That happened.

Not because a later had been promised in a pretty sentence.

Because there was someone she loved, and friends hiding behind a planter, and a cat in a stolen-looking bow, and a ring that fit, and she had said yes with mascara down her face.

She texted Su Li from the passenger seat: you are a terrible liar.

Su Li sent back a string of screaming stickers, and then: I KNOW NOTHING.

Eve put the phone down, looked at Julian's profile, and laughed again, quiet, because if she didn't laugh she was going to restart the crying, and the night had already used up her quota.
