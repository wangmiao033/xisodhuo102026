After the first sharing session, the air at the clinic went weird.

More precisely: the way people looked at Eve and Julian went weird.

Tang used to look at them like "Eve is so good" and "Julian is so solid." Useful looks. Work looks.

Now it was "oh my god," "are they going to kiss," "why haven't they kissed." Tang did not say these out loud, which somehow made it worse. You could hear them anyway. They lived in the way she mashed the calculator. The calculator had not asked to be involved.

Director Chen was worse.

One day Eve was editing the schedule at the desk, clicking between tabs like the tabs had personally offended her. He walked by, looked at her, looked at Julian coming out of an exam room, then sighed like a philosopher who had just understood the universe and wanted everyone else to suffer for it.

"Young people starting a business together. They've got that married look."

Her hand jumped. She almost turned the roster into a divorce filing. One extra click and Monday would have belonged to nobody.

"Director, could you be a little more precise with language?"

"I am precise." Completely confident. Thermos in hand, like a man delivering a diagnosis. "Look at you two. One treats. One makes money. Perfect match."

Julian walked up in time to hear the last two words, and his step caught. Not a stumble. Just a small, traitor pause, like his foot had read the room faster than his face.

Eve's ears went nuclear. She faked calm, because if she didn't fake calm she was going to argue with a man holding a thermos, and that was not a fight she could win on paper. "By that logic I also match with accounting. We both look at money. Accounting doesn't bring me leftover fruit, but the principle stands."

Director Chen looked heartbroken. "This child. You don't understand an old man's hints."

She stared at him. Opened her mouth. Decided the mouth was not going to help. Tang, from the computer, mashed a zero so hard the display complained. Eve did not look. Looking would have been asking for a subtitle.

She'd figured it out.

Besides actual work, the clinic's biggest hobby lately was watching her and Julian like a series. Not even a good series. A slow one. The kind where people in the comments typed "JUST KISS" and then kept watching anyway.

And she couldn't even fully deny it.

Because even she thought something was off.

Like how Julian now saved food for her, naturally. No announcement. No "I thought of you." Just food, existing, as if the break room had grown a habit.

If she missed lunch, there'd be a still-warm box in the break room, sticky note on it.

Eat first.

Not many words. Handwriting she'd know anywhere. The kind of handwriting that made even "eat first" look like a medical instruction, which, knowing him, it probably was.

The rice would still be a little hot. The vegetables a little too neatly divided. The box always had a lid that actually closed. She noticed that, because she was a person who noticed lids now, apparently. She would eat standing up, like sitting would make it a date, and then spend the rest of the afternoon tasting ginger and overthinking chopsticks. She told herself ginger was not a love language. Ginger did not argue. Ginger just sat there being correct.

Or when her shoulders ached from editing, he'd stand behind her chair for two seconds, like he was just passing by, then press the base of her neck.

Light. Short.

Short enough that she didn't have time to panic before he walked off, innocent. Innocent like a man who had not just put his thumb on a problem and then left her sitting there, blinking at a spreadsheet, thinking about rent so she wouldn't think about his hands.

That innocence was the lethal part.

Su Li heard it and slapped her thigh. The sofa took it personally.

"You know what that's called?"

"What?"

"Adult-coded subtle seduction."

Eve nearly died on soy milk. A dignified death. A breakfast death.

"Stop reading garbage."

"I read life." Su Li looked ancient and wise, which was unfair on a person eating chips. "Trust me. If a man only does this to you, he's a hundred percent interested. If he did it to everybody, Tang would already be on a stretcher."

Eve, surface-calm: "Oh."

Inside, a loop: a hundred percent interested. A hundred percent. Like a score. Like a coupon that might expire if she looked at it too hard. Eve went home repeating hundred percent like a scam call she had answered on purpose. Dumpling met her at the door, sniffed her shoes, and walked away, which felt like a review.

She tried to argue the food into being professional. Professional people also ate. Professional people also had necks. The argument collapsed immediately, because professional people did not leave notes that looked like prescriptions for rice.

That night she couldn't sleep. She got up for water. The cup was already by the sink. She still stood there for a minute, as if water needed supervising. The fridge hummed. A scooter went by downstairs, too fast, the way scooters did when the rider thought the whole street was a suggestion.

Dumpling was on the windowsill, staring outside. Night, streetlight, a motorbike going by too fast. Cat TV. He did not offer her a seat on the sill. The sill was his. She was the one with the romance brain. She could stand.

"You're up too?"

He looked back. Complicated.

Like he was condemning a woman whose romance brain had ruined his sleep schedule, and also like he had known this was coming and had still hoped to be wrong.

She sat with her cup, muttering. "What does Julian actually mean?"

Dumpling ignored her. Professional.

She kept going anyway. The cup was warm. The question was not. "If he just sees me as a partner, he doesn't have to take care of me like this. Partners split invoices. They don't press your neck like they're resetting a router. If he actually likes me, why won't he say it? Is there a form? Do vets need a form?"

He flicked his tail.

"Meow."

She narrowed her eyes. "Are you insulting me?"

"Meow."

"Say it again. I dare you."

He hopped down, strolled to her foot, hooked her pant leg. Not cute. Administrative. Then he walked to her phone and slapped the screen on, one paw, no hesitation, like he'd been waiting for the notification more than she had.

A new lock-screen message.

Julian.

Free tomorrow morning? I want to show you a place.

Her throat did a small stupid jump. She read it three times before replying, because one time was for the words, two times was for "our," which wasn't even in this message yet, and three times was because her brain had become a scam.

What place?

A small storefront that just opened up nearby.

I think it could be our first shop.

She stared at our first shop, and her mouth lifted, out of her control. She tried to press it down. It did not go down. She thought about the deposit. The deposit was not cute. The deposit still somehow felt cute, which was a medical symptom, probably. Our. Two letters. She had written worse copy for a living and never gotten this stuck on a pronoun.

The phone screen lit her fingers. She typed a joke, deleted it. Typed "sounds good," deleted it because it sounded like a meeting. Dumpling watched the deletions like a person watching someone fail a simple exam. She put the phone face down, then picked it up, then put it down again, like the screen might change its mind if she was rude. It did not. Our first shop stayed. The room felt too awake. The fridge hummed like it was in on it.

Dumpling jumped onto the sofa, found the best spot — her best spot, historically — and swept his tail against her hand before closing his eyes.

Meaning obvious.

Stop grinning like an idiot. Reply. I have already done the hard part, which was waking you.

She cleared her throat and typed, fake-calm, like she was confirming a meeting with a vendor and not walking toward a word that had "our" in it.

I'm free.

She hadn't even put the phone down.

Sleep early.

She stared at the two words like they might be a trick. Sleep early. From a man who had just said our first shop. As if the correct medical advice after a pronoun was bedtime. She wanted to screenshot it for Su Li. She did not. Su Li would say adult-coded again, and Eve would have to live.

She laughed out loud. Short. Embarrassing. The kind of laugh you do when you're alone and still look around, in case the furniture is judging you.

Okay.

She looked at Dumpling.

"Does this kind of count as a date?"

He didn't open his eyes.

She still felt like, if he could talk, he wouldn't bother with a thesis. He'd just say:

You asked for a calendar. He sent a time. Take the win and turn off the light.

She turned off the light.

The phone stayed face down on the table. Our first shop still sat in the dark, glowing nowhere, which did not stop her from seeing the words. She lay down anyway. Tomorrow she would look at a storefront like a person looking at rent, not like a person looking at a pronoun. Tonight she was allowed to be an idiot about two letters.

He snored, almost immediately, like a cat who had closed a ticket.
