


The next morning Eve stood in front of her closet for fifteen minutes, which was eight minutes longer than she stood there for job interviews, and she tried not to think about what that meant.

White shirt.

Too interview. Too "please take me seriously, I have slides."

Floral dress.

Too field trip. Too "I might buy a sausage on a stick."

She landed on a light apricot cardigan, high-waist jeans, hair loosely up. Not trying too hard. Also not like she'd rolled out of bed and lost a fight with a pillow. A respectable middle. The kind of outfit that could survive being seen by a landlady.

Su Li's review of the selfie was high, and also illegal.

"Good. Female lead who starts a business and accidentally starts dating. Do not change the hair. The hair is doing plot."

Eve ignored her. She was about to leave when Dumpling climbed into the carrier himself, no invitation, no shame, one shoulder, then the other, like boarding a train he had already paid for.

She stared. "You're coming too?"

He looked up, firm.

Meaning: if I don't, you two will take forever, and I have a nap this afternoon.

So the shop visit became one man, one woman, one cat. A very normal inspection party. Extremely normal. Nobody needed to comment.

The place was two streets from the clinic. Not big, street-facing, a florist and a cafe beside it, a community park across the road. Kids' voices leaked over in pieces. When the shutter went up, sunlight cut in. Dust floated on the floor, lazy, like it had been waiting for an audience.

Eve's eyes lit the second she walked in. She hated that they did that. She still walked in further.

"This is actually really good."

"Mm." He stood beside her, hands in his pockets, looking at the corners first, the way a vet looked at a room: exits, light, what would be easy to clean. "Not huge, but enough to test."

"Front for reception and small events, back for consults and a cat-and-dog settling area..."

She walked and gestured, getting more excited, voice brighter than usual. The empty shop made everything echo a little, so even her dumb ideas sounded official.

"Shelves by the window for basics. Corkboard on the wall for adoption info and guest notes. A workstation in the corner for Dumpling, or he'll feel left out, and then he'll punish us in public, and I cannot afford that brand damage."

Julian listened, a smile slowly showing, like it had been standing behind a door and decided the door could open.

"He needs a workstation?"

"Obviously." Completely righteous. "He's co-founder cat now. Co-founders get desks. That's capitalism. That's fairness. That's me not wanting to hear about it later."

Dumpling meowed, in agreement. From inside the carrier, even. He had opinions about real estate.

They finished the layout. The landlady left them to talk, which was a polite way of saying she had smelled a couple and did not want to interrupt the smelling.

The place went quiet.

Warm sun. The occasional bike bell outside. Empty inside, and somehow already imaginable: a kettle somewhere, a scratched chair, someone laughing too loud at a cat video. She could see the rent number too, hovering near the window like a ghost with a calculator. The ghost did not ruin it. The ghost just stood there, being honest.

Eve stood by the window and felt a kind of heartbeat she couldn't name, which annoyed her, because she liked naming things. Naming things was how you put them in a spreadsheet.

Not for the shop.

For the word later.

She turned to look at him.

He was crouching, teasing Dumpling through the carrier door with two fingers. The gray sweater went dull-gold in the light. The side of his face was clean. No hurry in him. He looked like a person who would wait for a kettle, and also wait for her to finish a sentence.

She looked away first, then looked back, because she was an adult, and adults could ask questions without making them sound like proposals.

If the future was opening this place with this person, being busy together, arguing about shelves, eating badly, closing up with the same set of keys...

That sounded like a very happy thing. It also sounded like a thing she wanted to win, which was a stupid way to think about a shop, and still accurate.

"Julian."

"Mm?"

"Why do you want to do this with me?"

She'd asked versions of this before.

This time the flavor was different. Less pitch. More: tell me if I'm standing in the wrong room.

He stood, looking at her across a strip of sun. Dust in it. Ordinary. Too ordinary to be this loud in her head.

"Because you're a good fit."

"That's it?"

"And because..." He paused, hunting for the word. Not decorating it. Finding it. "Working with you feels easy."

She didn't speak. Easy was a dangerous compliment. Easy meant he had noticed the hard parts and still called it that.

"You think things through, and you still put people in the picture." He watched her, not loud. "I got used to being on my own. A lot of ideas just stayed ideas. You pushed them one step forward."

Her ears went warm. She wanted to joke it off — that's just project management, Julian, I used to do this for people who didn't even say thanks — and the joke sat on her tongue and didn't jump. She thought about the deposit again, which was unromantic, and still somehow the most romantic number in the room.

She was about to answer when Dumpling shot out of the carrier and rolled, slowly, right between them. A full, committed roll. The kind of roll that said: I have seen enough sunlight on faces. I am a body. Use the floor.

The almost-something got flattened. Literally. There was now cat where a sentence had been trying to live.

Eve looked down. Then at Julian. Then down again, because looking at him was not helping.

Two seconds, then they both laughed. Hers came out first, ugly with relief. His was quieter, like he'd been holding it.

"Did he do that on purpose?"

"Probably."

Dumpling finished the roll and lay down, satisfied, belly up for exactly one second, then not, because dignity was a schedule.

Of course it was on purpose.

Important moments were not allowed to happen without him. He had climbed into a carrier for this. He was not unpaid labor.

After the visit, Julian took her to a nearby diner.

Small place. Plastic-covered menus. The owner lit up at Dumpling like Dumpling was a celebrity who had chosen this establishment personally, which, to be fair, he had.

"You two brought the cat to look at a house? Young couples these days. Smart. Let the cat pick. Cats know feng shui."

Eve nearly spat her water. She swallowed instead, eyes watering, which was not a dignified look for a potential tenant.

Julian handed her a napkin, face unchanged. "We're not a couple."

The owner, knowing, knowing, wiping a table that did not need wiping: "Not now. Later, though. I can tell. The cat already sits like a son."

Eve gave up on speech and studied the menu. The menu had too many items. She read "tomato egg" three times. Tomato egg did not become a safer sentence.

Dumpling sat on a chair, tail curling, looking calmer than both humans at the table. He accepted a small dish of water like a guest who had been here before.

After they ordered, her phone buzzed.

Julian had transferred her money.

Note: first event split.

She blinked. "Why are you suddenly sending me money?"

"You earned it."

"This is too much."

"It isn't." He said, "And later there should be more."

When he said later, it sounded natural.

Natural like they'd already agreed there would be a lot of laters. Natural like rent, and keys, and who bought the trash bags.

She looked at the transfer and had to blink again, harder. She'd made money before. Paychecks. Projects. Money that went into a hole called survival and never sent a receipt.

None of it had felt like this, every bit of it landing under her feet, like a road being paved one step at a time, and she was allowed to stand on it.

"Julian."

"Mm?"

"If you keep doing this, I'm going to get the wrong idea."

"What idea?"

"That you don't just want to start a business with me. You also want to..." She chickened out halfway, picked up a rib, hid behind sauce like sauce was a legal strategy. "Never mind. I didn't say that. The rib said that. I'm eating."

He watched her a moment. Then, low: "You can get the wrong idea."

She snapped her head up. Sauce on her thumb. Very CEO. Very founder.

He was already drinking soup like that blurry sentence had not come out of his mouth. Steam. Calm. Criminal.

Her ears burned. She looked at the window, then at Dumpling, then at the transfer still sitting on her phone like evidence.

Dumpling licked a paw, slow, and glanced at the kitchen, where something fried was making a serious sound.

Good soup. Good chair. The humans could keep tripping over later. He was occupied. There was chicken somewhere in this building, and unlike certain people, he knew how to want a thing out loud.

Eve put the phone away before she could screenshot the transfer and send it to Su Li with a string of unhinged dots. She could tell Su Li tomorrow. Tonight was ribs, soup, and a man who said later like it was already on the calendar.

"Eat," Julian said.

"I am eating."

"You're staring at the window."

"The window is part of my process."

He did not argue with process. He pushed the soup closer. Dumpling, hearing a bowl move, opened one eye, confirmed it was not his bowl, and went back to wanting chicken in silence.
