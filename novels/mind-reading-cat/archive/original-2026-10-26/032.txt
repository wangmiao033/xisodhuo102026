





Eve getting pregnant happened faster than anyone expected.

More precisely: on a very ordinary morning, she looked at soy milk he'd just heated, and a violent rejection rose up. Not a preference. A full-body no. The smell hit like someone had opened a wet sock factory next to her face.

The kitchen was the same kitchen. Same window. Same mug. Same man in a T-shirt, hair still smashed from sleep. That was what made it rude. Nothing about the scene had asked for a plot twist.

"No."

She covered her nose and stepped back. The mug was still steaming. Innocent mug. She wanted it gone. The smell followed her anyway, thick, sweet, wrong.

He looked up. "What?"

"This soy milk smells weird."

"It doesn't."

"It does."

"It doesn't."

He sniffed it, serious, like a man checking a patient. "It's the same brand."

"Then the brand turned on me."

Dumpling sat on the table, looked at one, then the other, and unhelpfully turned his head away. No comment. Not his department. His tail hung off the edge. He blinked once, slow, the way he did when humans were being loud about a smell he had already filed.

She counted days in her head, standing there in socks, and the whole of her went quiet. The calendar on the fridge was a cartoon cat. Very helpful. She stared at it until the numbers lined up in a way she did not like. One sock had a hole at the toe. She noticed that too, because her brain, in a crisis, liked to collect irrelevant evidence.

"Eve?"

"I'm going to the bathroom."

An hour later, two lines on the test, extremely clear.

She sat on the little bathroom stool, brain blank, like life had just hit restart. The test sat on the sink. She kept looking at it as if it might revise itself if she was rude enough. Water ticked in the pipe. Her hands didn't shake, which felt like a lie her body was telling.

It wasn't that she hadn't thought about kids.

Lately, seeing a cute baby, she'd even talk about it with him, offhand. Buy one get one. Daughter who looks like you. Spy notebook, dead in a drawer.

"Thought about" and "actually here" were not the same thing. Thought about was a joke you could still unsay. Two lines did not unsay.

She thought about calling Su Li, then thought Su Li would scream, then thought she was not ready to be a person who got screamed at before breakfast.

Holding the test, her first instinct was to call Dumpling.

"It's over."

He sat in the doorway, extremely calm.

The look said:

I knew.

Which was annoying. She wanted at least one creature in the house to be as unprepared as she was. He washed a paw. Not a full wash. A courtesy wash. As if this were a meeting he had already attended.

"You could act surprised."

He did not.

Julian knocked, a little urgent.

"Eve?"

The knock wasn't loud. It was the kind he used when he thought something might be wrong and he didn't want to make it worse by sounding like he thought something was wrong.

"You can come in."

The door opened. He saw what was in her hand immediately.

Neither of them talked for a second. The bathroom fan hummed like it had an opinion. Toothbrushes in the cup. His and hers. Morning light on the tiles, too honest.

She looked up, opened her mouth.

"I think..."

"Mm."

"We..."

"Mm."

"I'm pregnant."

He stood there, not moving, a full two seconds.

Then, very slowly, like he was afraid of startling something, he came over and crouched in front of her. Knees on the bath mat. White socks. Ridiculous. The mat had a cartoon fish on it. She had bought it as a joke. The joke was still there, under his knees, while her life changed shape.

"For real?"

"I wouldn't fake two lines at you."

He looked at her, looked at the test. That usual, very steady calm in his eyes cracked, for the first time, obviously. Not a movie crack. A small one. Like a chart with a spike he hadn't budgeted for. He reached out, stopped, then took the test as if it needed both hands.

"Do you feel bad right now?"

"Not yet."

"Dizzy?"

"I'm okay."

"Nauseous?"

"A little, just now."

"Then we go to the hospital. Now."

"Now?"

"Now."

She looked at him, the whole of him wound tight, and couldn't help laughing. He was already reaching for her jacket, which was in the other room, which did not stop the reaching. His ears were a little red.

"Julian. Calm down."

"I am calm."

"Your voice is tight."

He went quiet.

After a moment he lowered his head, and set his forehead against her knee.

The contact was warm. His hair was still morning-hair. They hadn't even brushed teeth like people with a plan. She could feel him breathing, too careful.

"I don't think I can be calm."

Her throat went tight. She rubbed his hair. It stuck up anyway.

"Then what do we do?"

"Let me sit with it a second."

He sat with it. She sat with him sitting with it. Dumpling, in the doorway, sat with both of them, which made three kinds of sitting, none of them useful and all of them necessary.

This time, he didn't come over to make it a scene.

Something careful in those round eyes, too.

Like he also knew: this house was about to get louder.

***

The first prenatal, Eve was pretty steady.

She filled forms. She joked with the nurse. She thought, if I act normal, maybe the universe will also act normal. The waiting room smelled like disinfectant and someone else's snacks. Julian sat beside her with the forms she had already finished, reading them again, as if a missed checkbox could change biology.

"I wrote my name," she said.

"I know."

"You're checking my name."

"I'm checking everything."

What actually stunned them was the doctor looking at the ultrasound, smiling, and saying:

"Two."

Eve stared. "Two what?"

"Twins." The doctor pointed at the screen. "We'll confirm later. Early look, boy-girl isn't a small chance."

Eve and Julian went quiet at the same time.

The screen was grainy. Two little blobs. Gray, flickering, rude in how small they were. She wanted to ask if one of them was a shadow. She did not ask, because the doctor did not look like a woman who joked about extra babies. The gel was cold on her skin. Julian's hand was on the back of her chair, not moving.

Three seconds later she turned, slowly.

"Husband."

"Mm?"

"Did you bless your own mouth?"

He was still in the shock. Slower than usual. He had the printout in both hands, like it might fly.

"What?"

"You said you wanted a daughter who looks like me." She pointed at the paper. "Great. Buy one get one, and it might actually be a boy and a girl."

This time he really had no words.

He stared at the ultrasound, ears going red, and finally smiled, very lightly.

"That's good."

"That's all you have? That's good?"

"What else?"

"You're supposed to be more excited."

"I am very excited."

She looked at him, carefully.

He was.

From the hospital to now, he'd been walking with the same arm and same leg. She almost pointed it out in the corridor. She saved it for the car, because some roasting needed a seatbelt.

In the car he sat for a second before starting the engine. Both hands on the wheel. The printout on her lap now, because she had taken it from him so he would drive like a person who remembered roads.

She finally laughed.

"Julian."

"Mm?"

"You don't look like a vet right now. You look like a first-time dad who just descended to earth."

The review got confirmed fast.

From that day, he officially entered Highly Anxious Expecting Father Mode.

She sneezed, he handed warm water.

She ate two bites less at dinner, he started researching nutrition. Tabs. Lists. A note on the fridge about iron that she immediately covered with a magnet. The magnet was Dumpling's face. She considered this diplomacy.

She turned over a little slow at night, and he could snap awake. Not a question. Just a hand, checking, like she might have rolled off the planet. The first time it happened she said, "I'm turning over, not leaving." The second time she didn't say it, because his eyes were already closing again, and the hand stayed, light, as if her shoulder were a monitor.

Su Li came to visit, watched for ten minutes, and landed a precise line.

She arrived with fruit, put the fruit down, watched Julian move a chair two centimeters closer to Eve, watched him refill a cup that was still full, and then nodded like a person completing a diagnosis.

"He doesn't look like your husband right now."

"Then what?"

"Like the attending physician you and the baby share."

Eve laughed until her stomach hurt. Then she had to stop, because laughing that hard now came with a warning label she was not ready to respect.

"Twins, though," Su Li said. "You really don't do things at a normal speed."

"We didn't order twins."

"That's what people say right before they buy two cribs."

Julian, from the kitchen: "We're not buying two cribs today."

"See," Su Li whispered. "Attending physician."

Dumpling sat beside the baby-supply list, looking at the table full of bottles, bibs, tiny socks, onesies, expression also complicated.

He was starting to realize he might no longer be the only one in this house who needed premium service.

When Eve put two palm-sized socks by his paw to compare, he still lowered his head, politely, and sniffed.

The socks were ridiculous. Too small to be real. One had a tiny cloud.

Then his tail swept, lightly, and circled both socks.

Like he was saving his seat in advance.

She took a photo. Did not post it. Some negotiations were internal.

Julian came back with water she had not asked for. She drank it anyway. Dumpling stayed on the list. The two little socks stayed inside the circle of his tail, as if the house had already begun making room, and the cat, being the oldest staff, had decided to inventory the new arrivals before they even had names.
