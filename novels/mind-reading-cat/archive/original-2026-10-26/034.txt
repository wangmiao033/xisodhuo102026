


Eve went into labor on a rain night.

It was hard not to think of the night she'd first found Dumpling.

Then, the rain had been a wall. Streetlights smeared. She had been a wreck, a hurt fat cat in her arms, sprinting into a pet hospital, talking too fast, promising things to a creature who could not sign a contract. She had thought, if I drop him, I will never forgive the weather.

Now the rain was a different rain, same city, same sound on the roof of the car, and Julian was carrying the hospital bag, and she was in so much pain she wanted to swear at him, and still had the bandwidth to correct him. The bag kept bumping his knee. She wanted to throw the bag. She needed the bag. Marriage was like that.

The street shone. She gripped the door handle and thought, very clearly, don't throw up in the car, then the next wave arrived and goals became optional.

"Don't walk that fast!"

"This is already slow."

"Your voice is shaking!"

"It isn't."

"You saying it isn't right now is exactly how you said it isn't when you were jealous!"

He shut his mouth. Rain on his hair. A look that said: I will not win this round, and I know it. Water ran down the side of his neck. The hospital bag was overpacked. She had watched him pack it three times. She had still added things. He had not removed them. That was also marriage.

She was sweating, and her roasting accuracy was still online. She was a little proud of that, in a stupid way, between the waves. If she could still roast, she was still in the room. The hallway smelled like disinfectant and rain dragged in on people's shoes.

The doctors and nurses wanted to laugh, and didn't dare.

One nurse bit the inside of her cheek when Eve told Julian his pacing was "not medically useful." Eve saw it. She did not have the extra air to be embarrassed.

Su Li and Director Chen had also come, standing outside like two large mascots, one chanting "smooth, smooth," one pacing with a thermos so fast it looked like racewalking practice. Someone told them to sit. They did not sit.

Su Li had cake in a bag and then realized cake was a stupid offering and held the bag like a secret. Director Chen's thermos clicked when he walked. He told a passing nurse that twins were high efficiency, then caught himself. Su Li told him if he said that near Eve right now she would end him.

Dumpling didn't come.

Mostly nobody dared let a cat roam free in this setting.

He'd been left at home, Tang on watch.

Eve had looked at him before they left. He had sat by the door, tail around his paws, the inspector expression on, as if rain nights were still his department. She had said, "We'll be back." He had not looked convinced.

Later Tang sent a voice note.

The phone buzzed in Julian's pocket. Eve told him to pick it up, because if she was going to be in this much pain, she wanted the cat report.

"Dumpling's been circling the door. I think he's more nervous than we are."

A second note, immediately: "He sat on the hospital bag's empty spot. He knows something left the house."

Eve heard it between contractions and wanted to laugh. Couldn't quite get there. Filed it. Later.

Before she went in, Julian held her hand. His palm was all sweat. Hers too. A disgusting, honest handshake.

The corridor light was too white. She looked at his face and thought, don't you dare faint, then the next wave came and she held on.

"Don't be scared."

"You don't be scared first." Her voice shook from the pain, and she still corrected him. "You're paler than I am."

His throat moved. He didn't look away.

"Eve."

"Mm?"

"You worked so hard."

Just that. Her eyes burned, suddenly, and she was furious about it, because she had a job to do and crying felt like extra admin. The words were plain. They landed anyway. They landed in the place where the shop nights lived, and the signup numbers, and the soy milk she could not stand, and the apple pieces, and the note wall.

She used to think she could take it.

She hadn't cried when she got fired. Hadn't cried at the hardest part of starting the shop. Hadn't really cried through the twin pregnancy, exhausted and not sleeping, only the dumb leaky moments over apples and note walls.

Here, him saying you worked so hard, and suddenly all that hard and all that pain felt like someone had caught a little of it, lightly. Not taken it. Just held the bag.

She sniffed, aiming for fierce:

"When the kids are out, you're on postpartum duty."

He bent, forehead to the back of her hand.

Rain still in his hair. His voice came out of that bent place, close to her skin.

"I'm on it. For life."

Quiet. Not a vow-voice. The same voice he used for "I'll watch the signups." She decided that was better. A nurse called her name. Su Li shouted something encouraging and illegal from the hall. Director Chen's thermos clicked. Eve went in with Julian's hand still in hers until they made him let go, and even then he stood there like letting go was the hard part.

When the twins were born, the rain outside had just eased.

Not stopped. Eased. The kind of rain that remembered it had been dramatic and was now pretending to be weather again. Someone said it was almost morning. Eve did not check. Morning was a rumor.

The brother came first, crying loud, like he was clocking in on time for the whole world.

The sound was huge. Unreasonable. A person that small should not have that much opinion.

The sister came after, a tiny bundle, not as loud, still very present. The second the nurse picked her up, she opened her eyes and looked around, like she was inspecting this noisy new world. Eve, half gone, thought: oh. That's Dumpling's look. Then she thought: don't say that to the nurse.

Eve was too tired to keep her eyes open. In the blur she heard the doctor laugh.

"A boy and a girl. Congratulations."

One thought got through.

Good.

Very, very good.

Buy one get one. The joke was not funny right now. It was still true. She let it sit. Julian's voice was there, saying her name like a tether. She followed it as far as she could, then slept a little without permission.

When she was a little more awake, she was in the room.

The room was quieter than the rest of the hospital and still not quiet. Flowers someone had brought too early. The smell of milk starting, and antiseptic, and rain drying in wool. Julian sat by the bed, the sister in his arms, careful as a cloud. He looked like he'd forgotten how elbows worked.

Director Chen had the brother, mouth almost split from smiling.

The brother was still conducting an audit of the air with his lungs. Director Chen bounced in a way that was not expert and was extremely sincere. "Look at this kid. So much energy. You can tell he'll run and yell later."

"Don't put that on the record," Eve said, weak.

"It's already on the record," Su Li said. "I have video."

Su Li was taking photos like a maniac.

"Oh my god, Eve, you skipped to the end."

Eve, weak: "Meaning?"

"Other people do dating, marriage, baby. Three acts." Su Li, moved. "You threw in a deluxe double feature."

Eve laughed until her stomach hurt, and quickly held the incision.

The laugh was a mistake and also the first good thing in hours that wasn't a baby.

"Shut up. Laughing is expensive right now."

Julian frowned at once.

"Don't make her laugh."

"See." Su Li wiggled her eyebrows at Director Chen. "New dad already defending his wife against all relatives."

Director Chen nodded, still bouncing. "Reasonable. The assets just doubled."

"Director," Julian said.

"I'm praising," Director Chen said, and then, because he could not help himself, whispered, "High efficiency," so quietly it still counted.

Eve turned her head to look at him.

There was obvious tiredness in his eyes. He probably hadn't slept all night.

Sitting there with a baby, though, he was quiet in a way that didn't look like him. Not soft-focus. Just... smaller. Like he'd turned the volume down so he wouldn't drop anything. The sister made a small sound, not a cry, a note. He adjusted immediately, as if the note had come with instructions.

She reached out and touched the sister's little hand.

The fingers were impossibly small, and they hooked one of hers, accurately. No poetry required. Just a grip. Rude. Effective.

"Julian."

"Mm?"

"We actually have kids."

"Mm."

"Two of them."

"Mm."

"How are you still this calm?"

He looked at her. His eyes curved, bit by bit. He didn't rush the sentence. When he got there, it came out low.

"Because from last night to now, I still haven't processed how happy I am."

She smiled too. Crooked. Exhausted. A little mean, because if she went full sweet she'd start leaking again.

A whole life was hard to put into one sentence.

Right then it was kids breathing, quiet. Friends laughing, low. Rain just stopped outside. The person she loved most sitting by the bed, holding a baby like a bomb that also needed burping.

Su Li finally put the phone down, which was its own miracle. Eve saw her looking at the brother as if she might name him on the spot. "No milk names in this room."

"I wasn't," Su Li said, lying.

Eve squeezed the tiny hand, once.

"Your son's going to be loud," she said.

"I heard."

"Your daughter's already judging the room."

"I saw."

"You're still paler than me."

He looked at her. Almost smiled.

The almost was enough. The sister blinked, slow, inspector-class. She thought of Dumpling circling the door, sitting on the empty spot where the bag had been, waiting for the house to come back louder.

"I'm on duty," he said.

She closed her eyes. Fine. He could have that one.
