The next morning, Su Li actually showed up.

Not a text. Not a "I'm thinking of you." She showed up. Cream knit set, heels, two cups of soy milk and a bag of youtiao in her hands, like a glamorous city inspector here to survey the people and also feed them. The first thing she saw was Dumpling sitting on the entryway cabinet, looking like he'd always lived there.

"You got laid off, fine." Su Li kicked the door shut with her heel. "Why did you also pick up a fat son?"

Eve took the soy milk with bed hair still intact. She hadn't slept so much as blinked in long stretches. "Watch your language. He's my roommate. We have an arrangement."

"What arrangement. You pay rent and he judges you?"

"That's most roommates."

Su Li crouched to say hi. Dumpling sniffed her fingers, then politely turned his head away, which somehow made it worse. Like he'd decided she was acceptable, just not interesting.

"He's so pretty." Su Li had the auntie-smile of someone already emotionally adopting him. "And the way he looks at people. That's not a regular cat. That's a smart cat."

Eve sucked on her straw. The soy milk was sweet. It helped. "I thought so too. Then I realized it might not be smart. It might be haunted."

She told her about the backup folder. The paw on the keyboard. The draft that had been sitting there like a receipt.

Su Li's first reaction was not shock.

It was envy. Immediate, shameless envy.

"Can he check if my ex is going bald?"

"That's your takeaway?"

"What else?" Su Li sat on the floor without caring about the knit set. "You actually think he can read minds? Eve. Come on."

Eve was about to say she also thought it was insane, that she was unemployed and sleep-deprived and assigning plot to a fat cat, when Su Li's phone rang.

Her boss. The ringtone was professional. The look on Su Li's face was not.

She switched to work-smile in one second, the kind that used the mouth and none of the eyes. "Hi, Mr. Zhang. Good morning."

Whatever he said, she kept going: yes, of course, no problem, I'll revise it this afternoon. Eve watched her best friend nod at empty air and thought, not for the first time, that adult life was just agreeing to things you already knew were stupid.

Su Li hung up and rolled her eyes so hard they nearly left her face.

"Bald bastard," she muttered. "He approved the plan himself. Client yells once and suddenly I 'misunderstood.' I understood him perfectly. That was the problem."

The second she finished, Dumpling lifted his head.

He stared at her for two seconds. Not cute staring. The other kind.

Then he slowly raised a paw and tapped the work badge Su Li had tossed on the table.

Mr. Zhang's extremely official ID photo stared up from it. The kind of photo where a man tried to look trustworthy and mostly looked constipated.

Then: slap.

The badge hit the floor. It didn't bounce. It just... committed.

Su Li froze with soy milk halfway to her mouth.

Eve froze too. The room felt smaller.

After a moment, Su Li managed, very carefully, "Is he... defending my honor?"

"Could still be a coincidence." Eve heard how weak that sounded. "Cats knock stuff off tables. That's like their sport."

Dumpling lowered his head and licked a paw, elegant and unhurried.

He did not look like an athlete. He looked like a behind-the-scenes boss who'd just finished a job and was waiting for someone to say thank you.

Su Li grabbed Eve's arm, eyes huge. "Eve. Did we just hit the jackpot?"

"Don't get excited yet." Eve's pulse was doing something unhelpful. "I'm more worried he'll find out I eat instant noodles at midnight and lose respect for me. I can't take another performance review this week."

"I don't know if cats can lose respect." Su Li's voice dropped. The joke leaked out of it. "But if he really picks up on people's feelings, or thoughts, you have to hide him. There are a lot of creeps out there. Forget a lab. Daily livestreams would already be a nightmare. Someone would put a ring light on him by Friday."

Eve nodded.

She only had a vague wrongness so far. She was nowhere near believing in mind-reading. Mind-reading was for novels, and she'd just gotten kicked out of the version of her life where she still had dental. Still. Dumpling was not a normal cat.

And so far, that not-normal was pretty much on her side. Which was... new. Being on the same team as something, after yesterday, felt almost suspicious.

At noon, Su Li went with her to the clinic for a follow-up. Eve had argued, weakly, that she could go alone. Su Li had said, "You just got fired and adopted a possibly haunted dumpling. I'm coming," and that had been the end of the meeting.

The receptionist lit up as soon as they walked in. "Dumpling's here!"

Like he was a regular. Like he had a membership.

That was how Eve learned yesterday's sharp-tongued vet was Julian, the most popular doctor in the clinic. Good with his hands. Even temper. His only problem, according to the girl at the desk, who said it like a fun fact, was that his mouth did not know how to take a scenic route.

"He's hot," Su Li whispered, as if Eve had not noticed, as if anyone with working eyes had not noticed. "The attitude's just branding at this point."

Eve hadn't even found an answer that wasn't embarrassing when the exam room door opened.

Julian had a mask on, chart in hand. His eyes found the cat first — which, fine, that was his job — then the extra person beside Eve.

"Follow-up?"

"Yeah." She set the carrier on the table a little too carefully. "Figured he should get used to this place. He's going to be a regular. Unfortunately for all of us."

Julian's eyes lifted. "Are you hoping he gets better, or hoping my numbers look good?"

Su Li laughed out loud. Traitor number two.

Eve's face didn't move. "I'm hoping you accumulate a little karma when you talk. You seem short."

His mouth might have twitched. Hard to tell, with the mask. That was probably the point of the mask.

Dumpling was weirdly cooperative during the exam.

At home he acted like a neighborhood inspector, limping around, checking outlets, judging the sofa. In Julian's hands he went still as a wad of cotton. Bandage change, fracture check, not even a symbolic struggle. No claws. No drama. Nothing.

Eve watched this betrayal in high definition.

"Why is he so good for you?"

Julian didn't look up. He was checking the wrap like it mattered, which it did, which was also annoying. "Means he has taste."

Eve looked at him. Then at the cat. Then back at him.

Su Li was already folded over the chair, laughing into her own sleeve.

"I walked through rain for him," Eve said. "I offered him a payment plan. This is what I get."

"He knows you're not going to hurt him," Julian said, still not looking up. "He also knows I'm the one with the gauze. Different jobs."

After the bandage, he went through a few care notes. Don't get the wrap wet. Don't let him jump. Don't feed him human leftovers, which Eve received like a personal attack. Then he asked, "How's the job hunt?"

Eve blinked. "How do you know I'm job hunting?"

"You said so yesterday." Completely flat. No softening. "Just laid off. No money. Wanted a payment plan for the cat."

Right. She had said that. Out loud. To a handsome stranger. While dripping.

Coming back out of someone else's mouth, it sounded like a public execution with a PowerPoint.

"Still sending resumes," she said, stubborn, because what else was there. "I'll find something."

He nodded. No empty "you got this." No "it'll work out." No speech about the universe opening windows. Then, right as she was zipping the carrier, he handed her a card.

White. Cheap cardstock. A clinic logo that had seen better design decisions.

"If you're not in a rush to start somewhere," he said, "the clinic's doing weekend adoption events. We need someone for posters, copy, short videos. Pay isn't great, but it's same-day. If you want, try it."

Eve took the card and stared at it for a few seconds, like it might change languages.

Thin paper. Weirdly warm from his hand, which she decided not to think about.

"How do you know I can do that?"

"Half your box yesterday was pitch decks." He paused, like he was checking whether this was too much. "And your laptop bag still had your company badge on it."

He said it like he was reading off an observation chart. No warmth. No pity. Just: I looked, and this is what was there.

Yesterday she'd crashed in here like a drowned dog: messy, pissed, broke and glowing with it. She'd assumed, to anyone else, it was just another late-night emergency. A wet girl. A fat cat. Next.

Turns out someone had actually seen the situation.

And then, instead of saying I'm sorry, which she couldn't have used, he'd handed her a stepstool. A small one. The kind you could refuse without dying. The kind you could take without saying you needed help.

She tightened her grip on the card. The corner bent.

"Thanks."

He made a small sound — not quite "you're welcome," more like "mm," which seemed to be his whole personality — and went back to writing in the chart. The strip of pale skin above his mask made the tips of his ears look a little red.

Su Li, of course, saw it immediately. She smiled like a woman who had just been handed a theory and a snack.

Eve missed it. She was still looking at the card, thinking about weekend pay, and cat medicine, and the fact that a person she had snapped at last night had gone home and... noticed her.

Dumpling did not miss it.

He lay on the exam table, looked at Julian, looked at Eve, then flicked his tail once, lazy, satisfied.

The expression was very much: I see everything.

And also: I'm not saying it yet. You're both too slow. I have time.
