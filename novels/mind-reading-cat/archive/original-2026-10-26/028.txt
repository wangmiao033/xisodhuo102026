


After that night, Eve suddenly had a very clear thought.

She wanted to go a long way with Julian.

Not the joking kind of "if we get the chance later."

Not early-dating, sugar-drunk "let's see."

She meant it: run the shop with this person, keep a cat, live those ordinary, chaotic, warm days. The kind with leftover yogurt and stolen slippers and bread eaten in a clinic break room at 11 p.m.

Once the thought was out, it rooted. She caught herself looking at him over a signup sheet and thinking, later, like later was a place you could walk to.

The sheet had a coffee ring on it. His handwriting was neater than hers. She looked at the handwriting too long and then pretended she was checking a date.

And a week later, on some random night, it almost became:

What kind of wedding do you want later?

She hit the brakes in time. Mid-sentence, in her own head. Almost said it while he was washing a mug. Almost. The mug would have survived. She wasn't sure she would.

The tap ran. He rinsed. Dumpling sat in the sink's dry corner like a supervisor who did not do dishes. Eve stood there with a towel and a thought that was too big for a kitchen.

Not because she was scared to ask.

Mostly because it was too sudden, like running a pre-marital market survey on her boyfriend. Hi, I like you, please select venue type and child count.

She held it in, and decided to take the long way around.

Su Li's review of the plan was extremely accurate.

"That's not a long way. That's a spy drop."

"You don't get it." Completely serious. She had a notebook. She should not have had a notebook. "Adults investigating marriage views need strategy."

The notebook was the cheap kind from the convenience store. She had written STRATEGY at the top, then felt insane, then did not tear the page out.

"So what's the strategy?"

"Start with something irrelevant. Ease in."

"For example?"

"Ask if he wants a boy or a girl."

Su Li was silent two seconds.

"Eve."

"Mm?"

"That is not irrelevant."

Eve also felt that was fair. She crossed it out. The notebook looked dumber with a line through it.

So she dropped a level, and decided to start with something safer, like "do you want to keep cats forever." Forever was still a lot. Forever was at least not a wedding color.

Dumpling, hearing none of this and somehow all of it, sat on the notebook later and refused to move. A veto. Or an endorsement. With him it was hard to tell.

She didn't even get to deploy it. The chance walked in on its own.

That afternoon, a young couple came in with a pair of one-year-old twins, a boy and a girl, plus a British shorthair fat as a loaf.

One kid babbling, one smiling at everyone, the cat in the bottom of the stroller looking done with life. The cat had the face of a person who'd been assigned two interns and no raise.

The stroller wheels clicked over the threshold. The shop suddenly smelled like milk powder and wet wipes. Dumpling, on the desk, opened one eye, saw two small humans, and closed it again, as if he had already clocked out.

Eve melted on the spot. She was supposed to be a professional. She crouched anyway.

"Oh my god. How is this combination legal."

The mom laughed. "It's actually very loud at home."

The dad, proud: "Loud is still cute."

While Eve did a simple consult, she kept looking at the twins. Tiny shoes. One of them grabbed her pen. The other tried to share a half-chewed biscuit with the cat, who declined with his whole soul.

The biscuit left a crumb on the floor. The British shorthair looked at Dumpling. Dumpling looked back, colleague to colleague, a brief, exhausted nod across species of chaos.

After they left, she had one thought.

Too cute.

Cute enough that she was still thinking about it back at the desk. The leftover biscuit crumb on the floor. The way the dad had said loud is still cute, like he'd already picked a side in a fight that hadn't started.

Julian swept the crumb with his shoe, then bent and actually picked it up, because of course he did.

"You like kids?"

Julian had appeared beside her, following her gaze toward the door.

She jumped, aiming for calm. Failed the calm. "They're... fine."

"Fine?"

"I mean they're cute."

"And twins?"

Her hands went stupid on the forms. How did he go straight to the point. No warm-up. No cat question. Straight to twins, like he'd been waiting in a hallway.

Her ears heated. She pretended to sort forms. The forms did not need sorting.

The paper was slightly damp from her palm. Humiliating. Dumpling walked across one corner, leaving a faint print, which at least gave her something to frown at that was not her own face.

"Twins are cuter, obviously. Buy one get one. Great deal."

He laughed. Quiet. Mean, in a small way.

"Is that how kids work?"

"Isn't it?"

"Do you want that later?"

Her brain buzzed.

Too fast.

Fast enough that even playing dumb didn't look natural. She could hear Su Li in her head: that is not irrelevant.

The shop noise went thin. A leash clinked somewhere. Someone asked Tang for a treat. Eve heard it from very far away.

"I was just talking."

He looked at her red ears. His mouth curved, slowly.

"I was just asking."

She glared up at him.

"Why do you keep teasing me lately?"

"Because you're easy to tease."

"How am I easy to tease?"

"When you're nervous, your ears go red."

She lifted a hand to cover them, on instinct.

Halfway through the motion she realized that was basically a confession. She left the hand there anyway. Damage done. The ears were not going to un-red themselves because she pretended they were a medical condition.

He laughed more obviously.

She couldn't take it, and poked his arm.

The poke was weak. His sleeve was warm. She wanted to take the poke back and also poke him again.

"What about you?"

"What about me?"

"Boy or girl?"

The second it was out, she regretted it.

Still too fast. Spy drop, failed. Notebook, wasted. Su Li was going to be unbearable.

He just looked at her, and actually thought about it. Not a cute pause. A real one. Like the question had weight and he wasn't going to toss it.

The fridge hummed. Dumpling sat down on the forms for real this time, a paperweight with opinions. Julian did not move him. Eve did not move him. The cat had, accidentally, given them a second.

"Either."

"That's so official."

"If I had to pick," he paused, eyes on her face, "I think I'd want a daughter who looks like you."

The shop noise dropped out for a second. Someone's dog sneezed in the back. She heard that, and nothing else.

Her mind blanked for a full three seconds.

Like her.

Not "if we have kids later." Not "maybe."

Hers.

She almost caught fire. She wanted to say that's cheating, that's not a fair sentence, you can't just put a whole kid on the table while I'm holding a pen.

The pen was, in fact, in Dumpling's vicinity now. She did not look for it. Looking for a pen would have required a brain.

She opened her mouth, and only got one sentence out.

"Julian."

"Mm?"

"You're cheating at dating."

He looked at her. Eyes all smile. Not poetic. Pleased, like he'd landed a shot he hadn't practiced.

"Want to keep going anyway?"

She slapped the forms into his chest. Paper made a stupid sound.

Dumpling jumped off at the last second, offended, then sat on Julian's shoe as if to invoice them for the disturbance.

"Yes."

"For how long?"

"Depends on your performance."

"Then I'll try to perform until I'm eighty."

She'd been trying to hold a face.

That last line got her. She snorted. Ugly. Real. The kind of laugh that meant her spy plan was dead and she did not care.

Laughing, she thought: maybe some laters had already been said, without either of them noticing, over and over, by this person. Not in a speech. In yogurt colors. In "slow is fine." In a forehead against hers in a clinic that smelled like disinfectant.

She took the forms back before he wrinkled them.

"Eighty is a long performance review," she said.

"I know."

"You might get tired."

"I won't."

She looked at his face. Dry. Sure. No extra poetry.

Fine.

She was going to be unbearable about this later, in private, into a pillow. For now she sorted the forms for real, ears still hot, and did not look at Su Li's name in her recent chats.

Dumpling, from Julian's shoe, looked up at both of them.

A daughter.

Eighty.

Forms.

Humans were always doing this: taking a perfectly good afternoon and turning it into a future.

He yawned, stood, and went to check the snack cabinet.

If they were going to keep performing until eighty, the cabinet would need to last that long.

He sat in front of it, tail wrapped, and waited to be promoted back to Chief of Hospitality, which, in his view, had never actually been up for review.

Eve stacked the last form. Julian took the pile before she could drop it, ordinary, like he'd been doing it for years already.

"Don't tell Su Li I asked about kids," she said.

"I won't."

"She'll know anyway."

"I know."

They locked up with the usual sounds. On the street her ears were still warm. Spy plan: dead. Cause of death: he answered honestly. She walked anyway, Dumpling heavy in the carrier, eighty sitting in her head like a date she had not written down and would not, tonight, send to anyone.
