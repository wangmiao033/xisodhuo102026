Eve discovered that starting a business and falling in love had the same opening move: getting too high on your own supply.

Difference: love-high made you grin at a chat box at midnight, thumb hovering, rereading a two-word reply like it was a contract. Business-high sat you at the dining table at 3 a.m., hair up with a clip that was losing the war, eyes empty, writing four characters on an A4 page like they could punch through the paper.

Pet Companionship Hall.

She stared at it until the characters stopped looking like language and started looking like a municipal notice taped to a stairwell.

"Does this sound like a nursing-home annex?"

Dumpling was eating freeze-dried treats at the edge of the table. Chicken flavor. The expensive kind, which meant she was bribing a cat at an hour when normal people were asleep. He looked up, chewing, as if to say:

you finally noticed.

She crumpled the page. Tried again, slower, like handwriting could save a bad idea.

"Pet Healing Life Society."

More like a scam. The kind that rented a WeChat group, posted sunrise quotes, and vanished on the fifth of the month.

"Cats Dogs Love Studio."

Neighborhood-committee talent show. She could already hear the aunties clapping off-beat.

She grabbed her hair and looked at Dumpling. "If we ever make it big, do we hang a giant poster of you out front? Chief Mind-Reader, Professor Dumpling. Academic gown optional. Very tasteful. Very threatening."

He was focused on the treats. He paused, considered her, then turned his ass toward her with the calm of someone declining a press conference.

She sighed. "Fine. The chief mind-reader prefers to stay low-key. Very celebrity of you."

The next day she went to the clinic on no sleep and shoved the pile of terrible names at Julian before she'd even put her bag down.

"Pick one."

He scanned them. Took longer than usual. Longer than a man needed to read six bad titles, which meant he was being polite, or he was choosing his word the way he chose a needle size.

"You thought of all of these?"

"Who else. Dumpling contributed by eating."

"They're very..." He searched. Not a long search. A careful one. "Alive."

Her face didn't move. She still had to win the sentence. "If you mean unhinged, you can say unhinged. I was there. I remember."

His mouth twitched. He pulled the blank page from the bottom of the stack and wrote six words, neat, no flourish.

You, Me & Paws.

She stared.

The name wasn't stunning. A little plain, even. The kind of plain that would look stupid in a pitch deck and fine on a street.

It was plain in a way that sounded right.

Not cold. Warm. The kind of place you might actually walk into after work, still in your commute shoes, and not feel like you needed a membership.

"Since when are you good at names?"

"Last night. Late."

"You stayed up thinking about this?"

"Mm."

"Why?"

He looked up. Even. No performance in it.

"Because someone sent me twenty-seven messages at 1 a.m. asking whether 'pet emotional companionship' or 'pet companionship emotion' sounded better."

She opened her mouth. Closed it. Looked at the stack like it might deny the charge.

Right.

Creative block last night. She had, in fact, attacked him. Voice notes. Screenshots of competitor names. One message that was just a string of dots, which in her language meant I am dying of this and you are hereby involved.

"You could've not replied."

"Replying was less work."

"Whose work?"

"Mine." A beat. "If you don't sleep, you keep bothering me."

Her ears went hot. She still had to win the sentence. "Julian, that sounded a little flirty."

He kept writing, like he hadn't heard. The pen didn't even pause. Very professional. Very fake.

The tips of his ears were red.

She saw it and wanted to laugh, then didn't, because laughing would make it a thing, and she was trying to pretend her ears were just warm from the clinic heating. She thought about rent instead. Safer. Rent did not flirt. Rent sat there being due, like a responsible adult.

First step of a business: figure out what you can actually sell.

She inventoried herself, Julian, and one professional slacker currently being forced to work.

Julian: the expertise. The part people paid for without arguing.

Her: content, ops, planning, clients, events. The part people argued about, then paid for anyway.

Dumpling:

"Sits by the door like a mascot and tricks people into spending money."

Tang, sipping milk tea, was brutal. No hesitation. No mercy. The straw made a rude empty sound at the end, like punctuation.

Director Chen objected immediately. "That's not tricking. That's using a core IP for organic traffic."

Eve clapped once, slow. "See, this is why you're the director. Packaging, elite-level. I was going to say 'fat cat with a face,' and you made it sound like a board meeting."

Dumpling napped on the desk. At "core IP," he didn't even lift an eyelid. He had heard worse titles. He had been called Professor at 3 a.m. and survived.

He was getting more celebrity lately.

Last time a little girl at a follow-up wanted a photo, he stared at her for three seconds, then put a paw on her, very reserved. Half a hug. That was the blessing. The girl froze like she'd been knighted.

It looked like a top star leaving a venue. Touching a sleeve was already grace. A full hug would have required a publicist and a velvet rope.

Worse: he picked who he performed for.

People who actually liked him got a belly.

People chasing content didn't even get the back of his head. They got the side of a cat tree and a sense of having been reviewed, then declined.

Eve's private review: "He's not a cat. He's a gate god with benefits."

The first trial event was Sunday evening: "First Cat, Don't Fall Apart Before the Cat Does."

Su Li saw the graphic and almost rolled off the sofa. The sofa was innocent. Su Li was not.

"Who wrote this title?"

"Me."

"Direct. Also soulful." Su Li nodded, already forwarding it. "Every newbie who texts me at midnight asking why their cat is staring at empty air should come. Bring a notebook. Bring dignity, if they have any left."

They borrowed the little break room behind the clinic. Ten people, max. Eve had worried nobody would show. She had a backup plan that involved buying milk tea for empty chairs and never speaking of it again. Spots filled in half a day.

Half the signup notes were the same flavor.

"My cat has me in a cult. Help."

"Mine does parkour at 2 a.m. I'm losing my mind."

"He ignores me. I think he hates me."

Eve laughed until it hurt and handed the list to Julian.

"Look. Modern mental health, fully transparent. No insurance required."

He glanced down, unhurried. "Or cats are just honest."

The day of, she wore a milky-yellow sweater, bright and soft. She was already pretty when she smiled. Lately she'd been doing better, too — sleep still terrible, skin somehow less terrible, which felt like a scam the universe had not explained. Tang kept making noises beside her, the kind of noises people made at puppies and new haircuts.

"Eve, you've been glowing."

Eve, modest, because the alternative was agreeing: "Entrepreneurship is a skincare routine. Stress, then oil, then spite."

Su Li, from behind: "Mostly love."

Eve went to cover her mouth. Fast. Not fast enough.

That was when Julian walked in with a box of cut fruit. Apple, pear, a few strawberries that looked expensive enough to be a line item.

"What love?"

One second of pause-button awkwardness. The room did that thing rooms do when two people hear a word they are not ready to define.

Su Li, lightning: "Love of the job. Dedication. Passion. Very professional. Very HR."

Julian looked at Su Li. Then at Eve. He did not ask again. He set the fruit down like the question had been satisfactorily answered, which it had not.

Eve found a sudden interest in the signup sheet. The paper was not that interesting. She still read it like it might save her.

Dumpling, from the chair, offered one "Meow."

Translation, probably: keep going. The fruit is here. The humans are still talking. This is fine.

The event itself was surprisingly good.

Mostly people in their twenties and early thirties. A socially anxious programmer who sat nearest the wall. A new teacher who apologized for existing twice before sitting down. A girl who wrote full-time at home, who opened with, very tragic, "I don't think my cat loves me. He only loves the auto-feeder."

Eve, immediately: "That's not not-loving you. Auto-feeders are just very stable. They don't go out. They don't check their phones. They don't say 'one more episode.'"

The room lost it. The programmer made a sound like a laugh that had been stored in a drawer.

Julian handled the actual medical questions. Diet. Vaccines. When staring into corners was normal and when it was a vet visit. Eve translated the unhinged ones ("he stopped licking me, is there another cat") into gentler common sense, which was mostly: cats are rude, and also sometimes they just get bored of your shampoo.

By the second half everyone had loosened up and started roasting their own pets.

"My dog barks when I do makeup. Does he think I don't deserve a face?"

"Mine sits outside the bathroom when I shower. Like he's holding a vigil."

"When I cry at night he looks at me twice, then goes to eat. Very cold older-brother energy."

Eve laughed until her stomach hurt. She had to put her pen down. The pen was not helping.

People and pets were so weird.

One of them couldn't talk. The other overthought everything. They still showed up for each other, clumsy and serious, like two people sharing an umbrella that was slightly too small.

At the end, a young woman in glasses stayed behind, hugging her cat carrier, hesitating. "Teacher Eve."

That "Teacher Eve" almost made Eve sit up straighter on the spot. She was not a teacher. She was a woman with a spreadsheet and a cat. The title still landed, like someone had handed her a name tag she had not ordered.

"Yes?"

"I've been in a rough place." The girl picked at her strap. The plastic made a small tired sound. "I used to think a cat was just a cat. Lately he comes to my pillow every night. I suddenly thought... if someone's there, it's not that bad."

Her eyes went red as she said it. Not a performance. The ugly kind, the kind you get when you've been holding a sentence for weeks.

Eve's throat tightened. Before she could find a useful line, Julian had already passed over a tissue. No speech. Just the tissue, like this was a clinic and people crying was part of the inventory.

"You're already doing well," he said. "If a cat stays beside you, it knows you're safe."

The girl nodded, sniffing, and left with her cat. The carrier bumped the doorframe. She apologized to the doorframe.

When everyone was gone, the break room went quiet. Fruit rinds. Stacked chairs. The smell of someone else's perfume that would take a day to leave.

Eve bent to gather papers, a little slow. The yellow sweater sleeve kept sliding. She kept pushing it up. It kept sliding.

"What is it?"

"Nothing." She smiled. It was a real smile, which annoyed her, because she had wanted to be cooler about this. "I just... this actually matters. I keep waiting for it to feel fake. It isn't."

He looked at her.

"Mm."

"And you were very practiced with that tissue." She tipped her head. "Do you comfort people a lot?"

"No."

"Then how are you so good at it?"

"Because I saw you cry."

She stopped moving. The papers made a stupid little sound.

"When did I cry in front of you?"

"The first night." He said it simply. Like a chart. "At the payment counter. You looked at the bill, and your eyes went red."

She went quiet. Looked at the floor, then at the fruit box, then nowhere useful.

She had been a mess that night.

A mess she didn't like remembering. The kind of mess that came with doing math out loud in a clinic lobby, pretending the numbers were about a cat and not about her whole life.

Someone had remembered anyway.

That clearly. Not the dramatic parts. The bill. The counter. The eyes.

Dumpling sat on the table, looked at one, then the other, then rolled a little tangerine toward Julian. The fruit bumped Julian's knuckle and stopped, like a delivery that had found the right address.

Julian looked down.

So did Eve.

The room was quiet enough that she could hear the fridge in the corner kick on. A humble sound. Not a soundtrack.

Su Li charged in from outside, volume high, already talking before the door was fully open.

"Eve! Any fruit left? I was out there doing PR and now I'm starving. I told three people we have a cat psychologist. Don't fact-check me."

The almost-something shattered. Fruit. PR. Cat psychologist. All of it, loud, useful, badly timed.

Eve put a hand to her forehead.

Julian turned away and laughed, low. He picked up the tangerine. He did not comment on the tangerine. That was, somehow, worse.

Dumpling shut his eyes, expressionless.

He had rolled the fruit. The fruit had arrived. If the humans wanted to keep standing there making faces, that was a them problem. He had a nap scheduled, and unlike some people, he kept his appointments.
