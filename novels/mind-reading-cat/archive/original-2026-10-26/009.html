If almost-something is a pot of soup, jealousy is the first steam off the top.

Eve used to think she wasn't that basic.

She used to think jealousy belonged to other people: the ones who checked phones in bathrooms, the ones who invented stories from a two-second pause. She had a fat cat, a draft, and a clinic she was not supposed to treat like a second home. That should have been enough personality for one Wednesday.

Then, Wednesday afternoon, she went to the clinic with a new script and saw the pretty woman with the ragdoll again.

This time she was sitting in Julian's exam room, a cup of water in her hand, looking as natural as a part-owner of the place. Legs crossed. Lucky on the chair like a silk cushion with opinions. The cup even had the clinic's cheap lid, which somehow made it worse. Like she'd already found the mugs.

Eve's feet stopped.

The hallway smelled like disinfectant and someone else's perfume. Floral. Expensive enough to have a name Eve wouldn't pay for. Dumpling's carrier bumped her knee. She adjusted her grip and still didn't move.

She didn't know why they stopped. He was a doctor. People came in. The woman had a cat. That was even more normal. Eve still felt a tightness in her throat, like she'd sipped lemon water that wasn't cold enough. The sourness rose slowly. She hated that she could name it. Naming it made it real.

Dumpling was sharper.

He stood up in the carrier. Ears up. The plastic door rattled, just once. Then again, softer, like he wanted a second look and also wanted her to know he had opinions about second looks.

Tang walked by with files, saw Eve, and dropped her voice. "You're here? Julian's already been siege-attacked by auntie-level matchmaking twice today. I counted. I have a system."

Eve blinked. "What?"

"The one inside. Cheng Yin. Julian's mom's friend's daughter. She's pretty, sure. Every time she comes she acts like the official wife doing rounds." Tang hurried to add, because Tang had a sense of self-preservation, "Don't get the wrong idea. He's just polite with her. Pure manners. The manners of a man who does not know how to be rude to his mother's friend's daughter. It's a disease."

Eve was about to say I don't have the wrong idea when Cheng Yin's laugh came from the room. Light. Practiced. The kind of laugh that belonged in a living room with good tea.

"Julian, your mom says you're always too busy to come home. Is the clinic that piled up? If you ever want to open your own place, you can talk to my brother. He has friends who can invest..."

Eve stood in the doorway. The lemon got stronger.

Open a shop.

Invest.

The words were little needles. She and Julian had been talking about later a lot. The account. Companionship. Pet healing. She'd thought it was a private, warm little consensus, the kind you built out of 380-kuai gigs and night-shift texts. Now it turned out other people could offer the future more easily, with money attached, and a brother, and the kind of family that said "friends who can invest" the way other people said "I know a guy who can get you a table."

She looked down at her jeans. At the script folder, slightly bent from the bus. At Dumpling's nose pressed to the carrier door, one whisker stuck at a heroic angle.

Holding Dumpling, she suddenly didn't want to go in. She could leave the file with Tang. She could text. She could be a coward with a good excuse.

Dumpling, extremely unhelpful, meowed.

Not quietly.

Both people inside looked over.

Julian was on his feet almost immediately. The chair scraped.

"You're here."

Three ordinary words. Out of his mouth, they somehow sounded like they'd known each other forever. Some of the sourness scattered. Not all of it. Enough that her feet remembered how to move.

Cheng Yin turned, saw her, smile still proper, eyes doing a scan. Eve knew the scan. Assistant, threat, nobody. She'd worn it last time. She was wearing jeans again. The script folder suddenly felt like a prop.

"Eve."

Eve smiled back. "Miss Cheng."

She handed him the file. "New draft. I was in the area."

"In the area?" Tang muttered outside. "Her place is the other direction."

Eve's ears twitched. She pretended not to hear. Tang's muttering had the carrying power of a fire alarm.

Cheng Yin leaned back in the chair, voice soft. "Do you work at the clinic now, Eve?"

"We collaborate."

"That's nice." A nod, still gentle. "Julian's very serious. Sometimes he doesn't know how to say no. If you run into anything on the business side, you can ask me. My brother's family does business. I know a little."

Pretty packaging.

To Eve it felt like being handed a rock in candy coating. She was about to answer — she had an answer, it involved the word collaborate again, and maybe a joke about still owing the clinic money — when Dumpling twisted, hit the floor, and went straight for Lucky, who was sitting there with his head up.

"Dumpling!"

He didn't fight.

He walked up to Lucky, stared for a few seconds, then turned around, put his ass toward him, and swept his tail, slow.

Lucky froze.

Cheng Yin froze.

Then it got worse.

Lucky actually followed Dumpling, trotted to Eve's feet, and sat.

Like he'd picked a team on live television. Fluffy. Traitorous. Extremely clear.

Tang bit her hand in the doorway so she wouldn't laugh. A file slipped. She caught it with her elbow.

Eve looked down at two cats and had no protocol for this. A smile showed in Julian's eyes too. He hid it in a chart, badly.

Cheng Yin's composure slipped for the first time. "Lucky's usually very clingy."

Eve looked down, scooped Dumpling — Lucky looked briefly betrayed — and said, very sincerely, "I can tell."

The air got delicate. Like a cup you weren't sure was clean.

Julian timed it. "Cheng Yin, Lucky's fine today. I already wrote the diet notes. Just follow those."

That was, basically, please leave. Eve heard it. Cheng Yin heard it. Even Lucky, possibly, heard it.

Cheng Yin looked at Eve, stood anyway, smile intact. The cup of water stayed on the counter, half drunk.

"I'll go then. Julian, don't forget to call Auntie back."

He hummed. Nothing extra. No I'll call tonight. No walk her to the door. Eve did not enjoy how much she noticed that. She noticed it anyway.

The second she was gone, Tang burst in with her files, delighted.

"I hereby announce today's palace drama: Dumpling, complete victory. Lucky, defection. Miss Cheng, please collect your cat at lost and found."

Eve nearly choked. "Who had palace drama?"

"The cats," Tang said, righteous. "Cat palace drama. We don't do people palace drama. We're a medical facility."

Julian glanced at her. "Don't listen to her."

Eve said "oh." The weird tightness in her throat was mostly gone. Lucky had been collected. The room smelled like disinfectant again, which was better than perfume.

She didn't expect the real loss of control to come five minutes later.

She was helping him sort charts. Offhand, because if she didn't say it the lemon would come back later, in bed, at 1 a.m.: "Miss Cheng said her brother could introduce investors?"

"Mm."

"You've thought about opening your own place?"

"I have."

Her fingers paused on a folder. "Would you take their help?"

His hands stopped. He looked up.

"Does that bother you?"

The honest answer stuck in her throat.

Yes, it bothers me. It bothers me like a soap-opera side character with no title, already jealous, standing in an exam room with a cat who had just committed a diplomatic incident. She looked away, stubborn, at the chart labels, at his handwriting, at anything.

"I just don't want capital to walk in later and stop playing with grassroots founders like us. We have a fat cat and a spreadsheet. That's not a pitch deck. That's a cry for help."

He watched her a few seconds, then smiled, very lightly.

"I won't."

"Why not?"

"Because I'm not planning to ask her."

"Then who are you planning to ask?"

"You."

The air went still. A drip from the sink, somewhere. Very loud, suddenly.

She looked up. His eyes were there.

Usually so controlled. Right now they were just looking at her, too serious to be a joke. No smile to hide behind. She wanted to laugh and couldn't find the joke.

"Eve," he said. "If we actually do this, I want to try it with you first."

She couldn't look at him. The urge to win the sentence died on the spot. She pretended to pet Dumpling, both hands, too much attention, like the cat had just returned from war.

The exam room suddenly felt too small for a sentence like that. Charts, a half-drunk cup Cheng Yin had left, Lucky's leftover fur on the chair. Ordinary mess. And then him, saying try it with you first, like they were discussing a weekend, not a life.

She wanted to ask what first meant. First as in draft. First as in if it fails we still have the clinic. First as in I already picked you and I'm waiting for you to catch up. She did not ask. Asking would have required a voice that wasn't currently hiding in a fat cat's fur.

"Okay," she said, to Dumpling, which was cheating.

Julian didn't call her on it. He went back to the charts, slower than before, like he was giving the air time to become air again.

Dumpling, confused, flicked his tail. Then he twisted, put a paw on her wrist, and made a short sound that was not matchmaking.

It was closer to: you are squeezing me. There is food in the back. I did my job. Pay me in cans, not in feelings.
