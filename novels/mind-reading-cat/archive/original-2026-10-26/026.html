The Cat Stole the Proposal Ring

The guy with the carrier was Qin, twenty-eight, a programmer, hair like a client had personally ravaged it. His eyes said this was worse than a production outage. He set the carrier down too hard, then patted it, guilty, like the cat might file a complaint.

The carrier plastic was scratched. The latch clicked twice. Inside, something calm and black-and-white waited, as if the emergency belonged only to the human.

"Please. I'm begging you." He set the carrier on the table, voice cracking. "The ring's gone. My girlfriend's birthday is tomorrow. I was going to rehearse the proposal tonight, and he just—he grabbed it and ran!"

Eve looked into the carrier.

A black-and-white cow cat, round eyes, extremely calm.

Did not look like he'd committed a crime.

Looked like he'd planned it. Sat there the way a person sits after deleting a group chat on purpose.

The shop still smelled faintly of leftover tea from the salon. Julian's "yes" was still somewhere in the air, unused. Eve put on her professional face. It kept slipping.

"Why would he take a ring?" She held in a laugh, trying to stay professional. Professional was hard when the cat blinked, slow, like he was waiting for her to catch up. "What were you doing?"

Qin almost cried.

"I was on one knee practicing! I even said the speech to him. I opened the box and he shot over, snap, grabbed it, gone!"

Eve opened her mouth. Nothing useful came out. She looked at Julian. Julian had the face he used for serious cases, except a muscle near his mouth kept trying to leave.

Tang, in the back, was pinching her own thigh so she wouldn't laugh. Eve could hear the tiny pained sound.

The plot was too stupid.

Stupid, with a thread of sense in it.

Eve couldn't help asking, "Do you two fight over attention a lot?"

Qin blinked. "You can tell?"

"I guessed."

"My girlfriend does like him more." Tragic. He said it like a man reporting a bug he couldn't reproduce. "She hugs him first when she gets home. Last goodnight is to him too. I think he doesn't want me going official."

The cow cat in the carrier meowed, lightly.

It sounded a lot like:

Good. You know.

Dumpling had been napping at the desk. He lifted his head, slow, and locked eyes with the cow cat across the shop.

For a second there was professional recognition. Colleague to colleague. Two cats who understood that humans were always about to do something loud with a small object.

Dumpling's tail ticked once. The cow cat's ears went back, then forward. A handshake, if a handshake could be done with silence and fur.

Eve crouched and let the cat out.

He didn't run. Didn't panic. Toured the shop, unhurried, sniffed a plant, ignored a hanging toy, then stopped by a display shelf, flicked his tail, and sat.

The plant was the mint. Or the cat grass. Dumpling watched the sniff like a landlord. The cow cat pretended not to notice the inspection.

The ring was not in his mouth.

That thing was already stashed. Of course it was. Amateur thieves ran. This one had a filing system.

"Where does he usually hide stuff at home?" Julian asked.

Qin wracked his brain. "Under the sofa, behind curtains, in my shoes, behind the washer... Last time he pushed my house key into the scratching pad."

Eve was respectfully silent. She wanted to clap. She did not clap.

"This cat has a future."

"This is not the time to compliment him!"

"Don't panic." She soothed, the way she soothed owners whose cats had eaten a hair tie. "A lot of cats don't steal to steal. They hide. Especially if they think the thing is going to take your attention, or they don't like you giving that attention to something else."

Qin, shocked: "So he really was trying to stop the proposal?"

She thought about it, as diplomatic as possible. Looked at the cow cat. The cow cat looked at a spot on the wall.

"From a motive standpoint, he's a strong suspect."

Two seconds of quiet.

Then Tang lost it, laughing on the floor. Actual floor. Director Chen's voice floated from the back, delighted and unhelpful: "Write this one down. This is content."

Dumpling yawned, huge, unimpressed by content. Content was a human problem. Rings were a cat problem. He stood, stretched until his back made a small curve, and went to work.

In the end, the ring hunt got done with Dumpling's help.

Specifically: Dumpling walked a circle around the cow cat, tail hooking, like he was asking something. Not cute. Administrative. The cow cat played cold, then maybe couldn't take the peer pressure, stood up slow, and led everyone to a woven basket in the corner of the event area.

The basket still had a leftover event smell. Someone's perfume. Dry grass from a decoration. Dumpling sniffed the rim first, as if checking protocol.

One paw pulled the cushion aside.

The ring box was lying there. A little lint on it. Otherwise fine, like it had been waiting for the meeting to start.

Qin lunged, holding it, almost in tears.

"You little ancestor. You almost ruined my life event."

The cow cat shook his whiskers. Zero remorse. He sat on the cushion he'd just vacated, as if to say: I returned it. Don't make it a scene.

Julian checked the ring. Fine. Band not bent. Stone still in. He handed it back like he was discharging a patient.

The box clicked shut. Qin held it in both hands, the way people held phones after they almost dropped them in a toilet.

Qin thanked them into the ground, then stopped at the door, a little squirmy. He looked at the box, then at them, then at the box again.

"Um..." He looked at Julian, then Eve. "Are you two already married?"

Eve nearly lost a breath. She made a sound that was not a word.

The door was half open. Evening air came in, cooler. She felt it on her ears, which was unfortunate, because the ears were already doing too much.

"Why would you think that?"

"You just... click." He scratched his head. "When you were helping me look, you really looked like a couple who've already been running a shop together for years."

Director Chen, in the back, nodded, faint.

"The people have eyes."

Eve's ears went red. She went to ruffle Dumpling, because Dumpling was a safer place to put her hands than Julian's sleeve.

Dumpling's fur was warm from the hunt. He leaned into the ruffle like payment. He had, after all, done customer service.

"This is your fault. You've been making this place feel like a dating show."

Dumpling meowed, innocent.

Why did every pot get put on his head. He hadn't even hidden the ring. He'd only done customer service.

After Qin left, she rolled "couple who've been running a shop" around in her mind until it got hotter. Running a shop. Years. Married. She pretended to wipe a table that was already clean. The cloth went in circles.

The table smelled like cleaner. The cloth was damp. Her brain was not helping.

She was pretending to be busy when Julian said, slow, beside her.

"He wasn't wrong."

She snapped her head up.

"About what?"

"The clicking part." He looked at her, mouth curving. "We do."

Her pulse jumped, stupid and obvious. She went stubborn on purpose, because if she didn't she'd say something worse.

"Who looks married with you."

"Not yet."

"Then later we also..."

She stopped herself halfway.

Because she suddenly found she could not get "later we also aren't" out of her mouth. The sentence sat there, half-chewed. She stared at the cloth.

The shop was quiet enough to hear Dumpling's slow lick, somewhere near their feet. A wet, peaceful sound. Unhelpful.

He watched her ears go red, didn't tease more, just squeezed the back of her neck, lightly. Thumb warm. Not a big gesture. The kind that said: I heard the half sentence. I'm not going to make you finish it in the shop.

"Slow is fine."

The squeeze went through her, down into her shoulders. It took her a while to say "oh," low. She sounded twelve. She hated that. She also didn't move away.

Dumpling licked his fur beside them, inner peace intact. One hind leg up. Full spa mode.

Nobody had even proposed, and these two were already rehearsing married life.

He finished the leg. Blinked.

Whatever. As long as the snack cabinet stayed stocked.

Eve kept the cloth in her hands a second longer than the table needed. Running a shop. Years. Married. Qin had said it like a bug report, and her brain had taken it like a fever. She could still feel the squeeze at the back of her neck, warm, not a claim, just a pause button. Slow is fine. She wanted to argue with the word slow, then didn't, because arguing would mean saying later out loud, and she had already failed at later once tonight.

Julian moved away first, to put the ring-hunt basket back where it belonged. Ordinary. Helpful. The kind of ordinary that made Qin's comment worse.

Dumpling rolled onto his side, still in spa mode, and showed her his belly like a person who had closed a ticket.

She scratched him once. He allowed it. Barely.

"Don't look so pleased," she muttered, not sure if she meant the cat or the man.

Dumpling's eyes were already closed. Julian, across the shop, did not look pleased. He looked like a person putting a basket down. Which, she was learning, was sometimes the same thing.
