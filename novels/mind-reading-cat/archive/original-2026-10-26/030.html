Eve and Julian's wedding was not a big one.

A little yard with a lawn, just outside the city. The weather was criminally good. Blue, not the washed-out kind. The wind was exactly right, which felt suspicious, like someone had filed a request. Eve stood in the porch shade before anyone seated her and thought, if a cloud shows up now, I'm taking it personally.

No huge stage. No complicated program. No host who talked like a shopping channel.

Just white flowers and green plants, and a big wooden sign at the entrance.

Welcome to Julian and Eve's human bonding ceremony.

Signed underneath.

Officiating cat: Dumpling.

The letters were a little uneven. Eve had painted them herself. Julian had offered to reprint it. She had said no. If the sign looked too clean, it would stop being theirs.

Su Li almost died laughing at the sign. She had to hold the fence. Her heels sank a little into the grass. She did not care.

"You actually wrote that?"

Eve was fixing her veil, eyes curved. The veil kept sticking to her lipstick. Very glamorous. She peeled it off, put it back, peeled it off again. The air smelled like cut stems and warm wood.

"Is it not true?"

"It is." Su Li nodded, serious. "Without Dumpling, you two would still be handing each other soy milk at the front desk."

"We still do that," Eve said.

"That's the problem. You got married and didn't even upgrade the beverage."

A lot of the people they'd met along the way came.

Director Chen, Tang, clinic staff, old clients, the young couple with the twins and the loaf of a British shorthair, the auntie who'd wanted a match for her dog, even Qin, whose ring had almost been stolen, with his girlfriend. Qin looked healthier. His hair had recovered. The girlfriend had the cow cat energy of someone who knew the whole story and had decided to stay anyway.

They came in a loose clump, like the yard had been collecting the last year and pouring it onto the lawn. Director Chen inspected the chairs, then the plants, then the sign. Tang filmed the sign first, of course. Vertical. The British shorthair loaf sat in a stroller with the expression of a creature who had attended too many human events and would like a refund. The dog-matchmaking auntie told Eve the flowers were good, and if they ever wanted to match a dog later she was available. Eve said thank you and did not commit.

And the first thing Qin said:

"You two had it way smoother than I did."

Eve laughed until she couldn't. Smoother. Sure. He'd missed the slipper theft, the clinic bread, the spy notebook. From the outside, maybe it did look smooth. Fine. Let it look smooth today.

"We'll take smoother," she said. "For one afternoon."

Dumpling wore a custom little bow, plus a tag: Do not pet. I am busy.

The bow was slightly crooked because he had sat down after they tied it. Several people still reached toward him. He looked at their hands the way a manager looked at unauthorized visitors. Most of the hands retreated.

He had been assigned ring duty.

More precisely: carry the box from one side of the flower arch to the middle.

Everybody had been nervous about this part.

The cat had a record. Stolen keys. Hidden snacks. Interfered with a proposal. Liked to comment at key moments. Eve had had a nightmare, two nights ago, where he sat on the box and refused to negotiate. In the dream she had offered him cans, then fish, then her dignity. He had licked a paw and stayed. She had woken up and made Julian confirm the box still existed.

If today he decided the ring should not be surrendered, the scene would be hard to explain. You could not put "cat ate the ceremony" on a wedding video and call it aesthetic. Tang would still post it. That was the problem.

Julian stood near the arch in a dark suit that made him look slightly too official for grass. He kept glancing at Dumpling, then at the box, then at Eve.

"He'll be fine," Eve said, when she passed him.

"I know."

"You don't look like you know."

"I know in theory."

Dumpling, to his credit, was extremely stable.

He did stop to lick a paw, and inspect a roadside flower, but he delivered the box intact to Julian's feet. Then he sat. Job done. Please stop staring.

A leaf landed on the box. He did not eat the leaf. Eve felt her shoulders drop so hard she was surprised they didn't make a sound.

The whole place clapped.

Eve stood there in her dress, laughing until the tears almost came. She blinked them back. She had already used the crying allotment at the proposal. Today she was trying for dignity. Dignity was not winning. The dress made a sound when she shifted. She thought, very clearly, don't step on it, then thought, that's the least interesting thought a bride has ever had.

A kid in the back asked, at full volume, whether the cat was getting paid. Eve decided the kid was the only person taking the officiant title seriously.

Tang was filming vertically, of course. Su Li was already wiping her face with a napkin she'd stolen from the drinks table. Qin's girlfriend had her phone up too, and Qin looked like a man watching a tutorial he wished he'd had.

She used to think the most important thing about a wedding was dignity.

Later she learned: no.

The most important thing was that it looked like you.

And this one looked like them.

A little loud. A little funny. A lot of everyday smoke in it. Every bit of it real. Someone's kid yelling. The British shorthair loaf in a stroller, done with life again. Director Chen's thermos, somehow present, like a plus-one. It sat on a chair in the second row as if it had an invitation. Eve caught Julian's eye over the box and almost snorted. He looked slightly too serious for a lawn. She liked that. She would never tell Su Li she liked that.

When they exchanged rings, he looked down at her. No big speech. Just the look he got when he was concentrating, except this time the thing he was concentrating on was her hand.

The box opened with a small click. The rings were simple. His fingers were warm. The wind moved the veil against her cheek. Somewhere behind her, Su Li made a tiny sound, like she was trying not to start again.

He slid the ring on carefully, as if her hand might startle.

"Wife."

Her ears went hot. The veil suddenly felt like a hat she hadn't agreed to. The word sat in the air, too new, too legal, too much like a joke they had been circling for months and had now said in front of people.

"You're changing it already?"

"What else?"

"Do I have to call you Husband then?"

"You can."

She blinked, and dropped her voice on purpose. If she said it loud, Su Li would scream. She could smell the mint from the drinks table, and the flowers, and him.

"Husband. Please take care of me from now on."

His mouth curved.

"You too, Wife."

Su Li, in the first row, pinched Director Chen's arm, hard.

"AAAA did you hear that!"

He grimaced. "I heard. Don't pinch me. I'm too old for shipping."

"You're never too old," Su Li said, and pinched him again, lighter, which he still counted as an attack.

After the ceremony, people yelled for a kiss.

Eve was still a little shy. Then Tang started it, and even the dog-matchmaking auntie clapped and shouted "one more," so she went all in, grabbed his tie, and pulled him down.

The place roared.

He actually paused, being kissed, then he smiled against her mouth, like he'd lost an argument and didn't mind. He tasted like the mint from the table. She was going to remember that and be annoyed about it later. Public kissing. Legal. Still embarrassing. Her palm was on his tie and she could feel how hard he was trying not to laugh. The veil finally gave up and stuck to both of them. She did not fix it.

"Eve."

"Mm?"

"You're pretty forward today."

"It's legal now. Of course I'm bolder."

He bent to her ear. "Then tonight we keep going?"

Heat climbed all the way to her hairline. She almost stepped on her own dress. Someone in the back shouted "I can't hear you!" which was a lie, because everyone had heard, including the dog-matchmaking auntie, who looked delighted.

"Julian!"

He didn't look sorry. He looked like a person who had said Wife in public and decided the rest of the day could also be a little illegal.

Photos happened in a clump, the way they always did: Director Chen insisting on one with the thermos "for history," Tang filming vertically even during the still shots, Qin and his girlfriend standing a little too far left until Eve waved them in. The British shorthair loaf in the stroller slept through the entire set. Eve's feet hurt. She did not sit. Sitting in this dress felt like a project.

"You okay?" Julian asked, when the photographer went to change a battery.

"I'm hungry."

"There's cake."

"I know there's cake. I want the cake and also I want everyone to stop looking at me for eight seconds."

He stood slightly in front of her, not a speech, just a shoulder. Eight seconds happened. She ate a strawberry off a passing tray like a crime.

Dumpling stood on a nearby flower platform, watching the newlyweds, calm.

Ceremony just over, and these two had already dropped the dignity.

Normal, though.

This household had never been that proper.

A petal stuck to his bow. He did not remove it immediately. The kid was still asking about cake. The loaf in the stroller had gone to sleep, which Dumpling considered the only correct response to a human bonding ceremony.

He licked a bit of pollen off his paw, decided the humans could handle the rest of the shouting, and closed his eyes for a minute. Officiating was tiring. Someone still owed him cans.
