


Eve did not expect He Qian to show up in person.

Monday afternoon, the clinic wasn't busy. Eve was teaching Tang how to read ad data — bounce, retention, the difference between a like and a person who would actually book — when the door chime rang. He Qian walked in on heels.

Hair and makeup done, smile appropriate, a bag of fruit in her hand, like a respectable person visiting an old friend. The fruit looked expensive. The fruit was doing a lot of work.

The lobby air shifted. Perfume, sweet, a little too much for a place that smelled like disinfectant on purpose. Tang's pen stopped mid-note. Dumpling, on the cat tree, opened his eyes as if a meeting had been added to his calendar without consent.

Tang looked blank. "Hi, are you here for an appointment or to buy something?"

"I'm looking for Eve." He Qian's smile was soft. "We used to work together."

The second Eve looked up, the smile at her mouth thinned.

She had to admire He Qian.

Some people were born with a talent: they could do every ugly thing and still look like they were the one who'd been wronged. The fruit helped. The heels helped. The "Eve" was going to help, in a second.

Eve's first thought was not a clever line. It was: of course. Of course the name would walk in wearing concern. The internet had not been enough. He Qian needed a room, an audience, and pears.

"Why are you here?"

"To see you." He Qian set the fruit down, all concern. The bag crinkled. "You've been doing well, haven't you? I keep seeing your videos. I'm really happy for you."

Eve laughed, inwardly, cold. Happy. He Qian had been happy for her in the old company too, usually right before a client exploded.

The fruit sat on the counter like a prop from a better relationship. Plastic tied in a bow. Tang glanced at it, then at Eve, then decided to become very interested in a blank form.

Dumpling had already come down from the cat tree, unhurried, and stopped at He Qian's feet. He did not sniff the fruit. He sniffed the shoes, then sat back like the shoes had failed a test.

He Qian looked down, fake-delighted. "Aww, so cute."

She bent to pet him. Dumpling stepped back, disgust extremely readable. No fluff. No polite sniff. Just: no.

Tang had never seen a cat this blunt. Her shoulders shook. She turned it into a cough that fooled nobody.

He Qian's smile froze, then recovered. "Same as always. Cats have personalities."

Eve didn't take the bait. Personality was the polite word for: he can smell you. "What do you need?"

He Qian knew the small talk was done. The fruit had not opened a door. Fine. She would use the other door.

"So, our company wants to try pet content. You know I've always thought you were very capable. And you're already doing this, so I thought, maybe we could talk. You could write up the ops model you've been using here, for internal reference. Pay's negotiable."

The front desk went quiet.

Even Tang, not the fastest, heard it.

Write it up. Internal reference.

Translation: come give us the playbook for free. Then we'll say we built it. Then we'll hire someone cheaper to run it. Eve had written the cheaper person's job description before, in a different life.

Eve looked at her and almost laughed.

This had been He Qian's move at the old company. Praise first. Get close. Then, in that "we're all family" voice, treat your brain like it lived on her desk. Family, in that office, had meant: you stay late.

The printer in the back clicked, uselessly. Tang held very still, like moving might count as taking a side. Eve kept her voice even, because even was a tool she had paid for with years.

"Sorry," Eve said, even. "That's not convenient."

He Qian's smile hitched. "Eve, don't be like that. We used to..."

"You weren't 'like that' when you dumped work on me either." Eve looked up, not loud, very steady. Her palms were already starting to sweat. She kept them below the desk. "He Qian, my temper's better than it used to be. My memory isn't."

He Qian's face finally slipped. "I think you have the wrong idea about me."

Even Tang rolled her eyes. The eye-roll was a whole performance. Eve loved her a little for it.

That was when Dumpling jumped onto the desk and slapped a folder sitting by Eve's hand.

The papers fanned out.

Printouts. The chat logs and timeline she'd saved the last time He Qian asked for a draft. She'd told herself she was being paranoid. Occupational paranoia from the office-drone years. Turns out paranoia had a filing system.

He Qian saw them. Her face went a little white.

Eve looked at Dumpling, then up. The picture was already clear. The cat hadn't found new evidence. He'd just... opened the drawer she already knew was there.

"You didn't come here just to 'collaborate,' did you."

He Qian's eyes flickered. "I don't know what you mean."

Eve picked up her phone and played a voice note, slow. Volume not high. High enough.

He Qian had sent it a couple days ago, then recalled it.

"Her account's doing okay. Pull the method first. If that doesn't work, just poach her."

The recall had been fast.

Eve had already saved it.

Thank you, occupational paranoia from the office-drone years. Thank you, the version of her that used to cc people and feel mean about it.

The air at the desk went dead. The fish tank hummed like it wanted out.

He Qian went from surprise to embarrassment to rage, all of it visible, none of it pretty. The respectable layer was a costume. It came off faster than the fruit bag.

"You recorded me?"

"You sent a voice note. I kept evidence. That's not illegal." Eve smiled. The smile felt like the one in the elevator after she got fired: fine, on purpose. "Please take your fruit and go. And don't come back. The cats have a standard. You didn't pass."

The respectable layer finally dropped. He Qian's face went hard. She grabbed the fruit and left, heels ticking across the floor like the last soundtrack of a lost round. The door chime rang again, cheerful, unhelpful.

The second she was gone, Tang clapped on the spot.

"Eve, that was so cool. I want to put it on the account. I won't. I'm saying I want to."

Eve let out a long breath and realized her palms were wet.

Not that she hadn't been nervous.

This time, she just didn't have to swallow it. No Mr. Zhao. No closed meeting room. No "young people shouldn't talk like that." Just a front desk, a folder, and a cat who had no interest in being polite to fruit.

She wiped her hands on her jeans, once, like that would reset her pulse. It didn't. It was still better than a meeting room. She had pictured this conversation for months, usually in a worse room, usually with her voice smaller. Tonight it had come out of her mouth like it had been waiting in a folder too. She still wanted to sit down. She still wanted a glass of water. Wanting those things did not mean she wanted He Qian back in the building. The fruit bag had left a faint sweet smell, like someone had tried to air-freshener a crime. Eve stared at the empty counter until Tang poked her arm, once, then thought better of a second poke.

Dumpling jumped to her legs and looked up.

She picked him up and rubbed his head, hard. Harder than was elegant. He allowed it, which was love, or at least hazard pay.

"You really are my workplace guardian angel."

He was so squished his eyes almost disappeared. He accepted the compliment anyway, then wriggled, then stared at the empty stretch of desk where the fruit had been, as if checking the threat had actually left the building.

Julian came out of an exam room then, saw her holding the cat with a leftover sharpness still at the corners of her eyes, and stopped.

"What happened?"

Tang answered first, and told the workplace revenge scene with full color commentary. Gestures. The fruit. The voice note. A reenactment of Dumpling's slap that involved both hands.

When he finished listening, his eyes were on Eve's face.

"You okay?"

She'd thought she was fine.

The question made her nose sting. She sniffed, aiming for light, missing by a little. "I'm great. I just suddenly realized people are sometimes worse than cats. Cats at least don't bring pears."

"Normal," he said. "Cats don't usually show up with fruit to poach your playbook."

She laughed. It came out shaky, then real.

It was strange.

Some hurts sat behind her teeth for years. Nobody was allowed to touch them. Once they got to Julian, they went a little dull. He didn't give speeches. He didn't say she was strong. He just stood there with a chart in his hand, asking if she was okay, like that was a medical fact and not a compliment.

Dumpling headbutted her chin, then looked at the treat jar on the shelf with the patience of a contractor submitting an invoice.

Eve got the jar down. Two treats. He ate both, then went back to the cat tree, done with human resources for the day.

Julian stayed a minute longer than the conversation needed. Not hovering. Just there, chart still in his hand, like the hallway had become a waiting room on purpose.

"If she comes back," he said, "you don't have to handle it alone."

"She won't." Eve wiped her palms on her jeans. "The cats have a standard. She heard it."

"Still."

"Still," she agreed, because arguing with a man holding a chart after a fruit-bag ambush felt like extra labor she was not going to clock.

The fish tank hummed. Monday kept being Monday. The empty stretch of desk stayed empty, which was the whole point.
