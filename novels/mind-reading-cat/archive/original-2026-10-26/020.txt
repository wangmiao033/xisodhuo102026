


If the soft opening was mostly a success, the only hiccup was the man who came in near evening.

Early thirties, shirt, watch, here to ask "how do I stop my dog from destroying the house." The question was normal. What wasn't: from the second he walked in, his eyes barely left Eve's face.

And the way he talked had that adult-man I-think-I'm-charming familiarity. The kind that leaned on a counter without asking if the counter was tired.

"Eve is the owner?"

"A partner, more like."

"That's impressive. You don't look that young." He smiled, set his phone on the table, screen up, like the phone was also joining the meeting. "Can I add you? If my dog acts up later, I can ask you directly. Faster that way."

Professional smile. She had a professional smile. It had paid rent before. "You can add the shop account. We check it."

"I'd rather add you."

The air paused. Even the little welcome bell seemed to think about ringing again, then didn't.

Eve kept the professional smile. Behind it she was already writing the polite no: we use the shop account for follow-ups, it's faster, it's also not my personal WeChat, please do not make me say the last part out loud. She had said versions of this at the old company. It never felt less sticky.

Before she could find a politer no, a hand reached over and pushed the work QR stand forward, calm. The stand scraped, small, final.

"Follow-ups go here."

Julian.

Standing beside her, not much on his face, voice mild.

That mildness, somehow, did not feel safe to mess with. It felt like the voice he used right before a shot, when the animal had not yet realized the shot was coming.

The man looked at him, smile going awkward. "Oh. Sure."

The atmosphere got delicate. Eve kept her hands on the intake form so they would have a job.

Dumpling had been napping on the reception counter. He opened his eyes, stared at Julian for a few seconds, and his tail swung, pleased, like someone had finally used the correct door.

When the client left, she pretended to sort papers, holding in a smile. The papers were already sorted. She unsorted them a little so the pretending would look real.

"Julian, were you a little scary just now?"

"No."

"You looked like you were about to vaccinate him."

"I just told him to add the work account."

"Then why didn't you smile?"

Two seconds of silence. He recapped a pen. The pen did not need recapping.

"No need."

She finally lost it. Her mouth lifted, bit by bit. She wanted to laugh. She also wanted to win.

"Were you jealous?"

"No."

"Really?"

"Really."

"Dumpling's wagging his tail."

He looked down at the cat, fully here for the show, pupils pleased. A muscle jumped at his temple.

"He's a cat, not a lie detector."

She laughed until her shoulders shook. "He's better than a lie detector. Cheaper, too. We should put him on the service list."

"Let me ask differently. Julian, would it bother you if someone asked for my personal contact?"

This time he didn't answer immediately.

He looked at her, face calm, the tips of his ears going red. She watched the ears. The ears were honest. The mouth was in management.

"It would."

She had to look at the QR stand so she wouldn't look too pleased. The QR stand had not expected to be a third wheel, and yet.

"Why?"

"You know why."

Not loud. No performance of feeling.

That kind of honesty was the most lethal. It left her with nothing to tease, which was unfair, because teasing was how she stayed standing.

The urge to poke him had been flipped on her. Her throat clicked. She coughed, trying to stay even, like this was still about accounts.

"Then I'll be honest too."

"About what?"

"Today a woman guest complimented your hands. Said you look like those cold TV doctors..." She glanced up, aiming for casual, missing by about a street. "I minded a little too. Which is stupid. Hands are for work. I still minded."

He paused.

Then smiled, very lightly. The small one. The dangerous one.

"Eve."

"Mm?"

"Are we jealous of each other right now?"

She'd meant to deny it, a little tougher. Partner. Shop. Professional boundaries. She had slides for this, somewhere, in a former life.

The words got to her mouth, and she found she didn't want to pretend. Pretending was extra labor, and she had already mopped in her head for later.

She lifted her chin.

"Yeah."

After that "yeah," the shop seemed to go quieter. She heard her own breath. She heard Dumpling's tail hit the counter once, like punctuation. She did not take the yeah back. Taking it back would have been extra labor, and she had already used up her pretending on the man with the watch.

Sunset came in through the glass, laying a thin gold on the floor, catching the edge of a cat tree, making even the sanitizer bottle look expensive.

He stood in that light, looking at her, a small smile showing up like it had been waiting behind his teeth.

"That's good."

"What's good about it?"

"Means it's not just me."

Her ears went hot. She looked away, then back, because looking away forever would also be an answer.

At this rate, "wait a little longer" might not wait that long. She thought about the opening rush, the invoices, the auntie who wanted a husband for Joy. None of it was a cold shower. All of it was still her life. The life just had this extra heat in it now, sitting next to the register.

***

After close, they stayed to finish cleaning.

She mopped. He wiped counters. Dumpling, dutiful, walked two rows of plum-blossom paw prints across the floor she'd just done, as if the floor had asked for a signature.

She pointed the mop at him, furious. "Are you helping, or making it worse?"

He sat, extremely innocent. Wet paws. No remorse. Chief of Hospitality, currently in interior design.

Julian laughed, head down, wiping a spot that was already clean.

"Don't yell at him. He worked hard today."

"He worked hard?" She pointed at the prints. "His hardest job was patrolling and eating snacks. I saw the snack. The snack was not a rumor."

"And blocking other people's flowers."

She paused. The mop hovered, unemployed.

He was wiping the table like it was nothing. The tips of his ears were red again. She wanted to press on the sentence. She also wanted to keep it, like the photo she had not deleted.

"He did well."

She suddenly had no words. The good kind of no words. The kind where if she spoke, she would make a joke, and the joke would be a little too true.

She kept mopping. Her mouth lifted, slowly. The paw prints stayed. She mopped around one on purpose, then hated herself, then mopped it.

In the corner of the event area, the lights flickered.

Then the whole shop went black.

"Power out?"

"Probably the breaker."

Dark dropped over them. She'd been fine, then her toe caught a box, and she pitched forward. The box had been waiting all day for this moment. Boxes were like that.

She didn't even get a sound out. A hand caught her waist, steady. Fingers firm. Not grabbing. Catching.

"Careful."

Julian.

With sight gone, everything else got loud.

She could feel the heat of his palm at her waist, the familiar clean scent on him, breath very close. The mop handle bumped her knee. Somewhere, a fridge made a dying little sound and gave up.

She didn't dare move.

"You okay?"

"...Yeah."

"Can you stand?"

"No."

One second of silence after that.

She heard it late — how much it sounded like clinging. Like she had filed a request to stay in the dark. She was about to fix it — I mean the floor, I mean the box, I mean I have a toe — when he answered, low.

"Then I won't let go yet."

That did it. Her pulse actually went off-script. She wanted to laugh, a small panicked laugh, and swallowed it, because laughing into his collar would be a choice, and she was currently choosing to be a person with a shop. His hand was warm. The mop handle was stupid against her knee. She thought, very clearly, about the breaker, and then about not thinking about the breaker.

In the dark, someone laughed.

Very light.

Very close.

Dumpling meowed from somewhere, not a cute meow. A wet-paw meow. A why-is-the-floor-like-this meow. A reminder that there was a third living creature in the shop, and that creature had standards about lighting.

Only then did Julian let go, phone flashlight on, heading for the breaker. The beam caught a corner of her sleeve, then didn't.

When the lights came back, she was still standing there, face hot like a low fever all over again. She picked up the mop because the mop was a prop, and props helped.

He turned, saw her red ears, and the smile in his eyes went deeper. Not soft. Specific. Like he had found the answer on a chart and was not going to announce it yet.

"Eve."

"Mm?"

"If you keep looking like that, it's going to be hard for me to keep waiting."

She looked up. The overhead lights were too bright, suddenly, like they had opinions.

He stood under them, still that contained face, voice low and serious.

"Give me a little more time," he said. "Once the opening rush is over, I have something I want to say to you properly."

She stared at him, and after a while managed a small "oh."

Just an oh.

She still couldn't press down the sweetness in it. She thought about the QR stand, the man with the watch, the word yeah. She thought about rent, because rent was still due, and somehow rent was not the loudest thing in the room.

Dumpling sat on the floor and shook one paw, offended.

The lights were back.

The floor was still slightly damp.

Nobody had apologized to the paws.

He flicked his tail once, as if to say: you two can have your later. I am going to sit on a dry chair, and if the breaker does that again, I will bite it.
