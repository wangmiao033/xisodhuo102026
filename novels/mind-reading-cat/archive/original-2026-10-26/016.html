Three days after looking at the shop, Eve and Julian started the most unromantic romantic activity of their lives.

Math.

Rent, utilities, basic renovation, furniture, cat trees, partitions, event chairs, sanitizing gear, cameras, signage, promo...

Eve held her laptop and got quieter the longer she looked. The sheet had colors. Colors meant she had tried to be an adult. The colors were not helping. The red cells in particular had a personality. She had renamed one column "please no" and then renamed it back, because Julian would see it, and she still wanted to look like a person who could run a shop.

He sat across from her, calm, like numbers were animals and he had already checked their gums.

"What is it?"

"Nothing." She stared at the sheet, soul leaving her body in an orderly line, taking the wifi with it. "I just suddenly understand why couples who are fine start fighting the second they talk renovation. Money makes people sober. Money also makes people want to lie down on the floor."

He glanced at the budget.

"It's still in range."

"Your range and my range may not be the same range." She was bleak. "My current range is beef rice or chicken-leg rice for lunch. That is the entire stock market I live in. If the beef place is closed, I experience a recession."

He laughed. Short. Mean, in a fond way.

"Which one do you want?"

"Beef."

"Then beef for lunch."

"Don't use CEO voice for a twenty-eight-kuai decision. I will not be managed at the rice level."

"I'll redo it." He sat up straighter, very serious, the same face he used for explaining medication schedules. "Eve, given your company's current operations, we are prepared to add one braised egg as additional investment."

She laughed onto the table. Forehead, then dignity, then neither.

"Deal. I'm in. Write it down. I want it in the minutes."

Dumpling sat at the edge, watching two adults budget six figures and then lose their minds over an egg. He buried his face in his paws.

Humiliating. For the cat. He had been present for real conversations today, about deposits and cameras, and then they had done this. He would remember.

Beyond the budget, style was worse.

Eve wanted warm and bright. Walk in and think, I want to sit down, drink something, zone out with a cat, maybe forgive my group chat.

Julian wanted clean and practical. Cat-and-dog areas had to be easy to clean. Equipment had to look professional. Not a selfie museum. Not a place where a golden retriever could destroy three thousand kuai of "vibe" in twelve seconds.

They stood in front of swatches and sample boards and had their first actual disagreement. The boards were innocent. The boards still looked smug.

"I'm not asking for a pink princess castle." She showed him the tablet. The tablet was on her side, obviously. "I just think if the space has zero warmth, why wouldn't people just stay at the clinic? The clinic already has that 'please don't touch the disinfectant' energy. We don't need two of those."

"I didn't ask for an operating room." He pointed at another board. "A lot of this looks good and dies the second a cat scratches it or a dog tail wipes it. Then we pay again. Then you look at red cells again. Then we eat chicken-leg rice in defeat."

"Yours is too cold."

"Yours is too busy."

"Mine has life in it."

"Mine saves us from paying for a redo."

Three seconds of quiet. The kind of quiet where both people hear themselves being right, which is the most useless kind of right.

Tang walked by with milk tea, immediately took three steps back, unwilling to die in the crossfire. The milk tea survived. Tang intended to as well.

Director Chen, thrilled, like a long-running show had finally updated after a hiatus.

"Fight. You'll be closer after. My wife and I did tiles for three months. Still married. Mostly."

Eve did not turn her head. "Director. Close your mouth."

Julian, at the same time: "Director. Don't help."

Director Chen clicked his tongue.

"See? Chemistry. Even the refusals match."

In the end, Dumpling ended the style war, which was embarrassing for two people who had gone to school.

That afternoon they were still waving two proposals around when he jumped out of his bed, stepped on Eve's warm beige cushion sample, then squatted on Julian's gray anti-scratch mat.

He looked at them while he sat.

The expression was: why can't adults have both? Also: this mat is acceptable. Do not make me move.

Eve went quiet first.

So did Julian.

After a moment she said, slow, "Actually... warmer up front, cooler in the work zones. That's not impossible. That's just... zoning. People zone. Cities zone. We can zone."

He nodded. "Fine."

"Light beige sofa, but a scratch-proof fabric."

"Fine."

"Corkboard and shelves on the wall."

"Fine."

"Consult-room lights not too white. Softer. I don't want anyone crying under interrogation lighting. We are not the tax bureau."

"Also fine."

She squinted. "Julian, why weren't you this agreeable ten minutes ago? Was the mat the missing stakeholder?"

He looked down at Dumpling, sitting between the samples like a master who charged by the hour.

"There was no mediator then."

She laughed. It came out easier than the argument had.

This was actually... good.

Not that they never disagreed.

After every disagreement, they both took a step toward the middle. Not a speech about teamwork. Just a step. She could live with a person who took steps.

That night she was still thinking about the little fight, which was rude of the little fight, because she had other things to think about, like whether the camera package included a cable nobody had mentioned. She made tea. She didn't drink the tea. She opened the sheet again, closed it, opened it. The beige was still beige. His gray was still gray. Dumpling snored like a small machine that had already voted.

She'd been worried, a bit. Partnerships died on clashing ideas. If every little thing turned into this, the business might grind them down before the feelings even started. She had seen it at the old company. People who liked each other in the pantry and hated each other in the budget meeting. She did not want this to become that. She also did not want to be the only one who cared about warmth. The wanting sat in her throat and made her check her phone too often.

Then he texted, late. The lock screen lit the pillow.

Sorry about today.

She blinked.

Why are you apologizing?

My tone was a little hard.

She stared at it for two seconds, then smiled, stupid, into the dark. He could have said the beige was impractical. He had said his tone. That was a different invoice.

Then make it up to me next time.

The second she sent it, she got nervous.

Too flirty?

Too close to whining?

She was about to unsend. Her thumb hovered like it was standing on a roof. He'd already replied.

How?

Her ears burned. She went all in, because backing up now would look like she had a strategy, and she did not have a strategy. She had a craving.

Buy me cake.

Okay.

That fast?

Because I already wanted to.

She held the phone and rolled on the sofa, silently, twice. Once for the cake. Once for already. The sofa creaked, unsupportive.

Dumpling watched her go feral like a monkey who'd forgotten evolution, and covered his face with his tail, deeply unimpressed. He had mediated fabric today. He had not signed up for night theater.

The next afternoon, Julian actually showed up with a small cake.

Not a chaotic influencer number. A plain strawberry cream slice, one bright strawberry on top, sitting in a box like it had a job.

Tang's eyes went laser. Tang had a nose for other people's desserts. It was a talent, and a workplace hazard.

"Julian, why only one?"

His face didn't move. "Because it's not for you."

Tang made a wounded sound, the kind of sound that wanted an audience and did not get one.

When Eve took it, her fingers were careful with the box, like the box might file a complaint. The cream smelled like sugar and a bad decision she was going to make anyway. Tang was still standing there, waiting to be included. Eve did not include her. She carried the box to the break room like it was confidential.

She asked, quiet, because Tang was still in range if you counted the hallway, and also because her throat had gotten interested: "If you make it up to me like this, aren't you scared I'll push it?"

He looked at her.

"You can try."

She forgot the next sentence. Completely. It left her. She could feel it go. She looked at the strawberry instead, which was safer, and also red, which was not helping.

She was blushing at an illegal rate lately.

At this pace, the shop might not open before she got admitted for excessively healthy circulation. She would leave Dumpling a note: if I die of cake, avenge me by sitting on the gray mat.

Dumpling sniffed toward the box, decided cream was a human problem, and went back to sleep.

He had done enough work for one renovation.

Eve ate the cake standing up, which was undignified and also the only way she trusted herself not to say something worse than try. The strawberry was too sweet. She still finished it. Julian washed the fork. Tang, from the hallway, said "I smelled cream" like a legal claim, and Eve said "workplace confidential," which only made Tang louder.

On the way home she texted him: the cake was good.

He replied: I know.

She stared at I know until the bus moved, then put the phone away, smiling at nothing, while Dumpling in the carrier judged the entire city for being this extra.
