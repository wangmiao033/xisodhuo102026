After We Start Dating, the Cat Gets Demoted First

The biggest change dating made in Eve was not the kissing, or the "wait for me," or even the fact that she now owned a man who would dry her hair without being asked.

It was that she started sharing things on instinct.

Funny stuff used to go to Su Li first. A dog that looked like a mop. A bakery that smelled like someone had melted sugar on the sidewalk. A video titled "why your cat stares at you at night (he might be on night watch)." She'd send it, wait for Su Li to roast her, and feel like the day had been properly filed.

Now it went to Julian first.

Not because she'd decided to be a girlfriend. Because her thumb just... went there. Before her brain signed off.

The chat with him sat at the top of the list like it had always belonged there. It had not. It had climbed. Eve noticed and did not demote it. Demoting it would have been a kind of lying.

He wasn't a big talker. He replied like he meant it, which was worse.

That dog looks like Tang's ruined bangs.

The third tip in that video is wrong.

I passed the bakery. I'll bring you some tomorrow.

She laughed into her phone so hard the woman in the next elevator shot her a look. Eve coughed, put the phone down, picked it up again. The bakery one had a photo attached: egg tarts in a paper bag, his thumb in the corner, like he'd forgotten to crop himself out.

The elevator dinged. Someone's perfume was too sweet. Eve stared at the thumb longer than was reasonable.

A thumb. She was losing to a thumb. She put the phone in her pocket, took it out at the next floor, and stared again.

Su Li's review, after three days of this: "It's over. You're in early-stage romance brain."

"This is normal couple communication," Eve said, offended on behalf of all couples.

"Then why did you run out of the shower halfway through to reply?"

"Because he said 'open the door, I have something.'"

"And?"

"I thought it was something serious." She paused. "He brought a box of fresh egg tarts. I was still dripping. I signed for egg tarts in a towel."

The hallway had been cold. The tarts had been hot. She remembered the paper box warming her palm and the water running down her calf and the fact that she had said thank you like a person receiving a delivery, not a person receiving a boyfriend.

Su Li was silent two seconds, then held her heart like she'd been stabbed in a drama.

"I can't watch this."

If anyone was hit hardest, it wasn't Su Li.

It was Dumpling.

He had become extremely clear: since these two got together, his household rank was under attack. Not in a vague, cat-is-dramatic way. In a spreadsheet way. He could feel the numbers moving.

Most direct evidence: Eve used to hold him on the sofa and watch shows at night. He'd sit on her stomach like a paperweight and judge the plot. Now she held her phone, video-called Julian about tomorrow's event flow, and used Dumpling as a wrist rest. A wrist rest. He had a name.

The phone was warm. Her voice went soft in a way it used to save for him and freeze-dried chicken. He heard "table on the left" and "don't forget the water bowls" and did not care about water bowls. He cared that the hand on his back was not petting. It was using.

Julian used to pet his head first, every visit. Now the first thing was whether Eve was tired, whether she'd eaten on time, whether that leftover salad in her fridge was still a salad or already a science project.

Dumpling was extremely unimpressed.

He decided to act.

Move one: steal the person.

One night they were on the sofa looking at a floor plan. Some event layout. Arrows. Little squares that meant tables. Eve's pen was in her mouth. Julian's hand was on the paper, pointing.

The lamp made a warm circle on the paper. The room smelled like printer ink and the tea she'd forgotten to finish. Their knees almost touched. Dumpling noticed. Of course he noticed.

Dumpling launched, landed exactly between them.

Not just between. He made a point of pinning Julian's hand. Full body. Warm, heavy, official. The kind of sit that said: this meeting has a new chair.

The paper wrinkled. Julian's fingers disappeared under orange fur. Eve exploded laughing. "What are you doing?"

He looked at her, looked at Julian, then lay down, slow, like he'd booked the slot.

Talk if you want. Don't forget who actually lives here.

Julian didn't pull his hand out. He used the other one to scratch behind Dumpling's ear, which Dumpling accepted as tribute and did not count as a win for the man.

The scratch was good. Dumpling would not say so. Saying so would weaken the filing.

Move two: fake injury.

One night Julian was heating milk in the kitchen. The pan made that small, cheap tick of warming metal. Dumpling let out a tragic meow at the door. Not the usual I-want-snacks meow. The long, broken, I-have-seen-war meow.

Eve didn't even get her shoes on. She went in socks, nearly slid, grabbed the doorframe.

The floor was cold. Her heart did a stupid jump, the kind that belonged to real emergencies, not snack politics.

The cat was fine.

Just lying next to his bowl, face saying I have suffered a great injustice, and I will not speak of it. One paw draped over the rim like a fainting auntie.

The bowl was empty enough to look sad and full enough to be a lie. She crouched and checked him for ages. Ears. Belly. The weird spot on his tail he liked to pretend was broken. "Where does it hurt?"

He patted the snack cabinet, which was half empty.

A soft thump. Precise. A PowerPoint slide with one bullet.

She sat back on her heels. Opened her mouth. Closed it. Looked at the cabinet, then at him.

"You—"

Julian laughed out loud behind her. Actual laugh. She turned. He had the milk in one hand and the other over his mouth, like he'd been trying to stay professional and failed.

Steam from the milk made the kitchen look briefly expensive. It was not. It was her kitchen, with a cat unionizing in it.

"He's protesting," he said.

"Protesting what?"

"That we've been ignoring him."

She blinked. Then she laughed until her shoulders shook and she had to put a hand on the floor. Of course. Of course the tragedy was snacks. She picked Dumpling up and kissed his head, hard, the way you kiss someone who's being ridiculous and also a little bit right.

His fur smelled like the sofa. Warm. Slightly dusty. Very Dumpling.

"Fine. Chief of Hospitality cannot be demoted."

He accepted the compensation, barely. Chin up. Eyes half-closed. A cat who had negotiated a raise and would still file a follow-up.

Move three was bigger.

That night she worked late, came out of the shower too tired to keep her eyes open. Her hair stuck to her neck. The bathroom fan was still rattling. The mirror was fogged in a stupid oval. Julian was at her place looking at a new event plan, glasses on, which made him look like he'd been born in a clinic and never left.

The apartment was quiet except for the fan and the paper. She liked that quiet. She also liked that he was in it.

He saw her yawning while she dried her hair, took the towel, and stood behind her to do it himself.

Not fast. Light. The towel smelled like her shampoo and a little like his shirt, which was a problem she was not going to examine.

Warm. Slow. The kind of care that made her want to go boneless and also to take notes, as if this were a service she might need to invoice later, which was insane.

She went lazy against the chair. Her eyes almost closed. She thought, very unromantically, that if this was a scam she would still sign. Then she thought: don't say that out loud, you sound like an idiot.

The next second, Dumpling charged in from the hall, Julian's slipper in his mouth, and ran.

Fast. A blur of orange-and-white and one sad indoor slipper, heading for the bedroom like it was a getaway car.

The slipper made a wet, ridiculous slap against the floor. Julian's hands stopped on her hair.

Eve froze half a second, then laughed so hard she almost slid off the chair. The towel ended up in her lap. Water ran down the back of her neck and she did not care.

"What's he doing?"

"Claiming territory," Julian said, very calm, like this was a case. "Or he's just jealous."

"A cat, jealous of you?"

"Why else would he only steal my slipper, not yours?"

She couldn't straighten up. She held the table. Wiped her eye with the back of her wrist. Finally, breathing weird: conclusion.

"Julian."

"Mm?"

"You have officially joined this household's palace-intrigue system."

He looked down at her. Mouth curving. Not a big smile. The small one that meant he'd filed the sentence somewhere private.

The glasses caught the kitchen light. She could see her own damp hair in them, which was unfair, because she looked like a person who had been cared for and then robbed by a cat.

"Do I have a chance?"

She tipped her head up. Her hair was still damp. She could see the kitchen light in his glasses.

"You do."

"How much?"

"Depends on your performance."

He bent and kissed the top of her still-damp hair. Didn't make a speech about it. Just did it, like checking a temperature.

"Then I'll try."

In the doorway, Dumpling turned around, slowly, slipper still in his mouth. The slipper dripped a little. He looked at them the way a middle manager looks at two employees flirting during a fire drill.

Great.

He'd spent half the night defending his rights, and these two used it to flirt.

This house was not going to have a place for him.

He took the slipper under the bed anyway. Insurance.
