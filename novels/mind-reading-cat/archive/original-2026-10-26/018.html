After the fever broke, the first thing Eve did was replay what Julian had said that night.

Wait a little longer.

I don't want you thinking I moved in on you.

The line was lethal.

It wasn't a confession that knocked you out. It was more annoying than that. It sat in her head while she brushed her teeth. It sat in her head while she checked the shop group chat. It sat in her head while Dumpling stepped on her bladder at 6 a.m. like a manager with no HR.

Su Li listened, hugged a pillow, and was silent for a full five seconds, which for Su Li was a religious experience.

"I hereby announce."

"Announce what?"

"Julian has passed the modern gentle-man exam. High score. Extra credit for not doing the speech while you were throwing up. Very rare. Very sexy. I am disturbed."

Eve laughed into her water.

"Can you be normal."

"I am normal." Serious. "You're the one who isn't."

"Me?"

"You smile at messages. You smile when you hear his name. Dumpling steps on your face and you smile first, then yell." Su Li counted on her fingers. "That's not a crush anymore. That's in. That's 'I would share a charger.' That's marriage in this economy."

Eve's reflex: "It's not that dramatic."

Her mouth, however, was honest, and it wanted to smile again, so she bit the inside of her cheek like that was a professional skill.

It was.

And not only was she in. She had started having some extremely unhealthy thoughts.

For example: she wanted to kiss him.

Not a movie kiss. Not rain. Just his mouth, in passing, like a thing you could do between checking a delivery and arguing about balloons. The thought showed up while she was brushing her teeth and again while she was answering a DM about cat diarrhea, which felt disrespectful to the DM, and also accurate.

The first time it showed up in the real world was the day she went back to the clinic, finally well, still a little floaty, pretending she had not texted I might be dying over noodles.

He was at the sink, sleeves rolled, water running over his knuckles. The side of his face when he looked down was clean, nose straight, lashes long. Even washing his hands looked like a celibacy-themed male-lead promo. She hated that sentence. She still thought it.

She stood in the doorway, watching, and her brain offered, with no shame and no application form:

That mouth would look good to kiss.

She shocked herself.

Turned around on the spot, scooped Dumpling, and inhaled cat like a crisis response. Dumpling was warm and offended. Cat smelled like cat. It did not help as much as the internet said animal therapy helped.

Dumpling had not consented. He nearly puffed.

"Meow!"

Very clear meaning.

Are you sick. Again. I just got off night shift.

The second time was Julian tying balloon ribbon in the event area, head down, concentrating like the ribbon had a pulse.

The third was him leaning in to show her a floor plan, finger on a little square that would be the consult room, close enough that she forgot what a consult room was for.

The fourth was him saying, "You look good today," in the same voice he used for "the lab results are normal," which made it worse, because he did not sound like he was trying.

She said thanks. Thanks was a stupid word. She went to the storage room and stood in front of a shelf of saline like the saline might explain her.

Eve discovered that liking someone had no logic.

It didn't file an application. It didn't make a case. It didn't even CC her.

It just showed up, in some ordinary second, and made you want to bite someone. Or kiss someone. Same family of unwise.

Of course she would not bite anyone.

The most she could do was think it, while maintaining the dignity of an adult woman who paid rent and knew how to send invoices.

Until that night, when they stayed late at the shop to watch the renovation finish.

The workers had just left. Boxes and packing all over the floor, that new-furniture smell in the air, sharp and optimistic and a little toxic, like a future you could get a headache from. She was on the ground opening a box of cat toys, and pulled out a pair of fuzzy cat-ear headbands.

She stared.

"What is this?"

He glanced. "Vendor freebie."

"Why does a cat tree come with cat ears?"

"Maybe they thought customers would need them."

She looked at the ears, then at him. A stupid idea stood up in her head, put on shoes, and walked to the front.

"Julian."

"Mm?"

"Help me out."

"With what?"

"Put them on."

His hands paused. "What?"

She held up the headband, sincere, as if sincerity could launder a crime.

"Just for a second. I need to see the effect. If we do events later, somebody has to try them, right? We can't sell a look we haven't quality-checked. That's operations. That's ISO. That's me being professional."

He went silent.

The silence said: is your fever still in there. Also: I am a licensed veterinarian. Also: I am going to lose.

She held in a laugh and kept going. "Be professional, Julian. This is for the business. Think of the shop. Think of the sign. Think of Dumpling's brand alignment."

He looked at her bright eyes, and finally lost. He took the headband. His fingers were careful with the fuzzy part, like it might sue.

"One second."

"One second."

Three seconds later, Julian stood in the middle of the new shop with black cat ears on, face blank.

Eve bit her lip so she wouldn't hit the floor laughing.

Too much.

Also too cute.

A man who usually measured every love-adjacent sentence, currently wearing cat ears, ears so red they might drip. The contrast was illegal. She wanted to take a photo. She also wanted to look away, because looking felt like stealing.

The damage was illegal.

"One photo." She lifted her phone.

"No."

"Please."

"No."

"I swear it stays private. Locked album. No story. No Tang. I will take this to my grave, which, based on this week, might be soon."

"Eve."

"Julian."

They stared for three seconds.

He folded first, looking away, very low: "Hurry up."

Her throat clicked. The phone almost shook. She locked her elbows like she was photographing a crime scene, which, in a way, she was.

When she hit the shutter, he was under the lights, black ears making that already-clean face even more of a contrast, tips of his ears red, eyes holding a kind of forced, helpless indulgence. He looked like a person who had agreed to a vaccine and then been handed a sticker.

She looked at the photo, and suddenly couldn't laugh.

Her pulse was going too hard.

Hard enough that she almost didn't want to stay where she was. Hard enough that she thought about the rent, the sign, the unopened boxes, anything with a number, and the numbers did not help. She had a picture of Julian in cat ears. She was going to die with this picture. She was also not going to delete it. Those two facts sat together and did not fight.

He put out a hand. "Done. Delete it."

"No."

"You said it wouldn't leave this room."

"Right. Not leaving this room doesn't mean I can't keep it. Rooms are large. Phones live in rooms. This is legally adjacent."

He paused.

"Keep it?"

She heard herself. Her brain went hot. The urge to walk it back stood up, polite, ready to save her.

She was about to walk it back when Dumpling climbed out of a box and stepped on Julian's pant leg, as if the pant leg had been the assigned meeting point all along.

The headband went crooked.

The ears got cuter.

Eve lost the fight, and lunged to fix it.

Too fast. He looked down at the same time. Their foreheads knocked.

"Ow."

"Ah."

They both covered their heads. The distance, suddenly, was ridiculous.

Close enough for breath to tangle. Close enough that she could see the grain of his sweater. Close enough that she forgot, for one stupid second, how to stand like a person who owned part of a shop.

She looked up.

He looked down.

Everything else went quiet.

Lights. Boxes. Wood floor. One cat sitting beside them, fully here for the show, tail wrapped around his feet like he had bought tickets.

She could feel her pulse in her ears.

If he leaned in even a little, she might actually kiss him. Not as a joke. Not as a test. As a decision her mouth had already made without copying her.

Then Director Chen's broken gong of a voice arrived at the door.

"Aiyo, I forgot my key—"

The rest died.

He stood in the doorway, looking at the scene, at Julian in cat ears, at two people almost pasted together. A man who had come for a key and received a web novel.

The air froze.

Three seconds later, Director Chen clapped a hand over his eyes and turned around.

"I saw nothing! Continue! The key can wait! I am old! I have seen worse in this industry!"

The door slammed. The slam had opinions.

Eve stood there with her mouth open, then shut it, because open was not a strategy. She could still feel the spot on her forehead. She could still feel the almost. Director Chen's footsteps retreated down the hall, too fast for a man who had forgotten a key, exactly fast enough for a man who had seen cat ears.

Julian took the headband off in one move, like removing evidence. The fuzzy ears hung from his fingers, still guilty.

Dumpling licked a paw, unhurried, then looked at the door, then at the two of them, then back at the door.

Good.

The director had the key now, probably.

The story, unfortunately, had legs.

Dumpling stretched, claimed the nearest box, and sat in it like a king who had just watched a play and would not be leaving a tip.
