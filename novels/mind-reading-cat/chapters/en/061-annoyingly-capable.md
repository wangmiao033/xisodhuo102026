# Chapter 061 — Annoyingly Capable

Claire moved the reflector six inches and made Julian look as though he had been designed for daylight.

Eve stood just inside the clinic's side room with a folder against her chest. She had come to drop off a signed copy of the general-information permission, not to inspect somebody else's shoot. Tessa had told her to leave it on the spare desk.

The spare desk was currently supporting a camera bag, two batteries, and a mug whose owner had remembered to put it beyond the cables.

“Sorry,” Eve said. “I'll come back.”

Claire looked up from the monitor. “No need. We're between takes. Give me a second to clear that corner.”

She lifted the bag, making space without disturbing the camera. Julian stepped out of the mark on the floor and greeted Eve with the same easy expression he had worn over noodles on Sunday.

That should have helped.

Instead she noticed that he looked easy here too.

Sunday had been good. The vinegar came separately, Julian remembered that she preferred a seat away from the speaker, and they spent almost an hour discussing a terrible sign outside a furniture shop. Eve had paid for neither meal nor explanation. He had invited her, and she had accepted. There had been no need to settle what to call it.

Now Claire said, “When you say first appointment, look just beside the lens. Not at me. You're correcting my expression again.”

Julian smiled. “Your expression is very concerned.”

“I'm listening through headphones. Everyone looks concerned through headphones.”

He returned to the mark.

Eve put down the folder very carefully.

Claire was filming a short introduction to preparing for a routine appointment. Tessa had arranged the session as part of the clinic's broader communication work. Eve knew that. She hadn't been promised every piece of work in the building simply because she had once made a useful video there.

Knowing it did not prevent the small hot feeling beneath her ribs.

Claire asked Julian to begin again, this time answering the question as if someone had asked at the desk. He did. His first sentence became shorter and more natural. Claire waited through the pause rather than cutting in to rescue it.

“That,” she said. “Keep that pause. People need time to understand what you're asking them to bring.”

Eve would have said something similar.

This was somehow worse than Claire doing it badly.

Tessa came in with a card showing the clinic's booking details. She checked the text with Claire, then pointed to a line on the shot list.

“We're not showing the appointment screen.”

“We don't need it. Hands and the blank example card are enough.”

“Good.”

No one had to give a long explanation. Claire had read the brief. She knew which surfaces weren't hers to film, and she checked the corridor before changing the angle toward it.

Eve had admired those habits when they met at the creator event. She could remember sitting beside Claire, learning to look at the bracketed terms of an agreement rather than only its headline fee.

Apparently admiration was less comfortable when it stood beside Julian holding a reflector.

“Would you mind passing the tape?” Claire asked.

Eve handed it over.

“Thank you. The cable has developed ambitions.”

A loose section had lifted near the edge of the room. Claire secured it, tested the clear walkway, then stepped back. Julian waited instead of offering to solve a technical problem he hadn't been asked to solve.

Eve realized she was watching the shape of their cooperation, not merely the film.

She could leave. Her errand was finished. Remaining in the room would not become professional research simply because she had opinions about the framing.

“I'll let you work,” she said.

Julian turned. “Are you going far?”

“Home. I have an edit.”

“See you later?”

“Perhaps.”

The word came out cooler than she intended.

His expression altered slightly. Eve could not tell what he made of it, and the inability to tell irritated her all over again.

Claire thanked her for the folder. Tessa walked with her to reception, where two clients were arriving separately with carriers. Eve moved aside to avoid the doorway.

“Everything all right?” Tessa asked.

“Yes.”

The answer was true in every practical respect.

“Claire's session should finish on time,” Tessa said. “It's nice not to be chasing a filming slot around appointments.”

Eve heard an accusation that wasn't in the words. She almost replied that her own sessions had respected the clinic's time too.

Then she looked at Tessa's face. Her friend was already watching the arriving clients, not assessing Eve's record.

“Good,” Eve said. “I'm glad.”

Outside, she walked half a block before admitting she didn't need to hurry. The edit waiting at home wasn't due that morning. She had invented urgency because leaving briskly felt less humiliating than leaving for no reason except that she disliked how she felt.

At the crossing, she stopped beside a woman with a shopping trolley. The woman was arguing into her phone about whether a shelf described as narrow could still be too wide.

Eve found herself wanting to tell Julian.

She didn't, immediately. Sending him a funny observation was normally pleasant. Sending it now to check whether he would choose her message over Claire's camera would turn something she liked into a test he hadn't agreed to take.

At home, Dumpling was asleep in the canvas bag. Eve folded the handles outward so he couldn't catch a leg through them, then went to her desk.

She opened the edit and found an actual problem: the opening image was attractive but didn't explain what the viewer would see. She tried three different first shots. The third worked. For ten minutes she forgot entirely about the clinic.

Then a message arrived from Claire.

*Good to see you. Sorry we filled the whole room. Could I ask whether you have capacity for a small joint project next week? Paid, with a defined role. No need to answer until you've seen the brief.*

Eve read it twice.

The generous interpretation was simple. Claire thought she could do useful work. The less generous one involved several motives Eve had no evidence for and an absurd belief that a joint project might be a way to supervise her.

She put the phone down.

Dumpling emerged from the bag and jumped onto the chair beside her. He sniffed the edge of the desk, then stared at a reflection moving across the wall.

“Don't look at me like that,” Eve said.

He wasn't looking at her at all.

She laughed once, reluctantly.

There was no nearby stranger for him to read, no way for him to interpret Claire through a message, and no reason to use an ordinary cat's attention as a verdict. The problem in the room belonged entirely to Eve.

She wrote back asking for the brief, the intended audience, and the proposed scope. She did not accept or refuse before knowing those things.

Claire replied that she would send them that afternoon. Then she added:

*The question is about helping owners describe an ordinary appointment without turning the animal into a dramatic before-and-after. I thought of your walking project with Mabel.*

Eve looked at the message for a long moment.

Mabel had not been the easiest subject to film. She had corrected Eve, slowed her down, and refused the version of herself that would have made an obvious story. Claire was naming precisely the part of that work Eve was proudest of.

It would have been convenient if she had said something foolish instead.

Eve finished the edit and sent the agreed version to her client. She made lunch, ate it at the table, and remembered the folder she had left at the clinic with an unnecessary amount of care.

Julian messaged just after two.

*Did you get the edit finished?*

She answered yes.

*Good. I thought you seemed in a hurry earlier.*

There it was: a chance to make her mood his responsibility. She could say she hadn't wanted to interrupt, hoping he would insist she never could. She could ask whether the shoot went well in a tone that made the wrong answer dangerous.

Instead she wrote:

*I was distracted. It wasn't anything you did. How did the filming go?*

*Well. I said “please call” six different ways and apparently two were useful.*

*An impressive conversion rate.*

*Claire was very patient.*

Eve let the sentence remain on the screen without answering for a moment.

He was allowed to appreciate someone else's patience. Claire was allowed to be good at her job. Neither fact removed Sunday's noodles from the world.

*She knows what she's doing,* Eve wrote.

It felt slightly like biting into a lemon, but it was true.

She went out for milk before the brief arrived. At the shop, two cartons stood beneath different prices. Eve picked up the one she usually bought, put it back, and spent a full minute comparing quantities she already understood. The shopkeeper asked whether she needed anything else.

“Apparently a simpler brain,” she said.

He looked at the milk. “We don't stock that.”

“Worth asking.”

On the walk home, she passed the clinic's side window. The filming equipment was gone. A cleaner was moving a mop across the floor where Julian had stood. The room had returned to its ordinary use without preserving the importance Eve had assigned to it.

She kept walking.

At her building, Nora was speaking to a customer beside the entrance, arranging a collection for later in the week. Eve greeted them without stopping to check whether the new time suited her own project report. The small conversation would exist whether she witnessed it or not.

Back upstairs, she put the milk away and found her tea cold. She made another cup. At least that problem had a direct, proportionate answer.

The brief arrived late that afternoon. Eve opened it on the laptop, where there was enough space to read without being interrupted by her own reflection in the phone glass.

Claire proposed a short owner-led film, no medical outcomes promised, with separate clinic information where appropriate. She wanted Eve to conduct the preparatory interview and shape the story. The fee was clearly marked as a proposal, with time for them to discuss it.

Eve read the document once for facts and once for the things her mood had tried to insert between them.

There was no claim on Julian. There was no instruction to make Eve smaller. There was a sensible project, a capable colleague, and a feeling she hadn't yet given an honest name.

She saved the brief and began a reply with two practical questions.

Before sending it, she deleted the sentence that offered an extra unpaid interview to prove how good she could be.

Claire had asked for her work. Eve could at least meet that request without making it a competition nobody else knew they were in.
