# Chapter 093 — I Need to Explain Something

At seven, Eve put the blue cups back in the cupboard.

She had not intended to arrange a demonstration. She had taken them out because seeing them might help her explain the old afternoon with Sophie. Then she had imagined Julian arriving to find three objects in a row and a cat waiting beside them, as if his evening had already been assigned a purpose he hadn't agreed to.

The cups returned to their shelf.

She left the notebook closed on the table. Beside it lay the new page, folded once. The kettle was filled. There was nothing else she could prepare without beginning to decorate her own anxiety.

Dumpling had chosen the open carrier under the sideboard. Eve left the route clear and did not coax him out for the occasion.

Julian arrived at seven-thirty, wearing the same coat he had worn yesterday. The familiarity made her want to discuss dinner leftovers and forget why they were meeting.

“Tea?” she asked.

“Yes, please.”

He sat where she indicated, not at a table set for an examination but on the sofa with enough room for both of them. Eve brought the cups over on a tray. They were ordinary ceramic cups, which felt like an unnecessary distinction only she understood.

She sat in the chair opposite him.

“There's a sentence I need to say before I find another way around it.”

“All right.”

Eve put both feet on the floor.

“I think Dumpling sometimes reacts to things people are thinking or feeling before they say them. Not everything. Not clear sentences. I can't prove that's what happens. But I've been behaving as though there might be something there, and you should know that.”

Julian's hand stopped beside the cup.

He didn't laugh. She hadn't known how much of her preparation had been built around the possibility until it failed to occur.

“What do you mean by reacts?” he asked.

“Moves toward something. Stops somebody. Looks in a direction at a particular moment. Things that could have ordinary explanations separately, but sometimes seem much too precise to me.”

“Has he done anything different recently?”

“No sudden change. This is something I've wondered about since after I brought him home. I'm telling you now because keeping it from you has started changing the explanations I give you.”

She could see him considering the words. Not agreeing, not rejecting. She wanted to interrupt that difficult middle state with an example so perfect that he would have to understand.

There wasn't one.

“I need to separate two things,” she said. “What I've seen, and what I've decided it means. I'm not very good at keeping them separate when I want an answer.”

Julian picked up his tea, then set it down again without drinking.

“Tell me what you've seen.”

Eve opened the notebook on her own lap. She didn't hand it over. Some pages contained ordinary work notes mixed with her observations, and she hadn't brought him here to browse other people's lives.

She described the early moments cautiously: Dumpling interrupting her at a point when she had been about to answer a message too quickly; his attention landing on an object she was already considering; the strange feeling that he responded to an unspoken urgency in the room. She said plainly where looking back had supplied a connection she had not recorded at the time.

“He didn't find my work backup,” she added. “I did. He interrupted me, and I remembered where I'd saved it. Those are different events. Sometimes I've told myself the first one explains the second more neatly than it does.”

Julian nodded, but she couldn't tell which part he was acknowledging.

“You haven't heard words?”

“Not a voice telling me somebody's complete thought. I don't have a transcript from him. Mostly I have a cat doing something, and then myself trying to turn it into information.”

“Mostly?”

“The impression can feel very specific. That doesn't make my interpretation reliable. I don't know how to describe that without making it sound as if I've verified more than I have.”

She took a breath. The room was quiet enough to hear a vehicle stop below the window.

The page on her lap had a neat arrow linking two observations. She remembered drawing it, pleased by the connection. Now she could see how the arrow made a claim the sentences themselves did not support. She turned the notebook slightly away from her own gaze.

“I've also remembered the striking moments better than the ordinary ones,” she said. “There are whole afternoons when he sleeps and nothing unusual happens. I don't want to leave those out because they make a worse story.”

Julian nodded for her to continue.

“I once decided his avoidance meant somebody was unsuitable. Talking to the person showed me I was wrong. I won't give you their private circumstances. What matters is that I made a judgment the cat couldn't justify.”

“And you changed it?”

“Yes. But having learned that didn't stop me wanting the shortcut again.”

Dumpling shifted inside the carrier. Both of them glanced toward the small rustling sound. Eve looked back first, unwilling to turn his timing into another exhibit.

She told Julian about the cups. Sophie had agreed to help with a brief, harmless observation, knowing what Eve wanted to watch. No food beneath the cups, no secret questions, no filming. They had recorded movements rather than treating every glance as a score.

“The first two contacts matched her choices,” Eve said. “Then he touched a different one. Then none. He also investigated more than one cup on the first turn. I wanted to count the beginning and forget the rest.”

“Did you control where you both looked?”

“No. Not well. We changed places, but that didn't remove all the cues. It wasn't a proper test of an explanation.”

“Does Sophie know you're telling me?”

“She knows I've wondered about him, and she agreed to that observation. I haven't asked her to explain it to you. I'm telling you what I did, not anything private she might have been thinking.”

He accepted the distinction with a small nod. Eve made a mental note to tell Sophie that she had discussed the exercise, without asking her to help persuade him.

They had also tried silent choices while Sophie was elsewhere on the phone, Eve explained. There had been no identifiable response to the choices. Ordinary sounds through the speaker had attracted the cat's attention.

“I don't think he can reach people through calls or photographs. I don't even know how much of what happens in the room I'm supplying myself.”

Julian leaned back. “What would make you decide the explanation was wrong?”

Eve looked down at the notebook.

It was a fair question, and not one she could answer by listing the moments she liked best.

“I don't have a single rule that settles it,” she said. “I know that makes it difficult. I've started writing ordinary explanations beside the things that struck me as strange, including when nothing happens. I can't honestly tell you I've resolved them. I can stop treating the uncertainty as permission.”

“Permission for what?”

She unfolded the new page.

This was the part she had wanted the first part to make forgivable.

“To watch him for answers about you instead of asking. At the table the other day, when you paused over the card, I looked at him because I thought his reaction might tell me what you didn't say. It wasn't just that he moved.”

Julian's expression changed then. A slight drawing together around the mouth, not theatrical disbelief.

“I asked you about that.”

“Yes. And I gave you an incomplete answer.”

She put the paper down. She didn't need to read what she had done yesterday.

“When you said you didn't want to discuss the work message, I nearly moved his cushion closer to you and asked a question to see whether he reacted. I stopped before moving it or finishing the question. You noticed me withdraw the question. You didn't know why.”

For several seconds Julian said nothing.

Eve held still. An explanation about loving him would not make the intention smaller. Saying she'd been frightened would be true, but it would also ask him to comfort the person admitting she had considered intruding.

“That is what worries me,” he said at last.

“I understand.”

“I haven't said everything I mean yet.”

“Sorry. Go on.”

He looked at the closed notebook, then at her.

“If I tell you I'm not ready to discuss something, I need that to remain my answer. Not the first stage before you try to find out another way.”

“Yes.”

“Whether the cat can do something unusual or not.”

“Yes. You're right.”

The words were insufficient, but arguing would not make them larger.

Eve rested her palms on her knees. “I won't use him to get around what you choose to tell me. No arranged questions. No positioning him to watch you. If I notice something, I won't turn it into an answer you're supposed to confirm.”

Julian looked down into his tea. It had cooled while neither of them remembered it.

“Have you told anybody else things you think you've learned about me?”

“No. I haven't made a record of your private thoughts as if I knew them. I don't know them. I've talked to Sophie about my own feelings, but I haven't given her a supposed account from him.”

“And your work?”

“I've had to correct myself there too. I don't use his reactions as an assessment of clients or adopters. The times I nearly did are part of why I don't want to keep hiding this.”

A noise came from the landing. Dumpling appeared at the carrier opening, looked toward the hall, and remained where he was. Julian didn't beckon him or inspect him. Eve felt an odd gratitude for the cat's freedom to have an unimportant evening.

“Would you like fresh tea?” she asked after a while.

“Yes.”

She carried the cups to the kitchen. Her hands were steady enough to fill the kettle, less steady when she set the tray down. Julian came as far as the doorway, not following close enough to make her feel cornered.

“I need to think about this,” he said.

“I expected that.”

She hadn't expected how much she would want to prevent it once he said it.

He stayed while the kettle heated. Neither attempted another account of the cups. Eve asked whether he wanted the same tea; he said yes. There was comfort in a question with an answer she could use without interpreting anything beneath it.

They returned to the sitting room. The notebook lay shut where she had left it. Tomorrow it might still contain observations she couldn't explain, and Julian might still doubt what she thought they meant.

She had not made the secret disappear. She had made it possible for him to respond to something closer to the truth.

For tonight, she waited while he took his first sip.
