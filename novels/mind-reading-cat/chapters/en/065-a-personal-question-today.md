# Chapter 065 — A Personal Question Today

Mabel objected to the word simple.

On Thursday afternoon, Eve sat beside her at the kitchen table and watched the rough cut pause on a neat image of the example notebook.

“Simple steps before your visit,” Mabel read from the screen. “Simple for whom?”

Eve took her hand off the trackpad.

“For somebody who already has a notebook and can get there,” Mabel said. “Some days the transport takes more arranging than the questions.”

“That is a fair objection.”

“I'm collecting those.”

Biscuit slept in his bed near the doorway. His legs moved once in a dream, then settled. Mabel glanced at him, saw nothing that required her to interrupt him, and returned to the film.

Claire had sent the cut that morning. Eve had watched it first against the agreed account, using the review time remaining after Tuesday's change of plan. Now Mabel was checking her own words in context. This was not a demand that she endorse advice for every animal or every owner.

They replayed the opening without sound.

“What about 'Getting ready for a visit'?” Eve asked.

“Less smug.”

Eve wrote it down.

The other comments were smaller. Mabel preferred the pause before her joke to remain, because without it she sounded as if she was criticizing the receptionist. She was happy with the notebook page. She did not want the unused arrival footage added later, even if someone decided that the film needed more movement.

“There isn't any,” Eve said. “We didn't shoot it.”

“A reliable way of keeping it out.”

Eve laughed and sent the consolidated comments to Claire. The clinic would check its own guidance separately. No one person's approval stood in for all the others.

Mabel pushed a tin across the table. “Take a biscuit before you leave. You look as if you've forgotten meals again.”

“I had lunch.”

“What?”

“A sandwich.”

“What sort?”

“Why is this harder than the interview?”

“Because you're giving less convincing answers.”

It had been a cheese sandwich, eaten while standing near the kettle. Eve accepted a biscuit, not because she had been exposed as an incapable adult, but because Mabel had offered something she wanted.

At the door, Mabel asked whether she had plans for the evening.

“Returning a clip to the clinic.”

“A small ambition.”

“It's a very useful clip.”

Mabel looked at her for a moment. “Then I hope it appreciates the journey.”

Eve refused to ask what that meant.

The clip was real: a second, larger one than the small clip Tessa had told her to keep. Tessa had lent this one to hold the example page open during filming, and it lay in Eve's bag beside the corrected running order. It did not, however, require a special trip after work. Eve could have left it at reception earlier or returned it another day.

Outside, she stopped beneath the hall's noticeboard and took out her phone.

Julian had said the previous evening that he expected to finish at six, though he could not promise it. Their conversation had begun with his complaint that a cupboard handle had come loose and ended with a discussion of food that did not involve washing three pans. Nothing in it invited Eve to interrogate him beside a consulting-room door.

She wrote: *If you finish with enough energy, would you like a short walk? I have something non-work-related to ask. No emergency.*

Then she read the phrase non-work-related until it began to look like a notice about pavement repairs.

She left it anyway.

His reply came while she was putting the phone away.

*Yes. I'll message when I'm actually out. I may still be terrible company.*

*I won't ask you to explain a cupboard.*

*That removes most of my current material.*

Eve smiled, then felt the full force of having made an arrangement she could no longer pretend was accidental.

She went home before meeting him. Dumpling had rolled the fabric mouse halfway under the bookcase and was sitting beside it, staring at the remaining tail. Eve retrieved it, checked that the toy was intact, and tossed it across the rug. He pursued it with sudden, undignified speed.

She watched him catch it.

There was no useful answer hidden in the cat's small victory. Julian was not there. Dumpling knew nothing about a conversation on a phone, and even beside Julian, a strong feeling would not become a complete explanation of another person's life.

Eve refreshed his water and put her bag on the table.

“I could ask about the cupboard,” she said.

Dumpling kicked the mouse with his back feet.

“A compelling alternative.”

She changed the blouse she had worn to Mabel's because there were biscuit crumbs inside the cuff. She did not dress for an imagined transformation. The blue scarf Julian had once forgotten was back with him; she had no borrowed object left that could conveniently explain why he mattered.

At six twelve, he messaged that he was outside.

Eve left Dumpling at home with his usual things and walked to the clinic. She returned Tessa's clip at reception. Tessa thanked her while sorting a small pile of forms, apparently unaware that the object had spent the afternoon carrying an unreasonable emotional burden.

Julian waited beside the gate, his work bag over one shoulder.

“Is the cupboard still standing?” Eve asked.

“Under supervision.”

“You were meant to bring other material.”

“I had twelve minutes.”

He looked tired, but not so tired that his smile seemed borrowed. They began toward the garden without a discussion of which route would make the occasion mean least.

At the crossing, a child dropped a mitten. Eve picked it up and passed it to the adult pushing the stroller. By the time the lights changed, Julian had moved aside to let the stroller through first. The delay was ordinary and therefore almost unbearable. She had arranged a personal question and now had to carry it across a road like everyone else's shopping.

“Good day?” he asked.

“Useful. Mabel caught a word we shouldn't have used.”

“Only one?”

“We're trying to finish the film, not test her limits.”

He laughed.

For a moment she saw him laughing beside Claire's camera again. The memory still pressed somewhere tender, but it no longer came with an accusation attached. Claire had done her work well. Julian had been comfortable with a colleague. Eve was the person who wanted information she did not possess.

They reached the garden's lower path. The bench nearest the entrance was occupied by two people sharing a bag of fruit, so they continued to the low wall beyond the beds. Julian set down his bag and rolled his shoulders once.

“Do you want to sit?” Eve asked.

“Yes. Without it becoming a comment on my age.”

“I'll save the comment.”

“For later?”

“For a weaker moment.”

They sat with a reasonable amount of space between them. Eve could hear a bicycle bell on the street beyond the hedge. Somewhere behind them, somebody was explaining to a child that the watering can was not a drinking bottle.

Julian waited. He did not supply a convenient professional subject.

“You said you had something to ask,” he said at last.

“I did.”

“Still want to?”

She appreciated the question so much that she almost used it to escape.

“Yes.”

He turned slightly toward her.

Eve looked at his hands resting on his knees, then made herself look at his face. This was not a question she could answer by collecting clues from the way he held a cup.

“Are you seeing anyone?”

The words were quieter than she had expected. They were also unmistakable.

Julian blinked. For a second his expression was simply surprised.

“No,” he said.

Eve nodded as if he had confirmed a bus route.

“All right.”

The bicycle bell sounded again. The world appeared prepared to continue regardless of what happened to her.

Julian's mouth curved, not quite into a smile.

“Why do you want to know?”

There it was: the next sentence, which she had failed to prepare despite having spent most of the day pretending preparation was her profession.

She could say she was making conversation. She could blame Mabel, or Sophie, or some nonexistent person who had asked. She could turn him into a possible match for somebody else and spend the walk home hating her own ingenuity.

“I wanted to know,” she said.

He stayed quiet.

“Not for work,” she added, then closed her eyes briefly. “That was obvious.”

“A little.”

She opened them. He was still watching her, but there was no ridicule in his face.

“I don't have a polished next sentence.”

“You don't need to make it polished.”

“Unfortunately, I don't have a rough one either.”

This time he smiled properly.

Eve felt heat rise into her cheeks. It was uncomfortable, but it was not the same as being in danger. She had asked him a personal question. He had answered. Neither of them had promised anything he would have to defend tomorrow.

“Thank you for telling me,” she said.

“Thank you for asking me.”

She looked away toward the beds. That answer did not make everything clear. It did make pretending she had been conducting a casual survey impossible.

For a little while they sat without speaking. Julian picked up his bag when a couple came along the narrow path, leaving room for them to pass. Eve shifted her feet. The small interruption gave her something ordinary to do with her body.

“Would you like to keep walking?” he asked when the path cleared.

“Yes.”

They stood. He did not take her hand, and she did not interpret the absence as a verdict. There would have to be more words, at some point. She had brought the first ones.

At the garden gate, Julian asked whether she had eaten anything since lunch.

“A biscuit.”

“That sounds familiar.”

“Mabel investigated.”

“I wouldn't interfere with her findings.”

They stopped at the small takeaway counter beyond the crossing and bought something each could carry home. Julian asked whether she wanted sauce before adding it to her order, and Eve answered without treating the question as a sign she needed to decode.

While they waited, Claire's message arrived: the opening wording was changed, Mabel's pause restored, revised cut ready for the agreed checks. Eve thanked her, then put the phone away.

“Work?” Julian asked.

“Nearly finished.”

“Good.”

He passed her the paper bag the server had placed on the counter. Their fingers did not meet. Eve almost laughed at herself for noticing.

Outside, they reached the point where their routes separated. Julian wished her a good night, then paused.

“I'm glad you asked,” he said.

She held the warm bag against her palms.

“I'm glad I did.”

On the walk home, Eve still did not have the next sentence. For once, she let the one she had actually said remain enough for tonight.
