# Chapter 231 — Not Here to Take Him Away

Eve put the good cups on the table, then took one away.

Three people. Three cups.

She had counted correctly the first time. The fourth cup was a small, useless attempt to prepare for somebody she had not invited: a version of Nora who might arrive certain, entitled, and disappointed by everything she saw.

The woman on the telephone had sounded nothing like that.

Eve returned the extra cup to the cupboard.

Julian watched from the kitchen doorway without offering to count for her. He had arranged to be home for the visit because she had asked for his company. They had both finished the work that genuinely needed completing before the agreed hour. Nobody was waiting at the clinic or the venue while they pretended an afternoon was free.

“Do you want the photographs here?” he asked.

“Yes. Just the comparison ones.”

He set the folder beside her chair.

Dumpling lay in his familiar box, with his blanket pushed into a ridge against one side. Eve had nearly straightened it before remembering that the arrangement was his. Across the room, the wardrobe held the clothes from Saturday's photographs. The new private picture of him asleep among the wrapping paper was still on her phone.

Today did not require another photograph of him.

She had also moved a laundry basket out of the sitting room, then brought it back because it contained clothes she needed to fold. Hiding every sign of an ordinary household would not make the cat better cared for. It would only leave her with a basket obstructing the bedroom.

Julian found her standing over it.

“Would you like me to finish that?”

“Yes. Please. Not because anyone's coming. Because I keep starting things and abandoning them.”

He folded two towels while she put away the breakfast plates. The small division of work helped her arrive at the door as someone who lived here, rather than a person presenting an immaculate application to continue living here with her cat.

At the knock, Eve's stomach tightened.

She opened the door to a woman holding a canvas bag and an envelope with one softened corner. Nora looked from Eve to the number beside the door, checking that she had come to the right place.

“Hello. I'm a few minutes early.”

“That's fine. Come in.”

“Only if it is. I can walk round the block.”

“It really is.”

Nora smiled and stepped inside. She wore a plain coat with a loose button near the cuff. No carrier hung from her hand. Eve noticed its absence with such fierce relief that she immediately disliked herself for looking.

She introduced Julian. Nora shook his hand, then waited while Eve closed the outer door. They had agreed not to make an arrival into a reunion performance. The cat could stay where he was.

“He's over there,” Eve said, and wished she had not sounded as though she were pointing out the most valuable object in the room.

Nora looked toward the box.

“Oh,” she said quietly.

Dumpling lifted his head.

Nobody moved closer. Nora remained beside the chair Eve offered until he lowered his chin again. Then she sat, placing the canvas bag at her feet rather than in the walking route.

“He looks comfortable.”

“He has strong views about that box.”

“Good.”

It was an unadorned answer. Nora did not add that he had preferred a different bed, or that she knew how to make him come out. Eve poured water into the cups and found herself grateful for things that had not happened.

Nora took the envelope from her bag.

“I brought the prints, and the original files are still on my phone. The ones I sent weren't the whole set. Some have other people in them.”

“We don't need those unless there's a reason.”

“I thought so.”

She set out two pictures. The first showed an orange-and-white cat on a low shelf beside a folded towel. His head faced the camera. In the second, he was stretching away from a chair, with his white shoulder and forelegs clear.

Eve knew the shape before she made herself look at it properly.

The unequal white border near the face. The orange patch beside it. The shoulder marking that Daniel's clearer photographs had finally allowed them to compare. These pictures offered more than a tilted ear beyond donated cups.

Still, she brought out the current comparison photographs instead of carrying the certainty entirely in her chest.

Nora watched her place them together.

“That's why I answered Leanne,” she said. “Not just because he was orange.”

They compared the visible markings without forcing Dumpling to stand in the same pose. Julian sat beside Eve, close enough to see but leaving the papers in her hands. Nora showed the original file dates and the accompanying messages she had already agreed to share.

They preceded the rescue. They fitted the period of temporary care, rather than the later date when a print had been made. The relevant message gave a reason for the photograph: someone had asked how the cat was settling during a short stay.

Eve felt the difference between a possible resemblance and this accumulation of ordinary, matching details.

“It looks like him,” she said.

“Yes.” Nora touched the envelope's edge. “It does.”

Neither tried to make the cat respond to a name. The evidence did not become stronger if he walked toward the right lap.

Eve turned the second picture slightly. A corner of a blue cloth showed behind the chair. She wanted to ask whether he had liked it, whether he had slept there, whether someone had always remembered when he needed dinner.

Instead she heard herself ask, “Were you his owner?”

They had discussed this on the telephone. She knew they had. The question came anyway.

Nora did not seem offended.

“No. I offered temporary care. There were gaps between rooms being available, and sometimes somebody could keep a cat at home for a little while. I was one of those people.”

“And now?”

The words were barely louder than the cups settling on the table.

Nora looked at her carefully.

“I'm not here to take him away, Eve.”

Eve looked down.

“I know you said that.”

“I can say it again.”

A hot, humiliating feeling rose behind Eve's eyes. She had prepared sensible questions and a clear place for the records. She had not prepared to be told kindly that the visitor was not the threat she had brought into the room herself.

“I wanted to hear about him,” she said. “I still do. I didn't expect to be this frightened.”

Nora folded her hands around her cup.

“I was nervous about coming. I didn't know whether you'd think I was making excuses.”

“For what?”

“For not knowing enough. For having cared for him and then not being able to tell you the whole thing.”

Eve looked up.

Across from her sat a woman with an old envelope, not a complete explanation disguised as a stranger. Nora's loose cuff button had caught on the rim of the bag. She freed it absently and smoothed the sleeve.

“I don't know the whole thing either,” Eve said.

“No.”

The shared absence made the table feel less like opposite sides.

Julian asked whether either wanted more water. Both said no. He set the jug down without using the interruption to tell Eve how fortunate she should feel.

Dumpling stood in his box.

Eve's attention turned so quickly that Nora followed her gaze. The cat stretched, stepped onto the floor, and approached the chair nearest the window. He sniffed its leg, then Nora's shoe.

She kept her hands on her knees.

“Hello,” she said, in the ordinary voice she had used at the door.

Dumpling inspected the hem of her coat. Eve could see his whiskers move against the fabric. He stayed there longer than he usually stayed beside an unfamiliar visitor, though she immediately knew she was choosing which visits to remember.

Nora lowered one hand, stopping well short of him.

He sniffed her fingers, turned his face, and rubbed once against her knuckles.

Eve's chest tightened again.

There was nothing harmful in it. Nothing had been removed from her. She knew that, and still felt the startled possessiveness of someone seeing an ordinary gesture she had thought belonged especially to home.

She made herself keep her hands still.

Nora did not scoop him up. When he moved away, she let her hand return to her knee. He walked beneath the table and settled beside Julian's foot, apparently finished with the available inspection.

“He always had a serious face,” Nora said.

Eve nearly asked whether that meant she was certain. Then she heard the sentence for what it was: a fond recollection, not another piece of identification.

“He still does,” she replied. “Particularly when we misunderstand a request.”

“Which is often?”

“According to him.”

They laughed quietly. Dumpling remained beneath the table, unconcerned by the small improvement in human relations.

Eve reached for the envelope.

“May I look at the other picture you mentioned?”

Nora nodded and brought out a photograph of the cat lying beside a low basket. It was not a beautiful image. Part of the foreground was blurred, and a cleaning cloth had fallen onto the floor at the edge. The cat's eyes were half closed.

Eve had imagined his earlier life in sharp extremes: either a comfortable home she had no right to improve upon, or a sequence of empty rooms waiting for somebody to blame. This photograph contained a floor that needed tidying and a cat who seemed, at that instant, inclined to sleep.

It could not tell her every night had been like that.

It did tell her one such instant had existed.

“Where was this?” she asked.

“At my place, during the short stay. I can show you which dates I can actually support.”

“I'd like that.”

Nora drew a thin folder from the bag. Eve noticed she had separated a few pages rather than bringing every old message she owned. There would be gaps. There would be things nobody had thought to record because they were trying to get through an ordinary week.

For the first time since the knock, Eve wanted to open the folder more than she wanted the visit safely over.

She glanced at Julian. He met her eyes without supplying an interpretation. She rested her hand briefly on his arm, then turned back to Nora.

“I'm sorry I asked you the same thing twice.”

“It's a very important cat.”

“He is.”

“That doesn't mean you have to make a good impression on me.”

Eve laughed, and this time the laugh loosened something rather than concealing it.

She moved the unused cup saucer out of the way. Three people, a few photographs, and an incomplete story could fit on this table without anyone being required to prove who loved the cat most.

Underneath it, Dumpling changed position and rested his chin near Julian's shoe.

Eve left him there while Nora opened the folder.
