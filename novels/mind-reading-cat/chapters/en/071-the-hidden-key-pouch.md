# Chapter 071 — The Hidden Key Pouch

At eight forty on Wednesday morning, Eve found a teaspoon in her work bag and no key pouch.

The spoon was not the problem. It had come home inside yesterday's lunch container and evidently decided to establish a separate career. The pouch contained her spare flat key, a folded emergency note, and nothing that ought to have developed legs.

Her usual keys hung where she had left them. She touched them before doing anything else, irritated by the need to reassure herself. She could leave. She could get back in. What she could not do was stop picturing a small yellow pouch lying somewhere anybody might pick it up.

She emptied the bag onto the table.

Notebook. Charger. Clean handkerchief missing too. Two receipts, one pen, and the corner of a cracker wrapped so carefully that it had survived three journeys without becoming useful.

Dumpling watched from the sofa.

“Don't look pleased. You haven't been appointed investigator.”

He blinked.

Eve checked yesterday's jacket before she checked beneath him. She had learned that much from the glove she herself had put in the laundry basket. The pouch wasn't in the jacket. It wasn't in the closed drawer where she kept her spare supplies. She remembered putting it in the bag the previous evening, before going to meet Julian.

The thought of Julian made her briefly forget the key.

She had asked him for a real date. The conversation had actually happened. Somewhere in the coming weekend there would be an arrangement they made for that purpose, provided she could stop smiling at a radiator long enough to organize her ordinary Wednesday.

The phone alarm sounded.

Nine o'clock departure. Half an hour at the print shop to check the hall's corrected notices, then delivery to the coordinator. It was a small paid finishing task, not an emergency, but someone had reserved time for it.

Eve silenced the alarm and crouched beside the sofa.

The handkerchief was visible under the front edge. Farther back, yellow fabric lay beside an orange paw.

“Oh, for goodness' sake.”

Dumpling withdrew the paw.

She lay flat enough to see the pouch. It was zipped, with the key's shape pressing against one side. The cat hadn't opened it. He had dragged it, probably by the little fabric tab she had always thought unnecessarily large.

Her relief arrived wearing the clothes of anger.

“I need my things to stay where I put them.”

He flattened slightly at her raised voice.

Eve stopped. The sentence was true, but delivering it more loudly would not teach a cat how a print-shop booking worked.

She sat back and took one slow breath. Then she fetched his fabric mouse, moved it across the rug where he could see, and waited. Dumpling came out after a minute, not because she had explained property rights but because the mouse had briefly become more interesting than the pouch.

She retrieved both objects with the long handle of the dustpan, checked the zipper and key, and put the pouch in the high closed cupboard. She checked the handkerchief for loose threads before dropping it into the wash.

A dust ball rolled past her knee.

“That,” she told it, “can stay.”

By the time she had repacked, the nine o'clock alarm felt like an accusation even though it was no longer making a sound. She took her ordinary keys, checked Dumpling's water and the room, and left at nine oh two.

He followed her to the inside of the door.

She did not push him aside with her foot. She waited until he moved back toward the rug, stepped out, and closed the door carefully. One complaint came through the wood.

Eve stood in the corridor for a moment with her hand on the bag.

Then she went downstairs.

At the print shop, the first proof was the wrong size.

“I sent the final dimensions,” she said, opening her message.

The assistant looked at his sheet, frowned, and found the earlier version beneath it. “You did. I used the first attachment.”

They compared the dates and corrected the order before printing the whole set. It was a problem with a clear cause, which Eve found almost luxurious.

She checked the type with the coordinator's actual wording, not the wording she remembered liking best. The change about quiet collection hours was there. So was the contact route for people who couldn't use the online form. No photograph of Nora or Ben had been added to make the notice feel warmer.

“Do you want a cat on this empty bit?” the assistant asked, pointing to the lower corner.

“No.”

He looked surprised by the speed of her answer.

“Sorry. The blank space is intentional.”

“Fine by me. Less ink.”

While the machine warmed up, she sent the coordinator a message saying she was on schedule. She did not describe her household search. The morning's annoyance didn't need an audience to count as real.

At the hall, the coordinator asked Eve to hold the new notice against the board before they removed the old one. Together they discovered that the existing drawing pins had all bent in the same direction. Finding replacements took longer than approving the layout.

By eleven, the notices were up, the remaining copies delivered, and her work bag mercifully lighter.

Mia came through carrying a folded laundry sack for the volunteer collection.

“Did the dog problem get solved?” she asked.

“Some parts got better. Some parts are still work.”

Mia nodded toward the notice. “That sounds less impressive.”

“It is much easier to keep true.”

They held the cupboard door for each other while fitting the sack onto its shelf. Mia mentioned a coat she'd nearly bought because it looked professional, then decided it would make her unable to lift anything above her shoulders. Eve told her about the teaspoon. Neither offered a lesson.

At home, Dumpling greeted the bag before he greeted her.

He sniffed its bottom seam, pushed his head toward the side pocket, and looked up. Eve lifted it onto the table out of reach while she took off her coat. His attention followed the movement.

She had meant to eat lunch and finish an ordinary caption edit. Instead she found herself watching him watch the bag.

The carton. The gloves. The empty tote dragged toward the sofa. Julian's scarf beside the cat's bed. The scenes sat close together in her mind, almost arranging themselves into a story.

Almost was dangerous.

She got the older notebook out and read what she'd actually written. Not what she now felt she had known all along.

Watched when carton opened. Gloves under sofa. Dragged empty tote after packing started.

No idea why yet.

She put today's details beneath them: yellow pouch and handkerchief under sofa; normal keys untouched; took bag when leaving; followed to door.

She had not seen him carry the pouch. It mattered to write that too, although the orange paw and the location made him an excellent suspect.

At lunchtime she opened the bag to put the cleaned container away. The cat rose from the cushion and approached. She took out the teaspoon. He ignored it. When she lifted the empty cloth lunch wrap by one corner, his ears came forward.

Eve lowered the wrap onto the table, away from the edge, and went to the kitchen.

A soft scrape made her turn.

Dumpling had climbed onto the chair. He caught the overhanging corner of the bag's lining with his teeth and tugged. The bag moved an inch, then stopped against the notebook inside. He tried again.

This time she saw the effort: braced front paws, a pause to reposition, another awkward pull. It would look very funny framed tightly enough to exclude everything else.

“Leave that,” she said, walking back.

She lifted the bag without pulling against his mouth. He let go and sat down abruptly. The action had not trapped him, but she checked the lining for a loose thread before folding it inward.

She had left it within reach again. That part belonged in the notebook beside his behavior.

Eve put the bag inside the wardrobe and shut the door. Dumpling remained on the chair, looking toward the place where it had been. Then he climbed down and went to the sofa.

She ate lunch before taking the next step.

The clinic's contact form was open on her phone when she realized she had begun writing the word separation. She deleted it. She could describe missing items and the dragging she had seen without arriving with a diagnosis chosen because it fitted a photograph.

She sent the observations through the usual care channel, including that he was eating and using his litter tray as usual, and asked whether an appointment was needed or whether there were practical changes to try first. She would contact them sooner if his ordinary health or behavior changed sharply. A message about bags couldn't establish that every future problem was the same one.

Julian's personal conversation sat one line below the clinic's.

She opened it, closed it, then opened it again.

She did not want every uncertain part of her life to become his evening work. She also did not want to turn asking for a date into a rule that she could never ask him an ordinary question again.

*I've sent the clinic a description of Dumpling stealing small things from my bag. Nothing urgent that I can see. I may need help moving the narrow cabinet later, if you're free. Not a house call disguised as dinner.*

She read it once and sent it.

His reply came after she had finished the caption edit.

*I can help with a cabinet after work. I can't promise the cabinet will like my technique. Please don't move it on your own before I get there.*

*I was going to leave the heavy things where they are and move the bag.*

*Excellent technique.*

She smiled, then went back to the task she had already committed to finishing. The edit needed a clearer opening, not another hour of staring at the wardrobe.

Late that afternoon, the clinic replied with a request for a few practical details and an offer to arrange a fuller discussion if the behavior continued or intensified. Eve answered the questions instead of treating a personal visit from Julian as a substitute.

Then she cleared the narrow route beside the cabinet, removing a stack of magazines and two boxes of unused labels. The cat watched from his bed. She kept that bed exactly where it was.

The flat did not become serene. Her useful things still wanted more space than the room possessed. Dumpling still inspected the wardrobe door. Eve still had a few minutes of work to do before Julian arrived.

But the keys were safe, the morning's job was finished, and she had stopped calling every inconvenient act of devotion adorable.

She picked up the teaspoon from the draining board and put it in the cutlery drawer.

At least one missing object had now been explained without involving the cat.
