# Chapter 191 — Between Staying Over and Moving In

Julian came back with fewer clean clothes than he had taken away and a folder too thick to close.

Eve met him outside the station after the final week of his course. She had offered to come, checked her work could finish in time, and left the venue in Robin's capable hands rather than promising she could be in two places at once. He appeared through the crowd carrying his bag on one shoulder, scanning faces with the tired concentration of someone who had spent too long reading signs.

When he saw her, he stopped looking for the exit.

She went to him. His bag slid against her hip as they embraced, and the folder pressed inconveniently between them.

“Is that your new qualification?” she asked into his coat.

“My collection of things I still need to learn.”

“Very substantial.”

He kissed her, then moved the folder so he could do it without injuring either of them.

The training was finished. He had arranged time to unpack before returning to his usual clinic duties, and he had questions to discuss with colleagues rather than a plan to reorganize everybody on his first morning back. Eve wanted to hear about them. She also wanted to take him somewhere with a chair and no departure board.

“Dinner at mine?” she asked. “As discussed. Nothing impressive.”

“Excellent. I've had enough impressive diagrams.”

They stopped at his place first so he could leave his luggage, then went on to hers with the small overnight bag he had packed after she invited him to stay. He put the course folder beside his own desk. The large bag stayed there too.

Eve noticed the distinction. She had wanted him back, and wanting him back had already begun to sound in her head like wanting him home. Those were not quite the same request.

At her door, Dumpling inspected Julian's shoes before granting access to the hallway. He rubbed once against his trouser leg and then walked away to investigate the shopping bag Eve carried.

“Reunion complete,” Julian said.

“You brought no food he wants.”

“A tactical error.”

They put away the groceries. Julian knew which cupboard held the bowls. He reached for a clean towel and found the shelf occupied by a neatly folded set of sheets.

“New system?”

“My mother left those.”

“Ah.”

“She didn't leave instructions about who ought to be sleeping under them.”

“That sounds restful.”

Eve moved the sheets to the proper shelf and found the towel beneath them. The visit had left small evidence all over the apartment: a container to return, a packet of tea, a chair shifted slightly to catch better light. Julian's things had left evidence too, though he had been away. His spare charger sat beside hers. One book lay on the low table. A shirt she had washed after an earlier visit waited on the back of a chair.

Nobody had officially moved in. Her home was nevertheless beginning to contain a persuasive argument assembled from objects.

They ate at the little table. Julian described the final exercise, in which he had managed to ask a question and then wait for the answer without providing three possible responses himself. Eve asked what happened next.

“The answer wasn't one I'd prepared for.”

“A flaw in the method.”

“Apparently a feature.”

She told him about a guest who had improved the instructions exercise by drawing a cupboard from the wrong direction on purpose. Robin had used the mistake to let the group compare how much everyone assumed. Eve had watched without jumping in to explain the point first.

“We're becoming dangerously tolerable,” she said.

“I wouldn't announce it yet.”

After dinner, he washed while she dried. They moved around each other with the familiar small inconveniences of her kitchen. He opened a drawer against her thigh. She put a bowl where he was about to set a glass. Neither error ruined the pleasure of his being there.

Then he said, “I could leave the overnight things here. Save carrying them back next time.”

Eve's hands stopped around the bowl.

It was a sensible suggestion. That was why she didn't want to answer it too quickly.

“How many things?”

He turned off the tap.

“Clothes. Toothbrush. The usual things.”

“There are already usual things.”

He looked toward the room, then back at her. “Does that bother you?”

“No. Not exactly.”

The bowl was dry. She set it down rather than continuing to polish it through the conversation.

“I like you being here. I also remember how strange it felt when staying over turned into something we assumed. We fixed that by asking. I don't want to lose the asking because your charger has established residence.”

Julian glanced at the cable on the table.

“It is quite settled.”

“I think it's applied for a vote.”

He smiled, but he kept listening.

Eve leaned against the counter. She had begun this conversation by objecting to a toothbrush. That was not the part she actually wanted to discuss.

“While you were away, I kept finding your things. Sometimes that was lovely. Sometimes it made me wonder what we're doing next, and whether we're letting objects decide because talking would be more embarrassing.”

Julian dried his hands.

“Are you asking whether I want to live with you?”

“Yes. But not whether you can put your bag down and have it happen tonight.”

He stepped closer, leaving enough room for her to move if she wanted.

“I'd like to talk about it.”

Her relief arrived before the rest of his answer.

“I want to know what it would actually look like for both of us,” he continued. “Not just that I wouldn't have to go home after dinner.”

“That's a very appealing part.”

“It is.”

“And a very incomplete plan.”

“Yes.”

Something scraped across the floor behind them.

They turned. Dumpling had pulled one of Julian's socks from the open overnight bag and was dragging it toward his bed with industrious determination. The sock caught beneath a chair leg. He tugged again, offended by the resistance.

Julian looked at Eve.

“I see somebody has begun the process.”

“Somebody doesn't pay rent.”

She crouched, freed the sock from the chair, and offered Dumpling his familiar cloth toy instead. He ignored it until she stopped trying to interest him, then pounced on it while Julian rescued the sock and zipped the bag.

Eve watched the cat settle beside the toy. Once, she might have treated that little arrangement of belongings as an answer. The impulse still amused her. It no longer supplied information about what two adults could afford or how either of them slept.

“Let's talk properly,” she said. “Tomorrow, when you've slept. Not while you're grateful somebody has fed you.”

“I can remain grateful tomorrow.”

“You'll have to separate it from the housing question.”

They chose a time after breakfast and before Eve needed to work. It was an actual conversation slot, not a dramatic deadline for deciding their future. Julian would stay tonight because she had invited him, and go back to his own place afterward to finish unpacking. His tenancy, her home, and Dumpling's routine would all remain intact while they thought.

The following morning, Eve woke before she expected to and found Julian reading beside the window. He had put on the rescued socks. Dumpling occupied the warmer part of the sofa, having evidently negotiated a better arrangement than either human.

Over breakfast, Eve took out a plain notebook. She had resisted making a presentation.

“I nearly used headings,” she admitted.

“I support recovery.”

She opened to a blank page.

“What would you miss?” she asked.

Julian looked at her for a moment, then toward the quiet window.

“Being able to come home and say nothing without anyone wondering whether I'm angry.”

Eve nodded slowly.

“I'd miss working late without feeling that I'm hosting someone badly.”

“Even if I lived there?”

“Especially at first, I think. I'd keep trying to make the evening good.”

They wrote those down without turning them into failures of affection. Julian liked early mornings. Eve sometimes needed to sort images after dinner. Both wanted somewhere to put things without having to defend the placement every time. Neither wanted the other to become the default person answering work messages, remembering appointments, or entertaining visiting relatives.

While they talked, Julian moved the empty toast plate to the sink. Eve started to remind him that they had agreed to talk before clearing, then noticed he was bringing back the water he wanted. She stopped herself.

“What?” he asked.

“Nothing. I almost managed a trip to the kitchen for you.”

“I survived it.”

“Encouraging.”

They laughed, and she left the plate where he had put it. Sharing a room would apparently provide plenty of opportunities to practice leaving an ordinary action alone.

Then Eve asked what they hoped would improve.

Julian said he liked ordinary breakfast with her. He liked hearing something about her day while it was still fresh, without needing a journey first. He wanted to find out whether they could build a comfortable shared place rather than simply become very skilled visitors.

Eve felt warmth rise into her face.

“That sounds good,” she said.

“It might also involve arguing about shelves.”

“We have relevant experience.”

She wanted those ordinary things too. She also wanted a room she could close when a deadline required concentration, and permission to be uninteresting in the same home. Saying so made the future less glossy and much more attractive.

They did not choose an apartment that morning. They agreed to look at options, including their existing places, with actual costs and travel times. They would check housing terms, cat arrangements, and notice periods before committing to anything. A short trial stay could tell them something their best weekends could not. Either could say the timing wasn't right without having to pretend the relationship was wrong.

Julian drew a small line beneath the notes.

“One more thing.”

Eve waited.

“I don't want you choosing a place mainly because you think I'll be easier to live with there.”

“I could ask the same of you.”

“Then let's both ask.”

She closed the notebook. It contained no final decision, only questions they had both agreed were worth answering. Somehow she felt closer to him than she had when his toothbrush first appeared beside hers.

Before he left, Julian packed his shirt and the recovered socks. He left the charger because Eve said he could, not because its presence proved anything. They arranged their next evening, then kissed by the door while Dumpling watched from a safe distance inside.

Afterward, Eve put the notebook on the table beside the book Julian had left there. She would return to her own work in a moment. First she opened the page again and added a question she had been shy about asking aloud.

What would make it feel like mine as well as ours?

She sent Julian a photograph of that line. His reply came after he got home.

Let's make sure we both answer that.
