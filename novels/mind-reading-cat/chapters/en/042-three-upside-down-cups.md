# Chapter 042 — Three Upside-Down Cups

The cups had been purchased for an outdoor event and retained because Eve couldn't bear throwing away something that had survived the weather more successfully than she had.

There were four of them, thick blue plastic, slightly scratched around the rims. She took three from the cupboard while Sophie spread butter on her second piece of toast.

“Is this breakfast becoming a meeting?” Sophie asked.

“No. Possibly a very unimpressive game.”

“What are the cups for?”

Eve set them on the counter rather than arranging them around the cat. Dumpling was on his blanket, awake but comfortably unoccupied. She had waited until they had eaten and until Sophie had stopped checking the clock. Even so, asking seemed more awkward than she had expected.

“Sometimes he reacts just before I do something,” she said. “Or goes toward something I was only thinking about. I keep deciding that means more than it might.”

Sophie put down the knife.

“Like when you decided he was my emotional spokesperson?”

“Yes. Exactly that.”

Eve rubbed her thumb along a scratch in the plastic.

“I wondered whether you'd help me watch what actually happens. Pick one cup silently, without anything underneath. See whether he goes near it. We wouldn't hide food or ask you to think about anything personal. No filming.”

“You want to find out whether he's following a choice we haven't said aloud.”

“Or our eyes. Or whichever cup looks most interesting.”

“Or breakfast.”

“Breakfast seems a strong candidate.”

Sophie leaned sideways to look at Dumpling. He blinked and resumed studying the wall.

“I'll give your research institution ten minutes,” she said. “Provided nobody writes that I possess unusual brain waves.”

“Your brain waves remain off the record.”

“And if he goes away, we stop.”

“Yes.”

Sophie picked up her toast again. “Then let me finish this. I'm not having him interpret my thoughts about butter.”

Eve smiled, but the ease of the agreement made her recognize something she had almost done differently. She could have placed the cups down and watched Sophie without explaining the question behind them. It would have looked harmless. Yet Sophie would have been doing one thing while Eve quietly assigned her another role. This way her friend could laugh, refuse, or ask what Eve intended to write down.

They cleared breakfast together. Eve wiped the low table twice, removing the crumbs that might turn the whole exercise into a demonstration of feline housekeeping. Nothing went beneath the cups. They stood upside down in a row, with generous space around them and the route to Dumpling's usual bed entirely clear.

Sophie sat on the sofa. Eve sat on the rug with an old notebook, using a half-empty page rather than ceremonially beginning a new one.

“What counts as choosing?” Sophie asked.

Eve had imagined she knew until the question required words.

“Touching one?”

“What if he walks past and his tail hits it?”

“Not that.”

“What if he sniffs between two?”

Eve crossed out the heading she had just written.

Already the bright little idea had developed unglamorous edges. A look could point anywhere. Approaching the table might mean wanting the people seated beside it. They agreed to describe the movements instead of giving them a score. If he put his nose or paw directly against a cup, they could write which one. Anything less could remain less.

“Look at you,” Sophie said. “Resisting a points system.”

“It's making me physically uncomfortable.”

For the first turn Sophie chose the cup nearest the window and wrote it on a scrap of paper folded under her hand. Eve looked away while she wrote. They both returned their attention to the cat without calling him.

Dumpling stretched, came down from the blanket, and investigated the table leg.

Eve held her pencil still.

He walked to the window end of the row and put his nose against the blue cup.

Sophie's eyebrows rose. She opened the paper.

*Window.*

A thrill went through Eve so quickly that she had already begun smiling before she remembered how small the event was.

“Well,” she said.

“Don't purchase a laboratory yet.”

They waited while Dumpling sniffed the other cups too.

Eve looked at her notebook. She had written only the first contact. After a moment she added the others, in the order she remembered them.

The first line was still interesting. It simply wasn't the whole visit.

For the second turn Sophie chose the middle cup. Dumpling stepped up to it and hooked a paw around the rim, making it scrape the table. Eve felt her pulse jump again.

Then the cup rolled onto its side, and he followed the movement across the tabletop with the same cheerful absorption he gave a discarded receipt.

Sophie retrieved it before it fell.

“I may have picked the toy.”

“You might have.”

“Did I look at it?”

“I was watching him.”

“There's a convenient arrangement.”

Eve didn't like how much she wanted to preserve the two matching beginnings. She wrote down the rolling cup. Under possible cues she added that neither of them had been watching Sophie's eyes.

For the next turn they changed seats, so the cat would not always approach from the same end. Sophie chose the cup by the door. Dumpling put his nose to the window cup and sat down beside it.

Eve waited.

Sophie unfolded the paper.

“Door.”

“He may go to—” Eve began.

She stopped.

The sentence would let her stretch the turn until almost anything became a match. She wrote what he had done. The pencil made an unattractively heavy mark because she was pressing too hard.

Dumpling yawned.

On the next choice he paid attention to none of the cups. He climbed onto Sophie's lap, discovered that her knees were poorly arranged, and got down again. She left the paper folded until he was back on the floor.

“Middle,” she said.

Eve recorded no cup contact.

“What was your method?” she asked.

“Thinking about the middle cup. Then wondering whether I'd left my washing in the machine. Then thinking about the middle cup again.”

“So you weren't concentrating throughout.”

Sophie looked at her over the paper.

Eve felt herself wince. “That sounded as if I was assigning blame.”

“A little. Also, he's a cat beside three cups. He hasn't agreed to your definition of concentration either.”

There was nothing to contest in that. Dumpling had never been told what the paper meant, and they had invented no shared signal. Eve didn't even know whether there was anything in a deliberately selected cup to interest whatever unusual perception he might possess. Her suspicion concerned moments that arrived charged with feeling, not a human solemnly naming tableware inside her head.

They could observe something curious. They couldn't compel it to become an answer.

Dumpling turned away from the table and went to his blanket.

Sophie glanced at the clock. “Nine minutes. A conscientious employee.”

Eve closed the notebook.

They washed the cups in the kitchen. Sophie asked for the fourth one to drink water from, then objected to being given the only cup that hadn't had the opportunity of a distinguished academic career.

It was easier to laugh once the objects were ordinary again.

While Sophie dried them, Eve looked at her friend's face and nearly asked what had been occupying her thoughts between the chosen cups. The unasked question still had the same familiar shape. She let it pass. Sophie had told her about washing because she wanted to. That wasn't an invitation to trace everything else.

They talked instead about the mystery film. Sophie had checked the ending after getting home. The gardener was innocent; the solicitor had merely been wearing bad spectacles; the murderer was a cousin neither of them remembered meeting.

“An unfair assessment,” Eve said.

“Your specialty this week.”

“Deserved.”

At the door Sophie remembered she had left a parcel waiting at a collection counter. She checked its opening hours, adjusted the repaired handle of her bag, and asked whether Eve wanted to try the cups once while she was somewhere else.

“On the phone?” Eve asked.

“If the question is whether he follows a silent choice. I could call when I'm home. Two minutes, not another research breakfast.”

Eve agreed, then set the cups aside and got on with her work. The waiting didn't fill the morning. She corrected a caption that identified a buckle as a clip, sent the finished file, and heated leftover rice.

Sophie's call arrived after lunch. She was at home, with the parcel collected and the washing finally removed from the machine. The screen showed only the call controls; there was no face for Dumpling to watch.

“I've chosen one of the three positions,” she said. “Same arrangement as before.”

Eve placed the cups on the low table. Dumpling stood nearby, drawn from his blanket by the noise of plastic touching wood. Sophie fell silent. Eve did too.

The cat looked toward the phone when a faint clatter came through the speaker. Then he put his front paws on the edge of the sofa and climbed up, bypassing the cups entirely.

Eve described this rather than asking him to come back.

“Window,” Sophie said after their agreed short wait.

She made one other silent choice. Dumpling stayed on the sofa, his chin on a cushion. He glanced at the phone when Sophie spoke again, but there was nothing Eve could connect to either position.

“Did you choose?” Eve asked.

“Yes. Door this time.”

Eve wrote that down.

She had half expected the physical space between them to matter. She still felt the peculiar disappointment of watching an expectation become the only thing that happened. There was no identifiable response to Sophie's unheard choice from another apartment. A sound from a speaker had interested him. That was all she could honestly put on the page.

“Enough cups?” Sophie asked.

“Enough cups.”

“Good. I'm going to have a nap without submitting it for analysis.”

“Excellent plan.”

After the call Eve put all four cups back in the cupboard. She didn't repeat the exercise alone to improve its ending.

At her desk she drew a line between two columns. In the first she copied the movements she had actually seen: nose at one cup, then the others; paw against a rim; a different cup; no contact. In the second she wrote the explanations that had raced ahead of them.

The second column needed more room.

She added one sentence beneath both: *I become more certain when I want the result.*

That was a result she hadn't been hoping for, but it survived inspection better than most of the others.

Dumpling came off the sofa in the late afternoon and rubbed against her shin. Eve stroked his head, following the direction he leaned, until he moved away. She still remembered the strange moments. Three cups hadn't explained them away, and two early matches hadn't explained them either.

Her notebook remained open while she made tea.

For once, she let the empty spaces stay empty.
