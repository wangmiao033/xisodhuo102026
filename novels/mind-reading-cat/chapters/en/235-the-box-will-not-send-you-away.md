# Chapter 235 — The Box Will Not Send You Away

The box was meant for folded cloths.

Eve had written that on the side in large, useful letters. She had also checked that it was clean, removed the loose tape, and found a place where it could stand without blocking the passage.

Dumpling disagreed with the label.

He climbed in while her back was turned and sat squarely in the middle, the top of his orange head appearing above CLOTHS as if it were his professional title.

Julian noticed first.

“We have a packing error.”

Eve looked over the stack in her arms.

“Oh.”

The cat watched her.

A few evenings had passed since Nora's visit. The records were put away, the private album had returned to the shelf, and their wedding preparations had become practical enough to occupy part of the sitting-room floor. A modest celebration still generated an unreasonable number of things needing to be gathered, checked, and kept out of the way.

They were not moving house. Eve knew that. She could not make the cardboard convey it merely by explaining.

She set the cloths on a chair and crouched several steps from the box.

“Hello.”

Dumpling lowered his body slightly. His ears remained upright, but he did not look away from the room as he usually did when a comfortable object had been successfully conquered.

Eve felt the immediate urge to pick him up, reassure him thoroughly, and transfer him to the bed he was supposed to prefer.

She kept her hands on her own knees.

Julian put down the roll of paper he was holding.

“Do we need that box tonight?”

“No.”

“Then we can use something else.”

“That sounds almost offensively straightforward.”

“We should try it before I complicate it.”

They left the box open, without loose packaging or handles the cat could catch himself in. Julian brought a shallow reusable container from the cupboard for the cloths. Eve checked the contents against what they had actually agreed to take, not the growing list of things that might theoretically become useful.

The first rejected object was a bundle of spare ribbons.

“Why do we have these?” she asked.

“You put them in the pile.”

“That's not an explanation I find reassuring.”

They went back into a closed drawer, where nobody could turn them into an evening's emergency decoration or a cat's unsupervised entertainment.

Dumpling remained in the box.

At first Eve kept glancing at him after every small task. She could feel the new pieces of his history trying to attach themselves to everything he did. The borrowed rooms, Nora's short stay, the messages asking who had collected which carrier. A cat sitting among wedding supplies could become a perfect illustration of the story she had refused to tell online.

She did not know whether he was remembering anything.

She knew there were several unfamiliar containers on the floor and two people moving objects between them. That was enough reason to make the room less busy.

“Let's do one at a time,” she said.

Julian looked around at the spread of things.

“I thought this was one.”

“Then our definition needs work.”

They cleared the unused containers to the spare storage space, safely stacked where they would not fall. The walking route reopened. Eve stopped sliding things across the floor and carried them instead. The quiet corner stayed quiet.

Only then did she notice that the cat's familiar bed was still exactly where he had left it.

She had nearly moved it earlier to make a temporary staging area. The thought had felt efficient. Now she was glad she had begun with the table instead.

Its blanket lay in the crooked fold he had made after breakfast. Nothing important needed that patch of floor more than he did.

Eve returned to the cloths.

Halfway through counting, she discovered that two differed in size. One had been folded to conceal the discrepancy beautifully. She unfolded it, looked at Julian, and held up the offending rectangle.

“An ambitious square.”

“We don't need them identical.”

“No. We need to know before somebody tries to use them as though they are.”

They separated the different sizes and labelled the container plainly. The work continued without a crisis, though it took longer than Eve's original estimate. She amended the time rather than pretending they had finished because the plan said they should have.

From the box came a small scratching sound.

Dumpling turned once, pawed the clean bottom, and sat down again. Eve looked over but did not move toward him. The action might be an attempt to get comfortable. It might be unease. She would keep watching the cat she could see rather than treating the missing parts of his history as a translation service.

Julian went to the kitchen to begin their ordinary dinner. He was responsible for it tonight. Eve had already told him when she expected to finish packing, and he had told her what was possible without rushing.

She could smell something warming in the pan while she checked the last few items.

The sound of her phone startled her more than the cat. A message asked whether she wanted to add one more decorative piece to their arrangements. There was a photograph attached, attractive and entirely unnecessary.

For a moment she pictured it in the room, then pictured collecting it, storing it, paying for it, and returning it afterward.

She replied with thanks and a clear no.

The wedding did not become less real because she had declined an object.

She put the phone away and folded the last cloth.

Dumpling watched her place it in the reusable container. His tail moved once along the bottom of the cardboard box. She wanted to tell him that none of these things would replace him, that no one would pack his bed without asking, that his home had finally become a permanent answer.

Some of those sentences were promises she could live by. None could be understood simply because she said it with enough feeling.

Instead she took the finished container away and left his familiar places available.

The room grew quieter.

Eve sat on the sofa with the printed list, checking the few remaining tasks. She crossed out one that had been completed days earlier and circled another that genuinely needed a call tomorrow. The circle would wait until tomorrow too.

Julian came to the doorway.

“Food in about ten minutes.”

“All right.”

“Do you want to stop there?”

“Yes.”

She had not finished everything she could imagine doing. She had finished what they had agreed to do this evening. The difference left her with energy to notice how tired she was.

Dumpling was still in the box.

Eve set down the list and leaned back. She did not move dinner nearer him, rattle a packet to prove he would come, or ask Julian to watch while she created a second chance at a reassuring response.

At his normal mealtime, she went to prepare his food.

The bowl made its familiar sound against the counter. She measured the usual amount, set it in the usual place, and stepped out of the way. There was no tap borrowed from Nora, no special summons intended to establish that the past had lost its hold.

For a little while nothing happened.

Then a paw appeared over the edge of the box.

Dumpling stood, looked toward the kitchen, and stepped onto the floor. He paused beside the chair holding Eve's bag, sniffed it, and continued to his bowl.

Eve watched him eat from a distance.

“He came out,” she said softly.

Julian nodded from the counter.

“Yes.”

She wanted to make the sentence larger. He trusts us. He knows. He isn't afraid anymore.

Instead she said, “I'm glad.”

“So am I.”

They ate their own dinner while the cat finished his. The conversation wandered to an inconvenient grocery substitution and whether the kitchen chair had remained silent long enough to deserve an apology. Eve laughed when Julian gave it a cautious test with one hand before sitting.

She did not get up halfway through the meal to inspect the empty box.

Afterward, Dumpling returned to the sitting room. He stopped beside the cardboard, sniffed the edge, then walked past it to his familiar bed. He settled with one forepaw extended over the fold in the blanket.

Eve kept washing the dishes.

The little surge of happiness did not require her to turn it into an event for him.

Later, when he had left the area to drink, they moved the unused packing box into the storage space with the others. The cloths were already packed elsewhere. There was no reason to leave a new obstacle in the room simply because it had become emotionally significant for twenty minutes.

His own familiar box remained beside the bookcase. His bed stayed where it had been throughout the evening. They had removed a piece of temporary clutter, not dismantled his places to rest.

Before the lights went out, Eve checked that the packing materials were secured and that nothing small or sharp remained on the floor. Julian put tomorrow's call on the calendar. They left the rest for another reasonable stretch of time.

In the morning, Dumpling followed Eve to the door.

She had hoped, without admitting it, that last night's choice would carry forward as a clear new stage. Here he was beside her shoes, watching the work bag she had just picked up.

She crouched briefly to greet him, then stood.

Yesterday had not been a cure. Today did not erase yesterday.

She checked the usual arrangements and left for her work without adding another test departure. Julian would leave later according to his own schedule, and neither of them would arrange the whole day around preserving a single successful moment.

At the venue, Robin had put the next week's draft rota on the table for their planned review. There were places where Eve still needed to decide what she could genuinely hand over before the wedding. She sat down to work through them with the person who would actually do the work, rather than promising herself that happiness would organize the hours.

When she came home that evening, the packed cloths remained in storage. Dumpling was washing a shoulder on the sofa.

Eve put down her bag. He paused, looked at her, and resumed the task.

She liked being welcomed with no sense of occasion.

In the kitchen, Julian had left a note about the ordinary food they needed to buy. Eve added one item, then went to sit where the cat could approach or ignore her as he pleased.

The wedding was still ahead. There were calls to make, things to carry, and decisions that would require both of them. None of those preparations had taken away the bed in the corner.

For tonight, keeping that true was enough.
