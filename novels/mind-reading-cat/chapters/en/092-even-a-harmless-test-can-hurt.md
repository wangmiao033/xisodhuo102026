# Chapter 092 — Even a Harmless Test Can Hurt

On Wednesday, Eve bought too many tomatoes.

This became clear halfway through cutting them, when the bowl was full and a respectable number still remained on the board. The soup recipe had said one punnet. Apparently, punnets came in philosophical rather than reliable sizes.

She moved half into a container for tomorrow and put the knife down before answering the door.

Julian stood outside with bread tucked beneath one arm.

“I wasn't certain what you were making.”

“Neither am I anymore. But bread should survive it.”

He smiled and stepped in after she moved aside. Dumpling greeted his shoes with an investigation, then returned to the kitchen doorway. Eve made sure the cat remained clear while she went back to the stove.

The meal was supposed to be simple. Something to eat together, with enough time afterward that nobody needed to talk while rushing for a bus. She had invited Julian before deciding whether tonight would also be the evening she explained the notebook.

Now the notebook was in a drawer. The clean page was with it. She had written less than she expected and crossed out more.

“Can I do anything?” he asked.

“Cut the bread. Everything else has become a single process.”

He washed his hands and found the board she indicated. Eve stirred the pan, watching the chopped tomatoes release more liquid than the recipe seemed to anticipate. The proposed thick soup was developing the ambitions of a shallow lake.

Julian looked over.

“Dinner appears to be thinking.”

“I'm giving it time.”

“A generous approach.”

His phone vibrated on the counter where he had set it. He glanced at the screen, frowned, and turned it over.

Eve saw only the movement, not the message. She had no reason to inspect it.

“Do you need to answer?”

“Not now.”

He cut another slice. It was thicker than the first two. After a moment he put down the knife and adjusted the loaf.

She could have left the answer where it was. Not now was intelligible, and he had not asked her to hold the conversation open while he attended to something else.

Instead she watched his hands.

The newly agreed name for their relationship seemed to have made her more alert to every small inconsistency, not less. She wanted to be the person he told things to. The wanting was reasonable until it began treating every unshared thought as a door she ought to open.

“Something difficult?” she asked.

“Something I need to consider. It can wait until tomorrow.”

“Work?”

He looked at her.

“Yes. I don't want to bring it into dinner before I've worked out what I think.”

“All right.”

She meant the answer when she said it. Then Dumpling moved close enough to brush her ankle, and an uglier idea arrived with startling convenience.

She could move the cat's cushion nearer Julian's chair. She could ask a casual question, something broad enough to sound like conversation, and see what Dumpling did. Nobody would have to know it was a test. She wouldn't ask about patients or anything serious. Only whether Julian was worrying about the clinic, or them, or a choice he hadn't mentioned.

The thought dressed itself in so many reassurances that Eve nearly failed to recognize it.

She reached for the cushion.

Dumpling was not on it. It lay near the end of the sofa, where she had placed it to keep his usual retreat clear. Moving an empty cushion was an ordinary act. The reason behind her hand wasn't ordinary housekeeping.

Eve stopped with her fingers against the fabric.

Julian carried the bread to the table. “Here?”

“Yes.”

She left the cushion where it was and returned to the stove. Her face felt hot enough to be explained by cooking, an excuse she had no intention of offering aloud.

The tomatoes had softened. She added the cooked beans and measured stock, lowered the heat, and set a timer. There were several minutes in which she could do nothing useful to the food.

“Do you sometimes worry that—” she began.

Then she stopped.

Julian turned from the plates.

“Sorry,” she said. “That wasn't a fair question.”

“I haven't heard it yet.”

“I was going to ask it sideways because you already said you didn't want to discuss the message tonight.”

He put down the plate he was holding.

Eve forced herself not to soften the admission with a joke about being naturally inquisitive. There was no charming version of deliberately circling an answer somebody had already given.

“You don't have to explain it,” she said. “I'll stop.”

“Thank you.”

He said it without anger. She wished, briefly, that he sounded angry enough to give her a recognizable scene to apologize inside. His ordinary acceptance left her alone with the part he couldn't see.

They sat down when the timer rang. The food was edible, if not particularly faithful to its instructions. Julian took a second spoonful without being asked to endorse it.

“The bread was a good decision,” Eve said.

“It often is.”

For a while they talked about the meal. He asked which part of the recipe she would change if she made it again. She said she would begin by establishing diplomatic relations with the punnet size. The small joke helped her settle enough to taste what she'd cooked.

Dumpling came as far as the doorway and sat there, looking at neither diner in any notable way. Eve didn't call him closer. She could let the cat be a cat even while her own thoughts behaved badly.

Halfway through the bread, Julian said, “I know I can sound final when I'm still sorting something out.”

“You were quite clear.”

“I didn't mean you should never ask.”

“I understand. But you answered, and then I kept trying to find another entrance.”

He nodded slowly.

“If it affects an arrangement we've made, I'll tell you. If I don't know yet, I'll say that too.”

“That would help.”

She could ask for a practice between two people. She could not require access to everything that happened before he found the words.

Julian asked whether she wanted another slice toasted. Eve said yes, then discovered she had set the toaster too low for the thick bread. It emerged pale and warm, only slightly changed by its journey.

“Again?” he asked.

“Only briefly.”

They watched it with an attention the appliance didn't usually receive. Eve stopped the second pass before the edges blackened, split the slice, and offered him half. The crisp corner broke into her soup. She fished it out with the spoon before it sank, pleased by something she could rescue without making anyone explain themselves.

They finished eating and cleared the table. Julian washed while Eve dried, because her drying cloth was easier to locate than a second place to stand at the sink. She let him decide which plate to wash next. The desire to direct small things seemed especially embarrassing tonight, when she could see the shape it took on a larger scale.

A scrap of tomato skin had attached itself stubbornly to the pan.

“It plans to live here,” Julian said.

“Then it can pay rent.”

She heard the phrase and glanced toward Dumpling before she could help it. Long ago she had made the same kind of joke about him often enough to dislike its echo. This was a scrap of tomato, not a frightened animal, but she put the clean plate away more carefully anyway.

Julian left the pan to soak. They moved to the sofa with tea, sitting close enough for their knees to touch. Eve leaned against him briefly. He shifted his arm so she had a comfortable place, not a position she had to hold for the sake of the moment.

She liked him. That was part of the trouble and none of the excuse.

The notebook waited in the drawer, becoming heavier without changing size.

“About the thing I mentioned when we were out,” she said.

Julian looked down at her.

“I haven't forgotten. I'd like to explain it properly. Would tomorrow evening work? Not at the clinic. Here, or somewhere quiet if you prefer.”

“Here is fine. What time?”

They checked. He could come after eating, and she had no client delivery that evening. They agreed on seven-thirty. Eve told him he didn't need to bring anything.

“Is there something I should be concerned about before then?” he asked.

“No immediate danger. And I'm not asking for medical advice about him.”

She needed to be precise rather than comforting at any price.

“It's something I've come to believe, and some things I've done because of it. I may be wrong about part of it. I don't want to keep giving you incomplete explanations.”

He was quiet for a moment.

“All right.”

The answer didn't settle her nerves. It did give the conversation a real place in tomorrow instead of leaving it in a fog she could continue calling preparation.

They talked about other things until Julian needed to leave. Eve did not turn the remaining time into a particularly lovely evening designed to make him more forgiving later. When he asked whether she wanted the last of the bread kept or taken away, she said kept. Tomorrow's lunch was allowed to remain an uncomplicated concern.

After the door closed, Dumpling approached the sofa and climbed onto the cushion she had almost moved. He circled twice, pressed the fabric with his front paws, and settled with his back toward the room.

Eve stood watching him.

There had been no test. She had stopped before placing him, before asking the suggestive question, before arranging Julian's private uncertainty into an experiment. That mattered.

It did not make the intention something she could omit from the explanation simply because it embarrassed her.

She took out the clean page. Beneath the two headings she added a third: *What I am responsible for even if my explanation is wrong.*

The first item took longer than it should have.

*I nearly tried to get around a refusal tonight.*

She did not write what Julian's message might have concerned. She hadn't learned that, and the absence was not a missing piece she was entitled to fill.

For a moment she thought of calling Sophie. Her friend would probably listen if she was free. But Eve could feel the particular kind of call she wanted to make: one in which somebody assured her she wasn't a bad person before she admitted what she had considered.

She put the phone aside. If she needed support afterward, she could ask honestly. Tonight she first needed to prepare an account that didn't employ Sophie as a character witness.

Eve reopened the old observation pages and marked the places where a description had become a conclusion. There were more than she remembered. She copied only the parts that belonged to her own choices, leaving other people's unshared worries out of it.

Then she stopped working. Tomorrow's conversation would not become safe merely because she rehearsed it until dawn.

She covered the bread, checked the kitchen, and turned off the light.

Dumpling stayed asleep. For once she felt no temptation to ask whether that was a good sign.
