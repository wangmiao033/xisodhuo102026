# Chapter 195 — Moving In Does Not Mean Swallowing You Whole

The first thing Eve carried into their new home was a box she had marked OPEN FIRST and then packed so thoroughly that she could not remember which end held the scissors.

Julian stood in the hallway with a smaller box against his hip.

“The scissors are in my bag,” he said.

“I knew there was a reason to live with you.”

“Practical blade access.”

“Among other things.”

He set his box down and went to find them. Eve remained inside the doorway for a moment, holding the weight against her stomach. The apartment smelled faintly of cleaned cupboards. Without the previous occupant's furniture, the living room seemed both larger and less convincing than it had during the viewing.

There was no photograph that would make this part look finished. Their sofa had not arrived. A rolled rug leaned against the wall with its label facing outward. Her desk was still downstairs.

It was theirs to arrange now.

Getting to that sentence had taken several weeks.

The trial had ended on the day they had agreed. Julian had taken his bag home, and Eve had spent an evening alone, discovering that she could miss him while enjoying the whole sofa. They had talked afterward about what had worked and what still needed changing. Neither had mistaken a manageable week for proof that nothing would ever be difficult.

The plain apartment had remained available. They had applied, checked the written terms, and received approval for both of them and Dumpling before signing. They had compared the confirmed start date with their existing notice periods, paid the agreed costs, and arranged the move without pretending one household could disappear overnight.

Now their old places were nearly empty, with final cleaning and handovers booked. The temporary overlap had a real end date and a cost they had included, rather than a surprise quietly handed to Eve.

That history was important. It did not help her find the scissors.

Julian returned with them just as her phone buzzed. The movers were downstairs with the desk.

“Workroom first,” she said, opening the box.

“Your side or mine?”

“We agreed the window side for the desk.”

“I meant which side of the door should I stand on so I don't become furniture.”

She laughed and moved out of the way.

Dumpling was not supervising the arrival. He was still at Eve's old apartment with Sophie, in the quiet room they had kept familiar while the heavy work happened. Sophie had offered a specific short visit between other commitments, and Eve had planned the cat's collection around it rather than treating her friend's afternoon as an endless resource.

The new bedroom was already prepared with familiar bedding, water, and a litter tray placed apart from them. The door would stay shut during unloading. They had checked the window and its fittings instead of assuming closed meant secure, and left the rest of the apartment to be explored later, after people and furniture stopped coming through the entrance.

Eve knew all that. She checked the bedroom once more anyway.

Then the sofa arrived and required her attention in a completely different way.

“Turn it toward me,” Julian said.

“Your me or their me?”

He stepped back so the movers could do the job they were being paid to do. The sofa cleared the door by less than Eve wanted to watch.

It landed facing the wall where the bookcase would go. The left cushion sagged immediately, faithful to its established character.

“I remember you,” Eve told it.

Julian looked wounded on its behalf. “It has been through a lot.”

“So have I. I don't require a folded blanket under my left side.”

“Yet.”

She threw the blanket at him. He folded it and put it under the cushion.

The desk went into the smaller room, followed by a narrow shelf for Eve's work things. Julian's bookcase stood in the living room, where it did not block either the doorway or the useful socket they had almost forgotten during measuring. His reading chair fit beside it. Her old chair went by the desk instead of being discarded because its fabric did not match.

For a while the apartment made sense as arrows on a plan. Then real boxes obscured the arrows.

Eve opened one marked KITCHEN and found three notebooks, a charging cable, and a single oven glove.

“That one was late in the packing process,” she admitted.

Julian opened his corresponding box. “I have a kettle.”

“Show-off.”

“And no plug adapter for the lamp.”

“Good. I'd hate to be outclassed in every category.”

They put the kettle on and made a small clear space on the kitchen counter. The first thing they had bought together for the apartment sat wrapped in paper: a plain blue cup, slightly wider at the base, chosen during an errand for cleaning supplies. Eve had liked the shape. Julian had liked that it looked difficult to knock over.

They had not bought an entire matching set. Their existing mugs were perfectly capable of surviving a relationship.

Eve unwrapped the blue cup and set it on the table.

“Who gets first use?” Julian asked.

“The person who finds the tea.”

He looked at the boxes, recalculating his chances.

After the movers left, they checked the rooms and the front door before Eve went to collect Dumpling. Julian stayed to put away the loose packing materials and make sure the quiet bedroom remained quiet. There was no ceremonial procession of friends waiting to greet the cat.

At the old apartment, Sophie was sitting on the floor reading something on her phone. Dumpling lay beside her, his tail across the edge of the familiar cardboard box Eve had almost thrown away before Julian suggested keeping it.

“He has contributed nothing to packing,” Sophie said.

“That box is his contribution.”

“Strong curatorial choice.”

Eve thanked her and checked the time. Sophie still had enough room to get where she needed to go. They talked about a venue booking for a minute, ordinary work continuing through the grand rearrangement of Eve's private life, then Sophie left.

Eve crouched beside Dumpling. The carrier had been left accessible before moving day, with bedding he knew inside. She let the familiar routine take the time it needed. This was not the moment to hurry because she was eager to see him in the new place.

When he was secure, she carried him out along with the flattened box.

The old room looked suddenly bare.

She stood in the doorway long enough to feel it, then checked the lock and left.

At the new apartment, Julian opened the bedroom for them and closed it again once they were inside. Eve set the carrier down and opened it. Dumpling stayed where he was, nose moving, considering smells for which no one had asked his permission.

She sat on the floor rather than drawing him out.

Julian placed the box a short distance away and left them to it.

After a while, Dumpling emerged low to the ground. He inspected the bedding, paused beside the bed, and retreated halfway toward the carrier. Eve kept her hands still.

His shoulders stayed low as he sniffed the familiar blanket, then looked toward the space beneath the bed. Eve could not know exactly what he made of the room. She could see his hesitation well enough to stop expecting him to explore it for her.

She left him choices and a closed, quiet door.

Eventually he climbed into the box.

The cardboard bent in precisely the old place beneath his weight.

Eve let out a breath she had been holding too carefully.

When she returned to the kitchen, Julian had found the tea. The blue cup waited beside one of her old chipped mugs.

“You win,” she said.

“I also found four teaspoons in the bathroom box. I don't want to discuss it.”

They drank standing up, watching nothing happen for several precious minutes.

Then Eve saw that he had placed a stack of her notebooks on the shelf beside his reading chair.

“Could those go in the workroom?”

“Of course.”

“I don't mean you can't touch them.”

“I know. I didn't know where you wanted them.”

She picked them up, relieved by the smallness of the exchange. A shared address had not removed the need to ask where something belonged.

In the workroom, she put them on her own shelf. Julian stopped at the doorway.

“Can we keep this room usable even when you're not working?” he asked. “If one of us wants to shut the door for a bit.”

“Yes. I don't want to turn every surface into an editing project.”

“You may need to tell the cables.”

“I'll arrange a meeting.”

They cleared the chair and kept a strip of the desk free. Her work would live there, but solitude did not belong exclusively to the person with the laptop. Julian's reading corner could be left undisturbed too. Neither quiet place was a punishment or an invitation the other had failed to earn.

Late in the afternoon, Eve wanted twenty minutes by herself.

She almost invented a useful task to justify it.

Instead, she told him.

“Okay,” he said. “I'll finish the kitchen shelf.”

She closed the workroom door and sat among the boxes. For a few minutes she did nothing. Her phone lay face down. Outside, she heard the faint clink of plates, then a pause as Julian presumably remembered how cupboards sounded through walls.

She smiled, but did not get up.

When she came out, she wanted to see him again. That seemed a useful discovery.

They ate a simple dinner with their knees touching under the table. Julian asked whether the tomato and mustard chicken could happen once they found the proper cooking pot.

“It survived the video lesson,” Eve said. “It can survive this kitchen.”

“That's a recommendation?”

“That's confidence.”

She added tomatoes to the shopping note on her phone, then put it down again.

Only one corner of the living room was fully unpacked. The cat was still in his quiet room, checked regularly, with no demand to perform delight. Eve would sleep nearby, and they would let his exploration follow his comfort rather than their excitement.

After dinner, Julian reached for the blue cup and found it full of scissors, a pen, and two spare screws.

“Our first joint possession,” he said.

“Already employed.”

He moved the screws into a small dish. Eve took the pen. Together they returned the cup to being a cup.

Later, she stood in the bedroom doorway watching Dumpling asleep in his battered box. Julian came beside her without crowding the opening.

“Home?” he asked softly.

She considered the unpacked bags, her closed workroom door, his familiar chair, the keys lying together without either set being thrown away before its last handover.

“Getting there,” she said.

He put his hand beside hers on the doorframe. She covered it with her own.

For tonight, they lived here. There was room for the rest to take time.
