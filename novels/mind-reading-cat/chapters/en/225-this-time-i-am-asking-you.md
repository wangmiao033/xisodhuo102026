# Chapter 225 — This Time I Am Asking You

At the noodle shop, Eve was given the seat facing a poster that explained three different lunch combinations.

None was available at dinner.

She studied them anyway because Julian had just taken off his coat and she needed somewhere ordinary to put her eyes.

“Are you considering a return visit?” he asked.

“The middle option has admirable clarity.”

“The middle option has soup.”

“Exactly.”

He smiled, and some of the nervousness loosened. They had reached the restaurant at the revised booking time, with no frantic departure from work and no friend stationed behind a plant. Eve had closed the venue after the day's planned tasks. Julian had finished his own shift. Dumpling was at home, fed and settled in the familiar quiet room, where the evening did not depend on his participation.

Their table was small enough that the menus overlapped.

Eve moved hers aside.

“I'm glad we came back here.”

“So am I.”

The restaurant had no obligation to recognize the importance they had assigned it. A server brought water, asked what they wanted, and went away to a larger table where somebody was trying to order for a person who had not arrived. In the kitchen, bowls struck a counter with brisk, useful sounds.

Eve liked all of it.

She had imagined that being this close to a proposal might make the world seem staged. Instead, the world kept refusing to arrange itself around them. Julian caught the edge of his napkin beneath his water glass and had to lift both together. She asked for an extra little plate because she had ordered something difficult to divide neatly.

The first food arrived before they had exhausted the subject of their afternoons.

Julian described a harmless misunderstanding about the location of a delivery cupboard. He kept the story about his own mistake, not about any patient's business. Eve told him that a client's preferred version had turned out to be the least elaborate edit she had made.

“Were you disappointed?” he asked.

“For twelve seconds. Then I remembered I could send the invoice.”

“An excellent recovery.”

She laughed and broke a piece of the side dish onto his plate.

The small sleeve in her bag pressed against her knee whenever she shifted. She could feel it without touching it, a secret of her own that had nothing to do with guessing his.

During a pause, Julian looked at her hand on the table. Eve noticed him noticing, and nearly spoiled both of them by laughing.

“Would you like me to give you something difficult to talk about?” she asked.

“Please don't.”

“I could complain about the chair.”

“The chair seems sound.”

“You've become very attentive to furniture.”

He shook his head, smiling, and took a drink of water.

They ate slowly. The noodles were hot enough to make haste unwise, which gave them a reason besides significance to take their time. Eve let herself enjoy the person across from her without silently demanding that he arrive at the important sentence before the next bowl.

He had already told her what he wanted. She had already told him. The question still mattered. It did not have to be a surprise about the answer to be real.

When the server cleared the table, Eve asked whether they could order one more small dish to share. Julian agreed. There was nothing urgent waiting outside except their own expectations.

“I've liked this week,” she said while they waited.

He looked briefly surprised.

“We've discussed several difficult things.”

“I know. I haven't liked every minute. I liked finding out you didn't need me to make every answer prettier.”

“You didn't let me do it either.”

“No. I was very generous that way.”

He reached across the table, palm up. She put her hand in his.

For several seconds they simply sat like that. Eve could hear the larger table laughing over something she had not followed. She did not envy their ease or feel responsible for matching it.

The last dish arrived. They let go so the server could set it down without performing an obstacle course around their romance.

After dinner they settled the bill in the ordinary way they had agreed for this outing. Neither made payment into a statement about how every future expense would be divided. Julian held the door while Eve put on her coat. Outside, the air was cold enough that she tucked her hands into her sleeves before taking his arm.

“Home?” he asked.

“Yes.”

They walked rather than inventing a more impressive destination. At a crossing, Eve found herself watching the reflection of both of them in a dark shop window. Their shoulders were close. Her scarf had worked loose on one side. Julian was carrying the little takeaway container they had requested instead of leaving good food to be thrown away.

She looked like herself, going home from dinner.

The realization made her unexpectedly happy.

In the apartment, Dumpling raised his head from his box, recognized them, and considered the possible significance of the container. Julian put the leftovers in the refrigerator. The cat, finding no change in his own meal arrangements, settled down again.

Eve hung up her coat and checked that the sleeve was still in her bag. She left the bag beside her chair.

“Would you sit with me?” Julian asked.

She turned.

He stood near the sofa, without the container or his coat or anything else to occupy his hands. His face had the particular seriousness she knew from conversations he did not intend to hurry.

“Yes.”

They sat. Dumpling's box was across the room, in its familiar place. No light had been rearranged. No camera was waiting. The lamp illuminated an untidy stack of books and the faint mark on the table they kept forgetting to clean properly.

Julian took out the small box.

Eve recognized its shape and smiled.

“I still don't know what was in it,” she said.

“I appreciate your patience.”

“It wasn't entirely graceful.”

“I didn't require graceful.”

He set it beside him unopened for a moment.

“I had a speech,” he said.

“Oh.”

“Parts of it were good.”

“That's unfortunate for the other parts.”

He laughed, then looked at her steadily.

“I don't want to promise that being married will make us better at everything we've been talking about. I think we'll still get tired and make assumptions. I'll sometimes try to solve a thing you wanted me to hear. You'll sometimes decide that taking on one more task will be quicker than asking.”

“An accurate and troubling opening.”

“I want to be the person you can tell when I'm doing it. I want to keep telling you things before I've decided what your answer will be.”

Her smile softened.

He took a breath.

“I love the life we have. Not because it's finished. I love you in it. I want to keep making a home with you, and I want you to keep being yourself there.”

Eve could feel the beating in her throat. For once she did not try to turn it into a joke before it became visible.

Julian opened the box. Inside lay a simple ring. He did not take her hand or begin putting it on before asking.

“This time I'm asking,” he said. “Will you marry me?”

“Yes.”

The word came easily. She had spent the week making room for it.

He stayed still for half a breath, then smiled with an openness that made Eve's eyes sting.

“Yes,” she said again. “I will.”

He leaned toward her, and she met him. The kiss was awkward for the first instant because both were smiling. Eve put a hand against his cheek and felt him laugh softly before kissing her again.

When they drew apart, the ring remained in its box.

“We'll check the fit properly,” he said. “I don't want the paper exercise to become a household injury.”

“Very romantic.”

“I'm trying.”

“I know.”

She looked at the ring without forcing it onto her finger. They could arrange the size together. The decision had already happened in words, with nothing painful required to make it convincing.

“I brought something too,” she said.

Julian looked surprised.

Eve reached for her bag and took out the paper sleeve. The moment she held it, she felt absurdly shy. It was card and ink, visibly handmade. Her hands had been perfectly capable of trimming it three days ago and were now uncertain about operating the opening.

“It isn't an equivalent exchange,” she said.

“I wasn't calculating one.”

“Good. That would be a difficult valuation.”

He drew out the bookmark.

For a second he only looked. Then he laughed, a startled sound that made Dumpling lift his head across the room.

“The ears.”

“I thought you should be able to revisit your public work.”

“One of them still has the price.”

“Historical accuracy.”

He turned it carefully to read the line beneath the drawing.

You can stop here and come back.

Eve watched his thumb rest beside the words.

“For books,” she said. “And other things, if you want. You don't have to finish every useful thing before you're allowed to come home.”

Julian looked up at her.

“I'd like to keep it.”

“That is the general arrangement with gifts.”

“I mean exactly as it is.”

Her laugh caught a little. He put the bookmark safely on the table, then took both her hands.

“I'd like that life,” he said.

“So would I.”

They sat close for a long time. There was no immediate burst of messages, no photograph of a hand held at the correct angle. Eve wanted a little while in which the answer belonged only to the people who had spoken it.

Eventually she asked whom he wanted to tell first. They agreed to speak to their families themselves and to tell close friends without making anyone responsible for preparations. They did not choose a date tonight. A wedding would need its own decisions, money, and conversations. Getting engaged did not reserve Sophie's weekends or make the venue's account available for flowers.

“Tomorrow?” Eve asked.

“Tomorrow is fine.”

She nodded and leaned against him again.

Dumpling turned over in his box, stretched one front leg, and went back to sleep. He had not brought the ring, exposed a secret, or interrupted to create a better ending. Eve watched his paw relax against the familiar cardboard.

“He's missing the occasion,” she whispered.

“He seems comfortable with that.”

“So am I.”

Before bed, Julian placed the new bookmark inside the book he was reading, leaving the ridiculous ears above the page. Eve closed the ring box and put it safely away until they could arrange the fitting. Then she checked the cat's water while Julian turned off the kitchen light.

At the bedroom door, she looked back once.

The room was almost exactly as they had left it before dinner. Books, chairs, a sleeping cat, a bag that needed putting away. Nothing had become complete by magic.

Julian held out his hand.

Eve took it, and went with him into the home they were still making.
