# Chapter 163 — Who Can Press Cancel?

The schedule looked generous until Robin counted the people.

“Two for the camera?” they asked.

“One operator, one assistant.”

“And you.”

“Yes.”

“And me.”

“You're already here.”

Robin made a small mark on the page. Eve disliked the mark immediately.

They were at the venue for an internal run-through, with no public guests expected. Eve had booked Robin for hosting preparation and a practical test of arrivals, setup, and clearing. Between two blocks of that work, she had inserted a short filming segment for her own venue introduction. The camera operator would come with an assistant, shoot a few simple angles, and leave before the invited walkthrough later in the week.

Nobody was trying to squeeze a crowd into the room today. That was what Eve kept telling herself.

Robin tapped the schedule.

“The filming block ends when our next setup begins.”

“Exactly.”

“Where does the equipment go while we move the tables back?”

“Outside, presumably.”

“Into the shared passage?”

Eve glanced at the door. “Briefly.”

They both heard it.

The word had accompanied enough of her mistakes to deserve its own line in the budget.

She had already learned that photography and a quiet corner couldn't occupy the same space at the same time. This schedule was supposed to respect that. A separate block for filming, no guests present, no drinks equipment, no animals. It had felt like applying what she knew.

She had simply omitted the minutes during which one activity became another.

“We could move the second test later,” she said.

“My paid finish is still the same. I told you I have another job afterward.”

“I know. I meant reduce the feedback.”

“That's the part we're here to get.”

Eve looked at the camera appointment on her phone. It had taken three messages to find an affordable slot. The operator was nearby on another job, which was why the short visit was possible at all. Moving it meant paying more or waiting. She could almost see the new line of expense sliding onto the page.

“Let's try the layout before we decide.”

Robin studied her for a moment, then agreed. “We can test it without equipment. But if it doesn't fit, I need you to accept that answer.”

She said yes too quickly.

They used folded cartons to mark the camera cases and a broom laid flat to show the footprint of a stand. No electrical equipment was connected. Robin placed their own bag where an assistant might need to reach it and asked Eve to walk through with a chair.

She turned sideways, lifted the chair higher, and got past.

“There.”

“Now put it down without moving a case.”

Eve looked for a gap.

She found one only by standing in the quiet corner.

“The corner won't be in use,” she said.

“It becomes storage during the shoot. Then we're still moving storage when the next block starts.”

“Could the assistant take things straight out?”

“Let's ask what they actually bring. We haven't.”

The camera operator called just then to confirm a slightly earlier arrival. Eve put the call on speaker after asking both people present. The operator described a compact setup, then mentioned two cases, a stand, and a small light they could use only if the site arrangements permitted it. There would also be the time to check the framing and pack safely.

“How short is short?” Eve asked.

The answer exceeded her filming block before they had recorded anything.

“I can do a simpler handheld shot,” the operator offered. “But you sent references with three different angles.”

“I did.”

Robin said nothing. That made it harder to blame them.

Eve promised to call back within ten minutes. The operator warned her that the agreed late cancellation charge would apply if she dropped the slot now. It was a reasonable reminder, delivered without hostility. She thanked them and ended the call.

“We can simplify,” she said.

“We can,” Robin agreed. “What do you want the video to accomplish?”

“Show that the room exists. Explain the sessions. Make it feel welcoming.”

“Today?”

“Soon.”

“That wasn't my question.”

Eve set the phone down harder than she intended.

“I know you're trying to help.”

Robin's expression changed slightly. “I am also trying to do the work you hired me for.”

The sentence brought the room into an uncomfortable focus. She had asked them to take responsibility for the venue rehearsal. Then she had treated every observation they made as advice she could thank them for and ignore.

A knock at the door saved her from answering. The invited preparation helper had arrived for the last part of the test. Their task was to play an arriving guest with a bag while Eve and Robin finished the reset. They were being paid for the short practical session, and they had another appointment afterward.

Eve welcomed them, explained the harmless carton markers, and started again.

Robin checked the time.

“Ready?”

Eve picked up the chair. The helper came in carrying a tote and stopped beside the table to ask where to put it. Robin tried to reach the entrance without stepping over the marked equipment. Eve backed up, caught the chair leg against a carton, and felt the whole artificial arrangement collapse into a real obstruction.

Nobody fell. The broom hadn't moved. Yet for a few seconds all three people were politely asking someone else to go first, and none of them had anywhere useful to go.

Robin raised a hand.

“Stop. The filming segment is canceled from today's rehearsal.”

The helper froze.

Eve heard the sentence as if Robin had announced it to a full room of customers. Heat climbed into her face.

“You can't just cancel—”

“I can stop this test. We agreed either of us could call a pause. I'm responsible for the setup, and I won't run the next block around this.”

Robin moved the broom out of the walking route before continuing.

“You decide what to do about the camera booking. I'm saying the schedule in front of us doesn't work.”

Eve stood holding the chair long after she should have put it down.

The helper looked at the floor, trying to become invisible. That was the detail that finally embarrassed her more than being contradicted. Someone she had invited to help was now trapped inside her argument because she couldn't bear to lose a filming slot.

“I'm sorry,” she said to them. “This is useful. We found the problem. Could you take five minutes while we clear the markers?”

The helper nodded with visible relief and stepped into the designated waiting space.

Eve put the chair down.

She and Robin cleared the cartons in silence. Without them, the room regained its ordinary proportions. Nothing had been physically damaged. The cost was on her phone, in a booking she wanted to keep because changing it would make her planning mistake expensive.

She called the operator back.

“I need to cancel today,” she said. “The setup and clearing won't fit. Please send the agreed charge. I'd like to discuss a properly timed separate booking later, but I can't confirm that now.”

The operator was disappointed. Eve let them be disappointed without asking for a favor disguised as understanding.

After the call, she wrote the amount beside the filming line. Crossing out the appointment didn't erase the charge.

“Thank you,” Robin said.

“You shouldn't have had to argue that hard.”

“I'd rather argue before the camera arrives.”

They brought the helper back and completed the arrival test with an empty route. This time the tote had a place to go. Robin could reach the door while Eve moved the chair. The reset took longer than Eve had estimated even without filming, because one table foot needed adjusting and someone had to check beneath the seats.

They recorded the actual time instead of rounding down.

During the feedback, Eve asked the helper what had felt confusing. They hesitated, then said they hadn't known whose instructions to follow when Robin stopped the test and Eve objected.

Eve resisted explaining how much the camera slot had mattered.

“That's something we need to fix.”

When the helper left, she and Robin sat with the schedule between them. They had enough paid time left for a short decision, not an entire policy manual.

“If you're running the room,” Eve said, “you need to be able to stop an activity when the route's blocked or the timing means we can't clear properly. Including mine.”

Robin nodded. “And I need to tell you promptly, not let a problem grow because you're busy.”

“For ordinary changes, we talk first. For an immediate safety concern, stop first. For extra spending or canceling an outside supplier, I handle the commitment unless we've agreed a limit in advance.”

“That sounds workable. Put it in the role description. Along with the setup time.”

“And if I'm the problem?”

Robin glanced toward the former carton maze.

“I think we've established a procedure.”

Eve laughed reluctantly. It loosened something in her chest without making the cancellation fee any smaller.

They revised the next schedule together. Arrival included coats and bags. Clearing included checking the floor. There was an empty interval between activities, a blank strip Eve's fingers itched to fill with something productive.

She left it empty.

Robin asked her to walk through one final scenario: the previous activity ended ten minutes late because a guest needed time to gather their things.

“I'd use the gap,” Eve said.

“And if it used all of it?”

“The next activity starts later. We tell people, rather than trying to recover the time by rushing them.”

“What gets shortened?”

She looked at the optional closing exercise and drew a small bracket around it. “This. We can lose the second round without losing what people came for.”

Robin made a note beside hers. Now the schedule had a fallback someone else could understand. Eve would not need to stand in the doorway inventing an answer while coats and bags accumulated around her. She photographed the marked copy and sent it to Robin before either of them forgot which version they had agreed.

Before Robin went, Eve paid the agreed session amount and confirmed that the canceled filming did not shorten their paid booking. Their work had exposed the mistake. It wasn't a reason to discount it.

Alone afterward, she took one plain photograph of the room on her phone. No equipment, no staged guests, no sweeping movement through a doorway. The image was too dull to serve as the introduction she had imagined.

It was useful for showing the operator the actual space when they discussed another date.

She attached it to an email and added the measured setup requirements. Then she deleted the phrase just a quick shoot from her draft.

Outside, a delivery driver paused at the wrong door and checked a number. Eve pointed them toward the neighboring entrance. She did not have to shift a camera case to let them pass.

When she locked up, the blank interval in the schedule was still there.

For once, she could see exactly what it was doing.
