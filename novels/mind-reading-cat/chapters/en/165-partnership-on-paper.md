# Chapter 165 — Partnership on Paper

Eve had bought a good pen for the signatures.

It refused to write on the first page.

Robin tested it on the corner of a spare sheet, drew three pale circles, and offered Eve the ordinary blue pen from their bag.

“Mine has no ambitions,” they said.

“It was working this morning.”

“That often happens to ambitious people too.”

Eve accepted the blue pen.

The documents on the table were much less impressive than the pen had been. No decorative folder, no photograph of happy animals, no sweeping language about building a shared future. There was a short agreement for Robin's trial work, an attached schedule, and a practical room checklist they had revised after the failed filming test.

They had exchanged drafts beforehand. Today was for checking the remaining details and signing copies, not surprising anybody with a stack of obligations over coffee. There was no coffee service, in any case. Robin had brought their own water bottle.

Outside, somebody rolled a bin across the pavement. Eve waited until the sound passed before reading the final question on her list.

“If I cancel the whole event, the preparation you've already done is still paid. The remaining booking follows this notice arrangement. Is that clear enough?”

Robin pointed to the next sentence. “It is here. On the schedule, you still call the entire amount a hosting fee. Can we separate preparation from delivery so nobody has to reconstruct it later?”

They corrected both copies.

Eve was grateful that Robin did not praise her for being fair. They treated clear payment as an ordinary requirement, which made it easier to stop behaving as if writing it down were an emotional confession.

The role covered the agreed trial sessions. Robin would lead activities and manage the room during those bookings, with setup and clearing included in paid time. Eve would handle bookings, customer payments, and supplier commitments. Either could stop an activity for an immediate safety concern. Routine changes would be discussed; spending beyond agreed limits would come back to Eve.

They had written what happened if someone was ill, which contact to use, and how they would tell guests if a session could not go ahead. The trial had an end date and a review, not a silent expectation that Robin belonged to the venue forever.

“What if I get an offer that clashes with a later date we haven't confirmed?” Robin asked.

“Take the offer if you want it.”

“And you'll book someone else.”

“I'll try to. Or I won't run that session.”

It was a plain answer. Eve felt the weight of it anyway. A room could remain dark for an evening without somebody having failed to care about her.

Robin checked the final schedule, then signed.

Eve signed below them, using the unambitious pen. She scanned the completed pages, checked that the signatures and dates were legible, and sent Robin their copy while they were both still at the table. Their next confirmed booking was on the calendar. No extra dates had appeared merely because the pen was out.

Robin packed their things.

“I'm glad we did the awkward rehearsal,” they said.

“The first one or the one where I argued with you about cartons?”

“Both. I know what happens after awkward now.”

Eve watched them leave with a little less of the anxiety she had felt when the rubber chicken first appeared. They were still getting to know each other. A signed page did not turn a new collaborator into an old friend. It did mean neither had to guess quite so much about what the other expected on Tuesday.

She put the good pen in a drawer where it could consider its behavior.

Julian arrived later, after his shift, carrying a light jacket over one arm. He held it up before she could comment on the cold.

“I've remembered it. I just haven't put it on.”

“An important distinction.”

He draped it over the empty chair, then moved it when Eve pointed out the copy of the schedule beneath it.

“The paperwork is breeding,” he said.

“Only until I've sorted it.”

Julian sat beside her rather than across the table. She showed him the updated room arrangements, including the line that removed any assumption he would be available whenever he happened to visit.

His possible professional contribution was on a separate, clearly unconfirmed page. They had reduced it to a proposal she could discuss through the appropriate clinic arrangements: a limited, booked session within an agreed scope, subject to his availability and any necessary workplace approval. There was no recurring unpaid shift, no on-call promise, and no claim that the space provided veterinary services.

He read it carefully.

“This I can discuss,” he said. “It still isn't a booking.”

“I've marked that in three places.”

“I see them.”

“The third is for me.”

He smiled and initialed only the note confirming what they had discussed, not a professional service contract. Eve added her own initials and the date. Any actual collaboration would need its own agreed terms before she advertised it. She saved the proposal separately from Robin's signed agreement so it could not become a confirmed event by proximity.

Julian looked at the empty pot now sitting on a temporary shelf. Eve had brought it in that morning because she liked the shape there, not because the room required a plant.

“Settled in?” he asked.

“For now. It may go home again.”

“It has more housing options than I do.”

She laughed, then fell quiet as he gathered the pages into their proper piles.

Everything was clear. That ought to have felt entirely good.

Instead, the evening had acquired an unexpected loneliness. Robin could leave after the trial. Sophie had work elsewhere. Julian would return to the clinic tomorrow. Even the pot was portable. Eve knew none of this meant abandonment. She still looked at the small agreements and felt how little any signature could promise about being wanted.

Julian stopped tidying.

“What happened?”

“Nothing.”

He waited, his hand resting beside the folder.

She sighed. “I think I imagined this part looking more romantic.”

“Robin's payment schedule?”

“No.” She nudged him with her knee. “Us. Doing something together. I know keeping your work separate is right. I wanted it that way. But these pages make it look as though we're carefully arranging ways to leave.”

The words embarrassed her as soon as she said them. She didn't take them back.

Julian glanced at the folder, then closed it.

“The pages are for work,” he said. “Would you still like to go on a date with me tonight?”

Eve blinked.

“You haven't asked me yet.”

“I am asking now.”

“I thought you were coming to look at the proposal.”

“I did. I've looked.”

“And then?”

“Then I was hoping you'd be free.”

She looked around the room. There were things to do. There would always be things to do now that she had keys which worked on ordinary days. One chair foot needed replacing. The booking description needed a final read. An entire shelf of tasks waited just beyond the edges of her vision.

“Give me ten minutes to close properly.”

“Ten minutes,” he agreed. “I won't offer to reorganize the shelf.”

“That is an extremely attractive quality.”

She checked the room, put the records away, and turned off the lights. Julian waited by the exit without finding a useful job to justify being there. When she joined him, he took her hand.

“Where are we going?”

“There's a secondhand bookshop open late two streets over. You said you wanted to look without buying something for a project.”

“You remembered that?”

“You said it while carrying six books you were buying for a project.”

The bookshop had narrow aisles and a bell that sounded tired of announcing people. Eve left her bag where the owner indicated so it wouldn't catch the low stacks. Julian went toward the shelves of old travel writing; she wandered into cookbooks, despite having no particular intention of improving dinner.

At the back, a stool stood beside a high shelf. Eve reached for a book, found it just beyond her fingers, and glanced around for Julian before asking the owner for help instead. The owner brought it down with a practiced movement, then asked whether she wanted the other volume too.

“No, thank you. I only want to look at this one.”

It was a book about restoring old houses. She leafed through photographs of rooms emptied down to bare walls and felt her attention drifting toward the venue again. One page showed a shelving arrangement that might fit by the door. She almost took a photograph to remember it.

Then she shut the book and returned it to the owner.

“Not what you wanted?” the owner asked.

“Very much what I wanted. Just not tonight.”

The owner accepted this without requiring a better explanation. Eve found Julian in the next aisle, reading a paragraph with his head tilted toward the light. For a moment she watched him from the end of the shelf. He hadn't followed her to approve a choice. She hadn't needed to prove she could do the whole evening alone.

When he looked up, she beckoned him toward a box of maps.

“Come and tell me whether this coastline resembles a sleeping rabbit.”

“It does from here.”

“You haven't seen it.”

“Then I'll withdraw my expert opinion.”

They met beside the box.

Eve had found a book of elaborate picnic dishes that appeared to require a kitchen larger than her apartment. Julian held a slim account of a walking holiday whose cover had lost one corner.

“Are you going to walk somewhere?” she asked.

“Possibly to dinner.”

“The author seems to have crossed mountains.”

“I'll start with the crossing outside.”

She opened the picnic book at a photograph of a molded dessert. It had the glossy shape of something nobody should transport on a bus.

“Imagine bringing this to a park.”

“Imagine catching it.”

They laughed too loudly, apologized to the owner, and then laughed again more quietly. Eve put the cookbook back. She found a battered collection of short stories instead, read the opening page, and wanted to know what happened next.

That was her entire reason for buying it.

Outside, Julian tucked his purchase under his arm and asked what she wanted to eat. Eve named a small place nearby, checked the prices in the window, and chose without calculating how many guest bookings the meal represented.

They paid for their own books. Julian offered dinner; Eve accepted, then said the next one was hers if their schedules allowed it. Neither wrote anything down.

While they waited for their food, she told him about Robin's pen. He told her about the first page of the walking book, which apparently began with the author forgetting the map.

“Promising,” she said.

“Very relatable.”

Her phone stayed inside her bag. The venue was closed. The signed papers were where she had put them, and the unconfirmed proposal would remain unconfirmed until the proper conversations happened.

Across the small table, Julian reached for her hand.

Nothing on paper required him to do that.

Eve turned her palm upward and met him halfway.
