# Chapter 128 — The Day She Wanted to Give the Dog Back

The message arrived while Eve was trimming six sheets of card to the same size.

I think I need to give him back.

Her scissors stopped halfway through a line. For a few seconds she could think only of the empty bed that would remain in Mia's room. It was an unfair place to begin, with an imagined object instead of the person asking for help.

She put the scissors down and called.

Mia answered before the second ring. “Please don't tell me to give it more time.”

“I wasn't going to start there. Where are you?”

“In the kitchen. He's with Leah in my room. She's staying until Nora comes.”

“You've called Nora?”

“Yes. She's coming at eleven. She asked whether I'd like someone else here. Can you come?”

Eve checked the clock. She could move the printing to later without moving a client deadline. That was different from promising an entire day she did not have.

“I can stay until one.”

“That's enough.”

Nearly two weeks had passed since Toby's arrival. Mia had extended her leave by two days and begun the shorter shifts with the arranged cover. The difficulty had not followed the neat downward slope everyone hoped for. A better night had been followed by a worse one. Toby had become distressed during a planned brief absence despite the careful transition, and the support plan was being reviewed.

When Eve arrived, Mia was sitting at the kitchen table beside a piece of toast with one bite missing. Leah's headphones lay on the counter. From Mia's room came the faint sound of a chair shifting.

“I lost my temper,” Mia said.

Eve sat opposite her.

“I shouted his name. I didn't touch him. He was scratching at the door and I shouted. Then he stopped, and that was worse.”

Her eyes were swollen. Eve wanted to reach across the table, but Mia had folded her arms tightly against herself.

“Have you told Nora that?”

“Yes.”

“Good.”

“It doesn't feel good.”

“No.”

The word stayed between them without decoration. Eve noticed crumbs on the table and kept herself from clearing them, as though tidying could make the conversation less difficult.

Mia explained that her manager had asked whether she could resume her usual hours sooner. She had not agreed, but the question had made every sound in the flat feel like a countdown. Rachel had lost sleep. Leah had postponed a recording. Mia had begun checking the clock whenever Toby moved.

“I don't want to look at him and think about what I can't do.”

Eve heard the shame in the sentence, but also something more useful: a description of what was happening, without the cheerful photograph placed in front of it.

Nora arrived with a notebook and no expression of emergency judgment. She greeted Leah, checked that Toby was comfortably supervised, and asked Mia whether she wanted Rachel included when Rachel returned. Mia said yes. The household had agreed to the dog; the household needed a voice in what happened next.

Nora sat down.

“Tell me what you need today.”

Mia looked at Eve, then back at Nora.

“I need to know if you can take him back.”

“We can discuss a return and make an appropriate arrangement. You won't be asked to abandon him or find somewhere yourself. Is that what you want to arrange now, or do you want to look at the support options first?”

Mia's face crumpled in a way Eve had not expected. The possibility of a real exit seemed to hurt and relieve her at once.

“I don't know.”

“Then we can start with today.”

Nora asked concrete questions. Had Mia been able to eat and rest? Who could safely remain with Toby while they talked? What had changed since the last call? What could the household manage for the next day without pretending it could manage indefinitely?

Leah came to the doorway. Toby stayed in Mia's room, visible from the hall, on the familiar rug.

“I can stay until two,” Leah said. “Then I have to go.”

Nora wrote that down without asking for more.

Denise had offered an afternoon support visit, already discussed with Mia. Nora checked the time by phone and confirmed that Denise could come before Leah left. It would give Mia several hours out of the flat, with somebody Toby knew. It was temporary help, not a solution disguised as a free foster service.

“Go where?” Mia asked.

“Anywhere you can rest,” Nora said.

Eve almost offered her own sofa. Dumpling would be home, her client work was waiting, and Mia had not asked to be hosted while ashamed and exhausted. Before Eve spoke, Mia said she wanted to sit in the library where nobody knew her.

“Then perhaps lunch first,” Nora said. “You can decide the rest afterward.”

They reviewed the records Mia had kept. Some entries were detailed; others were only times and a question mark. Nora did not diagnose Toby from the page. She recommended a veterinary assessment of the changes Mia was describing and helped her arrange the appropriate follow-up with the group's behavior support contact. They would work from observations, the veterinary findings, and the household's actual capacity.

Eve recognized the temptation to call Julian directly and get reassurance quickly. Instead, Mia contacted the clinic through its ordinary booking route. The appointment would be with the available veterinarian. There was no reason her friendship with Eve should turn a worried dog into a private favor.

“Could it be something I've done?” Mia asked after the call.

“There are questions the assessment can help answer,” Nora said. “We shouldn't decide the answer before it happens.”

Rachel returned just before noon carrying a bag of bread rolls. She put them on the table, took off her shoes, and asked where they had reached.

Mia began apologizing. Rachel held up a hand.

“I need sleep. I don't need you to apologize so much that I have to comfort you about needing sleep.”

Mia went still.

Rachel pulled out a chair. “I agreed to him being here. I didn't know it would sound like this at night. Neither did you. We have to talk about that part.”

The room grew painfully quiet. Eve wanted to smooth the sentence. Instead she asked whether Rachel wanted a plate, and Rachel said yes.

Over bread rolls, they separated immediate arrangements from the decision about keeping Toby. Rachel could not cover care after night shifts. Leah could not keep missing work preparation. Mia could pay for some additional help, but not an unlimited amount. Nora would establish what support the group could offer and what a return process would involve if it became necessary.

No one suggested that loving him harder would create an extra person between ten and two.

Mia stared at the open notebook.

“If I say I want to try the help, will you think I'm just wasting another week?”

“No,” Nora said. “But we'll set a review time, and we need honest information. That includes if you decide you can't continue.”

“And if I do return him?”

“We'll arrange it responsibly. We won't use it as a punishment.”

Mia rubbed both hands over her face. When she lowered them, she looked older and younger at the same time.

“I want to see what the assessment says. And I want this afternoon.”

“Those are decisions we can make today.”

Eve left at one. Denise had arrived, the next contacts were confirmed, and Mia was finding her coat. In the hall she stopped Eve with a hand on her sleeve.

“Do you think I should keep him?”

The answer Eve wanted to give was immediate and sentimental. She had seen the dog lean against Mia's leg. She could supply a whole future from that brief pressure.

“I don't have to live your days,” she said. “I can help you look at them. I can't choose them for you.”

Mia nodded, then let go.

On the bus home, Eve discovered a smear of butter on her cuff. She stared at it with disproportionate annoyance. The morning had not offered a satisfying rescue. It had offered an appointment, a temporary break, and the possibility that the outcome everyone wanted might still be wrong.

At home, Dumpling greeted her by walking directly past her bag to inspect the kitchen. His ordinary demands were briefly a relief. Food belonged in a particular bowl. A door was either open or closed. Eve completed the things she knew how to do, then finished trimming the card.

Two days later she accompanied Mia to the clinic because Mia asked for company, not influence. They sat in the appropriate waiting area, following the staff's arrangements for Toby. Eve carried the folder while Mia handled him. When the veterinarian called them, Eve stayed outside at Mia's request.

Tessa handed her a lost pen.

“Yours?”

Eve examined it. “Possibly. Does it have a history of disappointing owners?”

“It was under a chair.”

“Then almost certainly.”

She took it, grateful for a conversation that did not require wisdom.

Mia came out with written follow-up instructions and a plan for further support. Eve did not ask for the clinical details across the waiting room. Outside, Mia said the appointment had not produced one simple explanation that could be removed and leave a perfect dog behind.

“I kept hoping there would be a button,” she admitted.

“I've wanted those for several people.”

Mia smiled faintly. “They listened. I didn't feel stupid.”

“That matters.”

The behavior support appointment was arranged for the following week. Until then, Mia would follow the individualized guidance she had been given and keep reporting changes. Nora had set a review call, and the household had agreed only to the short-term arrangement they could genuinely cover.

At the crossing, Toby paused to sniff the base of a sign. Mia waited without pulling her phone out. Eve watched her shoulders lower by a fraction.

“He's still Toby,” Mia said.

“Yes.”

“And I'm still tired.”

“Yes.”

The light changed before Toby finished. They waited for the next one.

That evening, Eve met Julian for a walk they had arranged earlier. She told him, without Mia's details, that she had spent several days wanting a situation to have an obvious answer.

“Did it?” he asked.

“No. There were bread rolls.”

“Sometimes those are the available resources.”

She laughed and put her hand in his. They walked past the community center, its lit windows full of people attending something Eve had nothing to do with. She liked the sight of the room being useful without her.

At the corner, her phone vibrated. Mia had written that she had eaten dinner, slept for an hour that afternoon, and made the next support appointment. She did not say everything was better.

Eve replied that she was glad the appointment was set. Then she put the phone away and stayed beside Julian until they reached the place where their routes home separated.
