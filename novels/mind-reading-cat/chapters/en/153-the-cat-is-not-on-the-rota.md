# Chapter 153 — The Cat Is Not on the Rota

The photograph showed Dumpling in sunlight, looking as though he had invested heavily in the future of small businesses.

In reality, he had been asleep on his blanket beside her materials box, having reclaimed his preferred end of the sofa. She had taken the picture at home. Nothing in it showed the new unit, an activity, or any service a person could buy.

Above the picture she had written the new name. Beneath it, she had announced two small trial dates, with a link to the booking page.

The booking page used a photograph of an empty table. Eve had learned that lesson long ago. Apparently she had decided the lesson applied only to the last page a customer saw.

She would understand this before the afternoon was over.

For now, she was on her knees in the nonpublic side room, looking at one orange paw behind a cabinet.

“All right,” she said quietly. “You can stay there.”

The paw withdrew.

Dumpling had come for a short, quiet visit before the separately approved trial session. Eve had brought his carrier, familiar blanket, water, and litter tray, intending to take him home before the guests arrived. The morning had run behind after a missing table fitting required a replacement from the supplier. When she offered the carrier, he had retreated into the gap beside the low cabinet instead.

There was no dangerous place behind it. She had checked before letting him explore. There was, however, enough room for a cat to make clear that a human's schedule was not his schedule.

Eve called the person who had agreed to help with arrival checks. She explained the delay and asked her to begin setting out the materials while Eve stayed with Dumpling. Then she sat on the floor without reaching into his refuge.

The side room was separated from the activity area by a solid door. Eve kept it closed. From the other side came the sound of a box being placed on the table, then silence.

Dumpling showed the tip of his nose.

Eve looked down at her hands instead of making the sight of him into a new request.

Five minutes later he came out, sniffed his water, and stepped into the open carrier to investigate the familiar blanket. Eve let him settle. She could still take him home before the session if the journey went smoothly, but not if she spent another ten minutes hoping her original plan would become punctual.

She sent a message to the arrival helper, who was already booked for the whole trial: could she stay responsible for arrivals while Eve made the short return trip? The reply came immediately. Yes; one guest had just arrived early.

Then came a knock.

Eve stepped into the main room and closed the side-room door behind her.

The early guest stood beside the welcome table, looking past her.

“Is that him?” the woman asked. “I heard you talking.”

“Dumpling? He's in the other room.”

“Oh, good. I was worried I'd come on the wrong day.”

Eve's hand remained on the door handle.

The woman held her phone out. On the screen was the sunny photograph, with You, Me & Paws above it and the invitation below.

“I've followed him for ages,” she said. “I came early so it wouldn't be too busy.”

Eve felt her stomach tighten.

“I'm sorry. Today's session is the owners' story exchange. Dumpling isn't part of it.”

“But he's here.”

“For a separate visit before the session. I'm taking him home.”

The woman looked at the closed door, then at the table with its pencils and sheets.

“Not even for a few minutes?”

“No. He doesn't want company.”

The answer sounded harsher than Eve intended, but making it softer by leaving a possibility open would not help either of them.

The woman lowered her phone.

“I didn't expect him to do tricks. I just thought we could sit with him.”

Eve glanced at the image again. The cat filled most of it. The name sounded friendly. The announcement had said a place for the people who loved animals. She remembered approving that sentence because it did not explicitly promise anything she could not deliver.

It had promised something anyway.

“I can see why you thought that,” she said. “The picture gives the wrong impression. This isn't a cat-petting place, and I should have made that clear in the announcement itself.”

“The page said no visiting pets. I assumed that meant there were cats already here.”

Eve had written the sentence to keep owners from bringing animals to a human discussion. Read beside Dumpling's enormous face, it had acquired an entirely different meaning.

She pulled the spare chair out from the welcome table.

“Would you like to sit while I arrange a refund? You don't need to stay for something you didn't intend to book.”

The woman hesitated, then sat.

“I took the afternoon off.”

“I understand.”

Eve did not say she understood completely. Her own afternoon was also difficult, but for different reasons, and this was not the moment to put them side by side.

She checked the booking under the name the woman supplied and refunded the full fee. The payment service showed the refund accepted for processing. Eve explained the usual return time and sent the confirmation to the same address used for the booking.

The woman checked her phone.

“Will there be days when people can meet him?”

“No planned sessions like that.”

“Then you should really say so.”

“I will.”

It was tempting to give a speech about animal choice, or explain how hard Eve had worked to create something useful for owners. The woman had not asked to frighten a cat. She had bought the wrong afternoon from an announcement Eve had made.

Eve helped her check the return bus time instead.

When she left, she was still disappointed. She thanked Eve for the refund without congratulating her for the decision. Eve watched her go and resisted the urge to call after her with one more explanation.

The arrival helper came over from the table.

“Do we need to change the introduction?”

“Yes. I'll check the other bookings before we begin. Could you tell anyone arriving while I'm out that there's no animal interaction, and that they can have the same refund if that isn't what they expected?”

“Of course.”

Eve wrote the brief wording down so the helper did not have to improvise an argument at the door. Then she returned to the side room.

Dumpling lay inside his carrier with his paws tucked beneath him. Whatever strong thoughts he might have sensed through the morning, Eve could not sort them from unfamiliar smells, furniture sounds, or an ordinary wish to hide. She did not need an explanation to take him somewhere familiar.

At home, he stepped out, drank water, and headed for his bed.

Eve checked the usual things, then stood in the doorway for a moment with the empty carrier in her hand.

“I'll be back after the session.”

He turned once and settled.

She returned to the unit with three minutes before the advertised start. The helper had checked the remaining guests. One had also assumed there might be a resident cat but still wanted the activity. The others understood the format. Eve offered the refund option again herself without requiring anyone to justify taking it.

Four people stayed.

The session began awkwardly because Eve was still thinking about the empty fifth chair. She asked an opening question too quickly, then repeated it more simply when one participant looked puzzled.

They were sharing small household stories to practice making useful information understandable: the instruction that made perfect sense until another person tried to follow it, the label everyone overlooked, the object that moved without anyone remembering who moved it. It was a narrower activity than a general discussion of pet care, and it worked best when Eve listened instead of composing her next correction.

A man described searching three rooms for a brush that had been put away properly. Unfortunately, three people in his household had different definitions of properly.

Someone asked whether the brush had ever been found.

“In a drawer marked brushes.”

“That sounds reasonable.”

“It contained paintbrushes. I hadn't considered administrative cooperation between departments.”

The room laughed. Eve felt her attention return to the people in it.

By the end, one participant had rewritten a note. Another had decided to ask her partner which arrangement they actually used before buying containers for it. The person who had expected a cat said she liked the discussion but would prefer an evening next time.

Eve recorded that rather than treating her continued attendance as proof the announcement had been harmless.

After clearing, she opened the promotional post on her phone.

She could simply add a sentence at the bottom. That would preserve the attractive photograph, the responses, and the small satisfaction of seeing the new name shared. It would also leave the same image doing most of the work.

She replaced the image with a photograph of the actual table and materials, taken before anyone arrived. In the first lines she named the service: small hosted activities for people, with animals attending only when the particular event explicitly allowed it. There were no resident cats available for visitors to pet, and no booking included access to Dumpling.

Then she checked every place she had reused the announcement.

The short message to the notification list was worse than the public post. It had the picture and the dates, but readers had to follow the link for nearly everything else. Eve sent a correction to people booked on the next trial, stating what the afternoon involved and offering a full refund before they traveled if they had expected something different.

One person cancelled that evening.

Eve refunded them too.

The figures for the trial changed. The helper's agreed pay did not. Eve entered both refunds and the extra journey before calculating what remained.

At home, Dumpling slept while she ate a late dinner. Julian called at the time they had arranged, and she told him about the woman who had taken an afternoon off.

“I'd already learned this,” Eve said. “I took his photograph off the first booking page months ago.”

“And this time?”

“I made a new doorway into the same mistake.”

He was quiet for a moment.

“Is the doorway changed?”

“Yes.”

“Then you can see whether people understand it better.”

She rested her forehead against her hand. “I wanted the new name to look warm.”

“It can.”

“I know.”

They talked about his day after that, an ordinary account of a delayed delivery and a lunch he had actually eaten. Eve was glad not to remain the subject of the entire call.

When she went to wash her plate, Dumpling opened his eyes. He stretched one paw beyond the edge of his bed, considered following her, and decided against it.

On her phone, the revised announcement showed four chairs around a plain table.

The cat in her room had no opening hours at all.
