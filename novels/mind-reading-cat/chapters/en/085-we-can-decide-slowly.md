# Chapter 085 — We Can Decide Slowly

The first reply to Eve's new feature was about a missing pair of scissors.

Not an invitation to sponsor the series. Not a declaration that she had transformed everyday life. Someone kept finding the kitchen scissors in the room where they opened parcels and wanted to know whether the answer was buying another pair.

Eve read it on Monday morning and smiled.

Her post had gone up the previous evening after she finished the daylight insert and checked the frames. It had reached fewer people than the old clip of Julian handing her a pen. That was true. It had also prompted three people to describe small problems she could understand without knowing whom they were dating.

She answered that she couldn't decide the reader's household budget, but noticing where the scissors actually got used seemed a useful start. She asked whether they needed to travel between tasks at all.

Then she stopped. This was a conversation, not an unpaid personal organizing service she had promised to every stranger with a drawer.

The retailer's email remained open in another window. Her reply was due today.

Eve had prepared the revised concept and a fee for the smaller number of clips. Before sending it, she still needed the conversation she and Julian had deliberately kept out of Wednesday's filming slot.

They had arranged to meet at a café near the clinic after his morning work. It was not a romantic dinner she would quietly consume with business questions. They both knew what they were coming to discuss, and both had somewhere else to be afterward.

Eve arrived first and chose a table away from the counter's grinder. She ordered for herself, leaving his choice to him. The draft proposal stayed in her bag until he arrived.

Julian took off his coat and looked at the sugar bowl.

“Is this where we negotiate our soap preferences?”

“I have removed soap from the proposal. Too divisive.”

“A wise beginning.”

He ordered coffee, then asked to see what the retailer had actually requested. Eve showed him the original email, not a summary arranged to make her alternative seem inevitable.

He read the sample scenes carefully.

“They want a live question session too?”

“Yes. I've left it out of my alternative.”

“Good.”

She felt herself straighten at the speed of his answer.

He looked up. “That sounded abrupt.”

“A little.”

“I don't want to make our time together into a recurring broadcast. And I don't want viewers expecting individual medical answers because I'm in the room.”

“Neither do I.”

She waited while the coffee arrived. The interruption kept her from supplying a cheerful version of agreement before checking the part that still hurt.

“Can I ask something that's not about the job?” she said.

“Please.”

“When you say you don't want us used that way, I understand. I also sometimes hear that you don't want to be associated with me publicly. I know those aren't the same sentence.”

Julian set the cup down without drinking.

“I'm glad you said that.”

Eve looked at the rim of her own cup. She could hear the grinder start despite her careful choice of table. Apparently, equipment did not recognize the importance of personal disclosure.

“I don't want to hide that we're spending time together,” he said when it stopped. “If somebody asks whether we've been on dates, I'm not going to pretend we haven't.”

“All right.”

“But I would rather we chose what we are to each other before giving strangers a program built around it.”

She nodded slowly.

There were questions they had not answered, and she did not want a sales brief to become the place they answered them under pressure. That did not make the dates provisional in the way an unpaid invoice was provisional. It meant they were still learning to say things without somebody else supplying the next line.

“I want to be taken seriously,” she said. “Not necessarily watched.”

“I take this seriously.”

He said it plainly, without reaching for her hand to soften it into something less clear.

Eve finally looked up.

“So do I.”

For a few moments, they drank in silence. The coffee was evidently too hot; Julian put it down with an expression she would have enjoyed photographing if she had not also been trying not to laugh.

“What?” he asked.

“Nothing. Very dignified.”

“It has no warning label.”

“It's coffee.”

“An important clue.”

She laughed then, and the conversation no longer felt balanced on the edge of one wrong word.

Julian returned the phone. He was not willing to join the proposed commercial series. He would consider properly scoped professional work through the appropriate arrangements in future, but that wasn't a substitute yes to this inquiry. Eve accepted the answer without asking whether a shorter live session would change it.

She showed him her alternative: ordinary objects, practical use, no romantic premise and no implied veterinary endorsement.

“This is yours,” he said.

“Yes.”

“Do you want to make it?”

She checked her own response before answering.

“If they accept the smaller scope and fee. Not if I have to make twelve films for the price of three because the concept has less romance.”

“That sounds like a different conversation with them.”

“It is.”

They put the proposal away. Eve asked about the book rather than the clinic, and Julian admitted the photograph was still marking a page he had now read twice. He had begun using a second scrap of paper for his actual place.

“You've created a complicated system,” she said.

“Your next feature can assess it.”

“No. That one isn't for sale.”

He smiled.

When they stood to leave, he held out his hand outside the café. Eve took it. There were people on the pavement. Nobody was making a statement for their benefit, and nobody hurried to hide from them either.

They walked together to the corner where their routes separated. Julian had a bounded lunch break, not an afternoon he could stretch because a conversation had gone well. Eve let him return on time and went home to send her reply.

She reread the original offer once. It remained attractive in several ways. The representative had been courteous, the products were ordinary, and the fee might have eased the next month's accounts. Turning it down in its present form did not require her to discover something sinister about the company.

She wrote that she could not supply the proposed couple format or Julian's participation. She attached the short alternative concept, its deliverables, and the price. If that did not fit their campaign, she understood.

Then she sent it before fear could turn the last sentence into an offer to do the extra work for nothing.

Dumpling came to inspect the table as she closed the laptop. Eve moved her cup away from his nose and offered a hand instead. He sniffed, rubbed against her knuckles, and turned toward the window.

He had not witnessed the café conversation. He could not certify whether it had gone well. She did not need him to approach Julian's coat later as evidence that she had understood the man correctly.

She made lunch and washed the plate before checking for a reply.

The retailer responded in midafternoon. The representative thanked her for the clear alternative. For this campaign, the team wanted the original two-person personal format, so they would not proceed with her version. They would keep her work in mind for another brief.

Eve read the message, then opened the accounts sheet.

She had not counted the possible fee as confirmed income. Even so, removing the hopeful note beside it felt like losing something she had already touched. She deleted the note and looked at the month without it.

The numbers were manageable if the existing payments arrived as agreed. There was no room for the equipment purchase she had begun justifying to herself. She moved that item into the later column rather than pretending the refusal had made money irrelevant.

Then she sent a polite acknowledgment. No argument, no effort to persuade the representative that a commercial choice had exposed a moral failing. They had asked for one thing. She had offered another. This time there was no match.

For the rest of the afternoon, Eve finished a caption edit she had promised before the inquiry arrived. It was not exciting work. It paid a defined fee for a defined task, and the client needed it by tomorrow.

The supplied image had a problem she hadn't noticed in the small preview: the sign in the background gave an older collection time than the caption. Eve compared it with the client's last written confirmation. The new time was correct, but simply cropping the sign would leave the edge of a person's face floating in the corner.

She tried a tighter crop, disliked it, and looked through the approved alternatives. One less attractive photograph showed the table clearly without the misleading sign. She placed it beside the caption and sent the client a short explanation of the substitution. No additional shoot was needed. No dramatic story was missing. The useful task was making two pieces of information agree.

The client answered before five, approving the change and thanking her for catching it. Eve saved the final version, checked the export, and marked the job ready for delivery. It had taken concentration she might otherwise have spent rewriting the retailer's refusal into something more comforting.

She stretched her stiff hand and went to open the window a little, checking the secure screen before leaving it. Outside, somebody was laughing on a phone. The sound made the room feel less like the inside of her own account sheet.

Halfway through her final file check, a new response appeared beneath the scissors conversation. The reader had moved the parcel-opening supplies to one place and found a tape roll they had bought twice.

Eve laughed, then returned to the edit instead of immediately inventing a merchandise range around lost tape.

Near evening, she told Julian the outcome. He replied that he was sorry the alternative hadn't suited them. He did not say she had made the correct sacrifice for him, and he did not offer to replace the missing fee.

She appreciated both omissions.

*I still want to make the little practical posts,* she wrote. *At a size I can actually keep doing.*

*I liked the pen one.*

*You are a biased sample.*

*I have also lost pens.*

She smiled at the screen. Before replying, she put tomorrow's work in order and checked the date of the client's expected payment. Affection did not finish the accounts, but the accounts did not invalidate the pleasure.

Later, with the laptop closed, she opened the blue album. Their photograph occupied its sleeve without being asked to establish exclusivity, provide income, or reassure people who had never met them.

There was room beside it for something they hadn't done yet.

Eve left that space empty. Then she sent Julian a question about when he was next free, because taking their time did not mean waiting for somebody else to arrange it.
