# Chapter 072 — The Evening They Both Stayed

Eve had cleared a route from the door to the narrow cabinet. Standing in the entrance, she could now see that she had achieved this by putting every obstacle in front of the sofa.

Julian looked past her shoulder when she let him in.

“Shall we start with those?”

“One category of disorder at a time, please.”

He waited while she shifted a basket, then stepped inside. A screwdriver stuck out of his coat pocket. His shopping bag contained bread, which he put on the kitchen counter before returning to help.

“Do you think we need tools?” she asked.

“I thought we should look before deciding.”

“I appreciate this rigorous approach.”

Dumpling came out from behind the sofa, stopped, and watched Julian set his shoes beside the mat. The cat did not rush forward. He looked at the shopping bag, then at Eve, as if taking attendance without committing himself to the result.

Julian put the screwdriver on the table and waited for Eve to show him the cabinet.

It was narrow, wooden, and almost useful. She had positioned it beside the wardrobe when the rented table went back. Its lower shelf held equipment cases; the higher shelves had accumulated folded cloths and papers in a loose interpretation of categories.

“What do you want it to do?” he asked.

“Store my work things without offering them to the cat.”

“The cabinet or me?”

“You'd be a costly storage system.”

“I also leave the house.”

She laughed, and the ordinary sound loosened something in her. She had spent the afternoon afraid that the invitation would become awkward the moment he arrived: date conversation yesterday, furniture today, neither category knowing where to stand.

He looked at the cabinet's feet.

“We shouldn't move it loaded.”

“I know.”

“I'm saying that to myself. I was about to try.”

Eve fetched two clean towels for the equipment. They cleared one shelf at a time, placing the heavier items where nobody would trip over them. Julian picked up a folded reflector and stopped when it began opening in his hands.

“That one has opinions,” she said.

“It's nearly my height.”

“Don't fight it. Turn this edge.”

She showed him the fold. He copied the movement badly, tried again, and produced a shape resembling an injured pancake.

“Close enough?”

“For storing a pancake.”

He handed it back. She folded it properly while he watched without pretending the task had always been beneath his attention.

Dumpling climbed onto the empty chair.

Eve caught herself smiling at the domestic arrangement, then reached for another case. They were clearing a cabinet. Julian had not moved in, and a screwdriver was not a statement about the future.

The proposed new position failed immediately.

They turned the empty cabinet toward the wall beside the desk. Eve stepped back to admire the clear floor, opened the wardrobe, and struck the cabinet with its door.

“Oh.”

Julian folded his arms.

“Please don't say it,” she told him.

“I hadn't selected a sentence.”

“You were considering several.”

“Yes.”

They moved it back out, measured the gap with Eve's tape, and discovered that the original place was the only place it fitted without blocking something necessary. She had invited help to perform a three-foot circle.

“This is a disappointing renovation.”

“The room still has a door. I've seen worse outcomes.”

They returned the cabinet to its original position and checked that it stood steadily. Nothing required the screwdriver. Julian put it back in his bag without manufacturing a small repair to justify bringing it.

Before loading the shelves again, Eve tried opening the lowest case where it would actually sit. Its lid hit the shelf above. She pulled it forward, and Julian moved his foot out of the way.

“That explains why I keep leaving it on the floor,” she said.

They exchanged it with the smaller case. Now both lids opened without requiring the other person to hold something in midair. Eve knelt to test the arrangement herself, then stood and walked the route to the door carrying her usual bag. She could pass without turning sideways.

Nothing about this made a particularly satisfying photograph. It did mean she would use it tomorrow.

The useful change turned out to be inside the wardrobe.

Eve moved her work bag onto a shelf behind the closed door, leaving the handles tucked away. The yellow pouch containing her own spare key went in the higher closed cupboard, where it would not need to travel with ordinary filming supplies. She kept her usual keys on their familiar hook.

Julian held the empty equipment case while she decided which cables genuinely belonged in it. A surprising number belonged to objects she no longer used.

“You can put those aside without solving every cable tonight,” he said.

“I was beginning to enjoy the possibility of a completely improved personality.”

“Ambitious for a Wednesday.”

She made a small pile to sort later and shut the case.

Dumpling descended from the chair and began sniffing the cleared patch of floor. When Eve lifted his bed to sweep beside it, he moved closer, body low.

She stopped with the bed only an inch above the floor and put it down in the same place.

“Sorry,” she murmured. “I'm not moving that.”

Julian crouched a little way away, not reaching toward him.

“Is that what happened with the boxes?”

“Something like it. He watched the carton, then took the gloves. Today it was the pouch and a handkerchief. I saw him try to drag the bag after lunch.”

“Any problem with the key or fabric?”

“No. I checked both. Nothing missing from them.”

“Good.”

Eve told him about the clinic's reply and the details she had sent back. He listened without conducting an examination across her living room.

“I keep wanting to say he thinks I'm leaving,” she admitted.

“You are leaving sometimes.”

“Yes, but not forever.”

“That part is harder to explain with a bag.”

She looked at him.

He rested his forearms on his knees. “We can make the objects less disruptive. We can't know exactly what they mean to him because we found a pouch under a sofa.”

Eve nodded. She had known that before he said it. It helped that he said it while sitting beside the actual sofa rather than handing her a general theory that could be made to fit anything.

Dumpling sniffed the edge of his shoe.

Julian stayed still. After a moment the cat walked away, crossed the rug, and sat where he could see both of them.

“I thought he was trying to arrange us,” Eve said.

Julian looked up.

“The scarf. Putting things together. Obviously I'm not claiming he has a booking system.”

“I would worry about his cancellation policy.”

She smiled, but only briefly.

“What if it's less amusing than I make it sound?”

Julian glanced at Dumpling, then back at her. “Then we can stop making it amusing. Without deciding we know the entire reason.”

The bread bag rustled as it settled on the table. Dumpling's ears turned toward it.

“That reason might be lunch,” Eve said.

“He's already eaten?”

“On time. With a complaint about the service.”

“Then this bread remains mine.”

They restored the equipment, leaving the floor clear and the familiar bed untouched. Eve swept around it instead. The room looked less transformed than she had imagined, but she could find the bag, and nothing dangled temptingly from a chair.

When they sat down, Dumpling came to the middle of the rug.

He circled once. Twice. His front paws folded beneath him, then extended again. At last he lowered his chin, watching through half-closed eyes.

Eve noticed the change because she'd spent so much of the day noticing him tense. She wanted to point at it. She wanted Julian to agree that two people staying in one room had made something important happen.

Instead she kept her voice low.

“He's settled.”

“For now.”

“For now,” she agreed.

Neither of them stood up to test whether the peace would survive it.

Eve put soup on to heat. Julian sliced the bread at the counter with the only knife she had warned him was sharp. He asked before opening a cupboard, got the wrong plates when she answered vaguely, and made her laugh by holding both up for an inspection.

“Are these personal plates or professional plates?”

“One has a crack. That is the full classification.”

He selected the other one for the hotter soup. She took the cracked plate away entirely and found a bowl from the back of the shelf.

They ate at the table. Dumpling stayed on the rug, inconveniently uninterested in the scene Eve would have liked to remember as evidence of domestic harmony.

Julian talked about a book he had started three times because he kept falling asleep on the same page. Eve described the print shop's wrong-sized proof and the bent drawing pins. The conversation wandered into objects designed by people who never had to use them.

“Cupboard handles,” he said.

“You're going to explain the cupboard after all.”

“Only if there is a second course.”

“There isn't.”

“A sensible limit.”

After eating, she packed the leftover bread into a clean container for him to take home. Then she rinsed while he dried. He put one cup where the bowls belonged. Eve moved it without treating the mistake as a prediction of incompatibility. They had not reached the stage where either needed to know every shelf by heart.

Her phone lit on the desk with a message from the hall coordinator thanking her for the corrected notices. Eve read the preview and left the reply until later. There was nothing urgent in it.

Julian looked toward the rug. Dumpling had rolled far enough onto his side that one white foot pointed at the chair.

“I can go whenever you want the room back,” he said.

Eve turned off the tap.

It was an unexpectedly good offer. She could want him to stay without needing the cat as an excuse.

“I'd like you to stay a little longer,” she said. “If you want to.”

“I do.”

They sat on opposite ends of the sofa, with the folded reflector between them until Eve realized what she had done and put it away.

Julian watched her return.

“Was that supervising us?”

“It had a very strict brief.”

The cat opened his eyes at her laughter. He looked from one end of the sofa to the other, then lowered his head again.

For the first time, Eve wondered whether gathering their things was less a demand for romance than a smaller, sadder request she didn't know how to translate: the room remaining recognizable, the people not vanishing all at once. It was only a possibility. She didn't offer it to Julian as something the cat had told her.

They stayed another half hour.

When Julian finally stood, Dumpling lifted his head but didn't follow him immediately. Eve walked Julian to the door, thanked him for the cabinet's extensive journey, and accepted his equally serious congratulations on finding the spoon.

Afterward, she returned to the sofa.

Dumpling had risen. He watched the closed door, then came toward her. She let him lean against her shin and stroked him when he pressed closer.

The evening had not taught him that everyone stayed. Julian had gone home. Tomorrow Eve would go to work.

She reached for the book she'd left unfinished and made room beside her without moving his bed again.

Tonight, she could give him this ordinary room, and the rest of the evening they actually had.
