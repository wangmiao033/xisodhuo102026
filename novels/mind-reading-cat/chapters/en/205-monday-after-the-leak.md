# Chapter 205 — Monday After the Leak

On Monday morning, the contractor put two fingers against the damaged storage panel and lifted away a swollen strip of laminate.

Eve watched it bend.

“That part needs replacing,” he said.

She had hoped it might dry into its former personality. Apparently furniture did not feel obliged to reward optimism.

The building manager stood beside her in the cleared front room, holding the repair record. The rear recess was still separated from the usable space. Eve had arrived early enough to let the contractor in and stayed out of his way while he inspected the window joint, the marked wall, and the small area the water had reached.

The damage was local. It was also real.

“The outside seal is the source,” he explained when he came back to her. “We can deal with that first. The wall needs time and another check before it's finished. Covering it now would only hide the moisture.”

“How long?”

He gave her a likely range, then pointed out what would depend on the drying checks rather than the calendar.

Eve wrote it down exactly.

The electrical inspection had also been completed through the building's arranged service. The front room's supply and fittings were cleared for use; the isolated rear section would remain out of use while the work continued. She asked where the temporary boundary needed to stay and marked it on the floor plan instead of guessing from the position of a chair.

For several minutes the room seemed to consist entirely of things she could not move.

Then the contractor asked which parts belonged to the landlord and which were hers.

The window and affected building surface fell under the landlord's repair arrangements. Eve's storage unit and printed stock were separate. She took out the lease and the fit-out receipts, and the manager checked the relevant sections with her. They recorded what had been confirmed and what still needed a written answer. No one promised that every cost would be reimbursed.

When the contractor brought over the damaged carton, after confirming it could be removed, Eve opened it on a protected surface in the dry area.

The bottom packs had curled. Several display sheets were stuck together at one edge. The top section was untouched, separated by an inner sleeve she had almost discarded when packing it.

She counted the usable material first, then the damaged pieces. She took the necessary photographs and kept the record before arranging disposal through the ordinary route. There was no reason to display ruined paper in the front window.

The contractor gave her two options for her storage unit. A basic replacement panel and a simpler open shelf would restore the function. Replacing the whole fitted section with sealed cabinetry would cost much more and take longer.

He showed her a photograph of the larger option on his phone.

It was beautiful.

There were smooth doors, a continuous worktop, and no visible cardboard. Eve could immediately imagine a preparation area in which all her supplies behaved themselves. She could also imagine the public post announcing their improved space after a difficult week.

She asked for both prices in writing.

Then she looked at the actual cupboard behind the photograph.

Only one corner had swollen. The room did not require a new identity because water had entered through a window joint.

“Quote the basic repair for now,” she said. “Separate the necessary work from anything optional.”

“Of course.”

He seemed neither disappointed nor impressed. He had other jobs to visit and no personal investment in her triumphant reopening.

By the time Robin arrived for their paid shift, the outside repair was underway and Eve had three pages of notes, a smaller pile of usable cards, and a very clear desire to buy something that would make her feel better.

Robin set down their bag.

“Do we still have a business?”

“Yes. It has an unattractive corner.”

“So it remains recognizable.”

Eve laughed, grateful for the joke.

She showed them the confirmed limits. The rear recess would remain closed while it dried and repairs were completed. The front room could be used, but they needed to check the temporary layout against their ordinary access and capacity arrangements. Anything requiring the full room would wait.

“What about Wednesday?” Robin asked.

“That's today's decision.”

The booking was a small, adults-only instructions exchange. Four participants had signed up. It needed a table, chairs, paper, and somewhere to spread out sample household notes. It did not need the wet storage unit, the delayed mats, or a photographer.

It did need people to move through the room without squeezing past stacked furniture.

They tried the obvious arrangement first. Eve shifted a dry table toward the front, and Robin walked the route with a shopping bag standing in for someone's belongings.

At the second chair, they stopped.

“My imaginary visitor has become furniture.”

Eve moved the chair. The new position narrowed the entrance.

“No,” she said.

They put it back and stood in the middle of the room.

For months, Eve had occasionally looked at the empty corner and calculated what she might earn if it held another display. Now it held one chair and an empty flowerpot, with enough clear floor around both to be useful.

Robin followed her glance.

“The small table?”

“Let's try.”

They brought over the folding table already used for preparation, checked it was stable, and arranged four chairs around it without pushing them into the passage. Robin walked the route again, this time pulling out a chair as a participant might.

There was room.

Eve sat in each place. From one chair, the temporary barrier dominated the view. From another, the light came over the wrong shoulder for writing. Neither problem made the session impossible. They turned the table slightly and left the best route untouched.

The flowerpot went on a safe shelf, still empty.

“This isn't a storage area,” Eve said, mostly to herself. “If we fill the rest with boxes, it stops working.”

Robin moved one carton she had already begun edging toward the space.

“Caught you.”

“Thank you.”

They kept dry reserve stock on the existing front shelves, within their load limits, and left the floor clear. The setup was smaller and less photogenic than the usual one. It allowed four people to sit down and do the thing they had booked.

Eve confirmed the arrangement with the building manager against the restrictions he had supplied. The ongoing work would be kept separate from booked use, and the remaining repair visits would be timed accordingly. If the checks changed, the session would change too.

Then she contacted Wednesday's participants before the afternoon deadline she had promised.

She explained the temporary setup, the restricted area, and the unchanged content of the small session. Anyone who no longer wanted to attend could take a refund. She did not send an old photograph of the full room and hope the difference would seem charming on arrival.

Three replied promptly. One chose the refund because uncertainty around the repair made them uncomfortable. Eve processed it without trying to persuade them that the cleared area was safe enough for their feelings. The remaining participant replied later, confirming they still wanted to attend.

Three people would come on Wednesday.

Robin checked the revised preparation time and their booked hours. The smaller group would not produce the income Eve had hoped for, but it could run without hidden extra work. Larger sessions stayed postponed until the space was ready.

After the messages, Eve sat down with the quotes and Sunday's accounts.

The basic furniture repair fit within the repair reserve, narrowly. Reprinting every damaged display immediately did not. The optional cabinetry belonged to a different financial universe.

She crossed out the full reprint order and counted what was actually needed for the next two small sessions. The usable packs covered most of it. A few plain replacement pages would do for the rest.

The decorative display could wait. So could a new sign she had been considering before the rain. Robin's hours stayed in the figures. Eve scheduled another paid editing block for herself, using work already agreed with a client rather than assuming future bookings would repair the account by enthusiasm alone.

When she finished, the month looked tight but legible.

There was no spare column marked Julian.

At lunchtime, he sent a message asking how the assessment had gone. Eve replied with the actual result: local damage, staged repairs, three-person Wednesday session possible in the cleared area.

Then she added, I did not buy an entirely new cupboard to manage my feelings.

His answer came later.

A difficult loss for the cupboard industry.

She laughed in the quiet room, then went back to work.

The contractor finished the first stage and explained the next checks before leaving. Eve collected the written record and confirmed when access would next be needed. She did not let a repaired exterior joint become permission to paint over a wall that still needed drying time.

The damaged panel would be replaced after the conditions were suitable. The wall finish would follow. The display could wait longer still. Each stage had a purpose and a cost; none required the whole venue to become something larger overnight.

Late in the afternoon, Robin sat at the temporary table and tested the instructions exercise with a blank sheet.

“Where would you put this?” they asked.

Eve pointed toward the sample shelf, then realized her old answer sent them toward the closed section.

“Here,” she corrected, moving the sample to the accessible shelf beside the table.

They tried the exercise again. This time nobody needed to get up midway. Eve changed the room directions in the confirmation message and placed a simple label where visitors would look.

It was a small improvement she could actually finish today.

When Robin's shift ended, they left the table set without extra decorations. Eve remained long enough to check the next contractor appointment, put the stock record away, and move the waste bag to its proper collection point.

From the doorway, the empty corner no longer looked empty. It held a modest table, three waiting places for Wednesday, and enough space for somebody to change their mind about where to sit.

The rear area was still ugly. The budget still worried her. Tomorrow would contain more calls.

She turned off the lights in the unused section and looked once more at the small table. Months earlier she had resisted filling that patch of floor because people might need somewhere to pause. Now the room needed it too.

At home, she found Julian taking dry clothes off the rack. Dumpling was asleep beneath it, safely clear of the legs, with one of his toy mice tucked against his stomach.

“How much is fixed?” Julian asked.

“The part that stops more water getting in. The rest has stages.”

“Dinner also has stages. I've completed finding a pan.”

“I can contribute finding vegetables.”

She put down her bag and joined him in the kitchen. Her shoes needed drying. The business would need careful work for weeks. She was tired enough that peeling an onion seemed briefly ambitious.

Julian passed her the bowl, and she began.

The venue had a usable corner. Home had dinner underway. Neither needed to become a miracle before she could close the door for the night.
