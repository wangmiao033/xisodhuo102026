# Chapter 081 — The Audience Is More Impatient Than We Are

By Monday morning, three strangers had married Eve to Julian and one had expressed concern about the seating plan.

None of them had attended Friday's dinner. They were commenting beneath an old clinic video in which Julian handed her a pen.

Eve watched the exchange again. She had needed to correct a label. He had possessed a pen. Apparently, this was enough evidence to book a venue.

The comments were mostly friendly. That made them harder to dismiss with the brisk irritation she reserved for strangers who explained her own work to her. Someone had written that the way he looked at her was lovely. Someone else wanted to know whether Dumpling would carry the rings.

The cat was currently asleep with his nose against a chair leg. Eve declined to consult him about his availability.

She clicked away, answered a question about the label, and opened her work inbox.

The proposal arrived between a receipt and a request to move a deadline earlier without changing the fee. A small pet-supply retailer had seen the clinic collaborations. They wanted a series of short films: useful products, friendly conversation, a familiar face viewers trusted.

So far, ordinary.

Then Eve reached the phrase *the vet couple everyone is rooting for*.

She leaned back.

The offered fee was not enormous. It was, however, enough to make her read the phrase twice before objecting to it.

A sample outline suggested a home scene, a shopping scene, and a question-and-answer session about how the two of them cared for their cat. The email cheerfully acknowledged that schedules were busy and said they could work around both of them.

Nobody had asked whether both of them were available to be described that way.

Eve put the kettle on before replying. While it heated, she found herself mentally improving the sample scenes. The shopping sequence was weak. A comparison of ordinary storage choices might work better. She could cut the question session to three useful questions and remove the promises about product suitability.

She could make it competent.

That was not the same as wanting to do it.

Her phone lit with a message from Julian asking whether she had recovered the book she'd left beneath her coat on Friday. She had. It was on the table, carrying a receipt as a bookmark because neither of them had remembered a proper one.

For a moment the two conversations stood side by side: an ordinary question from somebody she was enjoying knowing, and an email assigning them an already recognizable identity.

She answered the ordinary question first.

*Found it. The coat is claiming credit.*

Then she wrote to the retailer, thanking them and asking for the proposed deliverables, usage period, and whether the concept could be developed without a personal-relationship premise. She made no representation on Julian's behalf. She did not forward his contact details.

The reply came before lunch. They were open to discussing specifics, but the pairing was what had interested them. Would she be available for a short call?

Eve agreed to fifteen minutes that afternoon.

She nearly sent Julian a screenshot with a joke about their new commercial marriage. Her thumb hovered over it. Making it funny first would make it harder to say she was actually considering part of the offer.

Instead she asked whether they could talk after his work, specifying that it was about an inquiry involving both of them and that she had agreed to nothing.

His reply was brief: after six, a call should be possible.

She put the phone down and went to collect the revised proof for another client. Outside, the pavement was damp from rain she had managed not to notice while reading strangers' opinions of a pen.

At the print counter, she found that the shop had centered a line she had deliberately aligned left.

“It looks more balanced,” the assistant said.

“The left edge needs to match the arrow. People are reading it while walking.”

He looked at the page, then held it farther away. “Right. I see it.”

They corrected the proof. Eve checked the final version rather than accepting that a good explanation had automatically changed the file. It took eight minutes. Nobody asked whether the arrow was in love with the heading.

She enjoyed the simplicity so much that she bought a pastry on the way home.

The retailer's representative called while she was scraping its last flakes from a plate. The woman sounded prepared, friendly, and under a deadline of her own. Eve liked her immediately, which did not improve the problem.

“We wouldn't need you to act,” the woman explained. “That's why your material appealed. You already have that natural ease.”

“The clinic films have a job to do,” Eve said. “They're planned collaborations, with review before release.”

“Of course. We don't want anything unprofessional.”

“I'm also not offering somebody else's private time as part of my quote.”

A pause, then the sound of typing.

“Understood. Would he consider joining as an expert instead?”

“That would require a separate discussion with him and the appropriate professional arrangements. I can't promise it.”

Eve described an alternative centered on owners demonstrating how they organized everyday supplies. No implied recommendation of a product for a particular animal. No home tour that exposed anyone's address. The retailer could show the actual dimensions and materials, rather than borrow intimacy to make the products seem trustworthy.

The woman listened. She asked sensible questions about length and whether Eve had examples.

Then she said, “I do think we'd lose some of the warmth.”

Eve looked at the crumbs.

There it was: the part of the job she could not improve through a cleaner shot list.

“Possibly,” she said. “I can send a short concept for the alternative. Not a finished treatment before we know there's a fit.”

“That would help. Our team meets next week.”

They agreed Eve would respond by Monday. She did not say yes to the series. She also did not announce that asking had made the retailer a villain. The call ended with two people knowing more clearly what was still undecided.

Afterward, she opened the old video again.

The pen changed hands. Julian glanced toward the label. Eve said something the microphone had barely caught, and he smiled.

She remembered the moment differently from the comments. She had been annoyed with a spelling mistake and pleased that he had waited for her to finish. There was affection in the memory now, but she couldn't reach backward and certify exactly when it had begun.

Below the video, someone asked why they wouldn't simply confirm what everybody knew.

Eve closed the page.

At six twenty, Julian called. She told him the inquiry's wording, the actual offer, and what she had said. She did not start with the most ridiculous comment in order to make him laugh before he could object.

“So the proposed job is us?” he asked.

“Partly. There are products. But they want the impression of us choosing them together.”

“We don't choose your soap together.”

“I know. You might have poor taste in soap.”

“Very possible.”

She smiled, then looked down at the email.

“I wanted to ask how you feel about people knowing we're going on dates. Separately from whether we make anything for this company.”

He was quiet long enough for her to hear a door close somewhere on his end.

“I'm not upset by people knowing that,” he said. “I don't want a company deciding what it means before we've decided ourselves.”

“I don't either.”

The answer should have settled her immediately. It didn't. Part of her had wanted something warmer, less qualified, and she disliked noticing that while he was answering the question she had actually asked.

“Does it bother you that they're interested?” she said.

“No. Does it bother you?”

“Some of it feels nice.”

She pressed the corner of the receipt flat.

“Some of it makes me want to skip to a part where nobody can wonder whether I'm imagining this.”

“You aren't imagining the dates.”

“I know.”

“Friday wasn't a label correction.”

She laughed. “An important distinction.”

He said he would rather talk about the work proposal in person than make a quick decision over a call after a long day. Eve agreed. Wednesday's clinic filming was already arranged, but she suggested they leave this subject until both were done, not slip it into a professional slot.

“We don't have to answer the personal part on a client's timetable,” he said.

“No. I'll keep those separate.”

They talked for a few more minutes about his lost umbrella, which had turned up exactly where he had put it to avoid losing it. Eve told him about the centered arrow. Neither story was likely to make a compelling sponsored series.

After the call, she sat with the small disappointment she had not entirely explained.

It was not that he had denied anything. He had been quite clear. She wanted the whole uncertain, exciting thing wrapped in a sentence that would survive being shown to other people.

She could want that without turning it into something he owed the retailer.

At the desk, Eve drafted the alternative concept. She kept it to one page: a person looking for a familiar item, a storage arrangement that failed, and a revised arrangement shown honestly. The objects would have measurable features. The people would not have to perform a relationship to make them worth looking at.

Before closing the document, she costed the alternative in the margin. Without Julian, she would need an ordinary demonstrator if she wanted hands other than her own in the frame. That meant paid time, not asking Sophie to drop by for a favor. The smaller fee she was considering would leave less after the extra person and editing hours. She reduced the proposed number of finished clips rather than hiding the work inside her weekend.

The arithmetic made the original offer look attractive again. She allowed herself to see that. Refusing its premise did not make the lost money imaginary, and a sensible alternative still had to pay for the person making it.

Then she returned to the public comments.

She answered the label question properly, linking the clinic's existing general information rather than dispensing advice herself. She left the affectionate guesses alone. There was no need to scold people for enjoying a possibility, and no need to reward their certainty with private details.

One new comment simply said the video had helped them prepare a question for their appointment.

Eve read that one twice too.

Dumpling woke as she stood to make dinner. He stretched, walked directly past the laptop, and sat near his bowl. Whatever he might sense nearby, he had no access to the strangers deciding the course of her life from another room, another street, another country.

She could be grateful for that.

She fed him, put her own food on, and moved the receipt into the book before she lost her place.

The page she had marked was still waiting. Nobody watching from outside could read it ahead of her.
