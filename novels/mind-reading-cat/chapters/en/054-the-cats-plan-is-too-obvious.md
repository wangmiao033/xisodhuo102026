# Chapter 054 — The Cat's Plan Is Too Obvious

Something blue was moving down the hall.

Eve watched it from the kitchen doorway, holding a piece of toast that had finally submitted to being cooked on both sides. At first she saw only the end of the scarf, sliding in small, determined jerks across the floor.

Then Dumpling backed around the corner with the other end in his mouth.

“Excuse me.”

He stopped.

The scarf remained between his teeth. His expression suggested that she had interrupted a private and entirely legitimate delivery.

“That isn't yours.”

He took another backward step.

Eve set her toast on a plate and followed. Dumpling released the scarf beside her bed, sat on one folded corner, and looked toward the doorway as if waiting for her to appreciate the arrangement.

There was cat hair on the blue wool. There was also a small damp patch where he had carried it, though no thread appeared broken.

“You could have chosen a sock.”

Dumpling blinked.

She crouched without pulling the fabric out from under him. His little mouse lay beneath the bedside table. Eve dragged it into view, paused, and moved it a short distance along the floor. He watched, shifted his weight, and finally stood to put one paw on it.

She lifted the scarf while it was free.

The cat immediately lost interest in the mouse.

“Remarkable coincidence.”

She checked both ends, gently shook off the loose hair over the wastebasket, and put the scarf on a high shelf to dry. She would ask Julian how he preferred it cleaned rather than give it a disastrous wash in an effort to be helpful.

Dumpling sat below the shelf and looked up.

For a moment, the whole arrangement seemed so obvious that she nearly laughed aloud. Julian's scarf beside her bed; the cat seated nearby like a person who had successfully reserved a chair. It was tempting to imagine a complete little scheme, full of intentions as neat as a story caption.

But Dumpling had carried familiar-smelling things before. He liked warm fabric and objects someone had recently handled. Whatever else might sometimes pass through that orange-and-white head, he had not explained this morning's destination.

The obvious part was her own reaction.

She wanted to tell Julian about it. She wanted him to laugh in the low, surprised way he had laughed at the park. She wanted another evening that did not begin with checking an invoice or choosing a useful subject.

Her phone lay on the kitchen table. Julian had replied the previous night while she was washing her face.

*Thank you. I realized halfway home. No hurry—I'll collect it another time.*

Eve had answered with a simple *Of course*, then spent an unreasonable amount of time considering what another time might mean.

Now she opened the conversation and typed:

*Your scarf has been kidnapped. You may have to come and rescue it.*

She looked at the sentence.

It was funny enough. It also made Dumpling responsible for an invitation she wanted to send herself.

She deleted it.

*The scarf is fine, though Dumpling has taken an interest. Are you free for dinner on Tuesday? You could collect it first, and we could go to the little place by the library.*

Her thumb hovered above the screen.

There were ways to make the message smaller. She could add *if you're passing anyway*. She could say she had been meaning to ask him about a caption. She could offer to leave the scarf at reception and remove the whole inconvenience of having said what she wanted.

Instead she checked that Tuesday really was free in her calendar, then sent the message.

The room did not change. The toaster needed emptying. Her breakfast had gone cold. Dumpling began washing his chest with the complete indifference of someone who did not consider human correspondence part of his duties.

Eve ate the toast at the table.

She had set aside the morning for laundry and a small repair to the loose lining of her bag. Neither task became unnecessary because she had invited someone to dinner. She sorted the clothes, left the scarf alone, and found the little sewing tin beneath a pile of spare charging cables.

The tin contained six buttons, three needles, several safety pins, and enough tangled thread to suggest that someone had been trying to raise it in captivity.

She took the bag to the window for better light.

A seam near the inner pocket had opened, allowing coins to slip into the lining. She had blamed the loss of two small amounts of money on her own forgetfulness before discovering that her bag had developed a savings account.

By the time she freed the second coin, she was laughing.

Dumpling came to inspect the sound. Eve put the needle back in the tin before he jumped onto the nearby chair, then moved the open bag to her lap where she could keep the thread out of reach.

“No financial advice from you.”

He settled with his back toward her.

Her phone sounded while she was trying to thread the needle. She glanced at the screen, saw a message from Sophie, and felt briefly ridiculous for the way her attention had leaped.

*Does this look crooked, or is my kitchen crooked?*

The photograph showed a small planter on a windowsill. Two pots stood inside it. One plant leaned toward the light with a lack of architectural discipline.

Eve enlarged the photograph, then resisted drawing a line across it.

*The plant has strong opinions. The box looks straight to me.*

Sophie replied with a photograph of a ruler laid along the sill.

*The kitchen is crooked. Vindicated.*

Eve called her. Sophie answered over the sound of water running.

“Don't tell me to grow herbs,” her friend said. “I have bought two plants that came with modest expectations.”

“I was calling to congratulate the ruler.”

“It has done excellent work.”

Eve finished the first stitch while Sophie described the disagreement between the planter measurements in the book and the space available beneath her window. The narrow box had fitted only after she removed an old stack of takeaway menus from the sill.

“An ecosystem was displaced,” Sophie said.

“Any endangered restaurants?”

“Two had already closed.”

They talked about ordinary things: a missing delivery, a pair of shoes that had become less comfortable rather than more, the book Eve had finished and still considered guilty of cheating.

When Sophie asked about the film, Eve told her the projector had broken.

“Oh, no.”

“It was actually a good evening.”

“Did they get it working?”

“No. We walked.”

Sophie waited for the rest, not with a theatrical silence but with the patience of someone watering a plant at the same time.

“He walked me home,” Eve said. “It wasn't on his way.”

“Nice.”

“I asked him about it.”

“And?”

“He said he wanted to keep talking to me.”

The water stopped.

“That is also nice.”

Eve smiled at the bag in her lap. “You're being restrained.”

“I have no objection to two adults enjoying a walk. I would object to being asked to analyze the punctuation of his next message.”

“I haven't asked.”

“Excellent progress.”

Eve told her about the scarf and the dinner invitation. Saying it aloud made the request sound smaller and more ordinary than it had looked on the screen.

“I almost blamed the cat,” she admitted.

“For inviting him?”

“For requiring him to return.”

“Does the cat have a reservation system now?”

“No.”

“Then perhaps leave him out of the administration.”

Eve laughed, careful to keep the needle away from her fingers.

Sophie had to go to the shop for something to put under the planter so water would not mark the sill. Eve did not offer to research every available tray. They said goodbye, and she completed a crooked but serviceable row of stitches.

Julian's reply arrived when she was putting the needle away.

*Tuesday works. I can be at yours around six forty-five after I change. Dinner sounds good. Is the scarf still in one piece?*

She read it twice, then answered.

*One piece. A little cat hair and a damp corner. I haven't washed it because I don't know what the label requires.*

*Please don't. I'll deal with it. He has good taste in fabric.*

*Questionable taste in storage locations.*

She started to describe exactly where Dumpling had left it, then stopped. The message would make the bed into a joke neither of them had chosen to enter. She could let an ordinary evening remain ordinary without pushing it forward for a reaction.

*Six forty-five is good. I'll check the restaurant's opening hours.*

The little place by the library was open on Tuesdays. Eve checked its current menu too, not because dinner required a presentation but because choosing somewhere she could comfortably afford was easier before she sat down. There were simple meals, soups, and a few tables by the window. The daily special was marked as subject to change. She liked the honesty of a menu that admitted Tuesday had not happened yet. She remembered the chairs as comfortable, a quality she was apparently learning to value.

She sent Julian the name and asked whether it suited him.

*It does,* he wrote. *I've been meaning to try it.*

There. A place, a time, and a person who had said yes. No cat emergency. No manufactured work errand. No need to turn a forgotten scarf into a recurring administrative failure.

Eve put the phone down and carried the laundry to dry.

By afternoon the flat held a row of clean sleeves and the faint smell of soap. She wiped the outdoor mats before rolling them away, shook grass from the carrying bag, and checked that the repaired pocket could now contain a coin without swallowing it.

Dumpling slept through most of this. When he woke, he went to the kitchen shelf and looked up once more.

“The owner is coming on Tuesday,” Eve told him.

His ears moved at her voice.

“But we're going out.”

He yawned.

She could have photographed him beneath the shelf and written a joke about his elaborate matchmaking plan. People would have supplied hearts and predictions. They might have made the uncertainty feel charming and shared instead of private and slightly frightening.

Eve left the phone on the table.

She picked up the fabric mouse, and this time she moved it because the cat was awake and might enjoy playing, not because she needed to distract him from somebody else's belongings.

Dumpling crouched. His tail made one small, concentrated movement.

Then he sprang past the mouse, skidded past her slipper, and attacked the mouse's shadow.

Eve laughed, following his untidy turn across the room.

Tuesday was arranged. For the rest of Sunday, she had nowhere else she needed to be.
