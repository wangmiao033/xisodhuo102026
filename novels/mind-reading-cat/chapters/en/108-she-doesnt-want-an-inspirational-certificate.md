# Chapter 108 — She Doesn't Want an Inspirational Certificate

Dr. Clark wanted to thank Tessa at the end of the film.

“Something about the people who keep everything running,” he said.

Eve sat with the revised caption sheet across her knees. The last appointment had finished, but the clinic had not emptied. Somewhere beyond the staff-room door, a drawer opened and shut twice.

“That could be a credit,” she said. “If everyone agrees to how they're named.”

“I was thinking of a message to the staff as well. At Friday's meeting.”

Tessa, standing beside the kettle, put down an unused mug.

“Could we discuss the work first?”

“Of course. I'm saying it deserves recognition.”

“Yes. I would like that recognition to include who covers the desk while I'm on a break.”

The sentence landed without raised voices. Eve suddenly wished her caption sheet were an actual reason to leave rather than an object she could stare at politely.

Dr. Clark glanced toward her. “Perhaps we should have this conversation separately.”

“We should,” Tessa said. “I don't need Eve filming it. But the film can't promise a private conversation with reception if I have to leave the counter unattended to have one.”

Eve looked up. That was the unresolved line she had brought them together to check.

“I can leave it out until there's a confirmed arrangement,” she said. “I don't need to know the details of anyone's employment discussion.”

“Thank you.” Tessa turned back to Dr. Clark. “I'd like a proper time for that discussion. Not the two minutes before you go home.”

He took out his calendar.

They chose a time the next morning. Tessa said she would bring a record of duties she was already doing, and Dr. Clark said he would bring the staffing schedule. Eve packed her caption sheet. Nobody asked her to turn the exchange into a stirring scene about finding a voice.

Outside, she discovered Julian waiting to collect his bicycle. He had not been part of the conversation and she did not supply it to him as news.

“How did the captions go?” he asked.

“One line is waiting for a real arrangement behind it.”

“Sounds sensible.”

“It is. I would also like to finish the job.”

“You're allowed both.”

They walked as far as the corner together. Eve told him about the other work she had to complete the next day, and he told her he was going to cook something that used the vegetables in his refrigerator before they became a personal accusation.

She wanted to invite him over instead. Then she remembered that wanting company did not mean his evening was unoccupied. She asked about Saturday, and they agreed to check their schedules the next day.

At home, she made a rough version of the film with the unconfirmed line removed. It still worked. The absence was smaller on screen than in the conversation that had produced it.

The next afternoon, Tessa asked whether Eve could meet for twenty minutes after her scheduled finish. She wanted to check the sample handover caption before the next edit, not discuss her meeting in detail. Eve offered another paid production slot instead of accepting unpaid review time simply because Tessa had suggested it.

Tessa paused on the phone.

“I'd prefer that, actually.”

“Then I'll ask Dr. Clark to confirm cover.”

When they met two days later, Tessa had a sandwich wrapped in paper and no intention of letting it go cold while someone talked.

Eve spread the caption options on the table. One said a member of staff would explain the delay. The other specified that reception would pass on the update available from the clinical team.

“The second,” Tessa said after swallowing. “I can pass on what's confirmed. I can't produce an explanation somebody hasn't given me.”

“Does that wording feel too formal?”

“A little. But don't solve that by making me responsible for a miracle.”

They shortened the sentence without changing who was meant to do what.

Eve resisted looking at the folder beside Tessa's elbow. Tessa noticed anyway.

“My own notes,” she said.

“You don't have to show me.”

“I wasn't going to. I may show you the part that affects the film when it's agreed.”

“All right.”

For a moment Eve felt excluded from a story she had helped bring into view. The feeling embarrassed her. She had wanted to see Tessa's work; that did not entitle her to accompany every private decision afterward.

Tessa finished half the sandwich, then said, “He listened. We didn't settle everything.”

Eve waited.

“He thinks some duties fit the role I already have. I think others were added without being discussed. We're going through them. There's a difference between arranging a room and becoming the default person who absorbs every empty space in the rota.”

“What would you like from me?” Eve asked.

“For the film? Accurate wording. For this? Don't tell me how brave I am and then ask for another favor.”

Eve winced. “Fair.”

“You haven't done it today.”

“I appreciate the provisional assessment.”

Tessa smiled around another bite. The conversation moved to a broken zip on her coat. Eve knew enough to recognize that the change of subject was not a failure of trust.

On Friday, Dr. Clark asked Eve to demonstrate the current film at the ordinary staff meeting. It was within the agreed review scope, and she confirmed how long they needed her before accepting. Julian sat on the far side of the table with a pen that kept refusing to write.

The film played without incident until the caption about handing clinical questions to the appropriate person. A colleague asked what would happen during the busiest overlap, when everyone was already doing something else.

Dr. Clark began answering with the usual arrangement, then stopped when Tessa opened the rota.

“That's where I need an actual named cover,” she said. “Not whoever notices.”

Eve paused the film. She wasn't chairing this meeting. She could make space for the people who were.

Julian put down his failing pen.

“If I haven't given the desk an update, that isn't a reception failure,” he said. “I can own my part of the handover. But I can't promise availability during every consultation either. We need a route that covers that.”

He did not announce what Tessa should accept, or look to Eve for approval. Another colleague described where the proposed cover would collide with a different task. Dr. Clark asked them to mark the actual times rather than arguing from a general sense of always being busy.

There were several uncomfortable silences. None was improved by Eve searching for a joke.

Eventually they agreed to test a limited cover arrangement for two weeks, with specific periods protected and a route for an urgent interruption. Anything requiring clinical judgment would still go to clinical staff. The exact pay and role discussion remained between Tessa and Dr. Clark; nobody asked her to negotiate it before the whole room.

“Then the film should show the trial arrangement?” somebody asked.

“Only if it's ready to be offered to visitors,” Eve said. “Otherwise we can keep the public wording narrower and update it when you know.”

Tessa nodded. Dr. Clark agreed to the narrower wording for now.

Before the meeting ended, Tessa asked who would tell the person covering the desk when a planned break had been delayed. The first answer was that she could ask around. She looked at the rota again. Dr. Clark crossed out the vague note and named the person responsible for confirming the handover that day. It was a small alteration, made with a pen that worked. Eve noticed Julian studying it. A few minutes later he asked where he should leave an update if he could not come to the counter himself. They agreed on the existing staff channel, with a person checking it during the trial rather than an assumption that everyone had seen everything.

After the meeting, he stayed behind while Eve unplugged her laptop.

“I hadn't realized how much the desk was carrying,” he said.

She could have replied that this was the power of storytelling. It would have made an attractive line for her own case study.

Instead she said, “Tessa's been able to describe it. The film just needed me to ask.”

He accepted that with a tired little nod and went to speak to the colleague arranging the first cover shift.

Eve packed the adapter into the wrong pocket and had to find it again. Julian, still at the table, offered her his pen.

“Useless,” he said. “Would you like it for your collection of production hazards?”

“No. I have cables for that.”

He put it in his own bag.

“Saturday?” he asked.

“Lunch, if you're still free.”

“I am.”

“Something not served in this room.”

“A broad field.”

Their date was arranged in three sentences, after which he returned to work and she left. Neither of them had resolved the clinic by being in love.

On Saturday, Eve found him outside a small noodle place, reading the menu as if he hadn't already seen it through the window twice. He asked about her morning. She told him about a client who had supplied six photographs called final and then asked which one she'd used.

“Obviously the final one,” he said.

“That's what made the conversation difficult.”

Over lunch, the clinic came up only when Julian mentioned that the new cover trial had exposed a task nobody had formally owned. He didn't give Tessa's private position or ask Eve to carry a message.

“Does that mean it isn't working?” she asked.

“It means we've found a task. Someone has to arrange it.”

Eve rolled a noodle around her fork, lost it, and tried again.

She had wanted the filming process to produce an elegant improvement she could point to. Instead it had produced changes people would have to remember on Tuesday, when there was no camera and everyone was tired.

After lunch they wandered through a secondhand bookshop. Julian found a book with a badly repaired spine and showed Eve the page that would fall out if he bought it. She found one containing somebody's old shopping list.

“Proof that readers have lives,” she said.

“Or forget onions.”

She bought the book after checking it contained all its actual pages. The list stayed between two chapters, an ordinary trace rather than a mystery requiring investigation.

At home that evening, Tessa sent the approved public wording through the agreed work channel, during the scheduled review period. No details of her compensation appeared in it. The cover arrangement was still a trial, and the film would explain only the current way to ask for help.

Eve made the final change and returned the review copy. She did not add a triumphant closing caption about a woman finally being appreciated.

Tessa had asked for something more specific than applause. Eve could respect that by finishing the work she had actually been hired to do, then leaving her colleague to decide what happened next.
