# Chapter 250 — Someone Else in the House Can Hear

When the sitting room grew quiet enough to hear a book being turned over, Theo turned his over three times.

On the third turn he discovered the back was still the back. He put it in Julian's lap to be dealt with. Nell leaned against Julian's other leg and studied the place where the end of his sock had gone thin. Eve could still smell warm sugar when she went past the dining room. The last of the visitors had left, apart from Sophie, who was in the kitchen cutting the loaf she'd brought. The knife went neatly through the crust. Each cut made her pause in whatever she'd been saying to herself.

Eve had meant to take the account book back to her work shelf when she put away the birthday things. It had only got as far as the hall cabinet, above the children's reach. She picked it up with the envelope still clipped inside. She didn't have another invoice to enter. She had spent a portion of one afternoon the previous week walking round a room that the team had reorganized while she was away, and she could now pull out her copy of the room plan without wanting to make them put everything back. The few hours she had chosen to work fitted the following week on the same terms.

She slid the book in beside the plans. There was a pencil in the shallow dish she used for loose things. She took it over to the other shelf and left it with the linen notebook, open just long enough to see the line she'd drawn around Dumpling's misplaced ear. Of all the things she'd wanted to show somebody on the day she got it right, this was one she had never felt she ought to send anywhere.

“Did we agree I was going to do something about dinner?” Julian called in a low voice.

“You agreed you were going to get the dishes down,” Eve said. “Sophie and I said we'd get the rest out.”

“I'm being used to turn a book over.”

“Does it make enough difference?”

He opened the book from the back. Theo made a sound she suspected was a complaint. Julian turned it the other way again and let him get an entire hand between its pages.

Eve went into the kitchen. She and Sophie set out the bowls they'd made room for earlier. Everything was already prepared and almost ready to bring to the table. The bread was all slightly thicker at one end. Sophie gave herself that end to check it and offered Eve the one beside it.

“I thought you and I were meant to wait and eat with the others,” Eve said.

“I was checking whether the knife worked.”

“I can report that it did.”

She took the piece. For a while they ate it against the counter, neither of them proposing that the other one fetch a plate. In the old apartment, when Sophie came over, she sometimes brought the food she happened to have rather than asking what Eve needed. Eve had worried then about how she would pay her back for all the little things that hadn't yet become a debt. They had reached a point where it was much easier to tell her to cut another slice, or to say that one was enough.

Sophie asked whether she wanted the photograph tonight. Eve said tomorrow would do. She hadn't forgotten it; she could still see the light on Nell turning away. Sophie wiped the bread knife and asked where she should put it.

“Up there with the other ones. I'll go and see if he's managed the book.”

She found Julian on the rug where she'd left him, with his back now against the sofa so the children had space in front of him. Nell had one palm on his shoe and one on the rug. Dumpling had come downstairs. He sat in the passage with his front feet just inside the sitting room, so it was still possible for him to decide he hadn't arrived. He had a full view of the shelves at the far end. His tail lay across his feet.

Eve sat down just inside the door, to one side where he could always go back. She'd thought of getting a cup of water on her way in. Now she stayed put. He looked up at the high built-in shelf over the low closed cupboards, above anything the children could reach. It ran almost to the edge of the bookcase. A shallow basket sat on it, tucked back from the front.

He didn't meow. He gave a little shiver along his back which might have been the effort of not getting up. The tip of one ear moved.

Nell turned and looked along his line of sight. Eve could see the side of her face and the effort it took her to bring one hand up without putting the other one down. She leaned back against Julian's leg. Her finger pointed past the books.

“Ball.”

The word wasn't perfect. The vowel had most of the room in it. Eve could count on her fingers the occasions when she might have heard it from Nell before, and one of those had been when Julian dropped a balled-up pair of socks and said it himself.

Julian looked at Eve. The hand he'd rested beside Nell moved to the middle of his lap. He didn't ask her to say it again.

Eve looked for a ball and saw a basket, an empty slip of shelf, and two uneven shadows. There were perhaps fifteen other things in the room Nell could just as easily have pointed to. From her low place she might have been pointing at the broader white line under the shelf. Or something above it might have been visible from there that Eve wasn't going to see from where she sat.

Dumpling was watching the same spot. He had, on many occasions, watched a blank length of wall with the same attention. Eve had once gone back three separate times to see whether anything had changed. Nothing useful had come of the visits apart from giving him time to take her chair.

She got to her feet.

“Can you keep them on that side?” she said to Julian.

He closed the book, moved farther along the sofa with both children, and sat with a hand behind Nell to keep her from coming after Eve. Theo went willingly, because this put him in reach of the corner of the book again. Eve let Dumpling have the open strip by the door, stepped around the far end of the rug and stood on the floor beside the built-in cupboard. The shelf with the basket was a little above her eye level, but close enough for her to take hold of the basket without climbing. She brought it down with both hands and set it on the counter below.

At first she could see only the wooden grain. When she moved to the left a little, something dull and round came into view where the back of the basket had been. She took it down with one hand and brought it into the light.

It was one of Dumpling's old play balls. The nap had worn flat on one side. She remembered seeing it roll across this floor, fast enough on the hard strip by the skirting board that he'd had to run through the rest of the room to get ahead of it. She didn't remember when they'd put it behind the basket. They'd moved his playthings well away from the rug when Theo learned how to follow his sister out of the middle of it.

Dumpling stood up. His head had lowered a little. Eve closed her hand around the ball. She put the basket back empty and showed Julian the ball once from across the room. His mouth moved very slightly on one side. She smiled at him. There were words she could have put beside the smile. She let it be small instead, a thing he could return without having to agree what they'd seen. Then she took the ball upstairs. Julian stayed on the rug with the twins. At the top she closed the stair gate behind her and waited inside the adults' room, where the floor was clear. Dumpling came in his own time. For a second he paid no attention to the toy at all. Then she put it on the floor and took her fingers away. He pounced so decisively that the ball hit the skirting board and came back between his front paws.

He pressed the ball against the wall, let it come a little way back, and pressed it again. The work was private and took a while. Eve sat without touching him and listened to the little muffled noise it made each time it struck the wall. It was the kind of sound she'd never have picked out from below while Sophie was getting food out or Julian was turning a book over.

After the third bounce Eve could distinguish, he walked away from the ball to the chair. She waited until he had settled on his blanket, picked the toy up from the skirting board, and closed it away in the high cupboard with his other small toys. She made sure the latch caught instead of assuming the sound meant it had.

Downstairs, Julian looked up before Eve reached the rug. He didn't ask whether Dumpling had played with it or try to turn Nell towards her. Nell had lowered her hand and was discovering that she could press the cover of the book flat from where she sat. Theo took the bottom corner as soon as she lifted her fingers. He wasn't thinking about balls, as far as Eve could tell. Neither of them was looking for a second one. Julian said, “Yes, that's the book,” when Theo handed it to him, and opened the front as if they hadn't previously had a dispute about whether it existed.

“Is that everybody ready?” she called from the hall. “Come and eat.”

Sophie brought the bread through. Julian said, “If you can take Nell, I'll take Theo,” and Eve took the short way round the table to help Nell into her seat. Julian did the same for Theo, then went to get the plates he had promised. He handed Eve the one with the smear of icing on the underside and kept the clean one until she noticed and swapped them.

“Good try,” she said.

“It was worth finding out.”

She took his clean one anyway. In a minute he gave her half his bread.

Nell was occupied with turning a piece of her own food the right way up. Theo, who had a similar piece in front of him, watched to see what she did with it before committing himself. The windows had gone dark behind them. There was no particular task waiting for Eve in another room, but tomorrow there would be work with her own name on it, a cake tin to wash properly, and probably five or six things she hadn't thought of yet. Julian's notes for the clinic were nowhere on the dinner table. She had no wish to ask him where they were.

From upstairs came a little scrape and then nothing, the sound of Dumpling shifting again on his chair, if Eve had to guess. Nobody went to bring him in.

Eve set her water down. Sophie was telling her about a dog she'd seen outside the bakery, standing well clear of the doorway with a paper bag at its feet. Theo leaned far enough toward the table that Julian put a hand beside his arm, without taking away the spoon he was using.

Across from Eve, Nell turned toward the door. She stayed that way for a little while, listening, or simply looking where the room went on. Then she turned back, found Eve looking at her, and smiled with her mouth still quite full.

Eve smiled back and passed Julian the bread.
