# Chapter 230 — Absent from the Wedding Photographs

Dumpling approached the carrier, looked inside, and walked behind the sofa.

Eve waited.

Julian, already wearing the shirt he had chosen for the photographs, stood beside the bedroom door holding a second shirt in case the first one proved too optimistic about breakfast.

“Do you think he'll come out?” he asked.

“Eventually.”

“For this?”

Eve looked at the clock, then back at the carrier.

“No.”

The answer surprised her by how little argument it required.

They had booked the small portrait session after checking the photographer's arrangements. It was early wedding preparation rather than the wedding itself, an afternoon they could manage while the larger date and formal details were still being settled. The studio allowed a short, optional appearance by a familiar pet, without making the animal essential to the booking. Eve had thought Dumpling might be comfortable with it. She had also agreed with Julian that they could leave him at home.

Here was the part where agreeing in advance needed to become doing.

She didn't move the sofa.

She didn't take out a treat to turn an optional outing into a negotiation he had to lose.

Instead, she put the carrier away and sent the photographer a brief message saying they would come without the cat.

The reply arrived while she was fastening her shoes.

Of course. See you both at the agreed time.

No disappointment, no suggestion that an unusually clever cat ought to manage ten minutes for a camera.

Eve exhaled.

“I had an entire apology prepared,” she said.

“Can you recycle it?” Julian asked.

“It's a very specific apology.”

“Save it for another cat.”

She laughed and went to check Dumpling's water. He emerged from behind the sofa as soon as she stopped attending to the carrier and settled near his familiar box. Nothing about the morning required him to be frightened or unwell. He simply wasn't choosing the outing she had imagined.

They left him with his ordinary needs met in the quiet apartment. Their time away would be short and within his usual routine. His bed stayed where it belonged. The care folder for Nora's Tuesday visit remained closed on the shelf, a separate matter with no camera attached.

At the studio, the photographer introduced herself as Beth and asked whether they wanted a few minutes before beginning.

Eve did.

She had chosen a simple dress she could sit in and shoes she could walk in. Julian wore the first shirt, which had survived breakfast. They looked like themselves after making a little effort, rather than two people dressed as somebody else's completed future.

The room was plain, with a chair, a low bench, and good light from a wide window. A small empty basket stood near the bench where Beth had left a possible place for the cat, if he came and liked it. It had no ribbon, sign, or instruction to look adorable.

“We can take that away,” Beth said.

“Maybe leave it for one picture,” Eve replied, then looked at Julian. “If you'd like.”

He considered the basket.

“He's usually absent from the place we provide for him anyway.”

“Historically accurate,” Eve said.

Beth smiled and left it near the bench, clear of their feet.

The first photographs were terrible in a way Eve could identify without seeing them.

She sat too straight. Julian held one hand as though he had been asked to keep it available for inspection. Every time Beth raised the camera, Eve began thinking about where the light fell instead of the person beside her.

“Would you usually sit that far apart?” Beth asked.

“No,” Eve said.

“Then you don't have to.”

Eve moved closer and immediately arranged the skirt so carefully that Julian looked down to see what had gone wrong.

“Nothing,” she said.

“I was concerned about the bench.”

“It's receiving excellent care.”

Beth lowered the camera.

“Tell him something ordinary.”

Eve looked at Julian.

“The dishcloth needs replacing.”

He stared at her, then began to laugh.

“I said ordinary, not urgent,” Beth said.

Eve laughed too, and the shutter sounded while she was still protesting that somebody had asked.

After that, the room became easier.

Beth gave clear, limited directions instead of praising everything they did as beautiful. She moved them a little away from the wall, checked that Eve was comfortable sitting, and asked before adjusting a loose fold of fabric. Julian stopped trying to discover a medically correct position for his hands.

At one point he put an arm around Eve exactly as he did on the sofa.

There was the person she had wanted a picture with.

She stopped watching the window.

They made a few photographs standing, a few sitting, and one with the empty basket beside them. In that last arrangement Eve glanced toward it by habit, expecting an orange head to occupy whatever part of the composition had been reserved for something else.

The basket stayed empty.

She smiled.

Beth took the picture without asking her to reproduce the expression.

During a pause, Julian brought Eve water. She drank, then noticed herself checking the time so the session would not run late.

“I can manage the timing,” Beth said gently.

Eve lowered her eyes from the clock.

“Sorry.”

“It's a familiar habit. You don't have to do my job as well as be in the pictures.”

Julian looked unhelpfully amused.

Eve pointed at him with the cup.

“Don't.”

“I haven't said anything.”

“Your face has.”

They resumed without adding an elaborate scene to compensate for the missing cat. No borrowed animal, no suggestion of holding an empty collar, no attempt to create a sentimental explanation for a perfectly ordinary choice to stay home.

The basket was one small absence in a set of pictures about two people who had chosen to be there.

Near the end, Beth showed them a few images on the camera screen so they could check whether the direction felt right. Eve wanted to scrutinize every technical detail. Instead, she looked at their faces.

In one picture, Julian was listening while she spoke. In another, her hand rested on his shoulder without trying to display the ring. The quiet closeness was visible, but neither looked transformed into a flawless version of themselves.

“I like these,” she said.

Julian nodded.

They agreed the delivery and selection process within the package they had booked. Beth would send the promised proofs privately. Public use wasn't part of their booking, and she didn't ask them to perform enthusiasm for a promotional post before leaving.

Outside, Eve discovered that her shoes remained comfortable. This seemed an unreasonable triumph until Julian admitted he had been worrying about the collar of his shirt.

“It looks fine,” she said.

“It also exists constantly.”

“Most collars do.”

“I'd like it to exist somewhere else.”

They stopped for an ordinary late lunch, sitting where nobody knew they had spent the afternoon trying to look natural. Eve ordered something warm and asked for extra napkins. Julian loosened his collar and immediately seemed more like the man in the photographs she had liked.

“Were you disappointed he didn't come?” he asked.

“A little, before we left.”

“And now?”

“I'm glad we didn't spend the whole session hoping he'd tolerate it.”

He nodded.

“I liked the basket.”

“So did I. It looked available, not accusing.”

She ate another mouthful before it cooled. They had paid someone to make thoughtful photographs, and the photographs did not need every loved creature included in order to be worth keeping.

At home, Dumpling greeted them from the sofa with a stretch that suggested their absence had permitted substantial work on his sleeping position.

Eve took off her shoes and changed out of the dress. She wanted to fold it properly rather than let a nice afternoon become a heap on the chair. The large, plain tissue sheet from its packaging was still in the shallow box on top of the wardrobe, where she had kept it clean.

She brought the box down, removed the tissue, and laid the sheet on the bed while finding the garment bag. There were no handles or ribbons attached to it. She stayed in the room, moving the plastic garment cover out of the cat's reach before opening the cupboard.

By the time she turned back, Dumpling had climbed onto the tissue and arranged himself into a deeply unattractive oval.

One ear had folded against the sheet. His hind legs pointed in directions no portrait photographer would have suggested.

Eve stopped.

“Julian.”

He appeared in the doorway wearing his ordinary soft shirt.

“The collar has been defeated,” he said.

She pointed at the bed.

Dumpling opened one eye and closed it again, apparently confident that possession had been established.

Eve reached for her phone, then paused long enough to check whether moving closer would disturb him. She stayed where she was. Julian leaned against the doorframe, smiling down at the cat, and she took one quiet photograph without flash.

It was crooked.

The edge of a laundry basket intruded at the bottom. Julian's face was only partly visible. There was no ring, no planned arrangement, and no evidence that anybody in the picture had respected the day's intended visual language.

Eve loved it immediately.

“Can I keep this for our album?” she asked him, showing the screen.

“Yes.”

“Not for the account.”

“I didn't think so.”

She saved it in their private folder and put the phone down rather than asking the cat to remain while she improved the composition.

Dumpling stayed for another minute, then rose to investigate a sound from the kitchen. Eve collected the tissue before he could begin chewing it, folded the dress, and put both away. The small scene ended without being repeated for a better result.

That evening, Beth sent a short message confirming the expected proof delivery date. Eve replied with thanks. The proper portraits would have their place too. She didn't need to declare the accidental image superior in order to love it.

Julian sat beside her on the sofa while she looked once more at the crooked photograph.

“He wasn't in the wedding pictures,” she said.

“He was in Saturday.”

She leaned against him.

Across the room, Dumpling returned to his familiar box rather than the attractive basket he had never seen. He turned twice, pressed down a fold of blanket, and settled.

Tuesday would bring a visitor and whatever limited answers she could offer. The wedding would arrive later, after the remaining arrangements and the work of making room for it. Tonight, nobody had to enter a carrier or face a camera to prove they belonged in the home.

Eve put the phone away. Julian reached for his book, with the ridiculous handmade bookmark still marking the place he had stopped.

She sat beside him without arranging the light.
