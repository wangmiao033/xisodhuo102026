# Chapter 075 — We Arranged This One

By Saturday morning, Eve had rejected three restaurants for reasons that would have embarrassed her if anybody had asked.

The first was too romantic. The second was not romantic enough. The third had photographs of food she actually wanted, which made it the most dangerous because choosing it would require her to stop pretending this was a research project.

She put her phone beside the breakfast plate.

Dumpling looked at the toast.

“You are not on the selection committee.”

He looked at the toast more closely.

“An excellent point about its lack of butter.”

She moved the plate out of his reach and finished eating. The yellow pouch was still in the high cupboard. Her work bag was behind the wardrobe door. No object had vanished overnight, although that did not mean he had accepted a new household constitution.

Julian had sent two suggestions the previous evening. One was a small restaurant he'd wanted to try for some time. The other was a café with a garden. Eve liked the restaurant's menu and disliked the way she kept imagining herself spilling something across its pale tablecloths.

She checked the prices before returning his message.

*The first one. I want the mushroom dish, and I can afford it without pretending a client is paying. Would Sunday afternoon work?*

She started to add that anywhere was fine, then stopped. Anywhere was not what she had been asked.

His reply came while she was washing the plate.

*Sunday works. Their lunch service finishes at three. Shall we try for two?*

*Yes.*

The phone rang a moment later. His name appeared on the screen, making the conversation feel abruptly less theoretical.

“Hello,” she said, drying her hand on a towel.

“Before I call them,” Julian said, “do you want a table by the window or farther inside? The photographs show a speaker near one corner.”

“Farther inside, away from the speaker. Unless the window has some extraordinary importance.”

“It is probably ordinary glass.”

“Then I can sacrifice it.”

She could hear a kettle in the background, not the clinic's reception noise. He was somewhere she hadn't seen, making his own Saturday morning. The fact gave her a strange little pleasure.

“I'll ask what they have,” he said. “And we can meet outside the bookshop on the corner at one forty-five, if that suits you.”

“It does.”

A small silence followed. Not a bad silence. A space large enough to put something clear into.

“This is the date,” Eve said.

The words came out more firmly than she'd expected.

“Yes,” Julian said. “That's what I want it to be.”

“Good. I don't want to arrive carrying a camera because I panicked.”

“I wasn't planning a professional introduction.”

“Or a cat consultation.”

“No.”

She leaned against the counter.

“Just us, then.”

“Just us.”

The kettle clicked off. Julian said he would confirm after speaking to the restaurant. Eve said goodbye before she could add seven instructions that would make the afternoon feel like a hall booking.

She stood with the phone in her hand for a moment.

Then she put it down and went to find the laundry.

Her favorite shirt was at the bottom of the basket. This seemed unnecessarily personal of the universe. She checked the care label, added it to the load she already needed to wash, and refused to spend the morning shopping for a new version of herself.

There were ordinary errands to do: collect the repaired strap from the mender, buy soap, and return two library books before she forgot again. She made a short list, took her usual keys from the hook, and put the books in a bag she would keep closed until leaving.

Dumpling rose when she put on her coat.

He followed her across the rug. At the door, he gave a sharp, unmistakable complaint.

Eve stopped with her hand on the handle.

It would have been easy to call this a sign. Yesterday she had refused to make his behavior a performance for strangers. Today she could still make it a private command, if she wanted a sufficiently affectionate excuse not to go anywhere.

She checked the room instead.

His water was fresh. He had eaten. The bags and loose straps were out of reach, the familiar bed where it belonged, and the route away from the entrance clear. Nothing she could see called for an emergency change of plan.

“I'll be back after my errands,” she said in her normal voice.

He moved toward her shoe.

She waited rather than opening the door against him. When he turned toward the rug, she stepped out and shut the door gently. She heard one more sound from inside.

Her stomach tightened.

She didn't ring Julian to tell him the cat had vetoed the weekend. She didn't go back in and start the departure all over again. She walked down the stairs, unhappy for a minute and still allowed to leave.

At the mender's, the repaired strap lay in a tray among a spectacular number of zippers.

Eve had left it there earlier in the week after its stitching began to loosen. It belonged to her smaller shoulder bag, not the work bag Dumpling had tried to move. The woman behind the counter showed her the reinforced seam and let her test it before paying.

“Don't put the whole library in it,” she said.

Eve looked at the books under her arm.

“Those are going back.”

“Good start.”

She paid, checked the stitching again because she liked seeing a repair that made sense, and went to the library. One book was overdue by a day. The small charge was hers, not a catastrophe or proof she couldn't organize a life.

Before leaving, she wandered into the travel section and opened a book about walking routes she would probably never attempt. A photograph showed a narrow footbridge over a brown stream. She read the paragraph beneath it, turned the page, and decided she wanted the book for the photographs rather than a plan to become adventurous overnight. At the desk, she borrowed it. It could live beside her chair and be interesting without becoming an assignment.

Outside, Julian's confirmation arrived.

*Table booked for two. Away from the speaker. They didn't offer an opinion on ordinary glass.*

Eve smiled so openly that a passerby looked behind her to see what had happened.

*Thank you. One forty-five at the bookshop.*

She saved the time in her calendar under one word: Date.

Not meeting. Not catch-up. Not the restaurant's name followed by a question mark.

The little square on Sunday remained the same size as all the others. She found that reassuring.

On the way to buy soap, she passed a shop window displaying earrings on small velvet stands. She stopped, looked, and considered going inside. Then she remembered a pair she already owned and liked. The purchase wasn't necessary, but neither did wanting to look nice require an argument against vanity.

She could go home and find the pair.

Her phone rang again, this time Sophie.

“Are you anywhere near the fabric shop?”

“Two streets away. Why?”

“I need thread, but don't buy anything. I need someone to read the number on the display if it's still there. The photograph I took is entirely of my thumb.”

Eve laughed and changed direction.

At the shop, she found the thread display. Sophie described the shade for the curtain repair she was finishing. Eve read the number aloud and resisted naming a prettier color. Her friend thanked her and said she would get it herself later.

“How are you?” Sophie asked.

“I have a date tomorrow.”

“With a person who knows it's a date?”

“We used the word on the phone.”

“Excellent administrative progress.”

“It felt less administrative at the time.”

“I hope so.”

Eve leaned against the wall outside, out of the way of the shop door. “I don't know whether I'm supposed to do something different.”

“You could listen when he answers questions.”

“I already do that.”

“Then you're well ahead of several events I've attended this month.”

Sophie sounded tired. Eve asked about her week rather than making the entire call a workshop on anticipation. A difficult work assignment had finally finished, though the next rota still contained a problem Sophie intended to raise on Monday. She wanted Sunday to herself.

“Then I won't send you photographs of five outfits,” Eve said.

“Send one if you want to. Don't make me approve a committee.”

They laughed. Sophie had to go. Eve bought the soap and went home without arranging her friend's free afternoon around her own romance.

Dumpling was on the cushion when she opened the door.

He stood, stretched, and approached her bag. Eve set it safely aside before crouching to greet him. There was no damaged room, no dramatic reconciliation, and no evidence that this one return had solved whatever worried him.

She could still be glad to see him.

After lunch, she took the clothes out of the machine and hung her shirt where it would dry. One sleeve insisted on folding itself inside out. She turned it properly, smoothed the cuff, and found the earrings in a little dish behind a receipt.

The repaired shoulder bag held her wallet, phone, usual keys, and a clean handkerchief. It would not contain a camera, a client folder, or a pouch of objects designed to make an encounter look accidental.

She put it away until tomorrow.

In the afternoon she checked the remaining practical things once. The restaurant address matched the booking message. The bookshop was on the corner Julian meant. Her work messages could wait during the hours she had already kept free. Dumpling's normal care didn't require her to cancel every ordinary afternoon out, though changes would need attention rather than a brave speech.

Then she closed the calendar.

There were hours left in Saturday that did not belong to tomorrow. She read a few pages of a book, made a simple dinner, and played with the cat until he lost interest and went to wash a foot.

Near evening, Julian sent a photograph of a handwritten sign outside another shop. The sign offered repairs to everything except the printer used to make the sign, which had apparently failed halfway through.

Eve laughed, sent back a comment about professional solidarity, and nearly asked whether he was nervous.

She could ask him tomorrow, if she wanted. She could also admit her own nerves without requiring him to have the matching version.

*I'm looking forward to it,* she wrote.

His answer arrived a few minutes later.

*Me too.*

She let that remain an answer instead of an opening for analysis.

Dumpling jumped onto the sofa and occupied the place where her hand had been. Eve moved her hand, not the cat, and rested it lightly beside him. He leaned against her fingers.

Tomorrow they would leave at the time two humans had chosen. The cat had neither selected the restaurant nor secured the table, and he would not have to make the afternoon successful to be welcome when she returned.

Eve turned off the lamp beside the desk.

For tonight, she had a clean shirt drying, a repaired bag, and somewhere she wanted to go.
