# Chapter 090 — Officially My Boyfriend

“I like you too,” Julian said.

For a moment, Eve had nowhere to put the answer. She had prepared so thoroughly for the difficulty of speaking that she had forgotten the possibility of being understood immediately.

“Oh,” she said.

His almost-smile became a proper one.

“I was hoping for something better than oh,” she admitted.

“I can wait.”

“No, don't. I'll start explaining the rice again.”

They laughed, and the small space between them stopped feeling like a distance she needed to interpret.

It was still Friday evening. The meal had not become a different occasion just because she had finally said what the jokes had been carrying. The towel sat on the table where she had abandoned it. Julian's sleeve was pushed back unevenly from washing dishes.

Eve liked him so much in that ordinary untidy moment that the force of it startled her.

“I want to keep seeing you deliberately,” he said. “Not just when our work happens to put us in the same room.”

“Yes.”

“And I'd like us to date each other. Not to keep acting as if asking the next question might spoil what we're already doing.”

She looked down at her hands, then up again.

“Does that mean exclusively?”

“Yes. That's what I want. Is it what you want?”

“Yes.”

The second yes came more easily than the first. Not because the question mattered less, but because she no longer had to guess which conversation they were having.

Julian held out his hand. Eve took it and moved closer, choosing the space rather than waiting for him to close it on her behalf.

“I haven't been seeing anyone else,” she said.

“Neither have I.”

“I know we hadn't agreed that before.”

“We're agreeing now.”

She nodded. It was a relief to give the promise a beginning instead of retroactively testing every day since the first time he had walked the wrong way to stay beside her.

Dumpling raised his head from the bed, looked at them, and put it down again.

“No comment,” Eve said.

“He isn't required to supply one.”

“I was hoping he'd at least appear surprised.”

Julian glanced toward the cat, then back to her. “I can try, if that helps.”

“Please don't. I've already used oh.”

His thumb shifted against her hand. Eve was aware of that small contact in a way that made the room seem both larger and very precisely located.

They sat without speaking for a little while.

She had imagined a confession requiring more scenery. Rain, perhaps, or a perfectly timed interruption that made the return to the conversation meaningful. Instead they had washed most of the dishes, and her socks did not match because one pair had dried faster than the other.

Nothing seemed missing.

Eventually she said, “Can I call you my boyfriend?”

Julian's answer was immediate.

“Yes. Can I call you my girlfriend?”

“Yes.”

She smiled so hard that she had to look away.

“There,” he said. “A much better response than oh.”

“Don't become difficult now. I've just made a decision.”

“I thought the point was knowing what you were choosing.”

She leaned her shoulder gently against his. He stayed beside her, warm and solid, without turning a new word into permission for everything they had not discussed.

After a minute, Eve looked at the cups on the counter.

“We didn't finish.”

“Do they need us this instant?”

“No.”

“Then perhaps they can wait.”

The pleasure of that was absurd. Not being rescued from housework, not being ordered to rest; merely being asked whether she wanted to remain where she was. She did.

Her phone lit on the table. A message preview appeared, ordinary and unrelated. Eve turned the screen down without opening it.

“I want to tell Sophie,” she said. “Not right this second.”

“That's fine with me.”

“And if someone asks, I don't want to invent a complicated answer.”

“Neither do I.”

She was quiet for a moment, remembering the retailer's brief and the photograph in the blue album.

“I don't want to announce a new programme,” she said.

“I wasn't preparing a schedule.”

“No couple livestreams included in the title?”

“None.”

She laughed, then gave him the serious answer too.

“I mean, I'd like people close to me to know. I don't want to hide you. But I don't want to decide tonight which bits of this are public content.”

“Then we won't.”

It was not a universal policy they had settled forever. A friend could know tomorrow; a photograph could remain private. They could ask again when a real situation appeared, instead of trying to write a perfect rule for every stranger who might one day notice them.

Eve thought about her mother and chose not to drag a future family conversation onto the sofa between them. She would speak for herself when the time came. Tonight did not need an audience to make the answer binding.

“What happens now?” she asked.

Julian looked at the clock, then back at her.

“In the immediate future, I should go home at a sensible time. I have an early start tomorrow.”

The plainness of it caught her before she could be sensible herself. A new label had quietly produced an expectation she hadn't even named: that the evening should keep expanding because neither wanted it to end.

He noticed her expression.

“I don't want to leave yet,” he said. “But I will need to.”

“I know.”

“I can stay a little longer.”

She checked what she actually wanted instead of agreeing to whatever would appear easiest.

“A little longer would be nice. And Sunday afternoon, if you're free? We could go for a walk. No work.”

“Sunday works. After lunch?”

“Yes. Somewhere neither of us needs to assess for a film.”

“We may have to travel a very long way.”

“I can behave.”

He looked unconvinced enough to make her laugh.

They chose a time and an ordinary route. Eve did not build dinner, shopping, and an urgent errand around it before he had even finished speaking. A few hours could be a complete plan.

Then she asked what he had meant earlier, about not acting as if a question could spoil things.

Julian looked at their hands.

“I've been careful,” he said. “Some of it was consideration. Some was avoiding an answer I didn't want.”

“You?”

“Yes, me.”

She felt embarrassed by her own surprise. His manner made uncertainty look tidier than hers; it did not remove it.

“I thought you knew,” she said.

“I hoped. That's different.”

Eve squeezed his hand once.

“I kept trying to give you answers that couldn't be rejected because they weren't technically questions.”

“The housing offer was ambitious.”

“I have withdrawn that document.”

“Thank you.”

They smiled, but she was glad neither used the joke to take the seriousness back. She had told him what she wanted. He had answered. There was no need to continue disguising it as something else.

The leftover rice needed a container. Eve found one in the cupboard and discovered its lid belonged to something wider. Julian held up a second lid; that one was too small. They tried three before she found the correct pair already stacked together behind a bowl.

“An excellent practical feature,” he said.

“Not tonight.”

“Fair.”

She put the food away and returned to the sofa for the few minutes they still had. Nothing in the small domestic interruption required a promise that he would always be there to find the lid. She simply liked him being there now, and liked knowing when she would see him again.

When they finally stood, the cups still existed. They finished them together, and Eve put the towel away this time instead of carrying it into another emotional discussion.

Julian collected his bag from the chair. Dumpling appeared at the sound of it moving and went ahead of him toward the door.

By the time Eve followed, one of Julian's shoes had shifted sideways against hers. Dumpling was nosing at the other, pushing it a little farther across the mat.

“Your transport is being reorganized,” she said.

Julian crouched and retrieved the shoe without pulling it against the cat's body. Dumpling followed his hand, then sat squarely in the place the shoe had occupied.

Eve had once been ready to make this sort of thing a message from a wiser creature. Tonight it looked like a cat interested in familiar smells and objects, possibly unwilling to have the arrangement disturbed. She could be fond of him without asking him to certify the relationship.

She moved her own shoes neatly to the side and gave Dumpling room to step away. Julian waited until he did.

“No joint custody of footwear,” Eve told him.

“An essential early discussion.”

They stood at the door, reluctant in an entirely ordinary way.

“I had a good evening,” he said.

“Even with the rice?”

“Especially the part after it.”

She smiled. They held hands once more, briefly, and then let go so he could leave. No promise to message continuously until morning was needed. He said he would let her know when he got home, and she said she would like that.

After the door closed, Eve remained beside it for a moment.

Dumpling looked up at her.

“Yes,” she said quietly. “I know where he went.”

She returned his displaced toy to the room and checked the water. The small tasks steadied the excitement without reducing it. Then she sat on the sofa and picked up her phone.

Sophie had sent a complaint about a storage cupboard with shelves installed for people who apparently owned no arms. Eve answered that first, asking whether the cable had finally found a home.

Her friend's reply arrived a few minutes later: yes, at last.

Eve typed, *Julian and I have agreed we're dating exclusively. He's officially my boyfriend.*

She read the sentence before sending it. It looked almost startlingly uncomplicated.

Sophie replied with a delighted string of punctuation, followed by, *Did you both actually use words?*

*Several. Some of them sensible.*

*Excellent. I'm very happy for you. Tell me properly when I'm not on a bus with a box on my knees.*

Eve smiled and told her to get home safely. She didn't request an immediate celebration from a friend who had already spent the day working.

Julian's message arrived later, as promised. He was home. He was looking forward to Sunday.

*Me too,* she wrote. Then, after a brief hesitation, *Goodnight, boyfriend.*

The answer came quickly.

*Goodnight, girlfriend.*

She laughed aloud, alone in her own room. Dumpling opened one eye at the noise, then closed it again.

Before bed, Eve took down the blue album. She did not change the date beneath their photograph or pretend the picture had always meant everything they had said tonight. It had held what they knew then. This evening added something new.

On a small piece of paper, she wrote the day and a short sentence in her own untidy handwriting.

*We asked each other.*

She slipped it beside the photograph, closed the cover, and put it back on the shelf. Sunday was already arranged. Neither the camera nor the cat had made the appointment.
