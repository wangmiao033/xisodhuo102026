# Why Do All the Villainesses Want to Marry Me?
## Volume One: A Betrothal Written in Blood
### Chapter 9: Let Me Bandage That for You

On the morning of September nineteenth, Adrian Reed felt the sudden urge to study the kingdom's legal system.

This was not because he'd discovered a passion for law.

It was because Mr. Hale, the Rosenbergs' attorney, had just explained that today's hearing wouldn't determine who was guilty.

It could, however, determine who had to spend the night in a cell.

"So this isn't the actual trial?" Adrian asked.

Hale was sorting papers in the carriage, his thin-framed spectacles perched low on his nose.

"It's an emergency procedural hearing. The judge will decide whether the Crown may continue holding the evidence, whether the accused must remain available to the court, and whether provisional detention is justified."

"And tomorrow?"

"If the Crown insists on an expedited review, a substantive hearing could take place on September twentieth."

Adrian swallowed.

In the game, Elena's execution was scheduled for noon tomorrow.

He'd imagined that if he could delay the case for one night, the danger would somehow pass.

Now he realized that even if the prince couldn't drag Elena straight to the execution platform, the Judicial Office could lock her away first and take its time finding something to convict her with.

Elena sat opposite him, listening without interrupting.

"What else can we find?" she asked.

"The evidence ledger isn't the only record," Adrian said. "The sealing wax went from the shop to the warehouse, then from the warehouse to the palace. Someone had to document those transfers."

"We have records for the first two stages."

"But not the supplier's original sales ledger."

Hale nodded.

"If the stationer agrees to produce it and an independent witness can verify the copy, we'll have stronger evidence of the delivery date."

"Then South Street first," Elena decided.

Their carriage rounded a corner.

Adrian watched the busy morning market through the window and felt a familiar knot in his stomach.

Henry's receipt from the warehouse had carried Edwin Clay's personal stamp. Hale had already preserved it.

But Clay would still be sitting beside the evidence at today's hearing.

A man possibly involved in forging the evidence would be in charge of explaining why nothing was wrong with it.

It felt like a game in which Adrian knew the enemy's next attack but couldn't convince the referee that the other player was cheating.

The proprietor of White Heron Stationers was a white-haired old man.

When he saw the Rosenberg crest, he shut the front door and set a thick account book on the counter.

"I sell stationery," he announced. "If two households buy the same color wax, that's not my problem."

"We're not here to blame your shop," Elena said. "We only need to verify an order and its delivery date."

The old man examined Hale's documents before allowing them to proceed.

September sixteenth.

Nine boxes of blue sealing wax.

Six delivered to the Rosenberg estate. Three issued to its deputy steward under a royal loan request.

"Henry," Adrian said.

The proprietor tapped a tiny note in the margin.

"The three boxes reserved for Royal Purchasing came from the same mixture. They asked me to add a touch of silver powder so the batch wouldn't be confused with ordinary blue wax."

Elena frowned.

"If silver powder turns up in the wax on the palace evidence, that connects it to these boxes?"

"To the mixture and batch," the old man said. "Not to whoever pressed the seal onto a letter."

Adrian looked at him with new respect.

"You're more cautious than half the officials at the Judicial Office."

The stationer grunted.

"Forty years in this business. I've watched too many people try to turn a lump of wax into the entire truth."

Hale asked permission to make a certified copy of the relevant page.

The stationer agreed, but insisted the original stay in his shop.

"What if the Judicial Office claims your ledger is fake too?" Adrian asked.

The old man reached beneath his counter and produced a stack of bound order slips.

"Then they can compare it with my inventory and the merchants' guild's monthly accounts. Selling stationery isn't writing a play. Someone has to pay for everything."

Hale checked the documents.

A royal purchasing officer had settled the order on September sixteenth, leaving a transaction number in the guild's records.

"That's another independent point of confirmation," the lawyer said. "It helps establish that this isn't an account the shop invented overnight."

Adrian remembered how he'd first arrived in the world, believing knowledge of the game's ending would give him knowledge of the truth.

Now an unremarkable receipt was proving more useful than any grand line of dialogue he could remember.

"Sir," he asked the stationer, "would you be willing to testify tomorrow?"

"If the guild and a notary verify my records, yes. If you want me to antagonize the Judicial Office on nothing but your word, you've got the wrong old man."

Adrian nodded.

"Fair enough. We do this properly."

They had just finished sealing the copies when someone knocked.

"Mr. Heron? Royal Purchasing. We're here for a collection."

The proprietor went still.

"There are no orders due today."

"Additional order from yesterday."

Adrian glanced at the door.

The man outside sounded impatient.

The proprietor moved toward the lock.

Elena raised a hand.

"Ask for the order number."

A beat of silence.

"Open the door and we'll show you!"

Adrian looked at the sealed document case in his arms.

Nobody had come to collect stationery.

"Is there another exit?" he whispered.

The stationer pointed toward the storeroom.

"Back alley."

Hale called to the assistants. The original ledger was returned to its owner, while the certified copies went into two separate sealed bags.

Adrian tucked one beneath his arm.

A heavy impact struck the door.

The latch gave way.

The door swung inward, clipping a glass jar on the nearest shelf.

It shattered on the floor.

Adrian stepped back to avoid the falling fragments, turning to shield the papers with his body.

A long shard sliced across his palm.

Pain flared through his hand.

"Adrian!"

Elena's voice cut through the commotion.

Two guards in civilian clothes had already positioned themselves between the intruder and the documents.

The man saw how many people were in the shop, hesitated, then fled.

The guards stayed where they were.

Hale reassured the stationer and invited the owner of the neighboring shop to witness the broken door, scattered glass, and apparent attempt to seize the papers.

Adrian looked down.

Blood was welling along the edge of his palm.

"It's just a scratch."

Elena looked even paler than he felt.

"Don't talk."

"Really. It's not deep."

"I said don't talk."

She brought him to the window and pressed clean cloth against the cut while someone fetched a local physician.

Adrian lasted perhaps twenty seconds.

"What about the documents?"

"With Hale."

"The original ledger?"

"Still in the shop."

"Good."

Elena looked up sharply.

"Can you worry about yourself for once?"

"If we'd lost the documents, I'd have cut my hand for nothing."

"We can get another ledger. Where are we supposed to get another you?"

Adrian went quiet.

The physician arrived, examined the cut, and declared it shallow. After cleaning it and stopping the bleeding, he produced a roll of bandage.

Elena held out her hand.

"I'll do it."

The physician hesitated, then explained how to wrap the gauze without restricting circulation.

Adrian watched her bend over his hand.

She was so deft with letters, documents, and her ever-present fan. But she treated a few strips of gauze with extraordinary concentration.

"Does it hurt?" she asked.

"Not much."

"Then why are you staring at me?"

Adrian looked down.

"Because... you've wrapped it a little tight."

Her fingers paused.

She loosened the bandage at once, expression blank.

"Now?"

"Much better."

"Good. Next time, bandage your own hand."

Nevertheless, she slipped the medicine bottle into his coat pocket.

Adrian smiled.

"Is this my reward for protecting the evidence?"

"It's to stop you bleeding on my carriage upholstery."

"Of course."

"Also, when you called it a scratch, you were white as a sheet."

He glanced at the cut.

"I thought you hadn't noticed."

"I'm not blind."

She looked away immediately after saying it.

From outside came the cries of the morning market. Sunlight fell across her golden hair.

Adrian was beginning to suspect that the supposedly arrogant villainess of *Crown of Fate* was even worse than he was at admitting when she cared.

"Thank you," he said quietly.

Elena looked up.

"For what?"

"For not deciding I'm just an incompetent servant who causes trouble."

"You cause an astonishing amount of trouble."

His smile froze.

"But," she continued as she put away the unused gauze, "you know when to stop, and you admit when you've misread something. Those are better qualities than always pretending to be right."

He stared at her.

She rose, went toward the door, then turned back.

"If you're worried about tomorrow's hearing, you can tell me. Stop answering every question with 'I'm fine.'"

"I am worried."

"About what?"

"If we lose, will you regret letting me stand beside you?"

Elena met his gaze.

"I'm more worried that you'll spend so much time fearing my regret that you'll stop doing anything at all."

Adrian nodded.

His palm still hurt.

The tightness in his chest had eased a little.

"Let's go," she said.

Their carriage took them directly to the Judicial Office.

An elderly judge presided over the emergency hearing, with the Crown's representative on one side and Hale on the other.

Clay sat near the evidence table, a sealed wooden box in front of him.

Adrian noticed immediately that he'd changed spectacles. The fine silver frames from last night had been replaced by thick black ones.

Irrelevant.

He forced himself not to chase the detail.

Hale submitted three requests: access to the original evidence registration, independent examination of the transfer records, and a prohibition on detaining Elena until those records had been checked.

The Crown's representative stood.

"We're dealing with a letter implicating a noble house in treason, not an ordinary purchasing receipt."

"All the more reason to examine how it was handled," Hale replied.

Clay glared at Adrian.

"Mr. Reed interfered in royal affairs in public last night. His behavior is suspicious in itself."

Adrian looked at him.

"I can testify to what I saw."

The judge lifted his head.

"Proceed."

"Evidence Item 714 was recorded with a sealing date of September eighteenth. I saw the entry on the seventeenth. I don't know who entered the wrong date or whether it was deliberate. That's why I'm asking for the original to be preserved and examined."

Clay spoke immediately.

"An ordinary clerical error."

"Then preserving the record should help clear it up," Adrian said. "Including clearing you of suspicion."

Someone in the public gallery stifled a laugh.

Clay turned a darker shade of red.

The judge rapped his gavel.

"We are not determining the letter's authenticity today. The evidence shall remain sealed under joint supervision by independent clerks. The Crown's application for immediate detention is denied for now. The chain of custody will be reviewed at noon tomorrow."

Adrian's shoulders sagged.

No detention.

They'd bought another day.

As they left the courtroom, Clay called after him.

"Mr. Reed."

Adrian stopped.

"You seem unusually concerned about that ledger."

"Most people named in a summons would be."

"Well. Let's hope it remains in good condition."

Clay smiled and walked away.

Adrian was about to mention the remark to Hale when hurried footsteps sounded along the corridor.

A Judicial Office messenger approached carrying a notice bearing a red stamp.

"Emergency incident!" the man called. "There has been a fire in the temporary evidence warehouse. The original registration page transferred last night may have been destroyed!"

Elena went white.

Adrian read the notice.

No complete inventory of damage had even been prepared.

Yet someone connected to Clay was already saying the exact page they needed might be gone.

Adrian passed it to Hale.

"How extraordinarily convenient."

Elena clasped Adrian's uninjured hand.

"If the original really is destroyed, can we still win?"

Adrian looked at her, then at the two independently sealed document bags in Hale's arms.

"Yes."

For once, his voice didn't shake.

"What burned was the copy they were responsible for keeping."

"Our supplier's accounts, the receipts, and Henry's statement that he's willing to testify—they're all still here."

A bell tolled outside the building.

Less than a day remained before Elena's scheduled execution.
