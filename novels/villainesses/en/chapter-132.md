# Chapter 132: On the Same Side, for Tonight

The east warming hall's clock struck eight before Isabella realized she was still gripping the back of her chair.

Her knuckles had gone white. When she let go, her fingers remained bent as though refusing to obey.

Elena did not tell her there was nothing to fear. She put warm water within reach, sat down, and waited for Captain Dacre to finish his initial report.

The armed man had been disarmed and was under guard. The injured guard's cut had been treated. The young attendant's wrist was not broken, though he still needed examination. Staff passages remained under search. Guests could leave after being accounted for and assigned transport. There was no basis to assume everyone working in the side passage had participated.

At that, Isabella looked at Elena.

“You think I would imprison them all?”

“I think you wanted something you could do immediately.”

The princess did not deny it.

Outside, palace lamps lit the steps as carriages approached one by one. This was not a spoiled dinner that fresh napkins could repair. Tomorrow's praise of her composure could become a discussion of how an armed man had passed through doors under her authority.

“They'll say I can't even manage my own banquet hall.”

“Perhaps,” Elena said. “Then let's finish managing this one.”

Isabella turned back. Her gaze was still sharp, but she no longer seemed prepared to use its edge on the nearest person.

“How far will you help?”

“Preserving what we saw and getting ordinary people home safely. Announcing an attractive conclusion for you isn't included.”

“I didn't ask for loyalty.”

“Good. I didn't bring any.”

Standing beside their chairs, Adrian felt he ought to be holding a tray of pastries. Even cooperating, they sounded as though they were testing each other's blades. At least the points now faced the same problem.

Ellery brought the replaced service notice. It was an ordinary wooden clipboard, intended to carry the evening's wine-service and staff-route instructions. The old sheet had been found in a waste basket, not burned. The replacement paper looked similar, but the attendant responsible for copying the notices did not recognize the writing.

That was not enough to arrest anyone on handwriting evidence. Ellery asked the guards to register where each item had been found. The two sheets went into separate protective sleeves; the board was retained. Curious people were not allowed to pass them around.

When the physician permitted the young attendant to give a brief account, his voice stayed low.

He had thought the man was a replacement worker. The silver knot looked familiar, and used glasses stood beside him. He hadn't checked the roster. The man said the princess disliked keeping envoys waiting, and the young attendant had taken the paper.

“Did you know him?” Dacre asked.

“No. I'd never worked with him.”

“Did you see him replace the notice?”

“No. I don't know when that happened.”

Isabella's tight mouth slowly relaxed. The answers provided no convenient mastermind's name, but she could hear how difficult I don't know had been to say.

“I never instructed you to omit verification,” she said.

He lowered his head. “I thought it would cause a delay.”

“Describe what you actually did. Any failure of duty will be assessed against your actual duties. Not knowing something isn't the same as protecting someone.”

Ellery recorded it. The physician took the attendant away to rest, rather than making him repeat himself until he produced a satisfactory name.

Adrian corrected one detail in his supplementary statement. He had heard Why isn't she here yet? Not Why isn't the princess here yet? Combined with the false instruction addressed to Isabella, the threat was serious enough. He still would not insert a title he had never heard.

Dacre had the clerk amend it in front of him.

“You didn't try to seize the knife. Good.”

Adrian took a moment to realize that wasn't a reprimand.

“I thought about it.”

“Not doing it saved us from having another person to rescue.”

Elena looked at him, the fear she had held back finally showing. He offered his right hand. She took it without releasing him merely because Isabella sat nearby.

The princess watched, then set down her water.

“You can go home.”

The words sounded stiff, like an unfamiliar implement being tried for the first time.

Elena did not become too moved to remember the unfinished work. “Once we have our acknowledgments and the guests are provided for.”

“I thought you would take the opportunity to demand more.”

“I haven't started.”

Isabella raised an eyebrow.

“Don't let yesterday's fiscal discrepancy become tonight's motive,” Elena said. “We haven't established that link. If your people announce it first, the next person examining the accounts will have a harder time separating fact from story.”

“Are you protecting my opponents?”

“The answer we haven't found yet.”

Isabella looked at Adrian. He nodded. The original accounts remained at the fiscal office; they had brought no original payment instruments tonight. The attacker's purpose, entry route, and funding required separate inquiries. An infuriating story would not replace them.

After a silence, Isabella asked Ellery to draft a short notice ending the banquet. An armed intruder had been subdued. The injured were receiving care. The guards continued their examination, and witnesses should provide their observations through the official contact.

No political rival's name appeared. Nor was the Temple inserted to give the statement extra weight.

Ellery warned that such a notice would prevent the security failure from being dismissed as a drunken dispute.

Isabella studied her hands.

“It wasn't one.”

No one applauded. The chamberlain crossed out his milder wording and began again.

Their cooperation soon encountered a second unromantic difficulty: too few carriages.

Some drivers would not arrive until the scheduled end of the banquet. Older guests could not wait on winter steps. Elena offered two ordinary carriages from the Rosenberg city stables for those needing rest. Ellery worried that sending royal guests home in ducal carriages would suggest the palace couldn't provide for them.

“At the moment, it can't provide for everyone,” Isabella said.

She asked about journeys and costs, agreed to ordinary hire records, and stipulated that no public statement should describe one side rescuing the other. Elena accepted. Her note to her steward requested transport, not political concessions.

“She still cares about that last condition,” Adrian murmured.

“I can hear you,” Isabella said.

“I know.”

She glared. It made her look more alive than her expressionless composure had.

As the guests left, the room grew quiet. No one replenished wine. Desserts intended for later courses returned to the kitchen rather than being pressed on shaken guests as compensation. Dacre arranged necessary security while preserving normal staff relief. Exhausted guards would not remain on duty simply because no one had found replacements.

While they waited for receipts, Adrian noticed that Elena still hadn't fastened her shawl. The ribbon looped around her fingers, twice, then fell loose. She tried to smile when she caught him watching, but it would not settle properly.

“May I help?”

She nodded. He straightened the ribbon and tied it below her collar, without using an injury check as an excuse to touch her. Watching his lowered face, she admitted that the instant she had lost him in the crowd, she had imagined the worst.

“I stayed with the old gentleman.”

“I know. I saw you afterward.”

She did not ask him never to help anyone else. He did not promise to remain in her sight forever. He simply described where he had gone and why he had stopped. Ordinary details filled the gap, reassuring her more than a promise that nothing would happen to him.

“I may not have many lovely things to say in the carriage.”

“You don't have to.”

“But sit beside me.”

“That answer is easy.”

She rested her forehead briefly against his shoulder, then lifted it. Isabella finished a report at the table. She neither ordered them to recover their decorum nor pretended their closeness meant nothing to her. She simply allowed them that moment.

When custody acknowledgments arrived, Elena and Isabella each received the one relevant to her. The false service instruction, old notice, replacement sheet, and clipboard were listed separately. The guards held the blade separately too. A receipt granted neither ownership nor the right to retrieve evidence at will.

Ellery paused before signing his account. He acknowledged that a subordinate had submitted one temporary replacement worker's card in advance. Final verification had not been fully reported back. That might identify a failure, or an unrelated mistake. It did not yet establish that the attacker had used that card.

“Why tell me only now?” Isabella asked.

“I've just confirmed it. It concerns my management.”

“Then record when you confirmed it.”

She promised no immunity and did not immediately strip him of his badge. His hand steadied. He wrote nine fifteen clearly.

Adrian thought of the fiscal clerk with her worn sleeves. Recording something against oneself could be harder than finding another person's mistake, and more easily taken for granted.

When they were ready to leave, Isabella stood and thanked Elena.

“For tonight,” she added.

“That's all I'm accepting.”

“We may disagree tomorrow.”

“I'm prepared.”

Adrian sighed softly. Both looked at him. He hastily explained that he was anticipating the opportunity to sit down in the carriage.

At the door, Isabella called him back.

“During our walk, you said the soup was good, though perhaps you were simply cold.”

He turned.

“Did you help tonight simply because someone was in danger?”

There was an expectation in the question she had not entirely identified herself. Adrian would not answer with a vague kindness that promised too much.

“Because you were in danger, and because we know each other and I want you safe. As for the position you're seeking, I'll still judge what you do next.”

She studied him, then nodded slowly.

“I see. You still won't give me only the half I want to hear.”

“The other half is true too.”

She asked nothing further.

A night messenger from the secretariat arrived with an envelope. Ellery verified its source against the normal incoming register. It contained a draft authorization appointing a proxy for succession proceedings, for Isabella to review the next morning. Unsigned, it was not an operative decision.

The princess did not ask them to stay for official business. Adrian was about to leave when the messenger asked whether an attachment should also be forwarded to him. It contained his name.

Isabella let him read the relevant passage.

The proposed witness statement said that at eight o'clock on the evening of January 8, Her Highness Isabella had verbally agreed to the proxy arrangement in the small western room, in Mr. Reed's presence.

Adrian looked up.

At eight, the clock in the east warming hall had struck. He, Elena, and Isabella had been here while the physician examined the injured.

They had never gone to the western room.

He laid the paper flat, leaving the unexamined endorsements untouched. He did not pronounce the entire document authentic or false.

“Your Highness,” he said, “I never witnessed this.”
