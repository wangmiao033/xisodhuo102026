# Why Do All the Villainesses Want to Marry Me?
## Volume Two: The Saint Won't Let Me Get Married
### Chapter 43: The Saint Refuses to Sign a Confession

On October twenty-third, Cecilia Saintclair folded a sheet of paper into quarters and put it in her pocket.

Adrian saw her do it.

He had a bad feeling about the paper.

"Please tell me that isn't another betrothal contract."

"It isn't. It's the statement they want me to sign."

"Then why fold it up?"

"Because if it stays on the table, I'll keep looking at it. Today, I'd rather finish breakfast first."

Martha was carrying a plate of fresh rolls into the room.

She nearly applauded.

"Finally! Somebody who believes breakfast is for eating."

Elena passed Cecilia the jam.

"You've decided not to sign?"

"Yes."

"Let your lawyer examine it anyway."

"Already done." Cecilia brought out a second copy covered in Hale's notes. "He explained that signing it could turn allegations we've disputed into matters I've personally admitted."

Adrian read the text.

Three short paragraphs.

Enough to decide the outcome of an entire hearing before the hearing had begun.

*"I acknowledge that personal feelings interfered with my professional judgment, leading me to disclose restricted divine prognoses to an outside noble household and cause reputational harm to the Temple."*

Beneath the paragraph, in a much friendlier typeface, was an offer.

If Cecilia signed, she could retain her title as a saint. Her access to historical files would be suspended for two months.

If she refused, Internal Oversight would request an expanded inquiry.

"That's their idea of an offer?" Adrian asked.

"It's a proposed settlement," Hale explained. "But a settlement shouldn't require someone to confess to conduct they say they didn't commit. Otherwise the inquiry becomes a formality."

Elena pointed to the phrase *personal feelings*.

"Are they talking about the historical contract naming you and Adrian?"

Cecilia nodded.

"An inspector told me I disclosed the prognosis to protect my private relationship."

Adrian nearly choked on his jam.

"We haven't even established whether that contract was authentic!"

"I told them." Cecilia spoke without irony. "They said my insistence on checking its authenticity proved how invested I was in you."

Elena was quiet.

"When people used the rumors about Adrian and me to challenge his evidence in court, I was furious."

"That happened to you too," Adrian remembered.

"Exactly. I know how easily feelings can become an excuse for ignoring the facts."

Cecilia traced a finger along the edge of her plate.

"I don't hate the Temple. I grew up there. People taught me to read. They stayed awake caring for the sick. If I sign this, outsiders will think Mira and the doctors uncovered registration errors only because I acted rashly over a romance."

Adrian put the document down.

"You shouldn't have to prove you've never loved anyone before you're allowed to be heard."

She looked up.

"The old betrothal doesn't establish what you feel today," he continued. "Even if you do fall in love with someone someday, that isn't evidence you falsified medical records. Nobody gets to take an unverified private relationship and use it to settle an entirely different accusation."

Color rose unexpectedly in Cecilia's cheeks.

She reached toward her notebook.

Then stopped before she could write down the exchange.

"You can be remarkably clear when you want to be, Adrian."

"I've practiced. I used to be unable to tie a bow tie."

"I'm not entirely sure those skills are related."

"Neither am I."

Elena set down her teacup.

"You've become quite good at comforting people."

Adrian glanced toward her, suddenly uncertain.

She wasn't angry.

But there was something more complicated than amusement in her smile.

"Once this is settled," she added, "I'd appreciate the same level of clarity when you tell me which dessert you'd like on our next date."

"I thought I owed you the entire pastry shop by now."

"Getting close."

Cecilia finally began eating.

Slowly.

But no longer as though each bite had to wait for a verdict.

That morning they visited the Temple's outer administrative hall.

The building was rectangular and unusually bright, with high windows and portraits of former saints helping people during disasters.

Cecilia stopped beneath one depicting a woman in old-fashioned gray robes.

"My mentor admires her," she told Adrian. "When the flood hit the western villages, she ordered volunteers to distribute emergency food before the Temple gave permission to open the storehouse."

"Was she punished?"

"Yes. She kept a full ledger showing where the food went. After the inquiry, the rules were changed."

Adrian studied the painting.

"So questioning Temple instructions isn't unprecedented."

"No." Cecilia took a breath. "That's why I still hope someone here will listen."

Two junior acolytes were helping elderly visitors complete medicine applications in the waiting area.

A girl much younger than Cecilia recognized her and started to call out *Your Holiness*.

The supervisor beside the girl touched her shoulder.

The greeting died halfway out.

After a moment, the girl approached Cecilia in a nervous little rush.

"Is it true they've forbidden you from blessing patients?"

"Some of my database access has been suspended," Cecilia answered gently. "If someone needs medical help, you should still take them to the physician. What happens to my credentials doesn't change what a sick person needs."

The girl looked down.

"Someone said you aren't worth looking up to anymore."

Cecilia went pale.

Adrian took a half step forward, then stopped.

She knelt to meet the girl's eyes herself.

"I may have made mistakes. If I did, I want to acknowledge them. But the confession I was asked to sign contains things I didn't do. That's why I won't sign it."

The girl nodded uncertainly.

When she'd gone, Cecilia remained beside the wall for a long time.

"That hurt," Elena said.

"More than what the investigators said." Cecilia looked up. "I was once a child who believed every saint was incapable of doing wrong."

Elena stood with her without trying to make it easier.

"At least that girl heard something worth remembering today. A person can deserve respect and still be responsible for what they say."

Cecilia nodded.

They continued toward the records desk.

An elderly disciplinary clerk sat behind the counter. When he saw Cecilia, he first instructed her to enter alone.

Hale submitted the authorization for legal consultation.

"The disciplinary hearing isn't until the twenty-sixth. Today she's filing a written response. She may receive advice and have an independent witness record the time of submission."

After reviewing the rule, the clerk allowed Hale and a witness to remain by the counter.

Elena and Adrian waited beyond the partition.

"Miss Saintclair." The clerk produced another copy of the proposed admission. "Wouldn't it be better to acknowledge an error of judgment and spare yourself the embarrassment of a formal hearing?"

Cecilia didn't reach for the paper.

"I'm willing to state that I informed a municipal physician about conflicting patient identifiers. I'll explain why I asked that the clinical records be checked."

She paused.

"But I did not give anyone the original restricted prognosis. I did not change a document to favor a personal relationship. I cannot confess to those claims."

"Don't you know the Temple's reputation has already suffered?"

"I know people are discussing the case. Whether their concerns result from wrongdoing on my part is something the evidence must establish."

She handed over the response she'd written.

Each contested allegation was answered separately, supported by document references that could be verified. Patients' names were withheld from the public copy.

The clerk frowned when he saw the municipal subsidy references.

"You obtained the earlier revisions?"

"Certified municipal payment attachments," Hale explained. "Not the protected original oracle."

The man looked at Cecilia again.

"Are you sure you want to take this further? Your education, access to medical outreach, and reputation as a saint could all be affected."

Her fingers tightened around the drawstring of the document pouch.

"I'm grateful to everyone who taught me. I don't want to lose the chance to help people who are ill." Her voice was steady, though her hands weren't. "But I cannot preserve the word *saint* beside my name by signing a statement about something I didn't do."

The room was quiet enough to hear birds outside.

After a long pause, the clerk stamped the reply.

"You may make your case at the hearing on the twenty-sixth."

"Thank you." Cecilia bowed.

Then she asked another question.

"May I have an inventory of the materials used to accuse me? I don't need the contents of unauthorized patient records. I need the specific document numbers, their sources, and when they were designated restricted."

The clerk looked surprised.

"You're refusing a settlement and still asking to see the evidence?"

"Yes. I want to understand exactly what I've supposedly disclosed."

Hale submitted the formal request.

The clerk promised a response under the applicable procedure.

When Cecilia left the counter, her shoulders had visibly loosened.

Adrian was waiting.

"Well?"

"I didn't sign."

"I know."

"How?"

"You told us you wouldn't before you went in."

Cecilia looked at him.

Then smiled.

"That's right. I made that decision myself."

Elena came over and put a light shawl around Cecilia's shoulders.

"You did well. We can prepare the rest properly."

"Not entirely well." Cecilia swallowed. "When they mentioned taking away my title, my hands were shaking."

Elena didn't promise she would keep it.

"Being frightened and telling the truth anyway—isn't that what you've always taught other people?"

Cecilia's eyes grew bright.

The formal receipt arrived at the estate that afternoon.

Cecilia's refusal was recorded.

Internal Oversight had not withdrawn the charge or restored her restricted file access.

Hale began organizing the permitted witnesses: Mira's account of the unsigned completion statement; Grace's medical testimony, limited to care and patient identities; and proof of the three municipal subsidy revisions.

Mira met separately with an adviser before completing her statement.

"I don't want to write that Cecilia has never made a mistake," she told Hale. "I wouldn't know. I only know she didn't tell me to put Jonah under another person's name. She told me to come back if I couldn't verify the records."

"Are you willing to say that formally?"

"Yes. But there's a sentence I need to change."

She crossed out the word *personally* in the phrase *the manager personally locked the door*.

"I heard the bolt being drawn," she explained. "I couldn't see who actually moved it."

Adrian thought of Evidence Item 714.

A false story didn't always require inventing an entire page. Sometimes all it needed was replacing *I don't know* with *I saw it*.

Hale let Mira make the correction herself.

"We can't guarantee the outcome," he told Cecilia. "We can insist that untested allegations don't become established truth merely because somebody drafted a confession in advance."

At dinner, Cecilia grew quiet again.

Adrian didn't ask about the hearing.

He cut the apple pastry into three portions.

"At last. The overdue dessert."

Elena inspected her share.

"How is it that the dessert you owe me gets smaller every time?"

"These are interest payments. The principal's still outstanding."

Cecilia laughed.

"Do I owe investigative fees as well?"

"No." Adrian shook his head. "You've spent more than enough courage today."

Color crept into Cecilia's face again.

She lowered her eyes to the pastry.

"Thank you. I'll try saying that without putting it into the official minutes."

Elena smiled.

"Now that's progress."

They had almost finished when Hale's assistant arrived with the first inventory response from Internal Oversight.

The office would identify the document numbers it relied on.

But it refused access to the two earliest prophecy revision drafts, claiming internal custody review was unfinished.

One of those originals had the same reference number as the annotated manuscript found in the municipal subsidy office.

Adrian stared.

"If the municipality holds a corresponding copy, why won't the Temple even establish whether its original is still where it should be?"

Hale examined the time limits in the reply.

"Tomorrow we'll need to request independent preservation. We cannot wait until the hearing to discover that the source material has moved beyond reach."

Cecilia put the unsigned confession back in its sleeve.

"Then tomorrow," she said, "I'll be the one to make the request."
