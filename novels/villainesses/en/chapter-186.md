# Chapter 186: The Amended Year

On the morning of February 14, Isabella lost a chess game outside the royal archive.

Strictly speaking, she lost a puzzle on a scrap of paper while they waited. Adrian followed its instructions for two moves. She inspected them and pronounced the problem badly designed.

“You said it had a solution.”

“A solution and a pleasant outcome are different things. I refuse to confuse them simply because somebody has printed a little diagram.”

Her right hand folded the paper; her left wrist rested on its support. This wasn't their unfinished game, and the scrap hadn't arranged their next appointment for them.

A clerk requested another quarter hour. Why? A volume required supervised retrieval, not a deliberate slight. She sat again. The old coordinator's badge wasn't at her collar. Princess couldn't summon an absent custodian instantly from the far corridor either.

They'd come for royal calendar amendments. Cecilia's festival records used two year systems; Viola's directions retained old street names. The questions could inform each other without three friends' agreement making them one date.

The actual table was uglier than Adrian expected: marginal additions and a correction pasted across its center. Isabella put down her pen, requesting identification of contemporary use versus later compilation.

“If Your Highness only needs unified years, the current table suffices.”

“I want to know who unified them.”

The clerk explained that a conversion table could show the result of the work without preserving every decision that had produced it. If she wanted those decisions, it was a different question from asking what number to put on a modern form. That required compilation notes. She requested their inclusion if authorized; otherwise they'd read what was already permitted, not demand irregular retrieval.

Beatrice checked copy conditions elsewhere, requesting no special convenience. Adrian unexpectedly appreciated the slowness. Someone wanting answers didn't make every different door open at once.

Waiting for the supplement, the clerk brought a wrong index. Its cover number differed by one stroke; its contents concerned monthly stable feed. Isabella raised an eyebrow at the oats.

“At least it's confident about its year.”

Flushing, the clerk apologized. She asked him to record the mistaken retrieval and replace it, without a lecture. Remembering Hale's corrections yesterday, Adrian swallowed another joke. A wrong digit wasn't their discovered enemy.

With the right volume, the clerk invited them to verify cover and first page. Isabella read each item; being a princess didn't exempt her from small checks. He gradually stopped making explanations sound like pleas for forgiveness, distinguishing what he knew from what required the custodian.

The first layer described an administrative reorganization assigning older year entries to a later registration year. Events hadn't happened again then; their records had been gathered then. Some subsequent copies omitted Received in and retained only the year. Later summaries encouraged readers to mistake it for an event date.

Adrian felt cold.

“Then the termination record—”

“We must check its layers. Not invalidate every old date.”

Several busy people omitting a little each could produce a chronology that never existed, without one person deliberately writing the whole lie. He looked at the tiny phrase that had been dropped. It was small enough to disappear from a hurried copy, large enough to move an event in the mind of someone reading afterward. His own desire for a single usable date would have encouraged exactly that kind of shortcut.

Binding relationships established the order of two inks, the clerk explained from an authorized supplement. Exact writing dates still needed material examination. He would distinguish visible sequence from invisible dates in the response.

“When did the royal administration recognize the confusion?” Isabella asked.

The room quieted. An early correction proposal had been addressed only in an appendix, not in every circulated summary. Cost and No substantial effect on retrieval were the stated reasons.

She read twice. Adrian watched her return to the explanation rather than immediately reaching for an excuse. The words about expense had not become less consequential because they were dry administrative language. Somewhere between that decision and the copies on other desks, a warning had become something most readers would never see. It hadn't been her administration, but she didn't use that to make it nothing to do with her.

“At least we mustn't omit it when citing it now.”

She requested the correction's page reference in the public response, not unrelated private entries. That could suggest the Crown admitted its records were unreliable, the clerk warned.

“It should acknowledge this particular defect. Not make someone look reliable for me.”

Her voice hardened, then softened. Ordinary procedure still applied; refusal should specify scope and reason. A fine speech shouldn't make one clerk absorb the Crown's risk.

At the window during a break, she set her cup away from her left wrist. Adrian didn't rearrange what her right hand could already reach.

“Will you regret showing me?”

“I regret not checking the other side of the chess problem.”

“I'm serious.”

“So is my attempt to postpone answering.”

He laughed. She didn't let that carry the question away.

“I'm a little afraid. People will ask why I'm dismantling my own house for you. You didn't crack it. Standing beside you makes pretending not to see the cracks harder.”

“You needn't prove my innocence.”

“I know. You needn't pretend every cost to me is nothing.”

Outside, a gardener caught a cut branch in a basket. She'd once counted every favor toward trust she could reclaim later, Isabella said. The impulse remained. Having it didn't mean writing it down as his debt.

He couldn't answer elegantly. Today's work mattered; he'd remember who did it. That wasn't a signed promise about future feelings.

Less pleasant, she agreed, but better than deliberate misunderstanding. She did not ask him to improve the answer until it sounded like the one she wanted. Adrian was grateful and uncomfortable at once. The uncomfortable part could stay; accepting her honesty did not require him to make a promise simply to smooth their walk back to the table.

Back at the table, three inquiries stayed distinct. Repair might change the Third Bell; West Passage might predate street reorganization; registration year wasn't necessarily event year. All required comparison, not a definitive chronology of an old world.

Adrian wanted a clear date badly enough to resent the empty box. He wrote Not converted, refusing to ask her to fill it for him. The blank looked conspicuous among the neatly identified pages and references. Leaving it there required more effort than copying the first plausible year. He could feel how quickly uncertainty began to resemble failure when he wanted it over.

Beatrice included those limits in the afternoon trial conditions. Correspondence with a bell, direction, or year couldn't authenticate a fragment. She also supplied an independent physician's assessment: one brief exposure through a separately made, inspected low-intensity comparison device. No old cartridge, no Temple original connected. Luna would advise and observe seated; an authorized technician would operate it.

The diagram came before the signature line. A positioning frame fixed the observation distance. A new developing sheet behind a transparent partition used a small, interruptible supply line. The trigger was limited to placing the scar within the designated light path: no cutting, no blood, no original in the circuit. Changes to the sheet could be examined afterward; it couldn't project his mind for spectators.

What if he reported something and the sheet didn't change?

Separate records, Beatrice answered, not forced corroboration. He might remember nothing while the device registered something irrelevant.

“I don't have to succeed for you.”

“We need an accurate account of what you experience.”

He reread that. Sometimes, afraid of becoming material, he also wanted a spectacular reaction to prove he wasn't evading questions. Neither feeling guaranteed honesty. Someone should enforce the agreed limits even when he tried to act brave, not wait for the performance to end.

The diagram did not look like the door he had once imagined opening onto a complete past. It looked like a constrained arrangement whose useful result might still be a failure to understand. He followed the supply line with his eyes until he reached the place the technician would disconnect it.

He studied stopping procedures. Speech or a falling card requiring no grip meant immediate disconnection. Loss of response also meant stop, not waiting for proof he could continue. Recording and access choices were explicit; refusing disclosure wouldn't cost him medical care.

These weren't guarantees of absolute safety. Headache, nausea, confusion, and nonhistorical images were listed without euphemism. He could decline today or change his mind on arrival. Beatrice asked whom he wanted inside and outside rather than automatically including everyone who cared.

He chose the physician, technician, Beatrice, and Luna inside. Elena could wait outside; afterward he'd decide how much to share. He wrote that himself, not asking Isabella to pass on a prettier version.

Their access acknowledgments remained separate. Isabella gained no promise restoring old powers. Her own response should come directly to her; she'd share within its authorized scope.

Leaving, the clerk caught up with a supplement. The correction's page reference was included, not all attachments opened. She thanked him by his role without turning ordinary procedural help into secret allegiance.

Beside the carriage Adrian asked whether there'd been another move in the chess problem.

“Yes.”

“Why didn't you say?”

“It loses too.”

She laughed with him and gave him the problem, entirely separate from their unfinished game.

Before lunch he spread the trial instructions on his own desk and ate the prescribed light meal. Chewing bread before something potentially enormous felt absurd. Yet someone was treating his body as a person needing food, not merely a container awaiting interpretation.

Elena's reply arrived afterward. She'd wait outside and accepted that he might not immediately want to speak. No Come back for my sake, no fear disguised as indifference. He folded it into his pocket. He wasn't taking an outcome he owed her, but knowledge that someone would be beyond a door he could still choose whether to open.

He checked the list of people again. Naming who could remain inside was not a declaration that everyone outside mattered less. It was a decision about a room in which he might become frightened, confused, or unwilling to speak, and he wanted to make it before those things happened.

He signed within the specified scope, acknowledging withdrawal at any time. Three incomplete inquiries lay beside it, the year still blank.

His careful signature did not resolve any of those inquiries. It said only what he had agreed to try this afternoon, under conditions he had been allowed to read and question. He left the date box beside the records untouched, even after the pen was already in his hand.

An empty box wasn't permission to fill it with fear.
