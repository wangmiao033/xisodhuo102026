# Chapter 227: She Opened Her Own Accounts First

On March 26, Elena removed the family crest from the meeting table.

Adrian thought she meant to replace it and offered the soft cloth beside it. She told him she was removing it, not polishing it.

“Today is about what can actually be supplied. The rose won't pay anyone.”

She wasn't concealing her name. Rosenberg appeared on the invitation, and a family financial agent would attend. She simply didn't want the silver ornament at the table's center to make an unspoken objection feel discourteous.

Academy works staff had reduced the research requisition: independent newly made low-capacity energy plates, nonliving substitute plates, ordinary connecting materials for nodes A and B, and a protective enclosure they might borrow. Ancient objects, the old energy case, and the Crown weren't included. Nobody's body was material to solicit from noble donors.

Adrian turned the list over. A space for sources of compensation remained blank.

“Could I write that I'll be responsible?”

“With which funds?”

He couldn't answer. Without laughing, she opened her own folder. The existing family shortfall, the small amount previously verified, and spending she could propose today were separate matters. Important research didn't cancel the twelve-hundred deficit or count the earlier hundred and twenty twice.

“We can promise to address damage seriously. Not promise a balance we don't have.”

When the visitors arrived, Elena first presented limited information from her own side. Not every asset or employee's income: borrowing conditions relevant to the research, small quantities genuinely available, and items that couldn't be diverted. Her agent was to identify anything still requiring authority. Her wishes wouldn't approve it on the family's behalf.

He crossed out an old storehouse. The family owned it, but it was leased, not empty space available for researchers.

Elena looked briefly displeased. She remembered old furniture stored there and had already imagined which wall should hold the enclosure.

“Remove it, then.”

A noble representative asked why she'd invited others if she couldn't even use everything belonging to her house.

“Because I have to check too. Listing things I can't give and asking you to follow would only produce a very wealthy sheet of paper.”

He smiled slightly and opened his ledger.

One visitor offered materials on condition that every announcement praise his family's generosity and exclude its name if the simulation failed. Another would lend an enclosure, with repairs subject to valuation rather than an assumption that borrowing entitled the researchers to buy it cheaply after damage. A third offered procurement contacts, not funds.

Adrian instinctively preferred the first. He had said yes; it sounded most like progress they could carry away. Elena asked whether excluding the name meant protecting private information or appearing in successful results while disappearing from records of failure.

The representative shifted his chair backward.

“My family shouldn't suffer reputationally for your technical errors.”

“Which is why we won't claim nobles approved technical safety. But the route of funds and materials cannot become real only on a successful day.”

She proposed no promotional naming, with the origin of supplies recorded within agreed disclosure limits and technical responsibility examined separately. He refused. Keep negotiating this way, he said, and no support would remain.

Elena watched his ledger close without pursuing it.

The room grew quiet. Adrian knew she, too, was wondering whether they'd now obtain the plates. Keeping conditions visible was harder here than declaring principles alone at home.

The enclosure lender retained his maintenance requirements. Elena asked academy staff to explain its use, the authorized operator, and preservation for valuation if damaged. Borrowing wouldn't permit arbitrary dismantling. After listening, he allowed one working session with the existing certified enclosure, its condition checked at handover.

Not a lavish answer. An answer they could use tomorrow.

An agent from a smaller house produced a stock sheet. A few new energy plates could be supplied for the stated purpose. However, two listed as available were already promised to a winter relief training course. He wasn't sure they could be reassigned.

Adrian nearly suggested the course could be supplied later. Seeing Cecilia nearby, he didn't wait for her reminder. He asked how many remained uncommitted.

Works staff checked the requisition again and removed duplicate sets intended to improve the presentation. What remained supported one limited simulation and ordinary preliminary checks. The uncommitted supply covered this small step, not two complete trials or long-term energy provision.

“And after failure?” the agent asked.

“Stop. Establish the reason. Apply again,” Luna said.

She attended only a short session, her right palm resting while an assistant turned diagrams. She offered no dazzling magic demonstration. She explained independent stopping, protective isolation, and no connection to the old system. Academy reviewers answered professional questions. Wanting to live didn't make her voice a safety assessment.

One representative asked whether, as a marked bearer, she could first sense whether the material would be useful.

“No. We haven't applied for human testing.”

“Only a look.”

“Looking can describe many operations. Please don't promise an undefined action is small.”

She had the question recorded for ordinary technical examination. No red light rose from her palm. Nobody opened a purse because they'd witnessed a miracle. Watching her insist that the drawing remain unmagical, Adrian found it harder to look away than during their earliest teasing encounters.

She noticed. Quietly, she asked whether he was mistaking the cup for the model again.

“I'm looking at the researcher.”

Her smile paused, then returned. “Don't delay the researcher's timely departure.”

He cleared a small table obstructing the exit instead of turning appreciation into a request for more of her time.

Outside the reception room, a family employee arrived with a tool bag. A steward had sent him to help move things that afternoon. He'd expected furniture, only hearing on arrival that he might be entering an experimental room.

Elena offered him a seat rather than making him stand for everyone's explanations. What had the original instruction said? The slip in his bag contained only assist with research. It had no finishing time.

The steward said he ordinarily did such jobs. Today merely changed the location.

“He needs to know what location changes,” Elena replied.

A public question about willingness wouldn't settle it by itself. Academy staff explained that moving empty tables and remaining during an operating trial were different jobs. The latter required suitable arrangements and qualifications. Accepting the former didn't require staying for the latter. The employee could move furniture, but not extend into the evening. He had an arrangement at home.

The steward glanced at Elena, apparently waiting for her to decide whether that reason was good enough. She didn't ask what the arrangement was. He should check genuine rota conflicts through the ordinary process, she said. This additional assignment wouldn't claim the evening by default.

Only after receiving a finishing time and a clear ordinary-moving role did the employee open his bag. He wasn't refusing all work. He didn't want unclear employment instructions to become participation in dangerous research.

Adrian accompanied him to see the table. It was wider than expected and needed two people at the corner, not one man straining. They requested the responsible movers according to their own work arrangements. Viola wasn't summoned to prop up a leg.

Returning, Adrian found Elena attaching a specific explanation to the original slip. The steward would issue a proper replacement. Her spoken permission shouldn't be all the employee carried to the next person giving instructions.

“You've lost a storehouse and a sponsor today, and acquired several more pages.”

“Also kept one person going home on time.” She smiled. “Don't put that in a fundraising letter. I finally asked what should already have been asked.”

After Luna left, financial staff read back the actual terms. Materials for one defined limited simulation. An enclosure borrowed and returned against recorded condition. Small research expenditure separate from valuation and compensation applications after damage. Nobody guaranteed researchers exemption from all costs or created funds from an unresolved family deficit.

The independent reviewer didn't stamp approval just because supplies existed. He checked separation from operating systems, energy limits, the enclosure, qualified operators, and stopping arrangements. Technical staff had to confirm independent stopping before work began. With those prerequisites met, permission covered a single small-scale aboveground handover simulation tomorrow, excluding bodies, ancient objects, and the core.

Failure meant stopping. Remaining time didn't automatically permit another attempt.

Adrian wrote that clearly. Records would use the available calibrated instruments, specifying what each measured rather than calling a total reading the complete state of every node. The waiting interval he'd proposed remained a model assumption, identified in the experimental record and implemented within the approved scope. It wasn't a fact borrowed from a historical calamity.

That afternoon, handover staff checked old scratches on the enclosure, quantities of connecting material, and energy-plate condition. Both sides retained records. Without connecting the A and B substitute paths, technicians tested independent interruption and confirmed that the approved action cut the supply. That established the stopping action, not the absence of unknown effects during transfer.

Adrian felt lighter when the preliminary check passed. He swallowed successful and asked when to arrive tomorrow instead.

After work, Elena kept looking at the crossed-out storehouse entry. Thinking she resented the withdrawn offer, Adrian sat with her.

“I'm not only angry at other people. For a moment I thought a Rosenberg building meant the people inside should step aside for me.”

“You didn't do it.”

“Not doing it afterward doesn't mean I can stop checking next time.”

She preserved the crossed-out entry and closed the folder. Adrian asked whether they still wanted their short walk. Last night had been too cold, so they'd had tea. Today the wind had dropped and the corner stalls remained open.

She agreed, but not to discuss what they'd do after success. They held permission for a trial. Nothing guaranteed tomorrow's diagram would behave as imagined.

He offered his hand as they walked. She noticed before taking it. Her palm was warmer; her pace half a step quicker. At a ribbon stall, she stopped and asked whether he intended to recommend every blue object to someone.

“Isabella told you?”

“She said your gray choice looked like a tablecloth.”

“I offered an opinion.”

“Fortunately she ignored it.”

Elena picked a dark red ribbon and asked his view. He studied it seriously and said it resembled the jam she'd spread on the plate rim at breakfast. After staring at him, she laughed, returned it, and bought a narrower ribbon.

Laughter hadn't made them forget tomorrow. Nor did he ask her, when they parted, to promise pride in him regardless of the result. She asked him to sleep tonight and work within the scope he'd signed tomorrow.

Back in his room, Adrian laid out the first record sheet. The results space remained empty beside the borrowed equipment's condition and stopping rules.

He left it under the lamp without writing a happy ending in advance.
