# Why Do All the Villainesses Want to Marry Me?
## Volume One: A Betrothal Written in Blood
### Chapter 2: That's a Bow Tie, Not a Noose

It took Adrian Reed five minutes to work out what he was supposed to do with the strip of black silk.

On the sixth minute, he gave up.

The man in the mirror wore a crisp white shirt and a charcoal tailcoat, silver roses embroidered at the cuffs. Everything looked respectable, provided you ignored the black knot strangling his neck.

Adrian gave one end another pull.

The knot tightened.

He coughed.

Martha, watching from the doorway, shook her head.

"Where on earth did you work before coming here?"

"Long story."

"I've got time."

"I haven't."

Martha glanced at the clock. "You have twelve minutes. Her Ladyship's carriage won't wait."

Adrian touched the crescent-shaped scar on the back of his left hand.

The man who'd died near the execution platform in the game had carried the same mark.

He tried to summon that half-forgotten face again, but the bow tie chose that moment to cut off his air.

At this rate, the royal conspiracy wouldn't have to kill him.

Formalwear would get there first.

"Hold still."

Adrian looked up.

A flash of deep red had appeared behind him in the mirror.

Elena Rosenberg was dressed for the ball. Her golden hair was swept up, leaving her neck bare, and ruby earrings caught the candlelight whenever she moved. She looked less like a young woman on her way to an elegant evening and more like someone preparing to question a suspect.

"My lady? The bow tie?"

"Your hands. Keep pulling like that and you'll walk into the palace wearing a noose."

"I've never worn anything like this before."

"That much is obvious."

She stepped in and undid the mess at his throat.

Suddenly, she was close enough for Adrian to catch a faint floral scent—not the heavy sweetness of perfume, but something like roses freshly cut after the rain.

He retreated half a step.

His lower back struck the dressing table.

"Stand still," Elena said.

"I *am* standing still."

"You nearly knocked over the table."

"The table's in a terrible place."

She looked up at him.

"Although," he added quickly, "I suppose that's also partly my fault."

Her fingers moved efficiently, looping the silk into a neat bow.

"You're shaking."

"I'm worried you'll be executed tonight."

"That's a much more respectable excuse than being embarrassed."

"I'm not embarrassed."

Elena glanced at the tips of his ears, bright red in the mirror.

She said nothing.

Somehow, that was worse.

She smoothed his collar and stepped back to inspect her work.

"There. Now you look less like a servant who's just escaped the kitchens."

"Your praise is overwhelming."

"I haven't said anything about your shoes."

Adrian immediately looked down.

Both shoes had been polished until they shone.

"What's wrong with my shoes?"

"Nothing." Elena turned away. "I wanted to see if you'd look."

He stared after her.

In *Crown of Fate*, Elena was the sort of villainess who seemed designed to annoy players. Every line dripped with aristocratic contempt. Every smile meant someone was about to be humiliated.

The woman in front of him was suspicious, sharp, and meticulous about evidence.

She also made jokes about people's shoes when she was nervous.

Far more troublesome than the version in the game.

Far more real, too.

"What are you staring at?" she asked without turning.

"Just wondering why you're so determined to go tonight."

"I explained that already."

"I know. If you stay away, it'll look like you're running. But if the prince really has a trap waiting—"

"Then we'll make him explain it in front of everyone."

She turned to face him.

"Adrian, I didn't decide to attend because I believe your predictions. I decided to attend because the prince has already started moving against me. Whether you exist or not doesn't change that."

Adrian fell quiet.

She wasn't sitting around waiting for a player to pick the right dialogue option.

She was making her own decisions.

A knock interrupted them.

Martha entered carrying a folded document in one hand and a case of cuff links in the other.

"My lady, the palace has sent the updated guest arrangements. And His Grace would like to see you before you leave."

"Where is Father?"

"His study."

Elena took the list but didn't leave.

"You said the prince intends to present a letter tonight."

"Yes."

"Do you know who's supposed to testify about it?"

Adrian hesitated.

Of course he did.

In the original route, Professor Alfred of Saint Rose Academy had been summoned. He'd supervised Elena's magical examination on the fifteenth of the previous month, putting him in a position to confirm her alibi.

Under pressure from the royal family, he remained silent at the banquet.

Adrian remembered that scene far too well. The game even let the player publicly humiliate the professor to gain affection points.

"Professor Alfred," he said.

Elena opened the document.

Her brows drew together.

"He's not on the list."

Adrian took it.

The palace had arranged its guests by family and official post. There was no Alfred among the academy's faculty.

Instead, the Royal Judicial Office had gained an additional attendee.

Edwin Clay.

A clerk.

"He shouldn't be here," Adrian said.

"You know him?"

"No."

Elena gave him a look.

"You sounded very certain a moment ago."

"I know what his job is." Adrian spread the list across the dressing table. "Professor Alfred can confirm you were at the academy during the alleged meeting with the rebels. What can a judicial clerk confirm? That he received a letter?"

"And if the letter is forged?"

"He can still claim he only accepted it according to procedure."

Elena considered that.

"So the person who can actually establish my innocence has been removed. And in his place, there's someone whose role is merely to handle a document."

"Exactly."

"That doesn't prove a conspiracy."

"I know."

Adrian studied the guest list again.

Something bothered him.

The clerk's name appeared darker than the surrounding entries.

He brought the page closer to the lamplight.

"Martha, could I borrow a pencil?"

She handed him one.

Adrian placed a thin sheet over the suspicious line and rubbed lightly with the side of the graphite. Indentations from an earlier entry began to show through.

*Alfred.*

He looked up.

"The professor was on the original list. Someone changed it."

Martha leaned in, astonished. "How did you know to try that?"

"I saw someone do it once."

He'd nearly said *in a movie*.

Elena examined the marks herself.

"An altered list doesn't automatically mean criminal intent."

"No. But it does mean this isn't an innocent printing error. We need to find out who approved the replacement."

She finally nodded.

"That," she said, "is evidence rather than prophecy."

"I can't exactly make a living on prophecy forever."

"You're still planning on making a living?"

"I'd like to be alive for breakfast tomorrow, at least."

The corner of Elena's mouth twitched.

Martha, however, remained unimpressed.

"My lady. His Grace is still waiting."

Elena gave the original list and the rubbing to Martha.

"Seal the original. Bring a copy in the carriage. Don't let anyone know we've noticed the indentation."

"Understood."

Elena looked at Adrian.

"You're coming with me to see Father."

"Me?"

"You wanted to know why I intend to attend tonight, didn't you? Perhaps he can help explain."

The duke's study was considerably larger than the sitting room. A Rosenberg family crest hung above the fireplace, and the desk was buried beneath neatly arranged papers.

Duke Edmund Rosenberg was reading a confidential report.

When he saw Adrian, his eyebrows knitted together.

"This is the servant who claims to know the date of your death?"

"Yes," Elena said.

"On what grounds have you brought him into my study?"

Adrian was about to bow when Elena spoke first.

"He's found evidence that the palace's guest list was altered."

The duke took the paper with the faint impressions.

He studied it for a long moment.

"Are you certain the royal household made the change?" he asked Adrian.

"No, Your Grace. It could have been changed before it was sent, or after it arrived here."

"Then why are you calling it a plot?"

"I'm not saying we've proved a plot. All I know is that someone doesn't want the professor at tonight's banquet."

The duke set the paper down.

"You're not quite as stupid as I expected."

"Thank you, Your Grace."

"That wasn't a compliment."

"Then I withdraw my thanks."

Silence.

Elena suddenly coughed into her hand.

The duke turned toward her.

"What's so funny?"

"Nothing, Father."

"I saw you smile."

"I was merely thinking his manners could use some work."

The duke sighed and looked back at Adrian.

"Very well. You may accompany Elena tonight. If anything happens, your first duty is to protect her. Don't start playing the hero."

Adrian nodded.

"I'll do my best."

"And don't imagine that being useful once gives you permission to pursue my daughter."

"Father," Elena said sharply.

Adrian blinked.

"Your Grace, I promise you, my main concern is whether I'll still be breathing tomorrow."

The duke appeared satisfied with that answer.

Elena, less so.

She walked out of the study at a pace that forced Adrian to hurry after her.

"My lady, does His Grace have the wrong idea about something?"

"What do you think?"

"He seems to think I'm trying to court you."

She stopped.

"And?"

"And I thought I should clear that up."

"Clear up what, exactly?"

"I have no intention of doing anything like that right now."

Elena looked at him.

Then she turned and kept walking.

"Good."

"Are you angry?"

"No."

"Then why are you walking so fast?"

"Because the carriage is leaving."

Adrian checked the clock.

Seven minutes remained.

He wisely kept that to himself.

The carriage was upholstered in deep-blue velvet. Beyond the window, the streets of the capital were coming alive with evening lamps.

Elena sat opposite him, reviewing a copy of the guest list.

Adrian considered asking about the duke. Instead, he checked his cuffs.

A glove covered the scar on his left hand.

He didn't know whether Prince Leon knew about it.

He didn't even know who the dead man in the game had been.

"What are you thinking about?" Elena asked.

"Professor Alfred."

"Liar."

"How can you tell?"

"You don't spend your time touching your left hand when you're thinking about guest lists."

Adrian managed a weak smile.

"There's still a lot I can't explain."

"I know."

"You're not going to press me?"

"Would it help?"

He shook his head.

"Then tell me when you're ready."

For a moment, he could only stare at her.

She was already looking out the window again.

"Don't get the wrong idea," she added. "If I find out you're using your secrets to hurt me, I'll still turn you over to the guards."

"That's the third time you've said that today."

"Then it seems to be sinking in."

They passed through the central square.

Workers were putting up notices for the Autumn Ball. Under the streetlamps, a few young nobles speculated about the prince's engagement.

"I hear His Highness is making some grand announcement tonight."

"Perhaps they're moving the wedding up."

"Who knows?"

Adrian watched them through the glass.

They'd been background characters in the game.

Here, they had their own voices, their own expressions, and absolutely no idea what was coming. They were laughing over the possibility of a wedding.

By tomorrow, those same people might be standing on street corners discussing Elena Rosenberg's treason.

The thought left a bitter taste in his mouth.

"My lady?"

"Hmm?"

"If we manage to stop the accusation tonight, what will you do afterward?"

"Find out who forged the evidence."

"And if the prince himself is involved?"

"Then I'll investigate the prince."

"He's royalty."

"I'm aware." She raised an eyebrow. "Just as you're aware I'm a duke's daughter, aren't you?"

Adrian remembered his earlier insistence that he had no plans to pursue her.

Why did that sound like a pointed remark?

"Those aren't really the same thing."

"Aren't they?"

He opened his mouth.

Then decided not to.

Elena looked rather pleased with herself.

The palace appeared at the end of the avenue.

White steps swept up from the square to towering gates, where torchlight flashed off polished armor. A line of carriages waited at the entrance while guards checked invitations and attendants' names.

When the Rosenberg carriage arrived, the gate officer bowed.

"Lady Elena."

She presented the invitation.

"And this is my attendant."

Adrian gave his name.

The officer lowered his eyes to the register.

"Adrian... Reed?"

His voice had changed.

Adrian noticed the man's finger resting on the name a fraction too long.

"That's right."

The officer looked up at him, then quickly away.

"One moment, please."

He called another guard over and whispered in his ear.

The guard immediately hurried through the palace gates.

Elena lowered the carriage curtain.

"Is something wrong?"

"No, my lady." The officer attempted a smile. "It's simply that His Highness left specific instructions."

Adrian felt his heartbeat falter.

"What instructions?"

The officer said nothing.

Elena's voice cooled.

"Is there a problem with my attendant?"

The man swallowed.

"His Highness ordered us to inform him immediately if Mr. Adrian Reed appeared."

Adrian stared at the open gates.

He'd only arrived in this world today.

Yet somehow, the prince had been expecting him.

"My lady," he said quietly.

"Yes?"

"I don't think you're the only person being lured into a trap tonight."

Elena said nothing.

She simply leaned forward and straightened the bow tie he'd managed to pull crooked again.

"All the more reason not to let them see you're afraid."

She drew her hand away.

"Stay close, Adrian."

Beyond the gates, a guard was already hurrying toward the prince's private rooms.

And behind one of those closed doors, someone had a sheet of paper ready.

Adrian Reed's name was written across the top.
