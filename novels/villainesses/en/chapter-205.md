# Chapter 205: She Handed Over the Box

No ritual lamps burned in the receiving room on March 4.

Adrian saw a table covered in pale cloth before he saw Evelyn Vale sitting beyond it. She wore a dark gray coat. A narrow wooden box stood beside her chair; her hands rested on her document case. She didn't greet him with an intimacy supposedly earned by their names appearing in old records.

Elena took the other invited seat. Their representatives had separately confirmed today's scope: observe inventory and provenance, not take custody of originals or question Evelyn about every historical case. Disclosure of private information remained a separate matter.

Evelyn's representative presented her written offer. She would surrender the entire volume without immunity from questions in exchange. Cooperation didn't cancel other inquiries or make her an eyewitness to every entry.

“Including marginal notes unfavorable to you?” the examiner asked.

“Including those. Please distinguish them from the old text.”

She produced a key without opening the lid herself. The custodian first checked the current seal and arrival register, recording the cord's arrangement. Evelyn and her representative had sealed it for this handover. It hadn't remained untouched since antiquity. A handsome seal couldn't erase her previous sole possession.

Inside lay a thin, dark brown ledger, its wrapping, and two custody slips. No energy source capable of illuminating Adrian's scar; no mechanism that opened at a spoken name. One corner had worn pale, and stitching had broken near the spine's foot.

The cover read Reset Duty Review Log: Working Duplicate.

He had seen reset in yesterday's notice. Finding it on a cover rubbed by repeated handling still tightened his chest.

“The title shows what the record keepers called this work,” the examiner said. “It doesn't establish that every entry proves the entire world began again.”

Adrian nodded. A few fragments of his own didn't make this the book entitled to answer every blank.

Evelyn explained that it wasn't a memoir she had recently reconstructed. The old volume appeared in a Temple historical-review project's custody catalog. During her current service she had formally borrowed it to compare later summaries. Her signature appeared on the present issue slip; the project registry retained a counterpart.

“Why wasn't it returned with the material from your former office?”

She glanced at her representative. “I kept it. I thought returning it would leave only the passages someone permitted to be quoted.”

“Your judgment. Was there a written instruction to retain it?”

“No.”

“An extension of the loan?”

“No.”

She didn't add that she'd therefore had no choice. The box stood in front of them. Her decision to retain material she should have returned was something they could investigate, not hide inside a reasonable-sounding fear.

The custodian separated its two slips. One documented current issue, the other the volume's earlier archival deposit. Different layers of time. Today's signature couldn't authenticate the old depositor, and an accession year wasn't automatically the year of the events inside.

The independent registry's comparison copy remained in another sealed envelope. It hadn't emerged from her box as a convenient third paper. Its retrieval request and dispatch record preceded today's opening. Number series, title, and recipient matched. The return space was blank, beside an existing reminder to surrender the volume.

“You received the reminder?” Adrian asked.

“Yes. I didn't answer.”

He didn't ask whether ignoring it had made her suffer. She didn't suggest that acknowledging it now erased the effects of her silence.

They examined the ledger itself. Its accession number matched the old catalog. Binding holes beneath the cover and continuous folds didn't suggest a recent wholesale replacement. Two missing leaves were unmistakable, however, torn stubs interrupting the sequence. The custodian marked their positions. Evelyn's recollection wouldn't restore them.

She said they had been absent when she borrowed it. The issue-condition sheet partly supported her, but its description of one gap was vague. The earliest inspection sheet still needed retrieval. Agreement about a gap didn't establish an innocent explanation for it.

“What have you added?”

“Three pencil notes. The current loose slips have been submitted separately.”

The first concerned calendar comparison; the second marked a transfer status omitted from a summary. The third read Do not circulate for now, with a short signature Evelyn could acknowledge as her own today.

Adrian studied it. She had recognized something unsuitable for a shortened conclusion. Her answer had been to keep it with herself.

“Why surrender it now?”

“Your supplementary statement didn't remove your shutdown.”

“And that makes you trust us to remove what for you?”

Her face changed. After a moment she shook her head.

“No. I meant it made me think you could distinguish things. I know that sounds like choosing somewhere more willing to hear my explanation.”

“I'm not the person receiving this material.”

“No. That's why I brought it here.”

She withdrew her hands from the case. No promise of complete transformation, no request that he endorse her trustworthiness in front of the examiner. He could want the book's leads without trusting its former keeper.

During the break he went to the water table. Two thin tin cups stuck together. Trying to separate them, he nearly struck them against the edge. Elena lifted the upper cup with a gentle twist and passed it to him.

“If I have to prove I can manage even that alone, this will be a long day,” he murmured.

“I merely refuse to let you drink two cups of water and count them as one.”

He wanted to laugh, but his throat tightened. She let him drink without asking whether he wanted to explain tears as well.

“When she said she trusted us to distinguish things, I wanted to agree. As if agreeing would persuade her to give us everything I don't know.”

“You don't have to give her the judgment she wants first.”

He nodded. “You didn't ask my questions for me.”

“I wanted to, several times. Especially when she said she thought it was safer.”

The earlier Elena's letter wasn't on today's table, but it left an echo they both understood. Adrian didn't say the women were the same. Keeping a book and handing over a person were different acts requiring separate examination. Either could postpone another person's voice.

He finished his water and set the cup on the washing tray himself.

The afternoon comparison didn't open every secret in the ledger.

The examiner selected an operating entry concerning the west relay court. The previously preserved works sheet retained its independent source. The February 28 assessment identified exactly which words had been examined then. Today's ledger couldn't add anything retrospectively.

Its entry said the reassigned bypass awaited retesting; reconnection approval remained outstanding. A room subnumber matched the works sheet, but the revisions occupied different places. The works sheet recorded equipment condition. The ledger recorded receipt of that condition report and an instruction to keep transfer suspended. Different responsibilities preserving related information, not one copy dressed in two envelopes.

The copying clerk read both sources back separately. Agreement connected this portion of the old ledger to relay work already verified and warranted further examination. It didn't authenticate every page, much less turn a privately held record into a witness to the entire world's end.

“Can we establish who ordered the bypass reassignment?” Adrian asked.

That field referred to another instruction not yet retrieved. He stopped reaching toward the next page and waited for the custodian to turn it within the authorized scope.

Near the end of the permitted passage came something new.

Warning comparison line changed. Obtain the central comparison baseline separately.

Beside it, the authorization-source field clearly named High Priest Oswin. Not his personal signature: a record keeper's entry identifying an instruction's source. This ledger established what its page said. One name couldn't establish a hand reaching into every mechanism.

Evelyn said the corresponding original instruction wasn't in the box. Its cross-reference in the old catalog could support a separate retrieval request. She produced no universal key from her sleeve. She identified where the paper had been registered. Its survival and access permissions remained for the repository to determine.

“Is the central baseline an apparatus diagram?” Adrian asked.

“Not on the strength of this sentence,” the examiner said. “First we establish what was compared, who approved a change, and where it was applied.”

A question about everyone's supposedly inevitable fate had narrowed to something a person might actually have authorized. Too small to explain every death. Small enough that they could seek a corresponding document tomorrow.

Adrian chose to permit today's authorized comparison concerning him to accompany the related application. His earlier account retained its original disclosure limits. He signed neither unrestricted access to his information nor a request to take the ledger home alone.

After inventory, the volume entered independent custody with its wrapping, current annotations statement, and gap record. Evelyn received a handover acknowledgment, not immunity. Further-questioning notices went separately to her and her representative. She checked the date and put hers in her case.

Leaving, Adrian noticed the empty space beside her chair. More tangible than a speech of sudden repentance. It didn't tell him how much she still hadn't said.

Dinner was ready at the house. Viola sat near the door with her left arm supported, studying the three-sword book cover Elena had sketched from memory before dinner. She didn't demonstrate with her injured arm. Her right forefinger indicated the blank space beside the knight.

“If we add a horse, a second sword could go beside the saddle. The third requires explanation.”

Elena picked up a pencil and added a few strokes. The horse's legs were straight, its neck almost nonexistent. After examining it, Viola cautiously suggested that the horse might need a representative of its own.

“At least it finally appears,” Elena said, putting down the pencil.

Adrian suppressed a laugh and secured a more convincing tail for the unfortunate animal.

“I expected you to convict the author immediately,” Adrian said.

“An author is entitled to explain why anyone needs three swords.”

Cecilia arrived with her soup and seriously asked whether the knight had a fourth, concealed hand. Viola said that would present a different training problem. Elena laughed hard enough to tilt her spoon. Adrian steadied the edge of her dish, then withdrew his hand.

Nobody required him to recount the entire session. He chose to say the ledger was deposited and still needed verification. Luna wouldn't add a technical session tonight. Her right palm and easily tired left hand needed rest; tomorrow's permitted copies would suffice.

Afterward Elena asked whether he still wanted to hear the book.

“Not tonight. I want to finish writing my questions.”

She agreed, leaving it on their usual little table rather than carrying it back to her room. Wanting closeness yesterday and a quiet evening now didn't make the new stitching useless.

At the stairs he stopped and volunteered, “I don't regret yesterday.”

“I know. Thank you for telling me.”

She asked for nothing more. Upstairs his lamp illuminated ordinary paper. He wrote Oswin's name and left space beside it for whatever tomorrow could establish.
