# Why Do All the Villainesses Want to Marry Me?
## Volume One: A Betrothal Written in Blood
### Chapter 8: The Missing Deputy Steward

The docks smelled nothing like the royal palace.

The night before, Adrian had been surrounded by roses, roasted meat, and perfumes that probably cost more than his yearly wages.

Now the air held wet rope, old timber, and the sour smell of river water.

Their carriage passed a row of squat warehouses.

Elena peered through the curtain.

"Are you sure he came this way?"

"The gatekeeper only saw the carriage turn toward the river." Adrian unfolded his notes. "We don't have proof Henry is anywhere nearby."

"Then?"

"We look for the carriage first. We don't start arresting people."

She nodded.

"Good. No treating an assumption as a fact."

"I'm trying to develop better habits."

Elena leaned toward the driver.

"Stop at the bridge. There's no need to display the Rosenberg crest at a warehouse gate."

At the bridge, they transferred to an unmarked goods carriage Elena had arranged to be waiting. Only two plainclothes guards accompanied them.

Adrian eyed their modest new transport.

"When did you arrange this?"

"The moment someone said 'river docks.'"

"How did I miss that?"

"You were too busy examining my romantic preferences."

"I was not!"

She gave him a look that suggested she knew better.

Adrian decided to keep his mouth firmly shut.

The South River warehouses were among the oldest in the capital. Since the new docks opened, the area had become a patchwork of small cloth merchants and grain carriers. Many of the wooden doors still bore numbers from a system abandoned decades ago.

A gray carriage was parked beside Warehouse Three.

A fresh iron band had been fitted to its right wheel.

Adrian stopped before approaching.

"The pass first."

One of the guards circled the carriage and returned.

"It's been removed, sir. But the roof has the same lamp fittings our gatekeeper described."

"That's a match," Adrian said. "It doesn't prove Henry's inside."

Elena gave a quiet hum of agreement.

He was beginning to sound like her.

An elderly warehouse keeper sat outside the entrance, peeling an apple with a small knife.

Elena presented a written inquiry prepared by the ducal household's lawyer and explained that they were searching for an employee who might be missing.

The old man looked up.

"This isn't Rosenberg property."

"I know," Elena said. "That's why we haven't barged in."

"Who are you looking for?"

"Henry. Thin, round spectacles. He may have come in a carriage displaying a Judicial Office pass."

The keeper turned the apple in his hand.

"A carriage came in earlier. Driver said he was collecting a parcel. Didn't leave with much cargo."

"And the man?"

"Someone was taken into Warehouse Three. The fellow in the gray uniform left a little while afterward."

Adrian went still.

"How long ago?"

"Quarter of an hour, perhaps."

"Any other exits?"

"A door at the back, onto the river."

Elena looked toward the warehouse.

"We'd like to ask the man inside whether he wants to come out. Would you be willing to accompany us?"

The keeper frowned.

"I look after cargo. I don't get involved in aristocrats' household disputes."

Elena set Martha's prepared inquiry on the table.

"You can verify our employee's identity yourself. I'll also sign your visitor register. If he doesn't want to speak with us, we won't force him."

The man read the document, then rose.

"Very well. But tell your guards to leave the goods alone."

"Of course."

Warehouse Three was dim as evening.

Empty crates crowded the high shelves. Torn burlap littered the floor, and a few old pieces of sealing wax lay near the wall.

Adrian had barely crossed the threshold when he spotted a crumpled sheet wedged into the split side of a crate.

He could just make out the words:

*White Swan Stationers.*

The false supplier name.

He didn't pick it up.

"Martha isn't with us," he told Elena quietly. "We need a witness."

The warehouse keeper understood. He retrieved the paper and had one of the guards record its location.

Only then did Adrian unfold it.

Not the original order.

A copy.

Someone had reproduced the deliberately incorrect supplier name exactly.

Their bait had traveled.

Something clattered behind the shelves.

"Who's there?" Elena called.

No answer.

One of the guards moved forward.

Elena stopped him.

"Tell him why we're here first."

Adrian approached the end of the shelving without coming too close.

"Mr. Henry? It's Adrian Reed. We haven't come to arrest you."

He heard rapid, uneven breathing.

"How did you find me?"

The voice was trembling.

"Through an order with the wrong shop name."

For a moment, nothing happened.

Then a thin man stood up behind the crates.

Henry's hair was disheveled. His collar hung crooked, and one arm of his spectacles was missing. He clutched an oilcloth-wrapped bundle against his chest as if it were the only thing keeping him safe.

At the sight of Elena, he dropped to his knees.

"My lady, I'm sorry! I swear I didn't know what they were doing!"

Elena didn't answer immediately.

She had the guards wait near the entrance, leaving the warehouse keeper to witness their conversation.

"Get up. Who told you to come here?"

Henry struggled to his feet, his hands trembling.

"A man in Judicial Office uniform. He had a notice stamped by Royal Purchasing. He said I needed to verify an order this afternoon."

"What order?"

"The resupply order. He claimed the Crown suspected House Rosenberg of illegally trading sealing wax, and I had to come explain."

Adrian and Elena exchanged a glance.

The bait had worked.

"Why didn't you tell the accounting office first?" Adrian asked.

"Because he said if I told anyone, they'd audit every purchase I'd ever handled."

Henry tightened his grip on the bundle.

"I did something wrong with the accounts. My younger brother needed medical treatment. I used an advance from the purchasing fund to pay for it. I paid the money back, every coin, but they knew!"

Elena's expression turned cold.

"Misusing estate funds is a separate matter. Right now, tell me who knew about it."

Henry lowered his head.

"Mr. Clay."

Edwin Clay.

The clerk who had read the evidence beside the prince last night.

"When did you first meet him?" Adrian asked.

"Six months ago. At first I just helped the Judicial Office reconcile the estate's stationery orders. He said both offices used the same shop, so we could save ourselves the trouble of duplicate stamps."

"And after that?"

"He asked for our wax issue records too."

"Did he ever ask for the wax itself?"

Henry hesitated.

"Yes. On September sixteenth, he sent someone to collect three boxes of blue wax. They issued a receipt in the name of the royal household. I thought it was routine."

"Do you still have that receipt?"

Henry reached into the bundle and took out a thin sheet folded several times.

The receiving stamp was intact.

The signature beneath it was barely legible.

Adrian didn't snatch it from him.

"Could you put it on the table where the keeper can see? We'd like to check it and make a copy without changing the original. Do you agree?"

Henry nodded.

Elena watched him.

"Did Clay himself come here today?"

"No. It was one of his assistants. He said he'd take me to Mr. Clay after I handed over the papers."

"Then?"

"I waited. Eventually a stranger came in and told me it would be best if I left the capital tonight."

"Did you agree?"

"I was afraid to refuse." Henry's voice cracked. "He said House Rosenberg would never trust me again. And that the Judicial Office wouldn't let me live long enough to explain."

Adrian noticed flakes of blue wax scattered across the floor.

A used sealing die lay among them.

He bent to inspect it.

"Don't touch it," Elena said.

"I only wanted to see the design."

"Let the keeper witness its recovery."

The old man found an empty wooden box and placed the die and wax fragments inside separately.

The die didn't bear the royal crest.

It bore a counterfeit Rosenberg rose, with one petal missing.

Elena pressed her lips together.

"Someone's preparing to put our household's seal on forged documents."

"We still don't know who left the die," Adrian reminded her.

"I know."

She said it quickly.

Henry lowered his head.

"My lady, I only want to live. I'll repay anything I owe. I'll accept an investigation. But will they kill me if I testify?"

Elena was silent for a moment.

"The estate will deal with the advance you misused according to its rules."

Henry looked stricken.

"But what you've told us today will be recorded by a lawyer and witnessed independently. Being an employee of House Rosenberg doesn't give me the right to detain you at will."

"Then where do I go?"

"Our lawyer can arrange somewhere safe. It's your choice whether to accept. But if you intend to testify, I strongly advise you not to travel alone."

Henry looked to Adrian.

"Mr. Reed. Do you believe me?"

Adrian didn't immediately say yes.

"I believe you're frightened. Whether every detail is true, we need to check against the receipts, account books, and other witnesses."

Henry gave a strained smile.

"That's more honest than simply saying you believe me."

Outside, wheels crunched over gravel.

A guard hurried in to report that someone was preparing to drive away in the gray carriage.

Elena asked the warehouse keeper whether the vehicle was still registered on his property.

"It is," the old man said. "The driver wanted to leave without signing out. I told him he'd have to complete the register."

Elena turned to her guards.

"Notify the harbor patrol. They can verify the pass under the local regulations. We're not chasing the carriage ourselves."

Adrian had been one breath away from rushing outside.

He stopped.

If the driver was an ordinary hired coachman, sending ducal guards after him would look like a noble household seizing civilians in the docks.

And Henry was right here.

They couldn't abandon a real witness to pursue someone who might lead nowhere.

When they finally emerged from the warehouse, evening was settling over the river.

Adrian accompanied Henry toward the carriage the lawyer had arranged for him.

Henry kept his eyes on the ground.

"Mr. Reed, there's something else."

"What is it?"

"At tomorrow's hearing, I heard Mr. Clay will be personally responsible for the custody of the evidence."

Adrian frowned.

"Wasn't that always his job?"

"No. Another clerk was originally assigned. The appointment was changed yesterday. Clay told me to strike out the original name on the handover list."

Adrian remembered the amended guest list.

A familiar trick.

At the last moment, someone had removed a person capable of checking the facts and replaced them with someone else.

Elena came closer.

"Are you sure Clay ordered the change?"

Henry nodded.

"He told me himself. And I kept the notice."

Very carefully, he pulled a tiny folded paper from inside his shoe.

The letterhead belonged to the Judicial Office.

At the bottom was Edwin Clay's personal stamp.

At last, the structure of the trap began to come into focus.

The person who would control the evidence at tomorrow's hearing was the same clerk involved in moving suspicious sealing wax into the palace—and in pressuring the man who could account for it.

The hearing was less than a day away.

Elena passed the notice to the lawyer for preservation.

Then she looked at Adrian.

"Do you still think he's only a messenger?"

Adrian shook his head.

"He knows enough to decide what gets handed to whom."

He glanced toward the warehouse gate.

The old keeper was there, calmly peeling another piece of his apple.

"But why would a clerk take this kind of risk?"

Elena didn't answer.

Neither of them believed a man could forge evidence against a ducal household with nothing behind him but a pen.

The river wind lifted the warehouse flags.

Henry boarded the carriage under guard, his old notice resting in a sealed evidence pouch.

Adrian looked at the final line beneath Clay's stamp.

**Evidence Custodian for the Hearing: Edwin Clay.**

He met Elena's eyes.

By tomorrow, they needed someone independent in that position.

The question was whether they had enough evidence to make it happen.
