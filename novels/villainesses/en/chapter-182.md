# Chapter 182: Put the Original on the Table

Elena didn't ask her father for a letter certifying her good character.

On February 10 she packed last night's index and her own application. Adrian submitted a separate request as the person concerned. Her name wasn't tucked beneath his signature.

“You needn't come with me.”

“I want to. Not to say it never happened for you.”

She fastened the folder. Neither covered the small sound with prettier words. There was no promise that the morning would clear everything up, and no demand that accompanying her should count as a declaration of what he would believe.

At the independent storage room, an official offered a clean transcript headed Historical associated material under separate verification.

Elena read to the end.

“This isn't the original I requested.”

Old material was fragile, he explained. Direct exhibition carried risks. The summary adequately established a referral record. If she merely wanted confirmation that no comparable application existed today, he could provide that immediately.

Adrian watched her fingers press the edge. A comfortable exit: she hadn't done it today, so go home without examining the earlier name. The official could give her a true statement that still did not answer the question she had actually brought.

“I know there isn't a current application. I want to verify the material used to enter this name in the historical index.”

“You distrust the summary?”

“It can't tell me the original wording, whether attachments existed, or who omitted what. I'll wait under proper conservation conditions. I won't exchange my question for something easier to prove.”

She didn't demand broken seals or plates in her carriage. She requested an explanation of viewable material, reasons anything couldn't be displayed, and alternatives. Not touching wasn't the same as not knowing.

Adrian waited at the other end of the gallery. They'd agreed to ask their own questions separately. By the window, Elena silently rehearsed her request.

“How will you explain it?” he asked.

“First, I won't explain.”

More unsettling than somebody must have framed me. More worth staying to hear.

Beatrice checked the permission and proposed limited exhibition. Yesterday's transcription receipt was the original surviving object in this collection, but not necessarily the first document recording every transcribed act. A separate old judicial file contained the associated application. Three specified pages had been authorized for delivery this morning. Unrelated private passages would be shielded, with the protected ranges separately identified.

An original object isn't necessarily a firsthand original, Luna had written in her submitted advice. She wasn't in the room. Reading bad news yesterday hadn't healed her right palm. She requested source comparisons, not a new human-reading experiment.

Elena read that distinction carefully and agreed to examine the layers separately. She couldn't oppose compressed facts while calling an old transcription the beginning of everything merely to reach innocence sooner.

Two custodians checked the seal on arrival. Three pages: application, receipt, accompanying note. Old watermarks edged the paper; protective covers stayed in place. Adrian sat straight, recalling the first future execution notice he'd encountered. Again a page threatened to decide someone's life. He watched the careful handling, the protected edges, the spaces between the three sheets, trying not to let his fear turn them into one inevitable conclusion.

Elena saw the signature first.

Elena Rosenberg.

She didn't identify it immediately as her own handwriting. Some strokes resembled her current signature; others differed. Dating, transcription relationships, and handwriting examination remained incomplete. Recognizing the name wasn't an expert examination, whether she wished to deny or accept it.

The application requested the Judicial Office receive Adrian Reed for questioning concerning the stated danger, with protective confinement if necessary. It cited an account of circumstances absent from today's three pages.

Protective was harder to look at than confinement alone.

Adrian's chest tightened. He didn't know whether that earlier person had been dangerous or what the applicant had known. Somebody could call a locked door protection. The person inside would still be unable to leave.

Elena didn't insist she'd meant well.

“I want the cited account. And the record of what actually followed receipt. Not merely the line with the comforting motive.”

The receipt established that this historical file recorded judicial intake. It didn't mean every requested condition had been approved. The accompanying note contained a later questioning reference, but not a complete decision. They saw more than last night, not enough to replay the whole past.

Did she want a statement emphasizing no current confinement request?

“Don't prepare my public defense first.”

“It could reduce misunderstanding.”

“Or suggest the earlier matter has been established.”

She indicated the missing attachment, asking that its absence appear in the authorized excerpt. Adrian noticed he was waiting for her to cry, as though sufficient distress would prove the woman before him wasn't behind that application. He was ashamed, unable to pretend the thought hadn't occurred.

During the break he left first. The gallery bench was hard. Elena stood beside it without sitting so close he had to move.

“I'm afraid you'll think I'm making excuses for another version of myself.”

“I'm afraid I'm using your face as evidence.”

She slowly took the far end.

“I have thought keeping you somewhere I could see would prevent anything happening.”

He turned.

“Now, too. That doesn't mean I signed this old request. I don't remember it. But I recognize the impulse to decide safety for you.”

She didn't trade an admission for an embrace. Adrian studied his hands, recalling yesterday's unfinished withdrawal. He too had just learned what he wanted others to change when frightened.

“What do we do now?”

“I won't turn that thought into an order. Tell me if you don't want my company. I'll be upset. I won't find somebody else to agree for you.”

After listening, he said he'd still like to go home together. That didn't remove his doubts or every fear. She understood. Her hands remained on her knees.

Back at the table, Adrian made his own request: access to his material shouldn't depend on Elena's consent. His name gave him an individual basis to apply. Being the indexed applicant shouldn't automatically give her all his private information either.

Elena nodded first. The official separated their scopes, linking overlapping questions and handling the rest individually. Separation wasn't a breakup or a declaration that she had no relationship with him.

Before noon they received authorized excerpts and acknowledgment of missing material. The file stayed in custody. Signature, transcription sources, cited circumstances, and subsequent action remained distinct questions. Beatrice recommended completing provenance and condition assessment before discussing safely examinable memory-anchor leads. She hadn't authorized Luna to cast or Adrian to touch anything with his scar.

Cecilia waited outside for her own appointment instead of entering to guarantee their characters. Would they eat first? Elena wanted something savory. Adrian said anything.

“Anything is usually hardest to buy.”

He considered it and chose a hot pie. She nodded as though that genuinely deserved his own decision.

The pie shop occupied a side alley. Its oven stood low; the owner used a long paddle to move the tray onto a rack. Cecilia bought one to take back to her appointment. Lunch didn't require all three to stay until the last person finished.

Adrian and Elena took a window table. One leg wobbled. She bent to inspect it, about to ask for a wedge, while he shifted an empty chair beside them so it no longer pressed across the uneven tile joint.

“You finally repaired a problem today.”

“The table might disagree. Nobody's touching it at the moment.”

She laughed softly, bit the hottest part of her pie, and stopped. He passed water without repeating the hot-apple joke. After drinking, she admitted she'd wanted to be dignified today, at least not defeated by pastry during the hardest part.

“It doesn't choose its moment.”

“Fairer than some people.”

At the next table a mother dividing a pie asked for their spare chair. Elena made room. Once seated, the child concentrated on removing onions, not asking why the noblewoman's eyes were red. Adrian looked out the window, grateful most people here didn't know them.

Elena wrapped her untouched half for her afternoon self, she said, not as another act of care needing explanation. Adrian wrapped his too. Each kept something unfinished. The remaining food could belong to an ordinary later hunger, not a gesture either of them needed to interpret as sacrifice, debt, or a promise about the relationship. Leaving, neither grabbed all the other's papers.

In the carriage she asked whether the meal had been too ordinary. Ordinary was good, he answered. Then he clarified that future difficulties needn't be met only by sitting down to eat. She settled the bag. Today they'd done what needed doing and eaten lunch with onions, steam, and a wait for cooling.

They returned before Isabella's half-hour appointment. Adrian hadn't forgotten or treated difficult work as an excuse not to communicate. He told her he could come as agreed; he'd prefer one quiet game.

The princess took out the pieces. Should she lose deliberately?

“No.”

“Excellent. I'm poor at making that look natural.”

Her right hand arranged them; the left wrist rested. They restored and checked their previous position before continuing the outstanding move. Adrian made a mistake, not because of an old world's guilt but because he hadn't noticed her rook near the edge.

“You could have warned me.”

“You refused a deliberate defeat.”

“That's different.”

“I'll happily investigate the distinction after winning.”

He laughed. She didn't use his softened mood to claim another afternoon. At half an hour she checked the clock and asked whether to pack up. The game remained unfinished. Yes, he said; they'd ask again next time, not promise all future free time now.

She carefully sketched the position without treating a laugh as a larger commitment. The unfinished game could wait in its box. She had not lost the afternoon merely because it ended at the time they had agreed.

That evening Elena gave him her separately prepared terms for the employment consultation. No decision was needed tonight. She'd listed what she could provide separately, rather than making today's company a substitute for professional advice.

Would she come get dinner with him?

She studied him, wondering whether this was consolation. Then asked instead of guessing. He could see the question form before she spoke, and was grateful she did not require him to read her expression correctly as another test of what they meant to one another.

“Because you want my company, or you're afraid I'll be upset?”

“I want your company. I also know today was difficult for both of us.”

She nodded and walked beside him toward the stairs. Neither required an oath about every old record before the other could still say yes today.

The copies on the table contained new gaps. Beside those gaps were specific sources and people to ask. Passing them, Adrian laid his application beside Elena's, without covering her name.
