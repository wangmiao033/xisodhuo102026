# Chapter 145: Expenses Without Names

There was no red carpet in the examination room on January eighteenth.

Elena paused outside to inspect her carefully cleaned shoes. More slush lay here than outside yesterday's assembly office. A driver delivering papers was squeezing snow from his hat.

“Father said I should look dependable the first time I act for his seat.”

“Your shoes have done their best.”

She glared, then held Adrian's sleeve to step over a loose paving stone. She released him inside.

The examiners confirmed which documents were available. Elena had her father's authority to present the case, not a key to the royal repositories. These records fell under the preservation request and today's permission. Unrelated inventories of royal private property remained outside the inquiry.

Simon Ellery set a document box on the table. Its seal was opened before both sides. Inside was not an imposing black secret ledger, but a thin gray volume labeled East Corridor Miscellaneous Expenses.

“It used to be filed under facilities,” he explained. “Your request to trace the same receipt led us here through the payment register.”

Hester Blythe checked the volume number before turning to November. A royal surname could not substitute for an actual figure.

Adrian recognized two entries.

P81: one hundred and twenty. P96: one hundred and twenty.

Both still cited RC61.

“We know this part,” Elena said.

“We know how the two payments went out,” Hester replied. “Today we look at where the second was entered afterward.”

She opened a folded sheet beneath the volume. P96 appeared as transport in the facilities column, but a marginal reference pointed to a suspense account on another page. There was no carrier's signature there, only an office receipt stamp and a month-end internal offset.

Adrian read it twice. “Transferred to special liaison suspense; not outstanding to the carrier.”

Not another carriage. Not unpaid escort costs. Not a newly discovered receipt conveniently filling the gap.

It was the trail left by one hundred and twenty silver coins slipping from one purpose into another.

“Does that prove theft?” Elena asked.

“It proves the original description was incomplete. Whether there was lawful authority, and where the money finally went, needs another layer. Don't put a conclusion I haven't reached ahead of my figures.”

Elena stopped her pen and nodded.

The back half did not contain every royal expenditure. It covered the old project's maintenance, document transport, and personnel liaison costs relevant to this search. Hester followed the references, separating paid expenses, internal transfers, and unresolved suspense entries.

The same sum appearing in two books did not automatically become two payments. Conversely, a payment marked settled in the main ledger might merely acquire another name below it.

Adrian read out references. On the third line he mistook a nine for a four. Hester caught it immediately. Elena circled his error without mercy.

“Your shoes are more dependable than your nines.”

He conceded and turned the paper toward the window.

What truly silenced the room was an old personnel allowance sheet folded behind the suspense pages.

Eight work positions. No names.

Each linked to an old works assignment number. The sheet stated that contact and departure arrangements were complete, the money transferred to designated collection, and the original employing office required no further notification. Yet that office's records still listed some workers as whereabouts unreported.

This did not prove eight deaths. Nor did it prove eight people had received money.

Hester covered identifying fields excluded from public release and checked the numbers she was authorized to compare. Adrian did not lean past her to read a concealed line.

“They match the positions held by the eight missing people,” she said at last. “But there are no personal acknowledgments of receipt on the collection sheet. We cannot say they took the money and left.”

Elena's fingers tightened around her pen.

For four years people had gone from office to office, while contact stood complete on paper.

“What exactly was completed?” she asked.

Nobody answered immediately.

Ellery found the linked approval sheet. It authorized temporary liaison funds; the execution note separately added proxy collection and closure arrangements. Two sets of handwriting, different dates. Ink color alone couldn't identify the writer. Nor did the original signatory automatically know every later action.

Adrian thought of yesterday's relief offer to Isabella. The similarity troubled him, but a new letter was not evidence of a four-year-old conspiracy. He did not join them into an established plot.

Could they trace who had received the proxy collection funds?

Hester indicated a reference. “The royal external liaison advances office. Beyond that we need its original book. It isn't here.”

Not the Temple. At least this page did not say so.

Elena left that space blank rather than supplying the most infuriating possible name.

During the afternoon break, they ate packed pies in the corridor. Elena bit too eagerly and burned her mouth on the filling. She pressed her lips together.

“The papers aren't escaping,” Adrian said.

“I know.”

“Neither is the filling.”

She held the rest toward him, as if inviting proof. Instead of eating it, he folded down the paper holder to let steam escape.

Watching his hands, she relaxed a little.

“I nearly wrote that they treated missing people as expenses already dealt with.”

“That can be your anger. It can't replace what the records say and what we still lack.”

“Why are you so irritating whenever I want to say something satisfying?”

“Because tomorrow you need answers, not just an audience.”

After a silence, she touched the back of his hand.

“Be irritating again tonight. For now, help me finish lunch.”

Inside, Hester had spread out the recent twelve-hundred allocation again. She made everyone look at the remaining balance: four hundred and twenty.

“Finding a false description doesn't return the money. The three hundred and sixty for storage and one hundred and eighty for maintenance have supporting records. Of the two hundred and forty called transport, an independent receipt supports one hundred and twenty. Today we saw the other payment's actual transfer. Record the discrepancy. Don't turn the balance into five hundred and forty.”

Adrian wrote that down. In a game, discovering the ledger would produce a completion notice and a reward. Here there was no such sound, only another cabinet reference to pursue.

The examiners allowed copies of specified pages, requiring redacted versions for public questioning. Originals stayed in custody; both sides confirmed page counts and gaps. The old sheet did not suddenly acquire eight handwritten signatures.

Before leaving, Elena asked whether Ellery had known about the gray volume.

“I knew there were miscellaneous files. Not these transfers. Record that too. If it turns out I should have known, you can ask why I didn't look.”

She studied him and wrote his exact answer. Cooperation did not exempt him from questions; royal service did not make him guilty.

At dusk they went to the inquiry office, not a printer.

Clara Senn was waiting there. Seeing the box, she stood at once. Had they found something her brother signed?

“We still have no acknowledgment signed by Milo,” Adrian said first.

She slowly sat down.

They explained what they could: expenses corresponding to the positions had been marked contact completed, but the available material did not establish personal receipt or a return home. Next came tracing the actual collection. They did not hand over the box and leave her to identify hope among unfamiliar numbers.

“So they spent money writing that nobody needed to ask about him anymore.”

Elena answered slowly. “We can show a liaison project closed on paper while people had not returned. We'll ask on what basis. We can't yet say who received each payment.”

Clara gripped her scarf. Eventually she asked whether she could bring the notice refusing her old application tomorrow.

“You can submit it. Whether it's read publicly is your choice.”

“I want it read.”

Then she asked if the family wanting privacy would be named through this sheet. Elena explained that identifying fields were concealed in public copies. Authorized examiners checked the originals. Clara's testimony could not be used to require someone else to appear.

Clara took the notice from a cloth pouch close to her body. Its corners were soft with wear. She kept the original and asked them to copy it.

That took time. Water had faded the last two lines. Adrian moved it toward the window but wouldn't guess one word.

“I remember application,” Clara said, then shook her head. “Perhaps submission. I've read so many notices I mix them up.”

He left a gap and marked the original unclear, not turning memory into ink.

After watching him, Clara asked if they found her troublesome. Each visit brought the same questions, while they had already found so much.

Elena took her coat off the chair, not to leave but to shield Clara from the draft at the door.

“You're asking about your brother. Not how much work we've done.”

Clara rested a hand on the paper. Milo used to eat tomorrow's breakfast before leaving and hide the empty plate behind a pot, making her think she'd forgotten to cook. She gave a brief laugh and wiped her nose with her hand.

Adrian didn't ask her to be brave. He marked the unclear passage separately and explained that she could show the original to an examiner tomorrow. It needn't be forced into completeness tonight.

Elena moved the untouched bread closer. Clara broke off a small piece and ate slowly. For a little while nobody counted evidence pages or hurried to extract another clue from her story.

On the way home, Elena carried no secret capable of toppling everyone at once. She carried papers that finally allowed questions to be directed somewhere specific.

Isabella listened without snatching the records. She first asked what the families knew, then whether her own old restrictions on inquiries could be included.

“People will question you,” Adrian said.

“They should. I can't use only the parts of the ledger that embarrass others.”

Refusing yesterday's conditions did not make her innocent.

They revised tomorrow's opening question three times. Who pocketed the missing people's money became: On what evidence was the old liaison project closed, and who was authorized to confirm actual contact?

Fewer words. No less sharp. Nobody had slipped evidence they did not yet possess into the question.

When Elena put down her pen, lamps glowed outside. She offered Adrian the last cold piece of pie, promising it couldn't burn him now.

He broke it in half and left her a share.

“Some people still won't answer tomorrow.”

“Then we'll record that they didn't.”

She wiped a spot of grease from the margin, lest tomorrow somebody treat a pie stain as another mystery in the old records. Adrian supplied clean backing paper. It made them both smile for a moment.

She nodded and ate, no longer trying to write uncertainty into a victory already won.
