# English Localization and Continuity Guide

## Editorial goal

Write **natural, publishable English serial fiction**, not word-for-word translation. The translation must be faithful to plot, motives, evidence, character knowledge, and chronology, but is free to recast sentence structure, punch lines, paragraphing, and dialogue.

- Use fluent contemporary American English as the default, with light courtly flavor for formal scenes. Avoid pseudo-archaic speech and stiff literal translations of Chinese address forms.
- Keep Adrian's dry, self-deprecating humor quick and spontaneous, not as long explanatory jokes. His fear should remain believable; he becomes increasingly capable without turning omniscient.
- Elena is witty, proud, pragmatic, and proactive. Her composure is partly learned; vulnerability surfaces through small actions rather than melodramatic declarations. Let her choose, question, and disagree.
- Political/legal scenes hinge on documentary evidence, witness statements, and chain of custody. Do not turn a suspicious discrepancy into conclusive proof until independent verification exists.
- The romantic-comedy texture relies on timing, subtext, mutual respect, and character-specific repartee. The accidental kiss happens only once in Chapter 4 and does not establish a relationship.
- Preserve emotional beats, foreshadowing, and information limits. Do not add early engagements, weddings, new magical abilities, omniscient exposition, or prematurely unmask the old timeline.
- Favor clean, vivid prose and varied rhythm. A one-line paragraph is useful for a joke or twist, but not every sentence needs its own line.
- Do not add explanatory translator's notes, summary bullets, or Chinese cultural calques inside chapter prose. Preserve key plot specifics even when reworking their expression.

## Evidence and chronology

- Chapter 1: September 17. Sapphire jewelry case hides a compartment that is found empty. Scratches, disturbed tacks, and moonstone-reactive ink reveal a future-dated execution notice.
- Chapter 2: Guest list was amended; Professor Alfred's expected role is replaced by judicial clerk Edwin Clay. Prince Leon has advance notice of Adrian's name.
- Chapter 3: Leon knows Adrian's name and asks about his covered left hand. A second sapphire case without the original scratch enters through the side door.
- Chapter 4: Accidental kiss during surveillance of the side door. **Evidence Item 714** is marked **date sealed: September 18**, despite it being September 17.
- Chapter 5: Prince publicly announces intent to end his engagement to Elena, alleges treason, and proposes that she leave the capital. She refuses and demands formal review. Hearing scheduled September 19; Adrian is also summoned.
- Chapter 10: Elena survives the September 20 execution deadline. That does not mean the entire treason allegation has been cleared.
- Chapter 16: Cecilia is only named in a letter. She must not appear in person until Chapter 30.
- Chapter 30: Elena may state she intends to pursue Adrian; no current valid marriage or new engagement is created.

Maintain continuity in later translations with the latest Chinese manuscript, not only the short plot outline.

### Additional translation guardrails

- The Temple's death registration in Chapter 18 is an archival status, *not* independent proof of Adrian's literal death seven years ago.
- Chapters 19–26 connect recurring payments and record transfers without proving the private guilt of Prince Leon or Evelyn Vale. Keep the lawyers' and witnesses' explicit uncertainty in the narrative.
- Adrian's feelings are progressively more openly expressed in Chapters 19, 24, and 25; there is no current engagement or lifelong contract.
- Nora is a professional archivist with her own investigative objective (including understanding Augustus Wynn's accident), not merely a romantic obstacle.
- Chapter 24's dance is willingly initiated by Adrian and completed without another accidental kiss.
- Chapter 25: Matthew is found by lawful municipal inspection, retains control of his original notebook, and provides verifiable copies through an independent notary.
- Chapter 26: the prince's meeting proposal is not accepted; the stamped document names Evelyn Vale as a verification contact, not conclusive proof she authorized the scheme.


## Volume One finale: Chapters 27–30

- **Chapter 27 (October 7):** Elena's rehearsed thanks to Viscount Harding are mistaken for a romantic declaration. Adrian confesses jealousy and admits he hopes Elena likes him, but they do not yet formally confess. The independent hearing moves from October 12 to October 9, with additional record verification on October 8.
- **Chapter 28 (October 8):** Physical contact with the worn brass railing in the sealed western courtroom prompts a fragmented recollection of Adrian on an earlier execution platform. A golden-haired woman appears in the witness box, but neither her identity nor her words are established. The vision has no standing as judicial evidence. Authorized clerks verify an incomplete R-074 handover record and a historical seal issue entry dated September 17 referencing Evidence Item 714. They must still authenticate who actually borrowed and used the seal.
- **Chapter 29 (October 9):** Following documentary and physical verification, the Crown formally withdraws the treason case against Elena, and the court terminates the prosecution. This does not convict Edwin Clay, Evelyn Vale, Prince Leon, or the entire Temple. Investigation of evidence tampering proceeds independently. Evelyn's office has been cleared, but nobody has established that she fled to evade justice.
- **Chapter 30 (October 10):** Elena explicitly confesses romantic interest and intends to pursue Adrian by choice. Adrian admits reciprocal feelings but requests a slower, serious courtship. Their working relationship remains under independent employment assessment; no engagement or marriage is formed. Cecilia Saintclair **first appears in person** after being mentioned in Chapter 16, corrects misleading wording from Felix Horn's prior report, and presents a certified facsimile of a historical betrothal record naming Adrian Reed and Cecilia Saintclair. Both reject any current legal obligation; authenticity, signatory identity, and date conversion remain unverified.

### Evidence, magic, and relationships

The fate-mark test **does not** scan Adrian's body or mind, consume the Temple's original contract, or independently prove that a past romance existed. It consumes the finite residue of an authenticated transfer medium and magical energy from Cecilia; blank and known-date controls are documented. The dual date marks may refer to different archival events or calendar systems, and *Cycle Seven Hundred Twenty-One* cannot presently be converted reliably to a modern year. Chapter 36 should examine which parts of the oversight restrictions are justified confidentiality and which might obstruct the participants' access to records about themselves.

Elena remains proactive about pursuing Adrian after Chapter 30; don't reset her to silent denial. Cecilia is not automatically in love with Adrian by virtue of the historical record, and she values charitable medical work and her religious principles independently of the romance plot. Adrian continues exercising real autonomy in consent, investigation, and personal scheduling.


## Volume Two: Chapters 36–40 (October 16–20)

- **Chapter 36:** Temple Internal Oversight restricts public circulation of speculative findings concerning R-074's dual-date anomaly. It has **not** canceled Cecilia's independent investigation authority. Both confidentiality for sensitive originals and participant access rights matter. Cecilia's junior attendant **Mira Finch (22)** goes to verify medicine receipts and bed-number correction procedures at Mercy Clinic; she does not return to the guesthouse as planned.
- **Chapter 37:** Hale, Elena, Adrian, and Cecilia trace Mira's recorded route through an amended Temple guesthouse entry, an unsigned temporary rerouting slip, and an independently witnessed medicine wagon transfer. **Toby**, a coachman, saw her near the vehicle but not necessarily boarding it. A note in the chestnut seller's custody asks investigators to check who really received the medicine. Missing status does not alone prove abduction.
- **Chapter 38:** At Mercy Clinic, Dr. **Grace Winter** prioritizes clinical care and verifying donated medicine before allowing it into the dispensary. Cecilia participates as a charity volunteer rather than claiming automatic medical authority as a saint. Jonah's mother cannot find her son after bed reassignment; another patient, **Elliot Morris**, says the Temple staff have called him Jonah. An anomalous medicine transfer and patient-reference trail lead to **Graybell Relief Station**.
- **Chapter 39:** With narrow patient consents and municipal medical supervision, they establish that **C-19 originally belonged to Jonah Payne** and **C-91 to Elliot Morris**, but an amended Temple register swapped the identifiers. An administrative report says a blessing for C-19 was completed when Elliot occupied the mislabeled bed; this does **not** show that prayer replaced clinical treatment, that a prognosis was scientifically true/false, or that a person recovered from illness. A letter possibly from Mira identifies Graybell's east sickroom; its writer is not independently authenticated yet.
- **Chapter 40:** Authorized municipal medical inspectors enter Graybell. Jonah is alive but requires ongoing physician-supervised care, and the clinic corrects his identity and care transfer. Mira is found alive behind a door bolted from the outside, and says she refused to sign a false completion record and was not allowed to leave. Authorities independently record her statement, the absence of a valid detention order, and the paper trail without attributing guilt to an entire institution. Elena organizes care and lawful inspections; Cecilia safeguards records and volunteers. The Temple subsequently accuses Cecilia of leaking a restricted divine prognosis, setting up Chapter 41.
- **Relationships:** Elena and Adrian have already voluntarily begun courtship; do not reset them to denial. Cecilia is compassionate, direct, and committed to her vocation, and her interest in Adrian remains exploratory. Adrian can make choices without being a prize to be allocated between women.

### Dramatic and epistemic guardrails

The clinic storyline must **not** imply that a clerical correction instantly heals patients. The betrayal is the substitution of identifiers and attempts to present the stable Elliot as evidence of an oracle's success while Jonah still needs care. The existence of a fraudulent administrative report does not settle whether all religious prophecy is false. Handle medical privacy, treatment, and welfare with independent clinicians and authorized inspection. Mira has her own motives and relationships: she draws suns for children and makes red thread bracelets, and her refusal to sign is an independent ethical choice. Temple charges against Cecilia should arise from a concrete procedural accusation, not the automatic assumption that the entire religious hierarchy already knows and directed the cover-up.

Avoid literal calques such as "fated patient was exchanged" when an idiomatic sentence like "the patients' identities were switched in the records" is more accurate. Keep the old legal and religious systems distinct.
