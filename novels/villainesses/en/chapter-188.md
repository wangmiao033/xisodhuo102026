# Chapter 188: Her Place on the Witness Stand

Adrian was resting upstairs when the notice reached Elena.

He didn't see the messenger. Coming down that evening, he found a narrow envelope beside the dining table. Its broken seal enclosed a blue slip: an appointment at the judicial repository, not a judgment.

“This morning?”

“After lunch. You were resting.”

It answered her application. She could share the overlapping material with him. His independent appointment notice had been delivered separately, never through her hands. She had promised to tell him promptly about shareable developments so he could decide whether to add questions.

The appointment was tomorrow, close to their planned departure. “I need to revise my questions.”

“I've noted the relevant pages.”

“I want to write my own.”

She immediately passed the slip. His fingers still occasionally felt unreal after the trial. Not constant numbness; looking at his left hand recalled the hand pressing a brass handle. Today's hand held an ordinary notice, ink caught beside a nail.

He didn't ask why she'd waited. Soup had arrived. He feared mistaking ordinary words for hostility again. She offered nothing further. The envelope remained between them like a step neither had stumbled over yet.

They arrived on time the next morning.

The new material wasn't the complete missing account of circumstances. The custodian explained: an associated passage and witness registration had been located in an old questioning register, with a continuation annotation. The originally cited attachment remained missing. Three comparable items weren't a complete case.

Elena wore no ring that might strike the table. Her bare fingers looked prepared for work requiring particular steadiness.

Beatrice checked the pages' condition, not the truth of testimony. Two custodians compared the catalog, removal seal, and internal sequence. Fibers continued across one join. Another corner had been repaired; blank repair paper couldn't supply vanished words.

Adrian sat diagonally opposite Elena, able to see both her and the page without constantly studying her face.

Her name occupied the witness field.

Elena Rosenberg.

Not the earlier request for judicial intake: questions and answers. Had the witness personally seen the person questioned enter the restricted site? Yes. Had she heard him say the decision should no longer be left to the existing overseers, even though evacuation remained incomplete? Again, yes.

Adrian stiffened. He remembered no such conversation. The words resembled the urgency in his fragment, but resemblance wasn't recognition of his own voice.

“The next sentence,” Elena said.

Had he accepted her warning?

No. He said I had not seen the price they would pay.

Adrian had imagined a coerced signature, a page merely borrowing her name. This testimony contained details and disagreement. Details still required checking, but the name was becoming harder to dismiss as a coincidence.

Elena asked to hear the preceding line again. She didn't ask for gentleness.

Near the end, will certainly do so again had been corrected to I fear he may do so again. A witness-confirmation initial matched the form of the closing signature. An accompanying attendance entry recorded her confirmation in person; more than a later index claimed she'd come.

“What does that establish?” she asked.

The records official explained that the surviving items supported an old examination conducted under her identity, with recorded personal confirmation. Handwriting and dating required further comparisons. One present-day signature couldn't settle everything. But there was presently no basis to dismiss the testimony as a clerk merely copying the applicant's name into the wrong field.

She briefly closed her eyes. A chair scraped as she steadied her feet. She didn't collapse.

“What followed?” Adrian asked.

The continuation annotation cited this testimony in a decision to retain him for further questioning. It also cited a technical report not fully produced today. Damage obscured the duration. They couldn't guess one night, or a lifetime.

But her testimony had been used to support keeping him from leaving.

“Keep that citation relationship in the excerpt,” Elena said quietly, clearly.

She didn't argue somebody else would have done it anyway. Adrian didn't suggest the additional report made her irrelevant. They were tracing one person's evidence into a decision, not selecting one person to carry all history.

He asked about a dash between answers. In the draft excerpt it resembled a paragraph break; on the original it occupied a whole line.

“A different questioner, or a pause?”

A marginal note indicated a pause. The resumption time was damaged. Nothing said who spoke to the witness during it. Elena didn't seize the gap as proof of coercion.

“Preserve that too,” Adrian said.

The clerk asked whether today's questions should enter the associated search. Yes. Advance questions submitted the previous afternoon had informed today's retrieval; new ones could still be accepted for later searching. Adrian assumed this was an ordinary delay. He missed Elena looking away from the blue slip.

He also asked about the restricted site's boundary. Without contemporary access rules, entered didn't establish how. The register cited an old site rule; today's temple map couldn't replace it. That map wasn't here.

Elena noted the question, then stopped. She seemed eager to ask everything for him, but didn't take his page. He found that reassuring. Later he would remember that reassurance could exist while he lacked information.

During the break he stood at the gallery window. Below, an apprentice maneuvered a broad empty box through a door the porter held open.

“I wanted to say that wasn't me,” Elena said beside him.

He turned.

“I don't have those memories. That's still true. But I nearly made it mean that, because I don't remember, it isn't worth examining.”

“I've thought that too.”

“You needn't make our situations identical to comfort me.”

He didn't add that everyone was at fault. Sunlight judged neither of them. Their shadows lay close without meeting.

“Why do you think she said it?” The question was too large as soon as he asked.

“I don't know. Her fear might have been genuine. Fear isn't proof. Recognizing the impulse now doesn't mean I understand her.”

Her mouth stayed tight as she searched for the words. It wasn't simply the shock of seeing a familiar signature. A signature could still make her think of copied strokes and mistaken names. The change inside the answer showed a person considering what she meant, revising it, and allowing the revised words to remain.

The correction troubled her most. That witness had apparently understood the difference between certainty and fear, yet supplied words later used to confine him. She wasn't simply an indistinct innocent stumbling into a trap without thought.

Adrian remembered pressing the handle after hearing the warning. Making his earlier self wholly foreign wouldn't guarantee today's choices were right either.

“I don't want you confessing for her. Or explaining everything because today's version of me needs comfort.”

“All right.”

Not relief or absolution. Agreement to leave a question unanswered.

She didn't become visibly easier after saying it. He had offered no certificate that every earlier Elena was innocent, and she hadn't offered a memory she didn't possess. Still, they could return to the table without requiring either fiction. Adrian watched her straighten her papers, leaving the source references where they could be followed rather than covering the most painful passage with her hand.

They checked their excerpts. Adrian recorded no corresponding memory of the testimony's scene, without claiming he'd never spoken those words. He requested the technical report's cited page, not immediate access to an unauthorized volume.

Elena requested further attendance and signature comparisons. Unfavorable material didn't make her withdraw.

Outside, she took her coat backward. Adrian indicated the collar. She gave an embarrassed smile.

“My turn.”

“At least you haven't greeted it twice.”

The brief laugh was real. He steadied the empty sleeve while she dressed herself. She looked at his hand, then asked before taking it. Yes, he said.

They held hands only to the gate, where she needed her skirts and he his papers. Letting go wasn't a verdict on their relationship.

A street stall sold exceptionally hard honey biscuits. Elena bit hers and suspected she'd received the display model. Adrian tried his. Equally formidable.

“Two samples don't establish the whole tray.”

“Please spare me that construction today.”

He apologized, laughing despite himself. She laughed too and requested wrapping paper. They would soak the biscuits in tea rather than require their teeth to finish another investigation.

At home Martha mentioned an inquiry about receipt of the appointment notice. Its delivery stub remained in the correspondence book. Did it need returning?

With Elena's permission Adrian took it. Besides the appointment, it offered submission of supplementary questions the previous afternoon.

The deadline preceded his coming downstairs.

He read the time twice, not because the figures were unclear. Yesterday, while he'd been trying to decide whether to leave his room, there had still been something he could have done. The inspection itself had happened. He had asked questions there. Neither fact returned the earlier chance to have those questions influence which pages were retrieved. He could feel the difference before finding a sufficiently quiet way to say it.

He said nothing at first.

Elena set down the biscuits. Seeing the line he was reading, she didn't pretend ignorance.

“I saw it.”

“Why didn't you tell me yesterday?”

“I wanted to wait until you woke. Then until you'd eaten.”

“Martha says you came to my door while I was awake.”

She nodded. She hadn't intercepted his independent mail. She had withheld the opportunity she'd promised to share promptly. He remembered footsteps while he sat on his bed, awake, uncertain how to begin an ordinary afternoon.

“I was afraid this, so soon after the trial, would be too much.”

He flattened the stub. “So you decided I didn't need that chance to add questions.”

“I didn't cancel your inspection.”

Her face changed as she said it. That wasn't what he'd asked.

He didn't raise his voice. He felt more exhausted than when shouting. He couldn't make the present injury simpler by accusing her of intercepting mail, because she hadn't. Nor could she make it vanish by pointing to the appointment he'd attended. The actual thing between them was narrower than either exaggeration, and still large enough to hurt. Today's carefully protected old pages connected suddenly with a new notice delivered too late. Old Elena's testimony didn't convict present Elena. But present Elena had actually done something they'd recently agreed she shouldn't.

“I want to go to my room.”

She moved half a step and stopped, leaving his way clear.

“Could we talk tonight?”

“I'll tell you.”

He took the shareable notice copy and his excerpts, not the biscuit. Upstairs he still heard someone calling Martha, a pot lid lifting. Closing his door didn't end everything outside it.

He spread the notice on his desk. For the first time, the hardest ink to look at wasn't old. It named yesterday's perfectly definite deadline.
