# Why Do All the Villainesses Want to Marry Me?
## Volume Two: The Saint Won't Let Me Get Married
### Chapter 34: The Saint Calls It a Small Test

On the morning of October fourteenth, Adrian Reed saw the inside of Cecilia Saintclair's equipment case for the first time.

It contained no radiant staff.

No sacred prophecy sealed in gold.

There were three little bottles of ink, two rolls of quartz-fiber paper, a compact moonstone lamp, and an instruction manual thick enough to cause a serious injury if dropped from a height.

"This is your fate-mark examination?" Adrian asked.

"One version of it," Cecilia replied. "Noninvasive archival resonance. It can sometimes reveal traces of old registration marks preserved in a medium. It cannot read someone's memories, and it certainly can't tell you whom they were in love with."

"Could you paint that last part on the outside of the case?"

"Of course." She reached for a pencil. "If that would prevent misunderstandings."

Elena half closed the lid.

"Before we do anything, I want to know the risks."

"So do I." Cecilia removed a sheet from the manual. "The lamp illuminates a disposable transfer medium. The quartz paper may be damaged. The operator has to supply a limited amount of magical energy, which usually causes fatigue. In an abnormal response, the operator or a nearby participant may feel dizzy or experience associations with familiar memories."

Adrian touched the cuff covering his left hand.

"Memory associations?"

"Symbols can remind people of things. That doesn't establish that the memory is authentic. Nor can I promise absolutely no one will react."

She looked directly at him.

"You can refuse."

"Is there a way to check the document without checking Adrian?" Elena asked.

"Yes. We begin with the certified registration medium only. We don't read any magical signature from his body. The result will answer fewer questions, but it may reveal whether the document carries marks formed in separate layers."

Hale marked the relevant paragraph.

"Then our authorization should say exactly that. No blood, hair, handprint, or personal magical sample. No retrieval of private historical records. If the process attempts anything beyond documentary examination, it stops."

Cecilia nodded.

"I'd also like independent technical oversight."

"Nora's arranged the academy laboratory," Elena said. "Ms. Carter has agreed to supervise the procedure."

Adrian glanced at the instruction manual.

"In a game, you just press Confirm and a test starts. Here you need a contract before you can turn on a lamp."

"You could decline to read the contract," Cecilia told him. "That wouldn't remove the risk."

"So I'll read it."

She produced two more sheets.

The gray one described the Temple's traditional identity verification, including a fate-mark sample from the participant.

The white one was her limited research proposal. It examined only the authenticated archival transfer medium, which had been created under supervision as part of the Temple's document certification process.

"Why is your version so much shorter?" Adrian asked.

"Because collecting no information from a person's body eliminates several procedures. It also means we can't answer every question the traditional test would attempt."

"Will the Temple allow you to deviate from the usual approach?"

"A limited examination can be authorized without violating current regulations. Some officers think it's too slow. Others agree it's safer."

Elena turned to the materials section.

"Will it remove magical traces from the original contract?"

"No. The original remains sealed in the Temple's archive. The certified transfer medium contains a finite residue for this experiment. It will be sealed afterward, whether or not the test succeeds."

"And any examination of Adrian himself would require a new request?"

"Absolutely."

Adrian looked at Hale.

"I really can say no?"

"Yes. Without a lawful compulsory order, you retain control over bodily sampling. Even if such an order were issued, we'd examine its scope."

Cecilia added quietly, "I wish every person called for verification received that explanation."

Adrian glanced at her badge.

She wasn't simply trying to arrange special treatment for him.

She wanted the ordinary procedure changed.

That afternoon, they gathered in Saint Rose Academy's laboratory.

It was far brighter than the old archive. Fireproof stone covered the long workbench, and copper ventilation fans stood beneath the windows.

Nora had prepared two identical reading boards.

One for controls.

One for the R-074 certified transfer.

The historical contract original remained sealed at the Temple. Nobody in the room had authority to remove it, open it, or substitute it for a convenient sample.

Ms. Carter checked the lamp, recording its serial number and the condition of the crystal before anyone approached the documents.

"We start with a blank," she said.

Cecilia placed clean quartz-fiber paper under the shielded moonstone light.

Nothing appeared.

Carter wrote down the result.

*No detectable registration resonance.*

Next came an academy record of known date, transferred onto a similar certified medium. A shallow silver line appeared under the lamp, forming symbols the researchers could identify from the control records.

"Positive control," Carter announced. "It establishes that the instrument responds to a known specimen. It doesn't mean every mark we see on an unknown specimen will be meaningful."

Adrian leaned toward Elena.

"At last, an experiment where glowing doesn't automatically mean we've found the truth."

Cecilia overheard him.

"Visibility is not interpretation."

"Exactly."

Nora fetched a blank historical-contract form with its own certified medium.

"If the blank sample shows the same markings as R-074, we may be seeing an artifact of the paper or instrument."

The moonstone lamp shone for several heartbeats.

Only the ordinary fiber pattern appeared.

Nora and Carter recorded that separately before replacing the sheet.

Adrian noticed that they changed the forceps and logged a new reference number every time.

"Is all that really necessary?"

"Quartz paper can retain traces of wax dust from the previous sample," Nora explained. "If a later sheet shows a similar mark, we need to exclude contamination from our own handling."

"So magical research also has to worry about copying yesterday's answer onto today's page."

"Especially that," Carter said.

Elena glanced at him.

"At least you haven't stepped on anyone's dress during this lesson."

"An achievement I'd like added to my performance review."

"Denied."

At last, Carter announced that they were ready for the R-074 medium.

Hale read the limited authorization aloud once more.

Adrian wasn't asked to extend his hand or remove his glove.

He only had to decide whether to remain and observe.

"I consent to a document-only resonance examination," he said. "No use of this procedure to register anything about my body."

His words were entered in the record.

Cecilia signed an identical consent as a named party.

"The conditions should apply equally to both of us," she said.

Elena signed nothing in Adrian's place.

She stood beside him.

"If you feel unwell, you can stop."

Adrian nodded.

Cecilia placed the single-use transfer sheet on the fireproof board. The moonstone lamp stood between transparent shields. She began supplying a measured amount of magical energy.

A thread of light appeared inside the lamp.

For several seconds, the page remained blank.

Then a faint silver line emerged.

A second line appeared beside it.

The first began near the recorded witness seal.

The second was thinner and paler, running so close to the first that Adrian initially mistook it for a reflection.

Carter marked the lamp temperature, illumination time, and the direction in which the lines appeared.

"Don't increase the power," she instructed. "They're visible. More energy could scorch the medium."

Cecilia stopped.

The lamp went dark.

Sweat glistened on her forehead.

"Are you all right?" Adrian asked.

"Just tired. I'll rest for a while."

Nora gave her water.

"That's precisely why we don't repeat this indefinitely," Cecilia added.

Adrian looked at the slightly browned edge of the transfer paper.

Even this restrained piece of magic consumed something.

It wasn't a free answer machine.

Carter used insulated clips to move the sheet to the cooling board.

"Two marks don't necessarily mean two separate ceremonies. They could reflect an original registration and a later restoration. Or one could be a transfer annotation."

"Can you read the dates?" Elena asked.

"Not yet. The old symbols need to stabilize as the sheet cools. Then we'll compare them with the historical calendar tables."

Cecilia studied the two lines.

"I've examined supplementary entries at the Temple. Usually the later layer crosses or covers the original. These are parallel, as though two time fields were deliberately kept apart."

"Your professional impression?" Hale asked.

"Only that. It isn't a conclusion."

Adrian thought of the handwriting samples.

Someone might have preserved his signature for repeated use.

Could the same thing have happened to the date of a contract?

"If the two lines carry different dates," he asked, "does that mean someone wrote the wrong one?"

"It could," Carter said. "Or the two fields may refer to different events under different calendars. We need to establish what the symbols actually mean."

After the lamp cooled, Cecilia carefully recorded the energy she'd used and the fatigue she felt.

She didn't describe the result as *fate refusing to reveal itself*.

She wrote down the damaged paper edge, the measured temperature, and the equipment limits.

"Does this leave any lasting effect on you?" Adrian asked.

"Not under ordinary conditions. I'll remain here until my energy returns to its usual level."

"Then don't run another test just to save time."

Cecilia looked at him.

"You're worried about me?"

"Of course. Working together shouldn't mean anyone has to push through an avoidable injury."

She smiled, small but genuine.

Elena passed her another glass of water.

"On that, I agree. The research can wait."

"Thank you both." Cecilia looked from one to the other. "I thought I might be treated as a nuisance for bringing the betrothal record here."

"You did bring the betrothal record," Adrian pointed out.

"True," she said sincerely. "I'm simply grateful you haven't tried to stop me from doing the work."

They laughed.

For a little while, nobody argued over whom Adrian was supposed to marry.

Only the quiet crackling of the cooling quartz sheet filled the laboratory.

Elena brought them tea.

Cecilia accepted a cup and studied her.

"You're not unhappy?"

"About what?"

"About my investigating a contract bearing my name and Adrian's."

Elena didn't answer immediately.

"Of course it matters to me. But if I used my feelings to stop you examining your own history, how would that make me different from the people who tried to make decisions for Adrian?"

Cecilia considered.

"You're brave."

"Not particularly," Elena said with a faint smile. "I've just learned that being afraid isn't a good reason to stop asking questions."

Adrian raised his hand.

"Could I ask one thing?"

"Of course," Cecilia said.

"Since none of us wants an old betrothal to decide anyone's future, could we stop discussing marriage at breakfast every morning?"

Both women laughed.

Carter, still working beside the cooling plate, looked up.

"I'm afraid tomorrow's breakfast may not be safe either."

"Why?"

She transferred the stabilized silver lines onto a record film.

A recognizable date field had appeared beside the first.

Another date field had appeared beside the second.

They used different systems of symbols.

Nora leaned closer.

Her expression changed.

"Those aren't merely two ways of writing the same date."

Adrian set down his tea.

"What do you mean?"

Carter sealed the reading film.

"Tomorrow we bring in a specialist in old calendars. Until we know what the two fields refer to, we can't tell whether they belong together."

Cecilia nodded.

She left her consent form, equipment expenditure record, and original test results in the independent laboratory's custody.

Nothing went back to the Temple without a documented authorization.

As they left the academy, Adrian watched evening settle over the capital.

He'd been afraid a magical test would suddenly announce whom he ought to marry.

Instead, it had produced two dates nobody could yet explain.

For the first time, the absurd betrothal record felt as though it might conceal a real piece of history.

Not another command demanding that he make an immediate choice.
