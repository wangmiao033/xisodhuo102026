# Why Do All the Villainesses Want to Marry Me?
## Volume One: A Betrothal Written in Blood
### Chapter 17: Why Is My Lady Practicing Her Smile?

On September twenty-seventh, Adrian discovered something considerably more unsettling than an official letter from the Temple of Fate.

Elena was practicing smiles in front of a mirror.

For a moment, he thought he'd walked into the wrong room.

The duke's daughter stood in a pale formal dress, a copy of the Temple's letter in her left hand and her fan in her right.

First, she offered her reflection a gracious smile that could have charmed an entire ballroom.

Then she let it slip into an expression suggesting she'd just decided someone deserved exile.

She tried again.

"This one," she murmured, "should be suitable for listening to a liar."

Adrian stood in the doorway for several seconds before knocking.

"My lady. Are you rehearsing an interrogation?"

The smile vanished.

"Who said you could stand there?"

"Martha asked me to bring the updated visitor list."

"Then why didn't you announce yourself?"

"I needed a moment to make sure I wasn't hallucinating."

Elena glanced at the mirror and lowered her fan.

"You saw nothing."

"Naturally. Just a distinguished lady trying to make her reflection confess to a crime."

"Adrian."

"Yes?"

"Put the list down."

He obeyed, but remained where he was.

"You're worried about the Temple envoy this afternoon?"

"Of course I am. He may use religious protocol to make us answer questions before we've agreed to them."

"So your plan is to smile until he becomes frightened?"

"My plan is to prevent him from seeing when I'm angry."

Adrian remembered the ball.

When Leon had broken their engagement in front of the court, Elena's face had remained almost unnaturally calm.

Others had called her arrogant.

Adrian had noticed how tightly she'd been squeezing the ribs of her fan.

"Could I make a suggestion?"

"You're going to teach me social etiquette?"

"I've been interviewed by the capital's newspapers three times now."

"And managed to make the situation worse every time."

"Exactly. I have practical experience in what not to do."

Elena appeared to find that argument absurd.

Nevertheless, she stepped aside to give him room at the mirror.

"Go on."

Adrian stood beside her, their reflections barely a pace apart.

He raised his hand to block the sight of himself so she could concentrate on her own face.

"You're smiling too hard. If the envoy says, 'Hand over your administrative aide,' and you lift your mouth any farther, you'll look as though you're deciding where to bury him."

Elena let out a surprised laugh.

"Then what should I do instead?"

"Don't smile."

"That's your advice?"

"You're entitled to refuse him. You don't owe a Temple envoy a pleasant expression. If you state your position clearly, why start by trying to look harmless?"

Elena considered her reflection.

Then she tried again, this time with a simple nod.

"Please explain the legal basis for your request."

Quiet.

Clear.

Steady.

"That's better," Adrian said.

She looked sideways at him.

"You're starting to sound as though you know what you're doing."

"I used to know dialogue options in games."

"That game again."

He realized he'd let something slip.

But he'd grown tired of inventing a new lie every time she asked.

"Think of it as a very bad textbook on conversation. Someone else writes every possible answer in advance."

"And today?"

"Today, we ask the questions first and decide whether to answer at all."

Elena folded her fan.

"That might actually work."

Martha chose that moment to enter with a stack of freshly pressed coats.

She saw them standing side by side at the mirror and stopped.

"Oh."

Adrian turned.

"Something wrong?"

"Nothing." Martha set down the coats. "I thought you were practicing how to stand together at the wedding."

Adrian almost swallowed his own tongue.

"We're preparing for a meeting!"

"Of course." Martha looked between them. "You were preparing for a meeting at the palace too, and somehow ended up kissing."

"That was an accident!"

"Yes, Mr. Reed. You've explained that many times."

Elena raised her fan to her lips.

"Martha, weren't you supposed to be attending to the clothes?"

"Certainly, my lady." Martha curtsied with exquisite seriousness. "Though if you wish to practice smiling at someone beside a mirror, I doubt you need to wait for the wedding."

Then she left at a pace suggesting she'd rehearsed that escape route.

Adrian stared at the closed door.

"I think Martha is more dangerous at questioning people than the Judicial Office."

"Then you should stop giving her opportunities."

Elena turned back to the mirror, reached over, and straightened his collar.

Adrian instinctively stood taller.

She didn't retie his bow as she had on the night of the ball. She merely adjusted the small silver rose badge on his coat, which had become trapped under the fabric.

"You'll be speaking this afternoon too," she said. "Don't spend the entire meeting hiding behind me."

"Didn't you tell me to stay close?"

"Close isn't the same as behind."

She lowered her hand.

It was a more useful lesson than any book of courtly manners.

By afternoon, Hale and an independent recording clerk had arrived.

The sitting room's round table held no private files. Only the Temple letter, their limited-consent reply, and a blank meeting record.

"What if he asks about R-074?" Adrian said.

"First establish whether he possesses the original case file," Hale replied. "Don't trade the portrait you saw at the academy for documents he hasn't even shown us."

"So we don't exchange one suspicious blue box for another."

"At last, a lesson has stuck."

Elena signed the corner of the meeting register.

Then she handed Adrian the pen.

"You too."

"Me?"

"You're the person this inquiry concerns."

He signed without hesitation.

Just as he set the pen down, a low carriage bell sounded outside.

The Temple's records envoy had arrived.

His name was Felix Horn.

He looked about forty and wore pale-gray ceremonial robes. His hair was combed with almost painful precision, but ink stains marked his cuffs. Adrian had expected a mysterious priest.

Horn looked more like a man who spent twelve hours a day at a desk.

He bowed to the Rosenberg household before turning to Adrian.

"Mr. Reed. We've been looking for you for a very long time."

The room went still.

"How long?" Adrian asked.

Horn smiled politely.

"A figure of speech in official correspondence."

"I hope so. I already received a letter registered before I started working here. If you've really been looking for me for years, I'm not going to sleep tonight."

Horn's fingers paused against the document case.

Elena did not smile.

"Mr. Horn, please state which office you represent and what authority you have."

"The Temple's Old Contract Records Office. We need to verify an irregular fate record associated with Mr. Reed."

"What exactly do you intend to verify?" Hale asked.

"Identity, magical signature, and any historical contractual relationships."

Horn removed a folded form and set it in the middle of the table.

Adrian glanced at the bottom.

Three signature spaces.

One for House Rosenberg.

One for the Temple.

And one marked:

*Subject's Voluntary Confirmation of Accompanied Transfer.*

"That's not an ordinary archive-access form," Adrian said.

Horn looked mildly surprised.

"It's a reception arrangement. Examinations in the inner sanctuary require us to arrange escorts."

"An escort doesn't mean you get to take me away. I haven't even agreed to attend."

Elena pushed the form back toward the middle of the table.

"No examination method, no independent witness, and no information about sample retention. Please explain those matters before asking anyone to sign."

Horn studied her.

His smile slowly disappeared.

"Lady Rosenberg, the Temple has always regarded protection of its registered subjects as a sacred duty."

"Excellent. Then protection shouldn't require the subject to surrender the right to refuse."

Hale took out a numbered page.

"Your answers will be recorded, Mr. Horn."

The envoy finally set his form aside.

"If your concerns are about privacy, we could discuss them during tomorrow's verification briefing."

"Would that require me to leave the Rosenberg estate first?" Adrian asked.

"Ordinarily, the briefing takes place at the Temple."

"Ordinarily. Not necessarily."

"...Generally speaking."

"Then please record that as a preference rather than a requirement."

Elena hid the beginnings of a smile behind her cup.

Horn glanced between them.

"There has been considerable public speculation concerning your relationship. I should confirm whether Mr. Reed can make his own decisions independently."

Before Hale could respond, Adrian spoke.

"Yes. Whether or not I like my employer has nothing to do with whether I sign this form."

Elena looked at him.

"You're finally separating two different questions."

"You taught me this morning."

"I told you to stop talking so much."

Horn appeared baffled by this sudden discussion of conversational technique.

He reopened his case.

"As to the registration date: the Temple activated the old index on September twelfth. Your updated employment information was inserted into the letter later. The full document wasn't completed on the twelfth."

Hale looked up immediately.

"So the text was amended. Who made the amendment, when, and where is the amendment history?"

"I would need to consult the office register."

"Then we'll record your explanation as unverified until that register can be examined," Elena said.

Horn hesitated.

Then nodded.

The meeting might have ended there.

But as he turned the papers in his case, an old, yellow-edged catalogue slip fell onto the table.

Adrian spotted the number instantly.

R-074.

"One moment. What is that?"

Horn snatched it up.

"An unrelated historical index."

"Unrelated? It bears the same case number as a record with my name on it."

The envoy hesitated.

Elena didn't reach for the paper. She simply asked Hale to note the question in the minutes.

"You may decline to show the document," she told Horn. "But please identify its archival category."

After a pause, he answered.

"Historical betrothal supplement."

Adrian nearly dropped his tea.

"Historical *what*?"

Elena turned just as quickly.

Horn lifted a hand.

"There's no need for alarm. The index concerned a historical relationship record that was canceled years ago. A canceled historical record does not establish any valid engagement today."

Hale spoke immediately.

"Who canceled it? On what authority? Who was the other party?"

"I haven't brought the full file."

"Then why did you describe it as unrelated?"

A bead of sweat appeared on Horn's forehead.

"Because it has no present legal force."

"We agree it can't create an engagement now," Adrian said. "But it's linked to R-074. Surely you can explain why it existed and why it was removed."

Horn looked at the unused transfer form.

"Those are questions you could ask at the Temple tomorrow."

It sounded less like an invitation than a door being held open onto a room with no witnesses.

Elena stopped smiling entirely.

"If you'd like us to visit, send the examination scope and document inventory to Hale first. Today's meeting is concluded."

Horn rose.

He looked displeased, but didn't object.

As Martha saw the visitors out, she whispered to Adrian in the corridor.

"How did Her Ladyship's smile practice go?"

Adrian considered.

"She hardly smiled at all."

"Then I'd say it was a success."

"Why?"

Martha glanced toward the closed sitting-room door.

"Because that envoy left looking more nervous than she was when she started rehearsing."

Adrian laughed.

Elena appeared behind him.

"What are you laughing about?"

"At how well you handled that meeting."

"Which part?"

"You didn't force yourself to smile."

She looked at him, then offered him a real smile.

"Then tomorrow, don't force yourself to say yes."

Hale emerged carrying a copy of the unsigned transfer form.

"There is one point we shouldn't overlook. Horn confirmed two things today. The Temple knows R-074, and that case is linked to a canceled historical betrothal record."

"Mine?" Adrian asked.

"We can't establish that from a case number alone. It would be irresponsible to claim that you were ever engaged."

Adrian nodded.

Whatever the old record belonged to, he wasn't going to let it make his choices for him now.

That evening, another Temple note arrived.

It bore Felix Horn's signature, but the wording was harder than before.

*September twenty-eighth: subject transfer and identity verification shall proceed under the Old Records Preservation Ordinance.*

Adrian looked at Elena.

"They're coming back tomorrow."

"I can see that."

"I don't think they're coming for tea this time."

Elena handed the notice to Hale.

"Then find out whether 'accompanied transfer' is a request or an order."

Adrian touched the crescent scar on his left hand.

The possibility of someone inventing an engagement for him was disturbing enough.

Someone using a record he had never seen to decide where he would be taken tomorrow was much worse.
