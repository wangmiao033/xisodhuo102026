# Why Do All the Villainesses Want to Marry Me?
## Volume Two: The Saint Won't Let Me Get Married
### Chapter 45: The Archive Was Locked the Night Before the Hearing

On the morning of October twenty-fifth, Adrian woke half an hour before the estate's clock.

For once, it wasn't because he'd dreamed about an execution platform.

He'd been reading a travel book the night before.

In one chapter, a traveler lost his way in a border valley and found the road home by following the sound of a monastery's evening bell.

As Adrian put the book down, an uncomfortable thought occurred to him.

He'd heard dozens of bells since arriving in the capital.

He'd never once thought to ask what made them ring at particular times.

Martha spotted him coming downstairs with the book in his hand.

"Planning to become a bell-ringer, Mr. Reed?"

"Just studying how sound can help people find their way."

"Then I recommend first learning how to get from your bedroom to breakfast without being late."

"I'm early today!"

"That's precisely why I'm suspicious."

Elena was already dressed for the visit to the Temple. She was checking the preservation applications with Vera and Hale.

Cecilia sat across the table without her investigator's badge, a series of seal references she'd organized the night before in front of her.

"We need to establish three things at minimum," Vera said. "Whether the originals still exist in their recorded locations, who authorized any transfer, and whether an independent reviewer will be allowed to examine them during the hearing."

Adrian looked toward Cecilia.

"Did you sleep?"

"Three hours."

"How is that different from not sleeping?"

"At least I dreamed about Mira drawing suns on the clinic doors again."

The answer was so quiet that Adrian couldn't bring himself to make a joke.

Mira had already submitted a formal statement to the municipal patrol. Jonah remained under proper medical care.

What remained dangerous was the possibility that someone could invoke *preservation* to move all the disputed records beyond scrutiny before Cecilia's hearing.

The Old Contract Records Archive stood behind the Temple's bell tower.

Its walls were white, but its windows were unexpectedly narrow. A maintenance notice had been posted beside the door. Workmen carried fireproof sand and copper water tanks through the courtyard.

There was real dust on their clothes.

"At least the fire inspection isn't completely imaginary," Adrian said.

"Real maintenance doesn't establish that every access restriction is justified," Hale replied. "We should check the two questions separately."

A work supervisor showed them the public schedule.

Ventilation shafts were due to be inspected in the morning. Fire barriers around the underground storage rooms would be checked that afternoon.

The old tower's linked warning bells also needed repairs.

"Will the bells still ring on schedule?" Adrian asked.

"The main ceremonial bell will. That's operated separately. The underground alarm circuit is out of service for a while."

The supervisor pointed toward a faded notice beside the tower.

"Don't touch the ropes in the courtyard. Somebody pulled one yesterday and nearly deafened the maintenance crew."

The notice listed the ordinary ceremonial bell times and the special registration required for additional tolling during memorial services.

Adrian made a mental note.

He hadn't yet realized how useful it might be.

A queue had formed outside the archive.

One visitor wanted to examine his grandfather's contract. Another needed a record of a family contribution to a charitable relief fund.

The guard's answer was identical every time.

"Access stops this afternoon. Wait for an announcement."

An elderly woman asked whether she could still examine a will she'd applied to see earlier in the month.

"Same for everyone," the guard snapped. "Come back later."

Cecilia stopped.

"Not necessarily."

The man recognized her.

"Miss Saintclair, hasn't your authority been suspended?"

"I'm not asking you to open a restricted archive for me. I'm asking whether this woman, as a relative of the depositor, has been informed about the process for obtaining her own authorized record."

Vera submitted the question formally.

Eventually an administrator emerged and distinguished ordinary civil deposits from protected Temple contract files.

The elderly woman received a queue number and an explanation of how to appeal if today's visit couldn't proceed.

Adrian leaned toward Cecilia.

"You seem determined to help a stranger in every building we enter."

"Is that inconvenient?"

"No." He smiled. "I rather like it."

Elena gently drew him to one side.

"If you're finished admiring Cecilia's work, you can carry my files. There are four of them, and they definitely aren't imaginary."

Adrian looked down at the single blank form in his hands.

He obediently took the folder.

At the reception counter, Vera submitted an emergency preservation request covering R-074 and the C-19 revision manuscripts.

She wasn't asking for unrestricted access.

She wanted an independently verifiable account of the originals' location, the seals, their custodians, and any scheduled transfer.

The records officer responded that the material was unavailable during the fire-safety inspection.

"We aren't asking you to open the vault," Vera said. "We need to know which inventory location was current this morning, who is responsible for custody, and whether anything has already been moved."

"Those are internal records."

"Sensitive fields can be withheld where necessary. But if your disciplinary panel intends to rely on those documents, an authorized reviewer must be able to establish whether they exist."

The man leafed through several pages of rules.

At last, he passed her a form entitled:

*Temporary Access Suspension and Custody Location Verification Request.*

Adrian read it.

"That's a spectacularly long name."

"At least the procedure exists," Cecilia said.

They completed the application under the observation of two independent notaries.

An hour passed.

The archive supplied a partial response.

The R-074 original contract file remained listed in Underground Vault B. Its most recent seal registration was dated October thirteenth.

The C-19 prophecy revision materials had been indexed in a pending-evidence slot in Vault A.

But an instruction dated October twenty-fourth had scheduled them for transfer to Vault B as part of centralized maintenance.

"Scheduled?" Hale asked. "Was the transfer completed?"

The records officer lowered his eyes.

"The system shows the arrangement. No completion entry has been added."

"Then we need today's custody state preserved," Vera said. "Otherwise, at tomorrow's hearing, everyone will be arguing over which room the file might have been in."

"Your requests will delay urgent safety work."

"We don't need anyone to interfere with repairs," Elena said. "A notary can verify the entrance seal from outside. We only want a record of custody responsibilities and any changes."

The archive finally permitted the notaries to inspect the seal at the entrance to the underground corridor.

They were not allowed into either vault.

Adrian watched two workmen carry a heavy iron door panel down the stairs.

One of them muttered to the other.

"Yesterday they wanted Vault B opened first. This morning they said no one could touch it. I'd like to know whose orders we're supposed to follow."

"Who ordered it opened yesterday?" Hale asked.

The workman glanced at the administrator.

"I'm a maintenance worker. They tell me to clean a ventilation shaft, I clean it. What's inside isn't my business."

They didn't badger him.

Vera simply asked the archive to preserve the work orders from both shifts.

A second notary compared the publicly listed custody number with the seal on the corridor entrance.

They matched.

"That establishes that the entrance seal is still in the recorded position," he said. "It doesn't prove every document deeper inside is untouched."

Adrian added six words to his draft:

*Vault B interior not independently inspected.*

The records officer looked surprised.

"I thought you were trying to make me sign a statement saying everything was safe."

"Would you sign it?" Adrian asked.

"No. I haven't been inside either."

"Then you shouldn't have to."

Elena smiled behind him.

"You're starting to sound like Hale."

"Then perhaps I should be paid at Hale's rate."

"Come back when you have ten years of experience," the lawyer replied.

Adrian decided his current position was acceptable for now.

At one in the afternoon, the first fire door protecting the underground section closed.

The tower bell sounded at almost the same moment.

Adrian looked up.

A shutter in front of the main ceremonial bell shifted in the breeze. The maintenance supervisor had said the underground alarms were disconnected, but the main bell remained in operation.

"Watching the bells again?" Elena asked.

"I keep thinking about that royal evidence case with a sealing date from the following day. If somebody records a transfer at a time that couldn't have happened, perhaps the bells might help prove it."

"People mishear bells," she reminded him.

"Then we'll need their operation register."

She didn't laugh at his obsession.

She simply squeezed his hand.

"Let's get through tomorrow's hearing first."

At three, the archive closed all old contract records to outside readers.

They still hadn't seen the originals.

Cecilia stood before the rows of locked doors.

"I used to believe that sealing something meant keeping it safe."

She looked at the new lock.

"Today, I'm not sure what the seal is protecting."

Adrian didn't announce that somebody was obviously preparing to destroy evidence.

The repairs were real.

The workers' schedules could be checked.

But the Temple had also imposed an additional custody restriction through Internal Oversight.

Nobody had adequately explained how the two instructions fit together.

Vera prepared a notice for the October twenty-sixth hearing.

Essential source materials remained unavailable for independent examination. The panel should not draw an adverse inference against Cecilia from the absence of evidence held by the very office bringing the charge.

Cecilia signed the request.

She made no attempt to flee back to the Rosenberg estate or persuade someone else to take the blame.

"I'll attend tomorrow," she said.

On the way back, Adrian examined the stamped custody extract.

A handwritten note appeared at the bottom.

*"Vault B receipt: after the fifth evening bell."*

There was no date attached.

Only the reference to a bell.

"What exactly is the fifth evening bell?" he asked.

"Possibly an obsolete time notation," Hale said. "Or a shorthand used by the clerk on duty."

"The workmen said the ceremonial bell runs to a schedule. If there's no fifth regular evening toll, we'd need the special-service register."

Elena studied the handwritten entry.

"Don't decide it's forged yet. Let the notary confirm the note is on today's copy, then ask the tower's custodians how many times the bells actually rang."

Adrian nodded.

The notation was strange.

Strange wasn't the same as false.

Back at the estate, Elena returned Adrian's travel book.

"Where did you get to?"

"Page twenty-seven. The traveler finds a village by listening for its bells."

"When we don't have a court deadline hanging over us, perhaps we could visit that valley."

Adrian looked at the little illustration of a stone bridge.

"You remember our plan?"

"Of course. I like you, Adrian. Not because filing legal documents with you is fun."

He laughed.

"Because it isn't?"

"Not particularly."

"Then what else could we do?"

"Walk. Eat pastries. Look at maps." She considered. "Perhaps spend a day doing absolutely nothing."

"That sounds like an extravagance only a duke's daughter can afford."

"Then you'd better stay alive long enough to enjoy it."

He took her hand.

For a long time, Adrian had imagined his future in this world as a sequence of dangers to survive.

Now, for the first time, he pictured a mountain path he hadn't yet walked.

Not a judgment waiting to be executed.

That night, Cecilia sat alone in the estate's little chapel for quite a while.

Elena knocked and waited for permission before entering.

"Afraid of tomorrow?"

"Yes." Cecilia looked toward the unlit altar. "I'm afraid the people who cared for me may have allowed the Temple to hurt patients. I'm afraid of what I might learn."

"You don't have to decide tonight whether you still believe in the gods," Elena said, taking a seat beside her. "Tomorrow you're being asked to account for what you did, and what you didn't do."

Cecilia looked up.

"Will you come with me?"

"If you want me there."

"I do."

They sat quietly together.

Far away, an evening bell rang.

Adrian stood in the corridor rather than interrupting them.

He was holding the custody extract with the note about the fifth evening bell.

Then he heard a short, unexpected echo.

Not part of the ordinary sequence of tolls.

He stopped.

His scar didn't glow.

No lost memory suddenly flooded back.

He simply remembered that the supervisor had said the underground alarm was out of service.

And the extra note sounded as though it had come from the direction of the old courthouse tower.

Adrian took out his notebook.

He wrote down the time.

**October twenty-fifth. 8:17 p.m.**

He didn't label it a prophecy.

He wrote:

*Possible additional bell. Verify tower duty roster and maintenance log.*

Tomorrow, before the hearing, there would be one fact he might be able to check without setting foot inside a restricted archive.
