# Chapter 174: Supporters Must Leave Their Names Too

Isabella laid a borrowed brooch on the table.

On February 5, an attendant had brought formal outerwear with a fastening shaped like the coordination badge she'd already surrendered. Not the badge itself, but close enough to mislead at a distance.

“Clothes can be borrowed. An office cannot.”

The attendant apologized. She requested an ordinary pin, then looked at Adrian.

“You're preparing to laugh.”

“I wondered whether you used to find that resemblance convenient.”

“Extremely.”

Her honesty briefly silenced him. After several failed attempts to fasten the collar with her right hand, she set the coat down.

“Help, please? I'd rather not become a pincushion to preserve my dignity.”

Adrian fastened it flat on the table and returned it. She regarded the neat collar with disappointment.

“You've discovered an entirely unromantic method.”

“Very safe.”

“I didn't object to safety.”

Smiling, she put it on, leaving room around her injured left wrist. Today she would seek an open vote, not borrow the appearance of authority.

Baroness Merriwell waited in the council's side room with two texts. Isabella expected her to choose the shorter one. Instead she returned it.

“This merely asks everyone to support your principles publicly. Opponents will ask what can actually be done tomorrow.”

Safeguard everyone's autonomy suddenly resembled a flag large enough to hide an unfinished road. Adrian didn't rescue the princess from the question. He too wanted to know where Beth could submit what afterward.

The longer text proposed a limited trial of ordinary intake, separate objection receipts, and explanations concerning relationship choices. Today's request concerned public discussion and a recorded vote, with practical conditions and reasons retained. Spectators wouldn't vote; applause wouldn't count as a member's decision.

Merriwell would introduce a procedural motion from her own seat if they removed a sentence treating every technical dispute as a delaying tactic.

“Luna wouldn't sign that,” Adrian said.

“I shouldn't have written it.” Isabella took her pen.

She deleted it, retaining the specific concern about excessive private information requested in safety's name. Merriwell agreed to introduce the revision. Passage wasn't guaranteed, nor would passage ensure diligent implementation.

Before they entered, a supporter offered an alternative. Special handling for the six named people could be negotiated privately, avoiding another public disagreement. Their documents might even be expedited without reopening the old demand for a compatibility partner.

Isabella paused. It would help her and Adrian. Looking brave didn't entitle her to refuse on his behalf.

She asked him.

“I won't withdraw my existing individual check. But today's intake problem concerns more than six people. The text should still make sense without my name.”

Her heading did say six affected persons. She changed it to ordinary intake procedures without quietly adding this case only.

“I won't decide for everyone else using your waiting time.”

“Nor promise that I'll wait forever using their circumstances.”

She nodded. Merriwell added that individual verification could continue in parallel. Nobody needed to sacrifice every personal application before requesting an ordinary route.

Inside, members were arguing over road expenditure. Chairs scraped; clerks checked the next papers. Isabella's ordinary pin gave her no additional vote. She took the designated speaking seat.

The first member to oppose the motion wanted to know why a vote had to carry names. They could count raised hands perfectly well, he said. Publishing the names afterward would make council members targets for insults from people in the street.

Merriwell replied that their private addresses formed no part of the proposed publication. But a decision made on a public agenda should not disappear into an unsigned summary. Those who supported the measure would be recorded alongside those who opposed it. This was not a plan to select opponents and parade them through the streets.

“Will you explain where the money comes from?”

“Yes. If funding isn't arranged, don't say it is.”

Adrian heard Isabella exhale. She'd wanted opponents' reasons exposed. Supporters would face questions too. Uncomfortable, but closer to her request than opening windows only on her enemies.

The representative from the registration office laid a staffing sheet on the table. The temporary counter had its existing workers, he said. Changing the title of a procedure would not produce twice as many people to carry it out. Separate acknowledgments meant additional lines to write, and the visitors bringing supplementary documents would not become fewer merely because those lines were clearer.

Allowed to respond, Isabella asked which fields already existed, rather than proposing an invisible new department. Date, submitter, and original reference were present. Actual material received and reasons for incomplete handling were missing.

“Start with those two.”

“Writing still takes time.”

“Then specify receiving hours. Lack of time to receive a page cannot mean the matter is fully concluded.”

The representative frowned. Nevertheless, he acknowledged that this was different from the wholesale reconstruction his office had feared. There were still questions about the cost of copies and the staff needed for reviews. Those belonged in the substantive discussion to follow. Nobody could make them disappear simply by saying that they should try it.

The chair told Isabella not to turn the procedural motion into a full defense of the proposal. She stopped. Permission to speak wasn't permission to continue until everyone agreed.

Adrian described only the differently scoped acknowledgments he'd received. An opponent mentioned the repaired reader. Had it proved all historical relationships invalid?

“It can't do that.”

Someone behind him sounded disappointed.

He explained precisely what had been repaired: the external mirror mount, which now made it easier to distinguish two layers of impressions on one authorized comparison piece. That did not establish which text was true or false. It certainly did not give a wooden instrument the authority to decide what relationship an ordinary applicant must enter. Obtaining the image hadn't required bodily contact. Only the permitted public portion was supplied here.

“You've brought an unresolved question.”

“I've brought the reason it shouldn't be recorded as resolved.”

Isabella didn't add that he'd actually won. Some supporters looked displeased. She let the sentence remain.

In the corridor during the break, she noticed his crooked neckcloth. Her right hand stopped halfway toward it.

“You fastened my pin. May I straighten that?”

“Yes. Not as an exchange.”

“I know. No obligation next time.”

She adjusted it carefully. This close, his expression wasn't something she could read for the next bargaining condition. Adrian didn't retreat or lift his face toward a kiss. He quietly asked whether it still hurt.

“My left wrist? A little. I've hardly moved it.”

“I meant supporters leaving their names.”

She looked up, then smiled with difficulty.

“More than my wrist. I planned how to make others own their positions. I hadn't considered that my supporters might dislike me for it.”

“They still can.”

“Can't you say something pretty?”

He considered, then complimented her pin. Looking down at the utterly ordinary fastening, she finally laughed. The sound echoed briefly in the stone corridor.

Back inside, someone proposed publishing only a later summary, not individual reasons. Merriwell objected. The previous rejection summary had forced a separate application for the full explanation. Majority opposed wouldn't tell people whether funds or documents were missing, or whether members opposed the choice itself.

Must every member compose an essay? The chair suggested concise reasons linked to formal statements and their access arrangements. Private identities would be protected; medical details weren't for publication.

They argued about format for some time. Isabella didn't call every shortening betrayal, nor accept every deletion because a compromise finally existed. When one revision replaced an individual's independent objection with a family representative's application, she identified a change in substance, not layout.

Merriwell insisted on retaining the original. The revision was withdrawn. Voting began.

The clerk called names, recording support, opposition, and abstention separately. Adrian heard someone who'd previously only nodded from a corner oppose the motion aloud. Isabella's shoulders tightened. She didn't turn to catch his eye.

The count was checked twice. The procedural motion passed.

That authorized public consideration of the limited trial that afternoon, with a recorded vote and reasons. It didn't reverse January 30's rejection automatically. The kingdom's laws hadn't become their preferred version before lunch.

Taking the confirmed agenda, Isabella's hands were steady. Her stomach growled instead.

Adrian pretended to inspect the clock. She glared, then admitted eating half a slice of bread that morning.

“You all criticized my half an egg.”

“I understand your circumstances better. I don't withdraw the criticism.”

They divided a hot flatbread in the corridor. She couldn't break the hard edge, asked for help, then kept the larger half. Supporters didn't need a demonstration of her consideration at hunger's expense.

A spectator congratulated them on being able to announce their shared engagement that afternoon. Isabella swallowed and corrected him. There was no such item on the agenda, she told him carefully. The discussion concerned how applications would be received and objections recorded, not the announcement he had expected to hear.

“But all five ladies are coming.”

“Some are submitting written views. Even if all five sat here, that wouldn't divide one person into five portions.”

Still disappointed, he departed with the correct title. She couldn't decide whether he believed it.

Merriwell emerged and asked whether Isabella was prepared for her opposing the substantive proposal.

The princess stopped with bread in her hand.

“You supported public discussion.”

“Yes. Not every sentence you've written. Responsibility for review costs remains unclear.”

For a moment Isabella thought the warning could hardly have come at a worse time. They had scarcely won the opportunity to be heard. Then she recognized that the freedom Merriwell was exercising was exactly the one she had spent the morning demanding. She put the last bite of bread back on its paper and asked, seriously, which provision worried the baroness.

Existing staff might rearrange shifts, the baroness explained. Money for additional copies couldn't grow from an unclaimed corner. Adrian found the passage. They'd prohibited charging applicants the original intake fee again for supplements but hadn't explained newly incurred costs.

The three didn't invent an appropriation in the corridor. They marked the unresolved point. That afternoon they would ask the responsible official whether existing forms and available copies could support the initial trial without promising services beyond current resources.

Merriwell nodded and went to her own lunch. She left no guarantee of unwavering support. Isabella placed the amended page on top.

Afternoon papers arrived through the usual contact. Luna supplied technical comments; Viola separately addressed voluntary attendance and travel protection. Neither woman had forced herself out of the house to prove that they stood together. Their separately written opinions could be heard without requiring their injured bodies to occupy matching seats. Elena and Cecilia checked the order of their own statements. This time they began by calculating how many minutes their actual points required.

Isabella removed the ordinary pin and secured her loosened collar again.

“This afternoon other people get to pick at my wording.”

Adrian spread out his own paper.

“Mine too.”

She looked at the two texts side by side. At last she stopped trying to press them into an identical sheet.
