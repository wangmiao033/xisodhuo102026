# Why Do All the Villainesses Want to Marry Me?
## Volume One: A Betrothal Written in Blood
### Chapter 16: An Invitation from the Temple of Fate

On the morning of September twenty-sixth, Adrian Reed received three letters.

The first was from the stationer on South Street, requesting payment for the extra paper they'd used copying his accounts.

The second was from Saint Rose Academy, explaining that full access to the discontinued case R-074 still required approval from the old judicial archives. A number on the back of a portrait was not, apparently, an acceptable reading permit.

The third was different.

Its envelope was pure white, edged with fine gold, and sealed with a five-petaled flower.

Martha lined them up beside Adrian's breakfast.

"You're becoming quite popular, Mr. Reed."

"The first two want money." He picked up the white envelope. "I'm hoping the third isn't a tax bill."

Elena had just entered the room.

The moment she saw the seal, she stopped.

"Don't open that yet."

Adrian put it down immediately.

"Is it cursed?"

"If it is, your mouth is the first thing I'd expect it to curse."

She asked Martha for a clean silver tray and examined the wax without touching it.

"An official seal of the Temple of Fate."

"Same Temple that sealed the evidence from Wynn's accident?"

"The same institution. Not necessarily the same person. That was an older evidence-sealing stamp. This is their correspondence seal."

Adrian nodded.

Since September seventeenth, one of the most important things he'd learned was that two blue boxes did not necessarily contain evidence of the same murder.

Still, the name *Temple of Fate* made him uneasy.

In *Crown of Fate*, the Temple handled engagement ceremonies, blessings for newborns, and records concerning fate. Players could pick up quests in the outer sanctuary, but almost never reached the archives behind it.

Adrian remembered a minor side quest where a priest refused to help a sick man.

*Fate has been decided.*

At the time, he'd thought the writers had simply given up.

It would be considerably less funny if those words appeared beside his own name.

"Does Father know?" Elena asked.

"He has been informed," Martha said. "Mr. Hale is on his way."

Adrian sighed.

"We need a lawyer to open the mail now?"

"Your circumstances are unusual."

"Because of the ball?"

"Because you challenged royal evidence in open court."

"...I'd rather it were the ball."

Elena raised an eyebrow.

"You could repeat that in front of Father."

"Suddenly I'm quite content not to."

When Hale arrived, he had Martha and the head steward record the envelope's condition and delivery time. Then he slit the edge carefully enough to preserve the seal.

The paper was thick as light card, with a golden sunburst printed at the top.

The Temple's Old Contract Records Office requested that House Rosenberg provide identification records for its newly appointed administrative aide, Adrian Reed, along with proof of origin and any materials relevant to verifying his fate record.

"Proof of origin?" Adrian repeated. "Do they think I'm a bottle of milk that just arrived from the dairy?"

Martha couldn't help laughing.

Hale continued reading.

"The individual is required to attend the Temple by September twenty-eighth with a signed authorization for record verification."

Elena set down her teacup.

"The heading calls it an invitation to assist with verification. Why does the body say 'required'?"

"That's precisely the problem." Hale indicated the footnote. "They haven't attached a judicial order authorizing compulsory access. Yet they've worded a voluntary request as though it were an obligation."

"What if I refuse?" Adrian asked.

"A voluntary archival review doesn't become grounds for detention merely because someone declines. If the Temple claims coercive authority, it must identify the law and seek the appropriate authorization."

Elena read the letter again.

"Father can decide whether to release ordinary employment records. The rest belong to Adrian. We can't consent on his behalf."

Hale nodded.

Adrian was tempted to explain that he didn't even know where his birth certificate would exist in this world.

But with servants and stewards present, he had no interest in making his origins the topic of the morning.

Elena didn't ask.

Instead, she moved the letter toward him.

"Do you want to be examined?"

"Do I have to decide right now?"

"You can decide not to hand anything over before you know what they're asking for."

Adrian thought of the seven-year-old signature in the academy records.

When he was hunting answers, he wanted every drawer opened.

When strangers demanded access to his body and personal records, he suddenly felt like a piece of evidence someone planned to catalogue.

"I'd be willing to examine their records and have an independent witness verify the documents," he said. "But I'm not signing a blank authorization. And I'm certainly not letting strangers take samples from me without explaining why."

"Then we'll put that in the reply."

Hale wrote carefully. The household could provide public employment details. Personal information would require Adrian's separate consent. Any test involving magical material had to disclose its method, risks, purpose, and how long samples would be kept.

"One more thing," Adrian said. "If they produce something from seven years ago with my name on it, they can't assume I signed it just because the name matches."

Elena knew he was thinking of the academy ledger.

"Certainly," Hale said. "We can require the original document, its date of creation, and its custody history."

Adrian took the pen himself.

Elena looked pleased.

"I didn't even have to remind you."

"I'm earning three times a footman's wages now. I should at least try to be worth the money."

"Three times a *footman's* wages."

"Must you keep stressing that?"

She laughed.

Hale set down the draft reply.

"Anything else?"

Adrian looked at his left hand.

"Does this fate verification involve blood?"

"There are several methods. Some only compare documents. Others require a medium carrying the subject's magical signature. The letter doesn't specify, so we shouldn't guess."

"Then let's ask them to specify."

"And what happens to samples afterward," Elena added.

Adrian turned toward her.

"You thought of that?"

"They switched a jewelry case. If someone takes a vial of your blood, how readily could you demand it back?"

He went quiet.

In the academy courtyard, she'd asked permission before examining his scar.

At the time, he hadn't realized how much easier it was to protect someone than to respect their choices.

Then he made an effort to lighten the mood.

"If they insist on taking my hair, can we negotiate a price? I'm barely earning enough to pay for this coat."

"You're planning to sell your hair to the Temple?"

"A negotiating tactic."

"Then I suppose Hale could attach a price list."

Hale looked between them.

"I strongly advise against that."

When Martha came to refill the teapot, Adrian noticed another name on the distribution list.

**Cecilia Saintclair.**

"Who's this?"

"One of the Temple's youngest saints," Elena said. "She's assisted with historical record reviews. But being copied on a letter doesn't mean she personally approved it."

"A saint?"

Adrian remembered a character card from *Crown of Fate*.

The illustration had hidden half the girl's face behind the light of the Temple sanctuary. Her storyline wasn't fully available until much later. He knew the name, but hardly enough to judge the person.

"Will she come here?"

"The letter doesn't say."

Elena took the paper from him.

"You seem interested."

"She might have worked with the old files."

"Only that?"

"...Her name's easy to remember."

Hale produced the world's most deliberate cough.

Elena ignored him.

"If you meet her, you should introduce yourself by telling her how passionate you are about the dates on official correspondence."

"Why would I do that?"

"So your investigation doesn't turn into a marriage interview."

"I haven't even met her!"

"Then that's settled."

Adrian was about to protest when Hale turned over the letter.

"Hold on."

Attached to the reverse was a formal dispatch register: office, case number, transit route, and filing date.

Hale tapped the final field.

"September twelfth."

"What's wrong with Septem—"

Adrian stopped.

He'd woken up in this world on September seventeenth.

If the registry date was genuine, someone had begun preparing to investigate his name five days before his arrival.

"Perhaps the date is wrong," Elena suggested.

"Or the office registered a blank form early," Hale added. "The dispatch number is consistent with the adjacent entries, but that doesn't prove the body text existed on the twelfth."

Adrian spotted something worse.

"The letter calls me a newly appointed administrative aide."

Martha looked up.

"You weren't appointed until September twenty-first."

"So even if the registration date is genuine," Hale said, "the text may have been filled in afterward."

"Can you tell from the ink?"

"Not without proper examination."

Hale laid out the envelope, letter, and register attachment separately. Martha consulted the household's incoming-mail ledger and the postal marks.

September twelfth: headquarters assigned a dispatch number.

September twenty-fourth: the capital delivery station received the envelope.

September twenty-sixth: delivered to House Rosenberg.

"Twelve days missing," Adrian said.

"It could have been held for routine reasons—or under a specific instruction. We'll request the delivery station's signed transfer record."

Elena examined a corner of the envelope.

"The courier used our old servants' roster. It doesn't mention your promotion."

"So the person delivering it didn't know I had a new job." Adrian almost laughed. "I guess they don't know everything."

"At least the delivery stage used an outdated roster," Hale corrected. "The body uses your new title. Those details may have entered the process at different times."

Adrian set the pages side by side.

Rather than declaring the Temple capable of predicting the future, they needed to find out who had assembled this letter and when.

But one sentence refused to leave him alone.

*Request for verification of the fate record of Adrian Reed, listed under old case R-074.*

R-074.

They'd only read that number from the back of the portrait yesterday.

The Temple knew it already.

"Do you think they have the old case file?" Adrian asked.

"Only that they have a way to learn its number," Hale replied. "The original case may still be in the judicial archives."

"Then we'll ask where they got the reference," Elena said.

Adrian returned the letter to its tray.

"And if they really do have proof that I went before the old court seven years ago?"

"First we establish whether it's genuine."

"And if it is?"

"Then we work out why it's there."

She didn't promise that everything would be fine.

For some reason, that helped him breathe.

Later in the afternoon, a Temple courier returned.

He didn't request entry to the house, merely delivered a note bearing the same five-petaled seal. A records envoy would visit on September twenty-seventh to explain the proposed verification process.

Martha recorded the courier's name and the delivery time, giving him an official receipt.

Adrian studied the note.

"Do you think they'll bring guards?"

"If they do, we'll ask to see their authority before anything else."

Elena handed him Hale's prepared reply.

"There's one decision still missing."

He looked down.

The signature line at the bottom didn't belong to the duke.

It didn't belong to Elena.

It belonged to Adrian.

He signed, consenting only to discuss the procedure with counsel present—not to surrender personal samples or submit to an unspecified examination.

For the first time since arriving here, he felt as though he was choosing his next step instead of waiting for someone else's summons.

"My lady?"

"Yes?"

"If they ask tomorrow whether you've already agreed to hand me over—"

"I'll tell them," Elena said, "that you're not a piece of Rosenberg property."

Beyond the windows, the courier's carriage rolled out of the grounds.

The September twelfth dispatch record lay on the silver tray.

Adrian didn't know who had entered his name before his arrival.

But this time, the next signature would be his choice.
