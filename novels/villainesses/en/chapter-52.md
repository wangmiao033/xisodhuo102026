# Why Do All the Villainesses Want to Marry Me?
## Volume Two: The Saint Won't Let Me Get Married
### Chapter 52: The Betrothal Had Already Lapsed Seven Years Ago

On the morning of November first, Adrian received a genuinely pleasant document in the Rosenberg estate's entrance hall.

It contained no ominous dates, no provisions for sharing risk, and no instruction that *the prospective husband must attend immediately*.

Just one sentence:

*Application to verify the status of historical betrothal R-074 approved. Access to the original archival transfer copy authorized.*

He turned it over and read it twice.

"Does this mean I can finally prove I've never been married?"

Hale was coming down the stairs.

"The civil registry already confirms that you have no valid betrothal now. Today's question is what status the old record held under the system that produced it."

"So another entire day of reading documents?"

"Correct."

Adrian sighed. In this kingdom, romance caused more misunderstandings than taxes, and clearing them up took more paperwork than a tax return.

Cecilia wasn't wearing her formal Temple robes. She carried an approved application to inspect her personal records. An independent custodian and the document examiner, Ms. Carter, accompanied them.

Walking beside Cecilia, Elena asked, "Would you be disappointed if it really said both parties had freely agreed?"

"Yes," Cecilia answered frankly. "I don't remember any of it. Finding my name attached to something like that is unsettling. But whatever we find, I won't ask Mr. Reed to accept an obligation today on the strength of an old document."

"Neither will I."

They exchanged a smile. Adrian began to suspect this might be the most peaceful investigation of an ancient betrothal he'd attended since arriving in the capital.

Fire-safety repairs at the Old Contract Records Archive were still unfinished. An independent reviewer had nevertheless authorized a controlled inspection in a designated safe reading room. Two custodians would present R-074's transfer file, checking it against the seal numbers. The originals would remain within the archive's jurisdiction, and nobody could uncover another person's protected identity fields without authorization.

Adrian's nerves returned when the cabinet was wheeled in.

The outer seal on the wooden case still bore the October thirteenth mark. Protective labels added during the recent repairs had been placed beside it. Carter found no evidence of an unrecorded opening, but warned that this said nothing conclusive about interference earlier in the file's history.

"We're verifying the current chain of custody," she said. "Nothing more."

A custodian opened the section they were permitted to inspect. Inside lay the historical betrothal cover, the incomplete confirmation fields for the two parties, and a transfer summary from seven years earlier.

The gray-gold stamp obscured a field at the lower right.

Carter adjusted the angle of the light passing through the paper. She used no fate magic. Gradually, the underlying words became legible.

*Term expired. Independent confirmation by both signatories incomplete. Status: lapsed.*

Adrian stared.

"That's it?"

"That's what it says," Carter replied. "We still have to check the date and authority of the lapse stamp."

Cecilia leaned closer.

"Then even if someone intended to establish a betrothal between the two parties, the required confirmation process was never completed?"

"That's what this file records," the custodian said. "It doesn't authenticate the signatures. It doesn't prove you never met in the past. It tells us that this registration was subsequently marked lapsed under the old system."

Hale produced the provisions governing that system. A betrothal in the historical Calamity Assistance category lost its eligibility to remain registered as a betrothal unless both parties signed independently within the specified verification period. Any transfer of risk required separate, informed consent from the person concerned. Keeping an old file in storage couldn't restore an expired obligation.

"And current law expressly rejects these historical registrations as a basis for binding adults to marriage today," he added.

Adrian finally smiled.

"So even 'it might have been valid once' isn't a reason for us to get married now."

"Correct," Cecilia said.

Elena smiled, too. "Perhaps you can finally stop waiting for a priest to authorize your love life."

At the reviewer's request, Carter compared the lapse stamp with publicly available samples from the same set of sealing tools used seven years earlier. A small worn patch on the stamp's rim matched the position described in a repair record from that period. The ink had penetrated the paper fibers rather than sitting on the surface like a fresh addition.

"Does that prove it was stamped seven years ago?" Adrian asked.

"It makes that period more credible. Wear alone can't establish the exact day. We have to assess it alongside the transfer register and the custodians' handover records."

The archive's public index supplied another detail. Someone had attempted to notify the original applicants that the registration had lapsed.

The outcome field read *Unable to contact*.

Adrian's relief faltered. "Why couldn't they be contacted?"

"This entry doesn't say," the custodian answered. "We can't supply a reason that isn't recorded."

"Then not everyone necessarily knew it had lapsed," Cecilia said softly. "Someone might have gone on believing they were obliged to fulfill it."

"All the more reason to state its status clearly in today's certified acknowledgment," Elena said.

She didn't turn the discovery into a victory over Cecilia. Instead, she requested that the results of both parties' current civil-registry searches be presented alongside the historical lapse certificate. No future clerk should be able to extract two names from a cover sheet and quietly omit the word beneath them: *lapsed*.

By afternoon, the three were still in the reading room, discussing what the document had meant by *betrothal* in the first place.

Cecilia found a passage in an old legal textbook her teacher had preserved. At one time, calamity-sharing vows and betrothals had been combined on a single sheet because families were considered more likely to accept responsibility for one another.

"But that assumes they're willing," she said. "Later laws abolished the practice and required a separate signature for sharing risk. Affection shouldn't be used to pressure someone into accepting an obligation."

"What if they really love each other?" Adrian asked.

"It should still be separate. Loving someone and agreeing to bear a loss are different decisions. You need to understand what you're agreeing to."

Elena nodded. "Courting Adrian doesn't give me rights to his wages, his schedule, or the use of the scar on his hand."

"Especially the scar," Adrian said at once.

Carter overheard them from the other side of the table and laughed.

Cecilia carefully summarized the discussion in her notes. At the bottom, she added: *Examples concerning private relationships are not to be included in the public report.*

Adrian approved of that addition.

Carter completed her comparison of the stamp impressions and transfer records. Taken together, the records established that the lapsed status predated the judicial transfer seven years earlier. It wasn't something added in fresh ink to accommodate today's investigation.

The earlier signing process remained a separate matter for independent examination. These findings did not establish who had written the two names in such unusually similar handwriting on the original document.

"Something still doesn't make sense," Adrian said.

He pointed to an inserted sheet. After the betrothal had lapsed, R-074 had continued moving between departments. It had even appeared in old judicial files and the Temple's risk lists.

"If the betrothal was over, why keep following those two names?"

The custodian said nothing.

Cecilia spared him the need to guess.

"Someone may have used the expired betrothal record as a reference in another risk system. But the number of transfers alone doesn't establish deliberate wrongdoing."

Hale added that question to the next independent inquiry. Carter issued a certified opinion on what they had actually established: the betrothal's status. She made no attempt to explain the deeper mechanism behind the continued transfers.

When they returned to the estate, Martha was carrying a tray of freshly baked apple pastries.

"How did it go?"

Adrian held up the acknowledgment.

"The historical archives didn't marry me off today!"

Martha looked at him. "They hadn't married you off yesterday, Mr. Reed."

"Could you let me enjoy this for a few minutes?"

"Certainly. Another pastry?"

He sat down immediately.

Cecilia gave Vera a certified copy to preserve independently, a safeguard against future misuse of the old record.

"When I first saw that betrothal, I wanted to know whether I'd once chosen Mr. Reed," she said. "At least I know now that the paper can't make demands on anyone for me."

Elena poured her some tea. "What do you most want to do next?"

"Continue looking into the medical relief records. And ask my teacher whether she knows why this lapsed registration was kept for so long."

Adrian couldn't help himself.

"You're not going to lose interest in studying me just because the betrothal has expired, are you?"

Cecilia looked up. After a moment's consideration, she answered with complete seriousness.

"The Adrian I know gets frightened, buys the wrong bread, and finds doctors for patients he's never met. None of that depends on whether a contract is valid."

This time, his ears went red.

Elena set down her teaspoon slowly.

"Miss Saintclair. You've learned to be very direct."

"He taught me."

Adrian concentrated on his pastry. Somehow this was more dangerous than any questioning he'd faced in court.

That evening, Temple Internal Oversight issued a notice. The original disciplinary case was closed. To prevent the R-074 and C-19 materials from improperly influencing one another, a new inspector who had taken no part in the previous investigation would coordinate external verification of the relevant records.

"Someone new?" Adrian asked.

Vera read the notice.

"That isn't necessarily bad. Once the reviewers whose involvement was disputed have recused themselves, an outside reviewer can rebuild the evidence inventory. It may be a fairer process."

"They could also be harder to deal with."

"Fair rules aren't always easy to deal with," Elena said.

Cecilia nodded. "As long as we're allowed to see the rules, I'm willing to cooperate."

After dinner, Elena put the travel book between herself and Adrian.

"Now that we've settled part of the betrothal question, could you stop staring at copies of your own name every night before bed?"

"I'll try."

"We said we'd visit that border valley when we didn't have to go to court every day."

Adrian looked at a little bridge on the map.

"I still don't know how long I'll be able to stay in this world."

"Neither do I. But nobody's asking us to sign an itinerary for the next ten years tonight."

He took her hand.

"Then let's go out of the city on our next day off. If it's dangerous when the day comes, we'll decide together whether to postpone."

Elena smiled. "That's the kind of promise I wanted."

From the doorway, Martha couldn't resist asking, "No official stamp?"

Adrian shook his head firmly.

"Please let me have one thing in my life that doesn't need a stamp."

Late that night, Hale checked the publicly released catalog status of the old case again.

After R-074 had been marked *betrothal lapsed*, the separate risk index linked to it had continued to circulate. Maintenance charges and applications for transfer equipment had kept appearing.

"The lapse didn't bring an end to every problem," he said.

Adrian nodded. "But it does establish that nobody can use the betrothal to make anyone participate in that risk procedure."

"Exactly. Now we need to establish whether the separate procedure has valid consent documents of its own."

He passed the outstanding document references to Vera and asked her to determine whether applications should go through the courts, the Temple, or the municipal authorities.

Sometimes solving one mystery revealed another door. At least this time, Adrian knew they ought to establish who held the key before anyone went through it.

He put away the most important document they'd obtained that day: the certificate of lapse.

The historical betrothal had finally been confirmed to have no force that could continue into the present.

Yet why did that expired piece of paper still seem connected to a death registration from seven years ago and the risk ritual arranged for yesterday?

The end of the betrothal hadn't answered that question.
