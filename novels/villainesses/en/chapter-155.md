# Chapter 155: She Can't Calculate Who Likes Whom More

Luna asked Adrian to arrange three bowls in a row.

“Empty?”

“If you want soup, wait until I've finished.”

On the morning of January twenty-sixth, her right palm still rested on its cushion. Her left hand held a short pencil. No glowing symbols decorated the table: authorized copies of old load tables, ordinary notebooks, wooden pieces, and dried beans borrowed from the kitchen.

Elena arrived to find him placing beans in the first bowl.

“New breakfast requirements?”

“I haven't established whether I'm the cook or the subject.”

“The person moving bowls,” Luna said. “The position requires no measurement of your heartbeat.”

Smiling, she turned over a label reading load-bearing subject and wrote bowl. Adrian visibly relaxed.

The review office wanted to distinguish ordinary voluntary accompaniment from a contract capable of transmitting fate-risk. Could existing technical records also establish that one particular person was safest?

Luna didn't call accompaniment risk-free. People could slip, arrive late, or judge badly. An ordinary invitation simply didn't automatically make those events an operative magical pathway.

Adrian positioned wooden pieces at different heights between the bowls.

“An illustration only,” she said. “Beans aren't magical energy, and bowls aren't any of you.”

“Thank you. I was afraid I'd need washing afterward.”

Elena gathered beans approaching the table edge, suppressing laughter.

Luna held the copy flat with her left hand so both could see the notes rather than merely watching the beans. The old table described a load under specified conditions, not every unknown side path. Its units, equipment limits, and reverse-side notes belonged with the figures. Reading only the maximum could make dangerous claims sound precise.

Luna asked him to place six beans in one bowl, then distribute them between two.

“Each has less,” he said.

“And the total?”

“Unchanged.”

“At minimum, that model doesn't support disappearance. Nor can this illustration guarantee every path in a real device.”

Elena pointed to two sets of conditions. Why not choose the lower result?

“One input wasn't measured. Unknown isn't zero. Treating it as zero gives a beautiful answer.”

Her voice sharpened. She glanced at her bandaged palm. Beautiful answers had once made the next step seem only one further test away. Now she had to rewrite them less attractively with her left hand.

Adrian didn't ask whether injury had made her cautious. He retrieved a bean from beneath the notebook.

“We were missing one.”

“Actual counting is more useful than an elegant conclusion.”

A note arrived from technical liaison. Having read the public findings, its writer requested a compatibility ranking of the six people before the hearing, for the chair's understanding.

“They want you to rank us,” Elena said darkly.

Luna read the qualifications first. Risk assessment only, not a marriage recommendation. That didn't fix the problem, but she couldn't honestly accuse the writer of expressly requesting a romantic league table.

She wrote two lines, then stopped.

“I can say the data are insufficient. I also need to explain why.”

Adrian moved the second bowl closer. Instead of shifting beans, she asked him to remove the names beneath the bowls.

“I didn't put any there.”

“Good. Don't.”

Her attempted smile barely formed. A historical connection didn't establish identical present physical conditions. A visible imprint didn't disclose total load or stopping conditions. Nor did someone's name in an old contract prove that person understood and willingly accepted it then.

“What if we offer more information?” Elena asked.

“Which information would you offer?”

Elena couldn't answer. Eager to escape other people's assigned boxes, she had nearly made more mean permission to hand over everything.

“Define the question first. Wanting to rebut them doesn't let me promise another round of bodily examinations for everyone.”

Elena nodded without treating withdrawal as cowardice. Her first impulse had been to offer whatever would keep the proposed table from turning them into empty spaces waiting to be assigned. Luna had made her notice how little that offer actually specified. No one at the table could tell another person which private record she meant by more.

Adrian noticed a crossed-out ring in the other notebook.

Luna opened it. Earlier, she had connected the third bowl back to the first and assumed the returning amount would equal the initial amount. Then she realized the table supplied no return measurement under matching conditions. She had completed a pleasing line on paper and mistaken her own addition for the apparatus's structure.

“Not a newly discovered third route?”

“Only my strong desire for one.”

She showed Elena the withdrawn line. Its reason remained beside it. This was a record of a mistake she herself had made that morning, not a newly recovered diagram from the old apparatus. She didn't tear it out before showing Elena, or let the clean lines on the rest of the page lend the ring an authority it lacked. Keeping the page would remind her not to redraw it as certainty at the hearing.

Elena held its edge. She admitted doing something similar when imagining fair procedures: the right people would have enough time, strength, and willing respondents. Only afterward would she remember that someone needing the procedure might lack the carriage fare for a second visit.

“That doesn't mean abandoning procedure,” Adrian said.

“Or calculation.” Luna's strained expression eased. “Just don't quietly seat wishes among the known conditions.”

Someone collecting laundry outside dropped a wooden peg against the sill. Luna instinctively moved her right hand toward the disturbed paper, stopped, and held it with her left.

“Does it hurt again?”

“Itches a little. I mustn't scratch. I'll mention it at this afternoon's dressing change.”

He didn't use that to prove she should never work. He paused the table's business and asked for the supplies to come at the arranged time. Returning, he found both notebooks closed except the page to copy. She neither declared herself incapable of error nor worked until everyone felt reassured. She asked Elena to draw the curtain across a patch of reflected glare.

Their answer contained no six-name ranking. It set out the limited risks existing records supported, where evidence was absent, and which conclusions exceeded the data. Publicly shareable sources accompanied each point. Private medical information or original objects required separate, lawful requests; the liaison note couldn't conjure authority over them.

By the final line, Luna's left hand ached.

Adrian offered to copy the remaining lines for her. She initially refused, then permitted the body of the statement, not her signature. He must also leave out her crossed-out sarcasm.

“Arranging romantic prospects as emergency exits?”

“You've seen it. You needn't circulate it.”

Elena laughed and supplied fresh paper. In the second paragraph, he wrote not present instead of not established. Luna caught it immediately.

He corrected it visibly.

“Perhaps moving bowls suits me better.”

“They can break too.”

“You make choosing a profession difficult.”

“I'm not the employment registry.”

After the laughter, the pen resumed. Nobody let the error ride along with the joke.

The comparison with an actual source still mattered. The bowls had helped explain the problem, but they could not authenticate an archival page. Someone authorized to verify the old table needed to check its citations. Before noon, Hartwell brought a certified copy of the same page. He checked the source and custody wording within his responsibility, not every calculation Luna had made. Hartwell placed the authenticated page beside their text and compared the wording directly. His presence did not mean he could certify the whole argument merely because he had brought a source into the room. They corrected a page reference and expanded an old unit abbreviation so tomorrow's readers wouldn't mistake unlike measures for equivalent ones.

Once he left, Luna read her position back, approved the wording, and signed left-handed. Nobody was asked to imitate her former elegant handwriting.

The outgoing messenger hadn't arrived. Elena left to handle her own papers, leaving Adrian a little packet of roasted nuts.

“The kitchen made extra.”

Luna raised an eyebrow at its knot.

“Do all the extras lack honey?”

“He said it was sticky last time.”

“I hadn't asked why.”

Elena flushed without denying that she'd remembered.

“Now you know.”

Afterward, Adrian opened the packet and offered Luna some. She chose one, held it steadily in her left hand, and found it slightly hard.

“You needn't like it.”

“I know. She wasn't remembering my preferences.”

It wasn't an unapproachable bitterness. Some things between other people didn't await equal distribution to everyone else.

Adrian returned the beans to the kitchen. When he came back, Luna was studying her paint-shop sketch. The absurdly round head remained.

“I can't go today. I need to rest this afternoon.”

“I hadn't asked.”

“I know. I was afraid I would.”

She folded the sketch, then offered him her pencil. Would he draw an ordinary bowl on fresh paper?

He did. A too-straight first line and an overcorrected second made its rim resemble a boat in the wind. He tried to turn the paper over. Luna pinned it left-handed. She hadn't finished looking.

He put down the pencil and awaited judgment.

Why was the base so narrow? He couldn't explain. While actually moving bowls, he apparently hadn't noticed. She pointed to the real one still nearby. Comparing them, he began laughing too.

“Were you truly entitled to mock the head I drew?”

“No.”

She laughed hard enough to nearly drop the sheet. Her right hand stayed down. They kept the drawing out of the technical submission and didn't rename it a rehabilitation test.

That afternoon, the building's safety confirmation arrived. Loading appointments were separated. A worker had acknowledged repairing the rail. The yard route must remain unobstructed throughout the hearing. The duty team accepted visitor management within its authority. Some of Viola's suggestions were adopted; implementation still belonged to the original supervisor, not to her name smuggled onto a roster.

Luna placed it beside her technical statement.

“At last, these two papers can explain different things.”

“Ready to send?”

She agreed. He sealed it within the existing disclosure scope, without helpfully appending all the drafts scattered beside her.

After the registered messenger took it, the table looked unexpectedly empty. Luna counted the borrowed bowls and checked the beans had gone back in their bag. She kept the crooked bowl drawing.

“Why not put that away too?” Adrian asked.

“It proves absolutely nothing.”

“So discard it?”

“So I may keep it. I like it. Hartwell needn't certify that.”

She laid it beside the paint-shop sketch, requiring no useful justification from him.

Before leaving, he asked whether she wanted the window seat at dinner.

“Yes. But if I'm tired, I'll eat here.”

“I'll save a bowl.”

“Yours or the kitchen's?”

“One that holds soup.”

Smiling, she waved him away left-handed. Tomorrow would still contain unanswered questions. Today, their table contained no ranking of the person most suitable to surrender.
