# Why Do All the Villainesses Want to Marry Me?
## Volume One: A Betrothal Written in Blood
### Chapter 4: That Kiss Was an Accident. I Swear.

"That depends on how many times you do it."

With those reassuring words, Elena led Adrian onto the dance floor.

He hadn't even had time to regret taking her hand when someone behind them set down a wineglass with a conspicuous clink.

"Isn't that the Rosenbergs' servant?"

"She's dancing the opening waltz with *him*?"

"But His Highness is standing right there!"

Whispers seemed to crawl up the back of Adrian's collar.

"Are we attracting more attention than the evidence?" he murmured.

"That's rather the point."

"You want the entire ballroom staring at us?"

"I want them staring at us instead of the evidence ledger."

Her expression remained perfectly composed.

Adrian glanced toward Leon.

The prince hadn't made any move to stop them. He stood beside Gray, the protocol officer, wearing a smile that was far too polite to be friendly.

"Why didn't he come over and ask you to dance?" Adrian whispered.

"You saw him just now."

"Yes."

"Then you saw him waiting for me to approach him."

"Another trap?"

"An insult." Elena spoke evenly. "If I go to him, tomorrow they'll say I begged him not to end our engagement. If I don't, they'll say he's already cast me aside."

"So your solution was to grab a servant?"

"At least you offered me your hand."

She said it so casually that Adrian couldn't decide whether to feel flattered or deeply sorry for himself.

"All right. Which way do we go?"

"First, let's establish that you can walk forward."

The strings struck the opening notes.

Elena guided Adrian's right hand to her waist and took his other hand in hers.

His gloves suddenly felt alarmingly thin.

"Don't hold yourself so stiffly," she said.

"I'm trying to relax."

"Then stop looking as though you're handling a magical beast that's about to explode."

"What if the magical beast is a duke's daughter?"

"Then you might consider not speaking."

She led him into the first step.

"Left foot."

Adrian stepped right.

Elena stopped for a fraction of a beat.

"That was your right foot."

"I know."

"Then why did you move it?"

"My feet and my brain aren't on speaking terms."

"That gives you at least three enemies tonight."

"The prince, the Judicial Office, and my own feet?"

"Excellent. You can count."

They moved around the outer edge of the floor.

Adrian tried to follow the rhythm. One step, two steps, turn. Just as he began to recognize a pattern, Elena changed direction, bringing him nearer to the side entrance.

"Where's the clerk?" she whispered.

"To the right. Behind the gold column."

"Don't look at him."

"Then how am I supposed to see what he's doing?"

"By dancing properly. Eyes up. Look at me."

Adrian obeyed.

She was close.

The chandeliers caught flecks of gold in her eyes. He found himself thinking that the Elena in *Crown of Fate* had never looked at a player like this. Every expression on her character portrait had been carefully drawn and fixed in place.

The slight irritation at the corner of her eyes was real.

So was the smile she was trying not to show.

"You're distracted again," she said.

"I'm studying your movements."

"By staring at my face?"

"It might help."

One eyebrow rose.

"Then let's see if it does."

She guided him through a half turn.

Adrian nearly collided with an older couple. He managed to sidestep them at the last moment, earning a sympathetic look from the husband.

"That man was laughing at me," Adrian muttered.

"No."

"How do you know?"

"He was laughing at me."

"Why?"

"Because I've been trying to teach you to dance, and you're still hopeless."

"We've been dancing for less than thirty seconds!"

Elena broke into a genuine laugh.

It was a far cry from the smile she'd given the prince.

Adrian was about to laugh too when he spotted movement near the side door.

Edwin Clay was handing the blue jewelry case to a palace guard. The guard turned the ledger around for an additional signature. Gray stood beside them, inspecting the lid.

"The box is changing hands," Adrian whispered.

"What?"

"Clay's handing it over to the palace guards."

"Go on."

"The guard has turned to a new page. He's writing near the bottom."

Elena kept her eyes on Adrian.

"We'll pass that way in a moment. Remember only what you can actually see. Don't fill in the gaps."

He nodded.

They circled toward the edge of the floor.

A gold column blocked half the table, but the guard moved the ledger into the light. Fresh ink gleamed on the page.

Adrian caught three digits.

Seven.

One.

Four.

"Seven-fourteen," he whispered.

"An evidence number?"

"I think—"

"Don't think. What did you see?"

"Seven-one-four, written beside the jewelry case in the registration ledger."

"Good."

"What about the date?"

"I couldn't see it."

"Then another lap."

"Are you certain I'll survive another lap?"

"You haven't stepped on me yet."

That reassurance lasted approximately ten seconds.

The music picked up. Woodwinds joined the strings, and couples began turning more quickly.

Adrian stepped back to avoid a lady who'd spun out farther than intended.

His toe landed on the hem of Elena's gown.

The fabric pulled taut.

"Don't move," Elena whispered sharply.

He realized what had happened and tried to free his foot.

Unfortunately, his attempt to step forward only dragged more of her skirt beneath his shoe.

Elena lost her balance.

She caught the front of his coat.

Adrian threw an arm around her waist to keep her upright—and caught his other heel on the polished floor.

Both of them pitched sideways.

One thought flashed through his head.

*Whatever happens, don't let her fall.*

A heartbeat later, his lips met something soft.

Just for an instant.

Then they were both perfectly still.

The orchestra continued playing.

The people immediately around them, however, appeared to have forgotten what music was.

A waiter froze with his tray tilted dangerously to one side.

An elderly lady on a bench opened her mouth.

No sound came out.

Adrian jerked back.

"I'm—I'm sorry!"

He nearly stepped on the gown again and had to pull his foot away at an absurd angle.

"That was an accident! I swear!"

Elena slowly straightened.

She touched her lips.

Her face revealed absolutely nothing.

"Mr. Reed."

"Yes!"

"Could you apologize a little louder?"

"What?"

"I don't think the people outside the palace heard you."

Adrian finally noticed the dozen or so pairs of eyes fixed on them.

Someone was hiding a smile behind a hand.

Nearby, a young nobleman whispered, "Didn't she grab him by the collar?"

His companion nodded gravely.

"I'm fairly sure she pulled him in for the kiss."

Adrian's stomach dropped.

The story had already become something else.

"I can explain," he whispered.

"Don't."

"But—"

"If you start explaining now, they'll assume I ordered you to."

"So we just let them misunderstand?"

Elena took a slow breath.

"Adrian. Could you remove your hand from my waist first?"

He looked down.

His right hand was still securely around her.

"I'm sorry!"

This time he said it even louder.

A lady nearby finally burst out laughing.

A faint flush reached the tips of Elena's ears.

She removed his hand from her waist.

But she didn't release the hand she was already holding.

"Keep dancing."

Adrian stared at her.

"We're still dancing?"

"Of course."

"But what just—"

"I know what just happened. You don't need to remind me every other sentence."

She lifted her chin, and her ruby earrings swayed.

"Besides, it was an accident."

"Exactly!"

"Why do you sound so pleased?"

"Because I'm relieved you haven't decided to execute me."

"I'm reconsidering."

Adrian straightened at once.

Elena looked away.

A light flush had crept along the side of her neck.

No walkthrough had prepared him for this.

There was certainly no guide explaining whether, when a proud young noblewoman was blushing, the safest option was to pretend not to notice or smile.

Adrian chose a third option.

The evidence ledger.

The incident had drawn a small crowd. Even the young guard stationed by the registration table had turned to watch.

Gray was frowning at a waiter, apparently ordering him to clear the spectators.

Adrian caught sight of the open ledger.

"My lady. Third column."

"What?"

"The ledger's turned toward us. The date is in the third column."

She guided him nearer the column.

"Make sure you've read it correctly."

They stopped trying to match the faster steps. Elena slowed their movement, using the final turn of the music to put Adrian directly opposite the registration table.

The guard lifted the ledger to show Gray the fresh entry.

For a moment, a cream-colored page lay fully exposed beneath the lamp.

**Item No. 714.**

**Contents: Sapphire necklace and accompanying case.**

**Date sealed: September 18.**

Adrian's breath caught.

Today was September seventeenth.

He didn't say anything yet.

He checked the year. Then the month. Then the heading over the date column.

There was no confusion between day and month.

No reservation or scheduled-delivery field.

The entry said *date sealed*.

"I saw it," he whispered at last.

"What?"

"Seven-one-four. Sealed September eighteenth."

"Are you sure?"

"Yes. And it's the sealing date, not an appointment date."

Elena's smile disappeared.

"Today is the seventeenth."

"I know."

"How can they seal an item tomorrow if it's already here tonight?"

Adrian nearly said *because the prince is an idiot*.

Then he stopped.

Maybe the entry had never been intended for anyone to see tonight.

Under the original plan, the Judicial Office would have completed the paperwork on the eighteenth. Everything would have appeared orderly afterward.

But the prince had moved the banquet forward.

Someone had brought the evidence earlier and forgotten to change the date.

"Not evidence from the future," Adrian murmured. "Someone's following the original schedule and forgot to update the paperwork."

Elena didn't accept that immediately.

"The clerk could simply have made a mistake."

"Exactly. We can't accuse him of forgery on this alone."

"At last. A sensible sentence."

"But we need to make sure that ledger stays intact. And that tonight's witnesses remember when they saw it."

Elena looked toward Gray.

"I can arrange that."

She let go of Adrian's hand and straightened her gown.

Prince Leon approached at last.

"Elena."

His expression was pleasant. His eyes lingered on her and Adrian for a moment too long.

"An unforgettable opening dance."

Adrian considered finding a convenient hole in the floor.

Elena spared him no such mercy.

"Since Your Highness didn't come to invite me, I found my own partner."

"A servant?"

"Someone willing to take my hand."

The prince's smile faded.

"I'm afraid the capital's nobles will have plenty to say about this tomorrow."

"Then at least we know they were paying attention to the dance."

She turned to Gray.

"Mr. Gray, under palace protocol, evidence delivered by the Royal Judicial Office during a ball must remain in palace custody until the formal transfer is completed. Is that correct?"

Gray hadn't been expecting to be addressed.

"In principle, my lady."

"Excellent. With so many witnesses present, I'm sure no one will object to having the registration officer and evidence custodian remain available until the end of the evening."

Gray glanced toward the prince.

Leon didn't immediately answer.

Elena gave him a gracious smile.

"So much has happened tonight already. I'd hate for someone to claim tomorrow that the Rosenberg household interfered with royal evidence before the ball ended."

On the surface, she was protecting the palace's evidence.

In reality, she was keeping its ledger from disappearing.

At last Leon nodded.

"Of course. Gray, follow protocol."

"Yes, Your Highness."

Gray and the guard withdrew toward the side entrance, but the ledger remained under their supervision.

Adrian let out a slow breath.

At least they wouldn't have to watch the evidence record vanish in front of them.

Leon glanced at Adrian's hand again.

"You dance rather well, Mr. Reed."

Adrian desperately wanted to know what performance the prince had been watching.

He bowed instead.

"You're very kind, Your Highness."

"Though some missteps," Leon added, "cannot be taken back."

Elena stepped between them.

"Then I suggest Your Highness watches his own footing tonight."

For an instant, the prince's eyes turned cold.

Then his smile returned.

"I shall."

The musicians began another piece.

Adrian stood at the edge of the dance floor and waited for his heartbeat to slow.

"My lady?"

"Hmm?"

"Were you just rescuing me from an awkward conversation, or threatening the prince?"

"Am I not allowed to do both?"

"I suppose you are."

Elena glanced at him.

"Was there something else you wanted to ask?"

He hesitated.

"About what happened on the dance floor... you really aren't angry?"

She looked at him for a long moment.

Then she reached up and straightened the bow tie he'd somehow managed to twist crooked again.

"I'm angry that you still don't know how to dance."

"I can learn."

"I should hope so."

"Would you teach me?"

Her hand paused.

"Survive tonight first."

She walked away.

The tips of her ears had turned pink again.

Adrian watched her go, momentarily relieved.

Then he remembered the ledger.

Seven-one-four.

Sealed September eighteenth.

A procedure that hadn't happened yet was already written down tonight.

In the game, the prince was about to produce the supposed evidence of treason.

Adrian clenched his hand and hurried after Elena.

"My lady. I think I understand."

"What is it?"

"They aren't getting ready to accuse you."

Elena stopped.

"They've already written the ending."

Her eyes moved toward the prince.

Leon was lifting his glass again, waiting for the master of ceremonies to make the evening's next announcement.
