# Chapter 151: I Can't Attend Five Dates at Once

The first reply Adrian received on January twenty-third confirmed that eating together still required no registration.

Luna was disappointed.

“They didn't explain the spoon.”

“They explained contracts.” He placed the stamped clarification on the side table. “You may write separately.”

“That would ruin my rare appearance of having no official business today.”

A supervisor had examined yesterday's rejected cover sheet. The emergency restrictions concerned new betrothal registrations and proposed arrangements establishing or altering fate-risk transmission between listed people. Ordinary witness statements, everyday purchases, and private relationships didn't enter that category merely by containing two names.

Their individual accounts remained in custody. With an explanation of its purpose, the cover sheet could join them. The original misclassification stayed on record.

One specific obstruction had been resolved. The emergency order had not been withdrawn.

Betrothal choices and new risk-bearing contracts still required special review. The clarification didn't explain the basis of compatible-candidate assessment. Nor did any page suddenly prove Adrian had five existing betrothals. He kept those omissions for the objection rather than letting permission to eat wash everything else away.

Today's immediate problem was five invitations specifying after the third bell.

He invited anyone wishing to explain hers in person to use the small sitting room briefly before lunch. Private answers were possible too. Not attending wouldn't count as withdrawing from a contest.

All five came.

“I am not submitting a bid,” Elena said on entering.

“Excellent. I wasn't selling the afternoon.”

She relaxed a little when she saw no seating placards.

Viola sat where she needn't twist her left shoulder and opened her note. A twenty-minute walk along the south passage, five minutes each way, two benches available en route.

Luna narrowed her eyes. “An evacuation route too?”

“Yes.”

Realizing her mistake, the knight immediately covered the reverse.

Adrian didn't laugh at her care. “Has your physician allowed that much walking?”

“Short ordinary journeys. It's a limit, not a requirement.”

“Then I'd change one thing. When you're tired, we sit. Reaching the endpoint needn't prove I came.”

After considering, she crossed out endpoint herself.

Isabella withheld comment on the dispatch-like invitation. Her own wasn't much better.

Half an hour. Ordinary tea. No official matters. Early departure by the guest would not count as breaking the appointment.

Hearing Isabella read the final clause aloud, Elena couldn't help herself.

“Did you include a prohibition on anger?”

“I deleted it. It wouldn't be true.”

For a moment the room went quiet. Then several people laughed.

Isabella calmly folded her note.

“I'll be disappointed. I don't intend to punish anyone for it. Both can be true.”

“That sounds better than the invitation,” Adrian said.

“I can rewrite it.”

“No need. We understand.”

She looked at him, almost believing some words didn't need a better format before they counted.

Cecilia's invitation was shortest: I'd like another breakfast with you. More slowly this time.

She too had written the third bell. Adrian pointed that out.

“I thought you might be busy in the morning.”

“Isn't the third bell late for breakfast?”

“A late breakfast can still be a date.”

Elena turned toward the teapot. Even Luna failed to translate that into research terminology.

“But I needn't give every meal the same name,” Cecilia added. “I enjoyed last time. I'd like to see you again. That's the whole reason.”

Adrian nodded without pretending he hadn't understood.

Luna's invitation was a rough street map to the paint shop, with two people drawn beside it. One head was noticeably rounder.

“Which is me?”

“The handsome one.”

“You should have drawn someone handsome.”

She glared and pulled the paper back left-handed. “My lines suffer from legitimate limitations.”

“Then the round one looks very spirited.”

She laughed, her fingertip resting on the shop's name.

“No reagents. I want to see the colored chalk. My right hand needn't draw for me to look. I'll decide whether to buy it there.”

Adrian remembered her mentioning the shop before. He asked about its steps and her medical appointment. She mustn't postpone treatment until evening to accommodate his free time.

“I checked,” she said. “That doesn't make you obliged to come.”

Finally, Elena.

She gave nobody her paper. She simply wanted to visit the secondhand bookshop for the next volume of their travel narrative, then walk home. No research for Isabella, no crate of books for him to carry.

“You can look for something else too.”

“I want to know whether the captain reaches the correct port next time.”

“Then you accept?”

“I'd like to go. Don't write in the entire afternoon yet.”

Luna shook her head.

“I have another objection.”

She wasn't pointing at Adrian, but at Isabella's tea invitation.

“You specified when he could stop, not when you need rest.”

The princess looked down. “Half an hour didn't seem long.”

“Perhaps not for someone uninjured. I used to be excellent at deleting myself from the restrictions.”

Isabella thought before promising to add it. Viola also reviewed her walk, adding either person to the provision for stopping if uncomfortable.

Cecilia had work of her own on the twenty-fifth. If her morning changed, she would ask again. She couldn't insist Adrian honor agreed times while endlessly relegating her own duties to last place.

Elena leaned back and unexpectedly smiled.

“Apparently, anyone might be busy.”

“Does this significantly reduce the success rate?” Isabella asked.

Luna opened her mouth, then laughed and swallowed her response.

Adrian said only, “It makes an acceptance more worth believing.”

Nobody added footnotes. A sparrow landed on the sill, pecked an empty flowerpot, and flew away. Plenty of the morning remained. They needn't schedule the rest of their lives immediately.

Adrian spread out his commitments. Around the third bell, he had to confirm the witness statements' completed filing, then deliver an incident question to the repository. He also needed food and rest. Every blank space wasn't waiting for someone to claim it.

The five invitations weren't thick. They could still tear an afternoon into five scraps that satisfied nobody.

He returned each to its author.

“I won't prove fairness by giving everyone equal minutes. Working together is one thing. Choosing whom to spend private time with is another. And I won't accept first, then try to divide myself afterward.”

Cecilia nodded first. Viola regarded her twenty minutes without reducing them into the cheapest offer.

Adrian told Elena that after his work, they could reach the bookshop before the fourth bell. If the window delayed him, he would send word. She accepted and put away her original time without displaying a victory.

Cecilia proposed the morning of the twenty-fifth. After checking existing obligations, they agreed to a longer breakfast with a clear finish.

“This isn't compensation for time I didn't give you yesterday,” Adrian said.

“I know. You owed me nothing yesterday.”

Viola's walk remained undated. Her left arm would be examined tomorrow; she wanted to know any revised limits first. They could discuss it afterward. Saying it today didn't require her to force herself through it.

Luna's mouth fell slightly when he couldn't visit the paint shop over the next two days.

“You needn't immediately invent a surprise for me,” she said before he spoke. “I'll ask again. The shop won't disappear because you're busy today.”

He had been about to say next time. Instead, he smiled and agreed.

No miraculous empty interval appeared for Isabella's tea either. Adrian couldn't accept today. She looked at the note and pressed a crease into it with her right fingertips. She didn't ask whether injury had made her unattractive.

“Then I'll drink it myself.”

“I hope it's hot.”

“I may demand that much of the kitchen.”

She smiled. Disappointment remained without becoming someone else's obligation.

The discussion ended sooner than expected, and not everyone had a pleasing result.

Watching them gather their different papers, Adrian realized he had more to learn than refusal. When someone accepted his no, guilt mustn't hurry him into another promise he hadn't considered.

That afternoon, Elena accompanied him to the registry.

The clarification now stood beside the posted order. The clerk checked their purpose before rejecting anything merely containing jointly. They obtained the combined custody acknowledgment and confirmed that both original accounts and the correction record remained together.

Elena asked why priority review was tied to compatible-candidate assessment. The clerk directed her to a separate objection desk. He couldn't erase his superiors' requirement here.

On that desk lay a form for individual noble exemptions. A family guarantee could support priority examination. It promised no approval, but brought an application forward sooner than an ordinary objection.

She read both sides without immediately taking it.

On the way back, Adrian asked what she was thinking.

“The lawyer may suggest requesting a family exception first.”

“Would it help?”

“Perhaps. Our family can provide assurances. Someone will receive our letters.”

Watching people wait at the ordinary window, she tightened her grip on her folder.

“But I don't want the Rosenbergs can answer for her to become proof that I deserve to choose you. Anyone without a family to write for them would stay outside forever.”

“You'll challenge the order itself?”

“I'll ask which part can be challenged. I haven't written it yet.”

She stopped and looked at him.

“You'd face questions too. I won't borrow your name to prove how brave I am.”

He would read the application carefully. That neither abandoned all the work to her nor demanded she bear public controversy simply because she liked him.

Before the fourth bell, they reached the bookshop during its quiet final stretch of opening hours.

The proprietor didn't recognize them or ask about the order. Which volume did they want? Elena requested the sequel. He fetched a book with a bluer cover. Adrian opened it: the captain had recovered his hat, only to leave his boarding ticket at the inn.

They laughed together.

“Read it before?” the proprietor asked.

“We've encountered his abilities,” Elena said.

She paid for her own book. Adrian didn't snatch the bill as though that would settle an entire day's care. Outside, she held the volume between them and merely asked his help turning a few pages.

“Must we time this walk too?”

“We only need to be home before dinner.”

He offered his hand. She took it without looking back to see who might notice.

Sunset lay on her shoulder like a small patch of warm metal. Remembering the five very different invitations, Adrian was glad he hadn't reduced this walk to another hurried obligation in an attempt to disappoint nobody.

Near the house, Elena closed the book.

“I'll see the lawyer tomorrow morning.”

“Would you like me there?”

“Let me explain my own reasons first. I'll ask when I need your answer.”

He nodded. She gently swung their joined hands, reminding him that this didn't mean leaving him outside.

The lights were already on indoors. Tomorrow, they would still be arguing for a door not yet opened.

Tonight, the road had been their own choice.
