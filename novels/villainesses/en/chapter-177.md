# Chapter 177: Not the Same Kind of Waiting

On February 7, Adrian found three notes beside his breakfast.

One requested the borrowed dictionary. Another asked about chess on February 10. The third said: Don't move the pot for me. I only want you to look.

He retrieved the dictionary from his coat. Martha asked whether the conservatory's gardener had needed legal vocabulary yesterday.

“I forgot.”

“That doesn't sound like anyone's conspiracy.”

Elena laughed. Reading her retained copy of yesterday's inquiry, she didn't reach across to organize his notes. He put the dictionary on the return shelf.

“More people waiting for a pleasant answer?” she asked.

“One only wants her book.”

“Return it first, then.”

She read on without asking who'd written the others. He didn't mistake restraint for permanent indifference. Yesterday she'd asked him not to offer everyone the same hope merely to seem kind.

Before he left, fastening her own cloak, she suggested something to eat that afternoon. He'd agreed to visit Luna and couldn't promise a return time. Disappointment crossed her face without becoming an accusation.

“I won't hold tea for you.”

“If I'm back early, I'll ask whether you're free.”

“Ask. Not announce.”

She left first. Yesterday's kiss hadn't signed her name to every hour today.

At the relief hall, Cecilia was arguing with the storeroom keeper over a blanket. It was clean but had a hard knot underneath. She wasn't objecting to its appearance: put it beneath someone's seated legs and it would rub the skin raw.

When Adrian returned the dictionary, she was cutting out the stiff old patch.

“Hold this edge, please.”

He held the cloth on the table. The scissors slipped sideways; she stopped and chose another thread, admitting she wasn't quick at sewing.

“I thought you'd learned everything.”

“Learning isn't doing well. You've read that dictionary and still forgot it in your coat.”

No defense there.

She stopped a helper throwing the patch away. Its edges could mend bags; keep it apart from scraps contaminated with medicine. Finding her sleeve wet, she rolled it up. No convenient light gilded her from behind. There was only difficult damp cloth. Catching his look, she asked whether her sleeve needed a witness too. He laughed and closed the scissors, pointing them inward.

She returned the sewing to someone skilled instead of asking him to mend everything. By the water buckets afterward, washing her hands, she asked whether he considered visits something he owed her.

“Sometimes I'm afraid you'll think I only come when something's wrong.”

“Fear shouldn't arrange your days.” She dried her hands. “I like seeing you, not merely being needed. That isn't an old record speaking for me.”

Someone called. She indicated a moment, not using work to escape her words.

“But if you only see a friend you can work beside, tell me. Don't always say later.”

His throat tightened. Saying she mattered would be easy, and wouldn't answer.

“I enjoy being with you. I trust you. But I can't agree to court you now. You shouldn't have to wait to prove those other things.”

She folded the cloth twice before its edges matched.

“It still doesn't feel very good.”

“I'm sorry.”

“I wasn't asking you to withdraw it.” She looked up. “I don't think I want another walk with you today.”

He said all right. When she turned away, he didn't follow to explain until she agreed he had meant well, or urge her to be at ease before she was ready. She returned to the blankets. He stepped aside for a man carrying buckets and took his regret away, instead of staying until she comforted him.

At midday, Viola was struggling with a loosened shawl in the inner courtyard. Her left arm remained supported. Two right-handed tugs had only lengthened one end. Her caregiver was nearby fetching warm water. Adrian asked permission to straighten it. She surveyed her work with disgust and agreed.

“Were those ten minutes on February 4 difficult to endure?”

He remembered the bench, dried fruit, and her irritation at mistaking a cough for a training signal.

“I think you wanted to evade going back to rest.”

“I meant sitting with me. Not medical advice.”

He returned the shawl's end to her right hand without tying it tight.

“No. I was glad to go.”

Triumph flashed, then she watched him closely. “There are different kinds of glad.”

He stopped pretending not to understand.

“I agreed to sit with you that day. I wasn't agreeing to a romance.”

She exhaled like someone stopping a punch halfway herself.

“I'm allowed to dislike that answer?”

“Yes.”

“And not invite you again for a while?”

“Of course.”

She rested her right hand in her lap, not pushing against the chair with her injured arm.

“I don't want recovery to become queuing for prizes. Sit most obediently and move to the front.”

It hurt. He didn't promise she'd never queue, when his own ambiguity needed changing.

“You don't owe me a pleasing recovery. My visits aren't rewards.”

After a silence she let her caregiver put the water close. She still shared two pieces of dried fruit, but declined his offer to read letters. Not punishment; she didn't want to give him the whole afternoon.

A delivery cart caught at the gate. Viola instinctively leaned forward, then stopped herself. The porter fetched help; two people pushed it over a wooden wedge without needing her to endure pain. Apparently, she observed, the world survived one less Blackwood moving things. Adrian didn't praise her obedience. He moved an empty plate obstructing her view. Wanting to watch the courtyard didn't make her its chief object of supervision.

Leaving, he heard her request another book—preferably without anybody sitting by a window awaiting a gentleman.

Luna's pot didn't need moving. A small plant with yellowing leaf edges stood beside three crooked left-handed sketches. Her right palm remained bandaged; fatigue had stopped her left hand too. She hadn't used magic to finish. A weight held the paper, the pencil well away.

“Which looks right?”

He crouched, studied all three, and chose the second.

“That's the pot.”

“I noticed. It looks most like a pot.”

She frowned, then laughed. The others had been intended as pots too; she hadn't wanted to admit it.

The plant wasn't a rare specimen, just an ordinary division the gardener had let her sketch. Someone capable still cared for it. She was looking, not proving how much an injured person could accomplish.

“Usually when I ask you to look, there's a reading or mechanism. Not this time.”

“I haven't prepared for an examination.”

“Are you bored?”

She seemed less to be requesting praise than preparing for his polite departure.

“No. But I can't come every time. Sometimes I'll want to be alone.”

“I don't mean every time.” She slowed her hurried answer. “Without those uses, would you still come?”

Yes, he said, explaining that he wanted to visit as a friend, not leave that yes standing for another promise. Her smile faded as she turned toward the window.

“You're being very serious today.”

“I've been too careless before.”

She didn't praise his honesty. Instead she told him to turn the second sketch over. Eventually he recognized the window catch on its back.

“This is better than the pot.”

“I know.”

Some lightness returned. Before he left, though, she said not to arrange the botanical-garden outing she'd wanted. First she needed to decide whether she still wanted the same kind of excursion.

He agreed without offering a more distant date.

She lent him the second sketch for comparison with a real pot, but only for one day. He asked whether she'd rather keep her drawing. Lending wasn't discarding, she replied. Nor could he show it to a repairer as proof of her hand's recovery. He understood. Waiting until the last eraser crumbs were cleared, he sandwiched it between plain sheets. She watched without offering her injured hand.

That afternoon, Isabella stood beside the study's long table holding a chess box. She wasn't demanding an immediate game; she'd come for a public copy of yesterday's vote. The box clasp was broken, so a handkerchief kept pieces from rolling among documents.

“I thought princesses' sets never lacked a knight.”

“It's inside. Did you mistake my handkerchief for one?”

He withdrew his hand. She collected the copy without asking him to reward today's inconvenience by accepting her invitation.

“February 10. Half an hour in the late afternoon,” he said. “If you'd still like to. I want to play chess, but not let chess imply a romantic answer I haven't given.”

She was quiet, smoothing the bundle with her uninjured hand.

“At least you're not making me guess the next yes from this one.”

She considered it rather than replying simply because he had finally offered a time. Then she said she would still like the game. Half an hour late that afternoon, in the common sitting room. Either would give notice if plans changed. Missing an appointment wouldn't license unlimited questions about the other's whereabouts.

“And don't lose deliberately to apologize.”

“I may not be able to win.”

“Different matter.”

At the door she reminded him that the public copy retained opposing votes. Passing the trial hadn't erased them. He said he'd read it. That separate business finished, she handed the copy to the waiting messenger. The game and the document weren't conditions exchanged, nor did they belong in one box.

She took her chess set away. Adrian watched her leave without deciding that because she had smiled, every disappointment he might have caused had vanished. She could keep an appointment without making his conscience entirely comfortable. Adrian wrote the time on his own note, not copying the same answer for everyone else.

Toward evening, Cecilia sent a notice through the relief hall's messenger, without a private greeting.

It came from the Temple's north annex concerning stored old plates. On February 8, designated escorts would admit inspection of the catalog and custody conditions for the batch associated with the overlapping impressions. Reading originals required separate permission. Beneath that arrangement was routine maintenance processing scheduled for the same morning.

She'd added one question: Do these arrangements concern the same batch?

Adrian flattened the paper. Coming from the Temple didn't make it a threat; calling it routine didn't make it harmless. He replied through the same messenger, requesting clarification of the batch and processing scope. Tomorrow's attendance remained within the inspection permission granted, not a right to force doors because he had questions.

Outside, lamps brightened one by one. Today's different farewells hadn't become easy merely because he'd finally spoken.

Luna's crooked pot lay at the desk corner, borrowed only until tomorrow. He found a dry place for it, clear of the water jug, and wrote a reminder to return it beside the notice. It was still her drawing, not a little object she had forfeited by letting him take an interest. Someone being willing to wait didn't mean he could go on leaving them unanswered.
