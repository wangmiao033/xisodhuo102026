# Chapter 206: The Line Someone Moved

On March 5, Adrian found Elena keeping a cautious distance from a saucepan of jam.

Martha handed the spoon to the cook. Nobody would prove their patience by watching fruit boil until they missed an appointment. Elena had wanted something new for breakfast. The fruit was sourer than the recipe promised, and sugar refused to dissolve through aristocratic determination.

“I haven't ordered it to behave.”

“Clearly. It retains strong opinions.”

She glared, then laughed. The cook offered two cooled samples, not a spoonful from the boiling pan. The first made his eyes narrow. The second was excellent. Elena noted his choice without substituting her own mixture.

“We can't take it into the reading room.”

“I wasn't planning to destroy history to demonstrate its flavor.”

She left a little jar on the breakfast table. They ate before leaving. The ledger hadn't replaced breakfast.

The associated inquiry had obtained the original present-day issue counterfoil mentioned yesterday. An independent registry custodian opened its envelope. Title, accession number, and borrower matched. The return column remained empty beside a reminder requiring its return. This supported Evelyn's acknowledgment of retaining overdue material; it didn't make private custody benevolent beyond inquiry.

The Reset Duty Review Log: Working Duplicate remained on its independent custodian's stand. Two missing leaves remained missing. One genuine counterfoil couldn't bridge those spaces.

Today's authorized comparison went beyond the final page. Following its reference, the issuing archive had supplied an original amendment order. A separate works repository supplied an implementation return. Each envelope was registered separately. Evelyn hadn't selected a pile for them to declare mutually corroborating.

High Priest Oswin's name appeared beneath the order.

Adrian first noticed the surviving hook of a crossed-out six. Eight stood beside it, initialed at the alteration; a full signature followed the text. The warning comparison line for a particular group of operating reports was to move from B-six to B-eight.

“Someone could have used his seal,” Elena said.

The handwriting examiner wasn't offended. Two routine signature samples came from independent correspondence files, verified within the same historical year grouping. The seal was only one element. Joined strokes, abbreviated signatures, and the amended numeral supported the same writer. Dispatch records described the original returning after the High Priest's approval, not delegated clerical approval.

No modern replacement was evident at the alteration. The samples hadn't come from the newly surrendered log. Combined evidence supported Oswin's personal approval, not merely a name in a column. Converting that old year into their calendar remained outside the conclusion.

Cecilia studied it. “Please copy the original number too.”

“Of course.”

“I want to see what he changed. Not only who signed.”

A young copying clerk brought a comparison diagram. Before Adrian reached its conclusion, Luna asked the assistant to turn it over.

“What's on the back?”

“A fragment of another scale.”

Reused practice paper had saved a sheet. On the front, however, different baselines placed six above eight. Adrian had nearly inferred from the drawing that the new line was easier to reach. He offered clean paper rather than having them revise the diagram until its original error vanished.

“Numbers don't establish direction by themselves,” Luna said. “Find the scale edition.”

That sheet lay beneath the other contents. The custodian searched against the inventory, without inviting extra hands into the envelope. Its damaged corner preserved only part of the edition number. Familiarity tempted the works examiner to identify it immediately. The reviewer asked for independently comparable features.

They checked unequal divisions and the paired short strokes unique to the B column's end. Surviving sections matched the order's diagram and the edition cited in the return. Receiving references connected both to the same amendment. The missing corner stayed missing; nobody drew it back for neatness.

Only then did Adrian correct his own lines. Delay hadn't erased the signature. It had given the decision beneath it definite meaning.

Cecilia asked that the mistaken diagram accompany the correction. Blushing, the clerk agreed, adding that direction must be read from the specified edition. He wasn't a conspirator revealing himself. He had nearly reversed a verifiable distinction. Adrian thanked him and waited for the ink to dry before filing both sheets.

The implementation return was smaller than the order, its corners yellowed by water. Its custodian showed the original directory, successive handovers, and receipt reference before placing it flat. Works staff had held it before yesterday's transfer; it wasn't an enclosure newly delivered with the log.

It cited the same amendment, scale edition, and report group. The duty entry recorded use of the new line. One subsequent reading reached B-seven. Classified under the revised rule, it was reported below warning level. The actual reading and former line remained written beside it.

Adrian read below warning level three times.

Six. Seven. Eight.

He drew three marks on his own paper, touching no original.

“Under the former line, that reading would have required a warning report?”

The works examiner opened the retained interpretation instructions. Same scale, same direction: reaching the comparison line required that report classification. Seven exceeded six but fell short of eight. The change had been implemented, not merely written and left undelivered.

“The actual danger didn't decrease.”

“Moving the line alone doesn't establish a decrease,” the examiner corrected. “Nor do three marks describe all danger. These were reporting settings, not a certain number of lives per division.”

Luna nodded from her chair. She was attending only this short session, right palm padded and left hand resting. Her assistant had drawn two colored lines from dictation and waited for her approval before circulating them.

“We've established that a person could change a warning comparison, and at least one report used it. Not that he could turn disaster from six to eight. There's room for an entire bad dissertation between those claims.”

Adrian didn't smile this time.

“What reason did he give?”

Reducing duplicate warnings and maintaining reporting order. Supporting grounds were referred to the central comparison baseline table, absent from this file. Reliable calibration, who knew the rule had changed, implementation elsewhere: the small return couldn't supply those answers.

“Can we check who first received the new line?” Adrian asked.

The examiner could locate this office's acknowledgment. But the circulation list contained crossed-out addresses; intended delivery wasn't actual receipt. Adrian had nearly asked for every address to be copied under recipients. He moved his pen away and requested separate columns for intended delivery and confirmed receipt. Someone who had never received the change shouldn't later be presumed to have known merely because a name survived on that sheet.

The reviewer added the question beside the baseline-table request, showing Adrian its position before withdrawing the original.

Cecilia paled. She recognized reassuring language, and had encouraged people to trust approved judgments. She didn't tear off her devotional ornament. She merely drew it back from the table edge so it wouldn't touch the papers.

“If there were grounds, I want to see them. If not, I want to know what people were asked to trust.”

Luna's assistant announced the end of her session. After checking that the diagram didn't represent a natural scale of danger, she left to rest. Adrian held the low curtain aside rather than asking her to stay for one crucial word.

“Don't erase your line either,” she said from the doorway.

He understood which line she meant. She wasn't asking him to add another accusation to the newly discovered order. She meant the action he had already acknowledged, still present on the drawing he had made for himself.

His own diagram still marked the wrong shutdown. Oswin's signature hadn't removed the brass handle from his earlier hand. Upstream anomalies, an unavailable bypass, distorted evacuation summaries, and today's reporting rules couldn't substitute blame for one another. They couldn't yet place the amendment at every fault's exact moment, much less call it the origin of all disaster.

“I won't.”

At lunch Cecilia collected a reply concerning her ordinary work from a nearby meal hall. She had already lost that earlier week's recitation. New evidence couldn't return the missed opportunity; future assignments still needed confirmation.

Elena and Adrian sat outside a clockmaker's shop while a repairer moved a carriage wheel blocking the pavement. Inside, an apprentice removed the twelve from a clock face, complaining that its position made deliveries always look late. His master told him to repair the slow gearing, not move the numbers to improve an employer's temper.

Adrian laughed, then looked down.

“Today I very much wanted someone who'd done more wrong than I had.”

Elena didn't ask whether he meant her.

“What would happen if you found him?”

“Perhaps I could stop being afraid of my own hand.”

He opened his left hand. The crescent scar lay quiet. A clock struck without summoning a new voice.

“I can't do that for you,” she said.

“I know.”

“But I'd like lunch with you. If you want, we needn't read signatures while we eat.”

He wanted that. They bought warm flatbreads next door and returned to the bench. Wind puffed out her paper bag. Adrian held his own instead of gathering everything into his hands. Folding hers shut, she asked whether he thought her incapable of managing bread.

“I've only seen your negotiations with jam.”

“It accepted the second proposal.”

“The cook did.”

Her shoe lightly touched his, an objection unworthy of a hearing. He didn't withdraw. After eating, he said they could save the jar for dinner.

That afternoon the examiner read back the bounded finding. Original order, independent handwriting samples, and dispatch records supported Oswin's personal amendment of this report group's warning comparison. The works return supported at least one instance of reporting under the new line. Neither authenticated an entire reset mechanism nor adjudicated all his culpability. Inquiry into Evelyn's present custody continued; surrender of the book purchased no immunity.

Adrian requested the line retaining the actual reading in his authorized excerpt. If they copied only below warning level, later readers would again lose sight of the moved threshold.

The custodian produced a directory. The central comparison baseline table belonged not to the western relay court but to a central comparison file among the Temple's sealed historical engineering records. The listing located material for an application. It guaranteed neither completeness nor access. Whether those records led toward the core they sought remained a question, not an entrance discovered through a place name.

Elena asked whether they should prepare the application grounds tonight.

“Send the diagram to the others first. I don't want merely to ask whether they'll accompany me.”

She nodded and left separate spaces for five answers rather than drafting agreement in advance.

The clockmaker's lamps still burned as they passed. Twelve had returned to its position, but the clock remained slow while its master worked on the gearing. Adrian paused without treating it as historical evidence.

He could simply see, now, that the hand which moved a line and the hand which pushed past a stop must each answer for its own action. Tomorrow they would decide how to question the records still closed to them.
