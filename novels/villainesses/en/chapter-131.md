# Chapter 131: That Isn't Her Highness's Door

On the evening of January 8, Elena did not straighten Adrian's collar as promised.

She stood in the entrance hall, looking at him for some time, before saying, “It's already perfect.”

He grew self-conscious. “Nothing needs correcting, or you've given up?”

“I wanted to look a little longer. I couldn't find an excuse.”

This time he blushed. She put on her coat and took his right arm, almost naturally enough to suggest long practice. Her fingers still hesitated before settling.

He offered his arm more firmly.

Luna waved with her left hand from beside the fire. She wasn't going to the palace. Her right palm had been dressed, and her drawings were put away. Viola would rest; Cecilia would join them for dinner after returning from her office. No one demanded that every invitation become a group expedition simply to avoid being left behind.

“If the desserts are sufficiently sturdy,” Luna said, “remember their names for me.”

“Not bring some home?”

“I should be asleep by then. Don't test our friendship with cold cream.”

Elena laughed and drew him outside.

The banquet hall's lights seemed gentler than those at the September ball. Adrian was willing to believe they were, at least. Attendants checked invitations, took coats, and explained the cloakroom and two open exits. The side passage was for staff, not departing guests. Anyone needing to leave could ask the duty attendants.

Chamberlain Simon Ellery checked the last temporary guest cards at the entrance. He looked more tired than at the council, but he did not substitute haste for verification.

“No accounts tonight,” he told Adrian.

“I'll try not to recognize numbers.”

“Excellent. You only need to recognize your seat.”

Isabella was greeting an elderly representative of a charitable society. Hard of hearing, he mistook her question about the roads for an inquiry about donations and began reciting figures. She showed no impatience, leaned closer, repeated herself, and asked an attendant to find him a place near the fire.

Adrian waited until she was free before approaching.

She glanced at his ordinary cuff buttons. “Your preferences are consistent.”

“Easier to fasten.”

“The pair you returned had no complicated mechanism.”

“Unfortunately, I've already inspected him tonight,” Elena said pleasantly.

“His clothes or their requisition papers?”

“Chiefly whether their wearer was willing.”

Isabella turned to Adrian. “Who wins that exchange according to etiquette?”

“If I say the cook, will you let me eat?”

“No,” they answered together.

He sighed and fetched Elena a small plate of food suitable for standing guests, leaving a piece for Isabella too. Rather than order it replated, the princess accepted it and ate before the next arrivals reached her.

When the music began, Elena did not wait to discover where someone else might assign him. She offered her hand and asked for a dance.

“Yes.”

They made a few unhurried turns along the edge of the floor. He remembered her steps, and that taking them seriously did not require staring at every seam between the boards. When he finally looked up, she smiled and said his collar deserved the praise after all.

“Only my collar?”

“Do you want everything said in front of this many people?”

“You could save something for the carriage.”

“Then don't fall asleep on the way home.”

He kept her hand until they reached their table. There was no stumble, no demand for another touch disguised as etiquette. She simply looked happier, and did not hide it when Isabella approached again.

The princess was about to give her brief address. Ellery had been called away to a dispute in the cloakroom. A young attendant delivered a folded service instruction: the arrangements had changed. After speaking, Isabella should take the western staff door to a small room to receive an envoy who could not be kept waiting.

She asked which envoy.

The attendant supplied no name, only a person on royal business.

“Who gave you this?” Adrian asked.

“The gentleman handling wine by that door. He said the chamberlain had arranged it.”

Adrian looked toward the passage. A man in dark livery stood near the doorway, a silver ornamental knot on his chest. He was watching the princess. He carried no tray. Used glasses stood beside him, but no one was requesting wine.

None of that proved anything.

Still, the paper claimed Ellery had confirmed an arrangement involving a door he had just identified as a staff route. Plans could change. An unverified message should not simply lead someone through it.

“Please wait. I'll ask the chamberlain.”

Isabella frowned at the instruction.

“It's time for my address.”

Elena took the paper Isabella was about to return, reading the visible text without disturbing its seal.

“Then give the address. Don't take that door.”

“What if the envoy really is waiting?”

“Let him wait again. It's less trouble than trusting someone who hasn't given a name.”

Isabella considered them. Guests were already turning toward her. Questioning an apparently normal arrangement would draw eyes to the paper. She could order a path cleared and make someone else carry the uncertainty.

Instead, she handed the instruction to Elena.

“Verify it. I'll stay here.”

Adrian did not rush into the passage. He approached a guard who could see the entrance and explained the instruction, the attendant's answer, and the man with the silver knot. He asked for confirmation, not the arrest of a stranger.

The guard called a colleague to remain on the guests' side while another went to find Ellery. Standing in the light, Adrian realized that the dark figure had left the glasses.

He was about to point this out when glass shattered in the passage.

A low, unmistakable demand followed.

“Why isn't she here yet?”

The young attendant backed through the doorway, his wrist trapped in another man's hand. The man with the silver knot followed. His impatience no longer belonged to a servant waiting to change the wine. Seeing the guards, he shoved the attendant forward and drew a short blade from beneath his coat.

Cold swept down Adrian's back.

This was no seal or old account awaiting an explanation at tomorrow's inspection.

“Back!” a guard shouted.

Adrian caught the attention of a guest near the door and directed him away from its opening. He did not leap at the knife. A guard shielded the frightened attendant while a second advanced. Together they forced the armed man back into the staff passage. A narrow cabinet overturned. The crash cut through the music.

The musicians stopped.

Elena was already beside Isabella.

“This way.”

“Don't let them think I'm running.”

“Then tell them where to go. You can move and still let them see you giving directions.”

Isabella drew a sharp breath, then raised her voice. Guests should follow the staff to the east warming hall, keep both routes clear, and avoid the side doorway. She did not announce the attacker's death or insist that everyone continue drinking to preserve appearances.

The elderly charity representative had missed the instruction and remained beside the fire. Adrian went back, crouched, and explained. He held the chair steady while an attendant came to help. They did not drag it through the crowd. When space opened, they accompanied the man slowly.

Someone was crying behind him. The young attendant clutched his wrist as a colleague led him to the physician. Red wine stained his shoe.

The color suggested something worse. Adrian made himself look properly before fear became an inaccurate shout.

Movement briefly stalled outside the east hall. A guest mistook a dropped shawl for someone fallen, cried out, and made people behind him turn back. Elena stood against the wall and explained clearly that the route remained open. They needed only to let an attendant retrieve the shawl.

Seeing that Elena was not pushing into the crowd, Isabella stepped aside too. Two noblemen who had tried to surround the princess finally cleared the doorway. The elderly guest entered with the attendant's arm to support him.

As Adrian followed, Isabella explained to the old man again: the address was canceled, carriages would be arranged in order, and he did not need to compete for the first. He still hadn't heard everything, but he saw that she remained and nodded.

She was unaccustomed to repeating an unprepared sentence three times. The third was clearer than the first.

Adrian's chest loosened a little. His fingers still shook. He left them at his side instead of making fists to hide it.

Captain Gareth Dacre arrived from the outer hall and took command. He had his guards confirm that both guest exits remained usable. The search proceeded inward through the staff passage while personnel were accounted for separately. No one locked the entire banquet inside.

The armed man was soon subdued. One guard had a cut across the back of his hand; the physician attended him. Duty personnel registered and secured the weapon and clothing. No one invited Adrian closer to claim a hero's share of the credit.

Dacre asked what he had seen.

Adrian described the silver knot, the seized wrist, the words he had heard, and where the blade appeared. He did not pretend to know a name or turn a look toward Isabella into a complete conspiracy.

“There are clear grounds to suspect Her Highness was targeted,” the captain said. “I'll protect her on that basis. His identity and any accomplices still require investigation.”

Elena had given the service instruction to Ellery, retaining an acknowledgment when it was registered. The chamberlain looked ill as he read it.

“This wasn't my arrangement. There was no private reception scheduled tonight.”

Isabella reached back for her chair. Now that the steady commands had left her, color drained from her face.

Adrian took a step toward her, stopped, and first asked whether she was hurt.

“No. You?”

“No.”

Elena touched his sleeve. He looked down. Wine marked the cuff; its ordinary button remained fastened. She did not wipe it. She briefly took his hand instead.

“I still have those things to say on the way home,” she murmured.

“I remember.”

She met his eyes, making sure he had heard, before releasing him to accept a shawl from an attendant. Adrian stayed where he was instead of looking again toward footsteps in the staff passage.

Dacre received another report at the door. The man was not on the wine-service roster. Whoever had supplied the silver ornament remained unidentified. A temporary service notice in the passage had also been replaced.

That did not explain how he had entered. It certainly did not transform yesterday's disputed hundred twenty silver coins into tonight's hiring fee.

Isabella asked whether all the attendants should be detained immediately.

Before Ellery could answer, Elena spoke.

“Keep the people who actually saw something available to testify. If you make everyone afraid to talk, tomorrow all we'll have is a quieter banquet hall.”

Isabella looked at her. Fear had not entirely left her eyes, but pride had returned first.

“Then stay. Not to decide for me. To help me ask.”

Elena first checked with Dacre that the guards had control of the scene. Then she pulled a chair beside the princess.

“All right. Let's start with the notice someone replaced.”
