# Why Do All the Villainesses Want to Marry Me?
## Volume One: A Betrothal Written in Blood
### Chapter 12: Are You Jealous, My Lady?

On September twenty-second, Adrian arrived at breakfast ten minutes early.

He didn't hold his knife backward.

He didn't accidentally sit in the duke's chair.

Frankly, he deserved praise.

So when Elena walked into the dining room and said, "You're punctual for once," he thought he was off to an excellent start.

"I've even learned which fork I'm supposed to use."

"That's the bread tongs."

Adrian looked down.

They were, in fact, the bread tongs.

Elena gave a quiet laugh and slid his actual fork across the table.

She wore a practical dark-blue dress with her hair tied by a silver ribbon. Without the formal jewelry and elaborate hairstyle, she looked almost like a student on her way to an examination.

"When do we leave?" Adrian asked.

"After breakfast."

"I was worried the archive might turn us away."

"Hale obtained the reading permit."

Adrian's eyes lit up.

"So we can go straight to Cabinet Three, Volume Seven?"

"Under a researcher's supervision," Elena said. "Not so you can carry it home under your coat."

"I was only asking."

"You asked about climbing through a window yesterday."

"You brought up the window!"

At the other end of the table, Duke Edmund lowered his newspaper.

"Are your breakfasts always this noisy?"

Both fell silent.

After a moment, Elena replied with perfect composure, "Father, my administrative aide is briefing me on today's itinerary."

"I heard him. Does the itinerary normally include forks and windows?"

"...Preliminary preparations, Your Grace," Adrian said.

The duke folded his paper.

"There are plenty of people at the academy who know Elena. Try not to give the newspapers another story."

Adrian was preparing an explanation when Elena stood.

"We should go."

As they reached the door, he heard the duke mutter, "At least nobody's announced a wedding this time."

Suddenly, the morning felt considerably less sunny.

Saint Rose Academy stood on the northern side of the capital. Its main buildings were pale stone, with ivy climbing both wings. Sunlight filtered through high arches onto students in dark uniforms.

The last time Adrian had seen the academy was on the map in *Crown of Fate*.

He'd thought it was far too large then. Every quest required walking from one end of campus to the other.

Standing at the gates in person, he discovered he still felt the same way.

"Where's the archive?"

"West wing."

"How far?"

"Fifteen minutes."

"In the game, you could just click the map."

"Pardon?"

"I said the campus is very well mapped."

Elena gave him a suspicious look but let it pass.

Students stepped aside to greet her as they walked. Some called her "Lady Rosenberg," while others recognized her as a former senior student. Several stole glances at Adrian.

Near the noticeboard, a boy murmured to his friend, "Is that the one from the ball?"

Adrian pretended not to hear.

Elena stopped.

"You don't look well."

"I'm discovering gossip travels faster than a carriage."

"Ignore it."

"You can ignore it?"

"Of course."

She reached up and straightened the small silver rose badge on his coat.

A chorus of poorly suppressed gasps rose behind them.

Adrian closed his eyes.

Elena glanced down at her own hand, then withdrew it with studied innocence.

"I think you may have made that worse," he said.

"Then we should walk faster."

The archive smelled of old paper, polished wood, and the dried herbs used to keep insects away.

Behind the reception desk, a young woman with short brown hair and glasses was sorting cards with a pencil.

"Welcome. May I see your read—"

She looked up.

"Elena!"

"Miss Bell."

"You came back! Why didn't you write? I thought you'd abandoned this place forever."

"I filed a request yesterday."

"That was yours?"

The young woman hurried around the desk and embraced Elena.

Elena looked mildly exasperated but didn't pull away.

Adrian stood to one side, unexpectedly fascinated.

This was a part of Elena's life the game had never shown him.

"And who's this?" the woman asked.

"My temporary administrative aide. Adrian Reed."

"Nora Bell." She held out her hand. "Research archivist. Please, call me Nora."

Adrian shook it.

"Pleasure to meet you, Miss Bell."

"Good heavens, there's no need to sound as if I'm a magistrate."

Beside him, Elena remarked, "You were always rather strict about proper forms of address in the archive."

Nora's smile widened.

"Only with students who insist on handling original manuscripts without permission. Adrian seems perfectly well behaved."

He blinked.

"How can you tell?"

"You haven't even touched the seal on your document sleeve."

"Elena—ah, my lady—said I'd have to pay if I damaged it."

Nora laughed.

"Well, that's an excellent reason."

Elena handed over their permit rather more briskly than necessary.

"Cabinet Three. Volume Seven."

"Right this way."

Nora led them among the shelves, explaining the archive's old cataloguing system as they walked. She knew which cabinets had suffered damp damage, which volumes had been rebound, and which numbering systems had been abandoned.

Adrian couldn't help asking questions.

"Why does the academy keep old judicial records?"

"Academy clerks used to receive training on court documents. When the Judicial Office changed systems, some of the old forms and index samples were transferred here."

"And 'Substitute'? What kind of classification was that?"

Nora stopped.

"Is that what you're here to investigate?"

"Shouldn't we be?"

"No. I mean, it's an unusual subject. Most students can't be bothered to find out which floor that term appears on."

She unlocked Cabinet Three and removed a thick gray transfer ledger.

"Volume Seven. You can read it at the desk. No removing pages."

She set the old book on padded supports so its spine wouldn't crack.

Adrian opened to the index and found a sequence of old-fashioned reference numbers.

Hale had allowed him to bring a verified transcription of his mysterious judicial card. Adrian set it beside the ledger.

The first four digits matched.

The last two had been struck through.

"Found it," he whispered.

Nora leaned over.

"Let me see."

She indicated a faint mark with the tip of her pencil.

"That wasn't erased. Something was added later. Different ink dries with a different sheen."

Adrian remembered using pencil shading to reveal alterations on the palace guest list.

"So the index was amended?"

"This line was," Nora said. "We'd need the amendment register to know when."

"Where's that?"

"Next cabinet."

Adrian grinned.

"You're more reliable than any game walkthrough I've ever used."

Nora blinked.

"Any what?"

"Map. I meant a map."

"You're an odd one, Adrian."

She caught his sleeve and pulled him toward a window where the lighting was better.

Adrian went along without thinking.

Behind them, Elena snapped her notebook shut.

"Miss Bell, aren't you responsible for the safe handling of these materials?"

"Of course."

"Then perhaps you shouldn't pull visitors around by their sleeves. If he stumbles into a cabinet, the collection might be damaged."

Nora glanced at Adrian.

Then at Elena.

"Oh."

She managed to stretch the single syllable into a complete sentence.

Adrian sensed he was missing something.

Before he could ask, Nora produced the amendment register.

"Here, Adrian. Help me with this column."

Elena's fan clicked shut.

"Miss Bell."

"Yes?"

"You're on first-name terms already?"

Nora smiled.

"It's easier than saying surnames every time we look up a record."

Elena stared at the register.

Adrian, whose survival instincts had apparently taken the morning off, ventured a question.

"My lady... are you jealous?"

The air went still.

Nora's pencil froze above the page.

Elena slowly turned her head.

"What did you say?"

"I only meant... do you object to Nora calling me by my first name?"

She looked at him for two full seconds.

"I object to turning archival research into idle chatter."

Nora bent over her desk.

Her shoulders were shaking.

Adrian decided to concentrate very hard on the documents.

The amendment register contained the matching reference number.

It listed **Augustus Wynn** as the original transferring archivist.

The reason for the amendment read:

*Transfer discontinued following accidental death.*

"Wynn?" Nora frowned. "I've heard that name before."

"Who was he?"

"An archivist here, years ago. He died after a fall on the stairs of the west clock tower. The academy classified it as an accident."

Adrian stopped turning the pages.

"He handled this file?"

"According to the record."

"Is there an accident report?"

"I can get the public summary. The full report would require permission."

She fetched an annual report.

Wynn had been found at the bottom of the west tower staircase in early September. No eyewitnesses were listed. The official cause of death was severe injuries from a fall.

Adrian recalled Nora mentioning renovations.

"Was the tower open at the time?"

Nora flipped to the maintenance section.

Then stopped.

"What is it?" Elena asked.

Nora turned the book so they could see.

"The west clock tower had been closed for repairs two weeks before the accident. Work continued until the following month."

"Closed doesn't mean nobody could enter," Adrian pointed out.

"Correct. Maintenance workers had access. We should check the entry logs."

Elena studied the two records.

"But the accident summary still doesn't explain why an archivist went into a closed tower."

"And the transfer he was handling was amended after his death," Adrian added.

Nora's expression became serious.

"I'll check his work records."

They divided the tasks.

Elena compared reference numbers. Nora examined amendment stamps. Adrian copied dates and cross-checked them against the building maintenance records.

Half an hour later, Nora returned carrying a narrow archival box.

"I found an evidence catalogue for the accident. The original item isn't held here, but someone kept a tracing of its evidence bag."

"May we see it?" Adrian asked.

"Yes. Just don't touch the fragments inside."

She spread the tracing on the table.

The academy's registration number appeared in the center.

In the lower-right corner was the imprint of a round wax seal.

Adrian didn't recognize it.

Elena's face changed.

"That's not an academy seal."

Nora shook her head.

"No. It belongs to the Temple of Fate."

"Why would the Temple seal evidence from a fall on an academy staircase?"

"Ordinarily, it would only be involved if the case concerned a religious rite or an exceptional fate record."

Adrian stared at the five-petaled seal.

The execution notice inside the jewelry box.

The old Judicial Office index marked *Substitute*.

A dead archivist.

Records from entirely different institutions, beginning to look as though someone had filed them under a single invisible heading.

"Can we find out who requested the Temple's involvement?" he asked.

Nora turned over the tracing.

There was a space for the requesting officer's signature, but most of the name had faded. Only a single initial remained.

Beneath it, someone had nearly scraped away a note:

*Related to Substitute records. Priority transfer required.*

Adrian looked at Elena.

Her fan was closed. Whatever awkwardness she'd felt around Nora had vanished.

"Miss Bell," Elena said, "we'll need this certified properly."

"I can prepare an application." Nora's voice was subdued. "But once the Temple of Fate is involved, these things tend to become complicated."

Adrian folded his notes into his document case.

He'd come to find out why a decades-old judicial number was attached to his name.

Instead, he'd found an archivist dead inside a locked-down tower and a Temple seal on the evidence from the accident.

Nora stood beside the shelves with the transfer ledger in her hands.

"Adrian?"

"Yes?"

"Are you coming back tomorrow?"

"If the request is approved."

"I can start looking for similar cases in the meantime."

Adrian was about to thank her when Elena stepped closer.

"I'll be coming too."

Nora's eyes traveled from one to the other.

A small smile returned.

"I know."

Outside the archive, Adrian couldn't resist.

"My lady, were you really worried about the collection when you told Nora not to pull my sleeve?"

"Naturally."

"Then why did you fasten my cuff so tightly?"

She looked down.

At some point, she'd quietly redone his cuff button.

"You nearly knocked over the inkpot."

"That was half an hour ago."

Elena stopped walking.

"Adrian."

"Yes?"

"If you want to come back tomorrow, I suggest you stop asking."

He nodded at once.

Two steps later, she added very quietly:

"And call her Miss Bell."

Adrian burst out laughing.

Elena did not turn around.

She simply walked a little faster.
