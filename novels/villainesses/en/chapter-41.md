# Why Do All the Villainesses Want to Marry Me?
## Volume Two: The Saint Won't Let Me Get Married
### Chapter 41: The Saint Is Accused of Leaking an Oracle

On October twenty-first, Adrian Reed finally understood why breakfast at the Rosenberg estate was always served on time.

If they waited too long, trouble would arrive first.

Cecilia sat beside the window, holding the disciplinary notice delivered the previous night. Martha had brought fresh honey rolls to the table.

Cecilia hadn't touched hers.

"Are they taking away your title?" Adrian asked.

"Not yet." She handed him the letter. "I have three days to submit a response. Until the hearing, I'm barred from accessing the Temple's restricted prognosis database. They've suspended my volunteer work at the clinics as well, supposedly to prevent further contact with materials relevant to the investigation."

Elena set down her spoon.

"Jonah's being treated. Mira's safe. Why would an internal inquiry stop Cecilia from doing ordinary charity work?"

"Because the notice puts both activities under the same temporary restriction."

Adrian read the allegations.

The first claimed that Cecilia had supplied a restricted prognosis concerning patient C-19 to House Rosenberg.

The second accused her of disclosing the Temple's conclusion about the blessing to municipal physicians.

The third was simpler.

*Damage to the Temple's reputation.*

"There's not even a date beside the third charge," Adrian said.

"Then they need to specify the conduct," Hale replied from the doorway. "Reputation isn't a fact somebody can establish merely by feeling embarrassed. We'll also need to compare precisely what materials Cecilia shared with what the first two allegations say she shared."

He'd already reviewed the notice the previous night.

Now he placed a table on the desk, listing where Cecilia had spoken publicly or submitted materials between October sixteenth and twentieth.

"You've spent days keeping records," he said. "Perhaps it's time to let those records speak for you."

"I thought they were mainly useful for investigating other people," Adrian said.

"Investigators are entitled to a fair examination of their own actions too."

Cecilia laid out copies of the documents she'd signed.

The request she'd shared with Hale was limited to correcting patient reference numbers.

The medical inspector had received records obtained by the clinic under its ordinary legal authority.

The independent review committee had been given an anonymized examination report.

None contained the actual text of the Temple's protected oracle.

"That doesn't mean I said nothing," Cecilia added. "I told the municipal physician that two patient numbers had been mixed up. That was a medical safety issue. It may also expose a mistake within the Temple."

"You're not going to deny that?" Elena asked.

"No. I did it. Being accused doesn't give me permission to pretend I didn't."

Adrian studied her.

Cecilia normally spoke like a remarkably polite handbook that had learned to turn its own pages.

Today, she sounded much the same.

But her fingertips were white where they pressed against the edges of the papers.

"You're frightened," he said.

"Very."

She paused.

"I entered the Temple when I was twelve. The first time I gave a blessing to someone who was ill, I forgot half the words. The patient's grandmother didn't laugh at me. She told me that having someone willing to stay beside the family mattered more than a perfect prayer."

Elena listened quietly.

"I thought," Cecilia continued, "that as long as I tried to do the right thing, the Temple would understand."

"Understanding isn't the same as a fair process," Elena said.

"I know. It's simply harder than I expected to challenge a place I've trusted for so long."

Martha arrived with another pot of tea.

She noticed Cecilia's untouched bread.

"Miss Saintclair, please eat. Mr. Hale says starving yourself won't make the evidence more reliable."

"When did I say that?" Hale asked.

"I asked whether he agreed. He did."

Hale chose not to dispute her interpretation.

Cecilia finally took a small bite.

A carriage stopped outside.

A messenger from Temple Internal Oversight arrived with a second notice.

A formal disciplinary hearing would be held on October twenty-sixth.

Until then, Cecilia could not use her investigator's authority to obtain restricted prophecy originals on her own.

"They're suspending her access before hearing her defense?" Adrian asked.

"Temporary measures can be lawful," Hale said after checking the document. "But they need clear limits, reasons, and a way to challenge them. A temporary suspension is not a finding of guilt."

Cecilia didn't try to seize her investigator's badge back from anyone.

She asked the messenger to record the exact effective time of the order on her receipt.

"October twenty-first. Nine forty-seven a.m.," she read softly. "Copies I lawfully obtained yesterday don't cease to exist because access changed today."

The messenger looked displeased.

"Your Holiness, Internal Oversight expects cooperation."

"I'm accepting service of the notice," she replied. "I'll answer the charges in accordance with the rules."

He left without another word.

"At least they're not requiring you to return to the Temple immediately," Elena said.

"Not yet."

Adrian noticed the distinction.

Hale examined the appendix cited in the accusation.

It referred to a document entitled *C-19 Prognosis Execution Summary*.

The allegation quoted one line:

*Patient recovered following the completion of the blessing.*

At Graybell Relief Station, Mira had refused to sign a completion statement using almost exactly those words.

"Could they be treating the unsigned statement as a finalized record?" Adrian asked.

"Possibly," Hale replied. "But matching phrases don't prove it's the same document. We need version numbers and provenance."

He also noticed that the temporary suspension barred Cecilia from accessing internal prognoses but was being used to prevent her from volunteering at a civilian clinic.

"Those are different roles," he said. "Her volunteer placement was approved by a charity association and the clinic. Internal Oversight can't necessarily withdraw a medical volunteer's credentials with one general statement."

"I thought all my work had been stopped," Cecilia admitted.

"That's exactly the problem with vague wording."

Elena indicated the charity schedule.

"We don't have to ask Cecilia to visit patients connected to this case while the restrictions are reviewed. But ordinary assistance shouldn't become an unannounced punishment."

Cecilia considered.

"I'll ask the clinic whether they still need me. I don't want to occupy a position merely because people recognize me as a saint."

Adrian almost smiled.

"You might be the only saint I've met who's more afraid of exceeding her authority than losing it."

"Is that a bad thing?"

"No. I'm only worried you'll start asking for written permission before passing the bread."

At last she smiled too.

That afternoon they went to the municipal medical inspector's office.

Dr. Grace Winter had brought redacted records establishing that Jonah still required treatment and that Elliot had been incorrectly identified under another patient's card.

"I'm prepared to speak about my clinical work," she told them. "I don't know who drafted a prophecy within the Temple, and I'm not here to argue theology."

An observer appointed by Internal Oversight stood near the door.

"Dr. Winter, are you admitting the saint told you what the oracle said?"

Grace looked at him.

"She told me there was a mismatch in patient identifiers. A physician needs to know which patient she's treating. She did not hand me the original text of a restricted oracle."

The observer said nothing.

Hale asked that the question and answer both be included in the minutes.

Before leaving, Adrian spotted Mira at the other end of the corridor.

She was dressed in clean clothes, and her short hair was still untidy. She looked much healthier than she had at Graybell.

A red thread bracelet rested on her wrist.

"Mira," Cecilia said, hurrying over.

The young attendant stood.

"Your Holiness. I heard they're blaming you."

"They're examining my actions. Not yours."

"But they have the statement I refused to sign. I don't want them to use it against you."

"You can give evidence if you want. You can also speak to your own legal adviser first. Don't claim certainty about things you didn't see merely because you want to protect me."

Mira touched the red thread.

"I know I didn't sign. That statement claimed Jonah had recovered after the blessing. He hadn't even received all his medicine."

"The medical conclusion belongs to his physicians," Grace reminded them. "Mira can testify about what she saw happen with the records."

She also insisted that Jonah's name not appear in a public defense brief.

"He's back under medical care. He needs rest, not reporters asking whether a prophecy sentenced him to death."

Cecilia immediately replaced identifying details with an internal case reference.

Mira seemed relieved.

"They told me that if I refused to sign, Jonah might lose his Temple aid."

"Essential medical care mustn't depend on false testimony," Hale said. "The clinic and the municipality have lawful ways to secure his continued treatment."

Mira took a breath.

"Then I want to testify. Not to avenge you or anyone else. I want to tell them I didn't sign."

She asked that her right to independent advice be stated in the written record.

Hale agreed.

Adrian realized that Cecilia's strongest support wasn't someone delivering an extravagant speech about her innocence.

It was Mira being able to speak in her own voice.

Something else occurred to him.

"The statement at Graybell was a final draft waiting for a signature. What happened to the earlier versions?"

Mira looked up.

"The supervisor had a pile of annotated sheets. He said the final copy was ready, and that I didn't need to see the previous drafts."

"Did you notice any version numbers?"

"At the top of the page. Two groups of digits. The last one said Revision Three."

"Where did the older sheets go?"

"He put them in a blue document sleeve. Said they were being returned to the Temple writing office." Mira hesitated. "But the relief station's delivery register went through the municipal medical subsidy office too."

Hale looked at her sharply.

"Why the subsidy office?"

"The blessing was being used to claim a completion payment. An accounting summary had to be registered with the municipality."

For the first time all day, Hale looked genuinely hopeful.

"If she remembers correctly, that office may retain formal payment attachments."

"Then they might have the older versions of the prophecy?" Adrian asked.

"They may have version numbers and revision notes. Whether they preserve the text itself is another question."

Cecilia asked whether Mira wanted to accompany the application.

"Yes. But I'd like to sit near the door. If anyone asks me to sign another statement, I want a lawyer close enough to see it."

"That's your decision," Cecilia said.

On the carriage ride home, Elena gently caught Adrian by the sleeve.

"How many times have you stared at Cecilia today?"

"I was thinking that yesterday she could still joke about biscuits, and today she's preparing for a disciplinary hearing."

"So you're worried about her."

"Of course. Aren't you?"

"I am." Elena didn't look away. "I also feel a little uncomfortable seeing how much you're worrying about her."

Adrian fell silent.

"But that doesn't mean you should stop caring," she continued. "When we're on a date, I like being with you. When we're investigating, you're still allowed to help a friend."

Adrian took her hand.

"I'm not helping Cecilia because of the old betrothal."

"I know."

"Does it ever feel like I keep putting other people before us?"

"Sometimes. But I wouldn't like the person you'd become if you looked away every time somebody was treated unfairly."

Her lips curved.

"After this hearing, we'll finally finish that dessert."

"I think I owe you an entire pastry shop by now."

"I'll keep the account."

"Your arithmetic is terrifying."

She laughed.

Their carriage had barely stopped at the estate when a messenger arrived with the medical subsidy office's answer.

Claims dated October sixteenth and seventeenth were indeed registered.

Only the final version had been formally archived.

Whether the earlier drafts had been retained was still being checked in the old intake boxes.

Adrian stared at the receipt number.

Once, they'd pursued a letter written to frame Elena.

Now they were pursuing a prophecy that might have been rewritten.

And the first place that could preserve the alterations wasn't the Temple at all.

It was an ordinary municipal office responsible for paying grants.
