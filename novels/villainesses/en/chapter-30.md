# Why Do All the Villainesses Want to Marry Me?
## Volume One: A Betrothal Written in Blood
### Chapter 30: The Villainess Says She's Going to Pursue Me

On the morning of October tenth, Adrian discovered that only two places had been set for breakfast.

It wasn't that the entire Rosenberg family had gone out.

Elena had asked the kitchens to serve breakfast on the little terrace overlooking the garden.

Two cups of tea.

Fresh apple turnovers.

And a pale-blue notebook placed neatly beside her plate.

Adrian's first instinct was to reach for a pen.

"Another witness we need to thank?"

Elena looked at him.

"No witnesses today."

"Documents from the review?"

"No."

"Has my probationary employment ended early?"

"Adrian."

"Yes?"

"Sit down."

He sat.

Yesterday, the court had formally terminated the treason prosecution against Elena. For the first time in weeks, the Rosenberg estate had passed a night without new summonses, emergency consultations, or anyone rushing to the river docks at an unreasonable hour.

Adrian had slept for eight full hours.

He was somehow more nervous than he'd been on the morning of the hearing.

Elena wore a white dress he rarely saw her in, her golden hair loosely arranged. She had no fan, no court jewelry, and none of the props Adrian had begun relying on to guess what she was thinking.

"Yesterday you promised," she said, "that once the case was settled, we could have a meal together. Without pretending it was work."

"So this is...?"

"Breakfast."

"Just breakfast?"

She looked at him.

"If you'd prefer, you can call it a date."

Adrian nearly put his teacup on top of a turnover.

No reporters stood outside.

No prince was waiting to announce an accusation.

Even Martha had been sent to oversee the greenhouse.

Apparently, an actual date didn't come with an orchestra.

It was simply Elena sitting across from him, waiting for him to choose an answer.

"I'd like that," he said.

She sipped her tea.

Her shoulders relaxed.

For a while, they ate in comfortable silence.

The pastry had cooled enough not to burn his tongue. The jam was exactly sweet enough. Adrian remembered their first lunch in town, when he'd nearly argued against accepting free jam because the baker's wife had called them a couple.

"These are really good," he said.

"Martha made them."

"...I knew that."

"You were about to compliment my baking."

"Not at all. I was going to praise her temperature control."

Elena laughed.

She moved the blue notebook closer, but didn't open it.

"There's something I've been thinking about since yesterday."

"The Temple's betrothal records?"

"Later. This is about us."

Adrian's hand stopped.

"I'm listening."

"On September seventeenth, you appeared in the hallway and told me I was going to die. I thought you were suspicious."

"You nearly had me arrested."

"Because you were suspicious."

"...Fair."

"After that, you kept thinking about running. But whenever someone truly needed you to stand up, you stayed."

Her eyes lowered to his left hand.

"You're afraid of dying. I am too. But you stopped thinking only about your own ending. And I stopped thinking only about protecting the Rosenberg name."

Adrian didn't interrupt.

He'd learned that when Elena had something important to say, the best thing he could do was listen until she was finished.

"I admire that you're willing to admit you don't know something. And that you disagree with me even when you think I'll be angry. You made me realize that help doesn't have to come from a sense of rank or obligation. Sometimes it comes from someone respecting a choice I've made."

She paused.

"I've practiced saying this quite a few times."

Adrian remembered the small sitting room two days earlier.

"So that rehearsal wasn't only for Viscount Harding?"

"What I said to him was sincere. So is what I'm saying to you."

"You can be direct."

"I am being direct."

She sat a little straighter.

A faint blush colored her cheeks.

"Adrian, I like you."

His fork dropped onto his plate with a soft clink.

Elena didn't laugh.

"Not because you saved me in court. Not because Father gave you a job. I like spending time with you. I like arguing with you about ridiculous things. I even like that you can be shaking with fear and still stop to think about someone else."

Adrian knew dozens of routes through *Crown of Fate*.

He had absolutely no idea what dialogue option belonged here.

"My lady, I—"

"You don't have to answer immediately. I'm not announcing an engagement."

She turned over the pale-blue notebook.

The cover wasn't a marriage register.

It wasn't a contract.

It was the working notebook they'd used while investigating together.

"I know you're still trying to understand your past. You don't even know whether you'll be able to stay here. I have my family and my own work, and I'm not going to turn my entire life into orbiting around you. But I don't want to hide behind the word 'employer' forever, either."

She met his eyes.

"So I've decided I'm going to pursue you."

Adrian stared.

"Pursue... me?"

"You can say no. You can tell me it's too soon. We can take time getting to know one another."

Her voice was steady.

Only the fingers around her teacup betrayed her nerves.

"I just wanted you to know that I'm willing to make an effort to be with you."

Adrian set down his fork.

"I'm not going to say no."

Now Elena was the one who fell silent.

"I have feelings for you too," he said. "I'm not sure when that happened. Maybe when you fixed my bow tie. Or when you told me in the greenhouse that I was allowed to admit I was afraid."

She lowered her eyes.

The tips of her ears were pink.

"You're becoming rather good at saying the right thing."

"I mean it."

He drew a breath.

"But I still want to know you properly, and I want you to know me. I don't want a mystery in an archive, one rescue, or a newspaper demanding a wedding to decide the rest of our lives in a hurry."

Elena watched him.

Then she smiled.

"Then we'll start by going on proper dates."

"No contract?"

"No."

"No seal?"

"No."

"No duke standing over us to review my salary?"

"That one I can't promise."

They laughed together.

Then Adrian remembered something important.

"If we start dating, what happens to my job?"

"You keep doing it."

"People already say I get triple wages because you like having lunch with me."

"People were saying that long before today."

"I don't want this to make running the estate difficult for you."

Elena grew serious.

"Are you worried your work won't be assessed fairly? Or that you're only valuable because of me?"

Adrian considered.

"Both."

"Then we'll keep the two separate. Father and Hale will evaluate your work against your written duties. Your wages stay under the existing contract. I won't promote you because you accept a date, and I won't reduce your salary if you turn one down."

Adrian looked at her.

He liked that answer far more than a soothing *don't worry about it*.

She'd offered an actual solution.

"You'd really do that?"

"Being interested in you doesn't mean I get to buy you."

Then her seriousness softened.

"Though if you burn your tongue on another turnover, I reserve the right to scold you."

"I promise to be careful."

"You've promised that three times."

Adrian raised a hand.

"Then put the fourth promise in our dating notebook."

She handed over the pale-blue volume.

"Very well. You can write it."

Adrian took her hand gently.

Elena clasped his in return.

That was when Martha appeared on the terrace, wearing an expression so carefully controlled that it was more alarming than an open grin.

"My lady. Your guest has arrived."

Adrian almost pulled his hand away.

Elena held on a moment longer.

"Which guest?"

"Miss Cecilia Saintclair, the Temple of Fate's special envoy."

Adrian looked toward the notebook.

"The person who wrote yesterday's letter?"

"Yes. She has formal visiting credentials and requests a meeting concerning the historical betrothal record associated with R-074. Mr. Hale has confirmed that she carries no compulsory summons."

Elena released Adrian's hand.

"Show her to the sitting room. Ask Hale to join us."

Then she turned to Adrian.

"You don't have to attend."

"I'd like to hear what she has."

"Then we'll go together."

A young woman stood beside the sitting-room window in pale-white and muted-gold Temple robes.

Her silver-gold hair fell past her shoulders, and her eyes were startlingly clear. She wore no ostentatious saint's crown, only a five-petaled investigation badge pinned at her collar.

At their footsteps, she turned.

"Mr. Adrian Reed?"

He nodded.

"That's me."

She didn't immediately reach for his hand or ask to take a magical sample.

"My name is Cecilia Saintclair. I've been appointed by the independent review committee to examine a historical contract whose date of origin is seriously disputed."

Elena stepped beside him.

"Is this your first visit to the capital in person?"

"Yes. And this is the first time I've met either of you."

Cecilia added, with complete seriousness, "Envoy Horn's report described Mr. Reed as 'uncooperative.' But I read the meeting record. He declined an unexplained escort and sampling procedure, not document verification. I've asked for the description to be corrected."

Adrian hadn't expected that.

"Thank you."

"Accuracy is my job."

Some of the tension left Elena's expression.

"Then what have you brought?"

Cecilia opened a white document case.

Inside lay an authenticated facsimile of an old contract, secured by two protective seals. She didn't break them. Instead, she presented the cover and registration reference.

R-074.

Classification:

*Historical Betrothal Registration — Authenticity Under Review.*

First signatory:

**Adrian Reed.**

Adrian stared at the handwriting.

The final stroke curved inward in the same peculiar little hook he'd seen in the academy's seven-year-old loan register.

"That looks like my signature."

"Resemblance isn't authentication," Cecilia replied. "The handwriting hasn't been verified, and the date is disputed. It belongs to an obsolete legal system. It has no binding force today."

Elena nodded.

"So it doesn't establish that Adrian is currently engaged to anyone."

"Correct."

Adrian began to breathe again.

Then Cecilia turned the facsimile over.

"There's something else you should know."

The second signatory line carried another name.

**Cecilia Saintclair.**

Adrian looked up.

"Yours?"

"Yes. But I don't remember signing it, and I don't accept that it binds me now. I came to find out why someone entered our names on the same historical betrothal record."

Elena studied her.

"So you're investigating your own identity as well."

"Exactly. Which means we may need to cooperate."

Cecilia laid the facsimile on a clean padded support and explained its custody.

"The original remains sealed at the Temple. Nobody here is authorized to open it. Comparing handwriting, paper, or identity markers requires an approved examination with the relevant parties notified."

Adrian pointed to the date field.

Instead of an ordinary day, month, and year, it used numbers from a discontinued cycle-based calendar. The reference R-074 had been added when the material was transferred into the judicial archives.

"Then it might not actually have been signed seven years ago?"

"Correct. Seven years ago is the earliest transfer date we can currently document. We don't have a reliable conversion for the original date. That's one reason this record worries me."

Elena picked up the archive-access application Hale had prepared.

"Then we verify the calendar and the provenance before debating what the word betrothal might mean."

"I agree entirely," Cecilia said. "If someone used my name, I want them held accountable. I don't intend to be held accountable *for* an unknown piece of paper."

Adrian studied the saint.

At least she wasn't here to command him to follow a document.

Not yet.

She and Adrian had the same immediate question.

Why did their names appear side by side on a record neither of them remembered creating?

Cecilia closed the folder.

"If Mr. Reed consents, I'd also like to ask him about any memories related to the contract."

"With a lawyer present?" Elena asked.

"Certainly."

"And me?"

"If Mr. Reed wishes it."

Both women turned toward Adrian.

They weren't arguing over who could take him away.

They were waiting for his answer.

He drew a breath.

"All right. But let's start with where the document came from."

Cecilia smiled, sincerely pleased.

"Wonderful. And one more thing."

"Yes?"

"I'm not asking you to fulfill this betrothal. But if our investigation ever establishes that we once chose each other, I'd like you to hear why we made that choice."

Elena looked at her.

"Miss Saintclair, you just said this has no present legal force."

"That's correct. I'm not making a legal demand."

Cecilia faced Adrian.

Her frankness was almost alarming.

"I simply want to know why another recorded version of me would choose this person."

Adrian had only just survived a treason accusation.

Only just agreed to go on real dates with Elena.

And now the Temple had delivered a marriage document whose alleged participants couldn't explain it.

Elena closed her fan.

"It seems we have a great deal to discuss."

She smiled.

"But there's one thing I'd like to make clear first."

"Please," Cecilia said.

"This morning, I decided I'm going to pursue Adrian."

The saint blinked.

"Oh."

She thought for all of one second.

Then smiled too.

"Then my timing is excellent. We can finally discuss past records and present choices as two separate matters."

Adrian sat between them and set his teacup down very carefully.

For the first time, he had the uncomfortable suspicion that historical archives might be more dangerous than the royal ballroom.
