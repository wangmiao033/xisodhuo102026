# Chapter 250: Room for Someone Else to Pass

Before the repair party left on April 14, Adrian reduced it by two people.

Neither had refused to help. One had just finished a night shift. The other expected to touch the Crown of Destiny. The first needed sleep. On learning that today's work concerned ordinary line separation at a suburban relay, the second put away his bag. Their empty places weren't marked betrayal.

The actual crew included the station's designated technician, workers familiar with the old transfer assembly, two handlers with agreed hours, and an independent examiner. Adrian organized materials and contact. He wasn't there to prove he could do every job himself.

They read the load list beside the cart. Yesterday's chest belonging to another customer wasn't aboard. Replacement supports came from authorized loan stock, accompanied by return conditions. Preliminary measurements still required confirmation on site. Of two apparently identical insulating pads, one had a deep compression mark and stayed behind.

Could they depart one short? They could depart, not call the shortage sufficient. Another willing supplier's material would follow by commercial cart. Nobody guaranteed it would arrive before them.

Luna's comments arrived before departure. The new node housing and accident-associated support mustn't be reinstalled first. Restore ordinary line safety, then verify a redesigned node position without people. Her injured right palm still limited work. An assistant delivered her written observations; she wasn't coming to labor all day.

Adrian had hoped to announce renewed preparations by noon. He crossed out the time.

“She'll think you're unhappy,” the assistant said.

“I am. I can unhappily accept a correct restriction.”

Suppressing a smile, she promised to relay that without improving it into delighted agreement. He asked her to add thanks, so honesty wouldn't consist only of its least pleasant half.

At the station, damaged parts were in custody. The independent examiner checked position marks and authorized limited ordinary-branch repair. The disputed priority order followed a separate channel. Today's permission neither withdrew that dispute nor required an exposed defect to remain until judgment.

The first problem came quickly. Avoiding the old lever's entire travel range would place a new support in the narrow water-cart passage. Returning it to the original holes retained yesterday's hazard. The choice looked simpler on paper, where a line could be moved without asking what occupied the space beside it. Here there were actual walls, actual wheels, and a route somebody needed while the technicians worked. Adrian could see why the worker wanted a straightforward closure. He also needed to discover who would be expected to absorb it. Could they close the path for half a day?

Adrian consulted the route supervisor. It carried water and medical supplies to shelter. The alternative had registered ground repairs today. Drawing another bend on a map wouldn't let wheels pass.

He nearly said only half a day. Yesterday's driver had taught him that phrase often meant its speaker wasn't paying the cost.

Viola found him comparing two plans. Her left arm remained secured; this was a short consultation, not patrol command. She suggested moving a nonstructural storage rack inward and turning the support along the wall, preserving passage width. Technicians must assess anchors and materials. Calling a rack unnecessary didn't authorize throwing it away.

“That adds working time,” Adrian said.

“Closing the path adds time to somebody else's work.”

She held down a windblown corner, waiting for his answer rather than enjoying victory.

He requested assessment of the alternative and withdrew his closure proposal. If no safe position existed, the line would remain unrepaired. Water carriers shouldn't bear their haste. Viola released the paper and sat down.

The storekeeper slowed matters further. Two borrowed gauges remained on the rack's lowest shelf. They weren't scrap. Staff recorded their move to an equally dry temporary cupboard; the keeper accepted custody before dismantling began. The space gained was small: enough for a worker to turn, not for anybody's heroic declaration. Moving the gauges properly took longer than sweeping the shelf clear would have. Yet leaving their custodian with two missing tools would merely transfer another cost out of the repair report. Adrian waited while the keeper inspected the temporary cupboard and accepted the arrangement. The delay belonged to the job, not to somebody being unhelpful.

Checking the wall anchors, the technician rejected the first bent frame in favor of two shorter standard supports. Their connecting plates were on the delayed cart. Someone proposed drilling now. He required examination of actual parts first, rather than making a measuring assumption permanent in the wall.

They did other approved work meanwhile. The old lever was secured safely under authorized control; an independent check confirmed it couldn't move inadvertently during repair. The ordinary branch remained disconnected, confirmed by staff at both ends. Watching them actually visit their positions, Adrian no longer considered this redundant beside I promise it's off.

A handler asked whether he could buy lunch and resume paid time afterward. His supervisor checked hours and arranged a genuine break. Waiting beside a cart on call wasn't rest already taken. Adrian bought lunch too, instead of using everyone's busyness to continue chasing the early completion Luna had refused to endorse.

Viola's assistant stored her light protective gear. The rest room was available for its agreed interval. Bringing water, Adrian was asked to sit outside and wake her only for an actual new emergency. He first handed his contact duties to someone on shift.

“Yesterday you looked as though you were guarding a besieged city,” she said.

“And today?”

“Waiting for a bowl of noodles.”

“More likely to make me hungry.”

She laughed and leaned back. Rest didn't always mean sleeping. Sometimes she wanted people to stop searching her face for orders.

“Including me?”

“Yes. Don't make being trusted an instruction to watch me harder.”

He turned his chair toward the passage. After a while she said that looked conspicuous too. Which way should he face, then? Whatever he actually wanted to see, she decided. He needn't become furniture to accommodate her.

Adrian looked at a horse drinking in the yard. Such an ordinary choice made them both laugh.

The commercial cart arrived a quarter hour late. Its plates passed inspection, as did the last pad. Workers fitted the supports in their new position, preserving a verified gap from the lever's movement and adding a visible, inspectable physical stop. A red line on paper wouldn't restrain iron.

Near completion, an old dispatch clerk brought a demand to restore the original drawing's arrangement. He frowned at the altered route. The next shift wouldn't find things where expected. The technician took that seriously. An unchanged drawing could cause today's repair to be damaged by tomorrow's correct-looking operation.

Adrian asked the clerk to identify the lever from the doorway using the old sheet. He pointed incorrectly and stopped. The small demonstration settled more than an argument. They couldn't leave the entire burden on someone remembering a special warning. The authorized local revision entered the station's working diagram folder. The old page remained historical evidence, not the current operating sheet. Preserving the record of what had gone wrong did not require leaving the same misleading instruction in the hands of the next worker.

Not all incoming staff had arrived. The supervisor arranged physical readbacks by those present and made remaining handover a condition of resuming use. The clerk wanted his superior to receive a copy. Adrian agreed to formal delivery, not treating it as betrayal. Other people used this system. Changes couldn't circulate only among those who believed them.

The final incoming worker noticed the new label would fall into the door's shadow at night. Moving an ordinary lamp to a safe position made it legible. Nobody promised perfect future operation. The next person at least needn't guess in darkness what they'd discussed by day.

Before restoration, the examiner required a complete unpowered travel check. Under controlled conditions the lever moved slowly through its permitted range without touching the new frame; minimum clearance stayed within the approved limit. After one pass, a worker reached for his tool bag. The second check found a slightly loose fastening. It was adjusted and the prescribed verification completed. A good first appearance didn't make the next check an insult.

Only the ordinary maintenance branch underwent an authorized, limited supply test. The new sharing node remained stored. Nobody was connected. Ancient energy and the core weren't in this operation's path. Staff read the starting conditions and stop positions; independent instruments recorded the bounded interval. Actual disconnection and residual handling were confirmed before that section was declared available.

Seeing one inspection lamp steady inside, Adrian felt happy, then remembered this wasn't national safety or a completed pilot. He remained happy. A small achievement didn't have to be denied because it was small.

The stationmaster requested handover signatures. The document specified altered positions, checked scope, the still-disabled new node, and the outstanding old order. Adrian signed for what he'd organized and witnessed. The technician answered for technical results. Yesterday's evidence-preservation request stayed in its separate file, not washed away by a light burning today.

A water cart rolled past without touching the new support. Its driver didn't even know the route had nearly been erased. He merely asked those by the door to move. They did. That demonstrated the retained width better than a special expression of gratitude.

During the afternoon Luna received the actual measurements. The new node position still needed its configuration reviewed. Ordinary line restoration didn't remove her restriction. Adrian sent word that this time he hadn't prepared a resumption hour awaiting her approval. She could examine the material within her permitted session.

After her short final consultation, Viola left with her assistant. Adrian didn't require her to remain for communal thanks. Pointing to the horse, she said he'd looked at it more than at her today: apparently he'd learned the rest-room lesson.

“Next time I'll ask whether the horse wants to participate too.”

“Don't send it an agreement.”

She shook her head, laughing.

Another kind of agreement was waiting for him.

Before he returned to the capital, the liaison brought a reply from a household in the noble rescue network. It offered storage for the next stage. The attached conditions weren't an ordinary loan. Under long-term assurance between the families, the proposed engagement of two adults from the families was tied to the aid, with Elena requested as guarantor.

Neither intended partner had signed. A witness field was already prepared.

Adrian didn't tear it up for Elena or count the supplies as received. He asked that the original reach her, with tonight's possible meeting left to her decision, and attached the actual resource shortfall. An offered arrangement was not a store of supplies already in their possession.

Today they'd left room for an ordinary cart to pass. Next they might need to preserve the same space for a person who hadn't yet answered.
