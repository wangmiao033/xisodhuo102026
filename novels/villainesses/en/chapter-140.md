# Chapter 140: The Fifth Space Has No Name Yet

Luna turned the first drawing upside down and announced that Adrian had studied at least half of it very carefully.

“And the other half?”

“Very carefully, and incorrectly.”

On January 15, her right palm remained bandaged and supported beside her chair in an ordinary palace reading room. The broad-gripped pen in her left hand had produced two remarkably similar crooked circles. Adrian turned the approved drawings as directed. Hartwell brought out the originals one at a time.

There were no stored-energy plates on the table, nor any apparatus awaiting a human palm.

They separated the five newly found column marks. Luna asked Adrian to cover the place names and the personal associations previously written alongside them.

“If I know whom you want to match them to, I may find a convincing reason for the lines.”

“Don't you already know?”

“Yes. All the more reason to cover them.”

Hartwell fetched a contemporary reference book of old registration symbols. It taught clerks to recognize column functions; it was not a prophecy containing five people's fates. Their permitted pages were specified. A damaged corner remained supported in its protective sleeve.

Beatrice Wren joined them. She would establish which lines belonged to the original marks and which might be paper damage or copying errors. Nothing needed awakening.

Luna eyed her magnifier.

“I've learned not to demand a third angle.”

“Then start with the second.”

Adrian finally heard them laugh together.

The first comparison soon produced a problem.

Each mark resembled an open ring with different short strokes. Adrian thought more strokes might mean greater danger. Luna showed him an ordinary storage-location symbol: a ring, lines, and an ink smudge remarkably like a crack.

“By your method, this sack of grain could destroy the capital.”

“I can resign as grain adviser.”

“Keep the position for now. Tell me what's different.”

After a moment, he noticed that the grain symbol's strokes faced outward. The finer strokes on the transfer pages bent into the ring and stopped at fixed positions. Similar outlines, different construction and use.

Luna nodded and had him record the distinction.

The real comparison appeared on the next page. The old book identified five positional forms used in fate registration, distinguished by where their inward strokes lay. A shared overall pattern, five different positions. Such marks could identify categories of imprint records on documentary media. A piece of paper could not assign a living person to one.

Under ordinary oblique light, Wren examined one original page. A fine line beside the third mark turned out to be the shadow of a paper fiber. Changing the light separated it from the ink.

Adrian reached to correct his drawing. Luna asked him to retain the first version.

“Record what changed. If someone sees an earlier copy, they'll know we didn't simply make it resemble what we wanted.”

Hartwell noted the correction's basis. No pencil touched the original.

They compared each drawing with the same-edition reference. The inward turn, ring opening, and starting stroke corresponded. The extra fiber in the third was excluded. These were not five clerks' coincidentally different decorations.

Luna studied her notes in silence.

Adrian understood. The five old records were linked by more than a date. They used a common set of distinct fate-registration marks.

“At least we can say there are five kinds.”

“Five recognized forms.” She looked up. “We've confirmed the documentary classification. Why they appear beside these records still depends on who entered them, and on what grounds.”

She left the identity spaces beneath the columns blank. She did not summon Isabella to remove her gloves.

At the midday break, a maid brought the remaining biscuits from yesterday's batch. The cook had stored them normally, she explained. No second royal catering council had been convened.

Luna picked one up with her left hand and examined its shape.

“This one looks like a border dispute.”

“Her Highness cut it.”

“Then I choose a peaceful settlement.”

She bit off a corner with evident satisfaction. Adrian was still studying the fifth column. She nudged the package toward him.

“It won't fill in a name because you eat one biscuit fewer.”

“I was wondering what she'll ask.”

“Are you arranging everyone's fears in advance?”

He fell silent, then took a biscuit.

Luna did not continue scolding. She watched a sparrow on the roof outside. Her right fingertips moved slightly, then stopped.

“Pain?” Adrian asked.

“A little. Some itching. The physician says that isn't evidence of recovery.”

He asked if she wanted to finish early. After checking the time, she decided to leave the afternoon's observation to Wren and keep only the written review. She moved to a position where she needn't lean forward.

Then she looked annoyed with herself.

“I nearly said there was only one last thing.”

“Last things reproduce remarkably well.”

Her laugh faded into a quieter question.

“Do you find me especially troublesome now?”

“Yes.”

She looked up sharply.

“You were troublesome before. You just used to draw the trouble precisely enough that everyone thought it was a necessary procedure.”

Luna stared, then slowly laughed. Her left hand closed around the broad pen. She stopped trying to prove anything with her right.

“Answers like that don't usually earn a second biscuit.”

“I already took it.”

“Because I permitted it.”

They ate quietly. Setting half of hers on her plate, she said that when her hand improved, she wanted to visit a shop that didn't register reading hours.

Adrian offered no date he could not promise. They could keep the shop's name and ask again when the time came.

She nodded, then added that it wasn't an equipment purchase.

“I want paints. Perfectly ordinary paints.”

“You needn't establish that beforehand.”

“I was afraid you'd ask for a blank control.”

After her final examination, Wren did not immediately close the reference book. From other authorized columns she selected two similar symbols for comparison.

One differed only in a small opening in its inner ring. The other was a reversed form used for canceled entries. Adding lines showing through from the reverse could make either resemble the fifth form.

Luna asked Adrian to cover the headings again and explained each distinction. She initially chose the reversed one incorrectly. Pausing, she asked Wren which side of the original they were viewing.

Wren did not conceal the hesitation.

“The front. The faint reverse impression is separately shown on the support sheet.”

Luna corrected her choice and marked the orientation.

“Keep the first round's notes. I know what I want to find. That's precisely why someone else needs to sit here.”

Adrian separated the sheets without covering her embarrassment with a joke. She glanced up, looking more relaxed.

After that check, the five forms remained distinguishable. The rejected look-alikes stayed in the attachment, so another reader could challenge the conclusion by following the procedure rather than deciding whether to trust Luna.

The afternoon's account was both firmer and more unsettling than Isabella expected.

A contemporary reference supported the identification of five positional forms. The columns used distinct types within a single fate-registration system. Wren confirmed visible lines; Luna explained their documented use. Each signature covered its author's actual work.

Isabella stopped at the empty identity space beneath the fifth column.

“The Old Palace Watercourse. Royal administering office.” She looked up. “Are you omitting my name to comfort me?”

“Because we haven't established that part,” Luna said.

“The first four places connect to things you've experienced.”

“Connections deserve investigation. Turning a connection between records into a physical diagnosis would make the next examination start from an error.”

Isabella's hand paused at her cuff. She left it fastened.

Adrian drew a chair closer, without sitting beside her, and asked whether she wanted to hear what most troubled him.

“Tell me.”

“Someone may already have used this system to assign positions to people. We don't know their grounds, or whether those people knew.”

Isabella studied the five little shapes.

“If the positions are already written, what good is an empty name?”

“It stops us finishing that person's last stroke for them.”

She did not answer at once. Trays clattered in the distant kitchen. Her gaze settled on the ordinary biscuit bag.

“Yesterday, I made six pieces of dough weigh exactly the same. The cook asked whether I feared they wouldn't be good enough, or wouldn't look as though I had decided everything.”

Luna suppressed a smile and waited.

“I thought him impertinent.”

“And now?” Adrian asked.

“I still do. But at least he could ask me directly.”

She left the fifth space blank, without requesting praise for her composure.

What broke that composure arrived half an hour later.

The secretariat summoned her to a provisional review of succession eligibility the following morning. The listed grounds included banquet security, disputed oversight of old projects, and whether publishing missing-person inquiries had exceeded her authority. She could submit an answer and bring one documentary adviser. The specific matters were attached.

The notice was verified through the office. It was not the blocked proxy draft and did not pretend she had agreed to withdraw.

That was precisely why Adrian watched the color drain from her face.

“They weren't waiting for the fifth symbol.”

Ellery stood nearby, waiting to hear what she would bring.

“Everyone involved should attend tomorrow,” she began.

Then she saw Luna's supported hand and stopped.

“The necessary written accounts. Ask anyone needed to explain their own work whether they're willing. Don't turn ordinary staff into a list of my supporters.”

Ellery noted the revision and left.

Luna did not promise to sit through the hearing injured. She would finish the verified account and keep her medical schedule. Further questions could be arranged at a suitable time.

Isabella nodded, still staring at the summons.

Before leaving, Adrian waited for Elena in the outer room. She had come from family business after receiving the news, without entering Isabella's discussion uninvited.

“How is she?”

“Trying to keep events within her arrangements.”

“Difficult.”

“Yes.”

Elena noticed that his scarf had remained tightly knotted all day. She loosened it. This time, she made no excuse about his collar.

“You'd like to arrange tomorrow so no one gets hurt.”

He looked down at her.

“I know I can't.”

“Knowing and being willing to stop are different.”

She settled the scarf and asked if they could eat together before reading the draft reply. He nodded and took her hand.

Back at the residence, Viola waited by the window as agreed. There were two chairs and no map. Adrian sat with her for a while. She told him that during her afternoon exercises she had finally refrained from arguing with the physician about one last movement.

When she asked about Isabella, he described only the review specified in the delivered notice.

After a long silence, Viola said, “Then she should at least be able to walk in safely, and walk out safely.”

She did not rise for a sword. She asked Adrian to leave her the permitted entrances and transport arrangements, so she could suggest a route appropriate to everyone's present circumstances.

The fifth space on the paper still stood empty.

Tomorrow, the princess's own chair might be removed first.
