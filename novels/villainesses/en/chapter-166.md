# Chapter 166: The Vote They Lost

Viola did not return from her examination carrying a recovery.

On the morning of January 30, the physician retained her support and existing restrictions. Brief sitting and walking were permissible if they did not worsen pain. Lifting, duty, and riding remained forbidden. Adrian didn't ask whether she was at least well enough to accompany them to parliament.

“Is today's chair still available?”

“Yes. If I can't sit that long, I'll tell you.”

She put away her medical note without circulating it. Visitor registration began before the debate, she reminded him. He shouldn't be late waiting for her good wishes.

She sounded as though she was dismissing him. With her right hand, she pushed his forgotten gloves across the table.

He thanked her seriously and put both gloves in his coat pocket.

Parliament's outer stone corridor was colder than the street. Isabella entered as an invited speaker, without her old coordination badge or instructions entitling her to command the guards. Elena and Adrian occupied approved observer seats. Cecilia kept an earlier commitment at the relief house; today's vote didn't cancel other people's waiting. Luna stayed home. Her limited technical explanation had already been submitted.

They did not offer their mutual-aid agreement as a model law. It described their own choices, not everyone else's consent, and proved no resolution of the risk.

The proposal was narrow: registration concerning personal relationships must not make selecting a compatibility partner a precondition for ordinary examination. Restrictions and deadlines required explanation, with independent statements and review available. Its sponsor hoped to pass that part before developing subsequent technical rules.

An opponent placed an accident summary on the table.

The copper base, burns, and unfinished inspection were real. Nobody needed to invent a larger explosion to make the room uneasy.

“You ask officials to surrender decision time without providing alternative examination capacity. Staff and instruments are limited. How does the ordinary route avoid leaving danger to develop in the city?”

Isabella did not call every risk an excuse. She acknowledged technical and staffing gaps while refusing to make choosing a person the price of entering a queue.

“And if someone refuses essential information?”

“Explain why it's essential. Don't demand everything private, then label refusal to surrender all of it dangerous.”

Someone tapped the table. Another member asked whether she would personally accept full responsibility, as a royal, for the next accident.

Her left wrist ached as she looked at the summary.

“One person's guarantee cannot replace the capacity you've just described. I cannot promise without limit on everyone's behalf.”

His slight smile suggested he had obtained evidence that she wouldn't accept responsibility.

Adrian tightened his hands around his gloves. The leather had warmed on his lap, but he suddenly noticed the cold at the ends of his fingers. Nothing about being permitted inside the chamber had made the question easier to listen to. Elena noticed without signaling that he must contain himself. She wanted to rise too, but an observer's seat was not permission to speak whenever she wished.

The sponsor requested a factual answer from an affected applicant: after refusing priority, was his ordinary application still being processed?

The chair allowed an answer limited to what had happened.

Adrian went to the designated place. His application remained active, but no complete technical finding had arrived before the old restriction expired. He had declined to fill in an assessment partner, not refused every risk inquiry. Yesterday's expiry notice and his original receipt could be checked.

“So the office did not reject the application.”

“No.”

He could see the next argument coming. He did not improve the fact for his own benefit.

“But if one choice gets an earlier examination, the reason needs explaining. Waiting shouldn't make a relationship decision for someone.”

How long would he wait? He didn't know the full time needed for the technical work and wouldn't invent a number. That was why he wanted specific missing requirements and progress explained, rather than being asked to select someone first.

The answer earned no praise. He had not supplied the neat admission the question wanted, but neither had he given his supporters a complete alternative they could place triumphantly on the table. He returned with that uncertainty still visible. Some thought he demanded too much without offering a solution. Watching him return, Isabella resisted decorating his uncertainty with more attractive words.

An amendment followed. Must not be a precondition became should generally be avoided, with alternative arrangements permitted for safety.

Elena's finger stopped on alternative. The door had acquired another name. It could still close from the inside.

Hoping to salvage something, a supporter asked whether Isabella would accept this version for now. She hesitated. Yesterday she had lost an introduction. If she refused again, the newspapers would say she had achieved nothing.

Adrian did not shake his head across the chamber and compel her to vote for his position. She looked down at her own signed statement and remembered preliminary understanding being crossed out.

“I cannot call this version a solution to the original problem.”

The amendment would be considered separately. The original went to a vote. Counting took little time: more opposed than supported it. Several who had been willing to hear explanations abstained.

Not carried.

It sounded less like a hammer than a lock clicking shut. Members arranged papers for the next item. Someone asked about lunch, as though only a page had lost its place.

Isabella remained standing, her right hand against her copy. Eventually she stepped back without calling defeat a victory in disguise.

Before the departing crowd cleared, a young clerk offered a withdrawal form. Would they dispose of their original application too, to avoid leaving duplicate pending work?

Isabella looked up. Adrian had already accepted the blank sheet.

Its title differed from what he expected. It withdrew an individual's application, not the defeated proposal. The printed reason stated that the applicant no longer pursued the claim. Was signing compulsory?

No, the clerk said. Some people gave up after a defeat. Having forms ready was convenient.

“We haven't said that.” Adrian returned it.

Embarrassed, the clerk explained that he hadn't meant harm. He had copied papers since dawn and still had a stack to deliver. Adrian believed he might simply be tired. He nevertheless asked that declining the form not be marked as withdrawal. The clerk corrected his task note and gave them the address for following their continuing application.

After he left, Elena admitted she had nearly noticed only convenient. Defeat made her want to finish the paperwork quickly, as though one less folder meant one less loss.

Isabella didn't laugh. She too had nearly signed merely to stop people asking what else she wanted.

In the outer corridor, a porter requested space on a bench for someone with difficulty walking. Adrian moved his folder; Isabella collected her cloak. Arguing important matters inside did not exempt them from making room for an ordinary person outside.

They checked their papers again. One contained only the voting summary. Full reasons required separate collection. Adrian marked the missing material on the cover rather than letting a thick folder imply completeness. It recovered no vote, but supplied something specific to do that afternoon.

They obtained the formal result and collection instructions. No new system existed today. Their individual applications and objections had not vanished. Yesterday's expired restriction did not revive because the proposal failed. Any later restriction depended on an actual decision, not a stranger announcing that parliament disapproved.

Outside, a reporter asked whether Isabella regretted rejecting political marriage.

Her expression chilled.

“Report today's vote accurately. Don't supply a price at which someone should have sold themselves.”

He protested that this wasn't an answer. She said she had answered and descended the steps, without promising never to feel regret.

At the corner, Elena swore very softly. Adrian stared. She immediately forbade its inclusion in any formal submission.

“It was precise.”

“Still no.”

Isabella briefly smiled, then asked to sit alone. Elena didn't demand that unity require a shared carriage. Isabella would return in her own, informing her chosen contact if she changed her destination.

By the other carriage, Elena asked whether Adrian was angry that she hadn't risen to help him speak.

“If you had, today's additional discussion might have concerned order in the visitors' gallery.”

“I know. I still wanted to.”

He briefly held the hand she offered, releasing it when the driver opened the door. She didn't take the touch as proof that he was no longer hurt.

At home, Viola had waited a little beside their agreed chairs. His expression told her the result. She had prepared no speech to restore morale. Did he still want to sit?

“Yes.”

She described almost interpreting the physician's question about lifting her arm as an instruction. Her right hand had been preparing to raise the left when he stopped her. Adrian didn't smile initially. When she admitted she needed to relearn her reaction to questions, laughter finally came.

“Someone asked how long I'd wait today.”

“Did you know?”

“No.”

“Then you didn't give him a false answer.”

She didn't say that was enough. Problems remained. One small honest thing needn't be erased with the defeat. Adrian could remain disappointed in the result without rewriting his own answer into a failure of courage. Viola left that thought beside him rather than asking him to feel better immediately.

Before resting on time, she asked about meeting tomorrow. He might need to collect formal material and couldn't promise yet. She requested an answer when he knew, not an attractive but unusable time.

After lunch, a brief notice of the parliamentary reasons arrived. It also requested an inventory of relevant risk-registration material before the office explained its procedural gaps. This wasn't unlimited authority to close facilities. It did make the old ledgers something the next argument required people to count properly.

Returning, Cecilia offered to ask the person who had checked the historical classification what they had actually done. Luna suggested recording missing pages, existing wrappers, and handling order before reassembling everything and claiming it had always looked that way. Her injured right hand still could not operate instruments. Her advice remained on paper.

Adrian wrote those questions beside his collection arrangement. The afternoon counter was still open; that did not entitle him to jump the queue. He would ask how the inventory affected collection rather than wait to be sent away with further notice.

When Isabella came down for afternoon tea, she brought the defeated wording. She wanted it retained, lest someone later claim they had asked for should generally be avoided all along.

“One other thing.” She looked at Adrian. “When you said you didn't know, I wanted to add a guarantee for you.”

“You didn't.”

“I'm learning.”

She pulled out her chair. There was no victory wine, but there was hot tea. Six people still had their own decisions to make about tomorrow.
