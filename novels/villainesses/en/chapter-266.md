# Chapter 266: What She Gave Them Wasn't a Key

On the evening of April 27, Evelyn smelled old glue as she opened the maintenance attachment.

The paper had been pasted to fit a wide drawing into a narrow file suspending printed copies, not to conceal a prophecy. Somebody had patiently flattened the extra edges so later readers could reach the conclusion without seeing how it was formed.

That somebody had been her.

“I remember this part.”

Across the table, Beatrice didn't ask whether they could be saved. She first had the custodian check the volume and deposit signatures. Did Evelyn remember the paper, or the events on it?

Evelyn looked up. No heroic redemption waited inside that question. She must distinguish what she'd witnessed from what she'd copied at a superior's dictation.

A maintenance passage reached the console's rear from the northern equipment room, following a raised service corridor. It didn't enter the core or cross the execution hall's damp side passage. Years ago, she'd seen workers use it to replace a jammed dial. Builders and engineers must establish whether it remained passable.

“Why suspend copies?” Elena asked.

“Circulated maintenance drawings would let local custodians demand independent checks of the console.” She paused. “More precisely, the notice claimed it would prevent unqualified entry. I knew the drawing served other purposes. I signed anyway.”

Elena pressed her fingertips against the table. She didn't need Evelyn to describe herself as wholly without choice. That would steal the names of people still making choices amid danger today.

“Keep both statements.”

Evelyn nodded. Nobody promised a reduction in her eventual responsibility.

The engineer checked scale and revision dates. Useful details weren't a mysterious red line but hinge positions, maintenance stops, and the direction in which the rear cover came off. Two people could barely pass at the corridor's end. Long equipment would block retreat. The northern room had another outside door, its key held by the current facilities custodian.

“We don't need her to open it,” the engineer said.

Evelyn's shoulders eased. She didn't want to become another person everyone must trust before passing.

Viola arrived with a cushion, left arm supported. From a position where she could see the whole drawing, she indicated the final corner. Would departing workers meet people pushing boxes inward?

“Yes.”

They changed the sequence there and then. People entered empty-handed first; equipment followed in short sections, with actual receiving staff outside. Viola would watch that handover, not brace a falling door with her injured arm.

“What can I carry?” Adrian asked.

She looked at him without glancing at his scar.

“Ordinary short sections. When the engineers ask. Don't choose whatever looks most important.”

“I'll want to.”

“Tell me beforehand. I'm good at making people put things down.”

He smiled. She was also admitting she needn't stand farthest inside to bring him back.

Luna's assistant discovered a thinner attachment and carried it to the resting room. During her permitted short consultation, Luna examined ordinary copies and text without touching a patterned medium. Her right hand remained supported; her assistant positioned the lamp.

She found no container capable of holding the kingdom.

She found two separately named circuits: basic shielding, and a drive supplying control movements for reset preparation. Their states could be read at the outer console. A stopping procedure for the preparation drive appeared separately in the maintenance book. It required verification of actual positions, current load, and the appropriate absorber.

A nearby inventory reference listed only small quantities of consumables for localized drive residue during maintenance—not empty storage for nationwide disaster.

Luna tapped the cushion once with her left hand, then stopped.

“Send this separately from yesterday's experiment. Calling both things buffers doesn't mean they receive the same thing. We don't even know whether this equipment still exists.”

Her assistant noted it. Studying the return position, she added that any future delay or stopping of the preparation sequence must not disconnect the basic shielding. Adrian needed to hear that, but not solely from her.

The engineer later confirmed the attachment described a mechanism worth investigating. Nobody would stop anything from an old drawing. The original book and actual equipment required separate examination. The clue stayed on the table instead of becoming an operation supposedly approved tonight. There was something specific to investigate now, with conditions written beside it. That mattered. So did the distance between finding those conditions on an old page and establishing them in the equipment still standing behind the wall.

While copies were returning, Elena encountered Evelyn outside the washroom. The recorder was trying to remove ink from her fingertips. Fresh water hadn't shifted the gray between the ridges.

“You don't need clean hands before testifying,” Elena said.

Evelyn stopped. “I don't want you thinking I'm delaying.”

“This moment isn't the delay I fear.”

Silence held between them. The remembered witness stand had awakened Elena at night. She knew what locking herself inside remorse felt like, and that it couldn't perform today's next right action for her.

“This drawing won't make me forgive you. But I won't ask people to ignore useful evidence merely to prove I hate you.”

Evelyn slowly closed the tap and dried her hands. For the first time that evening, she didn't ask how much more she must do to earn a kind word.

Adrian had left warm water for Elena at the table, not instructions for reconciling with the recorder. She drank and nodded. He nodded back. The blank testimony sheet remained at Evelyn's own place. Neither of them filled it for her. Elena had said what she could offer and what she could not; the recorder would have to supply her own account, in her own words, without a promise of forgiveness at the end.

Elena kept the cup between her hands for a moment. Nothing in the brief exchange required her anger to soften on command. Adrian had left her room to speak to Evelyn as herself, rather than turn a useful drawing into an obligation to be generous.

The harder door stood in another room.

A duty judicial examiner chaired the emergency hearing. The facilities supervisor submitted current custody arrangements; academy personnel explained equipment facts. Oswin's representative argued that prior deposit fell within preservation authority. Isabella sat in the applicant's seat, hearing herself repeatedly called Princess, as though the title proved she wanted power restored.

She didn't correct it. She defined her request: joint outer-console attendance, preservation of local records, and delivery of abnormality reports and operating requests to the responsible authorities. Not five lock keys in her hands.

“If I ask for my privileges back, refuse me. The facilities custodian's objection doesn't disappear because I've signed too.”

The examiner invited the custodian to speak for himself.

Under the representative's gaze, he began slowly. Then he produced the receipt for this morning's withheld duty token. Temple staff still recorded local readings, but the designated independent custodian could no longer see them. In an emergency, nobody could promptly verify whether issued instructions matched the actual preparation position.

He wasn't asking for dignity back. He remained responsible for work he'd been prevented from doing.

Elena submitted today's copied readings, the dismantling position, and physical signatures showing differences in the deposit's scope. She didn't label every discrepancy forgery. That made one clear fact harder to evade: the temporary equipment-preservation notice hadn't authorized ending independent evidence witnessing. The witness position had nevertheless been removed.

Outside, Cecilia waited with the clerk who'd dismantled the paper clip. He feared speaking would betray the Temple.

“I only did what I was told.”

“Then describe what you were told.”

“Will you say I did nothing wrong?”

After a pause, she said she couldn't decide unexamined matters in advance. She could accompany him and help ensure his testimony wasn't reduced to whom he supported.

He eventually opened the door himself. Cecilia followed without a hand on his shoulder or a borrowed brave voice. His account stumbled, but remained clear: once the clip was removed, no place remained for the independent custodian to keep reading.

The examiner closed the submissions and issued an interim preservation ruling.

Oswin's genuine hazard reports remained on record. Core-authority disputes continued; the five locks remained unchanged. The facilities supervisor and judicial enforcement officers would restore joint attendance at the outer console. If necessary, they could enter through a verified engineering entrance and recover the specified selector. Authority extended neither to starting or disabling the core nor to human receiving. Qualified Temple personnel could attend and preserve objections, but removing equipment couldn't be used to defeat enforcement.

Isabella finally released her pen. A deep ink spot marked the page.

The ruling hadn't turned her name back into a crown. Enforcement officers would lead tomorrow's entry; engineers would dismantle and assemble. She would attend as applicant and designated liaison witness.

Oswin's representative required his opposition recorded. Who bore responsibility if disaster developed overnight? The examiner ordered existing safe attendance maintained without unauthorized core changes, and simultaneous abnormality reporting. Nobody could shut real danger outside the room. At last, however, more than one person could speak about it.

Afterward, Evelyn remained beside her old signature suspending copies.

“Anything else?” Adrian asked.

“I thought if some people survived in the end, one less drawing didn't matter. Eventually, everyone who wanted to ask had to pass through me.”

Adrian remembered the delay missing from his own arrow. Dividing everyone neatly into them and us wasn't easy.

“Tomorrow you can identify places you've seen. You don't decide how far we trust them.”

She would remain at the designated witness position, Evelyn said, then return for questioning. A promise she could fulfill, not freedom purchased with a passage.

The northern entrance was inspected that night. Builders found old boxes against the door. Clearing them exposed intact flooring. A broken lamp was replaced with an ordinary working one. Hinges and the return route were examined section by section. The partition between corridor and core remained; people walking hadn't automatically connected any circuit. The supervisor marked the inspected limits on today's drawing. The damp side passage stayed excluded.

Adrian moved empty boxes into the permitted storage area. By the last one, his arms ached heavily. He felt oddly reassured: today's strength had moved things that actually blocked a door.

Elena waited in the yard with two paper-wrapped dinners.

“This route leads to a review office too,” he said apologetically.

“The way home doesn't.”

He approached and asked to hold her again. Setting the food on a temporary table, she opened her arms. He didn't turn the embrace into tomorrow's farewell. Against her shoulder, he promised to return as arranged for the handover.

She laughed softly. “Don't call a date a shift change.”

“Then I'll come back to find you.”

“Better.”

When they separated, night wind stirred the paper wrappers, releasing a little warmth and fragrance. There was finally a usable route to the door. Other people stood along it, with lamps and a ruling they must faithfully execute.

Evelyn hadn't given them a key. She had revealed the face she'd once concealed, so tomorrow's entrants needn't entrust their whole fate to one person claiming to know.
