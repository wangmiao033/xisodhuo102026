# Why Do All the Villainesses Want to Marry Me?
## Volume One: A Betrothal Written in Blood
### Chapter 27: How My Lady Rehearses a Confession

On October seventh, Adrian Reed discovered another responsibility that apparently came with being an administrative aide to House Rosenberg.

Helping Elena find the right words to thank someone.

Strictly speaking, she hadn't asked him to do that.

He'd simply been passing the small sitting room on the east side of the house when he heard her voice through the door.

"I've admired you for a long time. Especially the way you stood up for me when it mattered."

Adrian stopped walking.

Martha replied from inside.

"My lady, isn't that a little direct?"

"What's wrong with it? I'm recognizing his work."

"And what were you going to say next?"

"I hope we'll have more opportunities to see each other. In fact, if you're willing, I'd like to meet you privately—"

Adrian almost swallowed his tongue.

Well.

Only yesterday they'd been arguing with the prince over the fate of a witness. Today Elena was arranging a private meeting with some noble gentleman.

His first thought wasn't jealousy.

It was relief that he hadn't made yesterday's confession about wanting to stay beside her sound any more like a proposal than it already had.

His second thought was considerably less comfortable.

At the society dinner, she'd taken his hand when he asked her to dance. On the carriage ride afterward, she'd suggested they could go out for a meal without calling it an investigation.

Apparently, that was simply how kind she could be.

He began retreating quietly.

His heel struck a flower stand.

The vase remained upright, fortunately.

The porcelain rattled loud enough to wake the dead.

Silence fell inside.

"Who's there?" Elena asked.

Adrian closed his eyes.

Pretending he had just arrived would take more acting ability than he'd ever possessed.

"It's me. I have a report that needs your signature."

The door opened.

Elena stood holding a pale-blue booklet. She looked more surprised to see him than she'd been to see a forged court document.

"How long have you been there?"

"Since 'I've admired you for a long time.'"

Behind her, Martha pressed a hand over her mouth.

The tips of Elena's ears went pink.

"You misunderstood."

"Almost certainly," Adrian said quickly. "Anyway, I hope your conversation goes well."

He handed over the report and tried to leave.

"Wait. Who do you think I was talking about?"

"Well... a noble gentleman you think highly of?"

"Why noble?"

Adrian had no answer that didn't involve the unfortunate fact that his salary was still three times that of a footman.

Martha's shoulders began to shake.

Elena studied his expression.

Instead of explaining, she asked, "And if it were a gentleman, would you object?"

"Of course not."

He answered too quickly.

They both went quiet.

Elena closed the booklet.

"Very well. Come back when you're willing to give me a real answer."

The door shut gently.

Adrian stared at it.

Somehow, he'd managed to be a bigger fool than he'd been when he stepped on her dress.

Just before noon, Hale brought him something far more practical to worry about.

The agreement Leon had shown them yesterday carried a royal secretariat reference number and the liaison mark of a Temple records officer, Evelyn Vale. Under the secretariat's published document rules, an office that reviewed the substance of an agreement had to register the version it reviewed. Merely forwarding a draft didn't require that.

"We obtained the cover reference, not the agreement itself," Hale said. "We can't yet say whether Ms. Vale was an actual reviewer, a messenger, or someone whose department number was misused."

Adrian forced his attention back to the case.

"How do we establish it?"

"Apply for the version-control index, then submit the result to the independent review court. Ideally today."

"I'll go."

"You and Elena should go." Hale glanced at him. "She signed the preservation request from yesterday's talks. She's one of the people entitled to apply."

"She may have other plans."

Hale looked puzzled.

"What plans?"

"A private meeting."

"Where did you hear that?"

Adrian didn't answer.

The lawyer slowly took off his glasses.

"Mr. Reed, I investigate evidence, not the romantic grievances of young men. If you don't wish to accompany Lady Elena, say so. But please don't cancel her existing work arrangements on her behalf."

Adrian's ears burned.

He'd once stood in the Rosenberg sitting room and told Elena that he wasn't her property.

Now he was behaving as though her schedule belonged to him.

He collected the documents and went to find her.

She was opening a letter in the same small sitting room.

"My lady, sorry to disturb you. We need to request the secretariat's review index today. Would you be willing to come with me?"

She didn't look up immediately.

"I thought you were wishing me luck with my conversation."

"You can meet that gentleman first. I'll wait."

"Which gentleman?"

"The one you were practicing your thank-you speech for."

Elena raised her eyes at last.

"You think I'm in love with him?"

Adrian hesitated.

"I don't know. I heard you say you wanted to see him often. Privately."

She stared at him.

Then covered her forehead with one hand.

"Martha, bring me the rehearsal book."

Martha appeared with an expression of heroic restraint and placed the blue booklet on the desk.

The cover read:

**Draft Remarks of Thanks to Witnesses and Research Assistants.**

Adrian went very still.

Elena opened the first page.

"To Viscount Harding: Thank you for agreeing to observe the Crown's review of the evidence in your capacity as an independent public observer."

"Viscount Harding?"

"A member of the Archives Preservation Society. He has a lawful right to attend the hearing. I was preparing a formal acknowledgment of his support."

"But 'I've admired you for a long time'—"

"I admire his insistence that proceedings be recorded." Elena pronounced every word distinctly. "Where, exactly, did you hear a marriage proposal?"

Adrian wished he could turn into an account book and be shoved into the back of a cabinet.

"And meeting him privately?"

"To review the confidentiality measures protecting witnesses, so Matthew Holt's sister's name doesn't end up in the public record."

She closed the booklet.

"Any further questions?"

"No."

"Then why," Elena asked slowly, "did hearing me praise a gentleman make you so eager to wish us well?"

Adrian fell quiet.

This time he didn't want to hide behind the difference in their rank.

"Because I was disappointed."

Her expression changed.

"Why?"

"Because I thought the dance that night meant something. Perhaps you only agreed because I needed encouragement. Perhaps when I said I wanted to stay, you were only being generous."

"I was encouraging you," Elena said.

His heart sank.

"But not out of pity."

The sitting room grew very quiet.

She passed him the draft.

"Adrian, I don't want every kindness I show you to become, in your mind, a noblewoman rewarding an employee. You helped me. I want to help you. And perhaps we can eat together or dance simply because we enjoy each other's company."

"Then you're saying—"

"I'm saying I have the right to decide whom I grow close to. I also don't have to give you every answer today."

Adrian looked at her.

He didn't need to defeat some imagined aristocratic rival.

He needed to accept that Elena had work, friends, choices, and feelings that hadn't yet been spoken aloud.

"I'm sorry. I made up a story about you again."

"Third time, I believe."

"I'll try to keep the total down."

At last she smiled.

"Start by not mistaking my work for courtship."

He offered his hand.

"Then shall we go to the royal secretariat together?"

She looked at it.

"A professional invitation?"

"Entirely professional. When I ask you out for dinner again, I'll do that separately."

"Good."

She took his hand and stood.

Before visiting the secretariat, they stopped at the Archives Preservation Society's public records office.

Viscount Harding was checking the observer list with two notaries. He rose to greet Elena.

"Lady Rosenberg. I received your application. My right to attend has been confirmed, though I can only observe procedure. I won't be deciding anyone's guilt."

"That's precisely what we need. And thank you for being willing to create an independent record."

Adrian stood beside her, hearing the words she'd been rehearsing all morning.

They sounded perfectly natural now.

Harding smiled.

"You wrote that you admired me for standing up when it mattered. You're too generous. I'm only an old man fond of paper."

"Plenty of people love old paper," Elena replied. "Not everyone is willing to offend powerful people for the sake of what's written on it."

The viscount turned to Adrian.

"So you're the young man who caught the impossible date at the palace?"

"Yes, my lord."

"And what have you discovered since?"

Adrian explained the publicly verified accounts and the document version numbers, carefully separating what they'd established from what remained a hypothesis.

Harding listened and had a notary provide the approved format for submitting their final evidence list.

Outside the office, Elena asked, "Well? What did you think of this noble gentleman I admire?"

"Very professional. A little older than I imagined."

"And how old had you imagined?"

"...We should probably hurry to the secretariat."

She laughed openly.

That afternoon, Hale accompanied them as they submitted the version-index request.

The secretariat clerk confirmed three iterations of the royal cooperation agreement. The first had been drafted by royal counsel. The second had gone through the archives committee. Only the third carried the Temple records department's review number.

"That number tells us someone was working under that department's authority," the clerk warned. "To establish whether Ms. Vale personally reviewed it, you'll need the internal acknowledgment book."

Adrian nodded.

He didn't announce that they'd caught the mastermind.

They applied for the missing record and obtained an official receipt confirming that the three versions existed.

The clerk had barely stamped it when a Judicial Office messenger hurried into the room.

"Is Lady Rosenberg here?"

Elena turned.

"Yes."

The man presented a new procedural notice.

"The independent review court has advanced the evidence hearing to nine in the morning on October ninth. Both sides must submit their existing evidence schedules and requests for further verification by October eighth."

Hale frowned.

"It was scheduled for the twelfth. Why move it three days?"

"The notice cites the risk of continued damage to evidence. The court can explain its reasoning, and either side may apply for a reasonable delay."

Adrian remembered the convenient warehouse fire.

"Tomorrow is the filing deadline?"

"That's correct."

Elena verified the notice, then asked Hale to record precisely when it had been delivered.

"We should apply for limited access to the temporary handover record in the sealed western courtroom tomorrow," she said.

Hale nodded.

"I'll ask the court to authorize it. That way nobody can ambush the hearing with unexamined documents."

When they left the secretariat, Adrian almost offered another explanation of how little he cared about Viscount Harding.

Then realized that every explanation made him sound more jealous.

Elena spoke first.

"It's all right to feel insecure sometimes. You needn't pretend otherwise."

"I'm afraid I was behaving like those reporters," he said. "They see a man near you and invent an affair. I did much the same this morning."

"Not quite. You asked me. And when I answered, you changed your mind."

"Is that praise?"

"A provisional passing grade in social etiquette."

"Can my next examination be dancing? I've only just learned."

"You've certainly become more confident about invitations."

Adrian was quiet for a moment.

"Once this case is really over, let's have a meal. No purchasing lists. No account books. No pretending it's official business."

"Are you making a reservation?"

"Yes. You can say no."

Elena nodded.

"I'll say yes for now. We'll decide the day together."

The uncomfortable knot that had followed him all morning finally disappeared.

On the carriage ride home, Adrian looked at their stack of freshly stamped documents.

"No chance we'll be getting apple turnovers tomorrow, I suppose."

"You burned your tongue last time."

"I'll let them cool."

Elena watched him for a moment.

"Earlier, when you said you were disappointed. Was that because you wanted me to like you?"

His hand froze around the file.

The carriage rattled past the clock tower, filling a brief silence.

"Yes," he said. "I've realized I rather do."

She turned to the window.

But when he moved to sit beside her, the document case that had separated their seats was no longer there.

"Don't be late tomorrow, Adrian."

"For the investigation?"

"Naturally."

She paused.

"And I haven't finished my thank-you speech. Once the review is over, there's something I truly want to say to you."

He wanted to ask what.

But she was already arranging tomorrow's records.

For once, he decided to let her tell him when she was ready.
