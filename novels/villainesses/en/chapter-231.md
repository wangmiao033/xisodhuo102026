# Chapter 231: The People Outside the Door

On March 30, the men delivering documents to the academy brought two umbrellas.

One sheltered a man; the other sheltered their box. Watching them change hands on the steps, Adrian first remembered yesterday's tangled umbrella catch. Then the attendant summoned the research supervisor to receive a suspension approved by Oswin.

The corridor lights didn't go out. Students next door continued arguing over who had broken a measuring cup while washing it. A kitchen cart came through the side entrance, leaving wet wheel tracks. The order concerned specified Temple authority records, research loans, and associated external cooperation. It didn't suspend active public protection.

The corridor nevertheless felt narrower.

The receiving clerk wouldn't let Adrian sign for it. He participated in the project; he didn't receive official correspondence for the academy. She checked the issuance register and seal specimen, then asked which attachments accompanied delivery. A separately retained receipt could subsequently be checked. Recognizing the High Priest's name wasn't verification.

When Isabella arrived, an assistant took her wet cloak. She didn't reach for the heavy box with her injured left wrist. Using her right hand, she turned the authorized copy. It cited the failed simulation three days earlier and the recent examination of control-authority documents. One raised questions of safety assessment; the other concerned permitted use of records.

Both had been placed inside one suspension.

“No further copying of the originals. New Temple loan requests suspended. Existing loans held for examination,” she read. “This research project. Not every academy.”

“My timing board too?” Adrian asked.

Whose was it? His own ordinary wooden board. The clerk told him not to surrender it immediately. It wasn't on this inventory. The academy had separate responsibility for deciding whether it should be retained with the failure record. They shouldn't put everything into one box because it was convenient.

The research supervisor then announced that the academy wouldn't schedule further powered operation before tomorrow's joint examination of scope. That didn't mean Oswin owned every academy table, or that the academy considered all his concerns wrong. Independent safety review remained unfinished. There hadn't been permission for a second run anyway.

Adrian almost replied that they had nothing left to schedule. He swallowed it. The supervisor needed the actual situation, not somewhere for Adrian to discharge yesterday's disappointment.

Staff separated ownership on the inventory and recorded the unpowered condition. Independently made new components remained under academy supervision. The cracked specimen and scorched pad stayed sealed with the incident record. Nobody exchanged evidence for permission to continue. The five locks, core, and ancient objects acquired no new authorization through today's dispute.

Luna wasn't summoned to stand outside a door. An authorized copy went to her resting room, with an inquiry about whether she wished to submit comments within her medical allowance. Adrian wanted to take it himself and soften the worst passages. Isabella asked whether the softer version would make Luna believe equipment borrowing could proceed as planned.

He shook his head. He stayed to establish the scope, leaving himself only explanations he could actually give later.

During the Temple loan inventory, an administrator asked a young attendant to bring every box out of a cabinet so they could see everything at once. The attendant reached for the upper shelf.

From her chair, Viola asked him to stop.

Her left arm remained supported. She neither raised her voice nor struck the table with her good hand. “First tell him what he's moving. Are sealed specimens from a failed trial part of his assigned work today?”

The supervisor withdrew the instruction to move everything. Qualified staff would handle the listed material. The young attendant took only the empty trays for which he was responsible. Adrian opened the door for him. A tray had an old chip in its rim. Suddenly he understood how easily a dispute about authority became hurry up, directed at whoever was easiest to order around.

Viola didn't announce that she'd protected every powerless person. She requested the witness schedule so previously invited observers wouldn't travel unnecessarily. She would contact only people who'd agreed to her contacting them; other applicants belonged to their original channels.

Before noon, someone brought Isabella a proposal. Through a royal private-reading request, she could apply to see some material ahead of the project. The route had precedent; it wasn't an offer to steal documents. But material obtained that way might not be available to the independent reviewers. It wouldn't lift the suspension affecting civilian applicants either.

She read the proposal without tearing it up.

Adrian could see how badly she wanted answers sooner. She moved the page into better light and asked whether she could place what she learned on the same table as everyone else's evidence tomorrow. That would require separate permission, the official said. He couldn't promise it today.

“Then I won't call it a solution.”

She withdrew this request into the ordinary project procedure. She accepted that she couldn't choose its reviewers, receive copies not yet accessible to other parties, or surrender another applicant's confidentiality. The official reminded her that the ordinary form required a project contact and actual purpose. Her Highness takes responsibility wasn't enough.

This time she didn't fill the blank with a title.

A civilian who had offered material didn't want his address on a public meeting list. Isabella proposed that independent registration staff retain his identity while the public agenda used a verification code. That wouldn't conceal him from people authorized to check his evidence. The official needed to confirm the intake rules. She waited instead of marking her suggestion approved.

“Does this feel like losing something?” Adrian asked while sorting attachments.

“Yes.”

Her directness defeated the comforting speech he might have offered.

“I could have looked first,” she said, considering the withdrawn request. “Now I wait. People may ask questions in an order I don't like.”

“Should I tell you it's noble?”

“No. Find the page-number list. Nobility doesn't remember page numbers.”

He found the wrong paper first. She laughed and told him the umbrella shop's address wasn't it. Her assistant had left that yesterday. The catch worked, but a repairer should remove the remaining thread properly. Two unrelated small jobs lying side by side finally made the room feel inhabited by working people rather than subjects awaiting judgment.

Cecilia arrived with the lunch and news of notification arrangements. The relief kitchen hadn't stopped its midday service because research had stopped. She had completed the shift she'd promised before coming here. Seeing Adrian with cold water, she offered hot soup and explicitly warned him about the pepper.

“You didn't tell me last time.”

“You asked whether it was hot. Today I've voluntarily expanded the explanation.”

He laughed, then realized he hadn't taken a proper breath for hours. She didn't use the joke to suggest he should trust every arrangement. She asked where he would be this afternoon so she wouldn't save a dinner place for someone unable to come.

He couldn't give a definite time. She didn't reserve the chair. She wrapped bread he could carry instead.

While they waited for a hearing time, a repairer arrived to measure the borrowed clamp as arranged yesterday. Hearing about the suspension, he stopped at the threshold. By the time Adrian reached him, his gauges were wrapped again.

“This is damage measurement, not a trial,” Adrian explained.

“I know. Who authorizes me to touch it?”

Adrian stopped. An unpowered condition didn't answer every permission question. Nor should the suspension leave borrowed property indefinitely unexamined. He described the exact job to the supervisor and asked the receiving clerk to check the order's attachments.

It took time. The repairer asked whether they needed another appointment. He didn't want a morning whose status alternated between unpaid waiting and work. Adrian asked him to record his arrival time. Waiting charges required checking against their agreement; gratitude couldn't substitute for whatever they actually owed.

They eventually confirmed that an external visual and dimensional examination, unpowered and witnessed by the academy custodian, wasn't prohibited. Breaking the incident seals or adjusting the rig would require separate permission. The repairer measured only the clamp jaws. One corner differed from the pre-loan record and needed further assessment. That didn't establish that the whole clamp was ruined, or that it could safely go straight back to work.

Adrian sent the finding to the lender instead of deciding what they should accept. He'd hoped to return something today. Instead, he had one more definite anomaly.

Packing his gauges, the repairer asked whether Adrian considered the finding useless.

“No. I wanted it to be better.”

“So did I. But if I give you a pleasing measurement, the thing caught in that clamp tomorrow won't be paper.”

Adrian changed return on his task sheet to notify discrepancy; await further assessment. He didn't cross the job out or date a decision that hadn't been made. Outside, the kitchen cart passed again, its empty baskets rattling more loudly than before. The research door's closure hadn't spared its wheels another journey.

During the afternoon, the academy and Temple liaison office confirmed an examination of scope for the following morning. It wasn't a court retrying all history. Nobody would connect apparatus in the room to demonstrate courage. Three subjects were listed: independent safety conditions after the failed simulation, retention and copying of records, and the return or continued custody of borrowed equipment.

The confidential identity proposal was also accepted for processing. The applicant himself had to consent and provide necessary information to an independent registrar. Isabella didn't consent for him. His original contact went to ask. Later came a limited agreement covering this examination alone, not recruitment of further supporters.

Adrian's first response wasn't to add the man's name to their joint letter. He struck an unnecessary address from his own attachment and asked the clerk to remake it within the actual permission.

That evening he explained the suspension to Luna. She had read the copy already and asked three precise questions. He still couldn't answer one: if access to the borrowed records was held, could independently verified records already obtained be used by the safety committee?

“I'll ask tomorrow.”

“Write it down. Don't arrive there and become too busy proving you weren't frightened.”

He wrote it. Her assistant put the pen away so her tired left hand wouldn't be asked to work again. Luna looked at his bread and asked whether that contained pepper too.

“No soup. Just bread.”

She took a small piece and told him to bring the rest to Elena. Having encountered more doors today didn't excuse forgetting someone with whom he'd made an arrangement.

Elena waited on the porch beside ordinary rain boots, not the shoes she'd worn to the nobles' meeting. She intended to walk part of the wet road home. Would he come?

He almost said he'd spent all day locked outside. It wasn't quite accurate. Some doors had indeed closed temporarily. Some people had spent the day showing him that he wasn't the only person on the other side.

“Yes. Can we go slowly?”

“I brought boots, not because I wanted to run.”

He collected his coat and went with her into the street after the rain. Tomorrow's examination had a time. It didn't yet have a result. He wouldn't call waiting a victory, or let it cancel the stretch of road they'd agreed to walk tonight.
