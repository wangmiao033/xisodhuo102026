# Chapter 122: I Have No Betrothal for You to Buy Out

Dinner began with a small bowl of clear soup.

Adrian looked at its rim and understood why Luna had insisted he eat beforehand. The palace chef apparently believed affairs of state required very little nourishment.

Isabella sat at the other end of the long table. Seeing where his eyes had settled, she looked toward his bowl.

“Not to your taste?”

“Excellent. I'm checking whether this is everything.”

Elena lowered her face to her soup, shoulders moving slightly.

“There's more. I haven't resorted to starving guests into favorable terms.”

“How reassuring.”

Their brief moment of ease lasted until the roast fish came to the table. The attendants changed the plates and closed the door to the outer room, leaving only one of their number waiting some distance away. The three diners were not seated far apart and could hear one another without raising their voices.

Isabella's document case stood beside her. Rather than waiting for food to produce some magical opportunity, she opened it and handed Adrian an unsigned proposal.

“I'd like a more stable partnership than ordinary sponsorship.”

He read the title first.

Royal Betrothal and Joint Affairs Agreement.

Elena set down her utensils almost soundlessly.

The princess didn't pretend the title was misleading. A public political betrothal would give Adrian stable funding, formal channels, and necessary escorts as a royal partner. Investigating the old apparatus wouldn't mean repeatedly waiting for others to decide which office would accept their evidence.

The betrothal would not take effect that evening. Each party would first review the terms, confirm their agreement separately, and complete formal registration. The proposed relationship had a clearly defined duration. How it would be dissolved when that period ended remained open to negotiation. They would not be required to hold a wedding while the investigation continued.

“You have no current fiancée. This wouldn't force you to violate an existing contract. We can make the conditions sufficiently clear.”

Adrian didn't begin turning pages faster.

“What do you want in return?”

“Public, dependable support. Attendance at necessary functions. Truthful accounts for our investigation. Consultation before political decisions affecting our partnership.”

“Would this protection stop the unfinished accident inquiry?”

“No. I won't promise that.” She turned a clause toward him. “Formal representation and necessary expenses cannot change the facts. If Miss Ashcroft bears responsibility, she must answer. If she doesn't, the cost of repeated journeys shouldn't force her to accept somebody else's conclusion.”

These were things they genuinely needed. Adrian remembered the crooked application Luna had written with her left hand. He refused to make his own rejection easier by pretending that every advantage being offered across the table was worthless.

He read another line before looking up.

“Does this include personal relationships?”

Isabella glanced at Elena without avoiding the question.

“A royal betrothal cannot also be treated as an ordinary partnership one may leave at any moment. Arrangements conflicting with the public relationship must end or change. I would compensate the resulting losses.”

Elena raised her face. She looked capable, at any moment, of saying something that would bring the temperature in the dining room sharply down. Instead, she placed her hands on the table and waited for Adrian to give his own answer.

He laid the proposal flat.

“I have no valid betrothal to dissolve. Those records described as old vows aren't current relationships you can pay to cancel.”

“We agree on that.”

“Elena and I are voluntarily courting. That's not a liability requiring royal compensation.”

Isabella looked back at him.

“I didn't call her a liability.”

“This arrangement places our choices among losses you can compensate. I can't accept that.”

The distant attendant approached to change plates, then retreated at the silence. Despite the warm fire, Adrian felt sweat on his back. He wasn't outside a story selecting a refusal button. This woman really could decide whether many doors opened.

Isabella didn't immediately grow angry. Drawing the proposal closer, she opened an appendix.

“The funding is negotiable. Independent academy work needn't cease. Your friends may be engaged separately, rather than as your attendants.”

“Their work can be discussed separately.”

“We could prohibit compulsory cohabitation too.”

“Your Highness.”

He waited for her to look up.

“Another clause won't make this something I can sell you. I'll discuss an investigation. I won't use a betrothal as its entry pass.”

The dining room fell quiet. Elena's gaze remained on his face, but she wore no victorious smile. She seemed to know that before saying those words, he too had to face all the uncertainty they might bring.

At last, Isabella put the papers down.

“Are you rejecting me, or the method?”

The question abruptly left figures and titles behind. Adrian didn't offer a gentle-sounding answer that would invite further misunderstanding.

“We've met formally for the first time today. I don't know you well enough to promise that relationship. I am explicitly refusing this proposal to exchange protection for personal commitment.”

Her hand stilled on the stem of her glass.

“Most people would ask the price first.”

“You deserve someone who first asks whether they truly want a relationship with you.”

“Political betrothals needn't put affection first.”

“They still require my willingness. I'm not willing.”

After studying him, she turned to Elena.

“Miss Rosenberg, can you provide equivalent protection?”

Elena didn't immediately answer.

She remembered the short track at the tower. She remembered how, in court, she could obtain the right papers but couldn't wave her family crest and erase the problem. Claiming she could offer everything would place her on the same ground as the proposal.

“I can't guarantee equivalent protection.”

Isabella's expression shifted.

“I'll help him with the resources I have. They don't purchase his choices. Even when I'm afraid he'll face danger, I can't demand an unwanted promise to reassure me.”

The words cost more than Adrian expected. Only afterward did Elena's fingers slowly release the edge of the tablecloth.

He lightly touched the back of her hand beneath the table without letting the gesture replace his answer to Isabella.

“If our investigation finds evidence against your interests, what should I do?”

“Tell me truthfully.”

“If you don't want it disclosed?”

“That depends on the material.”

“Exactly why we need a partnership we can discuss and leave. A betrothal makes everyone ask whether I'm giving evidence or defending my fiancée.”

Isabella looked at the title. She'd wanted someone dependably on her side. Now she had to consider how that dependability would change what others believed when he spoke.

“You think I'm buying testimony?”

“I think your method would make even truthful testimony suspect.”

She didn't concede persuasion. Rising, she walked to the window. Palace lamps shone outside. Farther away came faint year-end celebrations. Several carriage-days distant, the research tower's ordinary lamps would be burning too. As for Luna, waiting at their lodgings here in the capital, Adrian suspected she was wondering whether this dinner was worth waiting to hear about.

Adrian didn't pursue Isabella through the silence with another explanation.

Eventually she turned.

“What if I insist that these advantages require the betrothal?”

His throat felt dry. He drank some water.

“I'd regret that. We'd continue through existing lawful channels.”

“Far more slowly.”

“Possibly.”

“And perhaps never find anything.”

“Yes.”

He didn't make refusal a guarantee of success. After those unadorned answers, she asked no third question.

Returning to the table, she closed the proposal and put it in the case.

“I dislike wasted preparation.”

“I'd rather you didn't get someone only pretending to agree.”

She gave a very small laugh, still carrying disappointment.

“You make my terms sound ugly.”

“I believe your resources are genuinely useful. That's precisely why refusing requires thought.”

At that, she paused for a moment. She did not take the document out again. Nor did she call for an attendant to have their carriage held so they could not leave.

“An ordinary partnership, then. Tomorrow we can discuss limited access. You each provide accounts you can confirm. I provide materials I'm authorized to disclose. Funding and attendance obligations are separate discussions. Disagreement won't require an explanation involving betrothal.”

“We'll first examine the scope,” Adrian said.

“You won't even give me an initial yes?”

“I'm willing to discuss it. That, I can give.”

Elena finally drank the water that had cooled in her cup.

Isabella ordered the case removed and hot food brought. The attendant performed his duties with such untroubled composure that it might have been a menu they had debated, rather than a royal betrothal. Adrian found himself genuinely admiring that steadiness. Soon he had sliced meat sufficient to keep an ordinary person alive until tomorrow.

“You may still eat this,” Isabella said. “Rejecting proposals isn't within the chef's jurisdiction to punish.”

“Then I'll keep my promise of sincere praise.”

“After tasting it. I've just learned to dislike agreement before review.”

Elena couldn't suppress a laugh. The women exchanged a glance. It didn't become friendship, but neither was it exclusively as sharp as a territorial boundary.

Dessert was a small apple pastry. Adrian tasted it, genuinely enjoyed it, and said so. Isabella asked that his praise reach the chef, then requested ordinary writing paper for tomorrow's time and entrance.

“The Crown holds old archives. I'll arrange access with their custodian present. I won't show them tonight and make you think I'm raising my price.”

“Please prepare their provenance and verifiable procedures too,” Elena said.

Isabella looked up. “My producing them won't be enough to convince you.”

“I believe that's part of why you invited us.”

Isabella handed over the sheet of paper. This time, she offered no further argument.

Adrian bowed as they left. Against the warm light, Isabella remained standing. For the first time, he saw her briefly uncertain what to say when somebody else was leaving.

Eventually she said only, “Until tomorrow, Mr. Reed.”

“Until tomorrow, Your Highness.”

Once their carriage cleared the west wing, Elena leaned back and released a long breath.

“Were you nervous?”

“Extremely.”

“I nearly tore off the tablecloth.”

“Fortunately, you didn't. Having refused funding, we shouldn't add furniture expenses.”

She laughed, then grew quiet and took his right hand.

“For a moment, I wanted to refuse for you.”

“I noticed.”

“Then I worried you'd think my presence meant you could give no other answer.”

Adrian turned toward her. A passing lamp lit part of the face she was trying to keep composed.

“I knew you'd care. All the more reason to answer for myself. Having you there did reassure me. It wasn't because you made the decision.”

She didn't hide her tightly clasped hand afterward.

He promised no future without temptation or danger. He simply held her hand firmly and told her the betrothal wasn't what he wanted. Leaving the palace tonight, he remained very sure he wanted to be in the same carriage as her.

Year-end bells sounded outside. Tomorrow would bring the archives Isabella would make available. Tonight, the document bag contained only an ordinary appointment.

No signature. No betrothal. Nobody purchased.
