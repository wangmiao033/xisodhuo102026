# Chapter 133: Send My Refusal Along with It

The messenger's outstretched hand stopped in midair.

Adrian did not hand back the attachment. Nor did he seize it. He rested his fingertips on his own blank notepaper, unwilling to let what he had just said become a passing remark someone might remember tomorrow.

“Please record that I deny giving this witness statement.”

Isabella sat down again. Her cloak was already on, its gold throat clasp rising slightly with each breath.

“I never gave this authorization either.”

Ellery asked the messenger to wait and summoned the night clerk. The clerk's first response was to cross out the passage and replace the sheet with a clean one.

“Don't alter it yet,” Adrian said.

The pen stopped.

“If someone asks tomorrow what arrived tonight, we need more than our word that it used to be wrong.”

The clerk set down his pen and recorded the draft's page count and the attachment separately. The paper still bore no signature from Isabella. Its terms, however, would allow the proposed proxy to receive succession notices and submit withdrawals on specified matters. The proxy's identity remained unconfirmed. The attachment had supplied the princess's consent already, even choosing her witness for her.

Her expression hardened at withdrawals.

“Someone wants me to sign this when I acknowledge receipt in the morning.”

“Possibly,” Elena said. “First, let's separate the acknowledgment from the authorization.”

The receipt lay at the back of the envelope, headed Confirmation of Delivery. Signing it would not automatically bring the draft into force. The immediate danger was that continued circulation of the false account of verbal consent might persuade people who had never seen the original that authorization already existed.

Adrian asked where else it was going.

“This copy is for Her Highness tonight. Copies are intended for the council registry and the candidates' affairs desk tomorrow morning.”

“Please return our objection with a request that they stop relying on it.”

“I only deliver papers,” the messenger said uncertainly. “I can't promise they'll stop.”

“Then give us an acknowledgment and ask someone with that authority to answer. You needn't promise for them.”

Isabella glanced at Adrian and lowered her hand from her clasp. She instructed Ellery to contact the secretariat's night supervisor and request a hold on the disputed attachment. Ordinary succession correspondence should continue. No guards were ordered to seal the office.

Only a few lamps remained lit while they waited.

Elena leaned back and stifled a small yawn, immediately turning away. Adrian fetched the gloves she had left on the table without teasing her.

“No more documents when we get home,” he murmured.

“You first.”

“I'll concentrate on getting myself into the carriage.”

“Apparently, supervision will still be necessary.”

She took the gloves, letting her fingertips rest in his palm. The evening had left little room for lovely words. That moment was enough to tell him she still wanted to go home with him, not merely stay until the work was done.

The supervisor's reply finally arrived. Circulation of the disputed attachment would pause; the original and delivery record would be retained. The parties must provide separate statements the following morning while its source was checked. Two denials had put no one under arrest. Neither could those denials now be treated as though they had never been made.

Isabella stayed to prepare her explanation. With acknowledgments of their objections, Adrian and Elena left the palace. In the carriage, he sat beside her as promised.

She did not close her eyes immediately.

“I hadn't finished praising that dance.”

“Tomorrow is fine.”

“Tomorrow will find something else to do.” She leaned lightly against him. “You finally looked at me instead of your shoes.”

Adrian studied her tired profile.

“Because I know you'll tell me if I get a step wrong.”

“I'll still complain.”

“I know.”

She laughed softly and closed her eyes. He left the western room unmentioned, kept his shoulder steady, and listened to the wheels press through thin snow.

On the morning of January 9, several statements lay in a small secretariat meeting room. Their authors had not passed them around.

Adrian first wrote where he had been when the east hall's clock struck, then where Isabella and Elena had been sitting. He did not describe the princess's private wishes or treat his fear at the sight of the blade as a guarantee of reliable memory.

Elena wrote separately. Isabella denied making the authorization and asked whether other opinions had also been attributed to her.

The duty supervisor, a woman with thinning hair, began by retrieving the guards' area records rather than asking which person deserved belief. The arrangements in the east warming hall around eight matched their accounts. The physician confirmed attending the injured there. Medical details were not copied into the succession file.

Verification took longer than Adrian hoped.

The young attendant who had delivered the false instruction appeared at the door, his wrist still supported by a bandage. He tried to bow to Isabella, caught his breath in pain, and hurried to say he would testify that she had remained in the east hall.

She asked him to sit.

“Could you see me at eight?”

His mouth opened. He looked at the supervisor. “The physician was in the way. I heard afterward—”

“Then don't testify to that part.”

He flushed, his uninjured hand clenching too. He looked as though he believed he had failed another simple task.

Adrian crouched to settle the coat slipping from his chair without touching the bandage.

“You've already told them what you saw last night. You don't need to have seen a little more to help us today.”

“But Her Highness didn't go there.”

“Not everyone has to prove it.”

The supervisor called his colleague over and established that he had accompanied someone to collect his belongings. He had not been formally summoned. Hearing that the scene was being checked, someone had casually suggested he answer questions too. She had word passed outside: people without a specific request need not queue, and testimony already preserved by the guards need not be repeated at every desk.

Watching him leave, Isabella admitted she would once have welcomed his offer to testify.

“And now?” Adrian asked.

“Now I'd like him to eat. He keeps looking at the meal trolley.”

Ellery asked someone to confirm the injured attendant's meals, not to reward his testimony with silver. Isabella watched the trolley turn the corner before sliding her statement back across the table. There was one fewer eager witness. The evidence was clearer for it.

“The western room was being searched,” the supervisor continued. “There's no entry recording such a meeting.”

“No entry alone can't prove no one went there,” Adrian said.

The supervisor glanced at him. “Which is why we also have independent records from the east hall. You needn't finish my next sentence.”

Elena lowered her face, visibly laughing.

He finally let the person conducting the inquiry finish her work.

The secretariat had prepared the main draft in its standard form. An urgent communication the night before suggested that Isabella might reduce her public appearances and could consider a proxy. That was not itself an order. The account claiming verbal consent had been added later, when the attachment entered the material awaiting transcription.

The final copyist acknowledged copying the submitted text. He could not attest to the meeting. He supplied the reference for his retained source draft, and the supervisor included its receipt chain in the inquiry. One transcription signature would not bear responsibility for every hand through which the material had passed.

“The source isn't a guard's account of the scene,” she said. “It also cites an unverified oral report. That should never have been presented as established fact.”

“What happens now?” Isabella asked.

“We withdraw the incorrect witness statement, noting the parties' denials and the absence of support in the scene records. The original version is sealed for examination. The draft cannot be cited as an agreed arrangement. A new proposal would require your initiative and the usual procedure.”

“I won't propose one.”

The supervisor wrote Not authorized in the appropriate field.

A delivery clerk arrived carrying the folder intended for the council registry. Hearing that the attachment had been held, he asked whether he could remove it and send the main document.

Adrian stood.

A draft arriving alone might still suggest that Isabella was arranging a proxy and merely lacked a witness sheet.

“Please explain the status of the whole matter. This isn't missing paperwork. The person concerned hasn't consented.”

The supervisor checked the dispatch list with the clerk. Neither copy had left the secretariat, but the affairs desk had already received an advance slip: Proxy formalities awaiting signature.

An informal notice had decided for Isabella faster than the formal document.

She pressed her fingers against the table rather than sweeping everything away.

“Send that desk a correction. Not authorized. Not awaiting signature.”

The supervisor signed it herself, required confirmation of receipt, and had the old copies returned from the dispatch folder for retention. Sitting down, Adrian discovered he had pushed his chair too far back. He nearly missed it.

Elena steadied the chair.

“The papers didn't carry you away. Don't let the furniture succeed.”

He laughed under his breath. Only then did the last of the night's chill leave his neck.

Near noon, the affairs desk's acknowledgment arrived. The erroneous advance notice was no longer in use, and the registered status had been corrected. The notice remained on file, not quietly erased.

Isabella kept her copy without demanding possession of every related communication. The supervisor would continue tracing the false oral account within her authority. They had stopped a process that appropriated consent, not settled the entire succession dispute.

Cecilia was waiting in the outer corridor when they left. She had brought an application concerning her relief work. After hearing what had happened, she asked whether Isabella wanted a passage from public procedure included in her explanation instead of placing a saint's opinion on the desk uninvited.

“Everyone is very keen to ask what I want today,” Isabella said.

“Because someone didn't yesterday.”

The princess looked at her, then nodded.

Cecilia provided the distinction between ordinary proxy authorization and witnessing an oath. It was not a Temple verdict on royal affairs. Once Ellery took it for registration, she asked about Isabella's New Year's Eve proposal.

“You told him he could refuse. Did he know before answering that ordinary access to records would remain available afterward?”

Isabella paused.

“I explained later.”

“Later is good. But people choose with what they know at the time.”

Adrian looked out the window, offering no reassurance on Isabella's behalf. He remembered calculating what refusal might cost him, including things he ought still to have been able to request through ordinary channels.

“I didn't hold a knife to him,” Isabella said.

“Neither did today's paper.”

Elena looked up at the bluntness. Cecilia did not press an accusation. The situations differed, she explained. But a person should not recognize the space a choice required only when someone stole her own.

Isabella stood quietly for some time. An attendant asked whether lunch should be sent for three as usual. This time she looked toward them before agreeing.

Elena had afternoon business. Cecilia must return to her office. Adrian was leaving too.

Isabella did not use the morning to keep them. She asked Ellery for one simple lunch.

At the steps, another note arrived from the secretariat.

This one had supplied no one's answer in advance.

Prince Leon requested a meeting with his sister that afternoon and hoped Mr. Reed would agree to attend. The first thing he wished to clarify was that he had never agreed to become the proxy whose name remained blank in the draft.
