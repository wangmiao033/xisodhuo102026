# Chapter 272: Put Me in the Witness Chair Too

On the morning of May 1, Oswin wrote his name as applicant, not adjudicator.

By the time Adrian reached the independent adjudicating body's reception hall, the request had been received. Someone repaired a broken chair seat by the window. A copper kettle steamed near the entrance. Neither altar nor game's final stage, the ordinary hall denied him the comforting thought that he had simply arrived to face the villain.

The high priest sat at the far end of a long table, old dust on his cuff as though he hadn't entirely left that night's control room. Two separate sets of papers lay before him: the request for public proceedings, and reports on the installation and affected districts.

“I ask for the old system to be tried, and the suspension decision examined.”

“By whom?” Adrian hadn't yet sat down.

“Not with me presiding.”

The recorder asked him to explain fully. Oswin sought acceptance by the competent independent body, with public attendance and verifiable records. The composition of the trial, witnesses, and evidence would be determined by that body. As a signatory to past instructions, he would answer questions. His signature wouldn't admit every unproven allegation.

He looked at Adrian.

“You needn't like me to hear this request.”

Adrian had already said something similar to himself. Sitting, he hung his coat over the chair and noticed Elena moving slightly aside rather than shielding him from the high priest's gaze.

“I'll listen.”

Oswin didn't begin with the goddess. He produced a cylinder consumption report. Could the equipment used for suspension immediately resume its original rotation? The engineering representative answered that the first had reached its usage limit. The second required inspection to establish remaining capacity. An ordinary academy maintenance job was delayed; the affected room remained closed.

“These are the price of success,” Oswin said. “Who else is paying?”

Isabella's brow moved. “That's not a question to hide. Nor does it automatically justify making five people pay with their bodies.”

“Then submit it to public examination. Let people know what you gained and what you didn't. Let them ask why I still defend the old method.”

Adrian had prepared for accusations. Hearing this, he couldn't retreat into the anger of being misunderstood. He didn't want suspension described as a completed replacement system either. Those changing classrooms because a room was closed had a right to ask what he'd done.

Oswin opened another report. No sensational map of universal destruction: a closed road, a warehouse roof awaiting repair, local readings from different dates. There were gaps, unresolved damage, and stations showing temporary improvement.

“I won't claim you caused all this. But you could stop preparation because existing protection kept working. How will you maintain it?”

“For now, existing duty staff, actual maintenance, and evacuation,” Adrian said. “I don't yet have a new method large enough for the whole kingdom.”

A noble attending the receipt briefing drew a sharp breath, as though that sentence were more dangerous than a bad report. Elena turned toward him.

“If you want to know, you should welcome an answer when the question is asked.”

The public needed confidence, he said. Elena didn't debate the word. Did he have a child in the classroom closed yesterday? No. Then he shouldn't decide for those changing rooms what could be omitted.

Her voice stayed low. Adrian nevertheless saw the hand clenched in her lap. Public proceedings meant old noble contracts would face questions too. Transporting a cylinder hadn't exempted her family from the witness chair.

Beneath the table, he lightly touched her knuckles. She looked at him and slowly relaxed her hand without losing what she intended to say.

“My family must answer for its part too. Don't require a guarantee that we'll win before allowing us inside.”

Oswin studied her without praise. Did that mean agreement to publish all technical material?

Luna wasn't in the hall. Her assistant brought a specific objection: public proceedings deserved support, but presentation of raw technical material required arrangements. Dangerous interfaces still in use, unverified interpretations, and assessment participants' private circumstances couldn't simply be emptied onto a table because someone said transparency. What independent reviewers could examine in full needn't be identical to what was posted in the street.

After reading, the assistant closed the statement Luna had personally heard read back. He didn't enlarge her consent.

Adrian studied the petition. He wanted his errors questioned. He also feared another simplified diagram outrunning actual equipment. A beautiful-sounding agreement could still push people behind that diagram.

“Today, let's establish what you seek to have tried. We'll answer how to disclose technology and risk with a workable plan. We won't hide failure behind secrecy. We can't consent to publishing other people's private information either.”

Oswin indicated the old-system papers. “Will you allow me that distinction when you examine my actions?”

“Yes.”

No applause followed the unappealing word. Cecilia lifted her head. Believers shouldn't be dragged forward as defendants merely because they still prayed, she added. Actions taken under supposed divine instructions required examination of identifiable conduct and responsibility. Oswin's demand for openness must allow that distinction to protect his opponents too.

He agreed to leave the matter to the adjudicating body rather than remove it himself.

A cup fell outside. Hot water splashed a messenger's sleeve. People stepped back in alarm, blocking the entrance. Cecilia rose first, asking someone to move the kettle and clear the doorway. No miraculous healing followed. She accompanied the messenger to cool water, asked where he'd been burned, and handed care to the duty attendant.

During the confusion, an observer asked why, if the saint could save people, she always refused to take one further step.

Near the door, Viola gently pushed an obstructing chair aside with her right hand. Her left arm remained supported.

“Because you should say what that step is. If you want her to call medical help, she's gone already. If you want her behind the locks, don't ask through a cup of hot water.”

Flushed, the man retreated to the wall. She didn't pursue him with humiliation or demand declarations of allegiance. She asked the actual duty officer to keep the entrance clear, then sat again.

Listening, Adrian understood that public proceedings wouldn't turn the public into one conveniently reasonable person. Fear, anger, and misunderstanding remained outside, alongside people asking serious questions. All those voices needed a place. None automatically became an order imposed on another body.

Cecilia returned with a damp cuff. She first told the man who'd spoken that the messenger had localized redness and pain and was being cared for. Then she resumed the earlier question, without using that care to extract an apology.

Once she was settled, Oswin offered another reason for seeking public proceedings. Private emergency decisions easily left two incompatible stories. One would say a priest had tried to force five women to their deaths. Another would say nobles had abandoned the kingdom to save themselves. If original records remained solely with the opposing parties, stories would outrun evidence.

Adrian looked at Evelyn. She sat with her representative at a side table, there only to confirm the materials she'd submitted. No executive position had been restored to her.

“I'll answer for what passed through my hands. Keep the originals and extracts together first. Don't preserve only the version that sounds correct now.”

The recorder wrote it down. That earned no pardon. The scope and timing of formal accountability still required separate handling. Oswin requested the attendance of former implementing personnel. Evelyn's representative requested proper notice and opportunity to respond. The chair accepted both, without treating cooperation as permission to bypass her rights.

At the midday break, Adrian went into the courtyard. Elena followed with a small packet of pastries. Nothing expensive; two had stuck together and crumbled at the edges when separated.

“Not to make you braver in there. To keep you from being hungry this afternoon.”

He accepted. Would today's receipt briefing be deducted from yesterday's ten minutes? She raised an eyebrow. Those minutes already belonged to them. No high priest had a right to reclaim them.

“What about next time?”

“When you want another date, ask me yourself.”

He nodded. This time he wanted somewhere genuinely free of archives. After consideration, she suggested the river, then remembered the damaged road. They looked at each other and laughed together.

“A bakery that's still open,” he said.

“Not one where you'll ask whether the oven has an old catalog number.”

Hand over his heart, he admitted his reputation needed maintenance. After laughing, she lightly caught his sleeve. Was he still afraid of people seeing his flawed diagram once proceedings became public?

“Yes.”

“I'm afraid of my name too. But I'd rather stand there hearing specific questions than hide behind another handsome summary.”

He shared the pastries. They finished before returning, without turning every private sentence into a declaration for other people to quote.

That afternoon, the adjudicating body issued its procedural response. The petition for a public trial was registered. Independent composition and the scope of evidence preservation would be settled first. The next preliminary inquiry was scheduled for May 3. This wasn't a judgment and relieved nobody of existing obligations. The dispute over Oswin's core authority remained open. The outer preparation's physical suspension remained in place; litigation didn't dismiss the installation's watch.

Both sides were required to preserve original records already obtained, retain failed results, and avoid treating a copy as the sole original. Protected private information would remain under existing safeguards. A concrete plan for disclosing technology and risk was required before the next inquiry. A sentence welcoming supervision wouldn't suffice.

Adrian accepted his notice. At the bottom lay an empty space awaiting an explanation of which materials, demonstrations, and accounts of risk could genuinely be opened to the public.

He didn't fill it immediately.

When Oswin rose, they looked across the table at each other for a moment.

“Put me where questions can reach me,” the high priest said. “I require you to stand there too.”

“I'll attend.”

It wasn't surrender or forgiveness. They had finally placed a dispute about how other people should live somewhere that didn't belong to their voices alone.

Leaving the hall, Adrian saw the kettle had been moved and the spilled water dried. The burned messenger rested in the shade, a fresh cup on a table corner he could reach without knocking it over.

Submitting a trial petition hadn't made the world just. Someone was nevertheless moving something that had hurt a person moments ago. The next visitor needn't pay the same price first.

Elena waited below the steps. Adrian joined her, told her about the unfilled page, and said he'd first hear Luna's objections that evening to the diagram he was considering.

“Dinner afterward?”

“At the agreed time. We won't stretch one objection until nobody can go home.”

Satisfied, she took his offered hand.

The request for public proceedings had a receipt and a date for the next meeting. Now they had to decide how real technology and real risk could both reach that empty page.
