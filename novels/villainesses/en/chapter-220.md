# Chapter 220: This Time, He Had No Answer

On March 19, Adrian wrote EVACUATE EVERYONE FIRST on a scrap of paper.

Large letters. Very little underneath.

At the academy's borrowed desk, the sentence looked both correct and empty. Yesterday's rescue records showed people who couldn't reach the safe place he'd imagined outside. Yet if he couldn't even propose evacuation, what answer did he have for Oswin?

Elena set breakfast before him without taking the paper away.

“Everyone? Or people in one particular place?”

He nearly asked what difference it made, then heard the problem himself.

“I don't know.”

“Start somewhere you can ask.”

That morning, a civic duty supervisor allowed them to observe a previously scheduled transport drill. It hadn't been staged to vindicate the Temple, nor did it test an alternative to the Crown of Destiny. A northern shelter was checking whether stretchers could follow its existing routes. Training frames carried rolls of cloth. No patient had to take risks for the investigators.

Adrian folded his scrap into his pocket instead of handing it to anyone as an order.

Viola remained in the level courtyard, left arm supported, observing movements. She didn't lift frames or replace missing labor. She checked how participants could stop before asking the recorder to begin timing. Isabella unfolded the current route with her right hand. Luna rested at the academy; yesterday's unfinished pages didn't entitle anyone to another morning of her time.

The first training stretcher passed through the outer door.

A different-sized frame stopped at the corner. Nobody had been clumsy. The doorway wasn't broken. The plan included the narrowest width but not the turning space required by a longer frame. The carriers set down their load and asked spectators to move while they considered another route through the courtyard.

Adrian almost said, Move that table.

The person behind it asked where.

It was temporarily shared by dispensing and registration staff. Unsealed supplies and patient records couldn't be piled on wet ground merely because the table obstructed an arrow on a plan. The supervisor asked the care staff to separate and secure their work before moving the empty furniture. They weren't slow. The delay was nevertheless real and remained in the timed record.

“In an emergency, they wouldn't be so particular,” a young spectator remarked.

“With actual patients, there may be medicines that mustn't be mixed,” Viola said. “Today we have cloth.”

He stopped talking and helped move the empty table. Viola didn't turn one thoughtless remark into a lifelong judgment. She also didn't subtract the delay to improve the figures.

The frame negotiated the revised corner. That wasn't the end of a failure, or the end of every problem. The new route crossed the kitchen delivery entrance. Someone would have to separate arrivals from outgoing stretchers. When a driver stopped in his usual position, two arrows that never met on paper occupied the same piece of ground.

Adrian moved against the wall to clear the turning frame.

His simple instruction supplied neither a person to manage that crossing nor a waiting place for the next stretcher. He couldn't call everyone who noticed a problem an obstacle to saving lives.

An elderly resident watched from a window. Hearing discussion of temporary room changes, he immediately refused. The supervisor explained that this was a route drill, not a move today. Any real arrangement would be explained separately. The man still wanted to know what would happen to the chest containing his family letters.

Nobody laughed at him for worrying about a chest.

Adrian wanted to volunteer to carry it, then stopped. He didn't know its weight or whether he'd be here during an actual evacuation. Instead he asked whether personal belongings could be included in the assessment, rather than argued over beside a waiting wagon.

The supervisor agreed, provided residents identified what mattered to them. Today's visitors couldn't complete the list on their behalf.

The old man looked at Adrian. Was he the man named in the notice?

The courtyard briefly quieted.

“Yes. I've requested corrections to some of it. It hasn't all been withdrawn.”

The man neither welcomed him warmly nor demanded his departure. He didn't understand those documents, he said, but anyone moving his chest should tell him first.

Adrian agreed. Offering help couldn't purchase a stranger's immediate trust.

At noon the record retained both results: the route that hadn't worked as drawn and the diversion that had. Nobody would erase the successful part because an investigator needed a compelling account of costs. The next tasks involved separating traffic, moving work surfaces, and routing different frames, not announcing that everyone was trapped.

Before leaving, Viola asked who would give the findings to the people responsible for the actual rota. The supervisor named the duty clerk, and receipt was recorded. Only then did she board her carriage. Noticing a problem wasn't the same as changing an order, but the results hadn't vanished into the investigators' private folder.

Back at the academy, Adrian unfolded his scrap, added a place name, then crossed out everyone. It hurt, as though admitting limits meant erasing the people beyond them.

Elena asked why he wasn't eating.

“I thought naming the limits would sound as though I didn't care about the others.”

“Pretending you know how to save them won't supply a wagon.”

He heard her exhaustion. She needed answers too. She wasn't here solely to teach him how to become a faultless good man.

“You're frightened as well.”

“Yes. I don't want you hiding me behind a piece of paper marked already safe because you're frightened too.”

He pulled his bowl closer. Eating solved nothing, but he could stop starving himself as proof of seriousness.

The brief afternoon meeting kept yesterday's calamity comparisons separate from today's route inspection. Oswin came to the agreed room, heard their request to correct overstated transit conditions, then asked whether Adrian still proposed replacing every existing protection with immediate evacuation.

“I don't have an executable evacuation plan for the whole realm.”

Saying it was harder than admitting he feared death.

“How, then, do you reject a measure already protecting many people?” Oswin asked.

Adrian's first answer was Because it hurts them. But the houses in the old drawings and the man at the window didn't cease to matter because he loved someone else more.

His silence lengthened. Isabella turned a sheet toward Oswin.

“His lack of an alternative doesn't establish that every designated person must bear this cost. Existing protection, designated sacrifice, and continued concealment aren't one indivisible package.”

“Will you accept responsibility for the consequences of separating them?”

“For investigating, and for decisions I am entitled to make. I won't use the word responsibility to sign an unlimited obligation on somebody else's behalf.”

Oswin didn't explain why every connection was indispensable. He identified dependencies supported by operating records and others based on estimates, agreeing they could be examined separately. Adrian wanted that distinction to reveal a broad road immediately. Instead it revealed more narrow passages requiring investigation.

The clerk asked whether the morning's drill time should be appended to the calamity projections. This time Adrian shook his head before explaining. Cloth didn't feel pain or need its dressings changed. Wagons in a dry courtyard didn't represent every road after a calamity had begun. That timing had a use. It mustn't become a city's escape deadline merely to support or oppose Oswin.

The examiner agreed to retain it as a route-assessment attachment. Oswin pointed out that existing records already showed shortages of transport and temporary accommodation. Asking for more data couldn't postpone every responsibility.

Isabella didn't deny it. She opened the existing resource-shortfall record, not a royal promise that today's conversation would fund the gap. Her lost endorsement hadn't returned. Confirmed support hadn't multiplied. She could make applications for defined purposes, not put her royal surname into amounts nobody had agreed to provide.

“Saying I don't know isn't saying we do nothing today,” Adrian said quietly. “The courtyard route has gone back to the duty staff for changes. We'll keep asking about missing resources. I can't describe that as a replacement for every protection you're discussing.”

Oswin didn't mock the concession. That made retreating into anger harder. Part of the problem he described was real. Adrian had to admit it while insisting that a real problem didn't automatically justify any proposed solution.

Elena returned the morning's diversion sketch to her folder. She didn't finish Adrian's answer for him. Yesterday they had spoken of wanting each other alive. Today that desire still had to endure looking powerless in front of other people.

He couldn't immediately refute the cost of losing protection.

That didn't mean agreeing to surrender Elena. Nor could he pretend the only difficulty was his failure to argue elegantly enough.

Cecilia returned from the meal service and read the meeting summary. Her first question concerned tomorrow's explanation: could ordinary people understand it? She refused to divide the world into investigators entitled to knowledge and believers entitled only to reassurance. She didn't request the publication of unrelated private names.

“We can explain the route problems already found and who is correcting them. Don't merely tell people they must continue believing us for everybody's sake.”

Oswin studied her without promising realm-wide disclosure. Today's agreement concerned preparing an accurate, limited explanation for the relevant desks. Its submission still depended on the responsible offices.

Afterward Adrian sat in the gallery. He didn't seek a private bargain with Oswin that might exchange something for the women's safety. Yesterday's door remained open. Looking at its frame, he finally admitted the temptation to Elena.

She sat beside him without praising restraint as payment for his past mistakes.

“What would you offer?”

“I hadn't decided.”

“Then you certainly shouldn't put yourself on the table first.”

He gave a short laugh. “I know. I'm saying it now because later I might persuade myself it would be easier.”

She stayed until someone lit the gallery lamps. Then she asked whether he'd detour to buy honey. Martha needed it for ordinary baked pears; the household jar was nearly empty. Not a victory feast or a consolation prize. Something genuinely required for dinner.

The smallest jar stood on a high shelf. Adrian asked the shopkeeper to bring it down instead of demonstrating competence on a crooked stool. Elena chose, then asked why he was smiling.

“Someone asked which jar I wanted. For the first time today, I actually knew.”

She smiled too. He paid, secured the jar, and they waited at the entrance while a delivery wagon passed. The street was narrow. The driver nodded; they stepped aside.

At home, Adrian put his evacuation scrap into the working folder. It held more questions now, and no impressive final answer.

For a moment he straightened its folded edge. The large original letters still showed through the changes. He left them legible, so nobody would mistake his first impulse for a plan he had thought through.

He didn't conceal it.

Tomorrow the other five would see it too.
