# Chapter 85: She Held the Bridge, and Remembered to Come Back

By the time the orders were written, the last daylight of November 29 had left the windowsill.

Colonel Ward turned the paper toward Viola. Under the emergency powers in her regional review commission to protect witnesses and case-related transport, she had temporarily assigned eight escort knights. Voss had signed a separate coordination order placing the south bridge duty personnel under the same field command for this emergency.

Viola would be responsible for the bridge approach and checkpoint. She was to clear the civilian wagons first, then prevent the vehicles under investigation from forcing their way out. She was not to pursue anyone beyond the south bridge. Her authority would last no later than dawn the following day and would end as soon as the reinforcing officer took over. She was also authorized to travel to and from the south bridge at night for this assignment, returning with the unit and reporting in afterward. The suspension of the seventeenth yielded authority only within this written assignment. Everything else remained under review.

She read every word, then added at the bottom that she could not carry a shield with her left arm.

“I need a deputy,” she said. “Someone to handle direct engagement and take over anything I can't physically do.”

Ward indicated the veteran sergeant standing behind Owen. He belonged to the regional escort unit and had never served under Viola. After hearing the arrangement, he nodded. He did not ask her to prove how many times she could still swing a sword.

The armorer brought a shortsword suitable for her right hand. It was a temporary issue, the leather on its hilt worn pale. Viola signed beneath its number, tested its weight, and hung it at her waist where she could reach it easily.

Her own sword remained at the garrison.

From beside the door, Adrian watched her fasten her cloak. She finally had a sword again. The first thing she did was secure her injured arm more firmly.

“You stay here,” she began, then frowned.

He waited.

“I'd like you to stay somewhere you won't be caught in the fighting,” she amended. “The bridge approach will be chaotic. Your chest can't take another injury.”

“Bellamy will be checking the vehicles in the waystation shed on this side of the bridge. She asked if I could help identify those civilian transport copies. I said yes. Only inside the shed, and I leave if there's trouble.”

Viola turned to Bellamy for confirmation. The investigator pointed out the location, separated from the interception line by a stone wall, with its own route back into town.

Viola studied it for a moment, then nodded. “There, then.”

“And you?”

“I won't try to do things I can't.”

That reassured him more than seeing the sword had.

Elena came over and tucked the end of his scarf inside his coat. She used her uninjured hand. Her movements were slow, and she was not smiling.

“When you come back tonight, choose a story for me.”

“I won't forget.”

“I'll be at the shelter hall, helping check the bedding deliveries. When you come back, we'll read the most boring one.”

He gently took her hand. “And complain about it together.”

She nodded, then let go first to give him room to climb into the carriage.

The south bridge was narrower than Viola remembered.

The bridge itself had not changed. Wagons stopped in the wrong places, a crooked barrier, and anxious people had squeezed the road smaller. A driver delivering bedding was desperately explaining that he had no document chests. An escort on another wagon held up a military supply pass and demanded that everyone move aside.

The paper snapped in the wind. No one could hear a complete sentence.

Viola did not begin by shouting about traitors.

She sent Owen and two others to open the riverside gate and direct people on foot behind the stone wall. The veteran sergeant turned the civilian teams one at a time, keeping the horses from facing the swaying spearpoints. The checkpoint's acting supervisor handed over the duty log. When he read out a vehicle number incorrectly, she made him point at the plate and look again.

“Move the bedding wagons first,” she said. “They aren't obliged to be our barricade.”

An old woman clutched a pillow she had taken from a wagon and refused to leave her husband. Viola let them go together. She did not separate them for the sake of an orderly line. Once the people had passed through the side gate, there was finally room to turn the civilian wagons back.

Then the supply escorts across from them lowered their spears.

“Keep blocking us and we have orders to force our way through!”

“Name the person who gave that order.”

“Captain Morrow!”

Viola handed Ward and Voss's joint instructions to a checkpoint officer to read aloud beneath the lamp. One young escort knight visibly hesitated at Voss's name. His spearpoint rose a little.

Behind him came the sharp crack of a whip against a wagon shaft.

The first freight wagon lunged forward.

Viola had already ordered the old barrier's connecting catches released. The crooked fence toppled into the empty space beside it instead of dragging the soldiers standing nearby along with it. Her right hand swept down. The veteran sergeant and two men hauled the second, angled timber barricade into position. The driver frantically pulled on the reins, and the horses turned aside.

A wheel sank into the soft earth beside the drainage ditch at the bridge approach.

The wagon behind it jolted to a halt. Chests slammed against the boards. Two escorts scrambled over the shafts and rushed a weak point in the barricade with short blades.

“Hold your positions!” Viola shouted.

She did not use her left arm to catch a blade. With the shortsword in her right, she pressed down the weapon reaching for her and stepped half a pace sideways and back, drawing the man in front of the veteran sergeant. His shield slammed into the attacker's shoulder. Another knight came from the side and pinned him down.

The second attacker slashed a guard's leg before being tripped into the mud. A cry of pain cut through every argument. For an instant, the bridge approach fell quiet.

Even so, Viola's turn had pulled at her left forearm. The wound felt as though a thin cord had suddenly cinched tight around it. Her vision flashed white. At once, she stepped behind a stone pillar and let her deputy take the front.

No one was left without orders.

Owen had closed the side gate and shouted the last two curious onlookers back out of sight. As the pain eased, Viola looked again at the narrow gap between the wagons.

A man in a military supply coat emerged from it.

Captain Cedric Morrow was younger than Adrian had imagined. He showed none of the disarray one might expect of a man facing arrest. He was even straightening a cuff that had caught on the wagon boards.

“Blackwood!” he called. “You have no command authority.”

“For this assignment, I do. The order has been read.”

“Of course you're willing to listen to whoever gives you a sword.”

She remembered receiving her first sword years ago. Her instructor had told her to look at the blade and explain what honor meant. Back then, it had seemed enough to memorize the answer well.

“Produce your orders so they can be checked,” she said. “Or put down your weapon and wait for verification.”

Morrow produced no paper.

He clapped the young knight beside him on the shoulder, ordered him to hold the front, and backed toward the rear of the wagon. The young man took one step forward. He saw the blankets showing behind the stone wall, then the blood running down the injured guard's leg.

He laid his spear on the ground.

Morrow cursed under his breath, lifted the tarpaulin at the rear of the wagon, and dragged out a leather bag.

By then, Bellamy had matched the vehicle numbers in the waystation shed. Adrian pointed to the second line of the carriage firm's copy. The wagon listed as empty in today's reservation still carried the numbered wooden plate that had not been returned the previous day. The firm's recorded plate-replacement charge matched the old plate in front of them, enough to connect the wagon to the same rental arrangement.

“Tell them that wagon must not be allowed to leave,” Bellamy told the messenger.

Adrian tried to get up as well, but the breath he drew tightened his chest. He sat again and steadied both papers, letting the messenger take the one he needed. Someone outside called his name to make sure the people in the shed were still there. He answered clearly.

The leather bag slipped from Morrow's shoulder toward the bridge parapet.

Viola saw that he meant to throw it into the river. She planted her right foot on the long strap trailing across the ground. He jerked at it. Instead of matching his strength, she cut through the end he held with her shortsword. The bag dropped on the inner side of the stone pillar.

Morrow lunged with his knife.

She stepped back, leaving the passage to the deputy who had already arrived. The shield rang. The knife flew from Morrow's hand. Owen and another knight forced his arms behind his back.

Viola did not rush in to strike a final blow.

“Bring the medical staff,” she said first. “The injured on both sides need to be seen.”

Voices returned to the bridge approach. No one was cheering. A driver wanted to know whether he could unhitch his horses. A guard insisted through clenched teeth that he could still walk. A town constable told Morrow to stop struggling.

Bellamy left the shed only once the area was safe. The leather bag was opened before two witnesses. Inside were settlement attachments, a cashbox of small coins, and a private payment ledger wrapped in oilcloth. She recorded where the bag had been recovered, who had been present, and the original state of its fastening, then sealed it again.

Adrian did not crowd around to speculate that one page would settle everything. He stepped outside and sat by the stone wall for a while, first recovering his breath.

Viola saw him and came over. She did not immediately ask about the documents, either.

“You stayed in the shed.”

“I tried standing up. My body raised an objection.”

“It was wiser than you today.”

“Please don't tell my lady. Her opinion of me is low enough already.”

Viola smiled, then winced as her forearm pulled again. He moved over to make room. This time, she sat down promptly.

When the reinforcing officer arrived, Viola handed over each part of the situation, and her authority ended with the transfer. The temporary shortsword was wiped clean and returned to the armorer. Its number was checked against the issue sheet. A new nick in the blade was recorded as well.

She did not find another reason to keep it.

The medical worker Dr. Shaw had sent secured her left arm again. The old wound had not reopened deeply, but she would have to reduce the strain on it over the next few days. Viola listened, then quietly admitted that she had forgotten to allow enough room when she turned.

In the carriage home, Adrian rested a hand on the cushion between them. Viola looked at it, then looked again.

“Is that for me?”

“If you'd like to hold it.”

She took it with her right hand. Her palm was colder than he had expected. When the wheels rolled over stones, she did not grip hard enough to hurt him. Her thumb rested lightly on the back of his hand.

“I thought giving the sword back would be difficult,” she said.

“And now?”

“I wanted to sit down.”

He laughed, and so did she. Afterward, she still did not let go.

Elena waited beneath the lamp outside the shelter hall. Cecilia had just handed over the clean linens needed by the medical workers and was warming two cups with hot water.

As they stepped from the carriage together, Elena looked first at their faces, then at the hands that had finally parted.

“Good,” she said. “At least I won't have to bring the boring story to a hospital bed tonight.”

Adrian started to offer to take Viola's cloak, but she called Owen to help return the heavy protective gear instead. Keeping only her own coat, she went into the warm hall.

When the hot water touched her lips, she finally let out a long breath.

“I'd like something sweet.”

Elena took a cake from a paper bag and placed it beside her. “No one expects you to hold a second bridge for this one.”

Viola took it and bent her head to bite.

Outside, the sealed leather bag was carried to the town offices. Inside, she ate half the cake and kept her hands around her cup. For the first time, she felt that coming back and sitting here was something she had protected, too.

A little later, Elena brought the travel book over and placed it on Adrian's knees.

“And the story you were choosing?”

He turned a few pages until he found a chapter about a traveler having his boot soles repaired. The first three paragraphs complained about the cobbler's prices. By the fourth, the boots were still not fixed.

Elena looked deeply satisfied.

“It really is boring.”

“There's another six pages.”

“Wonderful.”

She pulled her chair closer, letting her shoulder brush his. Adrian looked at her and stopped reading. Eventually, she asked why.

“I wanted to look at you.”

Color slowly rose in her cheeks in the lamplight, but she did not look away.

“While you were at the bridge, I kept thinking that if you traded yourself for something important again, this time I would be furious.”

“I didn't.”

“Which is why I would very much like to kiss you now.”

For a moment, he forgot the book in his hands. Then he nodded, quite seriously. Elena leaned over and kissed his cheek, lightly, before returning to her place and trying to look as though she had merely been checking the paper.

“Go on. The boot soles.”

Adrian looked down at the book. He got through two lines before realizing that he had started reading the previous page again.

At the next table, Cecilia made no pretense of having missed it. Stirring her hot drink, she frankly admitted to a little envy, then asked the landlady whether there was any honey left.

Viola raised her cup a little higher to hide the smile she could not control. She envied the courage it took to say what one wanted. And she was glad that tonight, there was already one thing she had truly wanted to do and had done.

The warmth of that hand on the journey back still lingered in her palm.

She set her right hand beside the cup instead of hiding it in her sleeve.

Outside, footsteps marked the changing of the guard. Ward sent for the list of those guarding the sealed evidence so she could check it. At the same time, she received an acknowledgment from the regional post station. The request for a transfer of jurisdiction she had sent to the capital through the regional military office on the twenty-second had received a formal reply. A special envoy was already on the way.

This time, the answer had to come from beyond Greymarch.
