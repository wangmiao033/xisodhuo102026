# Why Do All the Villainesses Want to Marry Me?
## Volume Two: The Saint Won't Let Me Get Married
### Chapter 50: Nobody Gets to Decide the Saint Should Hide

On the morning of October thirtieth, Adrian discovered that his desk had been divided into two territories.

On the left lay an application Elena had brought back from the Capital Bar Association: a request to suspend the risk demonstration scheduled for October thirty-first in the inner courtyard.

On the right was Cecilia's handwritten proposal: preserve the ordinary memorial service and public blessings, but halt any demonstration involving linked personal marks without consent.

Both documents were carefully argued. Unfortunately, they weren't asking for quite the same thing.

"Which one are you going to sign?" Elena asked.

"Could I have breakfast first?"

Cecilia took a seat beside the desk. "You didn't sleep well. Of course you can eat."

Elena pushed a cup of warm milk toward him.

"I'm not demanding an answer this instant. But if we want a formal injunction before tomorrow, we have to file today."

"And I'm concerned that canceling the entire event would leave families without the memorial service they've already arranged to attend," Cecilia said.

Adrian looked from one to the other. For once, neither was arguing that she had a better right to protect him. They had examined the same danger and arrived at two different, practical responses.

"Then let's hear both," he said. "Without starting from the assumption that the other person doesn't care about the patients."

Elena opened her application.

"The transfer ring came from the Old Contract Records Office. It's connected to the addition of Cecilia's name to a risk list for that same day. Until an independent technical assessment is complete, there's no justification for letting her near the central apparatus. The joint municipal and Temple safety rules allow a public demonstration to be suspended when it presents a serious hazard."

"I agree about suspending the linked-mark demonstration," Cecilia said. "But laying flowers for the dead and offering blessings for the living don't require that ring. We could even move those parts somewhere else."

"What if the organizers refuse to separate them?"

"Then we apply to suspend the whole event."

Elena paused. She hadn't realized Cecilia had already considered that possibility.

Hale knocked and came in. After hearing them out, he delivered a lawyer's solution.

"Both proposals can form part of a single, conditional safety application."

"Meaning?" Adrian asked.

"First, we request the removal of the disputed equipment demonstration while preserving activities that don't involve it. If the ring can't be independently isolated, or voluntary participation can't be established, we request suspension of the entire ceremony."

Adrian nodded. "Better than telling her to hide."

"I have another condition," Elena said. "Cecilia shouldn't go there alone tomorrow."

"I wasn't planning to."

"But you do want to go?"

"I want to understand how the event was arranged. I'll decide whether to take part with independent witnesses present. Nobody gets to drag me in front of that lamp because I'm a saint."

Adrian raised his head. "Do I get a say?"

Both women turned to him.

"Let's stop deciding who has to run away. We can obtain the equipment registration, the organizers' agreement to the safeguards, and the safety inspection results. If any one of those is missing, none of us joins the risk demonstration tomorrow."

Elena considered that. "Will you promise not to investigate the equipment on your own?"

"Yes."

Cecilia had a different question. "Suppose the independent technician judges the risk to be low, but I still don't want to participate?"

"Then you don't."

"And if I do want to?"

Adrian tightened his grip on his cup.

"I'll tell you why I'm worried. I won't forge your signature or use an old betrothal to stop you. I want you to hear what the risks are before you choose."

Cecilia nodded slowly. "That's what I needed to hear."

There was another reason she was reluctant to leave the capital. Mercy Clinic had arranged free consultations for bereaved families on the day of the memorial. The Temple had promised volunteers familiar with the lower district to help.

"If I disappear, it might go ahead," she explained. "Or it might be canceled because nobody knows who's coordinating it. Those families have already made time to come."

This time, Elena didn't tell her to send someone else.

"Which part do you actually want to do yourself?"

"Make sure those families have someone with them. I don't want to preside over a dangerous ritual."

"Then we separate those jobs. My family can lend a safe room for the consultations. The clinic supplies the professionals, and the volunteers decide for themselves whether to help."

Hale confirmed that ordinary charitable work required neither an ancient transfer ring nor a religious authority registering participants as risk bearers.

Watching them rearrange the schedule, Adrian realized protection could mean more than keeping someone away from danger. It could also mean making sure she was still able to do the work she cared about.

Before noon, they submitted the conditional application to the event administration office. A reviewer established that the old linked-mark transfer ring wasn't an essential part of the ordinary ceremony. The Old Contract Records Office had requested it as an additional demonstration.

"Can you suspend just that addition?" Elena asked.

"In principle," the administrator said. "But the department responsible for the equipment refuses to withdraw it. They say it's part of preserving our traditions."

Acting for Cecilia, Vera requested an independent inspection and temporary impoundment of the ring. At a minimum, someone needed to establish whether it was connected to mark samples belonging to living people.

The administrator looked uncomfortable. "That requires agreement from the Maintenance and Settlement Coordination Group."

An idea occurred to Adrian.

"It's equipment intended for public display. Could an independent fire and facilities technician inspect the circuits, the mounting frame, and the magical-medium connections? Even without permission to examine the internal runes, they could check for a connection to samples from a person."

Hale didn't immediately congratulate him.

"An authorized technician would have to make that determination."

"Then let's ask a technician. Please don't make me take it apart."

Elena laughed. "You've finally lost the urge to break into equipment rooms?"

"I'm worried about my wages."

That earned a smile from Cecilia, too.

She proposed a temporary replacement: a wooden model that couldn't generate personal data of any kind.

"If we're holding this service to remember the dead, why do we need a device that can affect the marks of the living?"

The administrator had no answer. Elena didn't make him suffer for it; she simply asked for the alternative to be formally submitted alongside their safety concerns.

As they left, an elderly man arranging the flower beds looked up.

"You remember the dead by remembering their names," he said. "Not by making the lights prettier."

Adrian glanced back at him. Sometimes an ordinary person understood the point of a ceremony better than an entire committee.

That afternoon, the Rosenberg estate and the Temple independent review committee jointly commissioned a technical inspector to examine the equipment's publicly available specifications. He inspected only the authorized external structures and left the sealed core untouched.

There were two versions of the wiring diagram. The first published version showed a single connection to a blank medium. A later supplement added a second port.

"Which version are they actually planning to use?" Adrian asked.

"That has to be established from the equipment itself and compliance testing. My authorization today covers the presence of the connections, not opening the core seals."

His external inspection confirmed two input ports. One connected to the blank medium used for demonstrations. The other could accept an old-style sealed plate bearing a personal mark.

"That could be intended as a teaching comparison," he said. "It could also connect to a specific person's registration. With the core sealed, I can't establish its actual purpose."

"So at the very least, its safety hasn't been demonstrated," Elena said.

"And the second port alone doesn't establish that someone intended to harm Cecilia," Hale reminded them.

They agreed on the wording: *Purpose undetermined; conditions for safety not independently verified.*

That was enough to invoke the rules for postponing a demonstration. The application went straight to the event committee.

By evening, the only reply was:

*Coordination with the relevant departments is ongoing.*

Adrian stared at the sentence with a familiar sense of helplessness. Every time an important document went unanswered, he wanted to march off and hammer on some official's door.

Instead, he requested an acknowledgment of receipt, checked the decision deadline, and made sure they had a backup plan for tomorrow.

Cecilia went to Mercy Clinic to see Mira. Ordinary charitable volunteering had been restored to her permitted activities. As long as she followed the clinic supervisor's directions and stayed out of the old oracle database, the visit breached no restriction.

Mira was braiding a red thread bracelet for a little girl.

"Will you be ringing the bell tomorrow?"

"If the ceremony is safe, I may go and lay flowers. But finding my name on a list doesn't mean I've agreed to magic I don't understand."

"What if they tell you to sign?"

"I'll read it first."

Mira smiled. "You're sounding more like Mr. Reed every day."

Cecilia gave that serious thought.

"He helped me realize that 'no' can be a complete answer."

On the carriage ride back to the estate, Elena sat beside her.

"I was too forceful this morning," she said suddenly.

"So was I."

"It wasn't quite the same. I realized I'd started treating you as someone I needed to protect without first asking how you wanted to face the danger."

Cecilia looked at her.

"And I nearly insisted on going into the inner courtyard to see everything for myself, despite your warnings. I was so afraid of losing the relief work."

"At least we know what we're each afraid of."

"Yes."

Adrian, sitting opposite, felt compelled to contribute.

"My greatest fear is that you'll settle everything and leave me carrying three different versions of the application."

Elena laughed and took half the papers from his lap. Cecilia claimed the rest.

He looked down at his empty hands. It was almost moving.

At dinner, Elena specifically asked Martha to set three places.

"No investigative conference tonight," she announced. "Just dinner."

Cecilia looked at the soup. "So I don't have to discuss the law?"

"You don't."

"Or betrothals," Adrian added quickly.

"Agreed," Cecilia said.

The silence lasted about ten seconds.

"If someone wants to tell another person she admires him," Cecilia asked, "how can she make sure it doesn't sound like a marriage proposal?"

Adrian nearly dropped his spoon into his bowl.

Elena raised her head very slowly. "Miss Saintclair. Are you quite sure we aren't discussing betrothals?"

"I'm asking about social phrasing."

Adrian took a breath.

"Tell him what you admire. Leave out historical records, handprints, and lifelong obligations."

Cecilia nodded earnestly. "That sounds sensible."

Elena couldn't help laughing. At last, they had found something to argue about that didn't involve mortal danger.

Later that night, the committee finally delivered an interim ruling. The public memorial and blessings could proceed the next day. Any demonstration involving the old transfer ring would remain suspended until independent verification was complete, and no living person's mark could be connected without that person's express consent.

"An actual decision," Elena said. "At last."

Cecilia read every line before signing the acknowledgment of receipt.

"I won't preside over that demonstration tomorrow. If the memorial needs me, I'll decide whether I want to take part in the ordinary service."

Adrian let out a long breath.

He'd thought that keeping her safe meant getting her out of the capital. In the end, what protected her was a rule that separated the dangerous part from everything else.

They were about to retire when Cecilia stopped at the window facing the bell tower. Something changed in her expression.

"I dreamed about a bell tower last night," she said. "It wasn't the first time."

Adrian turned. "You've dreamed about it before?"

"Yes." She rested a hand on the window frame. "Someone in the dream tells me not to sign for him. I can never see his face clearly."

The old scar on Adrian's left hand gave a faint sting.

He didn't tell her he must be that man.

"If you're comfortable telling me, do you remember anything else?"

Cecilia nodded slowly.

"Tomorrow. I'd like to write down what I remember first. I don't want today's events to make me think the memory was always like this."

Adrian agreed.

That night, none of them went back to deciding who ought to sacrifice themselves.

At least the October thirty-first event no longer had permission to make anyone bear an unexplained risk for someone else.
