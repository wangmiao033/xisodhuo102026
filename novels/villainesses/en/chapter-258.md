# Chapter 258: Not Here to Die in Anyone's Place

When the old execution hall opened on the afternoon of April 20, a carpenter entered first.

He extended a long-handled lamp, checking that fallen dust concealed no fresh cracks. Only then did he nod to the examiner. Adrian waited on the final step, his left hand in his pocket. His palm sweated; the crescent scar felt like cold iron.

He knew this doorway from broken memories and pictures he'd mistaken for a game. Neither could tell him which floorboards would bear his weight today.

“You can stay outside,” Elena said.

She didn't take his hand. At that moment, what he needed was the certainty that retreat remained possible. Even being led forward tenderly would not have given him that. He needed to know he could leave, not be gently guided inside. Reading the posted inspection limits, he agreed to enter. If memory displaced the present, she should stop him.

“How?”

“Say my name. Don't tell me everything's all right.”

“I won't defend this place.”

The morning's attempted final procedure still lacked required conditions. The dispute over compulsory movement remained unresolved. This was a limited inspection authorized by the building's proper custodian, not Oswin surrendering the core or permission to dismantle walls. The examiner retained the key and led them along the checked passage.

Evelyn identified the former registry desk without crossing the marked boundary. Her records needed comparison with physical evidence. Past involvement didn't make every present statement correct.

The hall was smaller than Adrian expected. He had spent so long imagining what waited beyond the doorway that ordinary measurements almost felt like a contradiction. The room refused to become as vast as his fear.

No endless ranks of faces watched him. Dust covered benches; a bucket had a chipped rim. Later brickwork shortened a side passage. A cloth concealed the central base. Damp boards near the passage were out of bounds.

He remembered a door beyond that wall—or perhaps another, similar room. Evelyn showed the examiner a renovation sheet. The passage had indeed been shortened. The space behind it now belonged to another custodial area.

“We can't open it today,” the examiner said.

“I know.” Too quickly. Adrian added, “I want to. Knowing we can't doesn't stop that.”

Only then did Elena touch his sleeve.

They were checking a facility condition cited in the final procedure: whether the hall's recording apparatus possessed the complete receiving capability claimed in a summary. An omitted accident couldn't be dismissed as ancient history. They could establish provenance and storage locations, not seat a living person there to demonstrate danger.

Isabella remained in the outer yard with relocation staff, waiting for an ordinary wagon. Two households needed to leave temporary rooms before today's borrowing period ended. The manager had permitted brief waiting in the side yard, never entry to the hall. Isabella entrusted evidence-taking to a recorder rather than abandon her promise.

Fetching a measurement sheet, Adrian found her arguing with an elderly man over a birdcage.

“It can't go beneath flour crates.”

“I didn't say it could. I said I'd ask the driver about another space.”

Recognizing her, he abruptly fell silent. She turned her cloak clasp inward. “Knowing who I am won't widen the wagon.”

Adrian offered help. She sent him to find whoever could actually decide the loading arrangement, not proclaim a royal solution. With the driver, they moved an empty basket from a protected corner. The bird stayed with its owner until boarding, not packed away ahead of him.

“You're remarkably obedient today,” Isabella said.

“You asked me to find a driver, not become one.”

She smiled, then asked if he feared the hall. He admitted it. She offered neither herself as substitute nor royal companionship he hadn't requested.

“I want to go inside. But I promised to finish this handover. Tell me what you actually see afterward.”

“Not the heroic version?”

“If you have time to invent one, I'll ask why you weren't moving baskets.”

There had been no comforting speech, no claim that courage would make the building harmless. Nevertheless, his steps steadied on returning. Someone wanted an account of the present, not another doomed legend.

The custodian raised one corner of the protective cloth. Beneath it lay worn stone, replacement fastener holes, and a narrow groove ending at a copper insert. Later plaster obscured part of its identification mark.

Within the authorized limits, the carpenter removed loose dust without cutting stone. Adrian saw an angled corner. His left hand clenched.

No light, no voice: only a thread pulling beneath his skin. He started forward. Elena said his name.

His shoe touched the boundary.

“Where are you?”

“The old execution hall. April twentieth. Afternoon.” He breathed. “You're on my right.”

He didn't claim to be fine. All contact paused while the medical attendant checked him. He could answer, and the scar hadn't broken, but nausea and sweating were real. The recorder noted symptoms, not a verified miracle.

Evelyn's expression changed at the insert. She recognized the numbering style but requested the original page before explaining. A note omitted from the summary required preserving the acknowledgment layer separately when the apparatus was replaced.

“Where is it?” Adrian asked.

“I don't know where it is now. It wasn't entered as ordinary scrap.”

It wasn't the answer he'd wanted. Still, there was now a particular absence in a particular set of records, something another person could check without first believing him. A searchable gap, not an answer. The custodian consulted transfer catalogs. Nobody lifted the base because its number looked familiar.

Waiting hurt. Adrian sat by the door, listening to a bird peck its cage. The bells imagined inside the hall slowly receded.

Elena offered a clean handkerchief.

“I wanted to put my hand on it,” he said.

“I saw.”

“I thought I might finally be useful.”

“You already are. You're not material for touching mechanisms.”

She sat nearby, leaving space for silence. He looked down at the cloth rather than immediately speaking. She waited to find out whether he wanted to continue, instead of turning closeness into another demand for explanation.

“Perhaps that's how I began before. One extra step each time. By the time anyone knew, they could only clean up after me.”

She didn't silence that with a kiss. Today he'd stopped. He could still let qualified people investigate. The past wasn't erased, but neither could it excuse every present mistake in advance.

He pressed the handkerchief against his knee and let the attendant examine his hand again.

A copying clerk asked why, if he remembered old operations, he couldn't sketch the necessary places and save half a day's searching.

There was no malice in it. Mud covered the clerk's shoes; he'd probably heard “wait” too often already.

“Remembering where I stood doesn't mean remembering every wall.”

Adrian asked him to record today's measured width and place the remembered doorway on a separate sheet. Less elegant than one complete plan, but safer than turning an old displacement into today's route.

At least the insert hadn't moved, the clerk suggested. The carpenter disagreed. Two newer fasteners didn't match the holes beneath them. It might have been repositioned; its current orientation couldn't simply establish the old one.

Another convenient answer disappeared. Adrian wanted certainty badly enough to feel the loss of even that small assumption. The carpenter had not made the problem by noticing it, however, and Adrian called him back before the observation could remain an informal aside. Adrian asked him to tell the examiner formally. If a readable location record emerged, they would need a trustworthy reference, not a possibly rotated copper piece serving as north for the entire world.

“You didn't punish the person who noticed the nails,” Elena murmured.

“Was I that obvious before?”

“You'd very politely ask whether we could do the next step first.”

He was silent for a moment, then admitted the phrase was embarrassingly familiar. He could hear himself saying it, politeness concealing the same impatience every time. She straightened his sleeve, checking whether touching the skin hurt. It didn't. For a moment he watched her hand fall back to her own lap. Then he asked if she could sit closer.

She moved her chair. No nail escaped inspection, but waiting no longer resembled solitary confinement.

Wheels struck stone outside. The bird called; someone laughed. Those sounds didn't make the hall a good place. They proved ordinary life beyond it wasn't waiting for his sacrifice.

The catalog entry emerged near the end of their permitted visit. The corresponding box was in an ordinary evidence store, its number and later transfer signatures intact. It contained a thin trace-receiving plate and a calibration sheet. Nobody in the room had yet examined the contents of that box. Its current condition remained unconfirmed; a catalog could not certify an undamaged object simply because the entry described one.

Evelyn recognized her handwriting among the transfer marks. She'd checked quantities then, not read the attachment. The examiner recorded what she could confirm separately from what she couldn't. Silence granted her no mysterious authority; speaking granted no immunity.

Luna wasn't there. Adrian nearly requested her immediately, then asked an assistant to deliver the record for a reply during her medically permitted working period. Waiting overnight wouldn't make the insert kinder. Keeping an injured person working overnight would have real consequences.

Before they left, the carpenter replaced the cloth and checked temporary markings. Five old locks remained closed. The passage stayed sealed. They had no secret shortcut to the core. Adrian looked back at the base, heart still racing, but could distinguish present footsteps from remembered voices.

The wagon had finally departed. Isabella sat on a step, her left wrist supported, holding a straw fallen from the empty basket in her right hand.

“The bird was satisfied before anyone else.”

Adrian told her about the catalog, nearly crossing the line, and the unread receiving plate. He gave her the report she had requested, including the moment when Elena had needed to stop him. He omitted neither fear nor uncertainty. A box number wasn't yet coordinates for saving the capital.

Isabella dropped the straw into a waste bin before standing.

“Tomorrow, then, we examine a box. We don't send you into one.”

“Preferably.”

Elena joined them and asked who was responsible for dinner. Isabella said today's remit had extended only to getting a bird away from an execution hall. Further tasks required someone qualified.

Adrian laughed and volunteered to ask the kitchen what remained.

Descending the steps, he felt another slight sting in the scar. This time he did not curl his fingers farther into his pocket and wait to see whether anyone noticed. He didn't hide it. He told both women.

If they approached that old anchor tomorrow, the first requirement wasn't fearlessness. It was letting other people know where he stood.
