# Chapter 179: Not Everything Survived

Beatrice Wren laid out her gloves without first touching the surviving tray.

“Who operated the heating valve?”

The gray-browed contractor raised her hand. She'd shut off the heat when instructed, but steam remained at the far end. The conservator asked her to identify the pipes, then sent everyone not involved in cooling outside. Adrian began to explain he'd been here all morning. Beatrice glanced at his boots.

“Time spent here doesn't make leather heatproof.”

He checked behind himself before stepping back onto dry tiles. Cecilia lowered her head, apparently hiding a smile in her collar.

“You too,” Beatrice said.

Cecilia straightened immediately.

On the afternoon of February 8, the northern annex no longer had merely a request awaiting tomorrow's answer. The reviewer arrived with the original preservation direction and defined the inventory's scope. Beatrice ordinarily worked on royal conservation; her supervisor had authorized assistance with these materials today. That didn't give her authority to investigate every Temple archive.

Rusk produced a genuine maintenance batch approval. It wasn't a sheet he'd just invented. But it covered confirmed duplicates. How the three disputed items awaiting interpretation entered that category remained unanswered.

“Establish what remains first,” the reviewer said. “Responsibility separately. Whoever is answering questions cannot simultaneously decide which pieces needn't be counted.”

The young custodian gave his account at another table without Rusk supplying sentences behind him. The contractor submitted her work order and asked that the threatened pay deduction be recorded. Her statement didn't authenticate the plates' contents.

Elena arrived without a Rosenberg guarantee to receive everything. She brought their authorized inspection copies, providing full sources for the three references. Through the existing contact, Isabella supplied the preservation request she'd made during the recent public procedural discussion. It established her request, not today's official direction.

Luna had sent advice after reading the available condition report: without confirming the medium's structure, don't separate layers or apply power merely for a clearer image.

The left-handed letters wandered. The conclusion didn't.

Had she written too long? Adrian asked the messenger. Only this page; she'd dictated the rest. Her medical restrictions remained.

Beside his acknowledgment, Adrian wrote a separate question: would she like the rest of the travel story tonight? Elena saw it, raising an eyebrow.

“How many people have you arranged to see today?”

“This is still a question.”

“I meant, have you left time to eat?”

He looked at her. She turned away. That didn't need recording as though she were competing for a place.

He offered a clean bag containing uncrushed biscuits. She took one instead of pretending not to be hungry.

A sharp sound interrupted them. Not an explosion. Condensation still dripped through the return pipe after the heat stopped. An old soldered seam in its receiving pan split, releasing hot water toward the threshold.

Adrian stepped forward. Beatrice stopped him sharply.

“Back from the doorway! Don't carry the tray!”

She didn't touch it barehanded either. The contractor pulled a dry rack aside with a long-handled tool. Following instructions, the custodian moved empty cases from the floor. Duty staff directed visitors toward higher ground rather than letting eager helpers block the only exit.

The water wasn't engulfing the courtyard. It pooled beside the low trough, enough to scald ankles and soak paper tags. Beatrice had an assistant surround its path with absorbent material while another worker handled the return valve. Adrian gripped his empty bag. Not rushing in required more effort than rushing would have.

Cecilia caught his coat briefly, then released it.

“People coming through.”

Two workers were bringing a replacement rack. She wasn't drawing him possessively closer. Adrian cleared the way and, when asked, moved three empty stools to provide a dry landing space. They weren't heavy or originals he had no right to take.

Viola didn't appear with a sword. She was resting at home under her physician's restrictions. Her written route advice was practical: separate incoming and outgoing traffic; keep empty cases away from doors. The responsible staff used what suited the actual room.

After ten minutes, no more water flowed. Nobody had been scalded, but the edge of a paper label on the first tray was soaked. Beatrice recorded the damage. The custodian compared the original catalog without filling illegible letters merely to make the inventory neat.

Rusk said this proved the old room was unsuitable for continued storage.

“It proves we need safer storage,” Beatrice replied. “Not that the contents should be washed away.”

The second tray went onto the new rack with its seal intact. Staff documented seal, surface, and references before opening the cover before witnesses. Three thin plates lay side by side. The middle one was dark, with a narrow strip of paper adhering to its edge. Nothing illuminated itself or recognized Adrian as its owner.

He felt relieved, then ashamed of the relief. Ordinary plates required patient reading. He'd almost wanted danger to save him the effort.

After checking the edges, Beatrice permitted the custodian to read the nonprivate catalog headings. One was Historical Sequence Termination Record; another, Transcription Receipt; the third, Overwritten-Layer Condition Sheet. Cross-references connected them to the earlier unclear copy. That didn't yet establish every citation was accurate.

“So they aren't three copies of one thing?” Elena asked.

“The headings and physical layer counts differ. Reading will tell us whether some content overlaps.”

The reviewer recorded that exact answer. Classifying all three as duplicates now required something better than pointing to the earlier classification.

The first tray took longer. Soaked films adhered or peeled away. Only one of two identifiable batch references belonged to the disputed group. Two further pieces couldn't be matched until stabilized. No method today restored erased lettering. What they could identify would be entered as identified; what remained uncertain would stay that way. A clean-looking list would not repair a surface, any more than the deacon’s insistence could put detached film back where it had been. Nobody called the loss a few useless papers.

The young custodian lowered his head. Last night he'd read the phrase dual-layer and still sent all three to treatment.

Rusk drew breath. The reviewer stopped him.

“Why did you send them?”

“I thought someone had approved it.”

“Who? On which page?”

He couldn't identify one. He cried quietly, without kneeling for forgiveness. Adrian didn't want to treat tears as innocence or proof of acting. Letting him describe what he'd done was more useful than completing a scene of repentance for him.

Late that afternoon, an existing independent storage room was designated for temporary custody. Not Rosenberg House, not Luna's private workbench. Beatrice recommended separate supports for the three surviving items, a separate case for damaged materials, and dry bags for the catalogs and work orders. Wet films mustn't lie on paper again.

Could they take a plate as assurance? Elena asked.

Adrian shook his head too quickly.

“We want it not to disappear. That isn't the same as keeping it ourselves.”

She pressed her lips together. Too many people had told her not to worry, then failed to deliver what she should have received. Instead, she requested itemized acknowledgments and the next verification appointment.

Authorized copies would be provided, the reviewer agreed. Two custodians would acknowledge the original objects' transfer together. Elena gained no plate to hide in her sleeve. She could ask where each listed item had gone. The references would travel with the objects, and the receiving custodians would have to acknowledge them, rather than promising in general that everything had been taken care of.

While they waited for the destination's response, a messenger handed Adrian a reply addressed directly to him: an appointment for independent employment advice. Elena had received her separate arrangement, with a statement of the costs she could cover. She'd intended to mention her part earlier, but he had kept watching the treatment room.

“I haven't selected a new job for you,” she said. “There's finally a time for the independent advice you agreed to consider. Borrowing my family's carriage again today doesn't mean you can't go.”

He opened his own reply, then read the separate fee explanation she offered. It wasn't to be deducted from future wages.

“Are you afraid I'll leave?”

“Yes.”

The answer came faster than expected.

“I meant to wait until you were in a better mood. But your mood always seems to have another box to open.”

He smiled, then placed the appointment in his own folder, away from evidence inventories. He couldn't promise never to leave, nor needed to resign immediately to demonstrate independence. The opportunity was in his hands; the decision still needed making.

“I'll listen. And I'll tell you what I decide. You won't have to guess from a duty roster.”

Elena nodded and slowly put on her gloves. Wind entered the gallery. Adrian started to adjust her scarf, then paused. She moved closer, allowing him to free the corner folded into her collar.

“This doesn't need three lawyers.”

“Just your advice.”

Color returned to her face. Someone called them to check the receipt. She went first rather than making his small answer a claim over all of him.

As the last case was secured on the cart, she stood beside him.

“I didn't like you contradicting me before I finished.”

“I shouldn't have answered for you. I can disagree, but it was your question.”

She studied him, then nodded. “Though I did want to hide it at home.”

“Where?”

“Your bookshelf. The untidiest shelf. Searchers might give up.”

He laughed, and finally she did too. Then she clarified that this wasn't an actual plan. He knew. His bookshelf would still be criticized tomorrow.

Before dark, acknowledgment arrived: the three intact items had been received individually, with damaged material recorded separately. They'd preserved these things, not recovered all of history. Rusk's authority and his choices after receiving the inspection notice would be examined separately. No missing project worker or disputed payment had been explained by a case of plates.

At home, Luna asked first whether the contractor had been injured, not whether the plates could be read immediately. Once she heard nobody had been scalded, she requested the condition copies, her right hand still on its cushion.

Tomorrow morning, authorized staff would examine specified material with the calibrated external reader, starting with safely legible portions. She would advise during a short seated period with rest, neither casting nor touching originals. Adrian could decide whether to see the record bearing his name.

“I want to.”

Luna didn't promise he'd finally discover who he was. She pointed out the appointment and asked whether the story could wait until after dinner.

Passing them, Elena lifted the biscuit bag. “Let him eat first.”

Cecilia said the goose had done its duty; the bowl could take over. From beside the fire, Viola asked what new guard assignment this was. Adrian explained at length before realizing nobody actually needed a report on the goose.

He sat, placing the empty bag beside him. Tomorrow the case would open. Tonight he let the soup reach his stomach while it was hot.
