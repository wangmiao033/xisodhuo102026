# Chapter 221: A Blank Is Not a Signature

On March 20, five forms arrived at the academy. Each ended with the same printed sentence.

I voluntarily continue to fulfill my established duties.

Elena read it, picked up the unused pen, then put it down.

“Continue?”

The delivery clerk hesitated. It was a temporary acknowledgment intended to maintain existing protection and prevent a gap in responsibility during the inquiry, he explained. Not a betrothal. It didn't require them to enter the underground chamber today.

“I didn't ask whether it was a betrothal. When did I agree to the duties described here?”

He consulted an attachment identifying historical volume references and mark categories. Those explained why five forms had been delivered. None established informed consent in the present timeline.

Nobody suggested signing first and correcting it later.

Isabella showed them another page. A single checkbox joined an undertaking not to interfere recklessly with existing protection to acceptance of the designated burden. Refusal of one could easily be summarized as refusal of both.

“Separate them.”

The clerk couldn't amend the standard form. He didn't demand trust in his good intentions and admitted he couldn't guarantee how returned papers would be classified. Viola requested an unchanged copy and an accurate delivery entry: unsigned, not refusal to assist.

After hesitating, he recorded the date and actual status.

That corrected this handover, not every future misunderstanding. Adrian remembered yesterday's evacuation scrap. Attach a desirable action to a conclusion that doesn't belong to it, and a person's blank space could disappear.

The morning's limited inquiry would take place in the academy's small council room. The women chose to speak individually rather than have Adrian answer for them. Each permitted nonprivate objections to go to the reviewing office. Wording for wider circulation still needed individual checks.

During preparations, Luna's assistant arranged the papers in an orderly line. Luna regarded her thickest stack.

“If seating follows page count, put me by the door. Easier to carry me out.”

“Nobody will carry you,” Viola said. “You'll leave when the physician told you to.”

“I wanted my opinions to appear weighty.”

“They already are,” Adrian said. “You needn't add the chair.”

She laughed, then asked her assistant to remove a passage she had dictated twice. Isabella called it the morning's first genuine reduction of everyone's burden. When the laughter faded, Luna settled her right palm properly. Reassuring other people didn't require shortening the care instructions.

Elena first spoke with the legal witness. She didn't request an exemption based on her father's title. She asked why inherited property, political position, and a risk category in a historical register could become authority over a body whose owner had never participated in the decision.

“If being a Rosenberg gets me removed and somebody with a weaker family put in my place, we haven't changed the right thing,” she told Adrian.

He nodded, then recognized that faster escape in his own thoughts. Remove her name, and the remaining questions might seem easier to postpone.

She noticed his hesitation without forcing him to deny it.

“I want to live. Not by exchanging my name for a stranger's.”

“I will want to put you first.”

“You can love me more. You can't count other people less.”

His chest ached. She hadn't asked him to distribute affection equally. Neither would she let love make fairness a game of obtaining one exception for her.

Oswin didn't address them from a platform. He sat at the same height as the independent examiner and heard their questions in turn. Today's inquiry concerned the temporary acknowledgment. One meeting couldn't abolish a national institution.

Elena didn't recite the entire noble register. She indicated continue and asked for the source of present consent, or removal of that implication.

Oswin replied that duties could arise from an existing position, not solely private choice.

“My position can impose responsibilities concerning property and public office. If you also use it to authorize decisions about my body, describe that power plainly. Don't use continue to make it look as though I already agreed.”

The clerk recorded her words. Adrian's hands felt cold, but he didn't soften the statement on her behalf.

Cecilia placed her everyday prayer cord before her. She wasn't renouncing belief or surrendering her title. Her words shouldn't need something in her hand to make them credible.

“My faith asks me to care for people. It doesn't allow somebody to choose my sacrifice first, then tell me my god requires it.”

Would she remain if believers faced danger, Oswin asked.

“Yes. Remaining to care for them isn't the same permission as allowing you to decide I cannot leave.”

She didn't promise fearlessness or demand that others imitate her. She resubmitted the meal service's request for accurate safety guidance. Protecting her right to refuse mustn't come at the cost of stopping ordinary people's help.

Viola's left arm remained supported as she spoke.

She acknowledged that a knight's oath involved risk. Training, guarding, and rescue couldn't happen entirely without cost. Orders still needed an objective and limits. Willingness to face one danger in the past wasn't an unlimited issue slip for every danger afterward.

“If I agree to go, I answer only for myself. Drawing us together on one diagram doesn't deliver my consent to the other four.”

Someone asked whether that could make timely action impossible.

“Then explain refusal rights and actual emergency powers beforehand. Don't wait until people reach the door and say urgency meant there was never time.”

Luna's final technical question used no grand language. Had the evidence established that no other arrangement could replace these five designated individuals? Or only that the arrangement previously used had produced certain protective effects?

“No alternative configuration has been validated for use,” Oswin replied.

“Keep the not yet. Wanting to live doesn't let me promise another system tomorrow. My uncertainty doesn't make your method the only possible one.”

She paused and indicated that she needed water. Nobody hurried her injured hand toward the cup. After drinking, she requested written checking of technical comments made after her departure. Discussion among the others mustn't automatically become agreement by all five.

Isabella waited until she finished, then placed the limited royal countersignature extract beside the form. She didn't transfer every responsibility of her house to the Temple. Nor did she promise that regaining power would solve everything.

“If the Crown participated in designation, investigate the Crown. Establishing responsibility should dismantle wrongful authority, not let me inherit a more attractive way to decide for others.”

Oswin asked whether she would explain herself to residents who might suffer because designated bearers refused.

“Yes. But the explanations aren't owed only by those refusing. Whoever chose the people, concealed possibilities, and retained dangers must stand before those residents too.”

Adrian understood why there was no shared speech prepared for him. These weren't five voices saying the same I don't want to die. What each defended differed, as did what each would willingly undertake. If he erased those differences into a splendid declaration of unity, he'd already completed the form's bottom line for Oswin.

The examiner didn't declare the designation system void. He required this temporary form to separate its undertakings and remove the presumption of continuation without present consent. This inquiry's summary mustn't treat unsigned forms as agreement to designated bearing, or as opposition to every protection. Other disputes remained for the appropriate proceedings.

A limited decision. But the clerk had to return with actual revision requirements, not merely five blanks awaiting names.

Oswin didn't accept that they had found an alternative. He agreed to attach each objection in full and required further discussion to address how protection would continue.

Nobody said that question could be avoided.

Before they left, a clerk handling inquiries asked them to remain briefly. Outside, someone had already heard that the five mark-bearers wouldn't sign. A daily notice was being drafted saying they refused responsibility. He brought the sample rather than pretending to understand everything himself.

Elena wanted the sentence struck out. Cecilia first asked where the notice would go. The relevant protection desks, he said, not every public board in the capital. She requested that the same notice retain the current service arrangements, so people wouldn't assume one refusal meant losing tomorrow's help.

“Don't say we've agreed to remain either,” Viola added.

The clerk considered the apparently opposing requirements. He eventually wrote that the original combined form remained unsigned and the undertakings were being treated separately as required by the examiner. Existing service arrangements would be explained by their responsible offices, not guaranteed by five absent signatures.

Isabella pointed out that they'd checked this notice only. It mustn't be described as correction of every notice elsewhere. He nodded and separately recorded the other distribution points still needing checks.

Adrian watched without finding the women less resolute. Winning a debate would have allowed them to leave after the most memorable sentence. They also had to account for people who hadn't heard the inquiry and depended on a desk's instructions to arrange tomorrow.

Elena rubbed fingers stiff from writing as she put away her copy. Adrian asked before picking up her document bag. She agreed, adding that none of its contents authorized him to choose dinner for her.

“I still intend to make a serious case.”

Outside the room, Cecilia retied her prayer cord. The knot faced inward; she struggled to turn it. Adrian offered help. She agreed, then smilingly reminded him this was no supplementary duty under an old betrothal.

“I only know ordinary knots.”

“Fortunately. Ordinary things are difficult enough today.”

Elena waited beside them without pretending to resent a knot. Viola reminded them that the carriage was nearly there. Luna had already left on time. Isabella remained to check her extract with the clerk. Nobody required all five to enter and leave together as proof of solidarity.

That evening, Adrian received a revised sample, not a final issued form. Several separate choices now occupied the bottom of the page. He completed none for anybody else and returned the samples to their individual folders.

He checked the accompanying note before putting the papers away. The revised sample was an invitation to consider separate undertakings, not permission to act on them before anyone answered. He asked the duty clerk to preserve that distinction in the covering message. For a moment, the empty spaces felt less like a demand. They were still empty, and could remain so until the people receiving them understood what was being asked. He had no right to hurry the pen into any of those spaces.

His evacuation scrap lay on the table too. Yesterday he had lacked an answer. Today he still did. On fresh paper, he wrote tomorrow's question: besides placing the whole risk on one person, which parts could they examine separately?

Elena glanced at it as she passed. She didn't change the heading to THIRD WAY FOUND.

She simply asked whether he'd visit the places where people had actually borne the work.

He said yes.
