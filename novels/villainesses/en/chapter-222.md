# Chapter 222: Something They Could Do Together

On March 21, Adrian carried a clean sheet into the works yard. There were no five figures drawn on it.

The duty craftsman glanced at the paper. Had he forgotten his diagram?

“There isn't one yet.”

“Better than bringing the wrong one. What do you want to see?”

Adrian felt relieved. The man hadn't pretended ordinary drainage equipment could answer every question about the Crown of Destiny. They were authorized to examine a retired demonstration rig disconnected from the working waterways, plus public maintenance-rota records. Nothing still protecting the city would be disturbed.

Elena and Viola accompanied him along the level path. Viola's left arm remained supported; an assistant carried her papers. Isabella was checking whether the earlier route correction had reached the actual civic staff. Cecilia had an appointment with the meal service's supplies coordinator. Luna remained at the academy, with a limited reading session planned. Searching for a third way wasn't a fine enough phrase to drag her around an entire works yard.

The rig held a large wheel, two short channels, and an axle with its belt removed.

Adrian promptly imagined replacing the wheel with several smaller ones. Before he could suggest it, the craftsman asked whether he wanted more people turning a wheel or water taking different routes.

“Those are different questions?”

The man's look was restrained. Elena suppressed a smile without trying to make Adrian's question sound cleverer.

Placing demonstration blocks in a dry channel, the craftsman explained that a blocked outlet wouldn't grow wider because two additional workers turned the wheel. Another route required knowledge of its capacity and when it could connect. Drawing three arrows didn't prove that each person would do one-third of the work.

“This isn't connected to live water. I'm describing this rig, not your destiny.”

Adrian erased his three little circles. He didn't crumple the paper because his first thought had been naive. Plenty of room remained for things he didn't know.

Viola asked how the maintenance rota handled someone suddenly unable to continue. The craftsman showed the public record's reserve shifts and mandatory stopping conditions. A willing replacement wasn't sufficient. The replacement needed the relevant skill, and the equipment had to permit a handover then.

“What if nobody can take over?”

“Don't pretend someone's there. Stop what must stop. Report what must be reported.”

He didn't claim stopping was harmless. A wait for qualified staff had once cost a drainage cycle; delayed work and additional repairs remained in the accounts. It wasn't a joke about laziness. Neither did that loss justify unlimited compulsory overtime afterward.

Elena asked which papers they could take. He offered rota procedures without private medical details. Individual leave explanations weren't included. She didn't demand to know who was absent most often or confuse revocable participation with abandoning a task without notice at any moment.

Adrian wrote three separate questions: Who is willing? Who can do it? When can responsibility change hands?

Then he crossed out THEREFORE APPLICABLE TO THE CORE.

“A good correction,” the craftsman said.

At the gate, Viola stopped beside a notice seeking skilled temporary help. A young man asked whether signing up would make him part of the force protecting the capital.

She didn't award him an impressive title through her authority as a knight. She indicated the stated tasks and hours. First he should ask about pay, tools, training, and work within his ability. Wanting to contribute was real; it needn't become a route into danger he didn't understand.

Disappointed, he nevertheless went where she pointed.

“You could have said something inspiring,” Adrian remarked.

“I want him willing to return tomorrow, not merely willing to sign today.”

She held her scarf with her right hand when the wind caught it. Elena sheltered her while the assistant secured it, leaving the injured arm undisturbed.

They met at the meal service at noon. Several registered sacks of grain stood before Cecilia. They hadn't suddenly acquired enough money to cover every shortage. An already promised delivery had arrived and needed division between today's distribution and reserve supplies.

Adrian spread ordinary empty sacks. Hearing that they wanted to understand shared contributions, the coordinator asked whether they meant donations or putting existing donors on a list consenting to some other danger.

“Not the latter,” Cecilia said immediately.

“Good. Someone promised this sack of grain. Don't turn that kindness into all next year's grain.”

She recorded the actual limits without taking offense. This wasn't a place from which investigators could borrow a heartwarming metaphor and leave. People were already waiting to eat what each sack held.

Someone suggested publishing the largest donors to encourage further contributions. Elena asked about households that didn't want neighbors knowing how much grain they stored. The coordinator explained that thanks followed each donor's permitted form. Giving supplies didn't authorize displaying everything about the giver.

None of this proved a calamity could be divided equally among more people. Adrian added that to the margin. He wouldn't tell Luna tomorrow that he'd encountered human kindness, therefore the core must be alterable.

A sack's drawstring tightened under his efforts. Cecilia offered rescue.

“Yes, please.”

She showed him the hidden end, then let him loosen it himself. For the moment, she announced seriously, she led their research into ordinary knots.

“Not what you said yesterday.”

“Different knot. You can't transfer a ruling between cases.”

Elena laughed hard enough to fold a sack opening the wrong way. Checking the shareable rota extract from her chair, Viola observed that this contest at least required no review tribunal.

Isabella arrived without a royal decree funding everything. The civic desk confirmed that the correction had reached the actual scheduling staff, but guidance at the kitchen crossing still awaited their confirmation. Not every problem was solved. She'd also found the ordinary application route for other historical protection-maintenance material. It conveyed no core-control authority.

“Nobody opened a cupboard because I'm a princess.”

“You sound slightly annoyed,” Adrian said.

“I'd hoped at least to join the right queue.”

She had waited at the supplies counter, query sheet in her right hand, left wrist supported, only discovering at the front that she needed the office next door. She hadn't demanded priority in the second queue. She had genuinely been annoyed for a while.

They laughed, and she joined them. Then she carefully recorded the correct counter. The next applicant needn't prove equality by repeating her error.

Late that afternoon at the academy, Adrian spread the papers before Luna's allotted reading session and began by saying he hadn't found a new form of magic.

“Excellent. I feared you'd brought a revolution home from a waterwheel.”

She had her assistant separate ordinary equipment, supplies procedures, and protection records. Similar arrows mustn't pretend they described one mechanism. The works yard offered questions: how did responsibility pass when someone couldn't continue? What could pause? What first needed a replacement? The meal service showed how a bounded agreement could avoid expanding with every use.

Whether the Crown of Destiny permitted anything comparable remained unanswered.

Adrian admitted drawing three small wheels to share the large one's load. Luna examined the crossed-out sketch without laughing at his ignorance.

“Drawing it and discovering it doesn't work is better than secretly treating it as the answer.”

“Can you make the real diagram?”

She didn't promise. First they needed to separate what they currently called cost: energy supply, maintenance, actual risk, designated individuals. Which were merely bundled into one word? Which demonstrably connected? Separating questions didn't mean dismantling machinery, or selecting someone to test it with their body.

“I can begin listing the conditions a model would need. I cannot give you a working system today.”

Adrian nodded. That limited undertaking deserved paper more than an impossible promise of safety tomorrow.

They recruited no new risk-bearers. Cecilia specifically excluded today's residents, donors, and would-be workers from any potential trial list. Those people had agreed to their own activities, not to become material in the search for another way.

Viola asked who would explain uncertainties if volunteers were needed later. Isabella said they first needed to know what anyone would be asked to accept. Elena required the same limits for her family's resources. Her willingness couldn't enroll every employee automatically.

A custody clerk brought a response. One requested maintenance volume needed its binding examined before transfer and wouldn't arrive in the hoped-for reading slot. Adrian nearly asked whether someone was obstructing the inquiry again. The clerk showed the conservator's recorded damage and offered a different directory that didn't depend on the affected pages.

After reading, Adrian withdrew the accusation he hadn't quite spoken. Delay could hurt them. Paper also genuinely deteriorated. If finding another road meant everyone must immediately obey his urgency, it already crowded that road with people unable to refuse.

They asked what the available material could answer first, leaving missing support beside the relevant questions. Elena insisted that an inadequate directory be called inadequate. They couldn't manufacture a result because they'd traveled so far today.

Looking at his erased circles, Adrian agreed. One hasty action had already cost him enough. Everyone's invested time mustn't become another argument for acting before they were ready.

The clerk asked what to call the day's material. Adrian considered alternative plan, then rejected it. They had questions, examples from ordinary work, and requests for evidence, not a design ready for approval. He asked for that plain description instead.

Elena checked that the copies they had permission to keep were separated from documents meant to remain with their custodians. Viola marked the rota extract as an example of work arrangements, not a promise from its workers. Isabella retained the civic response with its unresolved crossing question. Even their useful findings needed their boundaries kept attached; otherwise tomorrow's eagerness could quietly make them larger.

The discussion didn't produce five identical positions. It did stop revolving entirely around who should enter the door first.

Luna ended her session on time. Her assistant left two pages of questions requiring further evidence. Tomorrow they could continue establishing conditions. She wouldn't draw a seemingly complete model while exhausted. The five locks remained sealed, the core door closed. Nobody's body had become a trial run.

Clearing the table, Adrian discovered his morning sheet was full. The marks through three little circles remained, beside words corrected by an ordinary craftsman and blanks he wasn't entitled to fill.

Elena asked from the doorway whether to save baked pears for Luna tonight. She'd missed them two days earlier. Absence didn't mean dislike.

“Ask her. Perhaps she wants something savory.”

Luna hadn't gone far. Turning back, she announced that both options deserved consideration, but accepting pears wouldn't authorize more work after dinner.

Isabella thought that might be today's easiest bounded agreement to approve.

Laughing, they put out the reading-room lamps.

Tomorrow they would try turning questions into testable diagrams. Tonight, they could first deliver the meal already promised to the people who needed to eat it.
