# Chapter 171: They Didn't Say the Same Thing

On the morning of February 2, Isabella proposed a vote.

Adrian was cracking an egg. Hearing that they would decide who ate supper with him, he stopped with his spoon in the air.

“There are six of us,” the princess said. “At least we needn't argue until the food goes cold.”

Luna considered her. “Six people deciding one person's evening?”

“Including him.”

“He still only has one vote.”

Isabella's confidence faded. Elena and Cecilia had argued about the order of their statements last night. This morning they each held a different draft. Isabella had wanted something everyone recognized as fair. She'd nearly replaced whose agreement do we need with who has the most supporters.

“We can vote on a restaurant everyone wants to visit,” Adrian said, putting down the spoon. “Not on whom I must accompany.”

Cecilia glanced at her draft. “The rotation already demonstrated that.”

“Restaurants, then.”

“First ask who wants to go,” Elena said.

Isabella drew breath, then laughed awkwardly. “Apparently I can't even preside over breakfast.”

“You can eat it.” Viola indicated the egg with her right hand. “It's getting cold.”

They finally laughed. Adrian reminded them he'd promised Luna the travel book after supper. They would rearrange if the review ran late, not add another appointment now. Nobody voted to ratify that existing agreement.

Cold air outside the annex dispersed their amusement. The review wasn't inside the sealed building, and ordinary submissions continued. Two separate notices explained the premises-preservation review and individual arrangements after the seven-day registration restriction had expired. They weren't a single indefinitely extended prohibition.

Elena handed her papers to the clerk. Cecilia supplied another set. They sat together without signing for each other.

The reviewing officer asked whether they preferred a combined statement to prevent repetition.

“We can coordinate time,” Elena replied. “We don't hold identical views.”

Cecilia nodded. Sitting behind them, Adrian found that more reassuring than complete agreement.

The registration representative explained that the missing page and enclosure discrepancy remained unresolved. Unsupervised original transfers should stay restricted. Reopening everything could make later damage impossible to distinguish. Evelyn's written execution explanation was submitted; she wasn't speaking here. That didn't exempt her from further verification, nor make this representative answerable for every question in the older case.

Elena didn't demand every seal removed immediately. She placed a temporary-counter rejection record before them. Beth had been turned away when she tried again yesterday with her copy. Through the existing messenger, she had supplied a supplementary account and the counter's explanation. Elena had checked its permitted use that morning.

“Why does preserving originals prevent someone submitting a copy already in her possession?”

The representative suggested that staff might have doubted authenticity.

“Deferring a judgment about authenticity isn't the same as refusing to record when it arrived.”

She used Beth's experience with the name withheld in the public statement. The officer could see the identified account in its separate enclosure. Elena retained the admission that the original application lacked a page. She wasn't asking them to declare incomplete material complete. She wanted somewhere for a supplement to arrive.

The representative had been preparing to mention that missing page. Hearing Elena state it first, he stopped turning the paper.

Cecilia addressed complete. Her collector's acknowledgment covered only the delivery he'd authorized them to discuss, not patient identities or conditions. Someone recorded as having received everything had obtained an inventory. This didn't establish building-wide fraud. It did justify checking who received what.

“A procedure cannot stop hearing the person at the door merely because a record says complete.”

She didn't pronounce it as an oracle. She placed the permitted page on the table.

Adrian understood why the women had argued. Elena wanted a usable route. Cecilia wanted the word at its end not to lie. Neither needed to surrender before both could fit at this table.

Who bore responsibility if copies differed from originals? the representative asked. Elena proposed recording their source and unverified status. Receiving something needn't certify every assertion. Cecilia added that applicants must correct errors they knew about. Being mistreated didn't entitle them to say anything they liked.

Someone muttered in the back. She didn't amend her answer.

Adrian presented his own observations, not a conclusion prepared by someone else. The execution liaison's permitted powers must remain distinct from particular acts. He requested comparison of receipt complete with the attachment range, not a guilty verdict based on a signature.

The representative asked whether he would accept dealing only with his own material to end matters quickly.

Yesterday's limited check had helped: it confirmed that receiving his documents didn't require naming a compatibility partner. Adrian included that favorable reply. Anger wasn't a reason to hide it.

“My part can be handled first. I can't agree that someone else's receipt is fine.”

“You don't represent every applicant.”

“Correct. Please receive their own statements separately. I don't withdraw them.”

Elena looked down, almost smiling. Cecilia watched the clerk to ensure the suggestion wasn't becoming a request for Adrian to represent everyone.

During the break, Elena removed her gloves in the corridor. Cecilia followed. Neither initially looked at the other.

Adrian almost left them space to debate the sequence again. Instead Elena admitted she'd wanted to interrupt.

“You spent longer on complete than planned.”

“I know. That collector was stopped by the same sentence three times. I didn't want to rush past it.”

“I didn't say it wasn't important.”

Cecilia nodded, then asked how much time remained. They redistributed a few minutes. Elena would omit a passage already submitted in writing. Cecilia wouldn't repeat her first citation. Neither had to concede that the other's concern was nobler.

Someone brought hot water in thin cups. Elena took one and shifted it between her hands. Cecilia slipped an empty cup around it. The thanks was stiff, but Cecilia didn't turn it into a demonstration of how perfectly they now got along.

Holding his own cup, Adrian thought breakfast's vote really hadn't been necessary. These women weren't sitting here to win an evening. They were doing things they would carry back into their own lives.

“What are you smiling at?” Elena asked.

“I'm glad I don't have to chair your discussion.”

Cecilia inspected his crooked cup sleeve. “Chair that water first.”

Afterward, the representative noted that two willing copy-comparison witnesses had used the same writer. Their accounts might not be independent. Tension returned immediately.

Adrian remembered the material. Someone literate had assisted with transcription, and formats repeated. Identical phrasing wasn't naturally independent evidence.

“Question them separately about what they experienced. If something has a shared source, record that. Matching paper isn't two observations.”

Elena pointed out a handwritten correction and different times and locations. Cecilia didn't oppose verification; she opposed a writer answering what the witnesses themselves didn't know. A genuine weakness didn't require withdrawing everything.

The officer ordered separate supplementary questions for the two people, with the original handlers answering the corresponding acknowledgments. Voluntary attendance wasn't a compulsory summons. Authorized staff could deliver specified copies to an independent reading location without reopening sealed rooms.

Some historical writing might require specialist reading. Custodians must first supply equipment condition and safety requirements. An available researcher wasn't reason to activate an old device immediately. Luna wasn't here; nobody accepted repairs on her behalf.

Necessary original-preservation restrictions remained, with a missing-page progress report due before noon February 4. Ordinary intake, routing of verifiable copies, and specific objection receipts would proceed. The building wasn't returning instantly to normal. Yesterday's closure wasn't continuing without an explanation either.

Two applicants were willing to attend the west-side reading location tomorrow morning. Its staff already had proper authority to receive this kind of material; it wasn't House Rosenberg's private room. Ordinary travel protection could be requested, without making agreement to testify its price or treating refusal of a question as escape.

During readback, the clerk omitted the request not to publish names. Cecilia restored the application and its pending status, not a blanket exemption already granted. Elena verified that Beth could supplement her copy without paying the original intake fee again.

Each found something the other might have missed. Adrian counted his own pages and returned them to the folder. The outcome wasn't elegant. At least they wouldn't have to argue at home, from competing memories, about how far a door had been promised open.

As they left, a spectator congratulated the women on finally being of one mind. They looked at each other and almost simultaneously said no.

He stared.

“We'll still argue,” Cecilia explained.

“But you needn't write the reasons for us,” Elena added.

Outside, they laughed together. Adrian walked beside them, discovering that being considered by both women at once didn't necessarily mean declaring a winner.

At the bottom of the steps, Elena found her glove inside out. Cecilia offered to hold the folder while she corrected it. Elena accepted, then asked whether she'd been too sharp about the time.

“Yes,” Cecilia said.

Elena looked up.

“And I did go over it.”

Neither qualification swallowed the other. They walked a few paces before Elena admitted she'd been afraid the hearing would finish with her practical requests still unspoken. Cecilia had feared that polite brevity would make the missing receipt sound trivial. Both fears had fitted into the same morning without making them enemies or identical allies.

Adrian waited at the crossing while a delivery wagon passed. He didn't offer to decide which fear was more reasonable. When they joined him, Cecilia handed the folder back. Elena thanked her more easily this time.

They chose the sheltered side of the street. There was still work to do, and the wind still found gaps in every coat. Elena asked Adrian whether he'd finished breakfast. He admitted leaving half the egg. Cecilia looked appalled. For a moment, at least, their opinions were remarkably similar.

At lunch, Isabella didn't ask whether the result secured the votes missing on January 30. She first had the willing attendees asked about travel, then left arrangements to the people responsible.

“I can contact the carriage office. I can't command neutral guards.”

Viola would review routes as advice. Her physician hadn't cleared combat duty. Sitting in a carriage wouldn't repair her left arm. Adrian offered to wait at the reading location rather than cramming everyone into one vehicle to prove solidarity.

The written review arrived that evening when he'd read half the captain's lost-hat passage. Luna asked the messenger to leave it on the side table. Adrian needn't leap from the ship straight back into the registry.

“Two paragraphs remain.”

He finished them. The captain returned the wrong hat and preserved an undistinguished head. Luna turned aside laughing, her right hand still cushioned.

“Your handle,” Adrian said, “is in the book.”

“The escaping one?”

“It hasn't escaped again.”

She lowered her eyes and quietly said good. Beyond the door, Isabella asked who wanted hot breakfast tomorrow. This time nobody proposed distributing it by votes.

Adrian closed the book before opening the new copy. Not every door had opened. Two people could bring what they'd actually received tomorrow. For that specific thing, he was willing to keep his morning free.
