# Chapter 289: The Keys No Longer Wait for One Man

On May 21, Oswin brought an old leather bag.

It contained no crown. Only protective-main-bridge duty records, regional reports, and a piece of bread wrapped in paper. A young clerk stared when the High Priest took out his lunch, as though he should survive on something more elevated.

Noticing, Oswin closed the bag.

“Will this take us past noon?”

The clerk consulted the schedule and honestly said yes.

Across the waiting room, Adrian realized he no longer needed to imagine this man without an ordinary life in order to oppose him. Oswin grew hungry and waited. He'd also chosen to enter other people's names into an arrangement capable of killing them.

Today's independent authority hearing was separate from criminal proceedings. Evidence and objections had already been exchanged. The panel would decide whether Oswin could retain unilateral control over preparation selection, designation requests, and final facility-management approval. It concerned how he'd used his office, not an audience vote for the nicer person.

His representative first observed that restoring joint attendance in April hadn't removed every core-related authority. The outer selector and duty token were already in joint custody. They couldn't theatrically seize them from him again.

The judge agreed. Precisely why the remaining authorization chain required examination. An earlier preservation order couldn't be rewritten as completed reform.

Oswin submitted current reports. The main bridge remained burdened. One border observation lacked a second check. Experimental nodes had never borne the kingdom's calamity load. Independent engineers verified supported findings and preserved the gaps. Real danger wouldn't become a lie to make removing his powers more satisfying.

“You can remove my name,” he said. “Not the next surge.”

“Nobody has asked us to remove it,” the presiding judge replied. “Why does danger require you alone to decide who may not refuse?”

His representative argued that separating maintenance duties from designation authority would let everyone deny responsibility for anything beyond half a page. Adrian looked up. They too had written attractive responsibilities, until two uncovered shifts stopped their fine language at the door.

The engineering supervisor didn't declare everyone jointly responsible. Her proposed handover identified actual posts: authorized ordinary protective operations, independent checks, and requests outside that scope. Unqualified trainees remained assistants. The High Priest's departure couldn't suddenly create another engineer.

One shift had a circled name. The qualified replacement hadn't confirmed arrival.

“You cannot call that covered,” Oswin said.

“Correct.” The supervisor changed the entry to pending confirmation. “Dismantling can't begin before it's resolved. Existing qualified staff still cover today's work without restoring authority to designate a person.”

She hadn't solved every staffing problem, but she'd returned the argument to an actual shift. The panel required verified continuous coverage before new construction work. Missing coverage meant reducing work, not stopping the protective bridge for a ceremonial handover.

A duty engineer raised his own concern. He would maintain the facility, but not sign a blanket promise that no future calamity would harm anyone. Under the old arrangement, the superior kept decisions while failures could be blamed on the person on duty. Changing managers shouldn't merely produce a longer guarantee.

The supervisor removed that line. Its replacement confirmed known conditions, actual responsibilities, and reporting duties. Independent reviewers must genuinely examine the relevant records, not add signatures afterward. The engineer asked who prevailed if their readings differed.

Maintain protection within established safe limits, stop additional work, and refer the discrepancy to the identified technical review post. Rank or noble birth couldn't overrule an observation.

Only then did he set down his pen. This version could be shown to the next shift.

Adrian studied the deleted guarantee. He'd also tried to make people feel safe by promising to take responsibility for everything. Defining what could actually be done was less impressive, but someone could finally put a name beneath it.

Next came April's records: removal of the outer selector, exclusion of independent observers, and enforcement restoring joint custody. Oswin acknowledged acting under preservation authority. He denied that his sole purpose had been coercing the five women. An immature proposal couldn't be allowed to endanger the facility protecting the kingdom.

“Why exclude independent observation too?”

Outsiders could misjudge danger, he replied. The independent reviewer noted that observers hadn't been authorized to open the core. They'd sought observation and a route to submit requests. The evidence didn't justify excluding them all. Preventing mistaken operation wasn't the same as allowing only one person to explain whether a mistake had occurred.

Oswin didn't repeat that nobody else understood. He turned to a main-bridge record and demanded acknowledgment that the new arrangements still depended on old equipment.

The reviewer agreed. The acknowledgment was recorded whole, not buried after an although.

“Depending on equipment doesn't require an unchecked personal monopoly.”

At noon, Isabella collected her witness copy. She had no royal authority to decide the replacement. An old court acquaintance drew her aside. Why not assume final approval herself? People knew the princess. Strangers with engineering qualifications would hardly inspire the same confidence.

Delivering papers, Adrian heard her ask whether that approval still included designating people who refused.

It could have a more suitable name, the acquaintance suggested.

“You're replacing the cushion on the same chair.”

She returned to reception. Any formal proposal should use the actual submission route, not a corridor conversation beyond everyone's hearing. Adrian handed over her copy without lingering for a private smile. She checked the pages and thanked him normally. The distance they'd agreed on two days earlier didn't prevent today's work.

That afternoon, Cecilia was asked whether she'd accept the original powers on behalf of the faithful. She would advise independent oversight, she replied. Opposing Oswin didn't qualify her to inherit a position that answered for others.

“Who leads the Temple, then?” someone shouted.

The judge required orderly questions, then clarified jurisdiction. Worship, doctrine, and ordinary believers weren't appendices to a facility authorization chart. The case concerned particular management and designation powers supported by evidence. It wasn't deciding who could pray. Individual responsibility remained for separate proceedings.

Sitting down, Cecilia whispered that she'd wanted to give a tremendously authoritative answer.

“What happened?” Adrian asked.

“I remembered I couldn't even open my own box.”

He laughed softly. She smiled, then listened seriously to criticism of her oversight proposal. The joke wasn't permission to escape the question.

After final submissions on scope and execution, the panel delivered its ruling. Oswin's use of his office had concentrated risk interpretation, designation, and exclusion of external review. He could no longer retain those unilateral powers. His unilateral operating authority over the relevant facility, unilateral restoration of designation procedures, and associated final endorsement were revoked. They wouldn't pass intact to another individual.

Verified qualified duty arrangements and independent checking would continue maintenance. Neither the five locks nor the core's condition could change merely because authority changed. Controlled dismantling required separate approved limits, physical isolation checks, and staffing conditions. Review remained available, but applying wouldn't automatically restore revoked powers. This wasn't a final judgment on all criminal liability, nor enactment of kingdom-wide repeal.

Oswin asked only whether he could still submit technical opinions.

“Yes. With sources, subject to the same verification.”

After sitting quietly, he closed the old bag. He hadn't lost the chance to say danger existed. He'd lost the power to say it and prohibit any different answer.

On May 22, enforcement officers arrived at joint custody. From the permitted observation area, Adrian watched the duty token recovered in April emerge from its cabinet. Nobody presented it as today's trophy surrendered by Oswin. They checked the original seal record, subsequent receipts, and current location.

Registry entries still directing final approval to Oswin received the revocation reference and effective time. Originals stayed legible; no pages were torn out. An impression tool used solely for that authorization went into evidentiary custody. Ordinary duty equipment remained with actual operators after its use was checked. Nobody locked every maintenance key in court to symbolize an ending.

An older worker hesitated over the new acknowledgment.

“At this point, he used to come and say we could proceed.”

The officer didn't imitate Oswin's voice. He asked the supervisor to read the authorized scope of ordinary operations again. The worker read, checked one notation with the independent reviewer, and signed his own name. A posted request route covered anything beyond his scope. He needn't wait for familiar footsteps.

Before noon, the qualified replacement arrived. A real acknowledgment confirmed coverage at her previous post; they hadn't simply moved the shortage elsewhere. With these two shifts verified, the construction coordinator left later gaps visible. One arrival didn't establish that staffing would never run short again.

Oswin's representative checked the collected impression tools and found an ordinary document seal wrongly included. The officer compared its use, returned it, and corrected the inventory. Some observers frowned. Adrian saw the old duty worker relax a little. Today's order could be corrected without restoring yesterday's master.

The representative kept his copy. The corrected original remained in the custody record beside the reason for the change. It wasn't evidence that the entire ruling had failed, any more than removing the wrong seal would have made the ruling stronger. The people carrying out the decision had to answer for their own actual work.

A witnessing clerk wanted all duty workers beside the cabinet to sign together. The supervisor refused. The person reading the main-bridge gauge could not abandon that position to complete an attractive handover scene. Officers brought the necessary copy to the actual post, where another witness checked receipt. The others would attend when their shifts genuinely ended. The imagined orderly group never assembled. The duty record, however, continued without an empty interval. Adrian stepped aside to let the messenger through. What mattered was that the right person received the instruction, not that everyone could be seen receiving it at once.

At completion, the officer read the notice destinations. Facility staff, judicial custodians, and registration offices acknowledged their respective copies. Unconfirmed distant receipts stayed pending. Local execution was complete; distant silence wasn't converted into assent.

The independent engineering supervisor then checked preparations for May 23's first unpowered dismantling stage. Approval covered only independent selector rods and the recording housing, not the shared frame or main-bridge monitoring mount. Unsuitable isolation or load conditions would stop the work order from beginning.

Elena waited outside with an empty pastry bag.

“Did you save me any?” Adrian asked.

“The bag. You expected to finish fifteen minutes earlier.”

Noticing a smaller packet behind her other hand, he asked whether review remained available.

“Yes. Wash your hands first.”

They waited at the basin. Below, someone called the shift change; above, another person reported the main-bridge readings. No building had become light enough to float away. The world still required many people to perform unglamorous work.

Adrian washed and accepted half a pastry.

Downstairs, the keys no longer needed one man's approval to find their proper hands. Tonight's dinner wouldn't need to emerge from that same man's opinion either.
