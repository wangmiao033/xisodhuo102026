# Why Do All the Villainesses Want to Marry Me?
## Volume One: A Betrothal Written in Blood
### Chapter 6: The Worst Scandal in the Capital

On the morning of September eighteenth, Adrian Reed woke up to a newspaper hitting him in the face.

The paper didn't weigh much.

The headline, on the other hand, hurt.

**DUKE'S DAUGHTER PASSIONATELY KISSES MYSTERY SERVANT AT ROYAL BALL!**

Underneath was a lavish illustration. Elena had her arms around Adrian's neck, gazing into his eyes as though she intended to abandon the entire kingdom for love.

Adrian, meanwhile, had gained several inches in height and a flowing white cape.

"Well," he said, "the artist certainly has an imagination."

Martha stood beside his bed, looking as if he'd complimented an arsonist on the beauty of the flames.

"That's what you're concerned about? The artwork?"

"He made me look like a prince."

"Shall I have it framed for you?"

"Don't be ridiculous."

Adrian hesitated.

"Could I keep a copy, though? I've never been in the papers before."

Martha drew a long breath.

"Her Ladyship is waiting in the study. Get dressed. Now."

Adrian rubbed his neck.

He'd taken off last night's bow tie hours ago, but he could still feel an imaginary knot tightening around his throat.

He and Elena had accidentally brushed lips for a fraction of a second.

According to the morning papers, they'd practically announced their engagement at the royal palace.

And, unfortunately, the actual prince had broken off his engagement to Elena that very same evening.

No wonder the journalists were having the time of their lives.

Back in Adrian's world, the entire internet would have been fighting about it for days.

"Martha, is there any other news?"

"The Judicial Office has confirmed tomorrow morning's emergency hearing."

"I knew that."

"The Royal Protocol Office has declined to comment on the ball."

"Fair enough."

"And three newspapers have requested interviews with you."

Adrian stopped halfway through pulling on his boots.

"Interviews with whom?"

"You."

"Surely there's been a mistake."

"One paper wants to know how long you've been secretly seeing Her Ladyship. Another wants to know whether you're a prince in exile from the northern territories."

He looked down at his scuffed boots.

"Apparently my royal bloodline is news to me."

"You're not speaking to any reporters today." Martha opened the door. "Her Ladyship's orders."

The study curtains had already been drawn back.

Elena sat behind her desk with three newspapers spread out in front of her.

The first described their passionate kiss.

The second claimed Adrian had deliberately tripped Elena so he could seduce his way into a ducal family.

The third announced that House Rosenberg had secretly commissioned an engagement ring for him.

After reading them, Adrian said with complete sincerity, "The third one is the most ridiculous."

"Why?"

"I haven't even seen a ring."

Elena pushed a paper toward him.

"According to this, you told His Highness that you're the man I truly love."

"I never said that."

"It also says I begged you not to leave me. In public."

"Didn't happen."

"And you supposedly accepted my feelings and declared war on the royal family."

"I stepped on your dress."

Elena closed her eyes.

"I'm beginning to think that one misplaced foot is more dangerous than the prince's forged letter."

Adrian managed not to laugh.

She opened her eyes and saw his rigid expression.

"If you want to laugh, laugh."

"I wouldn't dare."

"Why?"

"I'm afraid you'll have my picture printed on a wanted poster next."

That earned the smallest hint of a smile.

Elena flattened one of the newspapers. Her fingers rested over the illustration of Adrian's improbably passionate face.

"It doesn't look like you."

"You noticed too?"

"You're not that tall."

"...Could someone in this house please find a different way to evaluate me?"

"And for the record, you tripped over your own feet last night."

She seemed to realize what she'd said and pressed her lips together.

Adrian suddenly had no idea where to look.

He remembered the ballroom lights. The pull of Elena's hand on his collar.

The kiss had lasted barely a heartbeat.

That didn't mean he'd forgotten it.

Elena lifted her teacup as though nothing had happened.

"Do not give interviews."

"Why would anyone want to interview an ordinary servant?"

"Because everyone in the capital knows your name now."

Adrian stopped smiling.

Leon had known his full name before they'd even met. The prince had asked about the scar hidden beneath Adrian's glove.

If the royal family was searching for someone called Adrian Reed, the newspapers had just provided a free citywide announcement that he'd been found.

Elena saw his expression change.

"You thought of that too?"

"I'm starting to think the prince may not be the only one looking for me."

She nodded, but didn't press him.

Another stack of documents lay on the desk, tied with a ribbon bearing the Rosenberg crest.

"Father has instructed our lawyers to prepare for tomorrow's hearing," she said. "You'll testify about the evidence ledger. Clay has also requested an explanation of how you obtained your information."

"He wants me to admit I knew what was in the forged letter before anyone presented it."

"Or make you invent an explanation he can dismantle."

Adrian pulled out a chair.

"Then we need something that will force a review of the evidence without depending on where my information came from."

"That's why I asked you here."

She slid a purchasing record across the desk.

"You said the jewelry case registered as Item 714 isn't the same one we sealed at the estate. Father had the original inspected this morning. It's still there."

"Do we have witnesses?"

"Martha and Father's clerk signed the sealing record. Anyone claiming we brought it back from the palace will have to explain how it remained in our custody the entire time."

Adrian nodded.

Not a victory.

But one avenue of attack was closed.

"There's also the sealing wax."

Elena unfolded another sheet.

Last week, House Rosenberg had purchased six boxes of blue sealing wax from an old stationery shop on South Street. The Judicial Office ordered wax of the same color from the same shop one day later.

Adrian frowned.

"Lots of people use blue wax, don't they?"

"The shop sells more than a dozen colors."

"Still doesn't prove the same person made the seals."

"Which is why I asked for the batch records."

She placed a thin sheet over the first.

The supplier had recorded the mixture used for each shipment.

The ducal household's delivery and the Judicial Office's additional order came from the same batch.

"If the wax on last night's evidence came from that batch," Adrian said, "we might be able to trace it through the purchasing and issue records. Find out who handled it."

"Don't turn a matching batch into proof of a crime."

"I know. Correlation isn't conspiracy."

Elena raised an eyebrow.

"Correlation isn't what?"

"Two things matching doesn't mean whoever handled them committed a crime."

"Very good. You said that more fluently than anything you managed last night."

Adrian reflected that his education in statistics had finally found a practical application.

Someone knocked.

Martha entered with a young bookkeeper in spectacles.

"The warehouse issue ledger, my lady."

The bookkeeper placed a heavy volume on the desk. He looked anxious enough to hold his breath.

Elena motioned for him to stay.

"Who normally signs for these supplies?"

"Deputy Steward Henry, my lady. Stationery, ribbons, ink, ceremonial materials—he checks all of it."

"And yesterday?"

"He signed for those as well. But he went out during the afternoon. Said the royal purchasing office needed some pages from our accounts."

Adrian looked up.

"Which pages?"

"I didn't see. Only heard him say the palace was in a hurry."

Elena asked him to assist with the reconciliation.

Adrian opened the ledger to September sixteenth and seventeenth and laid both pages side by side.

Most entries were unremarkable.

Two transactions in the wax column were not.

Six boxes had been issued for routine Rosenberg correspondence.

Another three carried the note:

*Temporarily issued on behalf of Royal Purchasing.*

Both entries bore the same signature.

Henry.

"Can a ducal household issue goods on behalf of the Judicial Office?" Adrian asked.

"Not normally," the bookkeeper admitted. "But royal departments occasionally borrow ceremonial supplies from the larger houses and settle the accounts afterward."

"Who approved it?"

The man looked down.

"There's no authorizing signature."

Elena's gaze hardened.

"So three boxes left our warehouse, and there's no record of who received them."

The bookkeeper went pale.

"My lady, I only copied what the deputy steward gave me."

"I'm not accusing you. Leave the original ledger here. We'll seal the relevant pages, and two employees who don't handle purchasing will witness it."

The young man bowed with visible relief.

Adrian stared at Henry's signatures.

If information and materials really were passing from the Rosenberg estate to the Judicial Office, the royal family might know far more about Elena's daily movements than Adrian had assumed.

Someone might even have reported the arrival of a mysterious servant before the ball.

"When did Henry take charge of these supplies?" he asked.

"Two years ago. He worked in the outer grounds before that. He became deputy steward six months ago."

"Who recommended him?"

"An old friend of Father's."

"Should we call him in?"

"Not yet."

"Why?"

"Didn't you say we shouldn't let someone with inside information know we're investigating them?"

Adrian blinked.

He'd mentioned it almost in passing.

She'd remembered.

"Then let's compare the other purchasing records first," he said. "See whether the wax is the only thing that doesn't add up."

They buried themselves in the accounts.

At some point, their tea went cold.

Martha brought in a plate of pastries.

Adrian reached for one.

Elena moved the plate out of reach.

"Wash your hands."

"I'm not a child."

"You've been handling old account books for the last hour."

"They aren't that old."

"Wash."

He surrendered.

When he returned, a small cream biscuit sat beside his teacup.

Elena was still reading, looking as though she knew nothing about it.

Adrian picked up the biscuit.

"Thank you."

"For what?"

"Nothing."

He took a bite.

Investigations, he decided, weren't entirely without their rewards.

That feeling lasted until the final reconciliation sheet arrived.

A little before the lawyers' meeting, the bookkeeper brought over two receipts.

The ducal estate's record and the Judicial Office's delivery record both carried the same receiving signature.

Henry.

The entry on the Judicial Office side read:

*September 17 — Delivered to Palace Evidence Reception.*

Elena put down her pen.

"We can at least establish that someone used our supply channel to move sealing wax into the palace."

"We don't know whether Henry helped knowingly or was used by someone else."

"Which means we need to act before he realizes we've examined his records."

Outside, a newsboy was shouting through the streets.

"DUKE'S DAUGHTER! MYSTERY SERVANT! NEW DETAILS OF THE PALACE KISS!"

Adrian thought of the morning's ridiculous headlines.

The city was staring at an accidental kiss.

Meanwhile, the three boxes of blue wax that might actually matter were sitting quietly in an account book.

"My lady," he said, "perhaps we shouldn't rush to deny what happened on the dance floor."

Elena looked up.

"Why not?"

"Everyone's watching to see whether we're getting engaged. No one's paying attention to what we're investigating in the warehouse."

She considered him for a long moment.

"Adrian."

"Yes?"

"You're finally learning to make gossip work for you."

"So you're willing to let it continue?"

"For now." She closed the newspaper. "But you're not answering any reporters' questions about my love life."

"Why?"

"Because I don't trust your answers."

She rose, the tips of her ears faintly pink.

Adrian wanted to argue, but his gaze fell on the two delivery records again.

Henry had signed the ducal ledger.

Henry had signed the Judicial Office's receipt.

The first person they needed to question finally had a name.

Whether he'd be willing to talk was another matter.

Adrian started to reach for the bell.

He could summon Henry immediately. Order the guards to hold him until he'd explained everything.

His hand stopped short.

Last night, he'd insisted that the evidence ledger be preserved because people needed the chance to examine the facts. If he now detained a steward without grounds, Clay could accuse the Rosenbergs of intimidating a witness.

Adrian slowly withdrew his hand.

"What is it?" Elena asked.

"I nearly did exactly what the prince wanted you to do last night."

Elena glanced at the two signatures.

"Then let's find a way to ask our questions without arresting anyone."
