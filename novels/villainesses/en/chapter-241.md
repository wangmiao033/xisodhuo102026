# Chapter 241: People After the Lights Went Out

The night wind smelled of wet stone on April 8.

By the time Adrian and Elena reached the riverside duty station, the third lamp was going dark. Not an entire row plunging into darkness together: one trembling point, then the stabilizing plate fading to gray inside its cover. Ordinary oil lamps in shop doorways supplied the remaining yellow light.

The bridge hadn't fallen. The river hadn't reversed. An elderly man pulling a handcart nevertheless misjudged a step. A wheel caught in a gap, a sack slid, and beans rolled through the stone joints toward the water.

Adrian nearly ran at the cart. Seeing its handles still in the man's hands as it tilted, he called for him to let go. An attendant braced the wheel with a long pole. Someone else helped the man back from level ground. Nobody jumped onto slippery steps to recover beans.

When the sack burst completely, the man swore. That was tomorrow's stock. Being alive wouldn't put it back in the bag.

Elena asked a shopkeeper to lend an ordinary lamp, obtaining permission before moving it. A works employee marked the unsafe steps. She sat the man somewhere dry while duty medical staff washed his scraped palm and checked for other injuries. Adrian carried intact sacks from level ground inside. He didn't mix sewage-soaked beans back in to make the loss look smaller.

“Are you here about the lamps?” the man asked.

“First, to see what's needed here,” Elena replied.

Noticing her clothes, he asked whether she could replace all his stock. She didn't say yes to avoid appearing heartless. Staff recorded the actual loss and scene. He could ask through the district's established damage-claim procedure; she couldn't promise an award. He turned away, disliking the answer.

Adrian disliked it too. He recorded sack numbers and the man's stated purchase price, marking what remained unverified rather than filling the gap with a comforting amount.

The duty technician opened a disconnected lamp's outer cover. Old and replacement plates rested separately on insulated trays. Two dark pieces weren't safe to handle merely because both looked inactive. The academy's east side and this street used different ordinary supply lines, the technician said, but had similar symptoms. Their observation times needed comparison. He kept the two pieces separate while explaining, refusing to let a convenient account of the evening merge a failed original with a replacement that had also behaved abnormally. Which item had done what, and when, could matter later.

“Could reset preparation be involved?” Adrian asked.

“Possibly. Or there's a common condition we haven't excluded. Wanting me to know tonight won't make me know sooner.”

Adrian apologized. The technician wasn't forbidding questions. He needed witness positions and the order of failures, without Adrian's theories placed in their mouths.

Inside, someone called this the consequence of refusing to let the five women bear the burden. Another insisted the Temple must have extinguished the lights to frighten people. Their voices rose. Clutching an uncounted sack, the old man muttered that neither had seen how his wheel fell.

Adrian asked staff to separate argument from witness questions and listened to him first. He'd reached the second step before the third lamp faded. He saw nobody from the Temple touch it. He didn't know what happened inside. Those unknowns remained in his account. Adrian did not offer the witness a choice between two satisfying explanations when the man could support neither one from where he had stood. They neither established Oswin's necessity nor proved a conspiracy for Adrian.

The next anomaly wasn't a lamp.

A night worker from the riverside protection station brought a sealed measurement strip. Ordinary water levels hadn't risen significantly, but the outer buffer's load had increased sharply. Staff had narrowed local passage under existing authority, retaining another ground-level route. Current protection was responding to pressure. The academy's new model wasn't operating here.

An independent academy observation post reported later. Different instruments and staff also recorded a disturbance, but clock discrepancies and ranges required checking. Similar-looking traces couldn't simply be overlaid and pronounced identical.

Did residents need to leave tonight? Elena asked. The duty supervisor ordered the riverside ground-level storerooms and stairway cleared for now, not the whole street. A further notice would follow if another indicator reached its established condition. Elena neither announced a citywide flight nor treated the lack of one as permission to keep goods in the restricted area.

She helped move light objects into shops. Suitable workers handled heavy crates. After two trips, rough rope hurt Adrian's palms. The crescent scar supplied no answer. It remained part of a hand that needed rest.

A young clerk tried to hang an oil lamp from the disconnected magical fixture. The technician stopped him, indicating the work zone and flammable wrapping. Adrian helped clear a safer place inside a doorway. Its light couldn't reach the entire street, but it illuminated the detour sign.

During the storeroom clearance, a shopkeeper refused further handling. The innermost sealed chest belonged to a tenant. His custody key wasn't permission to open it. Staff had wanted to unpack it to reduce weight. Instead, they asked whether two movers could carry it intact by a safe internal route into an upstairs common room.

The tenant lived across the street. While someone fetched him, the works employee checked the chest's base and prepared a sound carrying board. Adrian wasn't allowed to wrestle it upstairs through enthusiasm alone.

The tenant arrived with his trouser legs still untidy. His first words weren't thanks. His late wife's books were inside. They mustn't get wet, and nobody was to read them.

Elena showed him the intended place. He and the shopkeeper would jointly record the seal. Only after two movers set the chest down securely did he loosen his grip on the key. Adrian marked chest moved, not contents inspected. Danger could demand speed without granting every key to every memory.

A mover wiped his forehead and asked what came next. The supervisor checked his hours and arranged relief. Tonight's exceptional circumstances didn't remove everyone's need to rest. By the time they'd secured a little light and an unopened chest, the distant clock had passed midnight.

Elena brought Adrian water and asked about his hand. He showed her rope marks, no new wound. She didn't keep hold of it; another carrying party needed to pass between them.

“Were you going after the beans?”

“Yes. I wanted to recover something I could actually see.”

“You told him to let go. You recovered something visible then.”

Across the room, the man was cleaning his shoes. He didn't thank them. He said he'd never use those steps again. Adrian smiled without requiring that statement to become approval of him.

The hardest stretch came when nothing further broke. The disturbance wasn't spreading, but the technician couldn't declare restoration. They waited indoors while an ordinary lamp consumed its oil, workers made regular inspections, and measurements proceeded at a pace they couldn't hasten.

Was he cold? Elena asked. Yes. She told him to fasten his coat rather than surrendering her own shawl so one of them had to freeze. On the narrow bench their shoulders occasionally touched. Neither moved away.

“I'm frightened,” she said suddenly.

Her eyes stayed on the dark, blocked path outside.

“That it's real, and faster than anything we can do. And that someone will point to these lamps and say nobody would have to wait if I'd just go.”

Adrian nearly promised he would never let her. Then he heard what she'd actually said. She hadn't come to request permission to be protected.

“If that argument affects you, you can tell me,” he said. “I'm frightened too—of wanting to accept for you, or refuse for you, just to get us out of this moment.”

She looked at him and slowly nodded.

“Then let's promise not to secretly finish that decision for each other.”

“I promise.”

It gave the kingdom no new defense. It still loosened something in his chest. She was allowing him to know her fear, not offering endless courage as a resource he could use.

Could he sit closer? She shifted her shoulder toward his and warned that if he applied as formally as someone borrowing a chair, she'd start charging by the hour.

“I can't afford very much.”

“Tonight, one whole breath.”

He inhaled deeply and laughed. For once the laughter wasn't all effort spent holding up a room.

Later an attendant brought bread and a small pot of something hot. Nobody called it a date. Nobody required a crisis to contain only equipment talk. They discussed the travel writer who kept losing hats. Had Adrian begun recording every hat's whereabouts as pending verification?

“Almost.”

“Then his greatest good fortune is not having to attend our meetings.”

Wheels interrupted their laughter. Not miraculous rescue: a replacement shift. The existing station had arranged experienced technicians to continue the checks. The first technician finally set down his tools and drank. Lasting this long hadn't obligated him to last until everything ended.

Before dawn, the combined report became a little clearer. After clock checks, several independent records showed increased load during the same part of the night. Local lamp failures occurred nearby in time. The exact transmission relationship remained uncertain. That justified closer monitoring and ordinary protective preparations. It didn't prove a particular woman must die or that the academy's limited model could take over the capital.

Adrian recorded both easily discarded boundaries. His hand shook less now. They didn't possess an answer. They possessed something real that made waiting entirely on paper impossible.

When the elderly man collected his intact sacks, he asked whether he could sell elsewhere today. Staff checked a road outside the restrictions. A shopkeeper offered a small space near his door without a charge for the first day—only that day, not an indefinite promise. The man accepted without declaring allegiance to either side.

He retied the torn sack and borrowed a broom. Dry beans scattered at his feet went into a waste bucket, not tomorrow's stock. Adrian steadied the bucket. Neither gave the loss a prettier name.

Elena watched the two men discuss space and cleaning.

“Today, perhaps we ask what people can actually do,” she said. “Before making a list of people willing to die for the kingdom.”

Adrian nodded. He would propose a voluntary-contract pilot small enough to explain and refuse. First came conditions and inquiries, not signatures obtained through the fear of this night. Between a limited experiment and people stood many real obstacles.

The magical lamps hadn't all returned when they left. An oil lamp remained inside a shop doorway. An early baker used its light to move the detour sign where newcomers could see it.

Adrian remembered him bending to shift the board. Whatever they did next couldn't reduce a person like that to an available number for connection.
