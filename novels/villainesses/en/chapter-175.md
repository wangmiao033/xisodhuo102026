# Chapter 175: No Partner Goes in This Box

The afternoon's first new form still made Adrian their joint applicant.

The clerk said that the heading had been shortened that way to make it easier for people in the public seats to understand the purpose of the documents. Five ladies submitting views on one agenda item made more sense to spectators if they knew whom the women were protecting.

“I'm here too,” Adrian said.

February 5's afternoon light lay across the table. He turned the cover toward himself and indicated the relationship field: joint relationship pending confirmation. Polite words, apparently awaiting only six nods.

Elena's mouth tightened. Cecilia asked the clerk to wait before producing a clean copy. They weren't jointly applying for an engagement. Nobody had authorized personal relationships to become outstanding paperwork.

Red-faced, he explained that he'd followed the morning list without ill intent.

“Let's change it now.”

Adrian separated his statement of facts. Elena and Cecilia checked their own texts. Luna's and Viola's written views remained limited by their individual signatures; absence didn't permit merging them.

Isabella remarked that joint applicant at least sounded better than prospective fiancé. Elena looked up. The princess realized this wasn't a contest between pleasant-sounding descriptions.

“I'm using a joke to decide your title,” she told Adrian. “Make it Mr. Reed.”

“Thank you.”

“How cold that sounds.”

“My name is allowed in the text. You needn't add mister every time.”

Cecilia laughed. The nervous clerk asked whether to include his official position. Adrian was speaking for himself today. Employment didn't consent on his behalf. Eventually the field disappeared rather than acquiring a better title.

Returning, the chair warned that six separate papers didn't mean extra time for repeated arguments.

Elena replied that they had already arranged the sequence. She would begin with the practical barriers ordinary applicants encountered. Cecilia would examine the leap from a historical relationship to a claim of present consent. The technical and travel opinions would be read in approved extracts, and Isabella would answer questions about implementation. Adrian had kept time for his own statement. The women would not take turns explaining what he wanted instead of letting him say it.

Questions were sharper than that morning. If risk was real, why shouldn't the authority require selection of someone able to share it? Someone refusing to choose might still demand rescue after an accident. Was that freedom only for himself?

Cecilia didn't claim courage eliminated danger.

“If technical information is necessary, explain why. If an arrangement changes another person's burden, ask that person. Someone who hasn't agreed cannot be entered in advance as an available safety instrument.”

She indicated the corrected cover. A perfectly ordinary mistake, requiring no malicious clerk, could still tell the next reader that a relationship merely awaited signatures.

Elena presented the permitted facts from Beth's case. Missing material should be identified; supplementary material should receive a truthful acknowledgment. Families could help without becoming the door through which every individual objection must pass.

A member asked whether this encouraged children to disobey parents. She distinguished adults from minors. The proposal concerned people with independent standing to apply. Voluntary wasn't a way around every other necessary rule.

“Nor must everyone reject family help. I've used my family's resources. Help still cannot answer for me.”

She didn't look toward Adrian for approval. Their independent assessment of employment and intimacy remained unfinished. She couldn't announce herself free of every imbalance.

Viola's statement followed. Protected travelers could still change whether and how they testified. Guards handled danger through their actual responsibilities; assistance didn't purchase testimony. She'd specified that previously she had only advised on routes, neither claiming the neutral sergeant's work nor guaranteeing permanently safe roads through knightly honor.

Luna required genuine equipment checks. An injured researcher wasn't an institution's substitute for missing staff; a limited diagnosis wasn't a universal endorsement. Unknown had to remain in risk explanations. Untested couldn't become zero, followed by a demand that participants accept all known danger.

A member interrupted to complain that, if every qualification had to be included, each page would become so long that nobody at a public counter could use it.

Adrian rose with an ordinary receipt sample.

“This says received in full twice. Replace one with what was and wasn't received. It doesn't require larger paper.”

The registration representative took it. Listing each item in full would still overflow. Adrian proposed referencing the existing numbered inventory and its page count, not copying everything into the narrow box.

“Attachments get lost.”

“Then attachments held separately cannot be the only surviving description.”

Instead of arguing further, the representative asked an actual counter worker what would function. A woman with dried ink on her fingers came forward and turned their handsome sample sideways.

“This box is too narrow. Common explanations won't fit. We'll end up writing supplement required again.”

She used the deleted relationship field for the reason handling remained incomplete. Each side should retain a receipt; the applicant shouldn't take away the only original proof. Two aristocrats moved aside while someone who wrote these entries all day rearranged the paper.

Isabella didn't insist her lettering looked better. Could existing supplies and staff support this limited trial? The woman answered yes at a specified counter within its current hours. Recopying entire files would exceed that capacity.

Unfunded copying promises were removed. Necessary new costs still required proper explanation. Applicants wouldn't pay the original intake fee again for supplements, but nobody declared all future copying free. After checking the real scope, the supervisor accepted responsibility for a limited arrangement at one temporary counter.

Some supporters nodded. An opponent proposed a final condition: during the trial, accept only family-approved relationship objections, to avoid repeated disputes and withdrawals exhausting the staff.

Elena shook her head.

“That's the very door we're trying to change.”

The chair requested reasons, not mere disagreement. She moved Beth's material closer, keeping the public identity covered. An adult applicant's previous household relationship had ended. A nonexistent husband or uncooperative relative couldn't be required to authorize her submission.

Elena didn't perform grief. She read the necessary facts and stopped. The remaining silence was harder to avoid than another passionate speech.

The amendment was not included in the text put to the vote. The final proposal did not contain everything they had wanted, but it retained the essential parts: independent intake, an explanation of the material actually received, and individual choices recorded separately. Technical scrutiny would not disappear, and historical records would not be destroyed. What neither of those things could do was automatically stand in for a person's current consent.

During the recorded vote, Isabella stopped staring at someone who'd promised support. Merriwell voted yes and specified the scope she accepted. Another morning supporter voted no, citing insufficient reviewers.

It passed. The competent authority confirmed implementation within its powers at the designated temporary counter, followed by a report on intake and rejections after one week. This wasn't a new national law, removal of every risk restriction, or an engagement certificate for anyone.

Hearing passed, Adrian briefly forgot how to sit. Elena touched his sleeve, waited for him to turn, and moved the chair backward.

“You're blocking the clerk.”

He hurried aside. Her mouth finally curved. Victory didn't automatically reveal where one was standing.

Afterward they watched the counter worker test her revised form. An adult son was supplementing documents terminating his father's old guarantee. He'd heard nothing of the six people's arguments. He wanted to know whether he had to pay again.

The woman checked his original application before answering. He would not be charged that original intake fee again. He still needed to supply the missing certified document, however. She recorded the two pages she had actually accepted and did not describe his case as concluded. He looked at the new receipt for a while, then asked which piece of paper he should bring when he returned.

She indicated its reference and retained the other copy. Adrian didn't step forward to announce who had won this page.

Before departure, the clerk caught them and asked whether their six opinions could be bound together. He'd left collective proposal on the cover. Elena glanced at Adrian, expecting another sigh. He didn't object immediately.

“A joint proposal for this procedure is fine. Keep where our reasons differ.”

Each statement's signature and scope should remain. Absent contributors shouldn't become speakers present in the chamber. Cecilia added a corrected sentence. Isabella checked the result before the string went around the folder. Together didn't have to mean identical; separate needn't always resemble a quarrel.

Asked for a title, Isabella offered two long ones. Luna's messenger looked at the narrow label and observed that reform might not fit before the edge. Elena laughed and suggested date and subject.

February 5. Ordinary intake procedure.

Seeing the plain words written, Adrian finally put away the gloves he'd been holding. Every sheet needn't remember his name for this day to have happened.

In the carriage, Cecilia let her stiff back rest against the cushions. Isabella proposed celebrating, then asked who was free that evening rather than distributing places by votes.

Elena would ask those at home. Luna might be ready to rest. Viola shouldn't sit through another long session because the news was good. Adrian wanted food before presiding over anything else.

“Mr. Reed's first reform achievement is supper,” Isabella said.

“You may quote that.”

At home, Luna read the result before saying anything. Her first response was that she would not join a long review around the dining table. She was tired. If they wanted to tell her about it, she could listen for ten minutes beside the fire. Viola would sit for only a short time as well. Her left arm remained in its support; celebration was not a reason to remove it.

Martha brought soup and set six bowls down without attempting to make the arrangement symmetrical. The most convenient spaces were kept clear for injured hands. Anyone who could reach without difficulty moved a little farther away. Nobody treated that small difference in where they sat as proof that they occupied a lower place in someone's affections.

They described what had passed and what hadn't. No joint engagement, no selection of Adrian's most suitable partner. The counter worker's redesigned little box made them laugh. Luna observed that at least someone had admitted handsome paper could mislead.

After soup, Elena asked Adrian to go out with her tomorrow.

“Not delivering papers. Not discussing everyone else.”

He agreed, suggesting somewhere they could leave their folders at home.

She lowered her face to hide a smile, then asked whether he minded first helping with a question that genuinely belonged to her.

Adrian asked what it was before agreeing to it. She rested her hand on the table without reaching for a form.

“I don't want us to explain what we are only when someone else asks.”
