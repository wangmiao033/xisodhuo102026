# Why Do All the Villainesses Want to Marry Me?
## Volume Two: The Saint Won't Let Me Get Married
### Chapter 37: The Saint's Attendant Never Came Back

On October seventeenth, Adrian saw a saint put her breakfast knife down the wrong way.

It was a small thing.

Cecilia Saintclair was usually so precise that she seemed capable of cataloguing the crumbs on her plate.

This morning, the knife lay on the right side with its cutting edge facing outward.

If Elena hadn't quietly turned it around, Adrian might have mistaken it for a new Temple distress signal.

"Still no word from Mira?" he asked.

Cecilia shook her head.

"I went back to the guesthouse at ten last night. Her spare clothes were there. Her bed hadn't been touched. Sometimes she stays late helping at the clinic, but she always leaves a note in the visitor book."

Elena pushed a plate of bread nearer.

"Try to eat something. Hale has contacted the municipal patrol. They'll investigate her movements as a missing-person matter."

"I can look for her myself," Cecilia said.

"Of course you can. But you filed a formal objection to Internal Oversight's secrecy order yesterday. If you start moving your Temple staff around without keeping records, the same office will accuse you of misusing your authority."

Cecilia looked down.

"I don't want her blamed for helping me."

Adrian remembered Mira laughing about the dockworker who'd wanted apple pie instead of medicine.

A person with friends, favorite jokes, and a habit of drawing suns for children had somehow become two words on an administrative report:

*Failed to return.*

"We should find out what Mira chose to do last," he said. "Not decide what happened to her before we know."

Cecilia looked at him.

At last she took a bite of bread.

"I'm trying. But I'm frightened."

Elena didn't tell her there was no reason to be.

"Then we can be frightened and still check the facts."

Hale arrived with a short timeline.

At two in the afternoon on October sixteenth, Mira left the Temple guesthouse carrying a medicine transfer request.

Around two twenty, a coachman had seen her near the lower-district distribution depot.

After three, someone told the guesthouse she'd been redirected to the western oracle receiving office.

That messenger had not identified himself.

At four thirty, someone had added *assignment extended* to the guesthouse's exit register in darker ink.

Mira had not signed it.

"Different ink doesn't by itself prove forgery," Hale said. "A duty clerk could have made the note for her. We should speak to whoever was on duty."

Adrian nodded.

He had no intention of marching into the guesthouse shouting, *Who kidnapped Mira?*

Not yet.

The clerk at the Temple guesthouse looked distinctly uncomfortable when Cecilia arrived.

"Your Holiness, under the new oversight instruction, internal assignments are not to be discussed before outsiders."

"I'm verifying the safety of an employee," Cecilia replied. "I'm not publishing any findings about R-074."

"But she was delivering something related to a patient prognosis."

"She carried an ordinary medicine acknowledgment form, not the confidential prognosis. You're welcome to examine the instructions I signed."

The clerk read the copy twice.

Hale asked to inspect only the guesthouse records needed to establish Mira's departure and any later redirection. He did not ask for unrelated employees' personal movements.

After a long hesitation, the clerk called for the elderly doorman who'd been on duty.

He remembered Mira clearly.

"She asked if rain was coming. I told her there might be wind after sundown. She said to tell the saint she'd bring back the medicine receipt."

"Who said the assignment had been extended?" Cecilia asked.

"A courier wearing a gray cloak. Had a temporary reassignment slip from the Administrative Duty Office. Said the depot was short of medicine and Mira was needed somewhere else."

"Did he leave the slip?"

The old man took a folded sheet from his drawer.

It bore an Old Contract Records Office heading, but only a transit reference, not a named authorizing officer.

Hale requested confirmation of its origin.

Adrian noticed the smallest line of print at the bottom.

*"Acceptance of reassignment constitutes acknowledgment of a confidentiality instruction. Details of the new route need not be disclosed to the original supervisor."*

"Not even to the person who sent her out?" he asked.

The old man sighed.

"I thought it was strange too. But he was dressed as a Temple official and said patients urgently needed medicine."

Cecilia gripped the edge of the desk.

"I sent Mira to deliver a medicine form. That doesn't give anyone the right to change her assignment without telling her what she's agreeing to."

She spoke quietly.

Several junior attendants nearby looked up.

Hale recorded the temporary pass number and requested confirmation from the Administrative Duty Office.

They also filed the missing-person report with the municipality.

That way they wouldn't be relying entirely on the Temple's own account.

Outside the guesthouse, Adrian suggested finding the coachman.

His name was Toby. He hauled supplies around the medicine depot, sometimes for the Temple and sometimes for merchants.

With a patrol officer present, Toby confirmed that he'd seen Mira the previous afternoon.

"She was standing by the depot with a little parcel, like she was waiting for someone. Then a light medicine wagon arrived. Two boxes on it had blue-green seals."

"Did you see her get into the wagon?" Elena asked.

"I saw her approach the door. Someone called me away to unload flour. I didn't see her climb aboard."

Adrian stopped himself from treating that as a confirmed boarding.

"Did you see the wagon leave?"

"Yes. Turned toward Mercy Clinic."

It wasn't proof Mira had been in the vehicle.

It was a route they could check.

Toby remembered one more detail.

"The wagon had a wooden plaque on the side, with the Temple's five-petaled flower. It was hanging crooked. I was about to tell the driver, but he turned the corner first."

"Are you sure about the design?" Adrian asked.

"I've unloaded supplies here for ten years. I've seen the Temple sign often enough. Though the wood was darker than usual."

Hale asked him to sketch what he remembered and note that the drawing was a reconstruction, not a unique vehicle identifier.

Toby's artistic talents were limited.

The sacred five-petaled flower looked rather like a frightened crab.

Adrian stared at it.

"If that's really the Temple's crest, I suspect the gods will file a complaint."

Toby burst out laughing.

Cecilia carefully put the sketch in the witness file.

"Thank you for not pretending you saw more than you did."

Toby scratched his head.

"Just telling you what I remember."

Adrian thought of Mira taking what should have been a routine delivery assignment.

Why did speaking honestly about ordinary records so often turn into something dangerous?

The medicine depot's manager found the previous day's shipping entries.

Three crates of fever medicines and two bags of sterile cloth had been registered.

The destination was Mercy Clinic.

But the driver field had been amended to *Special Temple Dispatch*.

"Why the change?" Hale asked.

"The Administrative Duty Office sent revised instructions. Said the wagon needed to carry a corrected oracle-patient form too."

Cecilia looked up.

"Private prognosis records aren't supposed to travel in ordinary medicine crates. They're subject to a separate privacy procedure."

"We only move the goods," the manager replied. "We don't read the papers."

"Who signed for them?"

He pointed to a crooked blue stamp in the receiving column.

The lettering was faint.

Adrian could make out one code:

C-19.

"Wasn't that on the patient list you showed us yesterday?" he asked Cecilia.

"I remember a similar reference. I won't say it belongs to Jonah until we've checked the clinic's actual register."

They found a second clerk who'd worked the depot's morning shift.

She confirmed the wagon had been assigned to Mercy Clinic, though she couldn't say whether all the supplies were delivered.

"Did you see Mira?" Cecilia asked.

"A freckled girl who insisted the patient number had to match the medicine crate number? Yes. Someone complained she was delaying the departure."

"Who?"

"A man in a gray Temple armband. I don't remember his face clearly."

"Did you see him force Mira into the wagon?"

"No. I only heard them arguing about whether to go to the clinic first or to the records office."

Cecilia wrote the statement precisely.

*Argument concerning route. No witnessed forced boarding.*

Elena glanced at her.

"That wording might make the search look slower."

"If I write down something the witness didn't see," Cecilia said, "why should Mira trust me once we find her?"

They were preparing to leave when an elderly chestnut seller called out.

"Are you looking for the little girl with freckles?"

Cecilia turned at once.

"Did you see her?"

"Yesterday. She asked me to look after a little cloth purse and said she'd collect it when she came back. She never did."

The woman produced a small pouch.

Inside were a few copper coins, a red thread bracelet apparently made for a child, and a tightly folded note.

She didn't simply hand the purse over.

"It's hers. I don't know who should have it if she doesn't come back."

Cecilia respected that.

"Please keep it safe. A patrol officer can issue a proper receipt if you decide to give it to the investigation."

The woman hesitated, then unfolded the little paper for them to read.

A hurried line filled its center.

*If I don't come back, check who actually received the clinic's medicine.*

The handwriting still needed authentication.

Cecilia looked at it.

For the first time that day, her eyes filled with tears.

Adrian didn't try to offer a comforting explanation.

The red thread bracelet hadn't been part of any conspiracy.

Mira had meant to give it to a child, finish her work, and go home.

Elena rested a hand on Cecilia's shoulder.

"We'll look for her. First we establish where she went yesterday afternoon."

Cecilia closed her eyes.

"I don't want her reduced to a beautiful report."

"Then tomorrow we visit the clinic and look for a living person," Adrian said.

He thought of the date he'd shared with Elena the night before.

He was almost grateful she hadn't blamed him for taking time to enjoy himself. Nobody could protect every person they cared about by refusing to rest forever.

That evening, Hale filed supplementary material with the municipal patrol. Mira remained officially listed as missing. Whether anyone had unlawfully restricted her freedom was still under investigation.

Cecilia didn't want to return to the Temple guesthouse.

"If I go back tonight, they'll probably ask me to sign an apology before I can even begin looking for her."

Elena considered.

"You're welcome to use a guest room at the estate. You don't have to accept simply because I'm a duke's daughter. We have independent counsel and proper visitor records, and it'll be easier to leave together in the morning."

Cecilia accepted.

While Martha prepared the room, Adrian and Elena stood in the corridor for a moment.

"Our first proper dinner lasted one evening," Adrian said.

"One evening and half a dessert we never finished."

"I owe you the other half."

"You do." Elena took his hand. "But tomorrow, don't try to compensate by running to the clinic alone and playing the hero."

"I know."

She moved a little closer.

"I like that you want to help people. I don't want to have to read a missing-person notice to find out where you've gone."

Adrian nodded.

They were about to part when Cecilia emerged with Mira's original assignment copy.

"I remembered something."

She pointed to a note in the corner.

Mira had added it before leaving:

*If a patient's identity has been corrected, confirm the physician's signature before reprinting the oracle register.*

Adrian studied the neat handwriting.

"So she wasn't only checking the medicine."

"She knew somebody was changing a patient's name," Cecilia said. "She didn't realize somebody might change her route at the same time."

Elena looked out toward the darkening streets.

"Tomorrow we begin at Mercy Clinic. Not with the Temple's prophecy list—with the people actually receiving treatment."

Nobody objected.
