# Chapter 248: Open the Door Before Signing

The first person into the signing room on April 13 didn't ask for a pen.

She asked where the lavatory was.

Adrian indicated the side passage, then noticed the folding chairs stacked across it. He and the receptionist carried them back into storage. When he returned, the literacy teacher was sitting with her hat on her knees. She hadn't opened the agreement.

“Is that also the way out if I want to leave halfway through?”

“Yes. Nobody connects to any apparatus today.”

“Then tell everyone before you begin.”

Her voice was calm. Adrian thought of all his attractive withdrawal diagrams. It had taken a person actually wanting to leave for him to notice the chairs.

The independent coordinator didn't conceal the incident. She began by showing visitors the side door, waiting area, and room for private conversations. The version confirmed that morning lay on the tables, not the recruitment form struck out on April 9.

Before the formal explanation, independent examiners walked the exit route and checked stopping-rehearsal records, separate contact arrangements, and medical provisions. They required the cleared chairs to be recorded in that morning's site recheck. Only after outstanding items were resolved did the safety officer issue the limited written determination there, permitting explanation, individual assessment, and conditional enrollment in the pilot. Any operation involving a load still required the relevant equipment reinspection, medical suitability assessment, and confirmation for that occasion. A signature couldn't light those lamps early. The officer read the scope aloud before leaving the decision on the table for examination. Nobody had been asked to accept an unexplained promise of safety. What the decision permitted was narrower than the future undertaking Adrian hoped they would eventually make possible. He could be glad of the permission without quietly promoting it into that larger achievement.

Stopping arrangements were no longer rows of question marks. Independent attendants could halt work directly. Participants needn't first justify withdrawal to a researcher. A separate contact would receive withdrawals without publishing names. Residual simulated load could go only into approved nonliving buffers, not into the next person still seated. Anything beyond established limits couldn't begin. Courage wasn't a substitute to be demanded afterward.

The teacher read slowly. At the expenses clause, she asked whether someone assessed and then declining would owe back the fare.

The accounts clerk showed the allocated travel allowance: reimbursement for actual attendance, not eventual agreement. The earlier household shortfall remained elsewhere in the accounts. This restricted allocation hadn't miraculously paid old debts.

One visitor put down his pen. He wanted to speak to his family again. The coordinator supplied a copy to take home, without requiring a preliminary promise. Nobody applauded or sighed when he left.

The Temple letter arriving afterward quieted the room.

Its messenger used the ordinary delivery channel. The supplementary objection said old emergency assignments remained effective; new agreements affecting designated resources must demonstrate no conflict. It listed the five marked women's obligations first, then placed voluntary participation and replacement of established protection in one field.

Adrian wanted to call it obstruction. The safety officer first asked which resource named in the letter today's activity would actually use.

There was no core interface here, no ancient energy, no woman's mark serving as a starting condition. April 12 permission covered only unpowered installation and mechanical checks. This morning's independent determination permitted today's explanation, assessment, and conditional enrollment. The objection required an answer. A combined field couldn't make an unstarted replacement operation a present fact.

The coordinator sent written clarification and made the objection available to visitors. She didn't hide unfavorable material to obtain easier signatures.

“So they could still stop it later?” the repairer asked.

“Yes,” Adrian said. “Permission to sign isn't my guarantee of every later approval.”

“Who signs when you put it that way?”

“At least nobody because I left it out.”

The repairer studied his own copy. He was considering limited technical work, not bodily risk. Another visitor wanted subsequent suitability assessment, not immediate connection. Their answers belonged in separate sections. A broad heading would have looked impressive across the top of the page, but it would also have hidden the difference between repairing a support and allowing a future assessment of one’s own suitability. Adrian watched the repairer find his particular task and continue reading. The distinction gave him something definite to consider, instead of asking him to agree to a reassuring word whose contents might expand afterward.

Cecilia arrived after handing over her ordinary service shift, her cuff damp from washing dishes. A woman recognized her and sprang up to let her sign first.

“I'm not here to demonstrate who's braver,” Cecilia said. “I'm answering the question you left yesterday.”

If the woman's family heard she'd withdrawn and asked at the relief office, would they find out?

Cecilia didn't offer her character as the guarantee. Reception staff showed the separate records and response procedure. They rehearsed refusing to disclose a list, with the woman pretending to be her own husband. By her third fierce question she laughed, then admitted she probably wouldn't laugh in the real encounter.

“That's why you shouldn't have to handle it alone,” Cecilia replied.

Waiting by the door for his equipment questions, Adrian realized this awkward rehearsal was another connection they were repairing. Without it, you can withdraw might lead somewhere worse.

The visitor seeking assessment had brought his employer. The man placed a recommendation on the desk: his worker was strong and never missed a shift. The worker rubbed his hat's brim without agreeing.

Asked whether he'd prefer a private conversation, he glanced at his employer and said either was fine. The coordinator didn't treat that as a clear preference. Everyone had an individual explanation period, she said, inviting companions to wait outside.

Adrian was asked to leave too. In the waiting area, the employer complained that even supporters apparently had to be guarded against. Adrian accused him of no specific threat. But people might struggle to speak freely before someone paying their wages. Unconditional support wouldn't diminish because the worker spoke alone.

Holding an overhot cup, the employer admitted he'd hoped a participating employee would let his shop display helping protect the kingdom. He hadn't threatened dismissal. He had already boasted to neighboring businesses.

“That was the part you agreed to for him,” Adrian said.

After a silence, the employer asked to retrieve his recommendation. Staff could return the original, retaining only necessary reception facts rather than publicly humiliating him. He broke its seal himself and put it in his pocket.

The worker still wanted to proceed, but chose to return alone another day for medical questions. Before the coordinator, the employer confirmed that the decision wouldn't affect pay or shifts. Adrian didn't declare future pressure impossible. The separate contact explained how to seek help, then checked that the worker understood.

Returning a letter hadn't added a name. It had allowed someone already preparing to nod to finish speaking.

Before noon, the first agreement was completed. The teacher chose readability review and observation, with stated occasions and withdrawal arrangements—not load-bearing. The repairer signed for paid, limited hours. Two other adult residents separately completed conditional agreements for further assessment and voluntary pilot participation. Each risk page was confirmed; operation still depended on unmet conditions.

One signer disliked his crooked name and asked for a fresh sheet. The voided page was retained and confirmed by him. Nobody improved his handwriting on his behalf. The paper didn't glow. The wind outside didn't stop. Each completed document went back through the ordinary process of checking the copy against what its owner had actually chosen. The witnesses did not embellish that answer.

Adrian waited, then realized he'd been expecting a dramatic sound.

“What are you waiting for?” Cecilia asked.

“Something finally beginning, perhaps.”

She tapped the signed copy. “This one's quiet. You might have missed it.”

He laughed. She returned the copy to its owner instead of raising it as a flag answering for everyone.

During the afternoon, a woman who'd intended to sign declined. She feared losing the energy to care for her mother, even during assessment. Staff ended contact as requested. Her earlier observations remained only in the anonymous form she'd allowed.

Adrian walked her to the passage. Had she wasted their day?

“We needed you to be able to say no.”

“That's easy to say.”

He admitted he felt disappointed. That wasn't a loss she owed him compensation for. Finally she loosened her grip on her gloves and passed through the cleared door.

The coordinator released counts of actual commitments by role and outstanding conditions, not residents' names. Someone proposed a commemorative portrait of signers beside the princess. Isabella replied through her assistant that a willing individual's keepsake could be arranged separately, without pressuring nonsigners. Her absence couldn't become a claim that she'd witnessed every agreement.

Luna received anonymized technical questions at the agreed time. Her right palm still restricted work. Her assistant returned two points requiring further checks. Adrian didn't send today's signature count as a reason to extend her hours.

At dusk he carried a small food basket to the relief hall. Cecilia was discussing blanket drying with a temporary resident. The man disliked hanging his old patterned blanket on the prominent courtyard wall; his child might be mocked. She showed him another ventilated passage and asked the manager's permission. Warmth and dignity needn't be presented as mutually exclusive.

After her handover, Adrian put the basket on a little table. Two portions of root-vegetable stew and bread, no expensive sweets. She opened the lid and solemnly asked whether this was celebratory food.

“Ordinary dinner. If it needs another name, Dinner That Hasn't Gone Cold.”

“Then we should maintain its claim immediately.”

She took a mouthful before asking whether he was happy. He was, and frightened that signatures might make him regard people as resources already secured. Dividing an oversized vegetable with her spoon, she said a signature didn't end listening. It made pretending not to know whom to ask harder.

“You didn't sign today.”

“My decision today was to finish my work and eat properly. Are you disappointed?”

“No.”

“Then don't make this table another reception desk.”

He put his pen back into his inner pocket. Smiling, she said he'd finally learned to visit empty-handed.

A boy brought a folded paper bird for the person who'd helped his mother. Cecilia checked with the mother before accepting it. Its uneven wings couldn't fly, but it stood on the windowsill. Adrian moved it away from the water without correcting it into a standard shape.

Before he left, a liaison brought a short report from the suburban relay on the border supply route. Yesterday its own works office had separately authorized an unpowered housing-fitting work order and confirmed ordinary maintenance materials. This was not an extension of the academy premises permit. Today an old dispatch channel had issued a priority reassignment. Staff were checking affected lines; the planned isolation conditions could no longer be guaranteed.

Adrian didn't take newly signed agreements as a reason to operate first. He asked the coordinator to notify those affected that later arrangements involving that line remained stopped pending inspection.

The paper bird stood on the sill. Four separate copies had gone home with their owners.

The first volunteers had signed. Now the work was to prove that after names reached paper, people could still open their own doors.
