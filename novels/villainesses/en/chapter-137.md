# Chapter 137: Five Places, Not Five Answers

On January 12, Adrian found a map in the old-project archive.

It did not glow. Ink had bled across the lower left corner. Whoever drew the river had corrected the same bend twice, leaving an old roads office's amendment mark beside it.

What caught his attention was the five district sheets filed behind it.

Capital West District. Temple Precinct. South Bridge at Greymarch. East Ridge. Old Palace Watercourse.

The retired registrar had not brought the original himself. The custodian had checked his reference and confirmed that it identified an archived Old Project Hazard and Personnel Transfer Register. That morning's permission covered only district indexes, transfer destinations, and corresponding public works records relevant to the eight searches. Other people's identity pages remained beneath screening boards.

Isabella sat at the far end of the table, her request for supplementary explanations beside her. Her existing access remained valid. Answering a challenge did not turn the entire repository into her study.

Elena opened an approved copy and read the heading first.

“A risk register. Not an incident report.”

The custodian nodded. Hazards in the title included existing damage and potential dangers noted during maintenance. A warning of backflow did not mean the palace had flooded that year. Receiving maintenance workers did not mean every worker had disappeared there.

Adrian erased the five crosses he had just drawn on his own tracing of the map.

“I nearly announced disasters in five places at once.”

“Pencil is easier to correct than a public notice.” Elena passed him the eraser. “While you still can.”

They began with Milo Senn.

The receiving desk on Clara's slip matched an old transfer index. The subsequent handling page was not in this volume. It changed an aimless referral into a request that could name the next custodian, nothing more.

Isabella asked the clerk to inquire separately by reference. The reply to Clara must not say Found.

The second inquiry produced a different result. A worker with the same name had the wrong age and birthplace. He could not be attached to the missing person's record. Adrian excluded him as a possible match, reluctant but careful not to leave that reluctance in the wording.

At least they could tell the family which namesake had been ruled out, rather than hand over another empty closure slip.

The remaining cases required receipt books from different years. While the custodian fetched the permitted comparisons, Elena checked the map's scale. The five areas were not equivalent administrative units.

East Ridge covered an entire region. South Bridge was a specific crossing point. The Temple Precinct's boundary had changed in one year. Listing them together did not make them five equal-sized containers.

Adrian nodded, still staring at their names.

In Crown of Fate, the five bad endings had never concluded in the same place. He remembered some locations clearly. Others survived as vague routes and scraps of background text. These five recalled those memories, but no remembered image could authenticate the map.

The past months had taught him that familiar names could be fitted into the wrong story.

He put down his pencil and asked Isabella how the old register had been compiled.

“Hazards, administration, and personnel destinations. Projects reported by different offices were filed under old receiving points. Why these five district sheets appear together should be in the compilation notes.”

The custodian found the explanation. These were not the country's only five areas at risk. They were five groupings used for the old projects included in this volume. Original reports were held separately, with references in the current catalog allowing further searches.

One corner of Adrian's overly tidy answer came apart.

The cross-references that followed made it difficult to dismiss the rest.

Capital West District involved old relief property and neighborhood firefighting facilities. Its project numbers appeared in the historical family liquidation records Elena was checking. She laid her approved extracts beside it and verified the matching portions one by one. The name Rosenberg did not make the entire column hers.

“My mother's original restrictions on their use don't change because someone gives the collection a new heading,” she said. “These projects were brought into the liquidation later. We still need to separate the years.”

The Temple Precinct section contained relief facilities and personnel transfer indexes. One set of receiving desks connected with an old relief case Cecilia's office had examined. She was not present. Adrian recorded the source pages to ask her about, not a conclusion that the whole precinct belonged to one case.

South Bridge concerned winter passage, interrupted supplies, and reserve accommodation. Its civilian checkpoint matched a location established in Viola's border case. Whether it shared administrative stages with the falsified military supplies still required separate military and local records.

East Ridge listed old-equipment maintenance and abnormal ley-line activity as risk categories. Its transfer directions could be connected with the tower's old papers. That did not mean Luna had caused a ley-line disturbance. A category could not replace examination of the accident's remains.

The fifth section, Old Palace Watercourse, fell under royal old-project administration and supervision.

The very responsibility Isabella wanted, and for which she was being challenged.

She finished reading without saying Everything connects, as Adrian had imagined she might.

“If we're only considering duties, plenty of people in the palace could stand beside that column.”

“Yes,” Adrian said.

“Then why are you looking at me?”

He raised his eyes.

Elena looked at him too. She did not answer for him. Holding her extracts against the table, she waited for him to explain the part that had raced ahead.

“Because the first four sets touch things we've already been through, and the fifth places the question before you. I'm afraid they aren't unrelated. But fear can't choose who's responsible, much less prove what anyone carries in her body.”

Isabella slowly nodded.

“Then write that the records connect. Don't write that I'm the final answer.”

He moved the five names in his notes to a separate sheet, clearly identifying them as his own case connections, not a list in the old register. The source pages had not written five women's destinies in advance or named them as causes of disaster.

Looking at his separate sheet, Adrian nevertheless felt a genuine chill.

He did not know five interchangeable places in a story.

He knew Elena correcting the port in a book they read. Cecilia earnestly asking whether he liked a hot pastry. Viola finally choosing a bag of chestnuts simply for herself. Luna resting her injured hand and still seeing lines beneath an ordinary lamp. The fifth person sat at the table, trying not to write other people's choices into her plans.

If the old game's endings truly connected with these records, he could no longer treat them as routes waiting for him to select.

Nor could fear justify choosing one for the women first.

At noon, examination paused, and the original register returned to its cabinet. Isabella suggested visiting the side kitchen with such gravity that Adrian imagined more documents awaiting inspection there.

The object awaiting inspection was a bowl of flour.

She had arranged with the cook the previous evening to try making an ordinary pastry herself. To avoid disrupting lunch, the cook had reserved a small side worktop and ingredients for six. Isabella nevertheless offered a sheet specifying three fillings, two presentations, and the dimensions of a silver tray.

The cook read it and asked whether she intended to learn or convene a tea reception.

Elena laughed first.

“Six pastries still need a plan,” Isabella said, retrieving the sheet.

“They have one. Two may not look very good. We'll eat the other four normally.”

Isabella looked at Adrian. His attempt at solemnity failed.

She finally accepted a single apple filling. Elena washed her hands and volunteered to peel the remaining apples without taking over Isabella's dough. Adrian's assignment was putting the diced pieces in a bowl. He treated this so seriously that each cube might have required identity verification.

“They'll soften as they cook,” Elena reminded him.

“Good. I was worried this one's shape failed inspection.”

Isabella suppressed a smile, but her rolling pin pressed too hard on one side. The dough was thin there and thick on the other. The cook asked her to stop and examine her grip, not surrender the dough.

She slowly corrected it. For once, she did not hurry the next step.

With the pastries in the oven, the three sat on a bench outside the kitchen. Flour dusted Isabella's sleeve. Attempting to brush it off merely spread it farther.

“Ordinary things in a palace are harder than I imagined.”

“You can't have someone else do them and count that as knowing how,” Elena said.

“You're enjoying yourself particularly today.”

“Yes. I've had moments like this too.”

Elena did not produce a flawless anecdote to outdo her. Adrian knew how often she had put on a brave face over little things. He only smiled when she glanced at him. Their understanding seemed to unsettle Isabella. Still, she did not hurry to end this wait in which no one was winning.

One of the six pastries split, revealing its apple filling. It smelled excellent.

Isabella asked the cook whether he wanted to keep one, then let Adrian and Elena choose. She did not place the most perfect pastry before him or demand that he publicly name the best.

He took the split one because he could already see what was inside.

“Even eating doesn't stop you checking,” she said.

“This time, it's because I like it.”

Her little plate paused. He added that he liked apple filling. She glared, and Elena laughed so hard she almost failed to keep her own plate level.

Back at the archive that afternoon, approved copies of the district sheets were ready. A small group of symbols occupied each page's corner, all five different. January 18 appeared in the margins under different years, in the consolidated-transfer entries. These were not the dates on which each person had left home or a hazard had occurred.

Isabella asked whether the symbols were ordinary classification codes. The custodian found an old explanation that established only that they had been reproduced from the source forms. Their meaning was not given in this compilation.

Adrian did not ask anyone to place a symbol against her body.

He checked that the copies preserved scale and damaged portions. He requested permission to give Luna symbol sheets without private names for examination on paper. Cecilia could first consider whether the dates related to public observances. Each must decide what she was willing and able to do.

The note to Luna specified no opening equipment, no casting, and no request to draw with her still-bandaged right hand. Adrian read those lines once more before sealing it. An important discovery was no excuse for making a friend forget to recover.

As they left, Isabella gave Ellery her supplementary reply. The missing-person inquiries continued through their designated desks. Following five sets of symbols had not displaced eight people's whereabouts.

Elena stayed beside Adrian. Only after they passed through the courtyard gate did she ask why he had gripped his pen so tightly.

“I'm afraid I've seen another road I think I know.”

“Then tell me what's on it first. Don't decide alone which way to go.”

He nodded and slipped the approved copies into an ordinary document bag. Five places finally had sources they could investigate separately, rather than being five vacancies someone must step forward to fill.
