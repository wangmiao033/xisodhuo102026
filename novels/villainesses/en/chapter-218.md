# Chapter 218: Leave the Door Open

On March 17, Adrian tried to tie his cravat for the third time.

The first attempt had strangled him. The second leaned left. This one resembled a frightened butterfly.

Elena watched from the doorway, holding yesterday's meeting arrangements.

“Planning to distract the high priest?”

“If he watches my neck, perhaps he won't keep watching my hand.”

Her amusement faded. Adrian untied the knot and admitted he wasn't entirely joking. Oswin's name had appeared on paper often enough. Someone who remained behind seals and other people's accounts could acquire any face in the imagination. Today he would sit opposite them. Adrian still didn't know where to look first.

“His face,” Elena said. “Or the window, when you need to. You don't owe him a convincing performance of courage.”

She asked before helping. Adrian nodded and lowered his chin. Her fingers worked against the fabric, nowhere near his scar.

“What I told you the other day still stands.”

“I heard you. Every breakfast needn't become another declaration.”

She loosened the finished knot so he could breathe. He wanted to kiss her, then realized she was examining his collar for chafing. He asked. She smiled and agreed.

The brief kiss didn't remove today's fear. They still had to leave, but at least the remaining half-slice of bread wasn't forgotten.

The academy meeting room opened directly onto a level gallery. The Temple brought its own recorder; the investigators had a separate clerk and legal witness. Yesterday both sides had agreed: no descent underground, no display of bodily marks, no surrender of private papers. Attendance implied no acceptance of any proposed disposition.

Luna would attend only the opening portion. An assistant would later bring the agreed written record. Viola sat near the unobstructed exit, left arm supported. Isabella had chosen sleeves that left her wrist support visible. Discovering a royal document hadn't restored her former coordinating powers.

Oswin arrived early.

The lamps didn't dim. No armed escort filled the doorway. He wore a dark robe polished at the cuffs, with an ink stain on his right forefinger. Adrian first noticed him asking an attendant not to scratch the wall with his wet umbrella.

The ordinary voice tightened something in Adrian's chest.

He would have preferred a man who didn't sound ordinary.

Before sitting, Oswin glanced toward the open door. His recorder asked whether shutting it would reduce the noise of passing feet.

“Please leave it open,” Adrian said.

Faces turned toward him. He held his paper steady and resisted turning the request into a joke.

“We agreed anyone could leave. I know a closed door doesn't necessarily prevent that. Today I'd like to see the way out.”

“Certainly,” Oswin said. “If the record becomes unclear, we'll stop and read it back.”

A reasonable answer didn't alter what they had investigated. An open doorway couldn't cancel a signed order.

The clerk began with the agreed questions. Oswin didn't deny authorizing the warning-line adjustment. He identified the central comparison tables as its basis, acknowledging that a local implementation report didn't show all the comparisons he had used.

“Why did the people below receive only the revised conclusion?” Adrian asked.

“They were responsible for reporting and implementation, not recalculating risks across the realm.”

“So they couldn't see that the same reading had required a warning under the old line.”

“The old line wasn't erased from the archives.”

“Keeping it somewhere isn't the same as showing it to someone deciding whether to evacuate.”

Oswin looked directly at him for the first time.

Adrian felt no triumph. The high priest didn't shout or issue a cruel command that would conveniently settle the room's opinion. Instead, he asked who would allocate limited wagons if every locality acted on its most conservative estimate at once.

Viola moved her right hand. “A difficult decision doesn't excuse omitting the conditions faced by the people receiving it.”

“I haven't opposed corrections.”

“Then let those corrections affect present arrangements, not merely become another archived sheet.”

Oswin instructed his recorder to retain that sentence. He then requested an assurance that, during the investigation, nobody would demand the immediate shutdown of every protective installation still in service.

“Do you mean an unplanned shutdown,” Luna asked, “or any bounded inspection with proper preparations?”

“The former.”

“Then write the former.”

The recorder had already written prohibition of shutdown. He stopped and narrowed it. Oswin didn't object, which somehow made Adrian feel worse. Willingness to concede wording offered no promise of movement on the central question.

Regarding the noble register and related volumes, Oswin confirmed the Temple held those historical records and directed particular page references to their custodians for examination. He didn't authenticate every private signature, or pretend his presence made every old entry reliable.

“You wish to establish that people made decisions,” he said. “I haven't told you everything happened through divine will without human involvement.”

“But human decisions were read to other people as divine will,” Cecilia said.

“People need explanations they can understand.”

“That doesn't mean they must receive an answer already chosen for them.”

Her voice remained quiet. Adrian remembered the people waiting for soup. An oracle might have been the only assurance they received during a winter, not an old advertisement easily torn down.

Oswin didn't mock her faith. He asked how she would explain matters when she couldn't promise that every place would be safe tomorrow.

“I would say I couldn't promise that. Then stay and do what I could.”

“Some would leave the only place capable of protecting them.”

“That is a difficulty we must face. It isn't permission to invent their answer.”

Silence held. Luna's assistant discreetly indicated the time. She didn't dismiss the reminder because the discussion had become important. Before leaving, she requested that her unanswered technical questions remain unanswered in the record, not become consent supplied on her behalf.

Oswin waited until she had departed along the level gallery.

“I will let you examine the central comparison tables and their associated records. You won't find only things that make you angry.”

Isabella asked where the originals were held, who controlled them, and whether they could be compared against records outside Temple custody. She wouldn't accept a display in which Oswin selected the pictures, supplied every explanation, then demanded belief.

“You may request the corresponding material,” he said. “Not every repository answers to me.”

“Then material we cannot immediately obtain mustn't be described as nonexistent.”

He nodded slowly.

Adrian hadn't touched his tea. It had stopped steaming. Only when his hand brushed the cup did he realize how thirsty he was. Elena placed her unused water jug in the center of the table, available to anyone, without making his care another event the whole room had to observe.

Oswin noticed. He asked whether Adrian understood that the people he protected might choose something he couldn't bear to accept.

“Yes.”

“Including a cost others cannot carry?”

Adrian looked at Elena, then the other women. He didn't claim they could never choose that.

“They need to know the cost. And what remains unexamined. You can't hide the other roads, then call the remaining one their choice.”

“You believe other roads exist.”

“I hope they do. Hope isn't proof.”

For once, Oswin didn't answer immediately.

Before concluding, both parties read back tomorrow's arrangements: an upstairs examination of the central tables and obtainable independent material. No lock testing or human trials. Finding a real danger wouldn't automatically mean accepting designated bearers. Objections could remain in the record; attendance alone wasn't agreement.

Adrian received no key to the core. Nor did a clever sentence defeat Oswin. The high priest collected his umbrella, thanked the attendant, and stepped into the rainy courtyard.

Adrian studied the door that had remained open all morning. Exhaustion arrived suddenly.

“He looks like someone who remembers to return borrowed umbrellas.”

“Does that make it harder?” Elena asked.

He nodded. “I thought seeing him would tell me exactly what to hate.”

“You can still know what to question.”

She didn't replace his hatred with understanding or urge him to trust a polite man. They waited while the clerk corrected one final omission, then collected their copies. Viola asked them to stop blocking the exit. Her ordinary tone finally made Adrian smile.

They ate in the academy's side room. When the soup arrived, a young assistant asked whether Oswin's willingness to provide material meant the waiting public could be told that both sides were cooperating.

Isabella put down her spoon. Rather than scold him, she asked what listeners might think had been agreed.

“Joint verification?” he suggested.

“Then say joint verification. Don't sign the remaining blank space as well.”

Cecilia asked him to write out the actual arrangements and have both parties check them. She wanted believers to know the inquiry continued. A meeting without shouting mustn't become an announcement that the Temple and five mark-bearers shared a position. The assistant crossed out cooperation and asked whether pending applications for independent records should also be identified as pending.

“Yes,” Adrian said.

The young man read his revised sentence aloud. It sounded less impressive now, but nobody at the table could mistake it for a promise to enter the core or accept the register. Adrian listened to the plain wording and found that he preferred its awkwardness to the comfort of the original.

Today they had to guard against more than Oswin's words. Other people were eager to compose good news for them.

Viola pushed the sliced bread toward the center with her right hand. She understood the wish for progress. Even the waiting coachman had asked whether travel would become easier. She hadn't promised it would; nobody here could answer for every checkpoint.

“What did you tell him?” Elena asked.

“That he wouldn't have to wait another half-hour for me today. I'd leave when we agreed.”

Luna's absence left room for a joke she might have made. Adrian wanted her to know nobody had counted her silence after leaving as approval. He asked her assistant to include that with the reading record, then put away his pen. She needed accurate material to read after resting, not another lunchtime message demanding an immediate response.

When he finally began his soup, Elena turned the salt cellar. Its holes had faced the wrong direction; he nearly emptied it into his bowl. They stopped together, then laughed.

That small mishap answered nothing important. It did allow the afternoon to become something other than an extension of the negotiating table.

On the way home, the carriage latch caught twice. Adrian waited for the driver to repair it rather than joking about today's open door. Once seated, Elena offered her hand and asked whether he'd like to hold it for a while.

He would.

Tomorrow, more documents would lie between them and Oswin. Today he hadn't surrendered anyone's answer, or pretended he already possessed enough of his own.
