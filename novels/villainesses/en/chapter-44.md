# Why Do All the Villainesses Want to Marry Me?
## Volume Two: The Saint Won't Let Me Get Married
### Chapter 44: A Lawyer Isn't an Engagement Gift

On October twenty-fourth, Elena pushed a thick directory of lawyers across the breakfast table.

Colored tabs protruded from its pages.

Cecilia regarded it cautiously.

"You're looking for a lawyer for me?"

"I'm offering you a list of people you might choose. You're facing disciplinary proceedings within a religious institution. Hale knows the ducal household and the Judicial Office. That doesn't mean he specializes in the Temple's internal rules."

"I thought Mr. Hale was an excellent lawyer," Adrian said.

Hale lifted his tea.

"Thank you. My ambition is to know every law in the kingdom. I'm afraid I haven't quite managed it."

Elena opened the directory.

"Three of these attorneys have represented people before religious disciplinary boards. Two can accept funding through an outside legal-aid arrangement. One insists on direct payment from the client."

Cecilia touched one of the pages.

"Why would you pay for my defense?"

"Because a lack of money shouldn't decide whether you get to challenge these charges."

Cecilia looked at her.

"Is that because you're worried about Adrian?"

Elena didn't deny it.

"Of course I care about him. But even if he weren't involved, I'd still object to someone being pressured into signing a confession to something she didn't do."

Adrian felt the need to contribute.

"I could pay for some of the fees out of my salary."

Both women turned toward him.

He stopped.

Elena put a finger on the directory.

"You still haven't finished paying for your new coat."

"Why does everybody remember that?"

Hale cleared his throat.

"There's a practical issue here. Whoever pays a lawyer's fee doesn't automatically get to control the case. We should establish that from the outset."

He produced a draft legal-aid agreement.

If House Rosenberg helped cover necessary costs, the money would go through a neutral legal-aid account.

Cecilia would select her own counsel.

Only she could decide the position her defense would take.

The duke's household would not acquire access to confidential legal advice or the right to change witness statements.

Cecilia read the agreement slowly.

"What if I decline your help?"

"That's fine too," Elena said. "You may have personal savings, or you could apply to the Temple members' independent defense fund. I'm offering you a choice, not asking you to accept a favor."

"Then I'll apply to the fund first. If it can't cover the necessary costs in time, I'll consider an advance from the legal-aid account."

Elena nodded.

She looked faintly disappointed.

But she didn't argue.

Adrian remembered their conversation in the greenhouse weeks ago.

He'd insisted that being helped shouldn't mean becoming someone's property.

Elena was learning to extend that same respect to someone else.

Hale pointed out an easily overlooked clause at the end of the directory.

A disciplinary committee could ask for disclosure of a legal representative's funding source if another interested party supplied the fee.

That didn't automatically mean the representative or the witness had been bought.

"I don't want them to say House Rosenberg purchased my defense," Cecilia said.

"Nor do I," Elena replied. "You refused to sign the false confession before we discussed paying a lawyer. That order of events matters."

Hale added the timing to the background record.

"Not to invent a beautiful story about anyone's motives. Simply to prevent somebody reversing the sequence."

Cecilia thought about it.

"Then I'll accept limited help with procedural costs if I need it. I'd still like to pay what I reasonably can myself."

"Are you certain?" Elena asked.

"Yes. Whoever pays, the final signature must belong to me."

Adrian sighed.

"Since arriving in the capital, I've found that people with pens are much more dangerous than people with swords."

"You should have worked that out the day we met," Elena said.

After some consideration, he admitted that she had a point.

That afternoon, they visited the Capital Bar Association.

Cecilia interviewed the three candidates herself.

The first was an eloquent advocate who spent much of the conversation urging her to accept a settlement immediately, promising that keeping her title mattered more than winning an argument.

The second specialized in Temple property disputes and wanted access to virtually every private record she'd ever had.

The third was Vera Ash, a forty-year-old lawyer who had previously represented nurses and charity workers in internal disciplinary proceedings.

Vera sat down.

She didn't start by asking whether Cecilia was innocent.

She asked, "What do you want me to do for you?"

Cecilia hesitated.

"Prove that I didn't disclose a restricted oracle."

"That's a possible outcome. Which procedure do you want to pursue? Independent examination of the materials used against you? A negotiated withdrawal of the restrictions? Both?"

"I want to see the actual documents underlying the accusations. If I violated a confidentiality rule, I'll accept responsibility for that. But if they've described the correction of medical identity records as leaking an oracle, I want somebody to tell the panel those aren't the same thing."

Vera nodded.

"Then we start with disclosure and preservation requests."

She was surprisingly matter-of-fact.

Cecilia visibly relaxed.

"Could we ask for the hearing to be postponed?" Adrian asked.

"Yes. I can't promise the request will be granted. If essential materials have been withheld, the panel may reasonably allow additional preparation time."

Vera looked at him.

"But I won't ask for an unlimited delay simply because you suspect something sinister."

"Neither would I."

"Good. People in a hurry to protect someone are common. People willing to let her answer for herself are rarer."

Elena met Vera's eyes.

"Would you accept the legal-aid funding arrangement we've discussed?"

"If the client consents and the source is transparent. The funding party gets no access to privileged advice."

Cecilia looked at the draft engagement letter.

Then at Elena.

"I'd like Ms. Ash to represent me."

"All right," Elena replied.

"You're sure you don't have another opinion?"

"Of course I do. I initially preferred the first advocate's courtroom experience." Elena smiled. "But I'm not the one who'll be questioned before the disciplinary panel."

Cecilia laughed.

For the first time all morning, she looked as though she'd found a chair that actually fit.

Hale witnessed the retainer agreement without becoming a client.

Adrian glanced at the fees.

"Lawyers cost considerably more than I do."

Vera didn't even look up.

"Would you like to take over ten years of document cases for me?"

"No."

"Then consider the fee explained."

They all laughed.

Before taking the case, Vera asked Cecilia to identify three conditions she would not trade away for a more favorable outcome.

"First, I won't admit disclosing a document I didn't disclose. Second, I won't surrender Jonah's or Elliot's private medical histories in exchange for a lighter penalty. Third, I won't ask Mira to alter her testimony to make me look better."

"And fourth?" Adrian asked.

"I asked for three," Vera said. "But she's welcome to add another if she wants."

Cecilia considered carefully.

"Then fourth: I won't announce that every Temple blessing is a lie simply because someone falsified an administrative report. People have genuinely been comforted by prayer. I want to challenge the people who altered records, not condemn everyone who has ever prayed beside a hospital bed."

Vera nodded approvingly.

"That's important. Otherwise they'll try to turn a dispute over evidence into an attack on faith itself."

Adrian understood what they'd really achieved.

They hadn't found a lawyer whose greatest talent was making the other side look foolish.

They'd found someone willing to help Cecilia state what she believed without losing control of her own defense.

Vera gave them the first steps.

She would demand a specific inventory of the accusation materials and their proposed witnesses.

She would also request emergency independent preservation of the existing custody and seal information for R-074 and the C-19 revision documents.

"We can't simply move every Temple original into somebody else's possession," she said. "But if a file changes custodians before a hearing, the transfer needs a verifiable record."

Hale nodded.

"The certified municipal copies already exist under independent custody. The Temple cannot make them disappear just by restricting its own originals."

Cecilia noted everything.

She didn't ask Vera to promise that she'd keep her title.

She asked, "When will I be allowed to explain why I refused to sign the completion report?"

"October twenty-sixth, unless we obtain an adjournment. We'll ask that you be given a full opportunity to speak."

Afterward, Elena suggested visiting a bookshop.

Adrian blinked.

"Today?"

"You said you'd help me choose books yesterday. Three different legal documents interrupted us. There's no immediate danger at present, is there?"

Cecilia stopped by the guild doors.

Adrian expected another research proposal.

Instead, she asked, "Do you have plans together?"

"Yes," Elena said. "I've invited Adrian on a date."

Cecilia nodded.

"Then I won't intrude. I'm going to visit an older nurse at the relief home. I want to check that Mira is receiving the support she asked for."

"Would you like our carriage?" Adrian offered.

"Thank you, but I've already arranged transport. You're entitled to time together as well."

She went on her way.

Adrian watched her leave.

He felt a little guilty.

Elena didn't avoid the subject.

"You're worried about the hearing."

"Yes."

"You're allowed to worry. Being on a date doesn't mean we have to forget our friends."

They walked down a street lined with secondhand bookshops.

Faded maps hung outside the doors. A shopkeeper complained that the autumn rain kept curling the covers on his discounted books.

Elena selected a volume on historical contracts.

Adrian stared at it.

"More contracts?"

"It's a book I happen to want to read. Not everything in my life is about your ancient betrothal."

He set it beside a travel book filled with illustrations.

"I'll take this one."

"Planning to leave the capital?"

"No. I just want to know what's out there besides courts and Temples."

Elena studied him.

"When things settle down, perhaps we could choose somewhere to visit."

"As a date?"

"Yes."

He placed the travel book beside her history volume.

"Then let's make a proper travel plan. We won't set a day until we're both free to choose one."

Elena took his hand.

From behind the counter, the bookseller called out.

"Are you two engaged? There's a discount for newlyweds!"

Adrian nearly strangled himself on his collar.

"No!"

Elena laughed.

"We're only buying books."

The shopkeeper shrugged.

"Then you pay the normal price."

Adrian was beginning to suspect that fairness had its hidden costs.

That evening, Vera sent the preservation application to the estate for Cecilia to review and sign.

She would file it at the Temple the next morning. She made no promise the archive custodians would cooperate.

Cecilia looked at the pages.

For the first time, she felt as though she were preparing a defense in which she could participate, rather than waiting for someone else to hand down an answer.

She signed.

Later that night, a new notice appeared on the Temple's public bulletin board.

*"Due to inspections of fire-safety and preservation facilities, the Old Contract Records Archive will suspend public access from the afternoon of October twenty-fifth until further notice."*

Adrian had just opened his travel book when the news arrived.

He closed it slowly.

"So we have tomorrow morning at most?"

Hale nodded.

"The disciplinary hearing's on the twenty-sixth. The archive closes the afternoon before it."

Elena added the announcement to the file.

"That doesn't prove the fire inspection is a pretext. We'll verify what the closure actually covers and request an intact inventory of the records."

Cecilia looked at her signed preservation request.

"I'll come with you."

She paused.

"This time, I'd like them to tell me whether the originals are still in the cabinets where they're supposed to be."
