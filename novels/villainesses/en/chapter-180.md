# Chapter 180: His Name Was on the Line

Adrian unwound his scarf at the door and forgot whom to give it to.

On February 9, the independent storage room was already lit. No mysterious blue fire: an ordinary side lamp with a shielding tube. A calibration plate waited by the window; a stool supported Luna's hand cushion. Beatrice checked the mount. Nobody handed Adrian an unfamiliar device and promised that holding it would restore his memory.

Elena held out her hand.

“Scarf.”

He looked at her.

“You've greeted the porter twice while holding it.”

He glanced down and finally smiled. She hung it up without deciding whether he'd sit beside the table. He chose a seat, with a clear boundary still separating him from the originals.

Isabella reviewed her access: excerpts permitted to the joint applicants, not every private relationship record. Cecilia separately confirmed her own cited material. Viola hadn't come. Potentially important secrets didn't automatically cancel her rest.

Luna asked Beatrice to show the blank calibration plate first. Overlapping ordinary lines separated as the angle changed: differences in surface and light, not a machine deciding which statement was true. The repair stabilized external optics and supports. It hadn't created an eye qualified to convict history.

“What if the writing's too faint?” Adrian asked.

“Then it's too faint. We can't use your hopes as another lamp.”

She asked to move away from the cold window. Adrian rose to help, but Beatrice had an assistant shift the empty chair after Luna stood safely. Nobody needed to cross the object's boundary to care for her.

The custodian read the separately issued permission to inspect these originals: three references, external optical methods, and stopping conditions. The earlier permission for copied material hadn't been stretched to fit. Old energy components remained isolated and sealed, absent from today's equipment.

They began with the overwritten-layer condition sheet, not the termination record. The custodian read its current catalog entry. References and an old receipt linked it to the next plate; physical structure still needed comparison. Beatrice's edge diagrams supported two actual layers. The sheet alone couldn't establish that the lower one belonged to another world.

At a different angle, several crowded strokes separated. Luna asked the assistant to stop, without reaching for the optics.

“Transcription. Not transfer.”

Cecilia crossed out her first word. Both error and correction remained. Adrian felt marginally easier. They could still misread things; no power beyond doubt was announcing an answer.

The receipt recorded a historical transcription. Only part of its source remained legible, with a page reference pointing to the termination record. This wasn't the administrative forwarding of wrapper documents under NR-42 four years ago. Different times and objects couldn't be stitched into one person delivering every answer.

Beatrice called a break to check lamp heat. Luna watched the time and drank water, right palm cushioned. Elena breathed deeply beside the window.

“Do you still want to see?”

“Yes.” Adrian hesitated. “I'm frightened.”

She didn't say her presence made fear unnecessary.

“So am I. I'll be here. I can't guarantee nothing bad is written there.”

He nodded. They stood near the door without holding hands to prove belief in each other. Back at the table, he opened his blank notebook and wrote the date. The first stroke wandered.

When the termination plate entered its mount, the custodian read the clearer surface heading: Final Account, followed by a damaged sequence designation. Restricted transcription, for review appeared nearby. Those historical words didn't authorize publication of the whole page today.

The light changed. Faint marks beneath the ink layer gradually emerged.

Name: Adrian Reed.

Elena's fingers stopped at the table edge. His name occupied a narrow field like anybody else's. He looked for a misspelling, an unfamiliar second name, something. There was none.

A matching name didn't make the events his memories.

Luna reached the action field without guessing missing letters. Beatrice adjusted the optics for another observer. The separately read, legible portions agreed: disregarded evacuation advice; forcibly interrupted central transmission.

The next field recorded spreading instability. Adrian kept looking from one column to the other. The little space between them made the progression seem terribly simple: a named person, an action, and what followed. The narrow cells offered no room for the questions beginning to crowd him.

Adrian's breathing grew shallow. What central transmission? Advice from whom? How had the interruption happened? His body felt cold before those questions could be answered.

“Continue?” Beatrice asked.

He looked away and drank. The cup struck his teeth with an embarrassing click. Elena didn't steady it for him.

“Yes. If the equipment permits.”

The next line lay lower, with part of a causal character damaged. The custodian read the remaining text and checked it from another angle. Nobody raised their voice.

World termination in this sequence. Primary attribution: Adrian Reed's interruption.

All the air seemed to stop outside his throat.

Not a delayed application. Not trusting the wrong person. The line made the world's ending the consequence of his action.

He wanted to say impossible. Nothing came out. In his own world he'd seen failed endings in games, characters falling before a screen went black. After the black screen, he could put the controller down. Here somebody had entered the darkness into a record that required storage, as though someone afterward had still been responsible for filling in its cause.

The scar on his left hand didn't shine. No complete memory arrived to confirm or refute the claim. He remained a man in a chair, with a cup of water beside him and other people trying to read a difficult surface. Nothing in his skin supplied the missing source or the voice of the person who had written it.

“That's the recorder's attribution,” Luna said at last. “We've read it. We haven't verified it.”

Her voice stayed steady, but her left hand gripped the pen hard. She wasn't telling him not to fear. An accusation seen wasn't an investigation completed.

Adrian suddenly noticed the clerk was still writing.

“Wait.”

The pen stopped. Thinking he meant the reading, the clerk signaled Beatrice, and the angle adjustment stopped too.

Adrian stared at his name on the paper. He wanted to prevent the sentence reaching a second surface. If only one old plate carried it, perhaps he could still leave this room behind.

“What are you recording?”

“The legible text and who read it. Not your admission.”

The clerk turned the page where Adrian could see without crossing the boundary. The sentence was marked plate account, followed by a pending-verification note. Adrian hadn't signed it.

He read for a long time, then requested an addition: he had no corresponding memory and hadn't confirmed the recorder's attribution. Did he want that personal response included in today's observations? Yes.

Elena wanted his response at the top. She waited for him to choose its position. He placed it beside the relevant entry, not in a distant appendix, and didn't use it to erase what everyone had actually seen.

“There.”

Only when the page returned to the clerk did he notice his right nails digging into his palm. Cecilia pushed an empty coaster toward him without touching his hand. He could put the pen down. Its small sound on the table brought back the noise of a water cart outside.

For a moment he'd almost asked them to protect him by doing what he'd opposed: preventing an unfavorable record from being accurately documented. Realizing that didn't calm him. He simply left the pen on the coaster instead of gripping it.

Isabella moved half a step forward, stopping at the boundary.

“Who wrote it?”

Old impressions obscured the signature. The end of the date was damaged, the source page incomplete. The record claimed a termination and named Adrian its primary cause. This plate alone couldn't establish the event's scope, available choices, full causal sequence, or deliberate omissions.

“Then is it false?” Elena asked.

Beatrice didn't offer the answer she wanted.

“An old object doesn't make every statement true. A frightening statement doesn't make it a recent fabrication.”

Cecilia recorded both. She studied termination for a long time. An old relationship record had been used to demand present choices from her. Now the same movement reached toward something much more terrifying.

The lamp caught a worn edge mark that looked like an open mouth. Adrian remembered words he'd said many times when people needed help: save them first; don't wait.

What if an earlier version of him had said that too?

What if he'd truly believed he was saving people?

Those possibilities hurt more than straightforward denial. He wouldn't accept them as fact, but couldn't pretend this was research unrelated to him.

Luna's allotted time ended. She asked the assistant to stop adjusting. Beatrice removed direct illumination and checked the object's condition. Another line wasn't reason to extend Luna's pain. Adrian's agreement to continue wasn't permission to read indefinitely either.

“There's another cross-reference,” the custodian said. “Not clearly read today. We can't complete it.”

Adrian nodded. Even his question about the next appointment came quietly.

Preservation continued. Authorized excerpts would be checked by the observers and supplied with gaps and uncertainty, not guesses polished into complete sentences. His name on the plate didn't make it his property. He could request examination and choose not to keep staring at it now.

Outside, he misstepped toward the coat rack. Elena took down his scarf and asked whether he wanted to put it on himself. He wound it twice, leaving one end absurdly long.

She didn't laugh.

“You can.”

“I don't want to right now.”

Better than forcing ordinary cheer for him. He folded the long end back. His hands still weren't entirely cooperative.

Isabella suggested going home before deciding the afternoon, without issuing orders to silence every rumor. Cecilia stayed to establish which associated pages she could request, not to sign on Adrian's behalf. Luna went home to rest. Before leaving, she said she would record every certain letter accurately, and every uncertain one too.

Elena walked with Adrian to the gate. A chestnut seller argued with a driver about how many belonged in a bag. The horse snorted. The driver counted again, laughed, and tucked the bag into his coat.

The world was still moving.

For the first time Adrian couldn't easily use that fact as comfort. Perhaps the record meant another iteration. He couldn't say why the thought came so readily, only that it had entered the carriage with him. Disliking it wouldn't leave it outside.

Elena sat opposite without blocking his way out.

“Today we saw a terrible record.”

He watched her.

“Today we didn't learn everything about you.”

He closed his eyes and finally nodded. The authorized excerpt hadn't arrived yet. He already knew its first line. His own name had never seemed so much like a word he would have to learn to read again.
