# Chapter 290: The First Piece Removed

On May 23, Adrian was told to change his hat.

His own was suitable for corridors, carriages, and places where things didn't fall on people's heads. The workshop supervisor handed him a hard hat with an inconveniently long strap. Watching his third attempt from the waiting area, Isabella turned away.

“You may laugh.”

“I'm respecting your effort.”

“That sounds worse.”

She laughed. Her left wrist remained supported, so she didn't reach to help. She simply explained that the buckle was on the other side. At last he qualified for the observation area, acquiring an impressively straight red line across his forehead.

The old Crown of Destiny neither shone nor roared over Oswin's loss of unilateral authority. The low vibration behind the wall belonged to the protective main bridge, still working. People were on duty, and the next shift would have to relieve them. Someone was putting lunch into an insulated box. Neither yesterday's judgment nor the coming dismantling had removed the need to eat, work, and come back. The supposedly divine apparatus was, first, a workplace that couldn't be left unstaffed.

After yesterday's handover, the court had separately authorized limited decommissioning, based on the independent review and supplementary structural checks. Today's work concerned the parked preparation branch, not the main bridge or the core behind five sealed locks. The additional load assessment requested by the reviewers was now complete. The engineer who'd signed it attended in person; sending his name ahead wouldn't fulfill his responsibility. The allowed boundary had become a real work area, with people answerable for what happened there.

The shared frame remained against the wall: the main-bridge monitoring mount above, the isolated selector linkage below. Two equally old pieces of metal had quite different destinations.

A new helper suggested hanging a flag nearby.

“It would obstruct the sightline,” the supervisor said.

No flag went up. Work began.

Engineers rechecked local readings, isolation, and supports before the fitter loosened the first connection. A Temple technician familiar with the facility performed the cross-check. His qualifications and his involvement in orders had been examined separately. Remaining on staff didn't mean surrendering to the victors. He could still tell the independent engineer to stop.

He soon did.

The core hadn't awakened. Releasing one clamp had slightly shifted a reference mark on the frame. The movement remained below the operational alarm threshold but exceeded the limit approved for this job. The observer called its location. Workers secured their tools and withdrew. The neighboring shift checked the main bridge without shutting down protection.

Someone in the waiting area cursed quietly. After so many days, perhaps nothing would come out after all.

Adrian reached for his folder, wanting the page that proved they could continue. Recognizing the impulse, he put it down. Once, he'd wanted a plan to succeed badly enough to conceal uncertainty beneath “stable overall.” Today nobody needed him to argue on behalf of metal.

Inspection showed more load passing through the connection than the old dimensions suggested. A later-added shim pressed two supposedly separate sections together. It had been seen during inspection, but its effect during unloading had been missed. The supervisor recorded that mistake under his own responsibility and requested independent confirmation of revised temporary supports and removal order.

“Can work resume today?” Isabella asked.

“I can answer after verification.”

She nodded without reminding him that people outside expected good news.

Before noon, they had to leave the work area. Isabella chose a window bench. Adrian sat at the other end, leaving room for her wrist cushion. She observed that the hard hat was excellent at stamping commoners.

“At least I can take it off.”

Her smile faded slowly. After a pause, she asked whether he could stop being quite so careful.

He turned toward her.

“I know you love Elena, and that you won't be my romantic partner. You've answered honestly. Sometimes I'm still sad. That isn't a fault you must repair with every sentence.”

The reassurance he'd been preparing stopped.

“What should I do?”

“Private invitations are still paused. Today we're both waiting for the inspection. Talk to me normally during the work break. I'll say if I want to be alone. Don't suddenly pretend we never shared a dreadful meal because you're afraid I'll misunderstand.”

“The carrots were rather good.”

“That's what you should correct.”

She laughed first. He didn't promise her the same place under a different name. He agreed to listen to what she actually said, instead of making ordinary work conversations an apology. Her sadness could remain her own without requiring either of them to pretend the answer had changed. She asked him to untie her lunch bundle. He did, then returned it without arranging the following week for her.

Outside, a cart reversed under a woman's shouted directions. Missing the last words, the driver stopped and asked again. Isabella watched. She'd once thought perfect cooperation meant never needing another question.

“And now?”

“Now I hope he doesn't hit the wall. I haven't budgeted for another wall.”

That afternoon, the new external support passed physical checks. The independent reviewer approved resumption within the original authorization; the delay and changes were recorded. The fitter released the load again. The reference mark stayed within the permitted range. Finally, the selector linkage detached and lowered into its padded cradle.

It was shorter than Adrian had imagined.

No names, curses, or face to defeat. Just polished contact surfaces and old oil. Yet in its working position, it had transmitted one person's decision toward the next preparation stage while other people's refusals struggled to catch up.

The custodian recorded its original position, orientation, and receipt time before sealing the cradle. Someone suggested having the five marked women touch the removed piece as witnesses. Isabella shook her head; staff prohibited unnecessary contact with evidence.

“They needn't be called before the machine again to prove it's missing a piece,” Adrian said.

The linkage entered independent custody. A fixed barrier replaced it. Checks performed without connecting to the core confirmed that the old selector could no longer transmit preparation movement through this section. Someone might still wish to write an order. It could no longer travel this route.

The five locks stayed shut. Behind the wall, the main bridge hummed.

After the final checks, the Temple technician lingered before the empty mounting. When he'd first learned to maintain it, his teacher had told him not to ask where the lists came from. He'd thought avoiding the paperwork meant responsibility only for tools.

“Did you ask later?” Adrian said.

“Too late. I've submitted my duty records.”

He didn't request reassurance that he'd merely been a worker. Adrian couldn't supply it. They watched the gap, then the technician gathered his instruments. He would still attend prayers tonight.

“That's your choice.”

“I think so too. People here used to speak of obeying operating orders and keeping faith as if they were the same thing.”

The shift clerk stopped him to confirm his additional hours. The delay would be charged to approved engineering expenditure, not taken from residents' housing funds. He signed the actual finishing time. Losing an improper authority hadn't made an ordinary worker's labor free.

Adrian returned his visitor pass. He took only the authorized public result, not even a small bolt for Elena. The pieces belonged to a history under investigation, not to somebody's victory spoils.

At ground level that evening, Viola heard the transport report. Her left arm remained supported. With her right hand, she indicated mud left by a trolley. Tomorrow's recording-housing move must avoid residents' collection appointments. Important cargo didn't entitle its carriers to make everyone wait indefinitely. The coordinator adjusted two agreed passage periods and notified those affected. Viola needn't guard the route until midnight.

When Elena arrived, Adrian was wearing the hat again.

“Has the workshop left a copy on your head?”

He removed it. Her shoulders shook as she examined his forehead. Might she offer a little sympathy? Certainly, she said, and continued laughing. He offered his hand. She approached and took it, fingers warm.

“It really came out?” she asked quietly.

“One piece. Not everything.”

She wasn't disappointed. “Today I wanted that one piece to be real.”

They bought supper on the corner. The stew knew nothing of courts or revoked authority and remained resolutely salty. Elena requested extra water. They shared it seriously, without turning the evening into another victory speech.

On May 24, the recording housing's removal was quieter.

Conservators and engineers first confirmed that the drive connection to be detached held no residual requiring collection, isolation remained intact, and the adjacent main-bridge mounting hadn't changed. Supporting the distorted side, workers moved the whole housing with its designated supports into the prepared dry preservation room. Nobody pried it apart or smashed it. Retiring the selection mechanism didn't mean disposing of history.

A representative who opposed dismantling witnessed from the permitted position and requested that his concerns about preservation be recorded. The custodian obliged, then invited him to check the seals. He couldn't halt authorized work that met its conditions, but he could preserve specific objections and later examine what had happened.

Luna attended one scheduled consultation in the preservation room. Her right palm remained wrapped. She asked her assistant to open the May 15 diagram of the deformed housing rather than reach to steady the newly arrived casing.

“Uglier than your drawing,” Adrian said.

“My drawing wasn't commissioned to flatter it.”

Through the approved nondestructive inspection point, the conservator compared the earlier sample with its current state. Existing damp damage was causing part of a historical recording medium to separate. A further small section at the edge had become unreadable. Dry storage could slow deterioration, not restore the lost texture.

This wasn't a prophecy of destruction tonight. But writing “temporarily stored” wouldn't stop time either.

Did existing copies cover the affected section? Luna asked.

The custodian compared them. Ordinary operating records had independently accessible transcripts, but several old-loop tracks survived only with intermittent indexes. Direction markings differed between periods. Copying straight from the present catalog could join neighboring tracks as though they formed one sequence.

“Compare the preserved reader records first,” she said. “Where they don't match, leave them unmatched.”

Adrian watched her. He remembered that gray-silver color, and how badly he'd once wanted the fragments to show him a version of the past that was easy to forgive.

Staff brought out the existing anchor-comparison index. One location had previously corresponded with his old scar's response. It might help identify a historical position. That couldn't authenticate the entire housing's contents, and his memories couldn't fill gaps. Copying would still depend on the reader, certified transfer media, and qualified custodians. His body wasn't a new storage vessel.

“Show me the specific question,” Adrian said. “First tell me which work can continue if I stop.”

Luna nodded and turned the application explanation toward him. She didn't reach for his left hand.

Outside, duty workers carried an empty cradle back. The old selection structure was missing actual components for the first time. The protection still in service hadn't disappeared with them. Adrian finished the first page and turned it over.

They'd begun dismantling the route that forced people onward. Next, what had happened along that route must no longer remain imprisoned in the same machine.
