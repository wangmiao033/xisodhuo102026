# Why Do All the Villainesses Want to Marry Me?
## Volume Two: The Saint Won't Let Me Get Married
### Chapter 60: This Time, Let's Save Goodbye for Next Time

On November eighth, Martha brought two trunks to the breakfast table.

Adrian Reed looked up.

"We were only thinking of a trip outside the city."

"I know. Clothes on the left. Everything my lady said she didn't need on the right."

Elena opened the right-hand trunk. Inside lay the travel book she'd returned to its shelf the previous night, two pairs of gloves, and a box of apple pastries, all neatly packed.

"I only said I didn't need a third shawl."

"The third shawl is on the left."

Adrian decided against joining a noble family's struggle over strategic supplies. He reached for a pastry, but Martha gently held the lid shut.

"For the journey."

"We haven't decided where we're going."

"Then decide that first."

A knock at the door ended his negotiations.

The visitor wore a mud-spattered cavalry coat. A strip of leather formed a temporary repair around his left boot. He was in his twenties, pale from traveling without enough sleep, but his bow was precise.

"Corporal Owen Hart, Third Border Patrol. Captain Viola Blackwood sent me with a request for assistance."

Adrian's cup knocked against its saucer.

He knew that name.

In *Crown of Fate*, the female knight in black armor had always stood perfectly straight. In the end, she'd been stripped of her sword beneath a traitor's banner. But which road had the incriminating grain wagons taken? How had her orders been altered? All he remembered were scattered images from scenes he'd skipped and could no longer replay, however much he regretted it.

The game hadn't left him a single useful figure that might save someone.

He steadied his cup.

"How is she now?"

"At Greymarch, still serving," Owen said. "She's accused of refusing supply orders without authority and suspected of aiding the enemy. The allegations have been submitted for review. There's no conviction."

Hale was sent for. Owen produced his identification, a sealed letter of request, and a registration acknowledgment from the border review office.

The letter concerned a specific dispute.

A supplemental order required all of Willowford's winter grain to be sent to the western-slope military depot. The original allocation orders expressly reserved the villagers' winter share. A stone bridge along the transport route had recently been damaged. If the families accompanying the wagons evacuated by the new route, they would be stranded at an exposed mountain pass.

Viola had refused to acknowledge receipt of the supplemental order until its source was verified. Instead, she'd sent mounted patrols to escort residents along the river road, which remained passable, and retained the village's emergency grain sacks. The supply department had then accused her of obstructing military orders and suggested she might have delivered the supplies to merchants from enemy territory.

"Why ask us?" Elena said.

"She isn't asking House Rosenberg to put the army on trial. Greymarch's clerk was injured. The review office has authorized her to invite two outside witnesses to help compare commercial transport documents cleared for disclosure. She read the correction notice from the capital's aid-records case."

Rosenberg caravans also used that route. Two civilian transfer stations along it were leased by the family. Wrongly impounded wagons or interrupted winter grain deliveries could hurt both local trade and residents.

The invitation included an access authorization from the local quartermaster review office. It covered civilian transport and voluntarily supplied documents, with no access to military secrets or authority over troops.

Elena turned to the final page.

"She asks us to preserve evidence against her, too."

Owen nodded.

"She said if she only wanted someone to argue her case, friends would be easier to find than witnesses."

Adrian suddenly wanted to meet this captain.

This time, it wasn't because he remembered how beautiful she'd been in the game.

Hale refused to let an impressive seal substitute for verification. He sent a copy of the acknowledgment to the capital's border liaison office to check the request number and messenger list. The family's trading office checked the records of its two most recent returning caravans through Greymarch. Owen waited in the entrance hall over a bowl of hot porridge, not even hurrying to change his boots.

Adrian kept him company and asked how long the journey would take.

"Two days, if the horse changes go smoothly. Leave this afternoon, the eighth, and arrive the afternoon of the tenth."

Adrian looked at the trunks.

Martha stood nearby, suspiciously serene.

"Can you see the future?"

"No. I just know you both."

The liaison office's acknowledgment arrived before noon. The request had been registered on the sixth; Owen's dispatch details matched, and the limited invitation was valid. The trading office confirmed the damaged bridge, but couldn't identify who had issued the supplemental order.

Trouble supported by actual evidence was generally harder to pretend not to hear about.

Elena slid the acknowledgment toward Adrian.

"I intend to go. First to the civilian transfer stations, then to serve as a witness within the invitation's terms. You can stay in the capital. This trip isn't part of your usual work."

"What would I do if I went?"

"Keep your own account of what you see. Say so when you don't know something. Tell me when you're afraid."

"Sounds well suited to my talents."

He looked again at the request to retain unfavorable evidence and nodded.

"I'll go. But I've never fought in a war. Please don't introduce me as an expert on army provisions."

"Agreed. I wasn't planning to send you charging into battle, either."

"Could I have that in writing?"

Smiling, she handed him a pen.

While they packed, Elena took out an old map her mother had left her. Faded writing beside the trade route warned of cold mountain winds in late autumn and reminded the reader that the people hauling goods needed somewhere to sleep.

"I used to notice only the roses Mother drew on her maps," she said. "Now I see that underneath them, she asked for another room with a stove for the drivers."

Adrian moved closer without touching the paper.

"You hope the border station still has it?"

"I do. It may have been changed long ago. I'd like to see for myself."

She put away the map, then picked up a coat so thick it looked capable of standing unaided.

"This is for you."

Adrian put it on and struggled to find his hands inside the sleeves.

"If we're attacked, I can disguise myself as a small supply wagon."

"At least you'll be a warm one."

She reached to fold his cuffs, then stopped halfway through.

"May I?"

"Yes. I thought you were checking the weight of the cargo."

"Inspection complete. Far too much talking inside."

He lowered his head, laughing, and she laughed with him. Her fingertips brushed his wrist. She didn't hurry away.

Passing the open door, Owen kept his eyes straight ahead with admirable discipline. Once downstairs, he earnestly asked Martha whether people in the capital always needed such a lengthy uniform inspection before setting out.

Martha tucked the third shawl into a trunk.

"Those two do."

Before departing, they stopped at Mercy Clinic.

The advice room's notice was already up in the courtyard, with a little sun drawn by Mira at the bottom. It still looked rather like a fried egg, but a very proud one.

Cecilia was sorting two receipts that patients had voluntarily left with her. After hearing the news from the border, she first asked about their itinerary and the scope of the invitation.

Then she said, "I'm staying in the capital."

Adrian didn't need a single argument he'd prepared to persuade her.

"Sabine can inspect a batch of transfer indexes next week. I've promised to help two families check their receipts. And there's this." She touched the table. "It's only just stopped wobbling. I can't announce all my plans and immediately hand someone else the key."

"Aren't you worried about us?" Elena asked.

"Yes. But following you isn't the only way to care."

She took three sealed work letters from the drawer and gave them to Vera to arrange delivery to the relevant custodians. The historical transfer ring remained sealed in independent custody. Their departure wouldn't turn subsequent experiments into demonstrations on living people. Cecilia had her own leads to pursue and people with whom she could work.

Watching her, Adrian understood that staying this time didn't mean being left behind.

She had finally chosen where she wanted to stand.

Dr. Winter put a new notice on the board. The C-19 and C-91 identification errors had been corrected. The false completion record would no longer justify ending aid. Patients who required treatment would continue receiving it. Individual responsibility would be determined separately, according to the evidence.

Jonah's mother stood nearby, holding his next follow-up appointment slip.

"The nurse got his name right today," she said.

She didn't beam. She simply folded the paper with great care.

Perhaps this was what the end of an adventure really looked like, Adrian thought. No bells ringing throughout the city, no sudden disappearance of every illness. Just being able to return under your own name to the doctor you needed to see.

Vera arrived with acknowledgments confirming that every receiving office had recorded the withdrawal issued two days earlier. The decision not to open a capital prosecution against Cecilia had also been delivered. The document accusing her of sacrilege for shutting down a dangerous device could no longer hang over her. Dorian Mercer's conduct remained under separate investigation. The acknowledgment wasn't a judgment against him.

Cecilia read it and put it in her cabinet.

This time, she didn't ask whether another notice might follow.

"Can we say one thing is definitely over today?" Adrian asked.

"Yes. This one is over."

Elena embraced her.

It was brief, unplanned, and had nothing to do with rivalry.

"Look after yourself."

"You, too."

As they stepped apart, Cecilia looked at Adrian.

"About that bakery yesterday. I'd like to change the invitation."

Elena raised an eyebrow slightly.

"Put it on my account for later?" Adrian asked.

"No. Nobody owes me a date. When you come back, if I still want to invite you, I'll ask again. You can answer then."

Something inside him, always afraid of taking a wrong step, eased.

"I'd like that."

Cecilia held out a small paper packet.

"You can answer this one now. Mira bought too many honey cakes. Would you take some?"

"Yes. Much easier question."

"I'll tell her at least one diplomatic initiative succeeded."

Mira poked her head out of the room.

"Only three cakes. Please don't let Lady Elena think I sent a whole box."

Elena held out her hand.

"With the quantity so clearly declared, I'll claim one on behalf of the other travelers."

The three of them shared the cakes outside the clinic, immediately relieving the packet of any need to travel.

Dr. Winter had examined Adrian that morning and cleared him to travel by carriage. If he felt unwell on the road, he was to stop and seek medical help.

When they left that afternoon, the Rosenberg carriage carried an additional guard familiar with the northern road and plenty of changes of clothes. Original documents remained with their respective custodians; only the certified copies they needed would travel with them.

Hale would stay in the capital to handle the remaining procedures. He gave them contact details for local legal assistance at the border. Elena instructed the trading office to cooperate with the checks, without telling anyone to declare Viola innocent in advance.

Owen had changed his boots. He rode ahead.

Adrian climbed into the carriage and found the travel book open in the middle of the seat at the little bridge in the valley.

"We originally agreed to go sightseeing on a day off," he said.

"I remember."

"Now a bridge is damaged, a knight's accused of treason, and the trunks contain work documents."

Elena looked at him.

"Which is why I won't count the whole journey as a date."

She moved the book, leaving a place beside her.

"But after supper tonight, if the inn has somewhere to sit by a window, I'd like some time with you. As long as we both have the energy."

Adrian sat down and took her hand of his own accord.

"I'd like that, too."

As the carriage rounded the corner, Cecilia still stood outside the clinic. She waved, then turned to help an elderly visitor through the door.

Adrian watched her walk into the autumn light. He didn't think of her as someone waiting for him to return.

She still had a whole day's life to write for herself.

The wheels carried them slowly out of the capital. Two full days of road lay ahead, and their new troubles had yet to appear.

For now, Elena's hand was warm, and the last trace of honey from the cakes clung to his fingers.

And back in the city, no old betrothal could make her wait for someone else's permission to welcome tomorrow.
