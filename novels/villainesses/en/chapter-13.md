# Why Do All the Villainesses Want to Marry Me?
## Volume One: A Betrothal Written in Blood
### Chapter 13: That's Not How You Deny a Rumor

On September twenty-third, Adrian Reed made the front page for the third time.

The first illustration had portrayed him as a gallant knight kissing a duke's daughter.

The second had made him a cruel servant who'd rejected a noblewoman's love.

This time, the artist had him kneeling in the middle of a courtroom, presenting a diamond engagement ring to the judge.

The headline read:

**ONE KISS, ONE ACQUITTAL?**

"At least they finally got my height right," Adrian said.

Martha took the paper away.

"Could you stop judging the artwork for one minute?"

"What should I be looking at, then?"

She turned to page two.

There, tucked beneath the scandal, was a formal objection filed by the Crown's legal representatives. It questioned Adrian's testimony on September twentieth on the grounds that he was a Rosenberg employee with an intimate relationship to Elena.

Adrian read it.

His smile faded.

"They don't say the account books are forged."

"No."

"They don't say the sealing date was correct."

"No."

"So if they can prove I like Lady Elena, September eighteenth can suddenly come before September seventeenth?"

Martha tried very hard not to laugh.

"Her Ladyship is waiting in the study. But eat first. She specifically said you aren't to go out on an empty stomach."

"A new employee benefit?"

"She doesn't want to retrieve a fainted administrative aide from the courthouse steps."

Adrian picked up his bread.

Three times a servant's wages had its attractions.

Apparently, so did a certain amount of personal concern.

Hale had spread the relevant papers across the study desk.

"This is a challenge to the credibility of a witness, not a formal appeal of the ruling," he explained. "The Crown hasn't requested renewed detention. It wants the public to believe the judge relied on a young man who was trying to impress his employer."

"But there were other witnesses."

"Exactly. They're testing us. If we're desperate enough to defend our reputations, we might say something they can use."

Elena stood near the window with a cup of cold tea.

"Whether I say I'm in love with Adrian or that I'm not, tomorrow's headline becomes a romantic dispute."

Adrian glanced up.

It was the first time she'd put their names into a sentence about being in love quite so casually.

Elena noticed.

"What?"

"Nothing. Your analysis is sound."

"I should hope so."

Hale pulled out a blank statement form.

"We can apply to have an explanation of the court's procedures posted in the public notices office. But we must distinguish established findings from matters still under investigation."

"I drafted something last night."

Adrian handed him a folded page.

It listed public evidence numbers, dates verified by notaries, and the ruling that had prevented Elena's immediate detention.

He'd deliberately omitted Henry's address, the location of the original records, and any unverified suspicion about the warehouse fire.

Hale read it.

"You're learning caution."

"I have someone reminding me every day not to do anything stupid."

Elena lifted her chin.

"Someone?"

"A very frightening noblewoman."

"How reassuring that you remember her."

"Quite vividly."

Hale closed his folder and excused himself to arrange the carriage.

After he left, Elena pushed a plate of biscuits toward Adrian.

"How much did you sleep last night?"

"Four hours."

"Make up the other four tonight."

"Didn't I sleep properly the night before?"

"You also claimed you wanted to live a long life."

Adrian looked at the biscuits.

She seemed able to see fear even when he tried to hide it behind exhaustion and jokes.

The public notices office stood along the side of the Judicial Office, near the windows where ordinary citizens filed land and household records.

Someone ahead of Adrian in the queue was applying to dissolve an engagement.

He glanced at Elena.

"Would it be safer if I joined a different line?"

"You're submitting a statement, not getting married."

"I know. I'm not convinced anyone around us does."

She let out a small laugh.

Hale handed the statement to the registrar, who checked the supporting signatures and stamped a receipt.

"This certifies that we've received your application," the woman explained. "It doesn't mean we accept every claim. Publication is subject to review."

"Understood," Hale said.

Adrian actually felt better.

At least this registrar wasn't insisting a procedure was perfect while being unable to explain why evidence received on the seventeenth had been sealed on the eighteenth.

They'd barely reached the steps when reporters descended.

"Mr. Reed! Have you come to beg Lady Rosenberg's forgiveness?"

"Is it true your wages have tripled?"

"Do you sleep on the same floor as Her Ladyship?"

Adrian shut his mouth.

Hale handed out the application number for the public statement, while the Rosenberg guards kept the journalists below the steps without pushing anyone.

One young reporter shouted over the others.

"The Crown says you challenged the prince in court because you're in love with Elena Rosenberg. Is that true?"

Adrian glanced at Elena.

She didn't tell him to stop.

She didn't rush to defend him.

Her expression said he could make his own choice.

"I challenged an impossible date," Adrian replied. "Not the prince."

"But are you in love with her?"

"What would that change about the date?"

The reporter blinked.

Before he could try again, an older journalist raised his hand.

"Mr. Reed. Are you trying to protect Lady Rosenberg's reputation?"

"Yes."

The answer escaped before Adrian could reconsider it.

Elena turned toward him.

"Simply because she's your employer?"

Adrian faced the crowd.

"You've taken a woman who was falsely accused and made it sound as though she manipulated the court by kissing a servant. But she was the one who insisted the palace preserve its evidence. She stood up for herself at the hearing, too. She didn't ask anyone else to make her decisions."

He paused.

"I don't want what she did reduced to 'a duke's daughter fell for a servant.'"

For the first time all morning, the reporters were almost quiet.

Elena stepped forward.

"If you have questions about my feelings, ask me. Today, however, I won't answer them. My private life isn't an excuse for the Judicial Office to avoid examining its evidence."

She took the steps without waiting for another question.

Adrian followed.

It wasn't until they rounded the corner that he noticed how wet his palms were.

Elena stopped.

"Why didn't you tell me you were going to say that?"

"Say what?"

"That you cared about my reputation."

Adrian considered his answer.

"Because I wasn't saying it to make you happy. I genuinely don't like seeing people dismiss everything you've done with a silly story about us."

She looked down at her gloves.

"You're starting to sound like a real administrative aide."

"Does that come with a raise?"

"Don't push your luck."

She resumed walking, but more slowly, so he could fall into step beside her.

At the edge of the square, Adrian stopped near a roasted-chestnut stall.

"My lady, have you had lunch?"

"Are you going to start supervising my meals?"

"You've had half a cup of tea all day."

She gave him a look but didn't answer.

He bought a small paper bag of chestnuts.

"I make three times a servant's wages now. I can at least afford these."

"That's your grand gesture?"

"If you'd like something more expensive, deduct it from next month's pay."

Elena accepted the bag and peeled one. She handed him the second.

"Perhaps you should save the money to pay for your new coat."

He blinked.

"Pay for my coat?"

"Martha didn't take it out of the accounts for charity."

"...That explains why she said it was recorded under my name."

Elena laughed.

It was softer than the laugh she used when fending off an insult at the palace.

They hadn't left the square when Hale hurried after them with a newspaper clipping.

"There's something strange in this."

He pointed to a small line near the bottom.

*According to an informed source, the Rosenberg estate used the Blue Wax Loan Register to verify evidence delivered to the palace.*

Adrian frowned.

"That's not what our ledger is called."

"Exactly." Hale opened his case. "The proper title is *Blue Sealing Wax Issue Ledger*. 'Blue Wax Loan Register' is an incorrect abbreviation that appears in an internal Judicial Office intake summary."

"Was that summary ever read aloud?"

"No. The court mentioned purchasing records, but not that shortened title."

Elena took the clipping.

"So someone involved in the story has seen a copy of the internal summary."

"Possibly," Hale said. "But four papers repeating the exact same wrong name makes coincidence harder to accept."

Adrian looked through the other papers.

The wording wasn't merely similar.

Even a slightly damaged comma appeared in the same place.

"It's the same typeset copy."

Hale nodded.

"Then we can check whether the printers registered a bulk commission. We still can't force journalists to reveal confidential sources."

"Let's visit the printers' guild," Elena said.

Half an hour later, they were sitting in its public office.

The middle-aged clerk behind the desk refused to reveal any reporter's private identity.

"I don't want a name," Adrian explained. "Only whether four papers commissioned the same set of plates through a registered institution."

"Private orders are confidential."

"What about institutional orders?" Hale submitted the relevant application. "Guild rules require registered bulk printers to list the commissioning office and job number. We're requesting only public fields."

The woman studied the request, then sent it to another desk.

While they waited, a young reporter in a blue hat came in to collect proofs.

Adrian recognized him from the courthouse steps.

"Checking on a story?"

"Chasing our printer. Again."

"Did your paper run that line about the Blue Wax Loan Register?"

The reporter immediately became wary.

"I can't identify my sources."

"I'm not asking you to." Adrian showed him the article. "Did your editors write that incorrect title themselves?"

The young man examined it for a moment.

"Yesterday we were given a prepared palace piece from what we were told was an official source. Our editor changed the headline. Not much else."

"You saw the original layout?"

"Yes. But I'm not giving you a statement."

"Then I won't claim you did."

The reporter looked surprised.

"Aren't you supposed to threaten me until I hand over the names?"

"I said it outside the courthouse. If facts are to be believed, people need to be able to check them. Making someone repeat my version doesn't help."

"Then I hope you find the truth."

The clerk returned with the bulk commission register.

The four articles had indeed used the same typesetting plates.

The commissioning office was listed as:

**Judicial Office — Public Notices and Distribution.**

Adrian stared.

"The Judicial Office is paying to publish stories about my love life?"

"Let's not leap ahead," Hale said. "That establishes who commissioned the printing, not who wrote the text or who ordered the rumors."

Elena pointed to a dark square on the form.

"Why is this covered?"

The clerk moved the paper closer to the window.

"An authorizing seal should be there. Institutions must leave a reduced impression on the plate instructions. Someone covered this one with black ink."

"Can the guild show us the original authorization?"

"No. The commissioning office retains its own authorization copy. We only hold the public printing record."

Hale filed a request to preserve the plate records and commission entry, then obtained a signed receipt of the inspection.

Elena checked the date and signature herself.

"Every little piece of paper?" Adrian asked softly.

"The original evidence ledger burned on September nineteenth," she replied. "If someone can destroy a record, we need proof that we saw it before it vanished."

He stopped joking.

A signature.

A seal.

An ordinary receipt.

None of these would automatically put the person behind the conspiracy in a cell. But together, they made it harder to erase the truth.

Rain had begun by the time they left the guild.

Adrian took off his coat to hold above Elena's head.

She looked at it.

"You still owe the accounts office for that."

"Then consider this a short-term loan."

Elena didn't take it.

Instead, she caught his sleeve and pulled him under the nearest awning.

"We'll wait for the carriage. Neither of us needs to get wet."

Rain pattered against the stone pavement.

She looked up at him.

"What you said on the courthouse steps. Did you mean it, or was it another impulse?"

"I meant it."

"You'd thought it through?"

"Yes." Adrian hesitated. "I don't want to decide anything about your feelings for you. But when someone uses those rumors to hurt you, it still makes me angry."

Elena said nothing.

She merely pushed the remaining chestnuts into his hand.

"They've gone cold. Finish them."

He accepted the bag.

"Does that mean I've done my job today?"

"Half of it."

"And the other half?"

She raised the printers' guild receipt.

"Find out whose seal was hidden under that ink."

Adrian nodded.

Unlike the prince's forged letter, this wasn't a claim made by a potentially dishonest witness.

It was paperwork created by an actual institution.

And where an approving official's name ought to be, someone had left only black ink.
