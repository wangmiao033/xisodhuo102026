# Why Do All the Villainesses Want to Marry Me?
## Volume One: A Betrothal Written in Blood
### Chapter 20: The Thank-You She Couldn't Say

On the morning of September thirtieth, Adrian smelled something burning.

His first instinct was to check his cuffs.

Between the Rosenberg formalwear and Elena's gown at the palace, he'd learned that clothing was not to be trusted.

His cuffs were fine.

The smell grew stronger.

Martha passed him in the hallway with a breakfast tray.

"Mr. Reed, the kitchens don't require your assistance today."

"I haven't offered."

"You look like you're about to."

"But something smells burned."

Martha's expression didn't change.

"The cook is experimenting with a new pastry."

"What kind?"

"You should go to breakfast."

That answer came suspiciously fast.

Elena arrived several minutes late.

Her hands were clean, but a dusting of white powder marked the cuff of her right sleeve.

Adrian eyed it.

"Have you been handling flour?"

She took her seat.

"There's flour in the kitchen every day."

"It doesn't normally climb up to your sleeve on its own."

"Are you going to investigate every speck of dust as though it's evidence?"

"Occupational hazard."

She pushed the jam toward him.

"Eat. We're visiting the Treasury today."

Adrian let the matter rest.

Yesterday, they'd found a *Fate Maintenance Fee* paid every month to the Old Records Preservation Office, using a clearing reference marked B-17. They could trace its immediate recipient, but not the department that had originally supplied the money.

Hale had arranged access to the Treasury's public disbursement indexes.

"No turnovers today?" Adrian asked.

"You nearly burned your tongue off yesterday."

"It wasn't that bad."

"You couldn't put together a complete sentence for nearly a minute."

"I have trouble with complete sentences on an ordinary day."

Elena almost laughed.

"An excellent point."

The Treasury's public records office had none of the gilded magnificence Adrian had expected.

It was a room of endless cabinets, people waiting for numbered tickets, and a little brass bell rung whenever another clerk became available.

They waited nearly half an hour.

At last, a stern woman behind the counter looked at Adrian.

"Purpose?"

"Old Capital Court. Source of clearing transfer B-17."

"Reason?"

"Verifying public expenditure connected with historical court records."

"Authorization?"

He produced Hale's paperwork at once.

He felt absurdly proud of himself.

Only days ago, he'd been considering climbing through windows. Now he could sort forms in advance.

The woman checked the papers.

"The general disbursement index is open to you. Restricted fund accounts are not. If you need to identify the authorizing officer, you'll have to apply separately to the Royal Treasury Inspectorate."

"We aren't seeking private account details," Elena explained. "Only the category of expenditure and the issuing department."

After another check, the clerk led them into a small reading room.

Two colors of ledger sat on its shelves.

Gray volumes covered ordinary municipal spending. Dark-blue ones tracked transfers between departments.

Adrian assumed B-17 would appear in the gray books.

Five pages later, he hadn't found it.

"Wrong year?"

Elena turned her ledger around.

"Look here."

B-17 appeared beneath a column labeled *Clearing by Proxy*.

Next to it was another code.

R6.

"What's R6?"

The clerk leaned over the page.

"An internal budget classification. You can't identify a payer from two characters."

"Is there a public index?"

"Categories, yes. Not individual approvals."

She brought over a printed classification sheet.

R6 belonged to *Central Special Transfers*.

Adrian looked at Elena.

"Special?"

"Could be wartime reserves. Could be an old ceremonial obligation. Let's not guess."

They read on.

In the municipal ledger, the Fate Maintenance Fee was paid at the end of every month.

The central account reimbursed the payment three to five days later.

"Does that mean anything?" Adrian asked.

"It means the local office paid first and was reimbursed," the clerk said. "Or the entries were settled in the next accounting period. You'd need the original vouchers to distinguish them."

Adrian wrote down both possibilities.

Hale had reminded him that a series of matching numbers was not evidence of a series of crimes.

If Adrian wanted anyone to trust him in court, he'd have to show that he trusted records more than his own exciting theories.

The clerk found an index of old vouchers dating back seven years.

The originals required inspectorate approval. Public copies showed categories of payer and recipient, with sensitive accounts blacked out.

"Why is everything covered in black ink?" Adrian muttered.

"Because these are legally restricted fields," the clerk replied. "Not every redaction means someone is trying to destroy evidence."

Elena nodded.

"Thank you."

She moved the copy in front of Adrian.

"So we know the payment is monthly and centrally reimbursed. What don't we know?"

"Why it was authorized in the first place. Someone must have approved spending money on a court that no longer holds trials."

"Exactly."

The clerk brought out the historical classification changes.

R6 had once belonged to a broad group of central expenditures concerning obsolete institutions.

Four years earlier, it had been reassigned to a special reserve category used by the royal household's internal administration.

The specific recipient account remained restricted.

"So this wasn't simply coming out of the municipal repair budget," Adrian said.

"That's fair," the clerk replied. "But this index can't tell you whether the king's household knew about each individual transfer."

Adrian wanted to demand the approving officer's name.

Then he remembered Clay, insisting every question was an insult without providing a single explanation.

He didn't want to behave like that.

"What if we only request confirmation that an authorizing decision exists?" he asked. "No amount, no private names."

The clerk considered him.

"You can request a certificate confirming the existence of an approval. It usually takes a day. If the fund is fully classified, the response may simply say we can neither confirm nor deny it."

Elena turned toward him.

"Shall we apply?"

She waited for his nod.

Adrian thought of their argument a few days earlier.

Elena was learning not to decide for him.

He was learning not to interpret every act of help as a leash.

The application was finished shortly after noon.

On the carriage ride home, Adrian was still trying to connect B-17 to R6 when Elena asked a strange question.

"Do you have any plans for later?"

"Evidence review. A report. Perhaps another meeting with Hale."

"Other than work."

Adrian blinked.

"I was going to get more sleep. You've been telling me to sleep eight hours."

"Then you can rest early tonight."

She said nothing more.

But when she tucked her hand into her cloak, he caught another trace of pale-gold powder on her little finger.

The burnt smell from the morning.

The flour on her sleeve.

The two details came together.

"My lady, are you absolutely sure you haven't been to the kitchens?"

"No."

"Then what's on your—"

"Adrian."

"Yes?"

"Please apply your new investigative skills to something useful."

He shut his mouth.

The afternoon's work was comparatively peaceful.

Hale entered their Treasury findings into a memorandum and carefully noted that nothing yet established personal involvement by the royal family in any forgery.

Adrian asked that the evidence be kept in separate files: one for Elena's treason case, another for the investigation of old case R-074.

They could compare the two.

They couldn't merge them into a single conspiracy merely because some numbers looked similar.

Hale seemed pleased.

"You're beginning to sound like a proper administrative aide."

"You and Elena both keep saying that," Adrian replied. "Strangely, my wages remain unchanged."

"Keep it up and the duke may consider making your appointment permanent."

"Then I'd better make tonight's report impressive."

"Don't start yet."

Elena stood in the doorway, Martha behind her.

"There's something I'd like you to help with."

Adrian rose immediately.

"Where are we going?"

"The little tea room beside the kitchens."

"Who are we investigating?"

"A pastry."

Martha lowered her head so fast she might have injured her neck.

The tea room was usually used by household staff during breaks. Two pots of mint sat on the windowsill.

On the table stood a white porcelain plate.

On the plate lay six small round biscuits that had plainly tried their best.

Two had blackened edges.

A third looked like a rock sculpted by someone whose artistic vision exceeded their ability.

Adrian stared.

"Which shop sold you these?"

Elena faced him.

"They weren't bought."

"A new magical experiment from the kitchens?"

"I made them."

The room fell silent.

So that was the burned smell.

And the flour.

Elena was always graceful handling a sword, signing documents, or arguing with the prince. Faced with six biscuits, she looked as though she was negotiating the surrender of a hostile nation.

"I wanted to make something with apples," she said. "You liked the turnovers yesterday."

"How did that become biscuits?"

"I burned the filling twice. Martha suggested something simpler."

Martha stood to one side, her expression impossible to describe politely.

"Her Ladyship worked very hard," she said with solemn conviction.

Adrian chose the least blackened biscuit and broke it in half.

The inside was a little dry, but soft enough.

He tasted it.

Very sweet.

A faint bitter edge from the burned base.

"Well?" Elena asked.

"Do you want a polite answer or an honest one?"

"Honest."

"If you cut away the burned bottom, they're rather good."

Elena looked down.

"So I failed."

"Not at all." He split the biscuit again. "I like the flavor. Just use less heat next time."

"You know how to bake?"

"I know how to operate an oven." Adrian grinned. "My first attempt turned out much uglier than this."

Martha cleared her throat.

"Shall I take these back to the kitchen?"

"Not yet," Adrian said.

He offered Elena the other half.

"Will you try one?"

She hesitated before accepting.

After the first bite, her brows drew together.

"Too sweet."

"A little burned too."

"You said they'd be good."

"I said they'd be good *without the burned parts*."

She glared at him.

Not seriously.

"Can't you say something nice when it matters?"

Adrian looked at her.

Suddenly he understood.

The biscuits weren't a test of his culinary judgment.

Elena might have spent her entire morning trying to find a way to thank him without preparing a statement, signing a receipt, or calling a witness.

"Thank you," he said.

Elena blinked.

"For what?"

"For taking the time. And for yesterday. For coming with me to investigate. For letting me decide for myself when the Temple came for me."

She lowered her eyes and set her biscuit down.

"I dislike owing people favors."

"Then your accounts must be wrong."

She looked up.

"What do you mean?"

"I owe you just as much. If we spent every day calculating who helped whom, we'd need an entire shelf of ledgers in this tea room."

Martha finally laughed.

After a moment, Elena did too.

"So what do we do with these?"

"Eat the parts that are good. Try again another time."

"Are you certain?"

"You made six. I'll eat three. It doesn't seem fair to make you bear the consequences of culinary failure alone."

"You really are impossible."

Nevertheless, she broke the third biscuit apart and put the better half on his plate.

For once, there was no prince.

No court summons.

No terrifying historical record.

Only mint leaves shifting in the breeze beyond the window.

Adrian found himself wishing the afternoon could last a little longer.

Then Hale came in at a hurried pace.

"Apologies for the interruption."

He carried an official reply from the Treasury.

"The existence certificate for the B-17 authorization arrived early."

Adrian put down his biscuit.

"And?"

"The Treasury confirms that B-17's central reimbursements are drawn from a restricted reserve under the royal household's internal administration. The underlying approval is not public, but the fund category may be disclosed."

He unfolded the page.

The classification appeared in plain, dry administrative language.

**Fund Category: Royal Confidential Special Reserve.**

Adrian looked from the stamped document to the burned biscuits.

The old courthouse had been shut for seven years.

Someone was still paying a monthly Fate Maintenance Fee.

And, at least according to the books, the source of those payments was a confidential royal fund.

He didn't know who signed off on it.

He didn't know what was being maintained.

But for the first time, the trail from R-074 had reached the royal family's purse.
