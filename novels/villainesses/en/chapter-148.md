# Chapter 148: The Fifth Line Beneath Her Cuff

Isabella had deliberately chosen thick gloves.

Outside the old-project repository on January twenty-first, she pulled them straight and asked whether that would spare her Adrian's reminders. He examined her thoroughly covered wrists.

“I was only going to mention the ice on the steps.”

She looked down. Her toe was half an inch from it.

“You may mention that.”

She accepted his arm to cross, releasing it inside rather than turning one moment's help into arrangements for the entire day.

Today's viewing remained within yesterday's permission. Hartwell controlled the originals; Beatrice Wren examined the registration insert and its backing. Isabella and Adrian would stand behind the observation line, recording positions and references. Luna was absent. Her written advice lay at the table's corner.

No testing on living people. No added energy. No attempt to restore a failed device.

Wren read those three points again.

The insert was smaller than Adrian expected: a thin sheet half the size of writing paper, fitted into copper backing and coated in graying silver-salt film. An old label identified a registration check for the palace watercourse project. Later repair paper covered the personal-details field. Removing it required separate arrangements.

Today they could compare the marginal position mark and reference number, not awaken the entire coating.

A hinged frame allowed the object to turn slowly, exposing its rear custody number. Wren checked the retaining catch and had unrelated objects cleared. She neither removed the insert nor spoke Isabella's name to it.

The first reference matched the index.

The second incision was shallower than its copied drawing. Adrian took half a step forward, keeping his toes behind the line. Isabella was noting the number when the copper frame made a tiny click.

Wren stopped.

“Step back.”

Before she finished, the newer catch beneath the frame broke. The backing slid outward half a hand's breadth, exposing a narrow copper edge previously concealed in its supporting groove. Isabella was withdrawing her folder. The edge swept across her left cuff and caught the top of her glove.

She did not try to catch the plate.

Contact had already occurred.

A hair-thin white line flashed along the film. Isabella jerked away as searing pain struck inside her glove. Adrian reached to pull her back, but Wren shouted faster.

“Don't touch the metal! Clear the frame!”

He took Isabella's right shoulder instead and moved her away. The custodian used the ceramic-handled separator already beside him to break the contact between backing and stand. Wren closed the light shield. The line died, leaving a faint scorched smell.

There had barely been time to finish shouting Your Highness.

Isabella steadied herself, pale as the snowy window behind her.

“I didn't catch it.”

“I saw.”

He didn't ask why she had been hurt anyway. While the custodian summoned a physician, Wren required the gloves and other contact materials to be retained. Nobody was to wash the traces off the table. Adrian asked before taking Isabella's right hand to help her sit.

Once she nodded, she held on tightly.

The physician cut away a small piece of glove lining fused by heat. A red band circled her inner left wrist, with two tiny blisters at its edge. Within the burn lay another, darker line.

An open ring. A stroke turning inward and ending low on the right.

Adrian had seen that form before.

Not on her.

In the unnamed fifth space on the comparison sheet of the fifteenth.

Following his gaze, Isabella looked down. Her breathing briefly stopped.

The physician screened out direct sunlight and treated the burn first. Gentle cleaning removed the surface powder. The finer line remained beneath the skin, faintly brightening with her pulse. Away from the insert, it neither crept farther up her arm nor projected any writing.

“Is that—”

“Let him finish examining the injury,” Adrian said.

She fell silent without releasing his hand.

The physician asked about pain and dizziness. Her wrist felt clasped in hot iron; standing had made her faint. He kept her seated, checked her temperature, and recorded a brief rise and her finger movements. Enduring discomfort silently wouldn't prove she was well.

She nearly said there were more documents to see. Instead she asked whether today's work must stop.

“Yes.”

She nodded reluctantly.

Wren didn't demand another attempt to confirm their discovery. The room became an incident-examination site, its contents still held by the original custodian. Every movement and covering was recorded. Nobody could repair the stand and quietly put it back, or sweep away the broken catch.

Adrian accompanied Isabella into the next room. Noticing her grip on his hand, she loosened it slightly.

“You may leave. I won't order you to stay.”

“I know.”

He shifted his chair half a foot to avoid her injured wrist, then sat again.

Someone brought warm water. Isabella automatically reached with her left hand. Pain stopped her, and the cup knocked against the tray. She frowned, as though it too meant to remind her how many thoughtless little movements she had lost.

Adrian didn't lift it to her mouth. He turned the tray so its handle faced her right hand. Finding it awkwardly full, she asked for some water to be poured out.

“People used to do these things before I asked.”

“You can still ask for help.”

“I've barely learned not to command people. Now I must learn to ask them.”

She drank, watching her reflection. A strand of hair had escaped beside her ear. Twice she tried to tuck it back with her right hand, then let it fall.

“As a child, I stood on a stool for dress fittings. Everyone measured around me. They didn't hurt me. I hated it anyway. Whenever I moved, someone told me where my hand belonged.”

Adrian glanced at his own hands and returned them to his lap.

“You needn't suddenly move far away,” she said. “I didn't hate holding your hand.”

She looked at him rather than her pale reflection.

“I'll remember.”

“You needn't write it down.”

They both laughed.

Studying the gap between their chairs, she gave another small laugh.

“You really do make adjustments.”

“I don't want my knees against the table all day either.”

Her lips were still pale when the laughter faded.

“My first thought was that they could finally fill in my name.”

He couldn't pretend the mark was unimportant. It lay beneath her cuff, beyond the reach of reassuring words.

“First we write what happened today.”

“And then?”

“You decide who needs to know about your injury and who only needs to know there was an incident here.”

She looked toward the closed room. Her investigative commission didn't make her the incident's supervisor. She requested an exact record of her observations and position. Her title mustn't turn a broken catch into an attendant's carelessness.

That afternoon, Wren brought a preliminary account.

An old charge-retaining layer lay within the copper backing's edge, unlisted among the visible components on the current display card. Insulating pieces normally separated its ends. When it slipped, one end met a conductive strip on the newer stand. The other connected to the exposed copper edge. A burned track remained in the silver-salt coating. The layer was now markedly depleted.

The energy had not appeared from nowhere. Nothing suggested an endlessly repeating discharge.

The broken catch was a newer replacement, shorter than the recorded specification. It had barely engaged while the frame turned. Maintenance and acceptance records would establish who replaced it and why the display card wasn't updated. For now, neither a planned attack nor a chain of dangerous failures could be established.

“Did it draw the mark on her?” Adrian asked.

“The copper made a straight scrape, including on the fabric. The shape on her skin is much more complete,” Wren said. “I can distinguish those traces. I can't explain the bodily response alone.”

The plate's scorches and the skin pattern were drawn separately. No lines were added to make them agree.

Isabella asked for the paper. The physician allowed her to hold it right-handed, not return to the room. Her voice steadied as she read.

“How was the retaining layer left off the card?”

“That's the next question.”

She didn't turn an unanswered question into a ready-made culprit.

Toward evening, Adrian brought Luna the permitted diagram and contact record. Isabella followed with her attendant for one ordinary, contact-free examination. She could lower her sleeve whenever she wished. Anything she declined to share would stay out of externally available copies.

Luna's right palm still rested on its cushion. Seeing a fresh bandage on someone else, she lost her teasing expression.

“Let me give the bad news first. Following a no-contact arrangement doesn't prove the arrangement was safe enough.”

“I wasn't going to blame you.”

“And I wasn't going to make you prove that caution always works.”

After a silence, Luna used her left hand to position the January fifteenth comparison page under the lamp. Orientation first, then the ring's opening. Only then did she ask Isabella to expose her wrist at the angle permitted by the physician.

The inward stroke ended in the fifth position.

Neither an ordinary storage symbol nor a reversed cancellation mark matched. Persistence after cleaning and changes with the pulse agreed with known bodily imprints. Here they also had a definite carrier, contact, and evidence of depleted energy. None revealed when it had been laid, to whom it had originally belonged, or what would happen to a registered person.

But the name could no longer remain blank merely because nobody had examined her.

“What appeared on you is the fifth-position fate imprint,” Luna said. “I can stand behind that. It doesn't mean you agreed to bear anything.”

Isabella lowered her sleeve. Eventually she asked whether it would disappear.

“I don't know. No further stimulation now. First, let the injury stabilize.”

She nodded, asking for no comforting lie.

Adrian remembered his confidence that the game's princess faced only a struggle for the throne. Those guides contained neither a burned glove nor a woman trying to keep her drinking hand steady. He kept his incomplete knowledge to himself. It couldn't reassure her or vouch for the facts before them.

She asked whether he was afraid.

“Yes.”

“You didn't look it.”

“I was waiting for you to sit safely.”

Her shoulders finally stopped resembling the rigid back of a council chair.

Luna left the physician's observation instructions and postponed the remaining explanations until tomorrow. Before Isabella left, she offered her a sweet.

Isabella studied the ordinary box.

“Research compensation?”

“I don't want another question about work today.”

She took one and slowly unwrapped it.

That night her left sleeve felt too tight, yet rolling it up left her cold. Her attendant helped her into a loose nightdress and waited for permission to leave. Adrian brought the incident acknowledgment to her door without crossing the threshold.

“Could you ask the others to sit with me tomorrow?”

“I can ask.”

“Not a summons. Not so I can announce who's responsible for me.”

“I'll say that.”

She looked at the unwrapped sweet beside her.

“I'd like to know how the people who saw something like this before me ate breakfast the next day.”

Her commission couldn't obtain that answer for her.

Adrian promised to pass on the invitation. After the door closed, Isabella rested her bandaged wrist beside the pillow and lifted the sweet with her right hand.

It was very sweet.

For some reason, that made a tear fall.
