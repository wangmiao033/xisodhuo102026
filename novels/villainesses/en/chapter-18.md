# Why Do All the Villainesses Want to Marry Me?
## Volume One: A Betrothal Written in Blood
### Chapter 18: I'm Not Your Property

On the morning of September twenty-eighth, Adrian found a thick legal opinion waiting beside his breakfast.

Hale had bound it in gray, with a title that took up nearly half the cover:

*Response Concerning the Old Records Preservation Ordinance and Requests for Accompanied Identity Verification.*

Adrian flipped through two pages.

"Could I use this to stop a thrown knife?"

"No," Elena said.

"It's thick enough."

"If you tried, Father would probably worry about the legal fees before he worried about you."

Hale, seated across the table, gave him a weary look.

"Please, Mr. Reed. For once, try to focus on the contents."

Adrian obliged.

The Temple had claimed it would invoke the Old Records Preservation Ordinance to arrange his "accompanied verification" today.

Hale had found the original provision.

It allowed a records office to move endangered archival materials to a secure repository and, when necessary, contact living persons named in those documents to confirm details.

*Materials.*

Not *people*.

"They're treating permission to transfer documents as permission to transfer me," Adrian said.

"Precisely. If the Temple has some other compulsory power, it must produce the relevant instrument. It cannot conjure authority out of a polite request."

"What if they arrive with an actual court order?"

"Then we check who issued it, when it expires, and exactly what it authorizes. You may still object to anything beyond that scope."

Elena pushed a small plate of eggs toward Adrian.

"Eat."

"I'm too nervous."

"Then drink your milk."

"Are you trying to fatten me up before handing me over to the Temple?"

She slowly raised her head.

"Say that again and I'll supervise your lunch too."

Adrian picked up his fork.

At the other end of the table, Duke Edmund had been listening in silence.

When they finished, he set down his newspaper.

"Today's visitor is an envoy of the Temple, not the prince. That doesn't mean nobody behind him is watching." He looked at his daughter. "Elena, you may protect your employees. You must also avoid dragging this house into an unnecessary confrontation."

"I understand."

Then he looked at Adrian.

"And you. You're not a stray cat we took in. Whether you go to the Temple is your own decision."

Adrian nearly choked on his milk.

"Your Grace, that's an oddly accurate comparison."

"Then stay off the roof."

Elena laughed.

Just before noon, a Temple carriage entered the Rosenberg grounds.

Felix Horn had returned.

This time, two guards carrying short swords accompanied him, along with a records officer holding a sealed case.

Adrian noticed the swords at once.

His stomach tightened.

Horn had his guards remain in the outer hall.

"We're not here to arrest anyone."

"Excellent," Adrian said. "I've developed an aversion to that word."

Horn didn't smile.

"Today's procedure is straightforward. Mr. Reed needs to accompany us to the Temple's old records chamber so we can verify an entry concerning his identity."

Hale rose.

"Please show us the legal authorization."

Horn presented a document bearing a gold circular seal.

Hale read it line by line.

The authorization concerned the preservation of old records and verification of an individual's identity.

It had been signed by the head of the Temple records department.

Not a judge.

"This contains no order restricting Mr. Reed's liberty," Hale said.

"It's internal Temple authorization. We don't require a court order to conduct our work."

"Internal authorization can direct your clerks to report for duty. It doesn't automatically require a Rosenberg employee to leave with you."

Horn turned away from the lawyer toward Elena.

"Lady Rosenberg. You're his employer. If you consent, we can escort him to the Temple and return him promptly."

Elena paused.

"He works under my roof. I'm responsible for ensuring his safety."

Horn seized on the words.

"Then fulfill that responsibility. Sign the transfer acknowledgment."

Adrian looked up.

The signature line at the bottom had already been reserved for Elena.

As though, once her pen touched the paper, his own wishes would cease to matter.

A bitter frustration rose in his chest.

"My lady."

Elena turned.

"I'm not your property."

The room fell silent.

Horn's fingers stopped above the form.

Elena's hand, which had been moving toward the document, withdrew.

Adrian heard the tremor in his own voice.

"I know you're trying to protect me. But if you decide where I stay, where I go, and what examinations I'll submit to, how is that different from the Temple deciding for me?"

Hale said nothing.

The duke watched from across the table.

Surprise crossed Elena's face.

Then something very like hurt.

"I wasn't going to sign for you."

Adrian stared.

"...I'm sorry."

"Don't rush to apologize." She closed her fan. "You're right. Employing someone doesn't mean owning them. I don't get to make this decision for you."

She faced Horn again.

"The person you need to ask is standing right here. Please speak to him."

The envoy seemed not to have expected this.

"Mr. Reed, the integrity of Temple records may depend on your cooperation. Don't you want to know the truth about your own history?"

"I do."

"Then why refuse?"

"I'm not refusing verification. I'm refusing to be taken away before I've even seen the authorization."

Adrian lifted Hale's legal opinion and pointed to the marked provision.

"This says endangered documents can be moved. It says nothing about moving a living person."

Horn's expression changed.

"Are you comparing the Temple to kidnappers?"

"No. I'm reading the ordinance you cited."

Elena spoke almost pleasantly.

"And he's only just learned to read these things properly. Shall we go through the text together?"

Hale managed not to laugh as he placed a second copy on the table.

"We brought extras."

Horn stared at the matching documents.

He had probably not expected to be cornered in a noble household's sitting room by two young people, a lawyer, and a rule about transporting archival materials.

"I have no ill intent," he said at last.

"I certainly hope not," Adrian replied.

"Your demands for detailed explanations suggest a troubling lack of faith in the Temple."

"Then perhaps you can begin by explaining why you're in such a hurry to take me away."

Horn glanced at the records officer.

The man opened the sealed case and laid a thick sheet in a designated space on the table.

"This is an extract from Case R-074," Horn said. "It wouldn't normally be available for public inspection. But if you insist on refusing escort, you should understand why the matter is urgent."

Adrian moved closer.

Hale raised a hand.

"Please identify its source in the meeting record. We will inspect it without taking possession or acknowledging its authenticity."

The records officer wrote down the extract's reference and read the copy number aloud. Horn allowed them to retain a stamped record of what had been displayed, but no reproduction of the document itself.

Only then did Adrian look down.

An old-style circular stamp.

Case number R-074.

A heading: *Subject of Identity Verification.*

Name: *Adrian Reed.*

Exactly the same as the academy loan register.

Beneath it lay a heavy black cancellation stamp.

**Identity Status: Deceased.**

For a moment, Adrian heard nothing but a dull rush in his ears.

Horn continued.

"The death entry is dated September twentieth, seven years ago. The old system records the closure of a Substitute case. The cause field says 'terminated after judgment.' There's no medical death certificate attached."

"So," Hale interrupted, "you have an administrative death entry under an obsolete system, with no proof that the person named is this young man."

"Which is why we need him at the Temple for verification."

"Verification may be necessary," Adrian said. "Whether I have to be taken away is a separate question."

He was speaking very slowly.

September twentieth.

Seven years ago.

The same day of the year on which he'd just prevented Elena's execution.

His crescent scar didn't glow or reveal anything. He only felt the skin draw tight as his fingers curled.

If someone could establish that Adrian Reed had truly died here seven years earlier, then perhaps the fragments he remembered from the game weren't a game at all.

But he wasn't going to surrender himself over a registry entry without a death certificate.

"Does the extract contain any other identifying features?" he asked.

Horn shook his head.

"The full file concerns an old fate contract. Those details aren't included in this summary."

"Then how can you confirm the person named is me?"

For the first time, the envoy looked uncertain.

"At present, we cannot."

"Then let's record that as the conclusion for today." Adrian looked at Hale. "Please write down that they cannot confirm it."

Elena hadn't tried to speak for Adrian again.

He'd worried she might still be angry about his words earlier. Instead, she was listening and quietly writing down the numbers that needed to be checked.

At last Horn returned the document to its case.

"I'll report your refusal to accompany us."

Adrian shook his head.

"Please report what I actually said. I'll verify original records with counsel and an independent clerk present. I won't consent to an unauthorized transfer of my person or unexplained magical sampling."

Hale copied the words into their formal reply.

Elena didn't sign in Adrian's place.

She signed beside his name as a witness.

Horn examined the two signatures.

"I'll convey your position accurately."

He stood and bowed to the duke.

At the door, he paused.

"Mr. Reed, perhaps I shouldn't say this now. But I'd advise caution."

Adrian met his gaze.

"About what?"

"If that historical record truly belongs to you, then according to the Temple's archives, you have already died once."

The door closed.

Adrian didn't move.

The carriage rolled away outside. The tea had gone cold. Nothing in the room changed because of those words.

Elena approached him.

"I heard what you said earlier."

"Which part?"

"That you're not my property."

Adrian looked up.

"It came out harshly."

"A little," she admitted.

He felt worse.

"But I almost answered for you simply because I'm your employer," she continued. "I meant to protect you. You were right to stop me."

"I wasn't saying you're the same as the Temple."

"I know. It still hurt to hear."

Adrian stared at her.

Elena rarely admitted something had hurt her. Even when the prince broke their engagement in front of the entire court, she'd gripped her fan and chosen every word carefully.

Now she'd simply told him the truth.

"I should have asked how you wanted to handle it," he said.

"And I should have asked what you wanted."

She returned the pen they'd used to record the meeting.

"We can still look out for each other without deciding everything for the other person."

Adrian glanced at the pen.

"Then there's something I'd like to decide."

"Go on."

"Tomorrow, I want to investigate the old courthouse connected to R-074. Not let the Temple take me away. Not sneak into a restricted building alone. I want to apply for legal access—and invite you to come with me."

Elena's mouth softened into a smile.

"You said 'invite.' That's progress."

"Will you come?"

"Yes."

"Only for the investigation?"

She looked at him.

"Were you hoping for another reason?"

Adrian remembered the lights in the ballroom and decided this was a far harder question than anything in Hale's legal opinion.

"Just confirming our plans."

"Then don't be late."

Martha entered with fresh tea, noticed how close they stood, and began to wear that familiar expression.

Adrian interrupted before she could speak.

"There's no engagement, no kissing, and we're only discussing old judicial records!"

Martha stopped.

"...I was going to ask whether you'd like sugar."

Elena lowered her head and laughed.

Adrian reflected that he still had some work to do on knowing when to keep quiet.

That night, however, he didn't sleep.

Seven years ago, on September twentieth, someone had recorded a death under his name.

And until yesterday, he'd believed he'd only just survived the first date on which someone was supposed to die.
