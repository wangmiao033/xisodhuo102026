# Chapter 111: An Inconvenient Morning for a Summons

The process server arrived while Luna was arguing with an egg.

Holding a spoon left-handed, she was trying to crack an elegant circle around its shell. Half had yielded. The other half remained defiantly whole. Viola recommended an eggcup. Luna said that defeated the spirit of exploration.

“Didn't you decide to try mechanical solutions first?” Adrian asked.

She looked at him and put the egg in its cup.

A harness bell sounded outside.

There had been no further snow on the December 20 road, but frozen mud clung to the wheels. The man in a dark gray coat wiped his boots before presenting his East Ridge Tribunal credentials to the administrator. He had no armed squad to storm breakfast. He asked whether Luna Ashcroft and Adrian Reed were in residence.

Adrian set down his cup.

Luna's palm was still dressed. Hearing her full name, she gripped the spoon, then slowly released it.

The administrator took the visitor into the reception room rather than having him announce the matter to every breakfast guest. After Adrian agreed, Elena and Cecilia joined them. Viola first checked who would save the unfinished food. An official seal did not require facing it hungry.

There were two summonses, identifying each recipient and capacity.

Luna was called as research lead and signatory of an earlier technical report; Adrian as a witness to relevant observations. A preliminary review would take place the following afternoon at the Forbidden Arts Tribunal's local court in East Ridge. It concerned old contractual carriers, undeclared unusual coupling, and a custody dispute. The paper required their attendance and identified the capacity in which each would speak. It was a summons, not a finding that either had committed an offense. Adrian read the stated scope carefully: nothing on that page ordered him to submit to an immediate physical examination.

But there was an interim direction.

Equipment, original records, and damaged components associated with C-17 and D-17 must remain unchanged, with no independent disposal or transfer. The relevant laboratory was suspended from use. Court staff would seal the equipment room before noon with academy and tower representatives present. Living quarters, kitchen, ordinary passages, and medical care were outside the restriction.

“Apparently my left-handed fish don't qualify as forbidden magic,” Luna said.

The server looked up, established that she meant drawings, and indicated where to acknowledge receipt.

Adrian read the cited dates twice before signing. The application arose from the December 15 custody dispute and the subsequently supplied account of Luna's earlier employment. Issued by the tribunal on the nineteenth, it had arrived today. The eighteenth's accident report had only left the tower yesterday morning and might now be reaching the city. The court had not watched the return light across the mountains in real time.

“We should add the latest incident,” he told Luna.

“We will. Making things sound worse isn't a reason to wait until someone asks.”

She signed left-handed. The fish finally had a practical purpose. The server accepted her spoken confirmation with the administrator witnessing the effect of her injury. Nobody required her to remove a dressing for prettier handwriting.

Elena requested the procedural information sheet. Where to file objections, obtain copies, and submit a medical statement was printed on the back. She did not produce a Rosenberg crest and announce that the hearing was canceled. Nor did she hide Adrian in a ducal carriage.

After the server left, Luna laid the paper flat and stared at another phrase.

The renewed manifestation of an old vow's record requires consideration of its continued force.

That was the applicant's claim, not a conclusion the tribunal had adopted. Yet it quietly pushed a fragmentary date toward something more dangerous.

“An old vow,” Adrian repeated.

“They may be asking more than whether an accident occurred,” Cecilia said.

“Do you recognize that?” Luna asked.

“The passage it quotes. And the sentence they left out.”

She did not promise that one theological quotation would dismiss a case. She fetched her public ritual text and asked the administrator for the tower's catalog of older contract commentaries. A rebuttal must let others open the same page. Her status as saint could not substitute for checking her memory.

The sealing took time, but was quieter than Adrian feared.

The deputy inspector remaining at the tower accompanied two court officers to inventory C-17 again. The glass remained closed, the original support stayed in the cabinet, and seals went on the outside door without touching the gray-silver interface. The custody officer who had disregarded the earlier pause did not handle the object. His conduct remained under investigation, not already proven deliberate destruction.

The dissipation box containing D-17 was separately sealed as incident evidence. Nobody reopened it for a better image, or described the plate as intact and available for replication. Drawings, readings, and witness accounts retained their separate sources. Medical information was restricted to necessary review.

Luna stood outside with her left hand in her coat pocket.

Her usual place was empty. The chair remained. For someone accustomed to piling notes beside her cup, the seal looked very thin to exclude so much.

“I thought I'd swear,” she said.

“And now?” Adrian asked.

“I want my egg.”

“The kitchen kept your breakfast.”

She looked at him. “Did you predict this loss of dignity?”

“Viola predicted hunger.”

Back at the guesthouse, breakfast had become a small bowl of savory egg custard. The original egg was cold; the kitchen declined to let her test her teeth on it. After two mouthfuls, Luna's left shoulder relaxed.

Elena left tomorrow's travel arrangements at the table's corner. An ordinary carriage after dawn, time for icy roads and rest, arrival before noon, and lunch before court. Hensley advised against Luna traveling alone and would document her injury and restrictions. If symptoms worsened, they could request a real adjustment. Neither pretending she was uninjured nor inventing incapacity was acceptable.

“I'll speak for myself,” Luna said.

“You can. Finish eating.”

“Your orders are becoming difficult to dispute, Elena.”

“You've already tried all the easy ones.”

They smiled together.

That afternoon Cecilia opened the ritual text. No secret pages, only worn corners. She asked Adrian to read the sentence in the application, then what followed.

An old vow's record may call forth witnesses; it cannot replace the voice of those now present.

The commentary discussed documents surviving fires, promises involving missing persons, and recopied texts. It neither acknowledged time loops nor explained D-17. It did distinguish a surviving record, a former relationship, and consent enforceable now.

“They can't call the luminous part a miracle and the part requiring a person's answer unnecessary,” Cecilia said.

“Can that dismiss the application?” Viola asked, holding down the procedural sheet with her right hand.

“No. It challenges that inference. It doesn't excuse unsafe design or prove Luna's old report was sound.”

Luna nodded. She had signed S-48's premature conclusion about a coating flaw. Someone else's misuse of theology would not transfer that responsibility.

They prepared what each could actually attest to. Adrian described the gauges he saw, calls he heard, handles he used, and the subjective fragment lasting less than two breaths. Other people's theories did not become his observations. He added no faces he had never seen and asked no blurred date to support an entire life.

Elena stopped reading.

“What if they demand proof that you didn't see more?”

“I can only say I didn't.”

“They may dislike it.”

“I dislike not having an answer too.”

After looking at him, she left the statement unchanged.

While they packed, Viola folded a thick scarf beside the carriage cushions. Luna asked whether she feared witnesses freezing until they forgot their testimony. The knight pointed it out to Adrian.

“For your back on the rough road. You were still coughing on hills recently.”

“Much better now.”

“I know. I didn't bring a stretcher.”

Luna doubled over laughing, then straightened hastily to protect her hand. Viola gave her a look and produced two straps.

“For the medical case. Not you.”

“You explain before I have time to misunderstand.”

Helping secure the case, Adrian noticed biscuits underneath. Viola admitted they were hers and not an expense she would claim. Her ears still reddened.

“I didn't want arguments over who should get the last one.”

“Separate portions, then.”

She did so. Finding one extra, she put it in her own packet. Adrian was happier to see that than to hear another promise to protect everyone.

“Good.”

Her smile hid behind the scarf as she folded the bag. “I'm practicing too.”

The evening's difficulty was the night watch.

Viola wanted to remain outside the sealed room, worried that something might be moved while they were away. Authorized staff were already assigned, but she felt she might miss something in the guesthouse. Luna volunteered to join her: she at least knew which window leaked. Adrian thought he should take a look too.

Elena stopped all three at the sitting-room door.

“One injured left arm, one bandaged right hand, and a witness needed tomorrow. What are you defending? Three chairs?”

“Chairs deserve protection,” Luna offered.

“So does rest.” Cecilia held up the doctor's instructions.

They eventually went with the administrator to check the lock, exterior seals, and ordinary inspection route once, then left as agreed. Viola did not add herself to the night roster. Luna did not touch the cabinet under cover of checking a seal. Adrian did not extend a completed task endlessly because he still felt uneasy.

Back at the guesthouse, all three nevertheless sat on the same bench.

Luna on the left, Viola on the right, Adrian between them with tomorrow's document bag on his knees.

“Does this resemble escorting important evidence?” Luna asked.

“Evidence doesn't ask for water.”

Viola examined his cup and reached for the jug. Luna's left hand was already reaching toward it. They stopped in midair and looked at the man between them.

Adrian sighed and lifted his cup himself.

“I still have one dependable right hand.”

Elena took the jug. “Its most dependable work tonight is turning pages.”

Luna laughed and made room; Viola moved too. The bench no longer felt like a prison wagon setting off for war. Cecilia fetched another chair. Her ritual text stayed on her own lap instead of displacing the travelogue.

They read a little. Before a storm, the captain hung a flag upside down and was mocked by the whole crew. At least he finally remembered to call everyone in off the deck.

Luna's eyes were tired by the end. She did not insist on another revision of her testimony. She said goodnight first.

Adrian placed the document bag where he would find it in the morning. The summons lay above things they had genuinely seen, done, and not done.

Tomorrow they must take them somewhere nobody was obliged to believe them out of affection.

Cecilia closed her book with an ordinary paper marker at the omitted half-page.

“I'll read the rest as well,” she said.
