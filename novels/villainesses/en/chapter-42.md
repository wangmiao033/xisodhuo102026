# Why Do All the Villainesses Want to Marry Me?
## Volume Two: The Saint Won't Let Me Get Married
### Chapter 42: The Line Someone Crossed Out of a Prophecy

On October twenty-second, Adrian found a very long line outside the municipal medical subsidy office.

At the front, an elderly woman was arguing with the clerk behind the window.

"I was approved for two bottles of medicine last month. Why are you only giving me one?"

"Because the system says one, ma'am."

"Well, my original receipt says two!"

Adrian stopped walking.

Elena glanced at him.

"What is it?"

"If someone can turn two bottles of medicine into one by changing a ledger, maybe turning 'this patient still needs care' into 'this patient has recovered' doesn't require particularly powerful magic."

Hale followed his gaze.

"Then it's fortunate we're not searching for magic today. We're searching for draft histories."

Cecilia walked beside them in plain clothes, her investigator's badge absent.

The notice suspending her restricted database privileges was attached to Hale's formal request. Today, she was attending as an interested person in the R-074 matter and as someone preparing a disciplinary defense.

She wasn't invoking the authority she'd temporarily lost.

"Does it hurt?" Adrian asked her quietly.

"A little. People used to step aside when they saw my badge. Today I had to show an identity card just like everyone else." Cecilia watched the people waiting at the public counter. "But I realized that's what all of them have been doing for years."

Adrian couldn't think of anything useful to say.

When they reached the front, the clerk looked at Hale's application and Mira's limited written account.

"You're looking for subsidy settlement records relating to a Temple blessing?"

"Versions submitted on October sixteenth and seventeenth, involving the C-19 reference," Hale said. "We're requesting fields relevant to public expenditure and an independently verifiable revision history. The clinic retains private medical information."

The clerk read the authorization carefully.

"Only Revision Three was formally archived. Earlier versions might be in our old intake boxes. No guarantee we'll find them today."

Adrian wanted to demand how the Temple could force an attendant to sign a form while the municipality couldn't even say where its earlier drafts had gone.

He kept quiet.

The clerk hadn't designed the system.

Yelling at her wouldn't make a document appear.

"If the search fails," he asked, "can we have a receipt listing which locations you checked?"

"Yes. But you'll need to pay the reproduction fee first."

Adrian touched his pocket.

There went another small portion of his triple footman's wage.

Elena leaned closer.

"Official Rosenberg expenses."

"Oh, good. I was about to ask whether my historical betrothal came with a living allowance."

Cecilia looked up.

"An unenforceable betrothal cannot support an allowance claim."

Elena laughed first.

Even the clerk lowered her head, smiling.

Half an hour later, a records keeper named Orin led them into the old basement repository.

She was in her forties, with rolled-up sleeves, and was busy replacing damp cardboard backing on a series of boxed accounts.

"The reference you're after?" she said, examining the index card. "Temple blessing completion subsidy, suffix three. Yes, earlier drafts were received. But the municipality only retains the supporting material necessary to audit payment and compliance."

"And if the original oracle is protected?" Cecilia asked.

"Then it isn't ours to hold," Orin replied. "We keep summaries, submission dates, and the filing office. If somebody included a patient's entire confidential medical history, I'd send it straight back."

Hale looked reassured.

Orin placed the sealed box on the examination table and asked Hale to sign the viewing register. A second municipal employee witnessed the opening.

She didn't skip any steps because Elena's father was a duke.

Before unwrapping the blue document sleeve, Orin asked the witnesses to inspect its custody label.

"This was brought in under a three-part municipal seal on the sixteenth," she explained. "The box was later repacked because of moisture. Both transfers are recorded."

Hale checked the logs.

"All this detail so someone can explain what happened to the file later?"

"Because otherwise the next clerk would spend her life guessing what the previous one did. If all we wanted was to move boxes in circles, we wouldn't need an archive."

Adrian considered having that sentence painted over the Rosenberg records office.

He was beginning to find numbered receipts more exciting than breaking into a forbidden room.

Elena noticed his expression.

"What's so funny?"

"When we first went to the palace, I thought solving a conspiracy meant catching the villain and demanding an immediate confession."

"And now?"

"I'd ask him to produce his receipts first."

She laughed.

Inside the box were ordinary vouchers, three application covers in different colors, and a single page of handwritten corrections beneath a blue sleeve.

Revision One was marked October sixteenth.

Revision Two had been submitted later that evening.

Revision Three became the formal settlement claim on October seventeenth.

All three cited C-19.

But the budget categories changed.

"Revision One says 'Medical Monitoring Support,'" Adrian read. "Revision Two says 'Blessing Assistance.' Revision Three says 'Relief Following Fulfillment of Divine Prognosis.'"

He looked up.

"Someone turned funding a sick man's treatment into proof that a prophecy came true?"

"The financial description changed," Hale corrected. "We still have to establish why."

Orin placed the handwritten sheet under a transmitted-light board.

Different shades of ink crossed the text.

The earliest line read:

*"Patient requires continued observation. Maintain treatment and medicine supply in accordance with the attending physician's directions."*

A later hand had added:

*Blessing ceremony completed.*

Then a thin line had been drawn through much of the original sentence.

The surviving final text said:

*"Prognostic patient stable following the blessing. Associated care procedures completed."*

Adrian stared.

No divine voice had descended from the heavens to write this.

Someone had pressed a pen against paper.

Changed words.

Crossed out instructions that a patient still needed medical care.

"Can we identify the person who made the changes?" he asked.

Orin pointed to the corner.

"The earliest submission came through the Temple's relief coordination desk. The amendment field gives an office code, not a person's name. You'll need the Temple's authorization register to establish who used it."

Cecilia leaned closer.

She didn't immediately announce that the Temple had fabricated a prophecy.

She asked a narrower question.

"Why did an instruction to continue medical observation become part of a report about an oracle's fulfillment?"

Orin shook her head.

"I handle financial settlement. I can't answer theological questions."

"Then that's the question I need to answer," Cecilia said quietly.

Hale noticed another line in the approval notes.

*Confirmed by records officer. No further signature required from original clinic supervisor.*

"Which officer?" Elena asked.

"Only the Old Contract Records Office's department stamp. I check whether the requesting institution qualifies for a grant, not the names of everyone authorized to stamp a form there."

Adrian remembered the report Mira had refused to sign.

Apparently somebody had worked out how to bypass the person most likely to see that the patient wasn't recovering.

"But how can a medical subsidy be paid without a doctor's approval?" he asked.

Orin frowned.

"Because these are two different funds. Medical monitoring requires clinic confirmation. A religious blessing project is settled through the Temple's own acceptance process. If someone describes the outcome of medical care as a religious project's achievement, the problem is in the application. I can't simply rewrite the claim from my window."

Adrian considered that.

She was right.

An ordinary municipal cashier didn't necessarily know she'd been asked to pay for a misleading account of a sick person's treatment.

It was the separation between two legitimate processes that someone might have exploited.

There was another attachment in the box.

A material settlement form listed two required sources of completion verification.

One belonged to the Temple.

The other belonged to the institution receiving the aid.

"Why does Revision Three only carry a Temple seal?" Elena asked.

Orin checked the reverse.

"An exception was filed. Under the old rules, an institution that had closed or ceased operating could be replaced by a single Temple approval."

"But Mercy Clinic was operating," Adrian said.

"It was. Which means we need to know whether the exception applied to a particular program or whether someone used it improperly."

Hale marked the question for further verification.

Cecilia's voice grew softer.

"Someone may have changed the description of the patient's condition *and* removed the physician who would have objected to it."

"Possibly," Hale said. "We can't call it deliberate concealment before examining the authorization. But the question certainly needs an answer."

Adrian tightened his grip on his pen.

For once he didn't demand an immediate conclusion.

Waiting for evidence was not always the same thing as letting someone escape responsibility.

Sometimes it was the only way to ensure the right person had a chance to answer.

Footsteps approached the basement room.

A man wearing gray-gold shoulder insignia arrived with a secretary.

"I'm the liaison officer for Temple Internal Oversight. Reproductions involving restricted oracles must no longer be displayed. You cannot let private lawyers review them without headquarters permission."

Orin didn't hand him the box.

"These are municipal expenditure records. You're welcome to submit a formal sealing request. You cannot simply remove the originals."

"This interference is obstructing our internal inquiry."

Hale remained calm.

"What's your application reference?"

The man ignored the question.

"You're counsel for the Rosenberg family, aren't you?"

"Yes. And I hold a valid municipal viewing authorization. We have not been shown the protected original oracle."

Elena moved beside the records desk.

"If you're concerned about privacy, the municipality can keep custody and redact unnecessary personal information. But you cannot remove an audit trail merely because its revisions are inconvenient."

The officer turned toward Cecilia.

"Saintclair. Your investigative access was suspended."

"I haven't used it," she replied. "I'm an interested party witnessing a lawful municipal review of an accounting summary."

His jaw tightened.

"You're becoming less and less like a saint."

Cecilia met his gaze.

"If being a saint means helping people who are ill, I haven't abandoned that duty today."

Both municipal clerks stopped writing for a moment.

Orin spoke firmly.

"Sir, this isn't the room where you conduct your religious disciplinary hearing. Submit the paperwork if you want the documents sealed."

The officer left.

But his secretary paused long enough to say:

"You'd be wise to return every copy to the Temple by tonight."

Hale requested that the remark be included in the viewing log.

He didn't pursue the man into the corridor.

Before leaving, they obtained certified, appropriately redacted images of all three application covers and the annotated page.

The originals remained under municipal custody.

Orin added a receipt confirming that submissions containing different text had been received in sequence on October sixteenth and seventeenth.

On the steps outside, Cecilia stopped.

"I've filled out forms like those before."

Adrian looked at her.

"For clinics?"

"Winter supplies. Blankets. Sometimes fuel for heating." Her voice softened. "I always thought that if a grant was approved, the hospital would have another way to help the patients."

"And someone may have used that same system to claim a patient had recovered when he hadn't," Elena said.

"I know I can't answer for everyone who completes a form. I simply never imagined that a procedure designed to help could be turned into a way of concealing a failure."

Adrian bought Cecilia a cup of tea from a street stall.

"You once said the gods shouldn't sign someone's sacrifice agreement on their behalf. Then let's find out who changed these words and make that person explain them."

She accepted the cup.

"Thank you."

That evening, Mira reviewed her account of the Graybell assignment with independent legal advice.

She confirmed that she'd been asked to certify Revision Three as *blessing completed*.

She'd refused.

The timing fit the municipality's revision history. It still didn't prove who made the amendments or whether that person also restricted Mira's freedom.

Hale recorded the limitation.

The letter delivered after dinner was considerably less cautious.

Temple Internal Oversight wanted Cecilia to submit a written statement acknowledging that she had disclosed restricted prognoses because of an error in personal judgment.

A blank space for her signature had already been prepared.

Adrian read it.

"They've even written what you're supposed to admit."

Cecilia picked up her pen.

But didn't sign.

"Then tomorrow," she said, "I'll explain to them myself why it isn't true."
