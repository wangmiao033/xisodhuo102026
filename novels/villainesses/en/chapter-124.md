# Chapter 124: Genuine Paper Can Still Tell a Lie

“You think I invited you here to show you forged records?”

Isabella did not raise her voice. She merely straightened, and the small ease created by bread crumbs in the warming room vanished.

Elena laid her pencil on her own paper.

“I think you've shown us a dossier whose assembly has not yet been properly checked.”

“What is the difference?”

“The first statement accuses you. The second requires us to keep working.”

Hartwell's keys clicked softly in his palm. He had been about to ask them to lower their voices. Instead he looked at Adrian.

Adrian declined to choose a side for either woman.

“Let's find the rebinding record. If we can't, the person who sounds most convincing still doesn't get to decide when those pages arrived.”

The princess studied him, then nodded to the keeper.

No answer materialized in the cabinet. The rebinding register listed years and former shelf references, but the actual work order was elsewhere. Hartwell sent his assistant for it.

For the next half hour, the fourteen pages remained fourteen pages.

Isabella disliked waiting. Adrian could tell from her glances at the clock, although she did not send a servant into storage to hurry anyone. She picked up the approved catalog and reviewed her notes.

“I never said every page was written four years ago.”

“The catalog says fourteen original leaves,” Elena replied. “If I cite that tomorrow, my listeners will assume all fourteen belonged to the initial transfer. Would you correct me?”

Isabella's gaze rested on the line.

“Now I would.”

“Then this was worth checking.”

Elena did not press for a larger admission.

The assistant returned with two registers and an old work order protected between boards. Hartwell checked the changed shelf references before setting it on a separate support.

Two years ago, a leaking roof had forced the west wing's old collection to move. W-24 had originally consisted of twelve loose leaves and one separately stored object. For ease of reference, a military forwarding copy and a repair note, previously stored elsewhere, had been appended during rebinding.

The work order recorded the page count, old shelf locations, and a signature acknowledging return.

“So the additions were documented,” Isabella said.

“Yes,” Elena answered.

The princess waited for a qualification.

Instead Elena crossed out Unexplained additions and wrote Bound together two years later; verify sources separately.

Adrian knew she could not enjoy withdrawing a suspicion in front of the princess. She crossed it out cleanly anyway.

“You no longer consider it suspicious?” Isabella asked.

“I no longer consider the two extra pages unrecorded. There are other questions.”

Hartwell found the military copy's former storage entry, then the repair note's issue record. Both had existed before the rebinding. That did not make them documents completed on the same day four years earlier.

There was another line on the work order that had not entered the summary: Seven crates moved; two staff receiving.

“Only two?” Adrian asked.

“The other clerk was ill. The roof was leaking. They couldn't wait.”

“Why wasn't anyone else assigned?” Isabella asked.

“The request is behind the work order. Additional staff required approval. The water didn't.”

She read the paper twice. She could have chosen to find his answer impertinent. Instead she asked how many of the seven crates had required later inventory corrections.

Three. The others had no equivalent discrepancy and could not be dismissed because this file contained one.

Adrian glanced at the empty basin against the wall. Old water had left a white rim inside. In stories, conspirators hid records for someone brave to uncover. These papers had first needed to survive rain, sick leave, and too few hands.

Elena asked Hartwell to include the emergency inventory procedure in the next review. Pursuing only the person who wrote a bad summary might merely drive away the next clerk willing to report a mistake.

“I can request the staffing decisions from that year,” Isabella said. “I won't promise to clear anyone of responsibility.”

“We needn't decide anyone's guilt first, either.”

For the first time the two women were silent over the same document instead of studying each other. Adrian left the silence alone. It felt more useful than a formal declaration of cooperation.

The summary slip inside the cover, not counted among the text leaves, was later still. A cataloger had written it after rebinding, compressing several sources into one account.

“Who was the cataloger?” Isabella asked at once.

Hartwell named a former employee, then cautioned that the entry identified who had registered it. It did not establish who had reached the conclusion, or whether the cataloger had known it was wrong.

Slowly, the princess lowered her pencil.

Adrian copied the two passages they were permitted to cite side by side. One said Received for conservation. The repair note said only that an outer protective strip had been replaced. There was no performance check, signed restoration to service, or permission to reconnect it to historical equipment.

The summary had turned those facts into Overhaul completed; cleared for renewed use.

Not every word was invented.

But the step for which someone actually needed to take responsibility had disappeared inside a polished sentence.

“Could the clearance be somewhere else?” he asked.

“Yes,” Hartwell said. “It isn't in the material open to you today.”

“Then our conclusion is that we haven't found the supporting authorization,” Elena said. “Not that none ever existed.”

“You've supplied the Crown with a defense,” Isabella observed.

“I've supplied an answer to the next obvious question. You needn't thank me.”

“I wasn't about to.”

“Then we agree.”

Adrian tried to hide a smile behind his cup before remembering he had no cup in the archive. He examined his pencil point instead.

Hartwell coughed very quietly, preserving what remained of his dignity.

They continued until the afternoon light had moved across the window. Elena asked to compare an ordinary paper-purchase entry from the same batch. The mill's watermark matched. Old folds and broken fibers were consistent with lengthy storage, though she did not pretend they supplied an exact date.

“This supports the paper's age. It doesn't by itself date the writing, let alone guarantee the contents.”

Isabella finally asked a more direct question.

“What would you have done today if I really intended to deceive you?”

Elena considered her before answering.

“Preserved what we had seen. Stopped treating your explanation as evidence for the papers. Then decided whether to continue working with you.”

“Not taken Mr. Reed away immediately?”

“He would decide for himself.”

The princess turned to him.

“I'd ask you why,” Adrian said. “And after hearing your answer, I might still leave.”

“The two of you are remarkably good at ruining the mood.”

“We were discussing a paper mill. I don't think this room's mood was that fragile.”

She watched him for a moment, then gave a brief laugh. Not because he had flattered her, but because he had not treated every sign of her displeasure as an impending sentence.

She still had conditions of her own.

“I want a complete copy of the findings first. You cannot take away only the parts that favor you.”

“And we won't leave only the parts that favor you,” Elena said.

“Then the same standard applies.”

It was not a solemn alliance. Hartwell produced the copying register and had them identify the permitted passages page by page. Missing words were to remain missing. Isabella's personal attendant acquired neither the originals nor the right to certify a satisfactory conclusion in advance.

When the copying clerk wrote Records found unreliable on the clean report, Elena stopped him.

“Make that ‘The composite dossier's summary contains an unverified inference.’ The original receipt, later military copy, and repair note require separate evaluation. We cannot classify them all as forgeries.”

Isabella looked at her.

“That's considerably less useful to you than saying the Crown supplied false records.”

“I didn't come here to win a sentence.”

Adrian checked the clean copy and signed the field recording which pages he had seen. He was not authenticating the entire collection, or supplying the pledge of loyalty Isabella had failed to secure last night.

The princess recalled the brief she had been about to give her attendant.

Her pencil hovered over The old project has a documented basis for renewed use. After a long pause she struck it out and wrote Clearance documentation requires further examination.

Adrian did not congratulate her on finally doing the right thing. She knew that correction weakened the accomplishment she had hoped to present. She did not need him to disguise the cost as a prize.

Before they finished, Hartwell inspected the backs of the loose sheets. One early forwarding slip had a folded flap too fragile to flatten. He allowed them to look only at the exposed section.

Dark brown wax lay against the paper. The fine lettering around its rim disappeared into the fold, but part of a rose and a circular border remained visible.

Adrian thought he recognized the design. He did not immediately say Temple.

Neither did Elena.

Isabella looked between them. “Miss Saintclair?”

“She needs to see it, and we need the period's seal specimens and rules for its use,” Adrian said. “Otherwise I could mistake a receipt stamp for an order.”

Hartwell recorded the position and agreed to request the comparison book for tomorrow. The original went back onto its support. Nobody pressed the flap flat to save a little time.

The snow had stopped when they left the palace.

Only in the carriage did Elena remove her gloves. Her fingers were stiff from writing. Adrian gave her the hand warmer and waited for it to help before asking whether she was cold.

“Your questions were in the right order today,” she said.

“I'm improving.”

She leaned against the carriage wall and briefly closed her eyes. During the exchanges with Isabella, she had shown no sign of fatigue.

“Was I too eager to suspect her?”

“You found a discrepancy in the page count.”

“I also disliked her offer last night.”

He did not reassure her that those things were wholly unrelated. He thought of the line she had crossed out.

“So afterward you kept what could be supported and corrected what you'd assumed too quickly.”

She opened her eyes. “You noticed?”

“I did. It was beautiful.”

Elena looked down at her notes. “My handwriting?”

“Not just that.”

A wheel crossed a patch of packed snow, bumping her knee against his. They did not pretend the jolt meant anything. When the carriage steadied, they simply sat a little closer.

“When you compliment someone,” she said quietly, “you should allow time to prepare.”

“A day's written notice?”

“No need. I can practice.”

Dinner was not ready when they returned. Elena went to change. Adrian sorted the approved copies, marking the originals' storage locations. He did not turn his guess about the wax seal into evidence.

Then he found their travel book.

When Elena came back, he was sitting by the fire with the book open at their old marker, not a fifteenth document spread across his knees.

“You aren't chasing the next question?”

“The seal specimens will be available tomorrow. Tonight, the captain still hasn't found his way home.”

She sat down and placed the hand warmer between them.

“Read, then.”

Two lines in, she corrected his pronunciation of a port. Adrian glanced at her; she met the look without retreating.

“Did you consult the original?”

“It's in your hands.”

He laughed and read the sentence again. This time she did not interrupt. She rested her shoulder lightly against his and listened as a man with the wrong address knocked on another door.
