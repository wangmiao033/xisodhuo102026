# Chapter 242: Empty Places Counted Too

On the morning of April 9, Adrian crossed signature off a recruitment form.

He replaced it with willing to learn more.

Then he put the form away. Even an invitation to learn shouldn't greet people frightened by last night with rows of spaces waiting to be filled.

The academy had lent an ordinary side room for pilot preparation discussions until afternoon. Permission didn't cover installing apparatus or conducting human contracts. No power plates lay on the tables: only verified limited-simulation accounts, unresolved risks, and a blank diagram of different jobs.

Adrian intended to speak standing up. By his third sentence, he couldn't remember what he'd just said.

Elena pushed his water closer. “You promised not to let exhaustion answer for you.”

He conceded and asked the independent coordinator, already familiar with the material, to chair the opening while he sat and checked questions. This wasn't theft of his achievement. Nor did it give the coordinator authority over all research. Adrian retained what he could explain, rather than standing straight as evidence that he was alert.

Few people had come: a repairer with existing works duties, a literacy teacher, several interested adult residents, and staff responsible for ethical and safety intake. Invitations had clearly described a preparatory inquiry. Attendance registration couldn't become later agreement. Reception staff retained only necessary identity information, not a public list for the noticeboard.

The teacher asked first whether sharing meant everyone took an equal portion.

“We can't calculate it that way.”

Adrian divided the diagram's large circle into separate positions. Only a tiny nonliving model had produced limited results. Actual suitability, stopping methods, load limits, and what happened after withdrawal all required assessment and approval. Organizing a pilot meant assigning responsibility for those questions, not crossing them out with willing.

“Then what was the point of coming?” a resident asked.

He asked what troubled her most. If she couldn't continue later, would neighbors know she'd withdrawn? Would her children be mocked? He didn't classify the question as insufficient bravery. The coordinator recorded privacy and withdrawal-contact arrangements needing development. She could examine the eventual answer before deciding whether to hear more.

She didn't enroll. She allowed one means of contacting her personally.

The repairer asked about wages. He could offer limited technical advice outside his job, not take continuous leave. Daily equipment attendance would be work. Lost earnings shouldn't disappear inside volunteer.

The academy intake officer required jobs to be separated from contractual risk-bearing. Paid safety attendance wasn't consent to carry a load. Willingness to bear a specified risk wasn't an offer to perform every miscellaneous task for free. People could choose one role, or none.

Adrian redrew the diagram. It had fewer connecting lines and clearer questions. The repairer could point to the task he was actually considering without finding a second commitment concealed beneath it. Paid work, technical advice, and contractual exposure had looked tidy when gathered into one circle. Separating them made it possible for someone to give an intelligible answer.

Luna arrived for her agreed short session, right palm supported, her assistant carrying materials. On hearing equal shares described as safe, she offered the image of the smallest cup: a modest total could still overflow one place first. Then she qualified it. A comparison helped people ask; cups weren't measurements of bodies, and model readings couldn't simply become human capacity.

“If the researcher believes strongly enough, could she begin herself?” the teacher asked.

“Belief gives me no extra capacity. It doesn't make my injury occupy less of my body either.”

She wasn't mocking other people's hesitation. She wanted to continue and was learning to let wanting and being able stand apart.

Someone suggested using the five designated ladies as the center, with everyone else helping around them. Silence followed. The speaker said he meant no harm. It simply seemed quicker.

Isabella turned the diagram toward him with her right hand. Why must that central place belong to them?

“The old system already recognizes you.”

“That's the arrangement we're examining. It isn't consent we can conveniently borrow.”

She didn't order an apology. She asked Adrian to retain the proposal and the reason for rejecting it, so the next person wouldn't assume nobody had considered the shortcut. Five women weren't a set of components awaiting more considerate use.

Viola didn't attend the full discussion. Her checked written comments concerned practical entrances and requests for assistance. Nobody should be required to stop uncontrolled equipment with a body, or be deemed available all day because their role was guard. She would continue safety preparation within medical limits. Actual assignments came later; no knights were being sent today to block anyone's door.

Cecilia arrived after her ordinary service handover and listened from the back. A resident asked whether declining participation would affect food relief.

“No.” She answered promptly, then had the actual service supervisor confirm current eligibility rules. It must be more than her personal promise. Relief lists, eligibility, and research contacts remained separate. She hadn't regained her former leadership or acquired authority to answer for every relief office through good intentions.

What if someone did tie them together? The coordinator explained where to report it and which actual words to record. A recipient wouldn't be expected to argue alone with a manager. Cecilia offered defined times to help forward specific problems, without directing everyone's need to herself alone.

At lunch, Adrian stepped outside. Sunshine stung his eyes; the black river path seemed to linger behind them. He'd imagined organizing a discussion would create hope. Instead, he saw more unfilled positions on his diagram.

Elena brought him bread with jam. Must he prove he'd eaten half today too? he asked. No. She also wanted a little quiet.

They leaned beside a window without obstructing the path. Conversation continued inside. A child ran across the yard and his mother called out not to hit the princess's carriage. Isabella's assistant said her carriage was elsewhere today. This was an ordinary water cart; colliding with either would hurt.

Elena laughed. Adrian looked at her.

“Last night I wondered whether, without all these choices, I could just have an ordinary spring with you.”

“So did I. That's not irresponsible.”

“I'm afraid I'll turn that ordinary life into a reason to make you stay.”

“Then invite me. Don't decide for me.”

If their work was properly handed over tonight, would she have dinner with him without discussing the pilot? Yes. If something urgent intervened, they would send word rather than require guessing. It wasn't a promise of their whole lives after the crisis. Both took it seriously.

The afternoon grew harder. The safety officer said preparation couldn't list only ideal equipment. Who would stop a failure? Where would people go? Which validated method would handle the remaining load? Without approved answers, human operation couldn't begin.

Adrian remembered the old man who'd lost his beans. He badly wanted somebody to do more quickly when lamps failed again. Urgency couldn't put an honest name into an empty emergency position.

He explicitly withdrew the proposed signing time he'd intended to raise today. He had wanted the next dark night to find them better prepared, not facing the same helpless choices. But that wish was not an answer to the safety officer's questions. Leaving the time blank was more honest than attaching a date to arrangements nobody could yet explain. Independent explanation and individual consideration would be arranged after conditions were met. The coordinator called the result a pilot preparation proposal, not an operating pilot. No first set of formal risk contracts had been signed. Listening to this meeting hadn't made anyone a node.

The repairer relaxed. He could estimate the construction time for an ordinary support, but that was an estimate, not a promise to cancel existing orders. The teacher would read a future explanation for language ordinary readers might find difficult. She wouldn't persuade people to agree for the researchers. The resident who'd offered a contact method chose nothing further.

These small answers were more fragmented than Adrian's imagined common pledge. He had each person read back the specific commitment. Fragmented didn't mean nothing had begun. An estimate could be checked, a piece of wording could be improved, and a person who had offered nothing more could be left at exactly that answer.

Near the end, a young man announced he would do anything if his family could be spared disaster. He spoke quickly, as if the opportunity might go to someone else. The coordinator didn't accept it. Did he want a private explanation? Would he prefer to rest before talking? He nodded, eyes reddening.

Adrian accompanied him outside instead of recording the first volunteer. They talked about the lamps, the ordinary lights still working at home, and current protective arrangements he could ask about. Eventually the man admitted he didn't know what anything included. He would tell his family what he'd heard today. Whether he returned would be his answer.

After the door closed, Adrian struck through the unused recruitment form and filed it among drafts, where the next receptionist wouldn't mistake it for an approved form. Willing to learn more could be recorded only after someone understood the purpose. An anxious sentence couldn't be retroactively expanded into permission.

Luna had left on time and her assistant took her materials. Intake staff left the outstanding conditions. The side room returned to its usual class as arranged. Workers restored ordinary tables. No newly installed apparatus needed disconnection.

During the count, the receptionist asked how to record visitors who'd offered no further contact. The coordinator retained only necessary attendance numbers, not uncooperative or private remarks for later questioning. Adrian saw the blank and let it remain. A conversation without agreement didn't need to be preserved as a defect in somebody's character.

What they'd organized was a group of limited, verifiable preparation responsibilities. The distance between models and people remained substantial. Ordinary rescue couldn't wait for it to disappear.

At the receiving desk, Elena saw the riverside's temporary supply needs. Several noble households had unused vehicles and storage that might form more reliable aid under individual agreements. She hadn't obtained replies. She proposed inquiries tomorrow, noting workers' hours and ownership restrictions on her preparation page. It was a proposal to seek resources, not a functioning network already at their disposal.

Adrian didn't count her idea as another donation received. He asked whether dinner was still possible.

After checking the actual handover, she said it was.

They left the academy past the reset preparation notice. Nothing they'd done today made that paper weigh less. Adrian no longer wanted to cover it with ten names signed in haste.

His diagram still contained empty places. Some awaited technical work, some decisions. Some might never be filled by a particular person.

They were learning to leave those places in the real plan too.
