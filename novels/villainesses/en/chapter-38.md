# Why Do All the Villainesses Want to Marry Me?
## Volume Two: The Saint Won't Let Me Get Married
### Chapter 38: The Patient Matters More Than the Prophecy

On the morning of October eighteenth, Adrian found a carriage full of medical supplies waiting outside the Rosenberg estate.

It wasn't an elaborate attempt to get access to the old case files.

Elena had contacted a charity merchants' association that supported Mercy Clinic. They'd arranged to replenish sterilized cloth and fever-care supplies under the clinic's ordinary donation procedures.

No private guards were being sent to break into Temple warehouses.

The clinic had specified what it needed and how much it could safely accept.

"Does His Grace know we're donating half the household linen?" Adrian asked.

"Father approved the shipment," Elena replied. "He also said that if you try pretending to be a doctor, he'll dock half a month's wages."

"I have never wanted to pretend to be a doctor."

"Nor did you ever want to climb through a window."

She handed him a plain gray coat.

Cecilia appeared from the guest wing a moment later.

She still looked tired, but her expression had steadied overnight. She wore an ordinary volunteer's badge instead of ceremonial robes.

"I've asked permission to visit as a medical charity volunteer," she said. "Our first priority is confirming that patients are receiving treatment. Finding Mira is the next."

Adrian nodded.

"You're not here to pronounce whether somebody is fated to survive."

"I would never do that," Cecilia said. "I was taught that a blessing should comfort someone in pain, not replace a physician's judgment."

They reached the lower district shortly after dawn.

Mercy Clinic stood between narrow brick buildings, far from the marble halls of the Temple. Its sign was faded, and a line of patients stretched beneath the windows.

Freshly washed bandages dried on a line in the courtyard.

A handwritten notice listed the physicians' visiting hours.

Adrian had barely stepped down from the carriage when a child started crying nearby.

Her mother stood at the reception desk, clutching the little girl in her arms.

"We registered yesterday! Why can't you find her card?"

The receptionist looked overwhelmed.

"The revised list changed the numbers. I can't tell which medicine was already issued."

Cecilia approached the mother and asked whether she'd like help reaching the correct nurse. Only after the woman agreed did Cecilia ask the staff to check their own clinical register.

She didn't demand to see the child's private medical record.

Adrian watched the small scene unfold.

An incorrectly amended form didn't need to summon a monster to do harm.

It could leave a frightened mother holding a sick child in a queue for another half hour.

The clinic supervisor was Dr. Grace Winter, a short woman who spoke at extraordinary speed.

She read their authorizations and asked the most important question first.

"These supplies are an unconditional donation?"

"Yes," Elena replied. "No public thank-you notice for the duke's household required."

"Excellent. Writing those notices takes longer than sorting medicine."

Grace examined the shipment manifest and instructed her nurses to take the crates to the receiving room.

She stopped Adrian when he tried to carry a box directly into the ward.

"What do you think you're doing?"

"Helping?"

"First we check the seal, batch numbers, and quantities. I don't know what's inside until it's inspected."

Adrian put down the crate.

"Fair point. I'm getting used to it."

"Used to what?"

"Making sure my attempts to help don't make the situation worse."

For the first time, Grace gave him an approving look.

"Then you'll do just fine here."

She took them to a tiny office where old forms were stacked from floor to ceiling.

Grace knew Mira.

She'd even seen the red thread bracelets Mira liked making for children.

"She came here two days ago, didn't she?" Cecilia asked quickly.

"Yes. She was at the reception desk for a while. She checked the medicine delivery and asked why patient reference C-19 appeared in two places with different room assignments."

"Where did she go afterward?"

"Someone told her the missing correction register was being kept at the old relief station on Graybell Street. She said she wanted to verify who authorized the changes and went to find the relevant office."

"Did you actually see her leave?" Adrian asked.

"Through the front entrance, yes. Where she went afterward, I can't say."

Hale recorded the distinction.

Elena asked, "Did she seem frightened?"

"No. Angry, if anything. She said no one should be able to take one patient's name and put it on another person."

Cecilia lowered her eyes.

"That sounds like Mira."

In a narrow waiting room beside the stores, Adrian met Jonah Payne's mother.

Her hair was going gray. She held a patient registration card so tightly that it had developed four deep folds.

"Are you here from the Temple?" she asked suspiciously.

"No. I'm helping coordinate the donation. I'm also looking for a missing volunteer. You don't have to tell me anything about your son's illness."

The woman hesitated.

"Then can you tell me why someone said he wasn't supposed to collect medicine in the east ward anymore?"

Adrian couldn't.

He asked Grace to help.

The physician took the woman into a quiet consultation room, where a staff member could check Jonah's identity and treatment plan without making her explain the whole situation to strangers in the corridor.

"You don't have to prove who your son is to every person who walks through the door," Grace told her.

Some of the tension left the woman's face.

Adrian stood outside for a moment.

Until today, the wrong patient number had been a clue that might lead to Mira.

Now it was a bed, a course of care, and a mother waiting every hour for news.

A collision sounded in the supply room.

A nurse poked her head into the office.

"Dr. Winter, Jonah's family has been told he moved to the west observation ward. But the staff there say they don't know him."

"Jonah Payne?" Cecilia asked.

The nurse nodded.

Grace frowned.

"He was originally in the east ward. We still haven't properly reconciled the bed transfers after the sixteenth. I'll have a physician verify his last confirmed location immediately. No patient's care should be delayed because an administrative list changed."

She looked at Cecilia.

"If you'd like to help, the dispensary can use another pair of hands counting supplies. Please don't open any patient records you aren't authorized to see."

Cecilia nodded and rolled up her sleeves.

Adrian volunteered too.

A nurse handed him two bundles of clean sheets.

"Second shelf in the storeroom. Don't touch the medicine bottles."

"Understood."

As he carried the linen down the narrow hall, he saw Elena comparing the charity delivery quantities with a nurse.

No grand announcement that a duke's daughter had arrived.

No household guards blocking the doorway.

She stood there checking receipt numbers like any other volunteer.

Cecilia was at another table, folding clean cloth in careful squares.

Adrian realized that in a little clinic like this, both women seemed to know exactly what they wanted to do.

He put down the first bundle and returned for another.

A low spot in the floor caught his heel.

He almost slipped.

Elena caught his arm.

"No ballroom this time. No gown to blame."

"Will you ever stop mentioning that accident?"

"When you learn to look where you're going."

She helped him gather the sheets that had slipped from his arms.

She didn't release him quite as quickly as she needed to.

A nurse passing by smiled.

"You two make a lovely couple."

Adrian was preparing his usual denial.

Elena answered first.

"Thank you."

He stared at her.

"You didn't even correct her."

"I've told you I'm pursuing you. I don't need to pretend we're strangers every time somebody notices."

His face warmed.

"...Then I suppose I'll get back to work."

Cecilia, overhearing from the dispensary, smiled.

"It's good that you can catch one another. The floor really is slippery."

Adrian decided not to guess whether she'd meant that as a joke.

During the shipment inspection, Grace discovered that several crates had been covered with gray Temple paper.

"Not necessarily improper," she said. "Depots sometimes repackage medicines to keep moisture out."

Elena examined the packing list.

"But the new wrapper shouldn't hide the original batch references. The clinic needs to know exactly what it's receiving."

Grace had the nurses compare the seals with the merchants' association's original shipping records.

Only after the contents were confirmed did the supplies enter the regular storeroom.

Adrian was sweating by then.

"I thought we were coming to look for someone. I've spent half the morning checking boxes."

"If the supplies reach patients safely," Cecilia said, "the morning hasn't been wasted."

She wasn't trying to heal anyone with unauthorized magic.

She was folding cloth and following the nurses' instructions.

For some reason, Adrian found that more impressive than a hundred glowing spells.

In the old storeroom, Grace showed Cecilia a set of little suns drawn on the inside of a wooden door.

"Mira does that," she explained. "Children are afraid to come in because they think this is where we keep the bitter medicines. She says a sun makes the room less frightening."

Cecilia studied the crooked drawings.

"She never mentioned that to me."

"She has a life of her own here," Elena said gently. "Even someone she trusts doesn't have to know every little part of it."

Cecilia nodded.

"I hope she'll come back and tell me herself."

Adrian didn't promise that she would be safe.

They didn't know where she was.

He simply memorized the last drawing's location, planning to ask Mira why it resembled a slightly squashed fried egg when they found her.

By afternoon, the delivery had been properly inspected and accepted.

Grace thanked the group, but made her position clear.

"The medicines belong to the patients. Mira used to volunteer here, and I'll help find her. That doesn't mean the clinic has to support a noble household or the Temple in any investigation."

"Exactly as it should be," Elena replied.

They turned to the clinic's ordinary receipt records.

The depot manifest for October sixteenth said that all three medicine crates had been delivered.

The clinic's internal inventory showed one missing.

According to a later entry, that crate had been redirected to Graybell Relief Station.

"Graybell Street?" Adrian asked.

"An old temporary medical storehouse," Grace said. "Sometimes it takes overflow supplies. But we need those medicines here. I don't know why the crate would have been redirected first."

"Who signed the transfer?"

"The manifest has the Temple Administrative Duty Office's stamp. The nurse listed as recipient says she never signed it."

Hale arranged for the originals to be preserved without allowing either side to add new signatures.

At the same time, a nurse brought out an anonymized ward diagram approved for limited review.

The C-19 entry had moved from the east ward to the west observation room.

There was no corresponding physician-approved transfer order.

"Could still be an input error," Grace cautioned.

"Then we look for the original," Adrian said.

He wouldn't announce that someone had been kidnapped simply because a bed assignment was wrong.

Nor could he yet say whether Mira had been forced to travel to Graybell Street or gone there of her own accord while checking the documents.

Near dusk, a young man with a walking stick arrived at reception.

He sounded utterly exasperated.

"Why does everyone keep calling me Jonah Payne?"

Grace turned.

"What's your name?"

"Elliot Morris. I registered in the west observation ward yesterday. But a nurse keeps calling me Payne, because that's what the Temple's corrected card says."

Adrian met Cecilia's eyes.

One card.

Two names.

Grace guided Elliot to a chair and stopped bystanders from questioning him.

"First we'll confirm your identity and make sure you have the care you need. We'll handle the paperwork."

Once the consultation door closed, Adrian looked back at the contradictory registers.

Tonight's task was no longer figuring out which person a prophecy claimed would die.

It was finding out why two living people had been recorded as though they were the same patient.

Rain darkened the bricks beyond the window.

Grace's assistant brought over the duty notice for the Graybell Relief Station.

The temporary manager's name had been covered by a gray-gold administrative seal.

Only an office abbreviation remained.

It was the same format used on the Temple notice restricting discussion of the dual-date record.

Adrian handed it to Hale.

"Tomorrow we'll need to inspect Graybell. But first let's establish whether they're caring for someone who actually needs medical attention."

Cecilia nodded.

"Mira wouldn't abandon a patient just to chase a sheet of paper."

Elena closed the document case.

"Then we investigate both problems. Make sure the patient is being treated—and find out who changed those records."
