# Chapter 232: Permission Wasn't a Promise

The first thing removed from the examination room on March 31 was Isabella's high-backed chair.

Not as punishment. Sitting in it, she discovered that the two civilian applicants beside her had to look up at her. She requested a chair of the same height as theirs, then stood aside for the attendant instead of steadying it with her injured left wrist. Adrian moved his papers inward so the chair legs wouldn't catch their string.

An academy reviewer uninvolved in designing the model presided. A Temple representative could explain borrowing conditions; an independent safety member and custodians also attended. Isabella neither chaired the meeting nor had chosen its chair.

Oswin wasn't there. His authorized representative brought an explanation of the suspension, not the High Priest's entire authority installed in an empty seat. They could answer limited questions. Other matters required referral.

Adrian had prepared to explain why they must continue. The chair asked first what had happened on March 27, without evaluating what failure meant for their future.

Using authorized copies of the retained record, he described independent power, the two inert substitutes, his proposed interval, the local color change, and the safety officer's stop. He didn't call an effective cutoff a successful simulation. No injuries didn't mean no problem.

“Who proposed the interval?”

“I did.”

“On what basis?”

“The paper model and preparation estimates. It was labeled an assumption, not verified on this actual transfer path.”

Luna wasn't present for a whole day's questions. She had checked her own technical statement, distinguished what she confirmed, and retained uncertainties requiring measurement. Adrian could explain his proposal. Her absence didn't authorize him to accept every explanation for her.

The independent recorder laid out the timeline. The works witness described the window and actions he'd actually seen, without becoming the model's advocate. The person who had agreed only to inspect records submitted a record-checking opinion, not newfound eyewitness testimony.

The main gauge staying below its line and a local stop indication could both be true. The safety member required both in the revised risk account. Removing either would make the story tidier, not more accurate. The relationship between local heating and transfer delay still needed testing; material factors hadn't all been excluded.

The Temple representative observed that nonliving equipment could still endanger people nearby. Shielding, limited supply, and professional operation required renewed assessment. Failure had occurred in an approved model. Approved couldn't end the questions.

Adrian disliked how reasonable that sounded. He wrote the requirements down anyway.

The discussion tightened at document access. The representative believed copying of authority records might have exceeded the original purpose. Since safety review referred to research material, they argued, the suspension shouldn't yet be separated.

Isabella didn't accuse them of obstructing the world's rescue. She asked the custodian to separate the actual inventories: borrowed protocol and authority originals, independently produced trial records, and academy-owned model diagrams. A dispute over the first category didn't turn the others into Temple property through the phrase research material.

Would she guarantee that every copy was compliant?

“Not what hasn't been checked. Disputed copies can remain under custody requirements. Please decide whether authorized safety reviewers may use the independent records not in dispute.”

It wasn't the door she might once have imagined throwing open. It was enough room to pass one page through the correct place.

After checking copy numbers and sources, the custodian confirmed several independent records. The chair admitted them for today's safety examination. That didn't authorize taking the restricted authority volume home. The old orders and Evelyn's specific responsibility remained with their separate proceedings, not closed incidentally by a research decision.

A civilian applicant asked whether citation of his testimony meant he'd agreed to attend every future experiment. No, the chair answered: this limited authorization only. Future participation required asking again. An independent registrar retained necessary identity information. The public record used a verification code, not an address lacking permission for publication.

Adrian closed the supporting-name list he'd prepared. Making it longer wasn't today's task.

Late in the morning, the safety member declined immediate permission to try again. Each node needed separate local observation with suitable measurement ranges. Adrian's spoken timing couldn't be the sole handover criterion. Independent cutoff required the specified preliminary checks after complete new assembly. Failure still wouldn't permit an improvised retry.

Staff brought a blank node diagram. If Node B didn't receive its expected confirmation, who would notice and who would stop? Adrian pointed toward his timing position, hesitated, then moved his finger to independent observation.

“Don't erase the first arrangement because you know the answer now.”

On separate sheets, he drew the old arrangement and the proposed change. The old routine readings followed the same spoken cue. The safety officer had always been allowed to stop independently on seeing an anomaly, and had done so in the first trial. Shared sampling times could nevertheless miss a local change between readings. The revision separated continuous local watching from spoken timing, without waiting for his sentence to obtain a reading or call a stop. Authorized personnel still performed the actual operation. It wasn't permission for every spectator to touch equipment.

What if an observer was mistaken? A safety stop might leave a trial without a result, the member said. That cost should be explained beforehand. Avoiding embarrassment wasn't a reason to require certainty of danger before calling a stop. A concern could prompt stopping and professional examination; nobody should need to guess correctly to escape blame.

The works witness asked whether he now had to fill that position. He'd come to explain a record, not volunteer again.

“No,” Adrian said immediately. He wrote personnel to be separately arranged beside the new diagram. Someone willing to speak today wasn't an available name to insert wherever he needed one. The man settled back and submitted his comments. The reviewer retained both drawings as discussion attachments, without stamping the new one an approved assembly.

The conditions wouldn't guarantee success. They might make another failure easier to recognize in time.

“Could extra local observation only let us see it break sooner?” Adrian asked.

“Yes. That's also something you need to learn. An instrument isn't a prayer that prevents bad things.”

Adrian crossed reassuringly secure out of his notes.

At the lunch recess, Viola didn't remain for the whole day. Her left arm needed rest and the witness arrangements had been handed over. Before leaving, she pointed with her right hand at his untouched bread.

“Another research project waiting for permission?”

“I have authority over this one.”

“Then act.”

He took a bite. Elena waited by the window with a lender's written statement, not unlimited assistance. The first materials had been consumed; damage assessment remained unresolved. They couldn't claim the same support twice.

The lender would discuss extending the shield loan, provided its current condition was checked and a return deadline agreed. Yesterday's temporary retention wasn't automatically a new lending period. A complete second set of trial materials still had no confirmed source.

“I haven't promised for them,” she said.

“I know.”

She looked at him until he added, “I'm a little disappointed. Not in you.”

“That answer I'll take.”

They sat together and divided half the bread across the table. Did he still want to walk tonight? Yes; if the examination ran late, he would send word rather than leave her waiting on the porch. She smiled. That sounded more like an arrangement than when everything is over, I'll be with you.

During the afternoon, the chair read out the parts of the hold that could change. Work on the academy's independent, newly made inert apparatus could resume as unpowered model revision and qualified measurement. The disputed scope of Temple authority originals remained held. Verified independent trial records could support safety review within the checked purpose, not unrestricted publication.

Then came conditional permission for a second simulation. They could apply for one short slot no earlier than April 1: one independent, low-energy A-to-B handover, with no human, core, ancient-object, or active-protection connection. Suitable materials and local measurement arrangements must be submitted, independent cutoff checks completed after assembly, and the shield's condition and actual borrowing permission confirmed. An unmet condition meant the slot couldn't be used.

During the readback, a clerk entered April 1 in the operating-date field. Adrian asked him to stop. That wasn't a confirmed booking. It was the earliest day for which they could apply; materials were missing and the actual shield loan still required agreement.

An empty field might cause the request to be overlooked, the clerk explained. They added a pending-conditions column instead. The date remained the earliest application date, not an arrangement already secured. Each condition belonged to the person qualified to confirm it. Adrian would submit his own materials; he wouldn't sign a completed preliminary check for the operator.

Returning to his seat with the copy, he noticed wet ink on his fingers. Isabella passed him blotting paper before he planted the stain on the permission. He wiped his hand carefully. For once, a document that wasn't filled to every edge could still tell someone what to do next.

It wasn't certainty of running tomorrow. It wasn't a second success.

Adrian circled no earlier than and may apply. Once, he might have circled only the date.

The Temple representative confirmed the scope they were authorized to confirm and referred the remainder onward. Appropriate academy personnel issued its safety decision. Isabella didn't add herself as final approver. The two records retained separate boundaries rather than letting one impressive signature swallow the other.

Before closing, the chair asked for corrections. Adrian said he'd earlier called the damage a fragment. It also included the scorched pad and clamp discrepancy awaiting further assessment. Amounts were unknown; accountability remained unfinished. Today's permission didn't cancel either.

The clerk corrected and read it back. Adrian didn't rush to attach but at least we finally to the sentence.

That evening they took the result to Luna. Her right palm still rested on a cushion; her left hand rested too. At her dictation, the assistant reserved local measurement positions at both nodes on a new drawing, separating handover judgment from independent stopping. Permission to research didn't make lines on paper assembled equipment.

When she asked about materials, Adrian said honestly that they weren't confirmed. She glanced at the travel book. Did that mean he could read a page tonight without requiring her to design the entire future immediately?

“Half a page. I need to ask whether suitable instruments are available tomorrow. Not borrow something that merely looks similar.”

“You've started telling me conditions.”

“I always did. I often forgot them after saying them.”

She laughed without offering her injury as security for tomorrow. After the reading, she asked the assistant to leave the materials item blank rather than expected available. Whoever looked tomorrow morning should see what was missing.

Corridor lights came on as Adrian left. They hadn't entered the core, extracted a pledge of sacrifice, or established that the old institutions no longer faced real dangers.

Holding limited permission, he stopped at the equipment room's ordinary wooden door. The attendant was sorting keys inside. Adrian waited until the man finished, then asked whether they could examine appropriately ranged local instruments tomorrow, and where a formal request should go.

This time, he wasn't asking who could open every door for them.
