# Why Do All the Villainesses Want to Marry Me?
## Volume One: A Betrothal Written in Blood
### Chapter 5: The Trap Behind the Broken Engagement

Prince Leon raised his glass before the third dance had finished.

The musicians lowered their instruments. Conversations faded beneath the chandeliers, leaving only the faint sound of the fountain beyond the windows.

Adrian stood behind Elena, counting guards.

Eight at the doors.

Four by the stairs.

Two beside the evidence table.

Fourteen in total.

There had been six in this scene in the game.

Even if he counted both his legs as useful assets, his chances of escaping weren't looking good.

"What are you counting?" Elena asked quietly.

"Escape routes. In case this turns into a fight."

"I thought you promised not to run."

"I promised not to leave you behind. I never said we couldn't run together."

She almost smiled.

"The third door on the right leads to the gardens."

"Wait. You're checking escape routes too?"

"I'm telling you they've stationed two extra guards there tonight."

Adrian glanced across the room.

She was right.

"...So, the front entrance?"

"If we can reach it."

Her voice was steady, but he noticed how tightly she held her closed fan.

On the raised platform, Leon began to speak.

"Thank you for joining us at tonight's Autumn Ball. As many of you know, this evening was also meant to celebrate my engagement to Lady Elena Rosenberg."

Murmurs spread through the crowd.

Near Adrian, one noblewoman whispered to another, "His Highness must be furious about what happened on the dance floor."

"What happened?"

"Didn't you see? Elena kissed that servant right in front of him!"

Adrian edged an inch farther behind Elena.

At this rate, he was going to die either of treason or of gossip.

Leon let the silence settle.

"After careful consideration, however, I have decided to end our engagement."

Shock rippled across the ballroom.

Elena didn't lower her head.

She didn't plead with him.

She took one step forward.

"Your Highness, have you discussed this decision with my father?"

"The royal family has the right to determine whom its princes marry."

"The dissolution of our engagement still requires a formal response from House Rosenberg. You're free to announce your intention. You cannot sign on our behalf."

Adrian applauded her in the privacy of his own mind.

In the game, Elena had drawn a sword the moment the prince broke the engagement. He'd then used her outburst as proof of her arrogance and dangerous temperament.

The Elena standing in this ballroom had no intention of giving him that satisfaction.

A flicker of surprise crossed Leon's face.

"I'd hoped to spare you further embarrassment."

"Then perhaps the proper course would have been to send a letter to my father's study, rather than announce it in the middle of a ball."

Someone at the back failed to suppress a laugh.

Leon set down his glass.

"Regrettably, this is no longer merely a matter of marriage."

Gray signaled toward the evidence table.

Two guards brought forward a silver-rimmed tray. On it lay a folded letter and the deep-blue jewelry case Adrian had seen at the side entrance.

Adrian's stomach tightened.

So they were using that box after all.

"Elena Rosenberg," Leon said, "the Crown has received intelligence that someone disclosed the eastern gate's patrol routes to the northern rebels. This letter implicates your family."

Gray unfolded the document.

"On the fifteenth of last month, Lady Rosenberg met a northern agent outside the capital. Soon afterward, sensitive defensive information was compromised."

"On the fifteenth," Elena said, "I was sitting my magical examinations at Saint Rose Academy."

"The academy's written confirmation has not yet been verified," Gray replied.

"Then where is the professor who supervised me?"

"Unable to attend."

Adrian remembered the guest list.

The professor hadn't simply failed to show up.

Someone had taken considerable trouble to make sure he wouldn't be here.

He glanced toward Leon.

The prince was waiting.

Waiting for Elena to lose her temper, just as she had in the original route.

Instead, she asked, "Since you have brought physical evidence, may I ask who has been responsible for its custody?"

Gray paused.

"The Royal Judicial Office, naturally."

"Then please present its registration record."

Leon looked past her at Adrian.

"Did your servant teach you to say that?"

"I can read, Your Highness."

Even a nearby waiter had to lower his head to conceal a smile.

Leon's expression cooled.

"Gray. Bring the ledger."

Adrian kept his eyes on the table.

He and Elena had only glimpsed the date during the dance. It could have been a clerical error. They might even have misread the column.

But once the record was examined in front of witnesses, they would have something worth discussing.

Edwin Clay took the ledger and turned to a page marked at the corner.

"Evidence Item 714," he read. "Sapphire necklace and accompanying jewelry case. Date sealed: September eighteenth."

Adrian stepped forward.

"Mr. Clay, may I ask what today's date is?"

The clerk looked up.

"What?"

"Today's date."

"September seventeenth."

"Then am I to understand that the evidence won't be sealed until tomorrow?"

The whispers started almost at once.

Clay's face changed.

"It's an obvious transcription error."

"That's possible." Adrian nodded. "In that case, could we compare it with the original receipt and transfer records?"

"Who are you to interfere in the work of the Judicial Office?"

"An attendant of House Rosenberg."

"Then get back where you belong!"

Adrian didn't move.

His palms were slick with sweat.

Leon was watching. Gray was watching. Elena was waiting to hear what he said next.

He remembered the duke's warning.

A suspicion wasn't evidence.

"I'm not accusing anyone of forgery," Adrian said carefully. "If this is an innocent mistake, the original entry, the person who received the box, and the palace gate records should clear it up. I'm only asking that they be checked."

Clay opened his mouth.

"This is a ball, not a courtroom!"

"Then," Elena said, "why are we hearing a public accusation of treason against my family?"

She stepped beside Adrian.

"I request that the original ledger be preserved. The guard who registered the item, the people who transported it, and the master of ceremonies can all confirm when it was entered. They were here before the ball began."

Gray's gaze flickered toward the young guard at the side entrance.

Adrian felt a sudden chill.

He didn't want the poor man punished for doing his job.

"I'm not asking anyone to decide guilt tonight," he added quickly. "I'm asking that records which can still be checked are not allowed to disappear."

The room went quiet.

It was such a reasonable request that refusing it outright would have looked worse than granting it.

An elderly nobleman spoke up.

"If there's a discrepancy in the evidence, surely it ought to be reviewed. We have no shortage of witnesses tonight. Why the rush to judgment?"

Another noble nodded.

Leon raised a hand, restoring silence.

"Of course the evidence can be reviewed."

His smile returned.

"But a mistake in one date does not remove suspicion from House Rosenberg."

"I agree," Elena said. "And an unverified letter does not make me a traitor merely because you choose to read it aloud."

Adrian felt some of the tension leave his shoulders.

They didn't need to win a trial tonight.

They only needed to stop the prince from having Elena arrested according to the game's original sequence.

Gray murmured something to Leon. The prince listened, tapping a finger against his wineglass.

Adrian's eyes drifted back to the evidence table.

The blue case was sitting under the lamps.

At this angle, he could see its lower-right corner clearly.

Smooth silver trim.

No scratch.

Not the box Martha had sealed at the Rosenberg estate.

Still, he kept that observation to himself.

Without the original box present for comparison, the prince could simply claim it was a different case. Adrian's memory alone wouldn't prove a thing.

He whispered that to Elena.

She gave a small nod.

"Save it for the hearing."

"You're not angry that I can't prove it now?"

"I'd be angrier if you made up evidence to impress me."

Adrian didn't know what to say.

Then she lifted her chin slightly.

"And you handled yourself well."

"Really?"

"Apart from the part where you nearly shouted 'What date is it?' as though casting a spell."

"I was nervous."

"I noticed."

Her voice softened a little.

"Stop shaking your hands. People can see."

Adrian looked down.

He'd been gripping the hem of his coat so tightly the fabric had creased.

Leon spoke again.

"Elena, I'm still willing to offer you one final chance."

She turned toward him.

"I'm listening, Your Highness."

"Leave the capital tonight. I can have the Judicial Office treat this as a misunderstanding. Once the investigation settles, you'll be welcome to return to your father's estate."

Adrian's breath caught.

That hadn't happened in the game.

In the original route, Leon had only escalated his accusations until Elena snapped. He had never offered her a way out.

And yet this sounded almost merciful.

If Elena accepted, tomorrow Leon could announce that a woman facing a treason accusation had fled the capital overnight.

The most dangerous trap in the room wasn't the forged letter.

It was the choice she was being offered.

Elena regarded the prince for a long moment.

"Your Highness, are you advising me to leave the city in the middle of an accusation of treason?"

"I'm trying to protect you."

"Then may I have your permission in writing? A document stating that I left by royal authorization, rather than to escape prosecution?"

Leon's expression darkened.

"Must you insist on turning everything into a technicality?"

"I'm simply trying to understand whether you're protecting me or arranging for me to appear guilty."

For several seconds, neither spoke.

Gray leaned toward the prince, but Leon stopped him with one hand.

"If you insist on making this difficult, the Judicial Office will proceed with a formal investigation."

"That is my right."

"You may regret it."

"At least I'll know what I'm regretting."

Adrian watched her stand there.

She was frightened.

He knew because, when she closed her fan, her fingers failed to catch the rib properly. She had to try again before it folded.

But she didn't run.

The forged letter was still on the tray. The accusation hadn't vanished. Nothing about the danger had become less real.

This time, though, Elena hadn't handed her enemies the excuse they needed.

Leon turned to Gray.

"Preserve the evidence and the registration ledger for review by the Judicial Office. There will be no detention tonight. Schedule an emergency hearing for September nineteenth."

Gray frowned.

"Your Highness, isn't that rather soon?"

"Lady Rosenberg seems eager to clear her name."

Elena curtsied.

"You have my thanks, Your Highness."

Adrian heard the edge beneath the courtesy.

September nineteenth.

One day before the execution date recorded inside the jewelry case.

The nobles began talking again. It was as though they'd just witnessed a disagreement over the quality of the wine, rather than an accusation that could destroy a ducal house.

Some of them were already looking at Elena differently.

Others took care not to stand too close to the Rosenberg crest.

Adrian followed her toward the doors, an unpleasant weight settling in his chest.

"My lady."

"Yes?"

"You're angry, aren't you?"

"Of course."

"Then why haven't you shouted at anyone?"

"I'm deciding who's worth the effort."

"The prince?"

"And the clerk who managed to record tomorrow's date today."

"And me?"

She stopped and turned.

"You?"

"I dragged you onto the dance floor. Caused that whole scene. I made things worse for you."

Her gaze fell to his collar.

"You've caused more than one kind of trouble tonight."

"I can apologize."

"You've done plenty of that."

"Then what should I do?"

"When we get home, write down the exact date, item number, and sequence of transfers you observed. Don't leave anything out."

She resumed walking.

"As for the dance, we'll discuss that tomorrow."

Adrian could already imagine tomorrow's newspaper headlines.

He hurried to keep up.

"You're not seriously going to make me explain what happened to the entire capital, are you?"

"That depends."

"May I request a written guarantee?"

Elena glanced at him.

"Guaranteeing what?"

"That I won't be sentenced to death because my explanation isn't convincing."

At last, she laughed.

"You really are..."

She didn't finish.

Their carriage was waiting outside the palace.

Martha approached from the opposite side, visibly relieved to find them both still capable of walking under their own power.

"My lady, His Grace has sent someone to ask what happened."

"We'll tell him at home."

Adrian helped Elena into the carriage. He was about to climb in after her when a messenger in Judicial Office livery blocked his path.

"Mr. Reed?"

"Yes?"

The man held out two folded documents, each stamped with red sealing wax.

"Summonses for the emergency hearing. Please acknowledge receipt."

Adrian looked at the first.

*Elena Rosenberg.*

The second bore his own name.

He'd thought he was an unfortunate extra who had wandered into the story.

Apparently, the Judicial Office had decided to give him a speaking role.

"Why am I being summoned?"

"Because you raised an objection concerning the custody of royal evidence. Clerk Clay has requested your appearance to explain the source of your information."

Adrian looked up.

Elena leaned out of the carriage and took the summons from him.

She read it.

Then folded it and placed it in her document case.

"Please inform Mr. Clay that Mr. Reed will attend."

"My lady?" Adrian said.

"And that House Rosenberg's lawyer will attend with him."

The messenger's expression changed.

He bowed and withdrew.

When the carriage started moving again, Adrian finally let out the breath he'd been holding.

"That was a dangerous thing to do."

Elena raised an eyebrow.

"A servant corrects the Judicial Office in front of the royal court, then tells me *I'm* reckless?"

"I've always been terrified of dying."

"Yes. That's become clear."

"Then why are you helping me?"

She didn't answer immediately.

Streetlamps passed the carriage window, their light moving across her face.

At last, Elena said, "Because you were obviously terrified, and you stood up anyway."

Adrian had no clever reply.

She didn't seem to need one. She lowered her gaze and began sorting the documents in her lap.

The carriage turned a corner.

Adrian looked again at the hearing date on his summons.

September nineteenth.

In the game, Elena Rosenberg died at noon on September twentieth.

They had bought themselves less than two days.

But from this point onward, Elena wasn't the only person the prince intended to put before the court.
