# Chapter 167: Names Outside the Seal

Luna slipped the tea time among the contact arrangements, on exactly the same paper.

Adrian saw three o'clock, then please come alone. Looking up, he found her right palm on its cushion while her left hand innocently moved another page aside.

“A new technical explanation?”

“We could discuss your recent prejudice against desserts.”

He put the note down.

“You wanted me to think I had to come.”

Her smile faltered. The misunderstanding was small enough to laugh away. Precisely why she nearly did.

“Yes. I wanted it to be harder to refuse than an ordinary invitation.”

“We separated those things yesterday.”

“Which means attractive wording won't excuse it today. I'm sorry.”

She removed the note, returning the actual business papers. His frown was not failure to appreciate flirtation.

“I want tea with you. No work. Would you like that?”

He had to visit the counter that afternoon and couldn't promise three. She could ask when he returned, if she still wanted, but he couldn't guarantee being back before her rest.

Luna nodded, hot-faced. She had intended to make him blush. Instead she folded come alone into a private drawer.

“A paper saying tea can be difficult to write.”

“Better than participant understands.”

She glared, then laughed. After he left, amusement faded. An apology hadn't erased the trick. At least she could refrain from sending someone to press the same invitation upon him.

On the afternoon of January 30, Adrian reached the registration office to find ordinary rope across its entrance.

No magical barrier, no sudden catastrophe. Two duty guards stood behind it. Wind flapped the lower corner of a half-attached notice.

A man carrying a wooden box asked whether registration had stopped forever. Told to read the notice, he complained that being unable to read it was why he had asked.

Adrian waited for the final tack rather than forcing through the crowd.

Public entry to the registration archive building was temporarily suspended, as was external transfer of the specified records. An inventory had revealed a missing appendix to a transfer list and discrepancies between wrappers' knots and seals and the descriptions on the inventory sheet. Differences were established, not substitution, still less an identified offender.

The preservation authority's decision carried its responsible official and reference. The interim measure would be reviewed on the morning of February 2, followed by notice of continuation, alteration, or release. A temporary counter operated in the annex across the street. Closure did not withdraw existing applications. Urgent questions could be lodged there.

Adrian copied the date and his collection reference.

Yesterday's seven-day measure had expired. This rope did not revive it. Nor did it prohibit people from meeting, leaving the district, or going home. It closed a building and affected transfers; it did not imprison applicants inside their own names.

The box-carrying man pointed at his shoes. Was standing here a violation? A guard finally explained that people could pass outside the rope, keeping entrances and handling areas clear. The man complained that saying that first would have saved the argument.

A female employee emerged with a lunch bundle. Someone demanded why she could carry things out if applicants were excluded.

She went pale. Under the established exit arrangement, a guard directed her to show the ordinary food container to the responsible recorder, who checked it before she left. Spectators were not permitted to search her pockets.

Half a piece of bread remained inside. She might only just have learned that her afternoon's ordinary work was canceled too.

Adrian did not add her name to a suspect list.

The temporary counter was so low its clerk had to angle his knees sideways. Every question about whether everything had stopped required another reading of the notice. When Adrian's turn came, he gave his collection number rather than expecting recognition as a figure in the rumors.

The clerk searched, then said the relevant copy could not be retrieved as arranged.

“I need a statement that collection was unavailable today because of preservation, not because I failed to attend.”

“It's the same for everyone.”

“Then everyone should be able to get one.”

The young man looked exhausted. Adrian kept his voice level, supplied his arrival time, and asked whether an annotation to the existing receipt would work. He didn't demand a new set of seals out of thin air.

A supervisor checked the reference and approved an annotation of deferred delivery, preserving the original collection request. It proved today's request and response, he warned, not that every underlying record existed or remained complete.

Adrian wanted exactly that distinction retained.

The ink was cold. The first impression left half the seal missing. When the clerk replaced the sheet, Adrian asked that the earlier one be canceled under the existing procedure, rather than leave two apparently original papers with differing times. This time the clerk complied without irritation.

An argument broke out outside.

A small cart carrying covered wooden boxes was leaving a side entrance. Someone shouted that material was being stolen. Several people rushed toward the horse, whose driver hauled on the reins. Its head rose; a rear wheel struck the edge of a step.

Near the counter, Adrian stopped an elderly man trying to squeeze out behind him and asked to keep the passage clear. The man began swearing, saw the wheel, and moved against the wall.

Guards halted the cart and cleared people from the horse's path. Preservation staff checked its papers. The boxes had been scheduled for a repair room. Whether they contained protected material needed examination: routine delivery did not authorize immediate release, and cloth coverings did not establish theft.

Several steps from the rope, Adrian could not see inside. He recorded no claim that he could. The gap between the cloth and the box offered no secret glimpse of a page, no convenient title that would turn suspicion into knowledge. He kept that limitation in what he wrote. He saw the cart stopped, papers given to the supervisor, transfer suspended, and inventory begun with authorized witnesses.

Someone recognized him and urged him to take the chance to enter.

“Don't you want the truth?”

“If I go in, the first question becomes what I've touched.”

He stayed outside. Wanting answers did not make his hands incapable of leaving traces.

The spectator snorted. Adrian recorded the actual time, cart position, and suspension, not destroyed historical betrothals inside unseen boxes. Two willing bystanders separately gave contact details. The elderly man left without providing his name, free not to become anyone's witness.

By the time Adrian received his receipt, tea time had passed.

He paid an ordinary messenger with his own money to notify the previously designated contact: collection deferred for preservation, presently at the annex, returning late. Luna's private invitation was not included in that message. The messenger repeated the destination before leaving.

Adrian checked the notice again. His copy had missed a line. The closure concerned this archive building and identified transfers, not every registration office. The temporary counter still accepted ordinary applications. Whether they could be completed that day depended on their need for protected material.

He corrected his notes. Everyone saying closed didn't justify writing that all procedures across the city had stopped.

The employee collecting the hammer expected another dispute. Adrian only asked where to inquire on February 2. The annex, the man said. No individual attendance time existed yet. Anyone seeking to make a statement should register a request; the notice was no pass into an inventory.

Adrian lodged his request and received acknowledgment. Asked whether it should be combined with the princess's position, he said he would explain his own collection experience. Any joint submission required the others to read and decide first.

The clerk omitted the final letter of his surname. Adrian pointed it out. Both sighed. Correcting it now was easier than later proving who he was with a wrongly named receipt.

The stopped cart had not departed. Its driver had moved the horse clear while staff compared wrappers. Adrian recorded that transfer remained suspended when he left. He did not supply resolved for a result that hadn't happened.

Across the street, the man with the wooden box sat on a step. It held ordinary betrothal flowers, not a risk-transmitting instrument. He had expected to finish today; his intended partner waited elsewhere. With even copies inaccessible, could he do nothing but wait until someone chose to speak?

“You can record that you came and what you requested. I don't know when it will be finished.”

He disliked the second part but accepted the first. Before returning to the counter, he covered the flowers. Adrian promised neither reform nor that the stranger's disappointment proved his own case more important.

At home, Luna's tea had been cleared. She didn't demand to know whether he was unwilling or indifferent. She listened to what had happened. He produced his receipt and copied notice, leaving unseen matters blank.

“You didn't fill the gaps with guesses.”

“Someone wanted me to go inside and look.”

She glanced at her right palm without proposing a device to see through the wrappers tonight. That would cross the limits of her injury, preservation, and the principles they had just published.

Elena asked who had seen the list before inventory. Cecilia remembered the historical-classification counter. Its staff might explain how copies had been issued; remembering them was not testimony already obtained. Isabella would contact administrators she could approach in an ordinary capacity, not invoke her former role to open doors.

Viola considered the public access arrangement. Willing witnesses needed to be asked about movement and travel needs before approaching those actually responsible for protection. She still couldn't lead an escort. Luna distinguished technical binding questions from documentary authority. Seeing a knot did not make someone a witness to everything inside.

Adrian set five blank sheets on the table. Each person would record only what she could ask. These were not five answers. Willingness to inquire was not someone else's willingness to testify.

Tomorrow, January 31, they could separately seek the people who had actually handled the papers. He would organize the public notice and his own attendance record so they began with the same established facts.

After the others left, Luna asked again about tea, without an envelope or business schedule.

“Not today. I'm tired.”

She nodded and moved the empty cup back to her side.

“I'll ask tomorrow. You don't owe today's cup.”

Someone at the door asked about lamps. She requested only the corridor lamp and prepared to rest. Her private invitation was put away. The blank witness sheets remained where each person could claim them. The two things no longer lay on top of each other.

Adrian looked at her, then smiled and agreed. She did not convert that agreement into an appointment already made for tomorrow.
