# Chapter 152: Her Surname Cannot Sign for Him

Elena had prepared a magnificent opening.

The family lawyer read it, turned the paper back, and asked one question.

“If we remove Rosenberg, what grounds remain?”

The fire had only just caught at the family offices on the morning of January twenty-fourth. Elena stared at hereditary rights in her first line. Irritation came before recognition. An old habit had covered what she herself had said yesterday.

“I'm directly named in the order.”

“That's today's starting point.”

He wasn't refusing family assistance. Costs, access to existing decisions, filing procedure: all could receive lawful support. But an application seeking only an exception for noble children might leave Adrian exactly where he stood, waiting for a surname to open the door.

Elena crossed out her first paragraph, retaining the draft.

The second was more satisfying: immediate withdrawal of every restriction concerning imprint bearers.

The lawyer stopped again.

“Do you believe the carrier was harmless?”

Remembering Isabella's cushioned left wrist, she shook her head.

“Then is your argument that there is no danger, or that danger doesn't justify this restriction?”

“The latter.”

“Write the latter.”

She leaned back, regarding her two demolished opening paragraphs. Suddenly she wanted Adrian to come in and see her equally unsuccessful report.

But he wasn't waiting outside.

Last night she had asked him to handle his own affairs first. She would ask when she needed his answer. If she wanted that respected, she couldn't resent his failure to guess that she now wanted to see him.

She picked up her pen again.

The order acknowledged danger without separately explaining each person's actual load, contract type, or existing status. It permitted objections, but attached faster processing to acceptance of compatible-candidate assessment. Safety review could examine technical conditions. It couldn't take control of partner selection without explaining its authority.

Then she narrowed the requested outcome.

First, confirm that being listed didn't prohibit ordinary relationships or independent factual submissions, and correct existing misuse. Require the grounds, standards, and appeal route for special review. Whether the order should be withdrawn, and how to handle genuinely risk-transmitting new contracts, required affected people's accounts and a reasoned decision.

She separately requested a temporary stay of the registration restrictions during review. The lawyer warned that this wasn't final withdrawal and might be denied while the danger remained unresolved. She distinguished it from her substantive claims.

“You don't demand to be declared the winner today?”

“I demand that they accept the application today.”

At last, he nodded.

Adrian arrived before noon while Elena numbered two new pages. No joint petition already signed on his behalf awaited him.

First she showed him the crossed-out opening.

He read carefully.

“That sentence does sound satisfying.”

“I know. Please don't mourn it.”

“I was mourning the paper.”

She glared. Smiling, he sat beside her without immediately reaching for a pen.

She would object as a directly affected person. Her family supplied support, not consent for the other five. If Adrian joined, he must choose which facts and requests he would make public. Declining wouldn't cost him her help.

“I know you don't consider me an appendage.”

“I must still ask. Knowing isn't a signature.”

He read his portion. No current valid betrothal. Old-timeline records couldn't establish present consent. Willingness to hear specific risks, but no agreement that a registration permit should determine his romantic or betrothal partner.

At that last sentence, he paused for a long time.

“Someone will use this to ask whom I actually love.”

Elena's pen stopped.

Of course she wanted an answer. Sometimes another woman's glance alone bothered her. But if this petition required his complete declaration of feelings before respecting his choices, she would merely place another form before him.

“You can say that isn't what this registration review must decide.”

“Would you be disappointed?”

“At another time, perhaps.” She looked up. “I'm not asking for that on this page.”

He studied her, then slowly nodded and signed within the scope he had read.

She didn't have the lawyer move his signature beneath her family's crest. Two applications, two names, submitted as related matters. Neither represented the other.

Before they left, Duke Edmund Rosenberg sent a note asking whether they needed a carriage. No conditions for family support followed. Elena supplied two passengers and the agreed return point, then smiled at Adrian.

“Viola would approve that wording.”

“She has her examination today.”

“So I won't make her confirm the passenger count too.”

Their carriage passed streets where the ice was melting. Separate folders lay between their knees. Elena quietly moved hers so its hard corner no longer pressed his leg.

He noticed without turning the small kindness into another duty requiring thanks.

The Judicial Office's registration review section was busier than expected.

One person disputed a name in inheritance papers, another a shop deposit. An unmarried couple blamed each other for forgetting a supporting document. Nobody automatically yielded a place to Elena.

She took a number and sat beside Adrian.

The young couple soon reconciled, bending over their form with fingers on the same line. After watching them, Elena turned away.

“Am I very difficult to be with?”

Adrian didn't immediately answer.

“You'd better not be counting.”

“I was thinking that easy company and someone worth loving aren't quite the same question.”

She turned her face aside, ears red. When called, she almost forgot her number.

The receiving clerk checked that both were personally affected, rather than using a duke's seat to extend the old-project hearing into betrothal law. Then came the attachments: a certified copy of the order, service records, the window's rejection and correction, and their separate statements.

Elena hadn't enclosed Isabella's injury drawing or all of Luna's experimental records for extra weight. Limited conclusions cleared for disclosure already supported the existence of risk. If further material required specific examination, an authorized person could ask for it specifically.

The clerk explained that the registry acted on Temple advice. Both offices must reply separately. Review could accept the case, require reasons, and issue temporary directions about registration practice. It couldn't invalidate every Temple rule that day.

“Then please don't describe us as requesting that.”

Elena moved her revised requests into view.

While they waited, the clerk returned a page. Did jointly affected still represent separate personal accounts?

Elena almost sighed. They had only just corrected a joint cover; she was weary of the word. Adrian noticed that this time, however, the question wasn't whether something was forbidden. It was who stood behind which statement.

They specified their individual effects, leaving nobody to guess at their relationship.

After checking again, the clerk filed the page and gave them separate copies.

Watching the folder close, Elena quietly admitted she had nearly regarded anyone asking another question as an obstacle.

“I've done that too.”

“You needn't join every confession I make.”

“Then this time I'm only waiting with you.”

She smiled and moved her folder off the empty seat for a new arrival. The stranger merely thanked her, knowing nothing of whom she was challenging over her future.

That afternoon, the duty reviewer arranged a short preliminary examination. Not a great public trial. No audience awaited a sentence that would shake the capital.

The registry representative brought the enforcement grounds. He admitted the first handling of the joint cover had been too broad and was corrected. But a seven-day pause could prevent new contracts from creating irreversible risks. His office couldn't fully assess every named person on the day of service.

“Then why prioritize those accepting the designated procedure?” Elena asked.

“An established safety process requires fewer additional checks.”

“Does it examine how a contract transmits risk, or whom someone should sign with?”

He turned to compatible-candidate assessment without establishing the boundary.

He wasn't foolish and didn't confess a conspiracy. The phrase came from the Temple's applicability opinion. The registry implemented it; it did not independently interpret doctrine.

Elena asked the reviewer to record that answer.

When questioned, Adrian described his actual position. He had no current betrothal and didn't propose rushing into one to prove opposition. Nor did he demand all risk contracts proceed unchecked. He wanted to know why he was listed, what evidence he could offer, and whose decision he could appeal.

“What if a particular contract is ultimately found dangerous?”

“Identify the contract and the danger. Let us discuss conditions instead of deciding first whom we should choose.”

Only when he finished did he notice his damp palm. Elena didn't wipe it for him beneath the table. She moved her cup so he could reach his own.

The interim decision came before dusk.

Their two objections were formally accepted for related review. They were not registered as parties to a betrothal with each other. The registry must supply its grounds for listing and special procedures the next day. Names on the list alone couldn't bar ordinary private contact or non-risk-bearing document submissions. Corrections of mistaken decisions must remain recorded.

The temporary request to stay registration restrictions was denied.

Restrictions on new betrothals and risk contracts remained in force for now. Whether they exceeded authority or required narrowing would be examined after both offices replied.

For a moment, Elena wanted to call the first half victory and refuse to hear the rest.

Instead, she asked the clerk to read the response deadline too.

They left with an accepted case reference, an explicit enforcement boundary, and an order still obstructing several choices.

On the steps, Adrian asked whether she was disappointed.

“Yes. I prepared so much to say. They accepted only part of it.”

“Which part would you like to swear at first?”

She looked up and laughed.

“You won't remind me what we've achieved?”

“We can think about that after dinner.”

She took his hand without gripping harder than usual because of what she had argued for them today.

At the house, they informed the others individually about the relevant results. Two applications didn't amount to authorization from five women. Isabella read the interim boundaries and would consider submitting independent observations. Luna requested only the technical references and circled unresolved points with her left hand.

Viola returned from her examination still forbidden load-bearing and active duty. She didn't revise the physician's instructions because of the controversy outside. She asked whether ordinary access was clearly protected.

Elena showed her the passage.

Cecilia received the summary of the Temple opinion cited by the registry. One sentence sounded perfectly ordinary: Compatible candidates shall be determined through historical betrothal records and interpretation of imprint attribution.

Her finger stopped beneath historical.

“I need the full text they relied on.”

“It should accompany tomorrow's reply,” Elena said.

Cecilia nodded. She didn't declare the summary a forged oracle on sight. But she no longer looked as though she were reading an ordinary acknowledgment.

Tonight, the question had become hers.

Before going upstairs, Elena handed Adrian the bookshop sequel. Today's queue ticket marked the page, not the petition.

“Start where he loses the boarding ticket.”

“Has the captain lost it again?”

“I haven't read ahead. Don't invent an ending where he's already found it.”

Smiling, Adrian opened the book. Answers were still awaited beyond the window. Under the lamp, two people had kept a small piece of the evening for themselves.
