# Chapter 288: Read the Prince's Whole Name Too

On May 20, Leon waited outside the courtroom for a quarter of an hour.

His bench was the same polished, worn kind used by the public. Nobody was deliberately keeping him waiting. An ordinary dispute over payment hadn't finished. An elderly woman insisted that the clerk read out the three silver coins she'd already paid. When an attendant offered to remind the court who was outside, Leon stopped him.

Across the corridor, Isabella watched her brother press his sleeve. She knew that impatient gesture from childhood music lessons. Recognizing it made nothing easier.

“Where would you like me to sit?” Adrian asked.

“You have your witness seat.” Her tone was sharper than intended. “Our ordinary handover is still in the courtyard afterward. The recorder will be there. Don't change the private distance I asked for last night.”

He remembered. She didn't ask him to sit between her and her brother, as though the right chair could keep every relationship intact.

The doors opened. The officer called Leon Aurelian's full name, followed by his advocate's. This time, people didn't wait for the prince to take his place before finding their own.

The panel hadn't participated in the original investigation. The prosecutor explained the case: how Leon had used and represented the disputed evidence during last September's accusations and subsequent transfers. Elena's terminated treason prosecution wasn't reopening. His later assistance identifying Temple contacts would be considered separately, not exchanged in advance for immunity.

Seated among the affected parties, Elena listened with her hands on her knees. No signet weighed down anyone's statement.

First came the preserved copy of Evidence Item 714's registration. The contradictory sealing date, the other case entering through the side door, and independently verified transfers had separate sources. Laying them together didn't prove Leon had made them all himself.

His advocate objected immediately. Knowing Adrian's name beforehand didn't establish knowledge of a substituted case. A royal funding connection didn't prove the prince directed a particular payment.

The judge required an answer. Accepting that neither point independently proved participation, the prosecutor removed the relationship diagram from the center and substituted a comparison of statements and material received.

Someone behind Elena muttered about helping him find mistakes. She didn't turn. She'd already faced a table where every uncertainty counted as guilt. She wouldn't leave it waiting for someone else.

Adrian described the date he'd seen, the announcement he'd heard, and his own objection at the ball. The case's passage through the side entrance had separate testimony from a guard at October's review. He wouldn't borrow that testimony to enlarge his own observations. He didn't know whom Leon had spoken to beforehand.

“Did you believe the prince already intended Elena's death?” the prosecutor asked.

“I was afraid he would succeed. Fear can't let me overhear a conversation I wasn't present for.”

The advocate looked up without rewarding him with a friendly smile. His questions remained sharp. Had affection for Elena distorted Adrian's recollection? Had he ever overstated an inference?

Adrian acknowledged premature judgments. Today he relied on preserved statements and independently checked records to correct them. Later feelings didn't prove every early word accurate. Neither could he remove Elena from his life and pretend complete detachment.

Leon finally released his sleeve.

The central dispute concerned his own statements. The preserved account of his ballroom statement showed him insisting that a mistaken date didn't remove suspicion after the discrepancy was raised. His advocate argued that a prince could reasonably rely on judicial officers without personally examining every sealed box.

“Rely on which completed examination?” the judge asked.

Leon identified the October 6 conference record and October 9 review statement. He'd reported receiving a Temple warning, then acknowledged uncertainty about its basis. Those statements couldn't establish that he'd known every forgery in September. Today's question was what he'd understood when the date discrepancy confronted him and he continued maintaining the accusation.

After consulting his advocate, Leon chose to answer.

“I didn't see the substitution. I didn't write the seal.”

“Neither has been established against you today.”

“But I knew they hadn't answered the question about the date.”

Silence settled. He glanced at Elena, then returned to the bench.

“I let people believe it no longer mattered. If she left, things could end more easily. I wanted that result.”

His advocate requested an explicit distinction: acknowledging an unresolved question wasn't admitting that he'd commissioned every forgery. The judge agreed, then asked what Leon had actually said, and to whom, while continuing to use the material. Comparing the preserved record, Leon identified his own passage without confessing for absent people.

Adrian remembered the ballroom lights. He'd once imagined everyone taking positions for lines from a game. Now one person beneath those lights had to explain an actual sentence. Responsibility no longer floated somewhere inside invisible fate.

The advocate requested the ballroom statement's full context. Leon had agreed to review and no detention that night, saying Elena could return once the investigation settled. On September 20, he'd also promised that no unlawful action would be taken against her; on October 9, he'd withdrawn support for treason prosecution. These facts belonged in the record. Other royal representatives' applications weren't automatically his spoken orders. The clerk supplied the passages. Leon's shoulders eased.

Yet that addition didn't explain why someone awaiting review must first leave her life behind. Allowed to describe its effect, Elena said she was being asked to surrender the proximity that let her keep questioning, then wait for others to decide. She didn't claim everyone suggesting departure knew about the execution notice. Nor had they been proposing a holiday.

“Might agreeing have spared you subsequent danger?”

“It might also have cost me the chance to investigate. An outcome that never happened cannot prove I ought to have obeyed.”

The judge stopped questions requiring her to imagine a different life. The court concerned actual conduct, not the most benevolent alternative an affected person could devise for the accused. The advocate withdrew the question and obtained permission to verify the time the suggestion had been conveyed.

Elena discovered her water had cooled. She drank, then told her companion she needed a break. Answering well hadn't exempted her from exhaustion.

Outside, Isabella's sleeve pressed against her left-wrist support. She asked her attendant to adjust it instead of performing royal immunity to discomfort. Adrian remained in the witness waiting area. She didn't beckon him over, but went elsewhere with her caregiver. Momentary unease needn't break rules they were asking others to follow.

That afternoon, Leon's later cooperation was formally entered. The prosecutor accepted that it had helped trace contacts. His advocate requested renewed access to royal archives so he could assist further.

Why must an accused person enter the original records' custody area, the judge asked? An escort could attend, the advocate suggested. The prosecutor proposed precise references submitted through counsel for independent custodians to retrieve. Helpfulness shouldn't buy control over evidence.

The panel denied unrestricted access. Leon could request particular records and receive necessary defense copies; originals remained independently preserved. Royal offices must also stop using his name to endorse disputed material as authentic without verification. His associated certification function was suspended during proceedings and reassigned to existing qualified examiners.

The advocate asked whether this stripped every public function before a verdict. The judge explained that the measure addressed risks to custody and authentication in this case. It was not a sentence and did not decide unrelated status. Leon could challenge its scope through review, but the originals would not return to his control while that application was pending. He asked his advocate to note the deadline. Nobody demanded that he surrender objections to demonstrate repentance. An enforceable restriction and a meaningful defense could exist at the same time; neither had to be offered as a favor.

A royal records representative received the order in court. Nobody merely told the princess to watch her brother. The representative read the requirements back and entered the suspended endorsement route in that day's register. Leon didn't tear up the receipt.

The court confirmed the substantive scope: continuing to support accusations with material he knew remained unresolved. Whether that amounted to knowingly using false evidence, and what particular harm followed, required further examination of disclosed evidence. Wanting Elena gone didn't alone prove knowledge of the entire sacrifice scheme.

Dates for witness comparison and further defense submissions went into the notice. Leon acknowledged receipt. His assistance remained visible without covering his own words.

Afterward, someone asked Elena whether she was finally satisfied.

She watched the old registration return to its sealed envelope.

“I need what happened established. My looking satisfied isn't what makes this a hearing.”

Leon paused by the door. He didn't invoke his sister to ask forgiveness. He only hoped his admission wouldn't be rewritten as an attempt to protect Elena.

“Then pay attention to your next statement,” she said.

She left without looking back. Adrian almost followed, then saw her acknowledge her companion. He went to complete the day's handover. Elena noticed and signaled that proving his love didn't require transferring every minute to her.

The courtyard bench stood partly in sunlight. Isabella left half free, asking him to sit a little farther away. He did, without interpreting distance as punishment.

While the recorder fetched the receipt book, she watched the entrance.

“When my brother sat down, for one moment I wanted everything to stop. To take my family home first.”

“You didn't ask the court to stop.”

“That doesn't mean I didn't want it. And sitting farther away doesn't mean you must guess whether everything you do hurts me again.”

Wind lifted the edge of her skirt.

“I know,” Adrian said. “You don't have to turn being unhappy into a speech supporting me today.”

“Then what we agreed last night stands. Ordinary work, no private invitations for now. Don't quietly cancel the distance because today's hearing was hard.”

“I won't. Worry today won't give yesterday's answer a different meaning. When the recorder returns, I'll finish the handover and leave.”

After a moment, a little expression returned to her mouth.

“Your comfort isn't particularly charming.”

“I considered discussing the weather.”

“Worse. It's too hot to take a principled position.”

They both laughed once. Isabella didn't move closer. She finished her water slowly. She still had her brother to face and a proposal that wasn't law to advance. Neither had become an assignment awarded to her for romantic disappointment.

The recorder returned with the book. They checked the copies actually handed over, rather than allowing an ordinary exchange of papers to stretch into the private afternoon she'd declined. Isabella corrected a destination on the cover; Adrian checked it against the notice before accepting the correction. Her voice was steady without needing to sound cheerful.

Before he left, judicial staff passed with arrangements for tomorrow's hearing on Oswin's management powers. Isabella asked them to use the formal service route, not bring her the copy first to decide its fate.

Adrian picked up a fallen sunshade beside the bench. Its ribs had caught. Twice he failed to open it.

Watching, the princess finally said, “You may ask for help with that. It doesn't require independent personal responsibility.”

He called the courtyard attendant. Together they returned the shade to its stand. Leon's file hadn't become lighter; tomorrow's hearing hadn't vanished. But as they left the courtyard, neither pretended that one hand must hold every answer.
