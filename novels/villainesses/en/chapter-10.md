# Why Do All the Villainesses Want to Marry Me?
## Volume One: A Betrothal Written in Blood
### Chapter 10: She Lived Past Her Execution

Fog covered the capital on the morning of September twentieth.

Adrian sat in the carriage with a much-handled copy of the evidence records balanced on his knees.

Three dates had been circled in red.

September sixteenth: the blue sealing wax left the stationer's shop.

September seventeenth: three boxes from that batch entered the palace.

September eighteenth: according to the Judicial Office's ledger, the evidence was formally sealed.

Three simple entries.

They were giving Adrian a headache.

"You've read that twenty times," Elena said.

"Twenty-one."

"The dates won't change if you read them again."

"I might remember them wrong."

"Then read them aloud to me."

Adrian recited all three without looking.

Elena nodded.

"Correct. Now put the paper away."

"How are you so calm?"

She didn't answer immediately.

The carriage passed the capital's clock tower, and the first pale sunlight found its way through the fog onto her shoulders.

"I'm not calm," she said. "But someone in this carriage is already nervous enough for both of us."

Adrian turned toward her.

"You're frightened too?"

"Of course."

"Of what?"

"That you'll say something to the judge even more outrageous than what the newspapers printed yesterday."

"..."

Adrian folded the papers.

Hale, seated across from them, cleared his throat.

"This isn't a ball, you two. If you're quite finished, there's one final point we should review."

They both sat up straighter.

"The Judicial Office warehouse did catch fire last night," the lawyer said. "The original registration page for Item 714 has been reported destroyed. The evidence box itself survived, but its seal and custody record must be reviewed."

"Clay will say the date was just a mistake."

"And he'll argue that the supplier's accounts have nothing to do with the supposedly treasonous letter."

"Then we need the judge to rule that the broken custody record isn't enough to justify a capital sentence."

Hale nodded.

"Today's objective isn't to prove who forged the letter. It's to prevent an immediate punishment based on evidence that hasn't been properly verified."

Adrian stared out the window.

At this point in the game, Elena would already have been escorted to the execution grounds.

Here she was beside him, quietly straightening the ribbon on her folded fan.

He didn't know how much of the future he could change.

But he refused to let the day end the way it had before.

The courtroom was almost full.

The Crown's representatives occupied the front benches. Clay sat stiffly at the evidence table in his gray uniform, his face paler than it had been yesterday.

Farther back, journalists whispered excitedly over their notebooks.

"That's the servant who kissed the duke's daughter," one said.

Another lowered his voice.

"I heard they're getting engaged today."

Adrian closed his eyes.

The newspapers in this country were better at altering reality than the Judicial Office.

Elena touched his sleeve.

"Don't try to explain anything to the reporters today."

"I've learned my lesson."

"Good."

She almost smiled.

The judge entered. Everyone rose.

The Crown's advocate submitted an emergency application to hold Elena on suspicion of treason and transfer the case to a special royal tribunal.

"Although Evidence Item 714 was affected by the warehouse fire, the letter itself establishes a secret connection between House Rosenberg and northern hostile forces."

The judge studied the papers.

"Can you supply a complete chain of custody?"

"The original ledger was damaged in the fire."

"Alternative records?"

Clay stood.

"I can personally attest that every procedure was followed."

Hale rose.

"Your Honor, precisely because Mr. Clay was involved in receiving and holding the evidence, we request independent verification by other clerks and testimony from witnesses outside the Judicial Office."

Clay gave a cold laugh.

"You intend to undermine royal intelligence with a deputy steward who stole from his employer?"

Whispers spread through the gallery.

Henry, seated in the witness waiting area, went white. His hands were gripping the edge of his coat.

Adrian remembered Henry's warm tea left behind at the warehouse.

He was frightened too.

Far more frightened than Adrian, perhaps.

The judge rapped his gavel.

"Counsel for the respondent?"

Before Hale could speak, Adrian stood.

"Henry admitted he misused funds from the Rosenberg estate."

Elena looked at him.

Adrian gave Hale a brief nod.

"But the court doesn't have to take his word on faith. His receipt can be checked against the merchants' guild's monthly accounts. The transfer dates can be compared with the palace gate records."

Clay cut in.

"A servant presumes to instruct the court on evidence?"

"I wouldn't dream of it. I'd simply like you to answer a question."

"What question?"

"If Henry invented the story, how did the September sixteenth issue ledger, the supplier's original accounts, and the palace entrance records from the seventeenth all end up recording the same sequence?"

Clay did not answer at once.

Adrian's voice still trembled.

"And can you explain why an item brought into the palace on the seventeenth was recorded as sealed on the eighteenth?"

"Clerical error!"

"Then please identify the receiving officer, custodian, and person responsible for sealing it, along with the time each transfer occurred."

Clay opened his mouth.

Hale stepped forward with copies of the supplier's accounts certified by an independent notary.

The stationer had come to court himself. He confirmed that his original ledger remained in his shop and could be independently checked.

The keeper at the river warehouses confirmed that Henry had arrived in a carriage using a Judicial Office pass, but could not identify the other occupants.

Adrian spoke again.

"I'm not asking anyone to believe guesses they can't verify. I'm only asking that facts which *can* be checked aren't erased by a fire."

The judge turned to the Crown.

"If the evidence custody record cannot be reconstructed, Item 714 cannot, on its own, support immediate punishment. Do you have anything else?"

The Crown's advocate exchanged a look with Clay.

An attendant hurried in with a red-bound document and placed it before them.

Adrian recognized the insignia.

His heart lurched.

An emergency royal disposition order.

In the game, that was the document which sent Elena to the execution platform at noon.

Leon sat in the front row of the observers' gallery.

He hadn't spoken once.

Now he slowly raised his eyes to Adrian.

There was something in the look that almost seemed to ask:

*Do you still remember what happens next?*

The Crown's advocate rose.

"Under the kingdom's emergency provisions, we seek temporary detention of Lady Rosenberg and authorization for the special tribunal to issue an expedited ruling before noon today."

The judge frowned.

"What level of emergency disposition are you requesting?"

"The highest."

The courtroom fell silent.

A sharp pain ran across the old scar on Adrian's left hand.

He ignored it.

His eyes remained fixed on the red document.

Elena leaned toward him.

"Don't jump up and speak."

"I know."

"Let Hale handle this."

The lawyer was already standing.

"Your Honor, the highest emergency classification requires two conditions: an imminent, demonstrable threat, and a substantial risk that ordinary procedure cannot contain. The Crown has presented no new evidence of immediate danger. It asks this court to deprive a person of liberty based on evidence whose very chain of custody is in doubt."

The judge looked toward the Crown's table.

"Can you establish a threat likely to materialize before noon today?"

"The northern rebels may exploit the leaked defensive information to attack."

"'May' is not the same as 'will,' let alone evidence that an attack is under way."

At last Leon stood.

"Your Honor, I will personally undertake that so long as Elena cooperates with the investigation, the Crown will not subject her to any unlawful punishment."

Adrian stared.

The prince had never said anything like that in the game.

Elena looked at him as well.

Leon refused to meet her eyes. He sat back down, his expression troubled.

Hale used the moment to request that any attempt to detain or immediately execute Elena be fully entered into the record and subject to independent review.

The judge was silent for a long time.

Then he raised his gavel.

"This court finds material deficiencies in the custody of Evidence Item 714. Until an independent review is completed, that item may not serve as the sole basis for a conviction of treason."

The gavel fell.

"The Crown's application for immediate detention is denied."

A second strike.

"The request for expedited disposition by a special tribunal is dismissed. The matter is returned to ordinary judicial proceedings for further investigation."

A third.

Noise returned to the room.

First a few shaky breaths.

Then whispers.

Adrian didn't stand.

He was still staring at the red document on the judge's desk.

The very paper that should have decided Elena's death was closed and set aside.

No torches.

No execution platform.

No cold narration announcing the villainess's inevitable end.

Only a real judge instructing a clerk to write the ruling down word for word.

Elena tugged gently at Adrian's sleeve.

"It's over."

He turned to her.

"Really?"

"For today, at least."

Something in his chest finally loosened.

Outside, the clock tower began to strike noon.

One.

Two.

Adrian counted.

This was when Elena had died in the game.

By the twelfth stroke, she was still standing beside him.

Alive.

Breathing.

And apparently staring at him with growing concern.

"Why do you look as if you're about to cry?" she asked.

"I'm not."

"Your eyes are red."

"I was nervous."

Elena didn't tease him.

"I know."

She took his hand, careful not to touch the cut across his palm.

"Thank you."

Adrian suddenly had no idea what to say.

"I thought the judge might refuse to listen."

"I could tell. Your hands were shaking while you spoke."

"Didn't I look pathetic?"

"Being frightened and speaking anyway isn't pathetic."

He lowered his head.

"And you don't have to shield me from every danger," Elena added. "Today wasn't one person's victory. It was yours, Hale's, the witnesses', and the people in our household who helped preserve the records."

Adrian looked up.

This wasn't the villainess whose only purpose was to wait for a player to rescue her.

Elena fought for her own future.

They left the courtroom.

Reporters swarmed the steps.

"Lady Rosenberg, is it true you're about to become engaged to Mr. Reed?"

"Mr. Reed! After kissing Her Ladyship at the ball and defending her today, are you trying to win the duke's approval?"

Adrian drew a breath.

He had an answer ready.

Elena spoke first.

"Ladies and gentlemen, the story worth reporting today is that the Judicial Office's evidence procedures are being subjected to independent review."

One reporter persisted.

"But Mr. Reed is only a servant. Why do you trust him?"

Elena looked at Adrian.

"Because he supplied evidence people can check."

"Not because of a personal relationship?"

Her expression didn't change.

"His work doesn't need to be explained by his private relationship with me."

Adrian went still.

Elena turned back to the reporters.

"You're free to dislike him. You're not free to pretend everything he accomplished was actually my doing."

The words warmed him more than any elaborate declaration might have.

The Rosenberg guards cleared a passage through the reporters.

Adrian accompanied Elena down the steps.

Then the crescent-shaped scar on his left hand began to burn.

Not like the cut from the broken glass.

It felt as though someone were slowly tracing the old mark with a wire heated red-hot.

He stumbled.

"Adrian?"

Elena caught his arm.

"What's wrong?"

"Nothing. An old injury..."

Before he could finish, a young clerk hurried toward them from the courthouse.

"Mr. Reed! Wait!"

He carried a yellowed index card.

"When we verified your appearance details, an old index turned up among the records. It doesn't belong to this case, but the name is identical to yours."

Adrian took it.

The ink was faded but legible.

*Adrian Reed.*

The archival number followed a format discontinued decades earlier.

Beneath the name was a field marked *status*.

It didn't say witness.

It didn't say defendant.

It contained a word Adrian had never seen in the game.

**Substitute.**

He looked up, ready to ask what it meant.

The young clerk shook his head.

"I'm sorry. That's all the index contains. We haven't found the original file."

Elena saw the card.

Her expression slowly changed.

"Adrian," she asked, "are you absolutely certain you've never been here before?"

Adrian couldn't answer.

Because through the burning in his left hand, he could almost hear a clock tolling somewhere very far away.

Twelve strokes again.

Except that time, when the last bell faded, someone had died right in front of him.
