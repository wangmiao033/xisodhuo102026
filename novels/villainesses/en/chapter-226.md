# Chapter 226: The Princess in the Waiting Area

On March 25, Isabella turned over the appointment slip and found another sentence: Delegates cannot commit residents to bodily risk on their behalf.

“That isn't there specifically to stop you,” Adrian said.

She looked at him. “Your attempts at reassurance have become adventurous.”

They stood outside the local councils' liaison hall. Papers in her right hand, left wrist supported, she wore a sleeve widened by the tailor so it no longer rubbed with each step. No escort cleared a path; those responsible for her security stayed in their customary positions. She was meeting delegates already based in the capital and authorized to receive material, not ordering distant councils to arrive overnight.

A long row of chairs filled the hall. A merchant waited by the window for a road-compensation answer. Near the door, someone held a piece of damaged bridge railing. Isabella collected her registered number. Adrian steadied her diagrams on their board without raising them away from muddy shoes.

An attendant recognized her and immediately offered an inner room to spare her the wait.

“Which room was I assigned?”

“The third. The inner room would be for you separately.”

After considering, she asked whether using it would exclude the expected delegates and ordinary recorder. The attendant hesitated. The director could first hear her wishes and make other arrangements.

“I'll wait for the third room.”

Once seated, she quietly told Adrian she'd wanted to accept. The chair back was too straight; the window leaked cold air.

“You're allowed to feel cold.”

“Thank you. I feared equality prohibited shawls.”

Laughing, he offered her own shawl and asked whether she wanted it spread. She did. She secured a corner with her right hand, leaving the injured wrist alone. The merchant glanced over without rising to praise reform. He resumed checking his compensation figures.

When their turn came, the three delegates began by defining their authority. One could transmit documents to a local committee. Another could recommend spending within an existing small research allowance. The third could explain procedure only. None could enroll everyone outside the city in a new contract or guarantee future funding.

Isabella laid out yesterday's witness arrangements: two people conditionally willing to continue, the third uncommitted. She didn't call it unanimous citizen support.

“We'd like you to examine whether limited model research can be supported. We aren't collecting residents' consent to risk.”

The northern district's delegate examined the diagram. Would failure affect existing protection? Adrian explained that the proposed materials were independent, intended for aboveground work, with no connection to current defenses. Actual reviewers still needed to approve the technical scope. His spoken answer didn't replace that review.

“Why do you need local councils, then?”

Isabella had prepared to speak about a calamity confronting the whole kingdom. Instead she gave concrete reasons. Communities should know what the research asked and what remained unanswered. If willing, they could provide shareable information about ordinary maintenance conditions. Any later proposal involving local resources needed a local route for objections, not a finished capital-designed plan with an acceptance box attached.

The delegate wasn't immediately persuaded. Once documents reached the capital, who decided how they would be described publicly?

“The researchers and independent reviewers—” She stopped, hearing how she'd excluded the source communities again.

Another delegate moved her cup aside, clearing space. “We're only supplying things?”

Isabella admitted the omission. Extracts concerning a locality should be checked with their source for meaning and retain disagreements. Supporting research and permitting publication of material needed separate answers.

She requested the change recorded, not presented as something already considered.

The northern delegate took a public maintenance notice from his folder. It listed two repair shifts, one postponed after materials arrived late. If he shared it, would researchers describe his district as unable to protect residents, then demand its inclusion in an arrangement decided by the capital?

Isabella didn't promise that nobody would ever think that way. She asked what postponing the shift actually affected. He explained that the work replaced a reserve support. Operating equipment hadn't stopped. A subsequent sheet described the rescheduled repair. Reading only the first notice might suggest half the defenses were absent.

Adrian crossed out the sentence he'd begun copying: One of two shifts stopped. He had wanted evidence of scarce resources to justify supporting their model. He'd nearly misrepresented work that still functioned.

“These two need to be read together.”

“Nor should later completion erase the earlier delay,” the delegate replied.

Isabella asked that this correction remain available so future readers would see how they'd misread it. She proposed attaching a source-checkable version to every forwarded extract. Technical interpretations belonged on a separately attributed sheet, not quietly inserted back into the original record.

The third delegate still offered no recommendation. If only the capital ultimately benefited from this model, could a community supplying information refuse its costs? Isabella wanted to say that calamities respected no borders and everyone should contribute. Then she remembered the man outside waiting for bridge compensation.

“They can refuse a commitment they haven't made. If public duties become disputed later, actual grounds must be discussed then, not hidden inside today's request for information.”

He wrote that down and would forward it with the application. He didn't abandon his funding questions. For the first time, however, he put the request for material in the pending folder instead of pushing it back toward her.

It wasn't an endorsement. At least they needn't commit a whole locality's future to earn permission to read a maintenance sheet.

The hardest question followed.

“Do you intend to be the external coordinator?”

Her name appeared in the proposal. She no longer possessed her former special coordination powers. This was a request for a new, limited liaison role. She knew the offices and royal documents and believed she could get responses to the right desks.

“I'm a proposer and a person affected by this risk. That doesn't prevent me delivering material.”

“No. But if you also decide which objections enter the common summary, we still have to persuade a princess first.”

Adrian noticed her right fingers tighten. This wasn't a calculated insult. Its reasonableness made it impossible to dismiss through anger.

She asked for a specific alternative. The delegate proposed that the independent liaison hall retain incoming documents and complete objections. Isabella could be one contact, not the sole selector of replies, signer of local support, or approver of publication. Staffing and costs must also be stated. Independent didn't mean free.

Isabella read the amendment. The small stretch of control she wanted was precisely the part she thought might make things move faster.

“I agree to resubmit on that basis.”

“You aren't approving our responsibilities here.”

She paused, then nodded. “Correct. I accept limits on my own role and ask the hall to consider undertaking the work.”

Nobody applauded. The recorder preserved the correction. The hall still needed to assess its capacity. A noble sentence didn't automatically supply someone to do the work.

An argument sounded in the corridor. The man with the bridge railing had been told an attachment was missing. If the damage was visible, why did he need a drawing? A delegate excused herself to address it. Isabella didn't demand that her larger matter be finished first.

Waiting, she asked Adrian whether she'd sounded humiliatingly bad.

“A little unhappy.”

“Only a little?”

“A conspicuous little.”

She laughed, then sobered. “I thought I'd learned not to order people.”

“You didn't order them today. Being shown another problem doesn't erase what you've already done.”

She looked at the line removing her sole control over summaries. His recognition wouldn't become a blanket covering the whole difficulty.

The delegate returned. A broken railing sample established that piece was damaged, not which part of the bridge remained passable. The missing item was a location drawing, not a new demonstration that the visitor deserved concern. Staff had directed him to the ordinary drawing desk. Compensation wasn't immediately approved.

Adrian remembered that distinction. Their model contained real problems too. Reality didn't eliminate the need to establish where they occurred.

The meeting produced no formal endorsements from three councils. One delegate would forward the limited research explanation with her questions. Another supplied a directory for applications to public maintenance records. The third wanted funding sources and responsibility after failure clarified before recommending anything. The hall would receive further documents through its existing system, not acquire kingdom-wide powers.

Isabella kept the three outcomes separate. The delegate withholding a recommendation didn't enter an obstruction list. Researchers' preferred pace and the people delegates answered to weren't always the same thing.

Leaving after noon, Isabella discovered she hadn't returned her queue token. Adrian accompanied her back. The attendant thanked her; they hadn't collected the tray yet. Discussing important matters didn't entitle them to leave small problems for others.

“I gave up more than I obtained,” she said.

“You obtained the correct return addresses.”

“You do select the inspiring parts.”

There was humor in her voice now. They stopped at a small shop displaying ordinary clasps manageable with one hand. She asked a price and permission to try one, not for the shopkeeper to redesign every wrist support because she found it useful.

Adrian held her coat while she tested it with her right hand. The first attempt sat crooked; the second aligned. Frowning, she warned that praising a princess for doing it herself would be seeking reward for something excessively ordinary.

“I was going to say the color clashes.”

“Then find a better color.”

He chose dark gray. She thought it resembled an academy tablecloth and bought her original blue choice. She had plainly rejected his opinion. Neither needed a letter explaining it.

At the academy, Elena asked whether research supplies were secured. Isabella handed over the page that withheld support and said no. Willingness to examine documents didn't mean local delegates would fund noble research equipment. Money already committed to bridges and relief couldn't simply be redirected.

Elena would invite several family financial representatives already in the city to discuss specific, shareable resources. She wouldn't commit every member of her house. First Adrian needed to separate what a trial actually required from everything they hoped to obtain, with quantities checked by works staff.

Luna received only the written summary that day. During her permitted short session, she insisted that research resources remain separate from resources for any eventual sharing of risk. Buying a few energy plates didn't fund an alternative system.

Adrian added that distinction and filed the three replies separately. The clerk asked whether progress should read local support obtained.

Isabella shook her head. “Write that we finally know whom we need to explain each thing to.”

She sat in the ordinary waiting area for an acknowledgment of her limited-contact application. Her blue clasp gleamed under the lamp. The powers crossed off the page stayed crossed off.
