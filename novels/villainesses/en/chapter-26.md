# Why Do All the Villainesses Want to Marry Me?
## Volume One: A Betrothal Written in Blood
### Chapter 26: The Prince's Second Move

On the morning of October sixth, the Rosenberg estate received a letter that smelled of absolutely nothing.

Adrian found this suspicious.

The last royal invitation had been so heavily perfumed he could practically smell it through the envelope. It had praised noble etiquette and the joys of courtly celebration in extravagant detail.

That evening, Prince Leon had publicly broken his engagement to Elena and nearly sent her to prison for treason.

This letter was plain.

Its contents were worse.

*His Highness Prince Leon requests a private discussion with Mr. Adrian Reed at the Royal Council Annex this afternoon, concerning historical case R-074 and the ongoing Rosenberg proceedings.*

Adrian read it.

Then looked down at his shoes.

Martha, who'd brought the letter, sighed.

"What's wrong with them now?"

"Checking whether they've developed an emergency escape route."

"They haven't," Elena said from behind him. "But you do have a lawyer this time."

Hale had already confirmed that the invitation was genuine. It came from the royal secretariat, not the court. It wasn't a summons, and they were under no obligation to attend.

"You may decline," he reminded Adrian.

Adrian folded the letter.

"I want to go."

Elena studied him.

"Why?"

"Leon knew my name before I reached the palace on September seventeenth. He asked about a scar he couldn't see. Now he's bringing up R-074. If I don't meet him, I might never find out how much he knows."

"Then I'm coming too."

Adrian almost refused out of habit.

He remembered the greenhouse.

He remembered promising not to call exclusion protection.

"If you'd like to," he said, "I'd be glad to go together."

Elena smiled faintly.

"You're learning."

"Could you promise one thing?"

"What?"

"No dancing tests today."

"Relax. I doubt the prince will ask you to dance."

"That's a relief."

"At least, not without warning."

He suspected she was enjoying this far too much.

The Royal Council Annex stood outside the palace's inner wall. No ceremonial troops lined its entrance. There were only clerks responsible for recording visitors.

At Hale's insistence, they chose a meeting room where the conversation could be formally logged. They declined the royal secretary's offer of a private chamber with no minutes.

Leon was already waiting.

He wore a dark coat rather than court finery.

There wasn't even a wineglass on the table.

At the sight of Elena, he stood.

"I invited Mr. Reed alone."

"I know," she said. "He accepted. That doesn't mean he isn't entitled to bring counsel—or that I can't attend with him."

Leon looked at Adrian.

"Do you need her beside you for everything now?"

"It was my decision to invite her."

The prince fell silent.

"You're much more coordinated than you were at the ball."

Adrian let the remark pass.

He was determined not to spend another official meeting discussing one accidental kiss.

Hale recorded everyone present and invited the prince to state the purpose of the talks.

"I want to put an end to this dispute," Leon said.

"Which dispute?" Elena asked.

"The accusation that you leaked the capital's defenses."

"Then ask your representatives to withdraw their unsupported allegations in writing."

Leon regarded her.

"You still won't give me an inch."

"I'm giving you the chance to explain why you accused me in the first place."

He didn't answer.

Instead, he took a bound agreement from a drawer and placed it in the middle of the table.

The cover read:

**Royal Investigative Cooperation and Confidentiality Agreement.**

The proposal was simple enough.

The Crown would withdraw its support for any immediate punishment of Elena, cease opposing an independent review of Evidence Item 714, and recommend that prosecution be deferred while the evidence was examined.

In exchange, House Rosenberg would surrender Matthew Holt's work notebook, the records concerning Temple transfers, and Matthew himself for what the document termed *protective questioning* by officials appointed by the royal household.

Adrian read the last clause twice.

Then slowly put the agreement down.

"Your Highness, if you want to end the dispute, why does the first step involve handing over a witness?"

"Because I need to know who's falsifying these files."

"Then Mr. Holt can answer questions in court."

"Not every secret can be discussed in open court."

"He's still a person. Not a parcel."

Leon's eyes cooled.

"Are you questioning the Crown's ability to protect its own citizens?"

"I'm questioning a document that gives the witness no right to refuse. He was threatened through his family yesterday. We cannot place him under the control of another group of officials without independent oversight and pretend that's protection."

Elena sat quietly beside Adrian.

She had no need to answer for him.

Leon leaned back.

"You already have the documents you need. Why insist on taking this public? If I promise that Elena will never face another charge from the Crown, isn't that what you wanted?"

"No."

The refusal came so quickly the prince looked startled.

"Haven't you spent all this time trying to save her?"

"Yes."

"Then I'm offering you the chance."

"You can withdraw a royal application. You cannot issue a ruling on behalf of the court. If the people forging evidence remain untouched, they can prepare another letter tomorrow and drag her into another hearing."

Leon's fingers stopped moving on the table.

Adrian continued.

"Matthew agreed to testify because he believed his words would be recorded accurately. If I trade away his freedom for a result I want, what makes me different from the people who locked him in a warehouse and tried to make him sign a false statement?"

The room fell silent.

Hale wrote the exchange down.

The royal secretary raised no objection to the wording.

Elena finally spoke.

"Your Highness, why does R-074 appear in transfers from the Royal Confidential Special Reserve?"

Leon looked up sharply.

"You found B-17?"

The answer escaped too quickly.

He knew it.

Adrian forced himself to remain calm.

"We know B-17 is a clearing reference used over several years. We're still verifying the ultimate destination and purpose."

Leon closed his eyes for a moment.

"I was told it covered the preservation of old disaster records. A way of preventing the records from becoming unstable."

"Who told you?" Elena asked.

"Advisers from the Temple and the royal archives committee. I didn't personally inspect every receipt."

"Did you know living people were named in those files?" Adrian asked.

Leon didn't answer at once.

Finally, he said, "I believed they were cases that had already been closed."

He reached into the desk and withdrew an old letter edged in red.

But he didn't hand it over.

"Before September seventeenth," he said, "I received a warning from the Temple's archives committee. It claimed a historical Rosenberg record was about to lapse, creating a possible threat to the capital."

Elena stared at him.

"So your response was to accuse me of treason?"

"I tried to persuade you to leave." Leon's voice was strained. "I thought if you left the capital, there would be time to verify the records without dragging you through a trial."

"You never told me why."

"The committee said the details couldn't be disclosed."

"But destroying my reputation in public was perfectly acceptable?"

Leon said nothing.

Adrian resisted the urge to rescue the prince from that silence.

Even if the warning was genuine, it didn't explain the duplicate sapphire case or the forged evidence.

And it didn't erase the harm Leon had caused.

"Could we at least enter that warning into the list of documents to be examined?" Adrian asked.

"The original is classified."

"Then record its filing number and date of receipt. An authorized independent reviewer can inspect it later. We don't need to publish its contents today."

Leon looked toward Hale.

"That is a valid procedural request," Hale said. "It does not mean we accept the letter's authenticity."

After a long hesitation, Leon allowed his secretary to register the reference number, with a note making clear that the letter's contents were still only the prince's account of events.

"You can request it. I can't guarantee you'll be granted access."

Elena copied the number without thanking him.

"You were my fiancé," she said quietly. "I thought that if I were in danger, you would at least tell me what the danger was. Instead, you listened to people who wouldn't show you their reasons and decided which city I should be allowed to stay in."

"I thought I was protecting you."

"Then you should have asked me."

Adrian lowered his eyes.

That particular mistake was painfully familiar.

Elena kept her gaze on the prince.

"I'm not going to forgive you simply because you've admitted you were afraid. But I'm not going to stop you from presenting evidence through the proper channels either."

Leon's expression shifted.

"I know forgiveness won't come easily."

Hale asked both sides to acknowledge that none of the prince's unverified statements could be treated as established fact in a later proceeding. The secretary added the limitation to the meeting minutes.

"We don't need a more convincing story," Adrian said. "We need a system where the next decision about someone's life can't rest on one person's assurance that everything is fine."

Leon watched him for a long time.

The hostility Adrian remembered from their first meeting had become a weariness he didn't quite understand.

Then the prince brought up the palace gate on September seventeenth.

"I did instruct the guards to watch for your name," he said. "But I didn't draw up that list myself."

"Who supplied it?" Adrian asked.

"The Temple's historical case liaison office."

"Was its delivery recorded?"

"The secretariat should have the entry."

Hale noted the answer for independent verification.

Adrian didn't immediately ask how the prince had known about his scar. Leon had only explained the source of a name on a list. That didn't prove he had ever seen the crescent mark.

They were separate questions.

For the first time, Adrian considered the possibility that Leon too had been waiting for a person described in someone else's carefully prepared documents.

The prince might have known some of the danger.

He might also have been deliberately kept ignorant of the rest.

Perhaps he'd wanted to protect himself.

Perhaps he'd genuinely believed that making Elena leave would save her.

Both motives could exist in the same man.

Neither relieved him of responsibility for his own accusations.

"Your Highness," Adrian said, "we can cooperate on reviewing R-074. But we won't purchase that cooperation with a witness's freedom."

"What terms would you accept?"

"A public evidentiary review supervised by an independent judge. The Crown should be free to present its evidence and defend its conduct. Matthew should retain counsel and the right to decline unauthorized questioning. His past employment in the archives isn't grounds to treat him as a criminal."

Leon stared.

"You'd even give me the chance to defend myself?"

"Of course."

"Why?"

"Because I never want to stand in the witness box and hear someone say, 'You dislike the prince, so he no longer deserves an opportunity to explain.'"

The prince fell silent again.

Elena looked at Adrian with an expression so unguardedly warm that he almost forgot what he was doing.

Hale took advantage of the pause to present a procedural proposal.

It asked the Crown to withdraw its support for punishment based on unverified evidence, accept a public review, and permit independent investigators to inspect relevant accounts under appropriate confidentiality restrictions.

It demanded no immediate confession from Leon.

It promised immunity to no one.

The prince read it carefully.

"I need time."

"Then take it," Adrian said. "But Matthew's safety cannot be a bargaining chip while you decide."

"I understand."

Leon sounded tired.

Just before the meeting ended, he asked Adrian one final question.

"If you learned that you'd done something unforgivable at the old courthouse, would you still insist on being allowed to defend yourself?"

The fingers of Adrian's left hand tightened.

"First I'd want to know whether I actually did it. If I did, I'd take responsibility. But I won't let an old file nobody is willing to produce decide what I'm supposed to remember."

Leon gave a slow nod.

The royal secretary collected the unsigned draft agreement.

As Adrian stood, he noticed a small gray-gold seal on the inside of its cover.

Not the royal gryphon.

A five-petaled flower with the curved mark used by senior records officers.

Beneath it was a faint line of text.

**Old Contract Records Office — Verification Liaison: Evelyn Vale.**

Adrian didn't make an accusation.

He asked Hale to request confirmation of the number assigned to the document displayed at the meeting.

The prince followed his glance.

Something changed in his face.

Outside the council annex, Elena asked, "Why didn't you demand that Leon apologize in front of everyone?"

"Humiliating him wouldn't prove you innocent."

"He did hurt me."

"I know. We'll still demand accountability for what he did. But if he's willing to help us find out what happened, I don't see the point in slamming every door before we've heard him out."

Elena watched him.

Then she smiled.

"Sometimes you're more difficult than my father."

"Because I'm so reasonable?"

"Because you're terrified of dying, yet you keep insisting that everyone deserves a chance to speak under fair rules."

Adrian laughed weakly.

"Well. It might keep me alive a few more days."

Their carriage rolled beyond the palace wall.

Hale sent over the public reference number for the proposed agreement, confirming that it had originated in the royal secretariat. Why it also carried a Temple records seal would require separate verification.

Adrian entered the number into his notebook.

The prince's second move hadn't forced them into another trap.

It had exposed something more troubling.

**Even the agreement meant to trade away a witness had arrived with a Temple records officer's seal already on it.**
