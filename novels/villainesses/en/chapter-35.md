# Why Do All the Villainesses Want to Marry Me?
## Volume Two: The Saint Won't Let Me Get Married
### Chapter 35: One Betrothal, Two Dates

On the morning of October fifteenth, Adrian Reed discovered he'd developed a deep and personal dislike of dates.

Last month, the wrong date on an evidence ledger had nearly got him hauled off by the Judicial Office.

Now a supposedly historical betrothal had produced two different ones.

Perhaps his true calling in life wasn't administrative work.

Perhaps he should become a calendar salesman.

"At least a calendar can't invent an engagement by itself," he muttered.

Elena pushed a bowl of warm porridge toward him.

"Eat. Did you dream about the old courtroom again?"

"No. I dreamed you and Cecilia were chasing me with calendars, asking when I was available."

She stopped stirring her tea.

"Did you answer?"

"I requested proper authorization first."

Elena laughed.

Martha, passing through the doorway, added with a perfectly straight face, "Then you've made progress, sir. Previously, you'd have tried to climb through a window."

After breakfast, they returned to Saint Rose Academy's document restoration room.

Yesterday's reading film had cooled and been sealed under independent supervision. Carter required two assistants to confirm the seal numbers before she placed the original test record on a transmitted-light board.

Cecilia looked a little paler than usual.

On the academy physician's advice, she would not supply any further magical energy today. Her role was to explain the old calendar system and review the interpretation.

Nora had invited a specialist in historical dating systems, an elderly professor with round glasses thick enough to magnify his eyes.

He opened the meeting with a warning.

"If anyone here expects me to announce that this document came from the future, I'll ask that person to leave."

Adrian sat up.

"I'm only here to understand what the dates mean."

"Excellent. Then we have a chance of getting somewhere."

The professor opened two reference books and a supplementary table.

Carter first explained what the controlled test had actually shown.

The darker line of resonance was associated with the judicial archive transfer mark.

The fainter line appeared beside the historical contract's witness field.

Both contained date-like symbols.

But the second did not use the contemporary calendar.

They began with the darker line.

Its format matched a transfer record dated September twentieth, seven years earlier.

This agreed with the judicial archive entries they'd already obtained.

"So we can say the document entered the judicial records seven years ago," Adrian ventured.

"Subject to verifying that the transfer stamp itself is authentic," Hale corrected. "The independent records support it, but we'll still need the original for a definitive materials examination."

Adrian nodded.

Then the professor magnified the second line.

Its characters were not written in ordinary regnal years.

They belonged to an obsolete cycle-based system.

The provisional reading was:

**Cycle Seven Hundred Twenty-One. Ninth Month. Seventeenth Day.**

Adrian looked up sharply.

September seventeenth.

The day he'd woken up in this world.

The day he'd accompanied Elena to the palace and found a future-dated evidence entry.

But what was Cycle Seven Hundred Twenty-One?

The professor noticed his reaction immediately.

"Don't jump ahead. That isn't proof the entry corresponds to September seventeenth of this year. The old notation records a cycle and ordinal day. It doesn't map directly onto our calendar."

"Can we at least tell whether it comes before or after the transfer date?" Elena asked.

"Not reliably. Several essential conversion tables are missing. Some sources use 'cycle' for a ritual sequence; others use it for repeating sections of a long calendar."

Nora spread out the academy's surviving conversion sheets.

"Under one interpretation, the second date would actually fall after the archive transfer," she said. "That would reverse the normal order of signing a contract and placing it in storage."

"Unless it was added later," Hale pointed out.

"Or unless we're looking at two different events recorded under one case reference," Carter said.

Adrian stared at the dates.

He remembered the old court's death registration under his name.

The burning crescent scar.

The bells he'd heard in a courtroom where no bell should have been ringing.

He wanted to ask whether somebody had truly rewritten the same fate twice.

But that wasn't what the test proved.

"What we can actually establish," he said slowly, "is that there are two date records from different sources, and the surviving tables don't let us reconcile them reliably."

The professor nodded.

"That's the correct conclusion."

To demonstrate the problem, he worked backward from the known judicial transfer date.

He entered September twentieth, seven years earlier, into three surviving old-calendar conversion systems.

They returned different cycle numbers.

"What does that tell us?" Adrian asked.

"That we don't get to choose the table producing the most exciting answer. Two were used for religious rites. One belonged to judicial registration. Their starting points differ."

Carter turned the test film over.

The darker resonance carried the fixed mark of a later judicial transfer.

The lighter one sat near an old witness seal.

For now, they couldn't reliably establish which mark had been applied first.

"Even if the lighter mark is later, it could be a supplementary notation," she said. "We need the ink layers on the original, or the witness register from the time."

Adrian wrote that down in the most prominent space on his page.

He wanted to know whether an earlier timeline existed.

He didn't want his desire for an answer to become the evidence for one.

Cecilia studied the old cycle symbols.

"The word 'cycle' was also used in some Temple doctrines to describe the start of a new registration sequence after a calamity-sharing procedure had been completed," she said. "But I only studied annotations. I've never examined a surviving case that used it this way."

"So it's possible the number refers to a procedure rather than an ordinary year?" Elena asked.

"Possible. Not established. I need to find the underlying regulations before I interpret it."

Hale passed Cecilia a blank expert-statement form.

"Would you document what you know, cite the sources, and identify what's missing?"

"Yes. I'll sign it."

Her handwriting was usually steady.

This time, Adrian noticed a faint tremor.

"Are you still feeling unwell?"

"My strength has returned," Cecilia said. "It's not that."

She paused.

"I used to believe the Temple's records faithfully reflected divine will. Now I'm being reminded that they are, first, documents created by people on particular days."

She didn't proclaim that she'd lost her faith.

Instead, she added a line to her statement.

*The interpretation supplied by the original records officer requires independent verification.*

Elena watched her.

"It's all right to be afraid."

Cecilia looked up and smiled a little.

"I know. But being afraid doesn't mean I should stop looking."

At lunch, she barely touched her bread.

Elena slid a small bowl of soup toward her.

"You look much the way Adrian did when he first saw his death entry."

"I'm afraid that if I discover the Temple made a mistake, I'll begin doubting the good I've seen people do there as well."

"Then separate the people from the acts," Elena said. "My father can be extraordinarily stubborn, but he can also admit when he was wrong. The Temple has people who genuinely care for the sick. It may also have people who use procedures to hurt others."

Adrian nodded.

"Leon made serious mistakes. I still didn't want the court to refuse him a chance to give evidence merely because I disliked him."

"Have the two of you always been able to think like this?" Cecilia asked.

"Not remotely," Adrian said. "The lessons involved almost getting executed."

Elena added, "We'd prefer not to pay the tuition twice."

It was a terrible joke.

All three laughed anyway.

Nora then found another complication.

Beside the lighter date field was a small archival symbol resembling a circle split in two.

She consulted the old sign glossary.

Two translations were possible.

*Signature pending.*

Or:

*Existing registration re-entered.*

"Those mean very different things," Adrian said.

"Which is why we need the original seal plate," Nora replied. "The transferred image doesn't tell us whether a stroke was lost or deliberately omitted."

It was a frustrating conclusion.

It was also safer than pretending uncertainty had vanished.

If the second reading proved correct, the historical contract might have been a later entry referring to something already registered.

But that still wouldn't prove that Adrian and Cecilia had signed the same agreement in multiple timelines.

The paperwork couldn't simply substitute for missing memories.

That afternoon, the group prepared a joint examination report.

Hale required a source after every material conclusion: the judicial transfer register, the laboratory's test record, the surviving academy calendar tables, and the names of independent witnesses.

He changed one proposed heading from *Abnormal Dual Timeline* to:

*Two Date Marks from Different Sources, Not Yet Reliably Reconciled.*

Adrian sighed when he saw it.

"That title isn't exciting at all."

"It's a technical report," Hale replied. "Not a novel."

"I know. It's just three times as long as any newspaper headline."

"A little more length is preferable to making the court think we've established that time itself was reset."

Elena added a statement confirming that yesterday's test had examined only a certified archival medium, not either participant's body.

Cecilia checked it and signed.

Adrian signed as well.

Their names sat side by side on another sheet of paper.

This time, however, the heading said:

*Agreement to Disclosure of Verifiable Procedure Records.*

Not *betrothal completed*.

"This document doesn't frighten me," Adrian said.

"Because it describes what we actually consented to," Cecilia replied.

Elena accepted the sheet with a satisfied nod.

At the next table, she and Cecilia worked through another conversion book together.

They still disagreed about particular symbols.

They no longer treated a difference of opinion as grounds to stop cooperating.

Adrian watched them for a moment.

"That's a rare sight."

Nora looked over his shoulder.

"What is?"

"They aren't arguing about my engagement."

"Only temporarily." Nora grinned. "Once you sort out the date problem, your calendar will probably be very busy again."

Adrian sighed.

"I knew I should have sold calendars."

Both women turned.

"Why?" Cecilia asked.

"Because I'm maintaining three appointment books, and I still don't get to decide when I take a day off."

Elena slid his schedule across the table.

"Then you decide today."

Adrian examined it seriously.

"First we send the report to Hale. Then dinner and rest. Tomorrow morning, I'd like to hear both of you explain the difference between the old calendar systems. Anything else can wait until we know what we're authorized to see."

Cecilia nodded.

Elena nodded.

For once, he had successfully taken control of a calendar.

As they were packing up the laboratory, a porter arrived with a gray-gold-sealed letter.

It wasn't addressed to Adrian.

It was addressed to Cecilia Saintclair.

She opened it, read the first few lines, and lost her smile.

The document came from the Temple's internal oversight office.

The operative paragraph stated:

**"Irregularities involving dual date registration in case R-074 are temporarily classified for restricted circulation. Speculative interpretations shall not be disclosed publicly before headquarters review."**

Cecilia looked toward Hale.

"This doesn't revoke my authority to investigate."

"No," he said. "But we should examine exactly what it restricts."

Adrian glanced at the letter.

"What will you do?"

"First identify what the Temple can legitimately protect as confidential original material—and what the people named in the file are entitled to know about themselves."

She placed the notice on the table so Hale could record its delivery time.

"If someone uses confidentiality as an excuse to conceal wrongdoing, I'll challenge the instruction formally."

Elena looked at the two unresolved dates.

"It seems the Temple is in a greater hurry than we are to decide who may learn about them."

Cecilia didn't answer immediately.

She rested one hand over her independent investigator's badge.

Her expression was more resolute than Adrian had ever seen it.

Adrian looked down at his left hand.

The scar gave no new light.

No prophecy.

No convenient answer.

Tomorrow, they would no longer be dealing solely with an absurd betrothal nobody could explain.

They would be dealing with the people deciding how much of the evidence anyone was allowed to see.
