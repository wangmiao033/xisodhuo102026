# Chapter 213: The Register Had Never Asked Her

On March 12, Elena spread jam along the edge of her plate.

Adrian said nothing. Halfway through her toast, she noticed the red streak and looked up.

“Waiting to see when I start eating the crockery?”

“I was waiting for you to give it an expensive name.”

“A Rosenberg mistake.” She wiped it away. “No additional charge.”

He laughed. The relief didn't last. Her hands were steady, but her second slice remained untouched. Today they would read the noble volume still sealed inside its wrapper yesterday. Even when the carriage stopped, he hadn't worked out how to accompany someone to a list that might describe her death.

The ground-floor reading room smelled of fresh coal. The custodian presented the sealed item, and the examiner checked yesterday's receipt and seals. Today's permission covered Elena's associated entry and the rules explaining its columns. Other people's addresses and family details would remain covered. Further connections could support another application; reading didn't authorize publishing the whole book.

“May I stop at any page?” Elena asked.

“We'll record where you stop. We won't choose the next page for you.”

She turned to Adrian. “Stay. I may say unpleasant things. They won't necessarily be addressed to you.”

“I'll ask if I can't tell.”

“Not after every sentence.”

Her first real smile today. She drew her chair closer. Only after she sat did the conservator open the wrapper. The thin leather cover read NOBLE BURDEN REGISTER. A worn character after the title remained illegible.

The first rule was clear enough.

Listed persons must first be deprived of family and status protections. Once charges were formally entered, they were to be delivered to the receiving institution. The following sentence described a potentially fatal disposition. It didn't call the outcome natural death. Nor did it mention volunteering.

Adrian almost moved his chair away to give Elena room. He stopped. He didn't know whether she wanted space or someone beside her.

“This burden,” she asked. “Not a payment?”

The custodian displayed the definitions. Property assessments belonged in a separate financial column. Here, completion required delivery of the person. Closing an entry didn't require that person's survival. A red rule distinguished the two accounts with dreadful neatness.

The room was warm. Adrian could almost hear underground water dripping.

Before looking for names, Elena requested confirmation that the title and reference matched the outer directory. The inner accession slip, sewn page count, and separately preserved transfer inventory agreed. That supported identification of yesterday's volume, the examiner said, not the truth of every accusation inside it.

“Especially not that,” Elena said.

The conservator turned to the permitted entry.

Elena Rosenberg.

No portrait. Her house, status, and registered mark category appeared beside her name. Below them lay a September 20 disposition field and a treason allegation she knew too well. The examiner didn't impose a current year on the record's older chronology. Its wording and original case reference nevertheless matched retained papers from the withdrawn prosecution closely enough to associate this entry with the plan against her.

The charges hadn't been filled in first.

An earlier intake batch appeared beside her name. The charge column instructed the writer to supply the required judicial wording afterward. An attachment retained two alternatives: one if her family offered surety, another if they refused. Both led to removal of protection. Only the explanation changed.

Elena gripped a fold of her skirt.

“They weren't waiting for me to do something wrong.”

Nobody rushed to answer.

“They chose where I should end up. Then they waited for a sentence that could send me there.”

Adrian didn't say he'd known. He'd known a game's ending and a future execution notice. Some part of him had still imagined that defeating one false charge would free her from that position. These alternatives dismantled the comfort. With family backing or without it, someone had prepared a reason.

The examiner brought forward the authorized judicial comparison copy. An unusual inverted phrase matched the proposed wording; so did the subsidiary case reference. The two documents had arrived from separate repositories. Cross-references within one volume alone wouldn't have supplied independent corroboration. Who had written the final allegation, and who had carried it to the Judicial Office, still required investigation.

“This supports the charge being prepared to serve delivery,” the examiner said. “Not identical knowledge on the part of everyone involved.”

Elena nodded, studying the surety column. She knew how elegant such assurances sounded, how her father's title made gatekeepers step aside. Someone had made that familiarity useful. They'd prepared another route for anyone who refused it.

“Show me the acknowledgment.”

There was no consent from her. The custodian explained that RECEIVED meant the implementing office had accepted the list, not that Elena had accepted her fate. A later abstract crowded both ideas into one line. The original kept them separate.

Her shoulders shifted as though she'd finally exhaled.

“Copy that in full. Don't write that I accepted the burden.”

The clerk obeyed. Adrian didn't reach to sign for her. Elena's words occupied half a line, meeting an entire page someone else had written about her.

Other names lay beneath privacy covers. Were all those people dead?

Being listed wasn't proof of execution, the custodian answered. Some fields were blank. Others had receipt references. Their closing records weren't available today.

“Then don't call it a list of the dead,” Elena said. “And don't erase sacrifice because someone might still be alive.”

After a pause, Adrian offered a walk in the corridor. She shook her head, then changed her mind. One more question first.

A family-assistance attachment referenced Rosenberg repair expenditure. Its cross-reference could be checked against the gallery work examined on the ninth. That didn't establish that its authorizer knew this page's contents. Equally, Elena's inclusion couldn't prove that her house had never assisted the institution. She requested separate financial examination, without adding because she was also a victim.

The family representative hesitated. Could they first pursue the expenditure privately? Once word of this book spread, people might not distinguish a listed person from a house funding repairs.

Elena didn't rebuke him. That was a genuine cost she might face, not a concern only a villain would raise.

“Can you guarantee private inquiries won't keep this connection from the other investigators?”

He couldn't. He could seek a fuller family explanation, not dictate the external investigation's schedule.

“Then don't block it. Send the reference to the office already examining it. Keep private addresses protected.”

She needn't carry an entire surname, he began.

She wasn't, she said. Investigating a specific payment meant refusing to let a whole surname shelter particular people.

He folded away the private statement prepared for her signature. His attendant had heard. Their account at home might not please her. Watching them leave, she looked paler than when she'd read her name.

“Include that decision in your own extract?” Adrian asked.

“Yes. Not that I was brave. Where I asked for it to go. If I regret it later, I shouldn't be able to claim I decided nothing.”

Then Adrian opened the corridor door for her.

A plant with yellowing leaf tips stood on the windowsill. After a long silence, Elena asked whether he thought she'd been admirably composed.

“I don't know. Do you want me to?”

“No. I want to swear.”

“That plant has probably heard worse.”

She laughed once. Her eyes reddened. The absence of tears made the moment no easier. When she'd seen the alternatives, she'd immediately calculated which accusation would be easier to defeat, as though identifying one badly written sentence might save her from fearing the rest.

“You don't have to improve their writing today.”

“I know. I'll still think about it.”

He nodded. She offered her hand and waited until he reached for it. Someone passed carrying water. They moved half a step toward the wall without letting go.

“I wanted to cover the page,” Adrian admitted. “Keep you from seeing it first.”

“And then?”

“Then I realized I didn't even know whether you wanted me closer.”

“You can come closer now. Avoid my shoes.”

He did so very carefully. Her next smile looked like her. Holding hands hadn't repaired everything between them. Today, at least, fear hadn't become a decision made on the other's behalf.

For lunch Elena chose a corner pie shop instead of the family sitting room prepared for her. Only one table remained free. Steam clouded the window and hid half the Temple roof across the street.

The owner set down two pies. Did they want them cut?

Elena declined, discovered the filling was hotter than expected, and shut her mouth firmly after one bite. Adrian politely studied the view.

“You may laugh,” she managed.

“I'm admiring the architecture.”

“There's half a chimney.”

He laughed. So did she, though she didn't finish the pie. She asked for the remainder wrapped. It would keep until evening; proving an appetite wasn't worth burning herself. No noble title could order steam to cool. That ordinary disobedience seemed to please her.

Cecilia arrived at the agreed time and listened to what Elena chose to share. She didn't demand the pages containing other people's details.

“The attachment cites an oracle,” Elena said. “It says the listed people are born owing restitution. There's a liturgical reference. You may examine the quotation associated with me. You may not use my name to supply the consent it lacks.”

“I won't.”

Cecilia read the reference twice. The words commonly described as ancient revelation didn't end that way in the authorized prayers she remembered. She made no immediate declaration of forgery. Instead, she asked the examiner to seek limited access to the associated drafting papers and proclaimed version.

Outside, a vegetable cart caught a wheel between paving stones. The shopkeeper lifted its front while Adrian moved an empty basket out of the way. The cart went on. Not every strained voice needed a prophecy to answer it.

When he returned, Elena had packed her own remaining pie. Could they continue the story of the three swordsmen tonight? Preferably not the part where somebody hurried to die over a final message.

“I'll check the contents.”

“No secretly tearing out the page.”

“We can openly skip it.”

“We can.”

She sounded tired, not rescued. He was glad she had asked him to come.

By evening, the limited document request had secured a place for the following morning. The repository resealed the noble volume. Today's extract retained the lack of personal consent, the unverified outcomes, and the separate inquiry into family expenditure. Underground, the core door remained shut. None of the five mark locks had moved.

Elena put her own copy into her folder.

People had written a place for her without her knowledge. She couldn't make their book disappear from history today.

But she had read it.

And afterward, she still had an evening to spend as she chose.
