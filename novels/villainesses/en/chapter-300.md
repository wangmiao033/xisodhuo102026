# Chapter 300: This Time, Take My Hand

On the morning of July 13, Adrian studied the mirror and decided his tie bore him no ill will.

Martha said that was because he had undone his first attempt. She set down breakfast. A groom could not survive until noon on nerves.

“I thought eating was optional on your wedding day.”

“Then find a wedding that serves breakfast.”

He ate half a slice of bread. When Elena did not appear, he began to rise. Martha reminded him that she was packing in another room, not missing. He need not check whether she had changed her mind.

He touched the small ring box without opening it to count again. Martha moved the jam closer and warned him about his sleeve. He had never imagined that a day he wanted so much would first require him to negotiate a dish of jam.

He sat again. Elena entered to find a man chewing with exceptional concentration.

Her dress did not occupy an entire staircase. The hem let her walk unaided; she had chosen the pin in her hair. He looked long enough for her to ask whether something was wrong.

“I'm trying not to sound rehearsed.”

“You could admit you rehearsed.”

“You're beautiful. And I did rehearse.”

She laughed, inspecting his tie. “You'll survive the morning.”

No servant announced an auspicious hour. They checked their documents and rings and left for their appointment. Martha accompanied them as an invited witness; an ordinary registry witness would meet them there. Attendance did not determine two adults' choice.

Duke Edmund Rosenberg had sent a short letter. It imposed no condition about withdrawing Elena's statement, and a gift would not end the family's accountability. She read it and put it aside. She could accept a limited kindness without pretending all their disagreements were resolved.

Outside the registry, another couple argued about a surname's spelling. Adrian checked his form.

“Discovered an extra letter in Reed?” Elena asked.

“I'm developing sympathy for everyone who's misspelled their own name.”

“Don't demonstrate today.”

The registrar verified their identities, present marital status, and application. The historical index qualification appeared on its first page. Cecilia need not attend; nobody required five women's signatures to release him. The record described the past, not an occupied present-day place.

They confirmed their intentions separately. While Elena spoke at another desk, Adrian waited by the window, hearing an apology outside for misdelivered flowers. Her right to answer included doing so without him watching.

His questions were straightforward. Was this his choice? Did he understand that marriage was not an unlimited assignment of his body, property, or calamity burden? Did he understand the independent procedures for disputes and dissolution, rather than allowing a noble or the Temple to decide for them?

He answered and asked to say something of his own at their joint confirmation.

“Certainly. Please don't introduce an entire technical annex.”

“I haven't brought one.” He paused. “Really.”

Across the room, Elena smiled.

They stood together before the desk. Through a partly opened window came wheels on the street, mingling with turning paper. This was not a silence that abolished disagreement. It was a place where they could choose without hiding.

“Elena Rosenberg, I love you. I want to marry you, not because an old record requires it, or because it buys a way to survive. I want a life with you. Arguments about curtains. Remembering bread. Showing up when I've promised. When I'm frightened or wrong, I'll tell you instead of deciding your life first.”

Her eyes reddened. She took her time answering.

“Adrian Reed, I love you. I want to marry you. I'll still have my work, my impatience, and times when I need reminding. I can't promise to guess everything correctly. I promise to ask and listen, and not use your own good as an excuse to skip your answer.”

She smiled. “The curtains are settled. You may not change them back to gray.”

Martha laughed softly. Adrian agreed, laughing too.

They each gave their present, voluntary confirmation and signed. The registrar checked and certified the entry, formally recording their one current marriage and providing each with an authenticated certificate. No holy light fell on the seal. No distant bridge became maintenance-free because they loved each other.

They exchanged rings. Adrian's crescent scar showed in the sunlight, quietly supplying no permission. Elena slipped the band onto his finger and asked to kiss him. He said yes and bent toward her.

No skirt caught beneath a shoe. No misunderstanding followed. He did not need to shout an explanation beyond the palace walls.

Lunch accommodated the people they had invited who were free to come. Martha sat down to food she had not prepared and offered an honest opinion. Elena thought the soup too salty. Adrian liked the bread. Nobody had to praise every dish because it was a wedding.

The other four women had their own plans. Viola sent a brief good wish under her own name, not a protective oath; their private teas remained unscheduled. Luna's assistant delivered the institute's completed public explanation. Her note to Elena mentioned the revision that made it easier for ordinary readers, without attaching a reading invitation. Isabella sent a card by ordinary messenger, lending no royal seal to the marriage. Cecilia wrote no blessing. Yesterday, she had said she would keep her own shift today.

Elena put the messages away. Absence was not punishment, and a card did not prove that every sadness had ended. They were alive, with lives worth continuing. None owed the bride an identical performance of generosity.

That afternoon, the couple returned to their chosen home. The shortened blue curtains hung above the mint tray. There were too few chairs for every guest, but each of them had a worktable.

The first box of books had gone to the wrong place. The carrier had piled both people's belongings beside the larger table. Elena opened a book, found Adrian's notes, and stopped reading.

“Your notebook.”

“Could it go on my table? I'd still like some things of my own.”

“Of course. My letters are in that box. Marriage doesn't mean emptying two drawers into one.”

The carrier looked embarrassed. Adrian admitted he had labeled the boxes badly. They sorted them together, and he paid the agreed delivery fee. Wedding confusion was not a reason to make someone work for nothing.

Once the door closed, Elena rolled up a sleeve to wipe the dusty table. Adrian suggested sitting first. She looked at the cloth and laughed.

“Not half a day married, and we're negotiating who rests first.”

“A promising start. I vote we both sit.”

They shared the bread left from breakfast amid the unpacked belongings. Neither had to remain permanently on their feet caring for the other to deserve this home.

Adrian put their marriage certificates in the agreed drawer, not beneath the historical case files. Elena found his travel book and asked whether he wanted it on his desk.

“The shelf, for now. Today I'd rather hear you.”

“You've heard me all morning.”

“I intend to listen for a long time.”

She blushed, accusing him of rehearsing that more thoroughly than his vows. He hadn't. He received another expressly welcomed kiss.

Before leaving, they collected the palace facilities office's short visiting permit, issued through an ordinary application. It was neither a favor from a newly empowered Isabella nor permission to open every restricted door. The original ballroom had no event today. They could enter for the stated period and leave when staff closed it.

At the gate, Adrian gave their names without claiming to be transporting evidence. Elena touched his sleeve, apparently remembering that September 17 too.

There was no orchestra. Afternoon light crossed the floor from high windows. The golden pillar remained. A cleaning cart stood beside the side door. The registration table they had struggled to see was gone; an ordinary room need not remain unchanged for their memories.

At the dance floor's edge, he looked toward the door, then at her.

“I thought remembering the next step in the game would get you out of the ending.”

“You couldn't remember your own next step.”

He laughed. This time they meant his feet, not distant destinies awaiting correction.

She asked whether thinking about the old cycles still made him feel borrowed for a purpose.

“No. Those return records show I'd come before and tried to stop the sacrifices. My memory was stripped. Real history left the projection I thought I'd played as *Crown of Fate*. The scar kept the connection, not every answer.”

He looked at his left hand. It no longer needed to reach toward an apparatus.

“I didn't come because anyone owed me five romances. You weren't designated because you were villainesses. People changed the bearing positions, then used accusations to force people into them. In earlier cycles, people used betrothals to arrange cooperation and shared risks. Those relationships and agreements survived in recording media that carried traces across resets. Later, institutions used the old registrations as proof of present relationships and designated obligations. Not every preserved choice had been free, and none was a vow binding today. I can finally separate those three things.”

He knew neither the total number of cycles nor every dead person's name. Those remaining blanks did not leave the central questions unanswered. His old mistakes remained his to face. Today's marriage required no one else as compensation.

Elena said a wedding would not erase the historical version of herself handing him to the court. A happy ending did not require him to declare every fear worthwhile.

“I know. Choosing you doesn't mean ruling that the past never happened. I love the woman who'll face it with me, and despise my gray curtains.”

She laughed and lifted her hem slightly. Would he like a few steps?

Without music, they counted. Left foot. Pause. Turn. He did not watch the side door or use his partner to hide someone else's view. She did not need a dance to prove that a prince had failed to discard her.

After a few steps, he checked he wasn't standing on her skirt. She understood immediately.

“You aren't planning to repeat the accident?”

“I know I can ask now.”

“A late but useful improvement.”

He asked to kiss her. She agreed, provided his shoes no longer counted his brain among their enemies. Laughing, they drew together. They began the brief kiss themselves, and ended it themselves.

A staff member reminded them their time was nearly over. They requested no exception for newlyweds, straightened their clothes, and offered thanks. On their way to the door, Elena asked what he wanted for breakfast tomorrow.

“Bread. We could buy it together, if you want.”

“I do. But I have work in the morning. We can't go far.”

“The corner shop, then.”

Such a small future no longer seemed unworthy of everything they had survived. They could arrange a real morning, knowing the other had things of their own to do, and still wanting the company. That was enough.

Before the door that had once opened onto gossip and accusations, Adrian paused and held out his hand.

“This time, take my hand.”

Elena placed hers in his and closed her fingers by choice.

They walked out together.
