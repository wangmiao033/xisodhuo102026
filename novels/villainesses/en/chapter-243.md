# Chapter 243: Not Her Carriage

On the morning of April 10, Elena put her request for transport on a tea table worth more than the letter.

The steward saw her seal and immediately promised two carriages. His master would certainly be generous when the lady had come herself. Instead of taking the offered pen, Elena asked where his master was.

“In the country until evening. But I can arrange this.”

“The vehicles. Can you agree on the drivers' work for them too?”

His smile diminished. He opened the duty book rather than repeat the offer. The young driver outside had not complained or attempted to contradict his employer. He had simply put his food away, as though a visitor with a seal had settled the matter already. Elena waited for the steward to find the entries. The supposedly idle carriages were listed in the book. One would take kitchen staff to the market that afternoon. The other's rear axle hadn't been repaired. Outside, a young driver quietly pushed his lunch parcel back into his pocket.

Adrian had expected Elena to use her family name to make difficulties disappear. She pushed the easiest answer back across the table.

“Then let's discuss the carriage that actually works. And when it returns.”

The driver was called in. The steward was unused to someone arriving with an urgent request and then insisting that every hour still needed an explanation. The young man looked at him before explaining that if the kitchen staff carried their purchases home, he could go straight to the riverside.

“Could they carry them?” Elena asked.

Two barrels of oil. No.

Adrian remembered the old man's spilled beans. Saving one load by making another ordinary job vanish only moved the weight out of sight. He proposed dispatching relief supplies after the market trip and checking whether the recipients could wait. He wouldn't decide for them.

While they awaited a reply, the steward admitted another concern. His master might lend storage, but didn't want academy workers entering at will. The last borrowers had moved paintings off the walls, saying noble display was inappropriate.

Elena had thought they cared only about appearances. She turned the proposal over.

“You retain the keys. We use the approved space, don't dismantle anything, and don't put people in private rooms. More space requires another request.”

“Even in an emergency?”

“Actual danger follows existing rescue procedures. Inconvenience isn't automatically an emergency.”

She neither valued paintings above lives nor used lives to claim the entire house in advance. The steward fetched a floor plan. For the first time, he wasn't looking only at her seal.

The next household declined transport but offered an empty street-level shop. Before Adrian could accept, Elena asked about the doorway, leaking roof, and former tenant. The shop was empty because its tenant awaited roof repairs. Her lease hadn't ended.

The shoemaker arrived with leather dye on her hands. She would allow blankets in front, not another removal of her child's bed. Elena measured a space clear of the living area and asked the receiving staff whether it was useful. The owner's permission wasn't the tenant's answer.

“Have you always borrowed things this way?” the woman asked, noticing dust on Elena's skirt.

“Sometimes I didn't ask the right people.”

Adrian didn't supply but she always meant well. The shoemaker drew a chalk line: this side, not beyond.

Before noon, household representatives gathered in an ordinary sitting room. There was no grand national map. Three points could actually connect today: a paid cart with defined hours, the shop's front half, and a dry warehouse compartment available for two days. Generosity needed someone to open the next door.

A representative suggested Rosenberg flags everywhere. Elena agreed to clear collection signs, not to turning others' contributions into her family's charity. Another agent asked why his master should pay without a prominent crest.

“Name the actual donors. Don't require people collecting blankets to cheer.”

The man said nothing for a while. Elena left him time to consider the distinction instead of forcing a gracious answer before the others. He could retain credit for what his household genuinely gave; that did not require possession of the people receiving it. At last he withdrew a clause requiring recipients' names as keepsakes for his master. Verified donation totals could be public; private hardships would not. Negotiation hadn't made everyone noble. It had kept a few harmful conditions outside.

The riverside replied that existing stock would last until the evening delivery, provided further delays were reported. Adrian told the driver personally. The young man asked whether he must also sign the academy's contract.

“This is paid transport, not a risk-bearing trial.”

“I can't read the long words. I didn't want both places counting my answer.”

The registrar read the short job description aloud and let him explain it back. Exclusion of trial participation was written in the appropriate place. Payment came from funds actually committed and received for transport, not an imaginary solution to the old shortfall. Difficulty reading wasn't recorded as a reason to distrust him.

At lunch Elena was still checking a key handover. Adrian tapped the table.

“Nearly finished,” she said without looking up.

“Yesterday you wouldn't let me substitute nearly for eating.”

“Today I've discovered how irritating that rule is.”

He put bread clear of the papers and offered to check the last quantities. She explained which column meant received and which meant promised. Not distrust; those were the two their anxiety would most easily merge.

Only half the offered warehouse compartment was empty. Furniture occupied the rest. Instead of ordering it thrown out, Elena accepted the space available and asked about alternatives. Storage shrank; delivery became two trips. Her bread was cold before she finished, but she didn't describe the cost as deliberate obstruction.

Before the first cart left, a driver found damp wrapping on blankets. The donor insisted they'd been washed yesterday, not left to mildew. Inspection found rain on the outer layer. They still needed drying before storage. Two bundles stayed behind; available dry stock replaced some, and the actual shortfall went with the delivery notice.

Adrian wanted to recall the messenger and correct the handsome total. Elena notified the receiving point first.

“Tell the people affected before making our accounts look right.”

That small choice corrected a suspicion he'd kept to himself: that she might turn rescue into a competition in noble appearances.

After the cart departed, he admitted it. Elena leaned against the doorway.

“Did you think I'd always need the finest carriage to prove who I was?”

“I was afraid you'd feel only something enormous could convince people you'd changed.”

“I do want them convinced. But the shoemaker's child doesn't owe me that proof.”

She didn't demand he never doubt her again. He didn't compensate with you're always right. Next time, he would ask what she intended. At least, she replied, that might spare half a day guessing which document made him frown.

At dusk the warehouse sent an actual receipt. The driver returned late. A high threshold absent from the sketch had slowed unloading. Nobody was hurt; a suitable ramp had helped, but it couldn't depend on a last-minute loan every trip. Elena held tomorrow's departure time until a ramp and receiving attendant were secured. They were building a route that could work again, not arranging a completed victory picture.

During the key count, the shoemaker's child ran forward. Someone wanted to remove the rear curtain to admit more light. The servant had already reached up but stopped at the child's voice. He'd assumed borrowing the shop included rearranging it.

Elena asked the receiving attendant to produce the boundary sketch, then let the shoemaker explain what lay behind the curtain: space to change clothes and settle her child, not a storeroom.

The servant lowered his hand. Where could they put the table without blocking the light?

Adrian helped him turn it. The doorway lamp lit only half; the writer sat there while recipients waited on the other side. Seeing the curtain stay, the child returned to his mother.

Before leaving, the servant told the next shift that the rear room wasn't storage. Elena replaced her intended note, improve explanation, with the specific handover that had occurred. One visit couldn't make everyone arriving later know the same boundary.

By the last representative's departure, each of the three receiving points had a person who could be reached. Unanswered households weren't counted. The broken carriage remained under repair. The network was small enough for Adrian to say which bundle had passed through which door. Tomorrow's availability still needed those actual people, not an answer supplied on their behalf.

Night coverage used a separately arranged service shift, not donors' servants compelled to stay. Elena and Adrian remained only until the agreed handover, sitting by the shop's front door while the shoemaker put away her tools. Her child slept beyond the chalk line. Nobody calculated how many more people might fit in that bed.

“For a moment today I wanted to say I could get every carriage,” Elena admitted.

“When?”

“When the first steward decided I was difficult. I was afraid admitting I couldn't borrow one would leave me with nothing but a grand surname.”

Adrian had wanted to call every refusal brave rather than acknowledge that progress really was slow. Yet waiting for a carriage that would arrive wasn't the same as promising ten without drivers.

“What if tomorrow isn't enough?” she asked.

“We say it isn't enough. Then look for another carriage. We don't write you down as one.”

She frowned, then laughed, shoulders shaking.

“That may be the least suitable proposal I've ever heard.”

“Fortunately I'm explaining transport.”

“Keep doing that. Your marriage application is nowhere near ready.”

He laughed and held the hand she offered without converting the joke into an agreement. The conversation had given him neither a date nor a contract. What it gave him was Elena beside a doorway, admitting that being refused frightened her too. He could stay close without pretending her fear had disappeared, or turning his own relief into an answer about the rest of their lives. The actual replacement attendant arrived. They listened to the handover together before leaving.

Beside the new aid notice, someone asked whether nobles moving blankets meant disaster would strike tomorrow. Another wanted to know whether accepting one meant joining the academy's contract.

Elena stopped before they reached the dimly lit, quiet street. The question was not absurd to someone who had watched supplies move without hearing the conversations that arranged them. A cart could arrive honestly, every bundle counted, and still carry a frightening meaning nobody had intended. Vehicles and storerooms had begun connecting. People's understanding of the danger remained far apart.

Adrian asked the attendant to retain the actual questions for Cecilia's public explanation. Tomorrow they needed more than another carriage. People collecting blankets needed to hear that their names hadn't thereby become debts on somebody else's form.
