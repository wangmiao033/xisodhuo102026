# Why Do All the Villainesses Want to Marry Me?
## Volume One: A Betrothal Written in Blood
### Chapter 3: Why Does the Prince Know My Name?

When the palace guard announced that Prince Leon wanted to be informed the moment Adrian Reed appeared, Adrian's first instinct was to turn around and go home.

His second was to fake an illness.

His third was to fake an illness *and then* go home.

Unfortunately, Elena didn't seem inclined to let him do any of those things.

"Please tell His Highness," she said through the carriage window, "that since Mr. Reed has already arrived, he won't be standing at the gate waiting for a reply."

The guard paled.

"My lady, that wasn't what I—"

"Then let us through."

Only after the carriage passed the second iron gate did Adrian dare ask, "Aren't you worried he'll have me arrested?"

"Yes."

Adrian stared at her.

"Yes?"

"You're my attendant tonight." Elena straightened the edge of her glove. "If he wants to take you away, he'll have to give a reason in front of his guests."

"What if the reason is that I look suspicious?"

"Then I suppose we'll ask who you look like."

Her eyes flicked toward his left hand.

Adrian tucked it behind his back.

"My lady, could you occasionally pretend you haven't noticed I'm terrified?"

"Certainly. As soon as you stop touching your hand every thirty seconds."

He gave up.

The carriage stopped at the palace steps.

Elena got out first. A moment later, she heard a small, strangled noise behind her.

Adrian had caught the hem of his tailcoat under his shoe.

"See?" he said after recovering his balance. "This suit's been trying to kill me since we left the house."

"Then I suggest you don't let it join forces with the prince."

She brushed a speck of dust from his sleeve.

The movement was so quick he might have imagined it. When a reception officer glanced their way, Elena withdrew her hand and resumed the cool composure expected of a duke's daughter.

"Come along."

The ballroom was much larger than its background illustration.

Three tiers of chandeliers hung beneath a painted dome. Musicians played behind a gilded rail. Nobles clustered in bright circles of silk and velvet, their conversation rising and falling above the music.

Adrian had visited this room dozens of times on a computer screen.

Back then, the servants in the corners never moved, and every noble repeated one of three lines.

Now he could hear the clink of a glass being set down. He could see a young maid subtly flexing her aching wrist under the weight of a silver tray.

These people were no longer background art.

Worse, he had absolutely no idea where he was supposed to stand.

Too close to Elena, and someone might mistake him for her escort.

Too far away, and he wouldn't see if she needed him.

"What are you doing over there?" she asked.

"Determining the appropriate distance between a noble lady and her attendant."

"And your findings?"

"Insufficient data."

"Stand here, then."

She indicated a spot half a pace behind her shoulder.

Two passing noblewomen glanced at him.

"The Rosenbergs have hired a rather young attendant."

"I hear Lady Elena doesn't trust her older staff as much these days."

Adrian glanced at Elena.

She gave no sign of hearing them.

"Let's check the side entrance first," she murmured. "And don't stare."

He nodded.

"Especially not with that expression."

"What expression?"

"Like you've just discovered buried treasure."

He made a determined effort to flatten his mouth.

Elena hid a smile behind her fan.

Then the conversation around them grew quieter.

"Elena."

A familiar, pleasant male voice came from ahead.

Prince Leon approached with a glass of wine in one hand, two protocol officers following at a discreet distance.

He was almost identical to his character portrait: pale blond hair, blue eyes, and a white formal coat with gold embroidery bearing the royal crest.

Adrian could only think of the execution scene.

This was the man who had stood before the court and pronounced Elena Rosenberg a traitor.

"Your Highness." Elena dipped into a precise curtsy.

"That dress suits you," Leon said. "I hope moving the ball forward hasn't inconvenienced you."

"Not at all. Though I hadn't realized the engagement anniversary celebration was being moved as well."

The prince smiled.

"Some matters are best settled sooner rather than later."

*Such as framing your fiancée?*

Adrian kept that one to himself.

Leon turned his attention to him.

"And this is...?"

Elena opened her mouth.

The prince spoke first.

"Adrian Reed."

Every syllable was clear.

Not a question.

Not even a moment's hesitation.

Adrian lifted his head.

"Have we met, Your Highness?"

"I've heard your name."

"From whom?"

Leon's smile thinned.

"Is a servant interrogating a member of the royal family?"

"My apologies. I'm simply surprised Your Highness remembers the name of an ordinary attendant."

"I have an excellent memory."

"Then I envy you. I can barely remember what I ate yesterday."

Elena gave a small cough.

Adrian closed his mouth.

Leon studied him, his gaze lowering to the left hand at his side.

"Your hand. Have you injured it?"

Every muscle in Adrian's back went tight.

He was wearing white gloves. The scar wasn't visible.

"An old injury, Your Highness."

"Where did you get it?"

"I don't remember."

"You don't remember?"

The prince seemed far more interested than the question warranted.

Adrian forced himself to ask, "Have you seen a similar injury before?"

One of the protocol officers drew a sharp breath.

Leon only smiled.

"No. I take an interest in the people around Elena, that's all."

"Then I'm grateful for your concern."

Elena opened her fan.

"If my attendant is unwell, the Rosenberg household will see to him. You have a ballroom full of guests, Your Highness. There's no need to trouble yourself on his account."

Her words were perfectly polite.

The dismissal was unmistakable.

Leon turned to her.

"You're very protective of him."

"He helped me resolve a rather troublesome matter today."

"Did he?"

"If you're curious, Father can send you a report of his duties."

Leon set down his glass.

"That won't be necessary."

His gaze moved back to Adrian.

For an instant, the look in his eyes didn't resemble anger.

It looked almost like disappointment.

"Perhaps we can have a proper conversation another time, Mr. Reed."

"Preferably somewhere without guards, Your Highness."

Elena tapped his arm with her fan.

Message received.

*Stop talking.*

Leon moved off with his attendants.

Only when the white coat vanished into the crowd did Adrian let out his breath.

"Did I say too much?"

"Starting with your third sentence."

"Then why didn't you stop me?"

"I wanted to hear what he would ask."

Adrian blinked.

"You were listening for something specific?"

"You deliberately drew his attention. When he asked about your past, you gave him evasive answers."

"If he really knows me, I thought he might keep asking."

"And?"

"He asked about my hand."

Adrian looked at his glove.

"I don't even understand that part myself."

Elena didn't demand an explanation.

"Well," she said, "we can confirm one thing. The prince is considerably more interested in you than you expected."

"That doesn't sound reassuring."

"Have you met before?"

"Not that I remember."

"Not quite the same as 'no,' is it?"

Adrian met her eyes.

"All I can honestly tell you is that I don't remember meeting him."

"Then we'll work with that."

He'd been preparing excuses.

Suddenly, he didn't need them.

"You're really not going to ask why?"

"When you have an answer worth giving, I will. For now, we have more urgent things to do."

Adrian almost followed the prince anyway.

One of Leon's attendants hadn't gone with him. Instead, the man had slipped behind a column and was glancing repeatedly toward the side entrance.

"Do you know that officer?" Adrian asked.

"Gray. He used to work in the royal archives. He was reassigned to Leon's protocol staff last year."

"Why would an archivist become a protocol officer?"

"That's a very good question."

"And the answer?"

"I'd like to know that too."

Adrian committed the small silver badge on Gray's lapel to memory.

He'd entered the palace thinking his knowledge of the game's character list would let him identify everyone. Now he suspected that a minor change of office might matter far more than the scripted speeches.

"Stop looking at people like that," Elena said.

"Like what?"

"Like you've found a way to get out of washing dishes."

"Am I still expected to wash dishes?"

"That depends on how tonight goes."

He couldn't help smiling, but he kept Gray's name firmly in mind.

Elena pressed an empty wineglass into his hand.

"Fetch me some fruit juice."

"You're thirsty?"

"I want you to have a reason to approach the side door."

Adrian looked down at the glass.

"I'm beginning to understand why noble households employ so many servants."

"Why is that?"

"Because half the job descriptions are apparently lies."

Elena's mouth curved slightly.

Two palace guards stood near the side entrance. A young waiter was pushing a drinks trolley out from the kitchens.

Adrian selected a glass of apple juice.

The waiter noticed the silver rose on Adrian's coat and looked relieved.

"Sir, would you mind holding the door?"

"Of course."

Adrian braced the heavy door with his shoulder while the waiter pushed.

One of the trolley wheels caught on the threshold and let out an ugly squeal.

"Is this door always such a nuisance?" Adrian asked.

"Worse today." The waiter grimaced. "The Judicial Office's people have been blocking the passage since afternoon. Even the ice delivery had to go the long way."

"The Judicial Office?"

"Evidence officers. Bringing things into the ceremonial hall."

"Why not use the front doors?"

The waiter looked appalled.

"Can you imagine? Guests seeing sealed evidence boxes carried into a ballroom? Nobody would relax enough to dance."

"Fair point."

"That's why everything has to be registered here. Name, time, item. Every last detail."

*Registered.*

Adrian stole a glance toward the wall.

In the game, the side entrance had been part of the background illustration. No player would have stopped to talk with the man delivering apple juice.

"What if someone brought in a jewelry box? Would that need registering too?"

"Everything does." The waiter nodded toward a small table. "There's a ledger over there. Nothing goes through without a signature. Last month a guard missed one line and lost half his wages."

Adrian followed his gaze.

A thick ledger lay on the table beside a box of sealing wax.

He didn't press the waiter further.

Instead, he helped push the trolley through.

When he returned, Elena was waiting by a column not far from the side entrance.

"Anything useful?"

"Every piece of evidence has to be logged." He handed her the juice. "If the prince brings in that letter or necklace, someone should have recorded it."

"You're not touching that ledger."

"I know."

"And don't let anyone catch you watching it."

"I know that too."

"And whatever you do—"

"My lady, could you at least entertain the possibility that I possess a little common sense?"

She looked at the empty serving tray still in his hand.

"Based on the evidence so far, that would be a dangerous assumption."

Adrian decided silence was his best option.

A young nobleman approached and bowed to Elena.

"Lady Elena, I've heard you and His Highness are leading the first dance tonight."

"So I've been told." She offered him a practiced smile.

After the young man left, Adrian glanced at her.

"You're not happy about it?"

"Why wouldn't I be?"

"Your smile looks rehearsed."

"A noblewoman's smile usually is."

"Was the one you gave me in the study rehearsed too?"

Elena slowly turned her head.

"Are you suggesting I was laughing at you?"

"I'm conducting research into court etiquette."

"And how is your research going?"

"I've just identified a high-risk subject."

A quiet laugh escaped her.

For a second, Adrian forgot what he was doing.

Then she composed herself.

"Watch the door."

The side entrance opened.

A tall, thin man in the gray uniform of the Royal Judicial Office stepped inside, carrying a deep-blue jewelry case edged in silver.

Edwin Clay.

Adrian recognized the name from the altered guest list.

The clerk handed the case to a guard, who made him wait while he entered it in the ledger.

Adrian turned toward a painted wall as though fascinated by the artwork.

From the corner of his eye, he followed the box.

Blue velvet.

Silver edging.

A brass clasp.

Almost identical to the gift they'd sealed in the Rosenberg estate.

Almost.

The lower-right corner was missing a tiny scratch.

He remembered the mark on the original case—the uneven pale line beside the brass tack, the one they'd seen while examining the hidden compartment.

This box had an undamaged corner.

"My lady," he whispered, "the prince has a second jewelry case."

Elena's hand tightened around her glass.

"Are you certain it isn't simply another from the same maker?"

"The one in your house has a scratch. Martha should have it sealed by now."

"It could still be a separate gift."

"Then let's find out who he's delivering it to."

Clay took a pen from the guard and began filling out the register.

Elena glanced across the ballroom.

"If we approach him now, he'll know we're watching."

"So what do we do?"

"Make sure he's looking elsewhere."

The music stopped.

A master of ceremonies stepped into the center of the dance floor and raised a silver staff.

"Ladies and gentlemen, the opening dance of the Royal Autumn Ball is about to begin."

Adrian looked toward the prince.

Leon stood by the long banquet table, slowly lowering his wineglass.

The master of ceremonies raised his voice.

"In keeping with royal tradition, the first dance shall be led by His Highness Prince Leon and his fiancée, Lady Elena Rosenberg!"

Applause filled the hall.

But Leon didn't approach Elena.

He stood where he was, watching Adrian with the faintest smile.

As if he were waiting for a scene he'd rehearsed in advance.

"My lady," Adrian murmured, "he wants us to make a mistake."

Elena studied the prince for a moment.

Then she set down her juice.

"Does he?"

She turned to Adrian and offered him one gloved hand.

"Then let's disappoint him."

Adrian looked from her hand to the dance floor.

"You're not thinking of..."

"Dancing."

"I can't dance."

"I noticed."

Her hand remained extended.

"Mr. Reed, you had the nerve to ask the prince why he knows you. Are you telling me you're afraid to dance with me?"

Adrian glanced at the blue jewelry case still being logged at the side door.

Then at Elena's waiting hand.

He took it.

"Just one thing first."

"Yes?"

"If I step on your dress, please don't sentence me to death."

Elena finally smiled.

"That depends," she said, "on how many times you do it."
