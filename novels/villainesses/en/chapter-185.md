# Chapter 185: The Road Not Yet Walked

Watching Viola prepare, Adrian thought she was going to war.

A map, route annotations, and a physician's restrictions covered the table. Her cloak hung neatly over a chair. No sword. Her left arm remained supported. The small bag held a cushion, not a spare dagger.

“Why are you staring?”

“Wondering whether that has a hidden purpose.”

“Sitting.”

So earnest that he had to concede it was indeed a purpose.

February 13 was clear. An old battlefield appeared in the index concerning the western evacuation passage, but it wasn't open ground anyone could ride across today. Some land had been rebuilt; a disused route was closed and a slope unstable after rain.

Viola had already declined that slope. Reinstatement to certain duties hadn't healed her arm or made closure notices optional.

Today's itinerary covered the western veterans' hall and a nearby public viewing terrace: accessible level ground, copied garrison routes, and a retired quartermaster willing to distinguish what he remembered. Nobody promised a journey through ruined underground passages. Adrian had read the itinerary before leaving. It gave him somewhere to go without pretending every place marked on an old sheet still existed, or that wishing to understand what happened there entitled them to ignore the people responsible for it now.

Before departure she repeated the time limit, then noticed Adrian smiling.

“You may call me overcautious.”

“I was thinking you might once have hidden the cushion.”

“Now I'd like someone else to carry it.”

He took the bag. Her brief thanks weighed less than a courageous declaration refusing to need help.

Chairs awaited repair outside the hall. A gray-haired man tried to plant three legs while the fourth lifted. Seeing Viola's support, he indicated a seat without asking why she lacked full uniform.

His name was Hale. He'd kept logistical route material, not stood heroically at the catastrophe's center. He knew nothing about Adrian's name. If that was their purpose, they should save the tea money.

“We came about a road,” Viola said.

“On paper or underfoot?”

“Whichever you can distinguish first.”

He put down the chair and called for tea. The greeting was less grand than Adrian had imagined when he heard they could speak to a retired quartermaster. He was grateful for that. It left room to ask a small question without first making the man into someone who could answer every larger one.

The garrison copy had arrived during later cataloguing. Hale hadn't witnessed every action it described. He indicated its accession date, preventing Adrian from using his age to guarantee all old events.

The western passage looked straight, easily traversed. Hale weighted its entrance and bend with wooden buttons. There'd been a narrow doorway, too small for stretchers abreast. A later public map omitted the gatehouse while keeping the passage name.

“So evacuation was slow?”

“So calculating from this map omits a possibility.”

Another copy retained the gatehouse but lacked scale. They couldn't combine them into a more comforting map. Viola recorded separate sources and asked about actual use.

An equipment allocation sheet showed carts issued, not every return time. Hale produced it despite knowing they'd be disappointed. Missing returns didn't automatically mean deaths. Equipment might have gone elsewhere; he lacked those records.

At Stretchers, Adrian's eyes stayed on the map. He remembered nobody lying on one. Yet a narrow doorway gave weight to people he'd previously imagined escaping easily.

Viola noticed his grip on the cup without assuring him he hadn't been there. She asked Hale how he'd personally estimated empty passage versus evacuation.

“An empty road needs feet. A crowd needs information too.”

Adrian looked again at the buttons marking the entrance and bend. Their neat positions suggested motion without delay. He had supplied that ease himself; neither wooden piece had claimed it. Hale waited for him to stop looking at the line as though its straightness were a promise.

People unaware a rear exit was open could wait beside a broken door. Moving supplies before informing wounded people didn't mean everyone had passed simply because the floor was clear. Late messengers prolonged waiting.

Experience, not an explanation of the termination record. Adrian set it apart so a sensible statement wouldn't later become an established event.

An argument interrupted them at the rear door. Winter blankets had arrived, but broken chairs blocked the cart. A man with a walking stick insisted he could step over them. Hale stopped him and moved the chairs first.

Viola didn't rush to help. She stopped Adrian rising after him, indicating two workers already moving things. Squeezing into their arrangement would block the way.

A stretch shorter than ten paces took time to clear. Adrian could see the end of it throughout. That made his impatience more embarrassing, not less. Seeing the destination did nothing to move the chair, widen the crate, or tell two people which of them ought to proceed first. Then the blanket crate was too wide and needed turning. The man with the stick tried to yield; the driver thought he meant to advance. Both stopped.

“Let him sit first,” Hale said.

Clear instructions this time. They waited until he sat securely before continuing.

Returning, Hale was asked whether that illustrated information needing to travel.

“Today's example. Don't put the blanket cart in your ancient mystery.”

Adrian flushed; Viola laughed. Learning from life was welcome, turning it into convenient evidence less so.

Afterward a crate label came loose. Checking the receipt revealed not a missing blanket but a size sheet tucked beneath the lid. Adrian's hand paused before resealing the tidy box.

“Check first?”

“You're useful when you're not hurrying to prove it.”

Viola hid a smile in her tea. Adrian laughed too and stacked empty packaging cleared for removal. It didn't solve an old world, but widened the doorway.

Another veteran placed a peppermint on Viola's saucer. Not pretending she was well by overexerting herself deserved a reward. Was that a new rule? No, the sweet was too hard for his teeth. She accepted this undignified respect, allowing injury to coexist with an ordinary joke.

After tea they visited the terrace. Hale wouldn't accompany them downslope. He indicated a hollow between roofs, perhaps part of the old route. Changes to the ground prevented identification of an exact entrance by sight.

Beyond the railing a wood carrier descended a path. Going around would bring them closer, he said; not far.

Adrian almost asked for directions. Viola declined. That section wasn't arranged, and they didn't know its condition.

The man shrugged and left, not transformed into a mysterious obstacle. Danger didn't always announce itself solemnly enough.

Wind lifted Viola's cloak. Adrian steadied its corner on the chair and asked whether she was cold. She wanted five minutes sitting. He produced the cushion; she didn't pretend it was for him.

“Did you want to go down?”

“Yes.”

“Why was saying no so easy?”

“I considered it yesterday. Today needn't make the same question more heroic.”

She straightened the map with her right hand. Caution wasn't diminished courage, he realized. She refused to turn injury and other people's waiting into a demonstration of it.

Did the road make him feel worse about himself?

He considered. Before, the record had been an accusation. Now there was a doorway stretchers couldn't pass abreast. He didn't know its connection to him, but couldn't dismiss it as merely wrong wording.

“Remember the doorway still needs verification. And people on stretchers shouldn't become lighter because we need comfort.”

Not relief, but something possible to do: request the gatehouse measurements' source and equipment destinations instead of entering a closed slope.

Back at the hall, Hale had inverted the chair. Viola pointed out a loose joint beside the apparently short leg. She didn't kneel to repair it. He checked, agreed, and said that wouldn't cover the tea.

“I haven't been paid today.”

He laughed, collecting their cups. For the first time that morning, Adrian felt something other than the increasing weight of old things.

Before departure Hale asked whether they'd make him the final witness. No, Viola said: only his own experience, the material's sources, and today's statements were within his knowledge. Include that, he asked, not just the fine phrases that sounded like a soldier's account.

They received designated extracts, not the entire collection. A corrected direction prompted Adrian to ask again. West Passage was an old name, not necessarily due west from today's terrace. Street markers had since been reorganized; dating mattered here too.

In the carriage Viola tired, leaning against the cushion while stubbornly keeping her eyes open.

“You can sleep.”

“I'm afraid I'll wake to find you've promised we'll return tomorrow.”

“I didn't learn anything that bad today.”

Her mouth twitched. She closed her eyes. He narrowed the window opening to keep wind off her support, securing loose outer maps without reading the private letter tucked inside them.

Waking outside home, she first asked whether they'd overrun. No. He returned her bag. Tomorrow the cushion could still be a cushion, she said, not a monument to their shared adventure.

He agreed, laughing. He had been carrying the bag all morning and could still be trusted to return it without attaching a grander meaning to it. The little exchange did not reduce what she had helped him face. It meant they could come back from a difficult place and still speak about something other than being brave.

Later he delivered the notes to Beatrice. The passage wasn't an established catastrophe site. Two differently sourced maps weren't personal experience; a quartermaster's professional knowledge wasn't eyewitness testimony about that event. But if a fragment contained All clear, a short line on a map wouldn't be reason enough to believe it.

He sent Hale a brief acknowledgment identifying what they proposed to quote and inviting corrections. Not approval of their whole theory: a promise that the man's words wouldn't acquire a weight he hadn't agreed to bear. Only then did Adrian put the map away.

At his desk he crossed Hurry from his intended questions to Luna. He could not make himself unafraid of the next result. He could choose not to pass that fear down the line as an instruction that someone else should work faster, ignore a limit, or call an unfinished evacuation complete because the route looked so short.

Nobody had been rescued from the battlefield today. No exonerating road appeared. He had gone hoping the landscape might offer a simpler answer than the papers. Instead it had made one easy assumption harder to keep, and he could not honestly call that a wasted morning. He could still hear Hale insisting that his limits travel with his words, rather than being left behind at the hall. Viola came home safely; easily confused records remained separate.

The unverified narrow doorway stayed in his mind, no longer drawn as an effortless straight line.
