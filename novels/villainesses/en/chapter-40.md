# Why Do All the Villainesses Want to Marry Me?
## Volume Two: The Saint Won't Let Me Get Married
### Chapter 40: The Villainess and the Saint Join Forces

On the morning of October twentieth, Adrian discovered that the Rosenberg household had arranged three carriages for a visit to one relief station.

The first carried medicine and clean bedding requested by the clinic.

The second held Hale, a municipal physician, and two authorized health inspectors.

The third carried Adrian, Elena, and Cecilia.

"Are we investigating a relief station," Adrian asked, "or moving house?"

Elena placed the last inspection permit into her document case.

"You said yesterday that getting the patient treated mattered more than finding evidence."

"I didn't say we should ship the entire estate's linen cupboard."

"Father approved the supplies. You're responsible for helping check the inventory, not complaining about how much it weighs."

Cecilia sat opposite them with a thin charity register in her lap.

She'd hardly slept, but she'd made one thing clear to her review committee before leaving: she was accompanying the medical team as a charity volunteer and a person concerned about a missing attendant.

She was not invoking her historical-contract authority.

"Do you really have to identify which hat you're wearing at every stop?" Adrian asked.

"If I arrive as a saint, people may treat every suggestion as an order. If I claim to be an investigator, I may be accused of exercising medical powers I don't have."

"So which are you today?"

"Mira's friend."

For once, she didn't follow her answer with a paragraph of regulations.

Graybell Relief Station occupied part of a warehouse complex in the lower district. Originally built for temporary care after a disaster, it was now licensed for short observation stays and medical supply transfers.

A gray-gold sign above the entrance read:

**Temple Partner Relief Facility.**

Beside it hung a municipal operating permit.

One of the health inspectors confirmed that the station was subject to the ordinary requirements for medical safety and patient handovers.

They had barely reached the door when a manager in dark-gray robes stepped forward.

"This building holds restricted Temple prognosis records. Nobody enters without headquarters authorization."

Elena didn't argue.

She handed him the municipal inspection order.

"We aren't here to publish a prophecy. We need to establish whether a patient transferred from Mercy Clinic is receiving care. Is Jonah Payne here?"

The man read the permit.

His face tightened.

"We have a temporary observation patient registered under C-91."

"Then a physician will verify who the patient is," Grace Winter said, stepping down from the second carriage. "I'm responsible for the original transfer from my clinic."

"He's receiving a Temple blessing of repose. It shouldn't be interrupted."

"Whether the patient wishes to continue a blessing is for the patient and his medical team to decide," Grace replied. "We aren't going to delay treatment until a ceremony ends."

The inspector noted that the manager had initially declined to confirm the patient's name and recorded his stated reason.

After a brief exchange, the man opened the door.

The corridor smelled of damp walls and dried herbs.

Several narrow beds stood along one wall. A yellowed Temple prayer hung beside an old window.

A little boy sat swinging his legs from a bench.

When he saw Adrian, he called out, "Are you bringing medicine?"

"Medicine. And bedding."

"Any biscuits?"

Adrian was caught completely off guard.

Elena found a small packet in her bag.

She asked the attendant beside the boy whether he was allowed to have one.

After getting permission, she handed it over.

The boy looked delighted.

"She's much better at this than you are!"

"I've noticed," Adrian said.

Cecilia crouched to retrieve a wooden ball the child had dropped.

"Have you been here before?" he asked.

"No. It's my first visit."

"I know someone who likes drawing suns for children, though."

"Mira?"

Cecilia went very still.

"You know her?"

"She came a few days ago and drew one on a door. Then a man told her she wasn't allowed to wander around and sent her into the room next door to copy things."

Hale asked the recorder to preserve the child's statement as a lead.

Not as conclusive evidence that Mira had been detained.

Adrian looked toward the end of the hall.

A side door stood there with a metal bolt on its outer face.

He didn't run toward it.

He asked the health inspector to check what the room was used for under the authority of the inspection order.

Grace and the municipal physician continued into the east sickroom.

A thin man lay in bed with a damp cloth across his forehead.

He looked exhausted but awake.

"Could you tell me your name?" Grace asked gently.

"Jonah Payne."

She explained why she needed to verify his identity. Only after he agreed did she compare his original clinic record with the card beside the bed.

Graybell had registered him under C-91.

Jonah recognized her voice.

Some of the strain left his face.

"Is my mother still looking for me?"

"She is. We'll tell her where you are. First let's make sure you're getting the care you need."

The municipal physician examined the available treatment notes.

There had been basic observation, but the medicine receipt ledger didn't agree with the supplies actually present.

The doctor didn't make predictions about whether Jonah would recover.

She arranged for qualified staff to continue caring for him and prepared for a safe transfer back to Mercy Clinic when appropriate.

Cecilia stood by the doorway.

She didn't sweep past the doctors to perform a dramatic miracle.

"May I bring some water?" she asked.

"Please," Grace said. "Thank you."

Then someone knocked from behind the side door.

"Hello? I heard someone say Mercy Clinic."

Adrian's heart lurched.

He recognized that lower-district accent.

Cecilia reached the door first.

"Mira?"

A sharp breath sounded from inside.

"Your Holiness?"

The manager hurried over.

"That's a confidential records room. It cannot be opened without Temple approval."

The municipal inspector turned to him.

"Someone has just answered us. Is the person inside free to leave?"

The manager didn't answer.

Hale had the recorder note the silence and the bolt fastened from the corridor side.

The inspector explained the legal authority to check the safety of a person apparently confined in a licensed medical facility. A municipal patrol officer was called to attend.

The manager was instructed to open the door.

He finally complied.

Mira Finch sat at a writing desk.

Her short hair was disheveled. She looked pale, and ink stained both sleeves.

There were no obvious serious injuries.

On the desk lay an unsigned form.

**C-19 Patient Fully Recovered Following the Blessing Foretold by the Temple.**

Mira looked at Cecilia.

She managed one small smile.

Then tears ran down her face.

"I didn't sign it."

Cecilia stepped forward.

But even now, she stopped to ask.

"May I hug you?"

Mira nodded.

The saint wrapped both arms around her.

Adrian stood near the doorway, remembering the empty bed in the guesthouse and the chestnut seller's purse with the red thread bracelet.

At last, a name on a missing-person report belonged to someone they could see and speak to again.

Before asking Mira any detailed questions, Grace had a physician examine her and determine whether she needed rest or medical attention.

She'd eaten very little in the past few days. She was exhausted.

Even so, her first question was about Jonah.

"He's been found," Cecilia assured her. "A doctor is with him. Now you need to let someone look after you."

Mira's shoulders trembled.

"Have I disgraced you? They kept saying if I didn't sign, the saint would be blamed."

Cecilia held her hand.

"You don't have to testify to protect my reputation. Tell the investigators what happened. Whoever restricted your freedom must answer for their own actions."

Only then did Mira seem able to relax.

After some water and a little time, she agreed to speak in the presence of Hale and an independent recorder.

On October sixteenth, she'd gone to Mercy Clinic to check the medicine delivery.

There, she'd discovered that Jonah's reference had been changed from C-19 to C-91, while Elliot Morris's identity had been used for the report claiming that the patient foretold by the oracle was now stable.

Mira refused to certify that the prophecy had been fulfilled.

She refused to put Jonah's name under someone else's identity.

An official carrying a temporary authorization from the Administrative Duty Office told her to go to Graybell and assist with a correction.

Once she arrived, she was told that the material was confidential and she wasn't allowed to leave.

"They wanted me to sign the report before I went home," she said.

"When I was helping load the medicine wagon, I managed to slip a note into a supply receipt. I asked the driver to get it to the clinic."

"The note that said Jonah was alive?" Adrian asked.

Mira nodded.

"I couldn't be sure anyone would believe me. So I wrote down a room they could verify for themselves."

The health inspector documented her statement separately from the unsigned form.

The municipal patrol asked for any lawful order authorizing the manager to confine her.

None was produced.

"We'll still need to establish who gave the instruction and who knew about it," Hale warned. "We cannot attribute the conduct of one manager to everyone in the Temple without further evidence."

Mira had kept a copy of the reassignment slip and the unsigned patient statement.

Their custody was recorded before they were secured as evidence by the appropriate officials.

Meanwhile, Elena coordinated the clinic transfer through municipal medical channels.

Medicines would be dispensed according to verified patient identities and current clinical needs, not the altered prognostic labels.

Cecilia filed a preservation request with her independent review committee.

She expressly asked that Jonah's medical information and Elliot's private details not be publicly disclosed.

For the first time, Elena and Cecilia signed the same operational report as equal participants in one investigation.

Adrian noticed.

"Successful partnership?"

"Two people doing what needed to be done," Elena said.

"Exactly what I wanted to do," Cecilia replied.

The women looked at one another.

Then smiled.

Adrian was just beginning to relax when Cecilia turned toward him.

"Now that we've finished the inspection, could I invite you both to dinner? I'd like to thank you."

Elena looked at Adrian too.

"He promised we'd finish yesterday's dessert tonight."

Adrian cleared his throat.

"Could I choose the plan this time?"

Both women nodded.

"First I'd like to make sure Mira gets somewhere safe where she actually wants to stay. Then I'd like to check that Jonah's family has been told about his transfer. We can have dessert tomorrow."

Elena took his hand.

"All right."

Cecilia nodded too.

"Then I'll bring the pastries."

"Can you bake?" Adrian asked.

"No," she said without embarrassment. "But I know a shop where buying for two is cheaper."

Adrian remembered her demanding a written breakdown of the baker's discount and nearly laughed.

By evening, Jonah had been transferred safely back to Mercy Clinic under medical supervision.

His treatment records were corrected using his verified identity. Elliot received written confirmation that he'd been wrongly listed under C-19.

Neither patient had suddenly recovered because an entry changed.

But both had their names back, and the people caring for them knew which records were theirs.

Mira was offered accommodation through a municipally witnessed protection arrangement while she prepared a full statement about the restrictions placed on her movements.

Back at the Rosenberg estate, Hale sent the day's preservation and welfare reports to the independent authorities.

Not long afterward, another notice arrived from Temple Internal Oversight.

It wasn't addressed to Jonah.

Or Mira.

It was for Cecilia Saintclair.

The accusation read:

**Suspected Unauthorized Disclosure of Restricted Divine Prognosis, Causing Reputational Harm to the Temple and Risk to Contractual Records.**

Adrian read it.

Then looked up.

"But we never published the original oracle."

"I know," Cecilia said.

She didn't tear the letter apart.

She didn't prepare to flee the capital.

Instead, she asked Hale to record its delivery time, then set her investigator's badge on the desk.

"They've finally decided to question why I refused to let an incorrect register keep harming people."

Elena sat beside her.

"Then we'll give them an accurate account of what happened."

Cecilia looked at her.

After a moment, she nodded.

Adrian glanced from one woman to the other.

Somewhere behind them was a mysterious historical betrothal that no one could prove.

Right in front of him were two women who'd refused to let anyone be volunteered for sacrifice without being asked.

At the moment, he knew which mattered more.
