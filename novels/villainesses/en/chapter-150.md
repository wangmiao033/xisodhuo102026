# Chapter 150: An Order About Futures Not Yet Promised

The courier first asked who could accept service for everyone.

Five people looked toward Adrian.

He set down his empty plate. “I can accept my own.”

Luna lowered her head to laugh. The humor slowly left her expression as she heard the title.

Emergency Restrictions on Betrothal and Risk-Contract Registration for Fate-Imprint Bearers.

At noon on January twenty-second, citing the recent carrier incident and existing abnormal records, the Temple's contract registry required special prior clearance for the named people's new betrothal registrations and contracts carrying fate-risk transfer provisions. An enforcement acknowledgment from the registration authorities followed. The order applied for seven days from service. Any extension required further notice. Objections could be registered.

The attachment named five women and Adrian Reed.

It wasn't a marriage certificate. Nor did it cancel a betrothal the six had never jointly signed. Their future signatures were being made subject to permission; no existing vows between them were being undone. The distinction mattered, even beneath the same alarming title.

Cecilia asked the courier to repeat that distinction as clearly as the title.

“I hadn't assumed you were married,” he said awkwardly.

“Someone listening outside might.”

He repeated the relevant wording.

Luna glanced at Isabella. “A larger office has overtaken your emotional report.”

“They have no standing to review its final section.”

Isabella's right hand rested beside the receipt, her left wrist still cushioned. Yesterday's injury had already become grounds for someone else's new demands.

The courier explained that no medical attachment accompanied his papers. He had no authority to request one. The order cited the incident number already received by the competent office and earlier registration records. It did not publish the location of her mark.

She relaxed a little. The order became no more acceptable.

“Who authorized them to turn us into a category?”

He couldn't answer. He could only indicate the office handling explanations and objections, without promising an outcome.

Adrian finished every page before examining the two additional sheets.

One acknowledged service. The other requested priority review, with the applicant agreeing to a Temple-designated safety procedure and an assessment of compatible candidates.

“Why put these together?”

“For people with urgent needs.”

“Accepting the first doesn't require signing the second?”

“No.”

Adrian signed only the receipt, noting that receiving the attachments didn't endorse their grounds. Elena did the same. Everyone separately confirmed service. The princess decided for nobody else.

At compatible candidates, Cecilia looked up at the courier. She didn't challenge his understanding of faith. She noted the page requiring examination and asked for an answer from someone authorized to interpret it.

Her silence troubled Adrian more than an outburst would have.

After the courier left, nobody reached for the remaining crumbs.

Viola first asked whether they were forbidden to leave the house. Elena found the enforcement provisions. Nothing prohibited ordinary travel or authorized detention. They mustn't add restrictions the document didn't contain.

“If someone uses it to bar the road, they can explain their authority.”

“I meant now,” Viola said.

“Now you may still attend your exercises as arranged.”

The knight looked at the door, then slowly relaxed her right hand. A registration order was no reason to draw a sword.

Luna needed to rest. Gathering her copy, she asked whether Adrian intended to eat dinner.

“Why?”

“You look prepared to live on anger until tomorrow.”

“I'll eat.”

“Good. The Temple hasn't regulated soup yet.”

Isabella wanted to laugh and couldn't. Studying the unsigned priority form, she admitted she too had once placed an easier path before people, then called the choice free.

Adrian didn't deny it for her.

She filed the paper separately rather than crumpling it.

“I'll keep it. In case I tell myself again that I was only making things convenient.”

Before leaving, Luna opened the third page.

“They cite the load limits we acknowledged.”

Isabella read. It wasn't invented intimidation. Old contracts could transmit risk; carriers had caused real injuries. Warnings they had labored to make accurate now furnished the order's most reasonable-sounding argument.

“So we can't say the danger is imaginary.”

“No,” Luna said. “I wrote that we need to know the actual load and who can stop it. Not that one office may choose everyone's partner.”

Adrian recorded that and asked which material could accompany an objection. Luna indicated only conclusions already cleared for disclosure. Opposing the order didn't require surrendering everyone's experimental records.

Viola said the hardest old military orders to challenge often contained something genuinely important.

“Protect the road, for instance.”

“That doesn't mean driving everyone off it,” Elena added.

Nobody declared the argument won. Isabella finished the page carefully. Her hand shook less as she put it away. At least the two things frightening her didn't demand the same answer.

They went their separate ways. Their first gathering did not become a hurried oath of alliance. Nobody signed away everything with fear for ink.

That afternoon, Adrian and Elena submitted separate witness accounts. His concerned yesterday's incident. Hers supplemented procedures she had witnessed at the public questioning on the nineteenth. Both belonged to the old project; neither could testify for the other.

Isabella's commission remained valid, but she had a medical appointment. The original custodians received the material. She needn't carry an injury from window to window. These were factual accounts and receipts, not original artifacts. Existing preservation measures didn't await a betrothal.

At a street corner, a news stall already advertised a Marriage Ban.

Someone asked how often that footman had become engaged.

Elena stopped, her expression darkening.

Adrian spoke first. “I have no current valid betrothal. If you're selling news, don't print a registration restriction as a marriage record.”

The stallholder blinked and consulted the printer's sample. It did say new registrations were restricted. He had shortened it himself for the board.

“There isn't room.”

“Write New Contract Registrations Restricted,” Elena said.

After looking between them, he wiped part of the board. Others continued talking. One correction wouldn't erase every rumor.

Beyond the corner, Elena asked why Adrian had explained himself.

“When you're angry, you might buy the entire board.”

“I wasn't going to buy it.”

“Good.”

“I intended to make him read the original ten times.”

Adrian laughed and received a light swat on his sleeve. She briefly laughed too, then took his hand to cross a patch of melting snow.

The real obstruction wasn't the curious crowd.

The registry accepted the accounts but returned their joint cover sheet. Beneath jointly confirm the following matters, the clerk underlined jointly.

“Agreements between listed persons must be held.”

“This doesn't agree to future action,” Elena said. “It describes what we separately witnessed on different occasions.”

“The enforcement notice requires us not to overlook reviewable matters. A supervisor must decide.”

“Then accept the individually signed accounts.”

He hesitated and consulted the newly posted notice again. He hadn't suddenly decided Adrian had married five women. He feared passing a sheet that tomorrow would make him responsible for an unauthorized act.

Seeing other returned documents stacked behind the counter, Adrian understood. The order needn't name every door to make doors close themselves.

An older man holding a file stood behind them, quietly asking how much longer it would take. His warehouse lease had nothing to do with them.

Elena stepped aside rather than occupying the window until its clerk answered for the entire system.

“We want the grounds in writing, with the specific provision you believe applies. Retain the material under the existing preservation reference. Returning it mustn't count as our withdrawal.”

He agreed to ask and disappeared behind the cabinets.

They sat outside near an almost-dead brazier. Adrian remembered Isabella waiting here with a queue number six days earlier. His own position no longer seemed so different.

Elena's hand remained in his.

“What if that priority application were faster?” he asked.

She turned sharply. “You want to sign?”

“I'm asking myself, and you. Not asking you to approve it for me.”

She studied him, then slowly released the tension in her jaw.

“I'd be tempted. But I won't give an office that selects candidates the power to decide whether I may keep seeing you.”

“Even if they happen to select you?”

“That would still be awful.”

The speed of her answer surprised her.

“I want you to choose me. I don't want that part handed to me by them.”

Adrian looked at her. The footsteps around them seemed more distant.

“I know how we reached this point. A new notice can't change that.”

She lowered her gaze and held his hand more firmly, without forcing the words into a betrothal due today.

They were still side by side when the clerk returned.

There was finally a written decision. Their individual accounts would be retained under the existing matter. The joint cover would not yet be registered as a commitment of responsibility. They must explain its nature for a supervisor to examine tomorrow. Nothing had been destroyed. The misunderstanding wasn't fully resolved either.

Elena obtained the stamped custody receipt and had the original refusal attached. Getting the accounts retained mustn't erase the first obstruction.

Dusk was falling when they left.

Adrian had thought today's hardest thing would be a visible imprint. Instead, the strongest thing before him was an ordinary ink line under jointly.

Back at the residence, Isabella was resting as instructed. Hearing the account, she picked up her commission with her right hand to check whether the new order revoked it.

It didn't.

She kept pursuit of the missing old-project records on tomorrow's schedule. Neither her injury nor the order justified abandoning the searches. Adrian moved the papers within right-handed reach, watching her circle two unrelated deadlines separately.

“Sunset on the twenty-sixth is my commission's limit. They can't make me stare at the twenty-ninth until I forget what I promised first.”

From next door, Luna sent a left-handed note: Eating together transmits no magical load. Anyone disputing this should first explain the spoon.

Elena finally relaxed the shoulders she had held tense all afternoon.

“Don't put that in the formal objection.”

“I intended to begin with the soup's temperature,” Adrian said.

When dinner arrived, Martha removed his copy from beside the plate and replaced it with an actual spoon. His first mouthful revealed how painfully hungry he was.

Afterward, five differently worded invitations found their way to him. A walk tomorrow afternoon. Tea. Something resembling a meticulously planned itinerary. One ended with the added assurance: not official business.

Different seals. Different reasons.

Every one named after the third bell.

Adrian studied them, moved the emergency order aside, and fetched clean paper.

If fear decided whom they saw and when, they would finish half the restriction's work themselves before it ever reached ordinary life.

He first wrote down tomorrow's necessary work and rest, then prepared to answer each invitation.

His first question was perfectly ordinary.

Why did everyone keep assuming one person could occupy five places at once?
