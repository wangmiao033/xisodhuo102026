# Chapter 207: Names Beyond the Escort Column

The first reply on March 6 came on the back of a cobbler's business card.

Viola said the cobbler she'd mentioned earlier had time today. Would Adrian still like to go? She had checked the level approach and available chairs. No trip through the rear yard to inspect leather. His soles were wearing smooth; her buckle needed replacement.

“An invitation or a safety correction?”

She considered. “Both. You may accept only the invitation. The shoes remain yours.”

Laughing, he accepted both, then asked whether they might bring yesterday's diagram and talk briefly. Yes, but not about catastrophe while the cobbler measured feet. Someone might record the wrong size.

Her left arm remained supported; an assistant carried the shoebox. They followed the level route rather than taking the shorter stairs under repair.

The cobbler provided a stool. While Adrian removed his shoes, Viola held two sole samples in her right hand. She watched his step before observing that thicker wasn't always better.

“How do you know?”

“I once chased a horse in overly stiff boots.”

“Caught it?”

“It waited for me. My honor dislikes that detail.”

Adrian laughed hard enough to be asked to sit straight. Viola's ears reddened, but the considerate horse stayed in her account.

Silver-buckled boots hung nearby. Glancing between them, an apprentice suggested matching orders. Viola immediately explained that they had merely come together for repairs, then realized how thoroughly she'd sounded as though she wished to disclaim everything.

“I wanted to come. I'm not an assigned escort.”

“So did I. My shoes haven't issued you orders.”

The apprentice blinked. Suppressing laughter, his master sent him for plain buckles. The misunderstanding ended there, without requiring promises about their future.

Only after the cobbler turned to his workbench did Viola ask about the warning line. Adrian laid the authorized diagram on a glue-free stool and explained how the new line placed B-seven below warning level. She didn't interpret it as grounds to summon knights and search the Temple.

“I want to know what the people directing evacuation received. Did they lack the original reading, or see it and still use that classification? That tells us whom to question, and how future reports should work.”

“So you'll pursue the core?”

“I'll investigate its connection to orders. Not charge through an uninspected door for anyone.”

She indicated her sling and his shoeless feet.

“We're hardly equipped to pursue a horse together.”

His heel felt tight when he put the shoes back on. Adrian was about to say walking would fix it, but the cobbler asked for two more steps and found a folded edge in the lining. Adrian tried again after it was flattened, then paid. Viola didn't pronounce him comfortable. She asked whether it felt better. Yes—and she should try her new buckle rather than attend only to his shoes.

They returned with both pairs repaired. Her assistant carried the box; Adrian carried his diagram. Companionship hadn't become extra weight she must bear.

In the small dining room, Cecilia was removing labeled cards from the places. Martha had only asked her to identify the portion without mustard. Six cards had made lunch look like a formal conference.

Luna sat against soft cushions, right palm supported and left hand resting. The good news, she announced, was that dessert would not determine speaking order.

“The bad news?” Adrian asked.

“Five puddings.”

Isabella pushed her dish toward the middle with her right hand. They could share. Elena consulted the kitchen and returned: the sixth hadn't left its mold. Nobody needed to sacrifice dessert to prove cooperation.

“Already more productive than many official meetings,” Isabella said.

They ate cold meat and bread first. Adrian chose his chair, Elena beside him. Nobody else had to move away to demonstrate indifference. Luna asked her assistant to bring her cut portion closer. Her tired left hand wouldn't perform elegant table manners.

The diagram stayed on a clean side table until the plates were removed. Today wasn't another reading of Oswin's signature. They had to decide where to look, why, and what each intended to do.

Isabella wanted the verified amendment made public quickly. Correction of the old notice remained unresolved. Waiting for the entire baseline table would leave people seeing only Adrian's name.

Cecilia opposed making High Priest Suppressed Warnings the immediate headline. Not to conceal the amendment: a fact requiring explanation of scale edition and implementation scope might become a claim that the entire Temple deliberately manufactured daily disasters. Meal-hall workers and patients would suffer first. Actual decision-makers might become no easier to question.

“Explaining forever why we cannot speak can become silence,” Isabella replied.

Adrian didn't hurry to call both right. He wanted the notice changed and remembered how omissions had made his name the center of everyone else's fear.

Elena separated two sheets. “Submit the complete authorized excerpt today and ask the correction office to update the evidence considered. Draft any short public explanation separately. Each signatory confirms their own words. Joint inquiry shouldn't require accepting one sentence.”

Isabella still found that slow, but agreed not to turn an inference into a headline. Cecilia would explain the verified report without guaranteeing every old ritual's innocence. They didn't shake hands and pronounce all differences settled.

Luna had her assistant read her statement. She wanted the central comparison baseline table and corresponding scale edition, not permission to insert injured hands into unknown machinery. What grounds supported the change: calibration, convenient reporting, or another decision?

“I used to see a result and immediately want to open the machine. This time, let the papers establish which question warrants touching what.”

“What if the papers lie?” Isabella asked.

“Find independent evidence to compare. My curiosity isn't a safety authorization.”

Most of her allotted sand had fallen. The assistant read back sentences needing confirmation; the rest could be copied while she rested. Pain wouldn't prove her choice more sincere.

Cecilia had written that destined guilt must no longer replace specific responsibility. She wanted to follow ritual references to actual judgments. Were people being required to obey faith, or an alterable decision whose alteration hadn't been explained? Adrian asked whether she might lose more work. Possibly, she admitted.

“I also like you.”

He almost misplaced his water glass.

“Both are true. I needn't hide liking you to appear noble. Nor does liking you make investigation merely a way of persuading you to like me.”

He set the glass safely down and said he understood. Elena glanced at Cecilia, mouth tight, without answering for him.

“You may be jealous,” Cecilia added.

“I am. You needn't register it.”

Even Viola laughed. Nobody gained a romantic promise, but everyone could remain at the table with her own wishes.

Isabella turned her page toward herself. With her right hand she wrote that she would investigate how royal reports adopted Temple judgments. Lost co-sponsorship remained unreplaced. She couldn't order an office open, but could ask as an ordinary applicant about something her surname had once obscured.

Elena wrote last. She wanted the danger assessment cited in the historical judicial transfer examined: who, under what circumstances, had made it grounds for restraining someone? That wouldn't erase her earlier self's handover against Adrian's objection. Nor did it predetermine that every responsibility belonged to the threshold's signatory.

“I also want to keep living today with you,” she told Adrian. “That isn't a way of explaining yesterday away for you.”

He studied five sheets. Not where each woman would accompany him, but what each wanted to question. The core remained an object of inquiry, not a discovered room waiting for him to lead them there.

On a sixth sheet he wrote his own purpose: investigate connections between central judgments and the accident chain containing his shutdown. Retain his unfavorable action and refuse to let preceding changes disappear.

“I'll apply too. Not have five women request a door for me.”

Luna needed rest. Her assistant retained the confirmed statement for delivery while she withdrew to a quiet room. The others sorted authorized attachments. Lunch wouldn't become a meeting an injured person had to endure to its end.

When the sixth pudding arrived, the maid found papers occupying the table and began to retreat. Adrian asked her to wait while he moved empty plates. Elena removed the jam jar; Cecilia gathered menu cards. They cleared a place without official business in it.

“Who hasn't had one?” the maid asked.

Four people pointed at Adrian. He had forgotten his missing portion and suddenly felt embarrassed.

“We didn't vote,” Isabella said. “Your plate is simply empty.”

He accepted the spoon and ate while everyone else checked her own lines. Nobody declared that this proved who understood him best. Five desires weren't divided into five portions for him to repay. He liked these few minutes: just someone eating late, not the person responsible for holding the whole table together.

Elena asked whether it was sweet enough. Just right. Remembering yesterday's breakfast jam, he added that he'd like some of her chosen jar at dinner. She lowered her head to gather papers, unable to hide her smile. A carriage passed outside. Nobody hurried to rise.

Reviewing the walking-route page, Viola found her evacuation inquiry listed as a reason for accompaniment. She asked for it to be moved into independent questions. Not greater powers: a clear account of why she was participating. Adrian held the paper's corner while her right hand indicated the position on the copy.

That afternoon Adrian, Elena, and Cecilia went to the ordinary intake counter. The others' signed, limited statements and the assistant's record accompanied them. All five women needn't stand in a crowded hall until their legs hurt to prove a joint decision.

Directory references located the central comparison baseline table and sealed historical Temple engineering records. The application first sought accessible directories, confirmation of surviving files, and conditions for an engineering safety examination. Not power connection, or testing a door with old artifacts or bodily marks. The sealed energy case and three originals remained in their separate repositories. None had traveled here.

The receiving clerk checked each attachment and stamped their duplicate received. This wasn't permission to enter underground, he reminded them. A supervisor would review archive scope; works staff must answer about site conditions.

“We understand,” Adrian said.

An ordinary stamp opened no underground door. He nevertheless put the sheet away carefully. Five names no longer crowded the escort column. Six questions hadn't been compressed into one person's undertaking to bear consequences.

On the way home Elena asked whether his repaired soles felt comfortable. Stepping across level paving, he said he'd need several days to know. He wouldn't declare them eternally reliable on the first step.

Smiling, she offered her hand without taking his first.

He placed his empty hand in hers. The other still held his own receipt. Their shared inquiry had begun. The doors not yet authorized remained closed.
