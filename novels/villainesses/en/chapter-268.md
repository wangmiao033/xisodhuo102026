# Chapter 268: An Embrace Before the Peak

On the afternoon of April 28, the first alarm after observation resumed at the outer console sounded like a copper coin thrown into an empty bowl.

Adrian had expected a more frightening sound to be louder. An engineer put down his water. Before the cup settled, another chime followed. Behind the window, one needle climbed slowly. The other jumped two divisions.

“Which one?”

“The thin needle. Preparatory drive. The thick one measures the existing protective system's load.”

The engineer didn't guess through glass. Leaving a colleague at the console, he took a lamp to the rear measuring port. The mechanical selector had only been restored that morning; a jam could distort readings. Adrian followed as far as the boundary and watched an independent gauge connect. The readings differed by less than half a division.

The acceleration was real.

The five underground locks remained sealed. That hadn't stopped external shocks reaching the disaster-control installation. Preparations begun on April 8 maintained readiness for a final procedure that might receive authorization. With the disaster load rising, the preparatory side was drawing more driving power under its old settings. It had sacrificed nobody yet. Already, it competed for limited maintenance reserves.

Evelyn's maintenance drawing lay on a dry table. Comparing its branches with actual lines, the engineer indicated a small wheel behind a lead seal.

“This backs down the preparation setting. First we must receive what's accumulated here. Turn it straight back and the recoil hits the shared upstream mounting.”

Adrian's left hand curled. He remembered the copper lever, and gray mist entering the western passage after he'd believed he had stopped the sacrifice. He put his hand behind him and made room.

“The main bridge?”

“Stays. Anyone touches it today, I'll move their hand.”

“Including mine.”

“Especially yours.”

Hurried footsteps sounded outside. At the passage corner, Viola sat with her left arm supported. She tapped her chair with her right hand, stopping a runner before the turn. His equipment box had nearly struck a surveyor coming out.

“Separate the incoming and outgoing sides. You can't see people below that box.”

She neither seized it nor stood to reclaim military command. The responsible officer moved an empty rack obstructing sight and stationed a guide at the corner. Viola moved her chair farther back for the stretcher clearance check.

In the outer room, Cecilia intercepted a young technician finishing his shift. Pale-lipped, he insisted he wasn't sleepy; he'd missed lunch. She put bread into his hand and asked his replacement to take the lamp. Only after his first bite did she ask about the independent gauge's handover reading.

Seeing people still attend to these things kept the rising needle from consuming Adrian's entire attention.

Luna sat in the more distant observation room, her right palm cushioned. An assistant brought local readings. Casting wasn't her task today; an approaching peak wouldn't restore her hands. After hearing the measurements, she said to find the containers specified for backing down before debating how much time they could gain.

The inventory listed two absorption cylinders. In the side store, salt had crusted beneath one, and dark traces seeped through its seal. The engineer rejected it. Inspection of the other's casing, gauge, and examination tag established that it could receive one discharge stage under the procedure—not the entire present accumulation.

“Two on paper, one available,” the assistant reported.

“Then today we have one,” Luna said. “Don't dry the other with wishful thinking.”

A replacement was in the academy maintenance store, not the experimental node used on April 26. That small bench test had ended. Its empty loads had never received the kingdom's disaster burden. An equivalent maintenance cylinder could be borrowed, however, with actual transport arranged and responsibility established for its return and consumed life.

Elena went to the transport supervisor. The carriage she'd arranged to take them home remained outside. Its driver had time left on his shift; its carrying capacity was sufficient, but it lacked a suitable securing frame. Instead of ordering an immediate departure, she checked the engineering crew's supports with the supervisor. Once the handlers confirmed secure transport, she canceled her own return journey and signed for the defined loan.

“How will you get back for dinner?” Adrian followed her to the door.

“On my legs. Or ask for a seat. Both cost less than putting you inside a cylinder.”

He paused. Looking back, she wasn't joking.

“I haven't offered myself as a replacement. I remembered the copper lever, and told the engineer.”

“Good. Keep telling me when you come back.”

The carriage departed. Elena stayed, hanging her cloak somewhere dry and remaining at the resource handover point.

Oswin came from the front console through the main entrance to the rear inspection room and submitted a fresh objection. No drawn swords or oracle cleared his path. He lifted the day's load chart. Eastern and near-suburban maintenance stations were losing reserves simultaneously. Backing down preparation would require new checks and consumables, consuming the next startup window.

“You call it suspension. What will those without reserves call it?”

Isabella stood beside the liaison desk, her supported left wrist cushioned. She didn't promise the kingdom would understand. Beside the chart she placed two reports delivered that morning: one evacuation needed lighting, another needed receiving accommodation.

“They need people doing this work, not reports declaring them saved. More pressure on preparation hasn't fulfilled the conditions behind the five locks either.”

The independent legal officer and facility custodian had already come because of the alarm. They weren't receiving a princess's command. The engineer opened the rear panel, showing the supervisor the separated lines and the main bridge's still-sealed cover. The local gauge rose another half division. The preparation temperature indicator began changing color.

Oswin pointed to the drawing. The backing-down procedure had been written for a lower state of preparation. His objection stopped the technician, who recalculated the present accumulation. One discharge following the old appendix would exceed the qualified cylinder's limit.

Luna's assistant brought a revised staged proposal: stop new input to preparation while retaining the main bridge, then alternate two verified maintenance cylinders for the limited residual. Each stage would await actual completed reception.

April 26 had demonstrated only that separated containers and local stopping could prevent closely spaced residuals crowding into one layer. Today's engineers still had to establish these capacities, interfaces, and sequence.

“We don't move until the second arrives,” the supervisor said.

“Any sudden change at the main bridge, we stop at hold. No further backing down,” the engineer added.

After checking the applicable preservation provisions, the legal officer authorized limited emergency inspection and controlled parking of the outer preparatory drive, based on the present danger and engineering supervisor's signature. This excluded the five locks, human connection, and core execution. Oswin's objection remained on record; an emergency order hadn't erased all his authority. What had changed was the lawful basis for operating one specified wheel.

They still had to wait for the carriage.

The outer passage suddenly dimmed. Not the whole city: a nearby stabilizing light plate tripped during the load change. A watchman replaced it with an ordinary lantern. As shadows lengthened, Adrian glanced underground anyway.

Someone was crying near the loading entrance. A messenger apprentice sat on the steps, his shoelace broken, his lamp nearly dropped. He'd just returned from the riverside, where plaster had fallen from a wall. His sister upstairs refused to leave her cat.

Adrian crouched to ask the address and receiving post. The apprentice rose, thinking Adrian would go himself. Instead, Adrian passed the address to the liaison, requesting contact through the ordinary patrol route still open. Leaving his recording station to rush to the river would leave two posts without relief.

“I can't guarantee they'll bring out the cat. But I'll tell them why she won't leave, not simply report refusal to evacuate.”

The apprentice wiped his face and sat to mend his shoe. Viola offered a spare cord, holding one end with her right hand while he tied it. Could he continue carrying messages? Ashamed, he shook his head.

“Then someone replaces you. You needn't run until your legs break to have done your duty.”

Adrian remembered admiring knights who never hesitated. This knight couldn't carry the boy's boxes or remove his fear for his sister. She could firmly allow him to stop. Adrian nodded to her. She gave him a brief smile.

“Don't put your admiration in the handover. Call the replacement.”

He did.

Cecilia put half a piece of bread into his hand. He protested that it belonged to someone else. The technician had shared it himself, she said; refusing food wasn't respect. One corner was crushed. Adrian ate anyway, discovering as he swallowed how painfully empty his stomach had become.

Passing with technical pages, Luna's assistant noticed. His teacher wanted a separate report that somebody had eaten.

“What does she need that for?”

“Apparently, proof that you sometimes follow simple instructions.”

Adrian laughed. The bell rang again. He swallowed his last mouthful and returned to the station others had kept covered.

Dusk entered through the high windows. Fetching a fresh timing sheet from the outer passage, he left the underground heat and discovered his back was soaked. Elena handed him water that hadn't quite cooled.

“You stopped breathing again.”

“I breathed. Just not very convincingly.”

She didn't smile. Looking at his hands, she asked where he'd be next. At the recording station, checking received signals. The duty engineer would operate. If backing down met resistance, Adrian still couldn't cross the boundary to touch the wheel.

“I'm afraid he's right,” he admitted. “That we'll spend the last window and people outside will still be hurt.”

“So am I.” She stepped closer. “May I hold you?”

He nodded. Her arms went around his back. Resting his forehead lightly near her shoulder, he heard her own unsteady breathing. Holding someone they loved made neither of their judgments infallible.

“You needn't save everyone alone before you're allowed back beside me. But tell me what you're doing. I don't want to hold someone who's always preparing to become his own last words.”

He closed his eyes briefly. He promised to come out on time at the next handover, not to save the entire world. When she released him, he held her hand a little longer.

“Dinner may be awful.”

“Let's give it the chance.”

An axle sounded in the yard. Handlers wheeled the maintenance cylinder to the entrance, dust from the journey coating its straps. Inspecting it, the engineer found its interface gasket unsuitable for direct use. He replaced it with an equivalent new one from the checked spare kit. Those few breaths of waiting seemed as long as the corridor.

Nobody hurried him.

The next local reading put the preparation temperature at the warning boundary. Two maintenance stations reported load rising in the same direction. The engineer judged a real peak was approaching, though its exact height and timing remained uncertain. Continued drawing by preparation would consume some of their limited margin before the main peak arrived.

Oswin examined the second cylinder, then looked toward the five locks.

“There is a more familiar course. Fulfill the inner conditions. Begin positioning the first marked bearer, and request resumption of final execution.”

Behind Adrian, Cecilia set down her cup.

No god was choosing a sacrifice. A person entitled to submit a request was preparing, once again, to put a living person's name first.
