# Chapter 277: Let the Crack See Daylight Too

On the afternoon of May 5, Luna asked to move her chair away from the display.

She wasn't afraid of the gray fragment. Qualified staff had checked the preserved item for hazardous residual energy before its authorized transfer. She simply didn't want everyone leaning forward for a look to see her right hand first.

Her assistant moved the chair and reset its back support and palm cushion. Seated, she lifted her chin at Adrian.

“Now I look like the lecturer, not a complimentary damage sample.”

“Shall we put that on a sign?”

“No. With too many words, you'll omit the waiting interval again.”

He laughed, accepting a joke with a real edge. His old diagram was public now. Nobody needed to avoid it to keep the afternoon pleasant.

The back rows needed to see the receiving layer broken on April 23. One corner lay on padding inside its box. The pale crack looked unimpressive, like misplaced old crockery. Verified images showed the impacts on the protective shield. The damaged support stayed with its original custodian; a presentation didn't justify another cartload of equipment.

The chair first asked whether anything would operate today.

“No,” Luna said. “Preserved items, original strips, and explanations of measurements taken then. The test rig remains disconnected and sealed. There are no human connections here, and no line to the Crown of Destiny.”

A young spectator looked disappointed. He'd expected to watch a witch separate disaster. Seeing him, Luna sighed theatrically. She too had once hoped the research budget might cover some attractive smoke.

“What happened?” he asked.

“We bought things that let people get out when something went wrong.”

The laughter was brief. Looking at the fracture, Adrian remembered the small crack of sound and his own raised hand. He didn't reach toward anything again. He simply passed the two separate records to the presenter.

The academy's independent observer introduced the instruments he'd operated during the failed trial. Both loads were nonliving, supplied by newly made independent energy. No people, marks, or old-core energy were connected. Total input remained within permission. Shortening withdrawal intervals in the second stage nevertheless brought residuals together in one receiving layer.

The recording strip was secured beside a uniformly enlarged copy, with its checker and original reference identified. An ordinary pointer traced the lines. The node's own total gauge still appeared calm when the independent near-branch record crossed the stop threshold. The stop followed. External absorption contained the total residual, but the internal layer broke.

A man at the back asked why protection working without injury couldn't count as a successful trial.

Luna directed him toward the gray layer.

“The shield and independent stop did their jobs. The internal reception we wanted failed. Call both success, and next time we may put someone where that material stood.”

The smile had left her voice.

The observer submitted actual losses and subsequent suspension. They hadn't replaced a piece and continued the same day. Damaged items remained preserved. Lost use of the room hadn't vanished because nobody bled. Material defects and instrument response still contained unresolved questions. The public drawing identified the known and unknown rather than compressing every cause into one clever answer.

Next came April 26.

Real replacement, purchasing, and inspection separated the trials. A wrong-sized lining ring had been returned; the old layer wasn't mixed into new material. The support and external absorber had been assessed anew. Separate buffers retained their own margins, with independent near-branch measurement. Somebody announcing ready from farther away couldn't admit the next stage.

“Begin before startup, please,” Luna said.

The observer showed the cold rehearsal and actual configuration records before unrolling the stable trial's strip. After the first branch withdrew, the second wasn't forced to swallow its residual. Actual clearance and independent confirmation preceded another stage. Closely spaced withdrawals entered separate buffers. An unexpected stop within the authorized window had also been completed through the independent chain, without awaiting Luna's judgment.

A repairer requested another pause at the two withdrawals. The inlet was closed, yet the trace continued rising. Instead of saying that was how magic worked, the observer used common timing marks to isolate the strip between the closure command and completed reception.

“What was already on its way couldn't disappear because more stopped coming,” the repairer said slowly.

Luna nodded and asked him to follow until both readings genuinely fell. He'd repaired water channels, he said, and knew water remained after a valve closed, but didn't dare treat the hazards as identical. That caution was good, she answered. A familiar comparison wasn't grounds for deciding another person could enter. Seated again, he crossed out immediate withdrawal and replaced it with a sentence still carrying a question mark.

Luna leaned back and let the observer finish. Her assistant offered water only when she nodded, without using the cup to conceal her bandaged hand.

A supportive noble wondered why, after success, they couldn't make as many containers as there were people.

“Changing scale can change materials, transmission, delay, and failure,” Luna said. “Stability belongs to that verified configuration, those inputs, and those intervals. Drawing a little boat larger doesn't give it a keel fit for an ocean.”

“But we must believe it can be done.”

“You may want research to continue. Don't sign for places we haven't measured.”

Adrian saw the disappointment and felt a familiar urgency. One more hopeful sentence might win support; it almost resembled kindness. Instead, he stood and acknowledged having circulated a summary broader than his evidence. The correction and original diagram were available. Two empty loads still wouldn't be renamed a completed nationwide replacement.

The noble didn't immediately withdraw support. He asked what the next research expenditure would buy. That required a separate concrete proposal and approval, Luna said. This presentation wasn't gathering unlimited commitments. Unhappy, he sat, but hadn't been handed a completion date that didn't exist.

Oswin rose next. He neither attacked her injuries nor called her a liar. What did the main bridge still bear? Had the controlled parking on April 28 used these small nodes?

The engineering representative explained that the protective main bridge maintained existing shielding. Parking had used verified existing maintenance cylinders for bounded preparatory-drive residuals, separate from the academy rig. The cylinders' consumption and further inspection had their own records. Stable new nodes couldn't restore their capacity.

“Then these strips don't establish your ability to bear the next kingdom-wide peak,” Oswin said.

“They don't,” Luna answered.

He looked at her as though awaiting a qualification. She merely requested complete recording of the answer and let the engineer explain current protection. That boundary made neither the old manufactured charges true nor the five locks open.

Someone in the audience raised a hand and volunteered to try. A young copying clerk, sleeves worn shiny, said he understood the risk. If real people never tried, they would never know.

Adrian moved where he could hear without inviting him forward.

“There is no human trial or enrollment today.”

“Nobody's forcing me.”

“I believe you're offering freely now. But we don't have enough assessment to tell you what you'd actually be agreeing to.”

Flushing, the young man asked whether only educated people were entitled to volunteer. Cecilia explained that declining this spontaneous offer changed neither his right to attend, obtain public material, nor receive ordinary assistance. Nobody was grading his courage. He could leave questions. A hearing attendance record wouldn't become permission to use his body.

Still dissatisfied, he lowered his head, finally asking how he could recognize genuine arrangements if things changed later.

The archivist provided the existing public inquiry office's address, not an expression-of-interest form. He took it back to his seat. Today he remained simply someone who had attended.

That exchange used the last of Luna's allotted participation time. She checked the clock and signaled her assistant. Someone still wanted to ask whether other materials could work in separate buffers. She directed the question to the observer. Unstudied matters would remain for later; fatigue wouldn't become a reason to answer by instinct.

Adrian accompanied her to the quiet rest room. Once seated, her left hand trembled slightly. Her attendant rechecked the support under the existing care plan and advised against small print for now. This neither inflicted a new injury nor suddenly established that the old one had healed.

“Who was most disappointed today?” she asked.

“The person wanting smoke?”

“Me. Showing my cleverest work apparently takes half a day explaining how much cleverness it still lacks.”

He sat in the chair she permitted. “It does more than the previous version. You needn't hide that.”

Turning aside, she smiled faintly, genuinely relaxed. “You're learning to praise in the right-sized words.”

“Need them smaller?”

“No. Keep that one.”

She requested the travel book, selecting only a short passage about a traveler who repaired his boat and still had to wait for weather. At the author's complaint that wind refused to respect effort, she laughed aloud and proposed giving him a lesson in materials.

“Once he reaches port,” Adrian said.

“Yes. Let him reach port first.”

He closed the book on time. She didn't require him to remain as a guard over exhaustion. Could the next passage concern somebody without a boat? He would ask when suited her, he promised. She settled against her pillow, leaving subsequent work with her assistant.

In the hall, the independent observer answered what he could. Stable and failed strips were collected together, with public reading and verification routes maintained. There had been no power supplied, no new connection, and no renewed permission for the suspended configuration merely because people had seen it.

Elena waited at the door when Adrian returned. After hearing that Luna was resting, she asked quietly whether he wanted dinner without using containers as metaphors.

“Can't even discuss a soup bowl?”

“You may discuss whether it contains soup.”

He accepted, laughing, then confirmed that remaining documents could be handed over on time. Agreement wouldn't become another polite postponement.

At reception, Isabella's left wrist remained supported. With her right hand, she passed a checked catalog to the custodian. The royal co-signing order and previously obtained financial material would enter the next review under newly approved disclosure limits. Complete records stayed within the original custodians' and hearing officers' handover arrangements, not in the princess's private keeping.

The chair confirmed continuation with royal material on May 6. Nothing would be read prematurely tonight; a catalog didn't establish every responsibility. Oswin received his next attendance arrangements, retaining the opportunity to make lawful submissions.

Isabella watched the packet move to the shelf marked with tomorrow's date. Only then did her fingers slowly release the catalog.

Daylight remained outside. The gray layer returned to its padded box; stable traces rolled into their own holders. Today people had seen that a small portion of risk had genuinely found somewhere to go. They'd seen how much space remained empty too.

Nobody had been put inside to fill it.
