# Chapter 101: Old Notes and a Lunch Date

On December 14, Adrian reached the old equipment store a little before opening time.

Luna was already outside with two cups. There was no pen tucked into her hair today. A narrow ribbon held the dark strands behind her shoulder, leaving an unfamiliar little space beside her ear.

“Where's your pen?” he asked.

“You see me, and that's the first thing you look for?”

“You usually wear it more reliably than earrings.”

She handed him hot tea. “In my pocket. And you are not to announce that I changed my ribbon to impress the archivist.”

“I hadn't asked.”

“You may continue not asking.”

Adrian accepted the cup and took a proper look at her.

“It looks lovely.”

Luna opened her mouth, apparently searching for something that would restore her advantage. Fortunately for her, the door opened. Finding two people standing in the cold with tea, the administrator first moved them out of the way.

“The drinks table is outside the reading room. No hot water inside.”

“We were just discussing the most important rule here,” Luna said.

“Were you?”

“Yes,” Adrian agreed. “Don't spill the tea.”

They left their cups beneath the window and registered the volume they needed. Luna had permission to consult the tower's equipment records. Even so, the administrator confirmed that Adrian would be reading the relevant maintenance volumes, without staff members' private records being slipped into the same pile.

Opening the cupboard released the smell of old paper and dry wood. Adrian had expected old experimental notes to be bound in black leather and locked in a cursed chest. What reached the table was a faded blue cloth ledger bearing a repairer's oily fingerprints.

The first page counted replacement screws.

The second complained about a leaking window.

The third finally mentioned R-4, beneath a thoroughly unmagical stain left by the rain from page two.

Luna sat beside him. “Disappointed?”

“A little. I had a very serious expression ready.”

“Save it for the bill. Those tend to outlast curses.”

She pointed to a transcription reference in the margin instead of immediately declaring the passage their answer. The volume had come from the old equipment store. Thinner maintenance supplements remained stitched into their original places. First, they had to establish whether this entry concerned the gauge they actually had.

Adrian checked the casing number against the small print on the maintenance card. The gauge had received a replacement outer ring but retained its original spindle and damping chamber. Two recorded repairs matched the equipment drawing.

“Here.” He laid a paper marker beside the passage, making no marks on the original.

The old technician had written: Retain damping for stable readings during sustained heating. Use a fast-response instrument for brief excitation.

A smaller line crowded the binding: Needle at zero does not establish complete dissipation of retained heat.

Luna's face grew still.

“The new card copied the static calibration and left out the conditions of use.”

“Can we find out who omitted them?”

“We start with the transcription history. Someone might have shortened it for a new form, or failed to understand it. We don't get to choose the person we'd most like to blame.”

She copied the two passages side by side and asked the administrator to certify their source references. They then turned to the test diagram following the supplement. Its curve began slowly and only later caught up with the measured temperature. It explained yesterday's late needle remarkably well.

This was not a gauge secretly swallowing risk. Its damping had been intended to reduce fluctuations during long measurements. Under a short pulse, that useful feature became a delay serious enough to mislead them.

Adrian remembered skipping the small text beside equipment descriptions in games.

Now a line of small text determined which second was the right one to stop on.

The administrator passed the approved copies to Tessa. Downstairs, she checked the gauge against a stable warm block and another calibrated thermometer. She did not connect the paired plates or supply fresh power to yesterday's circuit. Once the temperature settled, the brass needle reached the correct mark. When she changed the block, it visibly lagged.

Using her left hand, Tessa unscrewed the little inspection cover beside the casing. Nothing about that ordinary repair work implied that she had agreed to a new observation of herself.

“The damping chamber isn't broken,” she said. “It's doing the wrong job. The attachment instructions need to travel with the gauge. Keeping them perfectly safe in a book doesn't help.”

Luna nodded and personally amended the out-of-service label. Replacing it with a suitable pulse gauge would require another inspection appointment. They could not simply repeat the trial today.

The lunch bell was ringing when they left the workshop.

Elena waited beneath the covered walk with two newly arrived commercial letters. She had not abandoned her own accounts to spend the morning waiting. She had come at the agreed time.

“Found the cause?”

“Found a cause that can be checked,” Adrian said.

“That deserves lunch.”

“I thought we deserved lunch either way.”

“You're getting better at defending your terms of employment.”

She tucked one letter into her book satchel and gave the other to the administrator for dispatch. The merchants needed to identify the people who had handled the goods. A Rosenberg mark could no longer stand in for an entire chain of custody. Those matters had not resolved themselves merely because they had left the border.

Luna went to wash her hands. Elena and Adrian waited by the door while the dining room made space. She picked a scrap of paper from his cuff, then asked about his afternoon.

“A walk with her. Then more records.”

“I know. I was at the table yesterday.”

“Is there something else you wanted to ask?”

She dropped the scrap into the wastebasket. After a moment, she said, “I was wondering whether I ought to make tonight sound more attractive. Perhaps the captain finally meets a beautiful woman who can keep track of her belongings.”

“That would have a serious effect on the length of the book.”

Elena laughed, but her hand remained near his sleeve.

“I do mind a little. But I don't want every little feeling to sound like a request for you to cancel someone else's afternoon.”

Adrian gave her his full attention. “What would you like me to do?”

“Come and find me tonight. Don't wait until we happen to meet in the corridor and then ask whether it's time to read.”

“All right. I'll knock.”

“And don't choose a chapter from the maintenance ledger.”

“Even if it stars an unusually faithful damping attachment?”

“Especially then.”

He took her hand and asked whether he could kiss her forehead. She glanced at the people passing along the corridor. Her ears slowly turned pink, but she did not move away.

“Yes. Don't keep the whole dining room waiting.”

The kiss was light. When Adrian drew back, her eyes looked brighter. Neither had promised never to feel jealous again. A wish that had been difficult to express had simply received an answer he could keep.

The dining-room door opened. Luna had finished washing and was sighing over a fingertip that apparently intended to remain ink-stained forever. Elena released his hand and made room at the table for the ordinary clerk who had just delivered the post, so he could eat before his lunch went cold.

At the table, Cecilia showed Adrian the bookmark she had finally finished.

It was still not entirely straight. A short tail of yarn remained at one end.

“That makes it easier to find inside the book,” she explained.

“I thought it was the flask making one last attempt to hold on.”

The saint laughed and set it beside his hand. “It's washable. Getting it wet won't cancel our friendship.”

Luna studied the bookmark and announced that she should learn a craft that did not require certification.

“Start by sewing your button back on,” Viola suggested.

Everyone looked at the loose button on the witch's sleeve. Luna lowered her eyes and finally understood why her cuff had recently developed such an interest in her soup.

“I thought it had an adventurous spirit.”

Viola pushed over the sewing kit and explained how to secure the thread on the reverse instead of doing it for her. Adrian moved the soup bowl out of reach and watched Luna wrestle with a very short length of thread.

“Don't laugh,” she said.

“I haven't.”

“Your eyes are preparing to.”

“They started practicing when you were eating honey yesterday.”

She looked up. Her ears reddened first, and the thread acquired an unnecessary knot.

He did not forget their walk after lunch.

The old lifting platform still bore its maintenance notice. Its repaired components awaited the administrator's acceptance. They walked slowly along the other side of the courtyard, past a line of drying aprons. Luna glanced at her newly secured cuff and admitted she had wanted to show him something she was better at.

“Such as?”

“Making an empty cup spin around a table. Much more impressive than sewing.”

“You'd have to explain whether it might fly off first.”

She sighed. “Even your compliments leave an escape route.”

“I actually liked watching you work on the button.”

Luna paused, then continued in slightly smaller steps.

“Because I didn't have the answer?”

“Because you were willing to learn. Also, every time you tried to pretend you'd known how all along, you had to glance at Viola first.”

“I should charge you an observation fee.”

Wind stirred the damp cloth on the line, and she raised a hand to keep it away. Adrian asked whether she wanted another circuit. She looked toward the open storeroom window, then shook her head.

“One circuit is one circuit. I still need to follow that cross-reference this afternoon.”

“I'll come too.”

She turned to him with a quieter smile than the one she had worn when she invited him.

That afternoon, Elena joined them at the reading table. The cross-reference led to an early comparative study of paired contractual carriers. Luna had marked the old diagram that morning because one return channel differed from the published installation drawing.

The notebook did not explain human betrothal for them. It recorded the temperature and deposition responses of blank carrier sleeves with different linings. A note explicitly stated that no signatories' names were included.

Beneath the drawing was a number still present in the current inventory: C-17.

The administrator consulted the custody register. C-17 was an old twin-channel contract sleeve held in the tower's collection. Its transfer into custody, subsequent openings, and condition inspections were all recorded. It was neither an original they had taken from Greymarch nor Cecilia's historical betrothal record.

“It's still here?” Adrian asked.

“In the inner cabinet upstairs. You can inspect its exterior. Removing the lining isn't covered by today's permission.”

They followed him up. The administrator entered the opening time in the register. Luna did not reach past him for the key.

The specimen was smaller than Adrian had expected. Two shallow channels lay side by side in the copper sleeve. A half-circle of gray-silver lining remained along the outer edge. In slanting light, an exceedingly fine boundary appeared and disappeared. The glass lid stayed closed. Adrian did not bring his left hand near it.

Luna bent lower, breathing lightly.

“The published drawing simplified this into one material. The original trial recorded two different treatments.”

“So our replica plates aren't equivalent to it?” Elena asked.

“No. Yesterday established something about the channel we made. To explain an old specimen, we first need to preserve the differences no one has remade.”

Her gaze settled on a technical care sheet attached to the box.

No pressure contact with surfaces. Do not detach lining. Keep inner support level during transport.

Those thin deposits were not paint that could be brushed on again. Cleaning or pressing a seal against them could erase the interfaces between successive materials, leaving a neat, intact copper sleeve that could never answer their questions.

Adrian asked the administrator to include the care sheet in the materials for tomorrow's inspection.

“When the inspectors come, we can confirm how it should be kept,” he said.

The administrator agreed. They returned the box to its place, shut the cabinet, and checked the seal. They did not give the old object an unapproved exposure merely to obtain an answer before the inspection.

On the stairs, Luna suddenly looked back.

“The walk today counts as the thing you promised me. It's finished.”

“I wasn't going to substitute the afternoon's research.”

“I know. Just making sure you don't say that one walk with a witch always ends in an archive.”

Adrian laughed. Elena waited below. When he reached her, she lightly touched his right hand.

“Tonight, then. The captain really ought to find a clock that keeps time.”

“I have doubts about his budget.”

“You can read to me while you doubt it.”

The notice for the fifteenth's inspection was posted beside the tower door. The last winter sunlight crossed the paper, illuminating the scheduled arrival time.

In the upstairs cabinet, the contract sleeve that could not withstand a pressure seal waited quietly.

They now knew why it deserved to be preserved.

Tomorrow, they would need the people with the authority to remove it to hear them out.
