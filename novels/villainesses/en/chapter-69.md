# Why Do All the Villainesses Want to Marry Me?
## Volume Three: The Knight Says She's Responsible for Me
### Chapter 69: The Saint Reached for the Bandages First

When the relief wagon stopped outside Greymarch's infirmary on the morning of November 16, the first thing Adrian Reed saw was a muddy boot.

Then the other one. Cecilia held the carriage door and negotiated the last step with great care. Only when she had solid ground beneath her did she smile at him.

“I understand the cushions now.”

“The cushions aren't enough. I'm going to suggest that posting houses rent out spare skeletons.”

She laughed, her breath briefly hiding her mouth. Two days on the road had left shadows beneath her eyes. A crooked cloth flower was pinned to her scarf.

Adrian recognized the stitching. “Mira?”

“She felt a relief party shouldn't travel with nothing but bandages. The office is covered, too. She and the others are taking turns, and Vera will handle visitors who need legal help.”

The appeal sent on the thirteenth had reached the capital by express relay early on the fourteenth. Cecilia had finished two receipt appointments already scheduled for that early hour, then left that morning with the licensed medical staff. They had changed horses twice. Last night's posting house had such a draft that she had stuffed spare socks into the window frame before managing a few hours of sleep.

“I'm glad to see you,” Adrian said.

She paused with her hands on her scarf.

“So am I. Especially standing here to meet me.”

She offered her hand. He held her cold fingers until someone behind the wagon called for room, and they stepped aside together.

Elena approached carrying the delivery register. She took one look at Cecilia's face.

“There's hot water inside. How much sleep did you get?”

“Enough not to fall out of the carriage.”

“Let's not make that the standard.”

They exchanged a careful hug. Elena kept the strain off her healing hand; Cecilia made sure not to catch it. Then both women called Adrian back from the medicine chests.

He raised his empty hands. “I was reading the labels.”

“Then read them from there.”

Even their tone matched.

Inside, Dr. Miriam Shaw was briefing the new arrivals. Her hair was wound tightly up, her cuffs faded from washing. The title of saint earned only a glance.

“What care work have you done?”

Cecilia listed assisting with dressings, checking supplies, and reconciling patient records. Dr. Shaw gave her a clean apron and pointed toward the basin.

“Wash first. Then follow the nurse to the east room. We handle medicines and wounds. The most useful skill today is listening before you fetch something.”

“I've had practice.”

“Good. Several noble visitors still haven't mastered it.”

Elena quietly withdrew behind the supplies table.

Cecilia first helped the nurse change bedding in the west room. A weaver was clutching her boots and refusing to surrender them. She had already lost her wool, loom, and winter clothes. These were all she had left.

Rather than pry them away, Cecilia brought a chair and set the boots within easy reach.

“They'll still be yours when they're clean. May we ask the doctor to look at your feet?”

At last the woman nodded. There was blood on her socks. While the nurse fetched Dr. Shaw, Cecilia stayed beside the chair, wiping mud from the other boot and listening to her talk about the loom she had owned for twenty years.

When Adrian brought fresh sheets, Cecilia was still holding a loose lace, keeping watch over something no one would be allowed to take from the woman again.

A separate guard post stood outside the east room, where Ellis was kept apart from the villagers. His discarded clothing had been secured as evidence; his clean shirt bore no colors. His wound had worsened overnight, and by morning he had a fever. Dr. Shaw was preparing to treat it again.

A middle-aged man bringing water overheard the arrangements and set his bucket down hard.

“He gets the fresh bandages first? My brother's still in the west room.”

His cuff was charred, the back of his hand badly scraped. An empty basket leaned beside the door. Even while he argued, his gaze kept returning to it, as though it were the last thing he owned.

Dr. Shaw glanced toward the nurse in the west room.

“Your brother's dressing has been changed. I'll see him again shortly. This patient is at risk from bleeding and fever.”

“He was part of the attack. We save his life so he can burn whose house next?”

The guard blocked the doorway without drawing his sword. Cecilia accepted the bandages the nurse handed her, staying where the man could hear.

“He'll remain under guard. Treating him doesn't erase what happened to you.”

“Will you ask us to forgive him?”

She was quiet for a moment.

“I won't ask that of you.”

His jaw worked. Cecilia didn't use his silence to launch into a sermon about mercy. The doctor called, and she carried the bandages inside.

Adrian didn't crowd in after her. He straightened a bed marker in the west room and helped the man find his brother. A nurse was helping the injured man drink. Recognizing his visitor, he managed to lift his unhurt hand.

The empty basket went down at the foot of the bed. Its owner finally sat.

Before noon, the clean cloth Elena had ordered and paid for arrived. She had the nurses inspect it before directing the driver to the storeroom. An old shelf had broken. Ruth brought two people to replace the boards and complained that the infirmary's accounts desk was too low.

An elderly woman from Willowford was sitting by the window, rolling cloth for outer dressings. When talk turned to the east room again, she kept working.

“When my son came here, the doctor didn't ask whose land he farmed. Let them get on with it.”

Nobody applauded. Some remained unconvinced. They stopped blocking the passage all the same.

During the treatment, Ellis saw an instrument in Dr. Shaw's hand and suddenly shrank toward the wall. The guard moved forward. Cecilia didn't reach out to stop him; following the doctor's signal, she asked him to make sure the patient was actually trying to escape.

“I'll tell you what I'm going to do,” Dr. Shaw told Ellis.

The nurse steadied his shoulder while the doctor gradually removed the soiled dressing. Cecilia passed what they needed. Every gasp of pain tightened her own grip on the tray. She didn't promise it wouldn't hurt. She repeated the doctor's explanation.

Once the treatment was finished, Dr. Shaw left a nurse to monitor him. Cecilia helped secure the outer dressing, releasing it only when told. Wisps of hair clung to her damp forehead. There was blood on her apron.

Ellis half opened his eyes.

“I haven't got any money.”

“These relief supplies have already been accounted for,” she said. “The infirmary handles the costs. Rest.”

“What do you want me to say?”

Cecilia handed the empty tray to the nurse.

“Nobody's trading you bandages for answers.”

His eyes closed. It was afternoon before the hand clenched around the blanket slowly relaxed.

Adrian was copying supply receipts outside when Ellis called to the guard of his own accord.

“There's something I can tell you. I don't know everything.”

Dr. Shaw examined him first. She permitted a short conversation, on the condition that she could stop it whenever necessary. A municipal recorder was summoned. Viola stayed outside and let the duty sergeant attend. Adrian moved his own papers to another table. The patient's name had no place among the donated supplies.

Through the open door, Ellis explained that the man who recruited him claimed to be hiring casual labor for a civilian freight contractor. He had never met the actual employer. He had received an advance. Before they set off, someone had distributed blue triangular pennants and told them to tie them to poles.

“They said they belonged to a foreign army.” He wet his cracked lips. “Taught us something to shout, too. I didn't understand it. There was extra money if I learned.”

“Do you belong to that army?”

“No. I've never joined it, anyway.”

The recorder took down his words without turning one man's account into an established identity for the entire party. Outside, Adrian thought of the previous day's order for flag cloth and spear shafts. Here was a specific account of a disguised raid they could investigate, rather than guesses based on knots in a banner.

“Who was in command?” the recorder asked.

“I don't know his name. Gray cloak. More than one man giving orders.”

“What were your orders?”

Ellis glanced toward the doorway. A villager had just passed on crutches.

“Drive them toward the high bridge. Don't let them scatter down to the river.”

His voice dwindled. “I thought we were only blocking the road. Then the fires started.”

Viola's fingers tightened beside her scabbard. She didn't supply an innocent explanation for what he meant by “then.”

Asked whether he could recognize the hiring yard, Ellis remembered a whitewashed wall and a broken gate. He couldn't even clearly say how many nights he had stayed there. Dr. Shaw ended the conversation.

There were things to check. His real name, the contractor, and the plan behind the attack could not be established by looking at his exhausted face.

Later, Cecilia washed her hands and found Adrian in the back courtyard. He was trying to stifle a yawn over a jug of water.

“Dr. Shaw says you haven't had any more chest discomfort today?”

“No. I've spent all afternoon behaving myself in a chair.”

She sat beside him. At last, her shoulders sagged a little.

“I think some people are going to hate me.”

“That man just asked a nurse whether the west room needs firewood. He hasn't left.”

She watched the fallen leaves for a while before smiling.

“Thank you for not telling me everyone will understand soon.”

Adrian poured her some warm water. She accepted the cup, brushing its rim with her fingers.

“But I would like to sit beside someone who's glad I'm here.”

He steadied the crooked stool beside him.

“He isn't going anywhere for a while.”

She lowered her head to drink. The weariness remained in her eyes, but her mouth slowly curved.

Elena found them, an escort schedule in her hand.

“I have a question.” She looked toward Viola, who had just entered the courtyard. “Eighteen hundred. Mr. Reed's supper. Captain responsible. Have the two of you made plans?”

Adrian nearly swallowed his water the wrong way.

Viola read the entry. Her ears reddened.

“Responsible for getting him to the inn. This is for the patrol, not the kitchen.” She unfolded the bottom half. “Everyone else is here, too. Visitors who want company can walk back together when work finishes at the infirmary.”

Elena found her own name. After a moment, she said quite plainly, “My mistake. I thought you'd invited him to a private dinner, and I was a little jealous.”

She turned to Adrian.

“I'd been thinking of asking you.”

“I was going to ask whether you'd still like to sit by the window with me tonight.”

“I would. After supper?”

“After supper.”

Viola took out a pen as though about to enter the amendment. Its point hovered over the paper, then slowly withdrew.

Watching her, Cecilia asked, “Captain, where does your own supper appear?”

It didn't.

“After the change of watch, if there's time—”

“You've arranged supper for the entire town,” Cecilia said, “and forgotten that knights need to eat.”

Adrian laughed first. Then he set his empty cup aside.

“Let's all eat together. Captain, that's an invitation. You don't need to put a duty beside your name.”

Viola looked down at the schedule.

“May I have the stew?”

“Did you think we only let guards eat bread?” Elena asked.

“I'd heard the inn was making stew today.”

All three of them laughed. After a startled pause, Viola joined in.

At supper, she did sit down. The landlady brought Elena a shallow bowl so she wouldn't need her injured hand to steady a heavy soup basin. Cecilia was about to praise this thoughtfulness when Elena rescued the scarf trailing beside her chair.

“You don't need to keep your armor on at the table.”

“For a moment, I thought you meant the captain.”

“Hers won't soak up the soup.”

Viola glanced down at her chest and discovered she had become an exhibit.

Cecilia was tired enough to mistake the salt for the sugar. Elena intercepted it. Adrian contributed half a piece of bread and announced that a perfectly innocent cup of tea had been saved.

Firelight warmed all four faces. It hadn't settled the arguments at the infirmary. For a little while, though, those arguments couldn't fill every chair.

Owen arrived with the next day's notice while Viola was eating her second spoonful of stew.

Major Voss required her at the garrison on the morning of the seventeenth to explain the patrol's actions after receiving the redirect order on the fourteenth, and her decision to bring an unidentified attacker inside the town.

She read the notice, folded it, and continued eating. Only the hand holding her spoon moved much more slowly.

Adrian didn't ask whether everything would be all right, making her promise it for everyone else.

He pushed the still-warm stew pot a little closer to her.
