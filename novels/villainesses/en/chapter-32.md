# Why Do All the Villainesses Want to Marry Me?
## Volume Two: The Saint Won't Let Me Get Married
### Chapter 32: The Temple Doesn't Get to Answer for Me

The letter that arrived on October twelfth gave Adrian Reed a headache considerably worse than anything printed in the marriage gossip columns.

Newspapers could, at most, announce that he was getting married.

The Temple's administrative office appeared to have decided that he'd already agreed to be investigated.

Hale read the notice aloud.

*"Mr. Adrian Reed, identified as a related party to historical contract R-074, is requested to report to the Old Contract Records Office at three this afternoon for verification of his registered identity. Failure to attend shall be noted in the historical betrothal register."*

Adrian read it again.

"So if I don't show up, they add another stamp beside my name?"

Hale examined the seal, authority line, and regulations cited in the footnotes.

"There's no judicial order here restricting your freedom. It's an administrative attendance notice. They can record that you didn't appear. They cannot establish that you're married or betrothed by recording your absence."

"Then why does 'registered identity' sound as though somebody plans to put me in a drawer?"

"Because it's poorly worded," Cecilia said.

She wore cream-colored day clothes rather than her ceremonial robes. Her badge from the independent review committee remained pinned at her collar.

She laid two documents side by side.

One was the administrative notice.

The other was an objection she'd written herself.

"The duty office claims to be following an old summons procedure for persons named in a historical contract," she explained. "That procedure was superseded when the present civil law came into force. It doesn't give them authority to compel attendance."

Elena stood by the window.

"Then he won't go."

"He can decline an unauthorized compulsory appearance," Hale agreed. "Whether he wishes to participate in a limited, voluntary records examination is his decision."

"I know," Elena replied.

She was already reaching for the reply letter when her hand stopped.

"Adrian. Do you want to go?"

He knew what she'd been about to do.

She wanted to step between him and another official request that might become a trap.

"I want to find out what's in that record," he said. "But I'm not going into a Temple questioning room alone. And I'm not agreeing to a physical examination without knowing what it involves."

"So you don't want to go."

"Not quite. Could we arrange a meeting in a neutral reading room at the academy? Counsel present, independent witnesses, documents only."

Elena was quiet for a few seconds.

Then she nodded.

"All right. Then those are the terms you should put forward."

Cecilia looked pleased.

"I can provide the legal basis. Under my committee's investigative authorization, the original custodian cannot use refusal of an unauthorized examination to block reasonable access to certified documents."

"Won't opposing your own department get you into trouble?" Adrian asked.

"It might cost me access to some internal records."

She said it as plainly as though discussing the weather.

"I'll put every objection in the committee's minutes. If I say nothing, there won't even be evidence that anyone objected."

Adrian watched her.

This was more than an argument over legal wording.

She was making a choice she might have to pay for.

They drafted a voluntary document-examination request with narrow limits: the materials to be shown, those authorized to attend, and the bodily tests expressly excluded.

Hale would attend as counsel.

Nora would manage the academy's reading room.

The appointment would take place only after both sides agreed.

Elena added one more condition.

"No one may call this meeting a betrothal ceremony."

"Agreed," Cecilia said. "No historical wedding vows, hand-clasping rites, or language declaring a marriage unless both adults expressly ask for them."

Adrian nearly stood to applaud.

"You may be the most helpful saint I've ever met in connection with an engagement."

Cecilia considered the compliment.

"Thank you. Though being good at clarifying betrothals doesn't mean I'm unsuitable for marriage."

Adrian's thanks caught in his throat.

Elena slowly turned her head.

"Miss Saintclair, that last part has nothing to do with the review."

"Correct," Cecilia said. "I'm practicing keeping my professional conclusions separate from my personal wishes."

She was so sincere Elena had nothing useful to say.

On the carriage ride to Saint Rose Academy, Adrian read the limited authorization again.

The final paragraph caught his attention.

*The participant may request a reasonable pause or end the interview without prejudicing the preservation of already secured evidence.*

"So I can stop halfway through?"

"Yes," Hale said. "Provided you don't alter the sealed material, nobody can force you to explain your entire history in one sitting."

"And if I simply don't want to discuss the betrothal?"

"Then you don't have to."

Elena answered so quickly that even she paused.

Adrian smiled at her.

"You're beginning to sound more like my lawyer than Hale."

"I'm reminding myself of something," she said quietly. "Liking you doesn't mean I'm entitled to every answer I want."

Across from them, Cecilia picked up her pencil.

"That's a very good principle for the Temple's examination rules."

"Please don't build religious law out of our dating difficulties!"

Cecilia looked genuinely puzzled.

"Why not? It applies to every participant."

Hale finally laughed.

"It is a sound principle. We simply don't need to cite the couple's private life in the rule."

Cecilia nodded and carefully crossed out their names.

The academy's east-wing meeting room had been prepared with two separate desks.

One for displaying documents.

One for keeping the official record.

There was plain paper, ink, and good daylight.

No ritual circles.

No impressive altar.

No apparatus capable of taking a magical sample without anyone noticing.

Nora stood beside the register, plainly satisfied with the arrangement.

A young records clerk had arrived from the Temple's administrative office.

He checked the restricted authorization and looked displeased.

"Normally, related parties are examined at the Temple's inner sanctuary."

"Under which current provision?" Cecilia asked.

He produced an old handbook.

"Article thirty-two of the previous regulations."

Hale placed a copy of the revised law beside it.

"That provision was replaced twenty years ago."

The clerk flushed.

"But this is how we've always handled these cases."

"Doing something routinely doesn't make it legal forever," Adrian said. "We're prepared to examine documents today. We're not consenting to samples or an escort elsewhere."

The clerk looked down at a blue pouch beside his chair.

"I still need Mr. Reed's identity confirmation mark. Otherwise I have nothing to report."

"What sort of mark?" Hale asked.

"His left hand pressed against the registration sheet. The standard fate-signature formula reads his imprint."

Cecilia put a hand on the pouch before he could open it.

"That is magical sampling, not a receipt for documents. Today's authorization expressly excludes it."

"Even ordinary birth registrations use handprints," the clerk protested.

"Then use an ordinary signature, existing identity papers, and the independent witnesses to acknowledge today's attendance," Adrian replied. "If a magical test becomes necessary later, send me the procedure and its risks first."

Nora slid the non-magical visitor register toward them.

It had a perfectly ordinary space for a handwritten signature.

After a long hesitation, the Temple clerk used a conventional receipt stamp and recorded that the procedure had been modified at the participant's request.

Cecilia visibly relaxed when she saw the words.

Once the clerk returned to his desk, she said quietly, "I used to think it was natural to take a magical handprint because everyone had always done it. I didn't recognize how different that is from consenting to receive a letter."

"I'm learning too," Adrian said. "You don't have to pretend you were born knowing every rule."

She looked up and smiled.

The actual document comparison could finally begin.

Nora brought out a certified tracing of the academy loan record from seven years earlier.

Cecilia displayed the authenticated image of the historical betrothal.

Hale contributed two recent forms Adrian had signed in person.

Three sets of signatures lay side by side on an angled reading board.

Adrian recognized the strange inward curl at the end of his name.

"They really do look alike."

"We can establish similarities in letter forms," Nora said. "We cannot establish who wrote them without examining stroke order, pressure, and signs of tracing or later reproduction."

The Temple clerk pointed to a circular mark in the lower-right corner of the old contract.

"That's the seal for joint attendance. Surely it establishes that both people were present."

Cecilia shook her head.

"A seal proves that somebody applied a seal. It doesn't prove the two people named were physically in the room."

Elena leaned forward.

"Were both allowed to refuse?"

The clerk had no answer.

Nora fetched a copy of the old regulations. The relevant note distinguished *intent pending confirmation* from *agreement completed*. The former could be entered provisionally. The latter required two separately verified signatures.

"So this might simply be a registration awaiting verification," Adrian said.

"Correct," Hale replied. "It may be significant evidence. It is not proof of a binding present-day engagement."

For once, the young clerk acknowledged that he'd never been taught to distinguish the two forms.

They spent the rest of the afternoon documenting precisely which documents they'd seen and which they hadn't.

Adrian read the line *participation does not imply acceptance of the historical betrothal* twice before signing.

Elena stood beside him.

"I nearly refused for you again this morning," she said quietly.

He turned.

"I know you were worried."

"But I told you yesterday I intend to pursue you. If I then make every decision on your behalf, that's just another way of acting as though I own you."

Adrian took her hand.

"You asked before deciding. I was able to tell you what I wanted. That's what matters."

She nodded.

Before leaving, Elena began arranging transport for Adrian while she stayed to finish the inventory.

He shook his head.

"I'll help you finish. Didn't we promise to go and choose books this evening?"

"You remember?"

"You told me you were going to pursue me yesterday. I shouldn't miss our first little outing because I've already forgotten it."

She flushed.

"It was only shopping for books."

"Then it can be only shopping for books. I want to go with you. We don't need to define every moment."

Elena straightened his collar, which for once was not crooked.

"That was rather well said."

Farther down the corridor, Cecilia was arranging a future library appointment with Nora.

She didn't interrupt.

Adrian noticed.

"She's not upset?"

Elena looked toward her.

"She has work of her own. You have yours."

He liked that answer much more than any declaration that somebody had won.

Back in the meeting room, Nora removed a translucent tracing sheet from the angled board.

"There's one problem we haven't dealt with."

She aligned the final letters of the two historical signatures.

The peculiar inward curve wasn't just similar.

Even the tiny break at the place where the pen appeared to hesitate sat in almost exactly the same position.

"Handwriting shouldn't reproduce the same small accident every time," she said. "Especially not on documents from different institutions and years."

Adrian stared at the superimposed shapes.

"Someone could have traced a single sample onto both documents?"

"Possibly. It still needs forensic examination."

Cecilia took the copied image.

Her expression grew serious.

"If those signatures came from one stored template, tomorrow we need to answer a different question."

"Which one?"

"Who preserved a specimen of Adrian Reed's signature—and who had the authority to insert it into documents supposedly created years apart?"

Adrian looked at the old contract.

For once, he wasn't merely asking whether he'd signed it.

He wanted to know who had been holding the pen.
