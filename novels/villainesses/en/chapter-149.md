# Chapter 149: Six People, Six Chairs

When Martha brought in the sixth chair, Adrian finally understood that the table was rather small.

Five cups already surrounded the teapot on the morning of January twenty-second. Wherever he put the sixth, someone's elbow seemed to evict it. He moved the pot to the sideboard. Martha inspected the freed space.

“Shall I remove the documents too?”

“For now.”

“At last, something I wanted to hear.”

Everyone had accepted separately. This was not a royal summons. Luna would stay only until she needed rest. Viola wasn't responsible for the guards outside. Cecilia had to return to her office before noon. Isabella had asked none of them to cancel their other commitments.

She arrived first, thin gauze around her left wrist and her cuff loosened. She brought a little box of uneven biscuits without a crest on the lid.

“The cook made them.”

“I hadn't asked.”

“Otherwise you might praise my progress because they aren't round.”

She chose the window side, where she could rest her wrist. It was farthest from the head of the table, though the table had no head.

Elena arrived next, checking her expression without making her recount yesterday. She offered a soft cloth for padding. Isabella accepted and spread it herself, right-handed.

Cecilia brought warm, rather than hot, honey bread. The physician hadn't forbidden sweetness, she explained, and she wasn't prescribing honey as treatment. Viola checked the chair legs for tripping hazards before sitting, her left arm still supported.

Luna came last, her right palm on its small cushion and a folded sheet in her left hand.

“I thought of flowers. Downstairs they told me there was no room for a vase.”

“We could put it on the paperwork,” Elena said.

“I approve.”

All five were finally seated together. Not exchanging papers between separate rooms, nor briefly passing in the same corridor.

Adrian took the remaining chair beside Elena.

Isabella glanced across without asking anyone to rearrange the seats.

Silence lasted until Cecilia offered him honey.

Elena said his bread already had some. Luna asked whether this had been established in a blind test. Viola earnestly inspected its surface. Isabella moved the honey jar within his own reach.

Adrian picked up the bread.

“I shall now independently decide to take a bite.”

Martha laughed by the door and finally felt safe leaving it ajar.

Isabella had prepared an opening. She meant to thank everyone for their help through her suspension, commission, and yesterday's injury. By her second sentence, Luna was examining the princess's folded note.

“You made an outline.”

“I haven't taken it out.”

“You said firstly.”

Isabella paused, then voluntarily laid the little sheet on the table.

“I didn't want to forget anything.”

There were indeed three sections. Thanks and injury came first. The final heading read: Changes in assessment following private interaction.

Luna's eyes brightened.

“An emotional report?”

“It wasn't submitted for your review.”

“Then I request access to the final heading.”

“You've already seen it.”

“Very rigorous. Willingness to approach increases. Returns on forcibly prolonged conversations diminish.”

Isabella turned the sheet over, unoffended.

“At least my heading doesn't describe a cup of tea as nonmagical paired synchronization.”

Luna straightened.

“Who told you?”

“You said it yourself last time.”

Adrian was beginning to see a weakness common to everyone.

After a glance at her own folded paper, Luna opened it. Not her full experimental diary, only that morning's reminders for attending: no measurements, no questions about the old scar, no claim that unfinished research justified staying late. A crossed-out line at the bottom was written more faintly.

If we're only talking, will I seem to have nothing useful to do?

The laughter briefly paused.

She folded that line under with her left hand. “Exhibition closed.”

Isabella put her own note away. “My conclusion isn't submitted either.”

“We can reject each other's papers.”

“On what grounds?”

“The subject has a right to breakfast,” Adrian said.

“You aren't a subject,” Luna answered immediately. Realizing how quickly she had spoken, she reddened. “Not today, anyway.”

“You needn't rename the project today either.”

Viola nodded solemnly and pocketed her note of departure times. Elena noticed without exposing her, simply turning the knight's cup toward her usable right hand.

Slowly, Isabella smiled.

Yesterday she had wanted to know how people ate breakfast the next day. Apparently, they disputed honey, prepared things to say and dreaded having them read, and worried that being useless might make them unworthy of invitations.

The pain beneath her cuff remained. It no longer filled the room.

Halfway through the food, she finally asked how afraid she should be.

Cecilia set down her cup without saying not at all.

“When I was most frightened, I thought that if someone gave the thing a definite name, I'd know what to do.”

“And afterward?”

“The name didn't decide for me. I still had to ask who was hurting today, and who needed an answer.”

Viola looked at her supported arm. “Sometimes I turn fear into arrangements. If they're thorough enough, perhaps there's nowhere left to fail.”

Luna glanced at the pocket containing her note, but didn't tease.

“I turn it into another verification. The last one usually isn't.”

“I get angry,” Elena said.

Isabella looked up, almost relieved.

“That sounds most like me.”

“I didn't recommend it.”

“None of you said I must prove I'm calm before sitting here.”

That quieted the table. Adrian moved his cup clear of her padded cloth and waited.

Isabella studied her sleeve.

“Once, I'd have asked whether this could give me a more important position. Yesterday I only wanted to know whether it would burn again tonight.”

“What did the physician say?” Viola asked.

“It hadn't spread at today's examination. A mild fever last night was recorded. They still don't know how long it will remain.”

Nobody needed to applaud her answer.

Luna reiterated that today meant ordinary images already obtained, not another stimulus. She wouldn't cast magic herself. Any future research required a separate discussion of questions, risks, and stopping conditions. Sitting together now wasn't agreement to an experiment.

“I came for tea,” Isabella said.

“That consent I accept.”

The room had barely relaxed when Isabella asked whether they should at least consider a betrothal if one could share the danger.

Adrian put down his bread.

Seeing it, she quickly added, “I'm asking. I didn't bring a contract.”

“I know. But I don't want one frightening incident deciding whom anyone should love next.”

“Even if it could mean less pain?”

Luna returned her empty cup to its saucer without answering for him.

“A specific method can be examined,” Adrian told them. “I won't sign something I don't understand. And I don't want any of you thinking that yesterday, or something earlier, means you owe me your future.”

Elena touched his fingers beneath the table. She didn't use his words to declare everyone else should leave.

Isabella flushed slightly, remembering how readily she had once offered precisely that future as a bargaining term.

“May I still ask when I want to see you?”

“Yes. I may also say I haven't time today.”

“You've demonstrated that part.”

The others laughed at her composure. Adrian discovered that five people remembering his refusals wasn't an entirely effortless form of freedom.

Cecilia noted that her breakfast hadn't been extended.

“You needn't compensate for every refusal with a new acceptance.”

She seemed to be telling herself too. She still wanted to ask him out, still wished for longer than half an hour. She didn't pretend the wish had disappeared.

Then, unexpectedly, they talked about other things.

Cecilia described teaching children to mend lanterns without making every crooked one identical. Viola admitted secretly keeping an exercise count until the physician confiscated her chart. Luna objected to confiscating records, then learned Viola had always marked pain as no impediment and conceded that the chart was seriously defective.

Elena said her father had asked whether she'd finished the travel book. Its captain finally entered the correct room, then discovered he'd taken someone else's hat.

“I no longer trust his seamanship,” Isabella said.

Adrian defended him. Even Viola insisted he should check personal equipment before departure. They argued for some time without mentioning the old project.

When Isabella looked at her wrist again, the tea was cold. For over ten minutes she hadn't counted the intervals between painful throbs. She replaced it with half a cup of warm water without announcing an achievement.

Near noon, the documents were allowed back onto the table.

Not to transform tea into an alliance ceremony. The repository supplied the incident reference, its register describing the insert, stand, and one injured person. Isabella separately agreed to send the limited finding about the fifth-position form to specified participants in the old-project examination. Injury details would stay out of public copies.

She read the recipients herself before signing right-handed.

Elena checked that preservation of the incident evidence didn't displace the personnel case. It didn't. The missing resubmission and payment records still had to be supplied. The searches for eight people continued. A princess's injury mustn't postpone them into another indefinite future.

Viola asked whether the display stand had been taken out of use. Once she saw written confirmation, she put down the page without offering to guard the archive herself.

Luna wanted the replacement part retained unchanged, not altered to make its short edge match the diagram. Cecilia checked that the account didn't describe Isabella voluntarily joining a ritual. She could attest to the text actually received, not an accident she hadn't witnessed.

Each addressed only the gap she could see.

Adrian returned the observations to their individual authors instead of combining them into a declaration that all five unanimously agreed.

“We'll argue again,” Isabella said.

“We're already arguing,” Elena replied. “About where the honey belongs.”

“You answered for him first,” Cecilia pointed out earnestly.

Elena opened her mouth, then failed to dispute the fact.

“Next time he can take it himself.”

“I already did this time,” Adrian said.

Luna lifted her cup left-handed, laughing too hard to drink. Isabella moved the sugar box toward her more slowly. The others waited instead of all reaching to finish the movement for her.

As they prepared to leave, Isabella said the final point of her report remained unfinished.

“Inviting me to coauthor?” Luna asked.

“No. I've just discovered that spending time together needn't always produce a conclusion.”

Adrian watched Luna pocket her folded reminders. She didn't ask whether she could return another time without bringing work.

Martha came for the plates and found two biscuits left. Viola would keep one for after her afternoon exercises. After considering, Isabella took the other.

No simultaneous reach produced a fated touch. They divided the parcels and agreed to ask again if times changed, like six people who genuinely still had a day to finish.

Footsteps stopped outside.

The porter brought in a Temple courier, asking him to verify six recipients' names. An urgent-service stamp marked the envelope. A registry's same-day enforcement acknowledgment was attached.

Adrian looked at the newly cleared table.

The document didn't even need to know who sat closest to him.

It had printed all six names in the same column.
