# Why Do All the Villainesses Want to Marry Me?
## Volume Two: The Saint Won't Let Me Get Married
### Chapter 31: The Saint Says This Isn't a Proposal

On October eleventh, Adrian Reed discovered that his breakfast came with another document about his engagement.

Or rather, his alleged engagement.

The title read:

**Authenticity Review Plan — Historical Betrothal Record R-074.**

Under *Persons Concerned*, his name appeared beside Cecilia Saintclair's.

And directly below that, in clean, precise handwriting:

*Participation is voluntary. No current betrothal may be presumed before verification.*

Adrian read that sentence twice.

"Those may be the loveliest words I've heard all week."

Cecilia looked up from her notes.

"Which ones?"

"The ones saying I haven't accidentally become engaged to you."

"That's a statement of the law, Mr. Reed. It isn't romantic."

"That's why it's so beautiful."

Cecilia considered his answer, then wrote something in the margin.

Adrian leaned forward.

*Person concerned expresses appreciation for current law.*

"Please don't put that in the official record!"

She paused.

"I thought it was your formal observation."

"It was a joke."

"Oh." She examined her notes. "Should I classify it as humor?"

Adrian decided that challenging her filing system was likely to end with his entire life being archived under *Unusual Behavior*.

Elena entered the dining room in a pale-blue morning dress.

She paused at the sight of Adrian and Cecilia studying the same page.

"Miss Saintclair. Are you examining historical records, or organizing a wedding?"

"Examining records," Cecilia replied immediately. "A wedding requires a valid current engagement and the informed agreement of both adults. Neither condition exists."

Adrian wanted to applaud.

Then Cecilia turned a page.

"Historical procedure did include a witnessed vow. Both parties were expected to join left hands and declare that they would walk together through calamity and glory."

Elena's eyes dropped to the crescent scar on Adrian's left hand.

Cecilia added, "I'm describing the old procedure. I'm not asking either of you to demonstrate."

"Could you put that part first next time?" Adrian asked.

"Certainly."

She rearranged her notes on the spot.

Martha was pouring tea.

"Should I arrange a regular place for Miss Saintclair?" she asked Elena, barely keeping the smile from her voice. "It looks like she'll be visiting often."

"If it's permitted," Cecilia said earnestly, "I'd appreciate the use of a quiet room for our next few examinations. The Temple guesthouse has too much traffic. It wouldn't be suitable for protecting private identity records."

Elena looked at her.

"You're remarkably direct."

"Indirect language creates unnecessary ambiguity. Envoy Horn reported that Mr. Reed refused cooperation when he actually refused an unauthorized escort and unexplained sampling. An imprecise description caused a serious problem."

There wasn't much Elena could say against that.

When Mr. Hale arrived, they moved into the study.

Cecilia set her white document case in full view of everyone present. Before she opened anything, she showed Hale the custody numbers, the certification date, and the record confirming that the original remained sealed at the Temple.

The document she'd brought was still an authenticated facsimile of the R-074 historical betrothal.

It was not the original.

"I can review when the record was made, examine its text and seal references, and research the legal system under which it was filed," Cecilia explained. "I can't enroll anyone in a new betrothal or authorize a fate-mark examination without separate consent."

Hale checked the protective seal.

"What if this old betrothal was connected to the transfer of calamity?"

"Then it's even more important to establish whether the signatories knew what they were agreeing to."

She smoothed the edge of the document sleeve without touching the seal.

"I'm not here to make the contract operative. I'm here to find out who placed our names on it, and why it was eventually transferred to the judicial archives."

"Why did the Temple let you investigate your own record?" Adrian asked.

"At first, it didn't. I was only given permission to observe. I petitioned for limited independent authority, arguing that a review has little meaning if the person named in a record is forbidden to challenge it."

"Did your superiors agree?"

"Some did. Others thought it would be wiser to seal everything away for another few years."

She said it calmly.

Adrian was beginning to suspect she'd had to fight quite hard for the right to sit at this breakfast table.

"So you really don't remember ever meeting me?"

"There's no evidence in my education records or travel permits that I met you before yesterday. But the absence of a memory isn't proof that something never happened."

She looked at him.

"You understand that, don't you? It's the same problem with your seven-year-old death registration."

Adrian nodded.

For all her formal manners, she wasn't trying to flatter him.

That made her considerably easier to trust.

He turned to the classification on the old document.

The record wasn't filed with ordinary property or inheritance betrothals. It belonged to a category labeled *Calamity Assistance*.

Under the obsolete system, the word translated as *partner* could mean a prospective spouse.

It could also refer to two adults jointly registered to witness or assume a risk.

Neither meaning translated neatly into the modern civil marriage system.

"So this might have been nothing but a cooperative agreement?" Adrian asked.

"Possibly," Cecilia replied. "But the cover uses a betrothal classification. We shouldn't choose whichever interpretation makes us feel better."

Elena produced a current civil-register search certificate.

No valid engagement between Adrian Reed and Cecilia Saintclair appeared in the capital's marriage records.

Hale checked the date and search scope before including it with the review documents.

"At least we can establish that much," Elena said.

"Yes," Cecilia agreed. "I'd like the certificate attached. Otherwise someone may use an old sheet of paper to demand that we register a marriage immediately."

Martha arrived with more tea.

"You speak about marriage so seriously, Miss Saintclair. You sound as though you're terrified of being married by surprise."

"I'm not afraid of marriage," Cecilia said. "I'm afraid of other people declaring that two adults must marry without their agreement. If two people genuinely love one another, that's a different matter."

Adrian nodded.

Cecilia added, "That principle applies to me as well, of course."

His tea nearly went up his nose.

Elena passed him a napkin.

"She's discussing a legal principle."

"I know."

"Then why are you blushing?"

"Hot tea."

She glanced at his cup, which had gone cold long ago.

She said nothing.

Hale next pointed out a faint indentation in the corner of the authenticated image. It looked as though a ruler had once covered part of the text.

"That may simply be damage caused during binding," he cautioned. "Without examining the original, we cannot treat it as evidence of concealment."

Cecilia immediately requested an independent examination of the material.

"I don't want to call ordinary wear a criminal act merely because I'm investigating the Temple."

"You're not in a hurry to catch the villain?" Adrian asked.

"I'm in a hurry to get the answer right. Those aren't always the same thing."

She divided her working notes into two versions.

The public version concerned provenance, dates, seals, and custody.

The private one covered any personal memories the two named individuals might later choose to disclose.

"Why two?" Adrian asked.

"The court needs to know whether the document is genuine. It doesn't need to know who you first fell in love with. Nor do I want my childhood history passed around for entertainment."

Elena nodded.

"On that point, we agree."

Hale prepared a limited authorization. Adrian signed it for inspection of the facsimile only. It didn't extend to magical samples, his scar, or any future procedure.

Cecilia thanked him for cooperating.

"I'm agreeing to examine a document," Adrian reminded her. "Not agreeing that we're betrothed."

"I understand," she said, smiling. "I'll make that distinction explicit."

As they worked, Cecilia took an old wooden bookmark from her sleeve.

A sentence had been carved into it by hand:

*Do not put someone who was never asked on a list of those who have agreed.*

"My mentor gave me this when I first entered the Temple," she explained. "I believe faith ought to teach us to respect people. If a records officer used the gods' name to make someone else's sacrifice seem compulsory, I want to know who and why."

Adrian studied the worn wood.

He remembered the execution platform he'd glimpsed in the old courtroom, and the unidentified woman in its witness box.

He still didn't know what had happened there.

But here was someone raised within the Temple who was willing to admit that a record might not be just.

Martha brought a plate of biscuits.

A little label beside them said *Better Value for Two*.

Cecilia read it.

"How can we establish that? There's no unit price."

"It's a joke from the kitchen," Martha explained.

"Oh." Cecilia considered the matter. "That happened yesterday too. A baker told me two servings were cheaper, so I asked him to provide the calculation on the receipt. He eventually asked me to stop blocking his other customers."

Adrian laughed so hard he nearly dropped his biscuit.

Elena smiled.

"Not much experience shopping by yourself?"

"The Temple normally has a purchasing staff. My work involves legal study, medical assistance, and records for the orphanages." Cecilia hesitated. "I missed two sessions at a charitable clinic while obtaining permission for this investigation."

"Will patients be affected?" Elena asked.

"Other workers are covering them, but I promised I'd attend. I don't like abandoning work simply because my own name has become part of a dispute."

"House Rosenberg has longstanding ties to the northern clinics. We can help arrange replacement supplies, if that's useful. It's not a trade for your cooperation."

Cecilia's eyes brightened.

"Thank you. I'd be grateful."

Adrian watched them discuss something that had nothing whatsoever to do with marrying him.

For the first time all morning, he relaxed.

Martha promptly spoiled it.

"Look at that, Mr. Reed. You haven't caused either lady to become angry today."

"Am I the household's entertainment now?"

"Only when everyone is under stress," Martha replied.

Adrian elected to eat his biscuit in silence.

Later, Cecilia reviewed the obsolete date stamps. The contract used a discontinued cycle-based system. Seven years earlier was the earliest documented transfer date, not a verified signing date.

"The old conversion tables are incomplete," she said. "We cannot convert this stamp into a modern calendar day yet."

At the bottom, another instruction read:

*Where both related persons are required for verification, notify the second if the first is absent.*

"So if I refuse to appear, they'll keep calling me a related person?" Adrian asked.

"The office may mark the identity as unverified," Hale said. "It cannot force a marriage obligation on that basis."

Cecilia produced a formal objection she'd already lodged.

"The administrative duty office called your refusal to attend 'failure to cooperate with betrothal registration.' I've asked them to replace it with 'identity verification not yet consented to.' We don't even know whether a valid registration ever existed."

"Who approved that description?" Elena asked.

"There's only a department seal. No named officer."

Hale wrote it down for a separate inquiry.

As the afternoon drew to a close, Elena opened the pale-blue notebook she'd used during the investigation.

On the inside cover, she wrote:

*Personal feelings and evidence review are separate matters.*

"For me?" Adrian asked.

"A reminder to myself. I've already told you I want to pursue you. I shouldn't turn every working meeting into a competition for your attention."

Cecilia thought for a moment.

"If I ever develop personal feelings for Mr. Reed, I'll say so openly too. I won't insert them into my findings."

Adrian almost choked again.

Elena set down her pen.

"No need to put hypothetical romantic developments into today's report."

"Understood," Cecilia said.

They could finally discuss their schedules.

For roughly fifteen seconds.

"Mr. Reed," Cecilia said, "would you accompany me to the academy's old-calendar library tomorrow? Your observations may help us identify some of the symbols."

"He's already promised to help me choose books tomorrow afternoon," Elena said.

"Then we could go in the morning."

"He has work."

"Before breakfast, perhaps?"

Adrian cleared his throat.

"Ladies? Could we let me look at my own schedule?"

Cecilia immediately handed him the pen.

"Of course. Please decide."

Elena nodded.

"I was just about to ask you."

Adrian had serious doubts about that.

He'd barely begun filling in the appointment slots when the head steward appeared with a letter from the Temple.

Only one recipient was written on the envelope.

**Adrian Reed.**

Cecilia examined the administrative duty office's seal on the back.

Her smile faded.

"This didn't come from my independent review committee."
