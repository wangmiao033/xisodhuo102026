# Chapter 257: The Hand Reaching for the Key

On the morning of April 20, Isabella crouched beside a temporary shelter's bench and admitted she'd brought an elderly woman the wrong blanket.

The woman needed a short one clear of her wheelchair's wheels. The princess had supplied a handsome long blanket which trailed beneath them. Rather than move the woman into another chair to suit her generosity, Isabella checked the actual need with a duty caregiver and exchanged the unused blanket.

Adrian followed carrying a water jug. Apparently even blankets were being examined today. Before laughing, she advised, he should tighten the lid. Looking down, he watched a drop land on his shoe. Both fell silent briefly.

Noon was the earliest conditional final review, not a bell guaranteed to destroy the city or a promise of safety until then. Waiting for it didn't excuse neglecting people needing shelter now. Another unsafe dwelling had been evacuated last night. Urgency had increased demand without producing beds.

Duty staff assigned actual spaces. Two remaining households went to another confirmed destination, not one which hadn't replied. Isabella checked luggage and receiving papers without lifting boxes on her supported wrist. A man recognized her and requested priority for his mother because he'd once worked for royal carriage crews.

She asked the caregiver to assess the mother's needs instead of ranking former employers. The woman did need a place near the washing area. That reason, not royal connection, was recorded. Disappointed but relieved his mother could sit, the man accepted.

During a luggage transfer Adrian found an unclaimed tin box. Children guessed money was inside. Instead of opening it, he asked who could describe its damaged edge. A woman finally found the key in her bag and opened it to reveal an old family portrait. Her hands shook. Losing this had frightened her most during the move.

Isabella didn't ask its value. She changed miscellaneous belongings to personal property reclaimed. Adrian held down the page corner while she admitted she'd once have noticed only whether the most expensive carriage arrived.

“You still can. Just don't let the carriage answer for people.”

She asked whether he'd help finish checking shoe sizes afterward. Not a special invitation; she genuinely struggled with faded small numbers. If review and handover permitted, yes. If not, he'd tell her rather than make certainty cover the unknown.

“A rejected royal invitation increasingly resembles ordinary life.”

“This one hasn't failed. It simply doesn't close the city streets.”

She pretended to tap his jug, felt her wrist move, and substituted a look. The brief joke reminded them why these people's days shouldn't be exchanged for five convenient names on a form.

They left ordinary work with the people taking the next shift instead of assuming their departure excused an unfinished handover. The portrait box had its owner, the blanket had been exchanged, and the remaining sizes were still written down as remaining. None was a solution to the larger danger. None became pointless because a larger danger existed. At the open preparation room, Adrian first noticed a hard case on the duty table, not Oswin. Its custodian stood beside it. The intact seal covered physical keys requested for a subsequent handover, not five locks already opened.

Had they been delivered? No; actual conditions must be decided first. Adrian recorded the answer rather than announce a final sacrifice begun merely because he'd seen a box.

Oswin arrived neatly dressed and without visible frenzy. He submitted new calamity reports: damaged nodes, affected homes, incomplete relocations. Sources and times were distinct. None established everywhere collapsing simultaneously. None let them dismiss the danger as mere intimidation either.

“You've protected a few doors. I don't deny that. Pressure outside won't stop because we explain permission accurately.”

Adrian thought of the woman holding her portrait, without offering her gratitude as a rebuttal. Oswin named a real unresolved problem. Their proposal hadn't completed a human trial; sharing nodes hadn't demonstrated real-scale capacity. Luna's expenditure wasn't a certificate of national safety.

The High Priest indicated the preparation pages, requesting current danger be judged sufficient necessity, external procedure advanced to final execution, and key delivery begun. The noon bell sounded in the distance. No golden light pierced the ceiling, but a hand actually moved toward the case.

The custodian didn't push it across. He looked to the independent legal officer responsible for review. Oswin's clerk noted that external preparations were complete; under precedent, delivery was a subsequent formality.

Adrian placed Evelyn's registered comparison lead at the designated spot and requested the inner and outer volumes checked. He didn't shout that a witness knew everything or seize the case. The recorder located the present original. External registration was complete. Inner activation still cited the necessity finding under consideration.

“Which we are now making,” Oswin said.

“So it isn't already made,” the legal officer replied.

That sentence couldn't indefinitely postpone danger. The reviewers immediately required actual answers: which damage existing measures couldn't control, why designation of five people remained necessary, whether the stay on compulsory movement had lawfully changed, and what alternative evidence could be checked within what real period.

Oswin didn't deny the five women's cost. He would accept guilt for choosing, he said, rather than watch more people die through refusing to choose. Cecilia asked whether accepting guilt could substitute for the bodily consequences borne by the selected people. No, he answered. Somebody must still decide.

Elena's hand tightened at the table. She didn't let Adrian announce whether she'd go. She stated her own refusal of transfer and rejected past designation as automatic passage through today's review. Other present people's actual views were separately recorded. A collective we couldn't replace those absent.

Adrian admitted the new proposal hadn't earned the word successful. He would submit its failures and blanks together. Familiarity with the old plan didn't replace uncompleted necessity findings. If they crossed unmet conditions now and signed afterward, paper would merely chase people already taken away.

Oswin asked which innocent person must die first before Adrian admitted time had run out. Silence carried the sound of wheels striking gaps outside.

Adrian didn't know who would be harmed first and couldn't guarantee delay cost nothing. It wasn't a beautiful answer. He requested the existing stay preserved, immediately checkable material examined, and ordinary evacuation and relief continued—not an announcement that danger had paused.

Isabella didn't conclude his speech with royal status. She submitted that morning's actual receiving capacity, remaining shortages, and confirmed handovers. Two households relocated didn't prove the city evacuable. They did make nothing has been done inaccurate. She didn't exaggerate empty places into room for everybody.

The questions did not disappear because either side could describe the consequences of a wrong choice. The original volumes were still required: not a sympathetic summary, not another copy selected by the person asking for the keys. The reviewers recessed to check original records. The half hour was painfully long. In the corridor, Adrian looked at his left hand. Urgency repeatedly tempted him to regard himself as material to plug a gap. Elena asked what he was thinking.

“A terrible shortcut.”

She waited for him to finish: could his going first buy time? Saying it exposed what he lacked—knowledge of the channel, any guarantee of the result, and consultation with those whose circumstances his action might change.

“Thank you for saying it first this time.”

She offered her hand. They stood together briefly without promising a wedding after a certain homecoming. She asked him to keep the hand wanting to fill everybody's blank here, at least until he actually understood what it would do.

On resumption, the independent legal officer refused progression to final execution in this review. The compulsory-transfer stay hadn't lawfully been lifted. External preparation couldn't replace the necessity finding and inner conditions. Keys remained sealed with their custodian, not delivered to execution staff. Hazard reports remained valid; external preparation and outstanding disputes weren't all withdrawn. Examination must continue.

Nobody called that decision a guarantee that every person outside would remain safe. Nor did it grant their new proposal powers it had not demonstrated. The unfinished work remained on both sides of the table, along with the real consequences of failing it. Oswin didn't overturn the table. He submitted his objection and insisted subsequent review couldn't be delayed indefinitely. Before leaving, he told Adrian he hoped the new door really existed. His calmness made it harder to turn him into a madman who merely needed defeating.

While they awaited resealing, Oswin's young clerk returned for a forgotten pen. Did Adrian believe every one of them wanted the five women dead?

He didn't know every private thought, Adrian replied. Not knowing didn't mean minimizing what had happened at the table. The clerk said his sister lived in an affected street.

“Then I hope she has somewhere to sleep tonight. If not, ask the receiving office. You don't have to agree with us first.”

Without thanks, the young man requested the address, confirmed it lay near his route home, and left. One exchange didn't turn him into a secret supporter. Watching the pen depart, Adrian thought how difficult it was that someone could fear for his sister while still placing strangers' names in another document.

The hard case was resealed. Only after checking the actual number and receipt did Adrian confirm today's transfer hadn't happened. A temporary stop wasn't destruction of the old mechanism. The case remained an object held under a real person's responsibility, not a symbol they could declare defeated and forget.

The independent facilities custodian then issued a different, limited permission. On the registered old index and present examination need, an accompanied visit to the old execution hall was authorized that afternoon, April 20. They could compare cold floor contacts and the operating catalog. No power, bodily connection, or alteration of the five locks. Unsuitable site conditions meant stopping entry.

Evelyn's copy went into a separate bag, the missing page still marked. Luna wouldn't be dragged from rest to cast. Available technical examiners would take actual measurements and refer later questions onward. Adrian signed for entry and observation, not for his scar to start anything. The limit applied to what they would do, not merely to the comfortable wording before departure.

Before leaving, the limits were read alongside the permission. A signed visit could not become an excuse for powering a device merely because they discovered something interesting inside. Isabella sent a message to the shelter: staff on duty must continue today's shoe-size work, and she'd later clarify when she could actually return. A princess who'd spoken soundly in a review room couldn't leave ordinary people waiting indefinitely.

At the old street entrance, wind lifted Adrian's coat. Noon had passed. The world hadn't been saved, nor had a bell automatically assigned five people its burden.

The keys remained in their box. Through the next authorized entrance, they must discover how the earlier door had been shut.
