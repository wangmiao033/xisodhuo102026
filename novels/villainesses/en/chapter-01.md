# Why Do All the Villainesses Want to Marry Me?
## Volume One: A Betrothal Written in Blood
### Chapter 1: My Lady, Your Engagement Is a Sham

Adrian Reed realized he'd been transported into a game while scrubbing the third red stain off a marble floor.

For the record, it wasn't blood. It was red wine.

That was hardly comforting.

He knew this corridor. He knew the mounted antlers on the wall, the double-lion crest carved above the door at the far end, and even the particular angle at which the afternoon sun struck the marble.

He'd seen it all before.

In *Crown of Fate*.

Only last week, he'd finished the hidden route of that ridiculously punishing romance game. The corridor was a background illustration from the east wing of the Rosenberg ducal estate. In the game, though, the floor had been considerably cleaner.

"Are you planning to stand there all day?"

Someone nudged Adrian's bucket with the toe of a polished shoe.

Adrian looked up.

A middle-aged man with a narrow mustache stood over him, a ring of keys hanging from his belt. He had the weary expression of someone who'd been given too many servants to supervise and not nearly enough competent ones.

"I'm talking to you. Lady Elena leaves for the palace tonight, and everyone in this house is run off their feet. Yet here you are, daydreaming in the hallway."

Adrian looked down at himself.

White shirt. Black waistcoat. Damp rag in his hand.

A servant.

Of course he was a servant.

In the opening sequence of *Crown of Fate*, the Rosenberg household prepared for a royal banquet. Three hours later, Prince Leon publicly broke off his engagement to Elena Rosenberg.

Soon afterward, she was accused of treason.

Three days later, she was executed.

Adrian was standing in the very house where it all began.

"What day is it?" he asked.

The man scowled. "Have you lost your mind?"

"Let's assume I have."

"September seventeenth. Now finish the floor."

September seventeenth.

Adrian's hand went still.

Wait.

The engagement banquet in the game was supposed to take place on September twentieth.

"What banquet is tonight?" he asked.

"The Royal Autumn Ball. Have you forgotten that too?"

Adrian let out a breath.

Then he still had three days.

Three days to find a way out of this house. Three days to avoid becoming collateral damage in a villainess's spectacular downfall. Three days to—

"Though what His Highness is thinking, I couldn't tell you," the steward muttered. "He suddenly insisted Lady Elena attend tonight. They've even folded the engagement anniversary celebration into the ball, instead of holding it on the twentieth as planned."

Adrian dropped his rag into the bucket.

"I'm sorry—what?"

"I said get back to work!"

The steward marched away, keys jangling with every step.

Adrian remained crouched beside his bucket, staring at his reflection in the water.

Twenty-four years old. Black hair, dark eyes. The same face he'd had in his own world.

Almost.

A thin scar ran across the back of his left hand.

He'd never had that scar before.

He dug a fingernail into his skin.

It hurt.

"Right. So this isn't a dream."

Footsteps sounded farther down the corridor.

Two maids hurried past with a dress box between them.

"Lady Elena still won't try the gown?"

"She says something's wrong with the sapphire necklace the prince sent last night."

"Quiet! Do you want someone to hear you?"

Adrian shot to his feet.

The sapphire necklace.

He remembered that item.

In the game, Elena received it from the prince before the engagement banquet. The royal crest was engraved on the inner setting. Later, it became one of the key pieces of evidence against her: the prince claimed she'd passed royal property to a northern rebel contact.

Except the necklace never left her rooms.

The evidence had been switched.

If Adrian could get to that necklace before the banquet, he might be able to stop the frame-up.

He abandoned the bucket and hurried after the maids.

"Wait!"

Both women turned.

"Who are you?" the younger one demanded.

"Adrian. I work in the east wing." He tried for a harmless smile. "I wanted to ask where Lady Elena keeps the sapphire necklace."

The older maid's expression changed at once.

"Why are you asking about Her Ladyship's jewelry?"

"I heard there was a problem with it."

"From whom?"

Adrian opened his mouth.

What was he supposed to say?

*A walkthrough on my computer?*

"I shouldn't have asked." He bowed his head. "My apologies."

The older maid gave him a final suspicious look, caught her companion by the arm, and walked away.

Adrian stood in the corridor, his stomach sinking.

Too eager.

He had no standing in this household, no proof, and no idea who was responsible for what. Marching up to the duke's daughter and asking about her jewelry was a wonderful way to get accused of theft before the main plot even started.

He was still trying to think of a better approach when a voice came from the other end of the hall.

"You want to see my necklace?"

Adrian turned.

A young woman stood at the top of the short staircase, a maid a pace behind her.

Her golden hair was gathered neatly at the back of her head. She wore a dark green day dress and carried a slim account book. There was no scarlet ball gown, no arrogant smile from her character portrait.

The real Elena Rosenberg was more beautiful than the artwork.

And considerably more intimidating.

Adrian bowed before his body could remember how.

"My lady."

"Answer me."

"I heard there might be something wrong with the necklace."

"What sort of problem?"

"Someone may have tampered with it."

Elena closed her account book.

"Who told you that?"

"No one."

"Then you saw it yourself?"

"No."

She studied him for a few seconds.

"Take him to the captain of the guard."

The maid stepped forward.

Adrian felt his heart drop.

Of course.

A servant she'd never met had been asking questions about her jewelry. What other response could a sensible person have?

"Wait!" he said quickly, lowering his voice. "My lady, the prince is going to break off your engagement at tonight's ball."

Elena didn't even pause.

"That's hardly a secret."

"And then he's going to accuse you of conspiring with the northern rebels."

The maid gasped.

Elena turned.

"Go on."

Adrian glanced at the maid.

"Could we speak privately?"

"There's no need. Martha has served me for twelve years. If you're about to invent a story, another witness will be useful."

Fair point.

Adrian swallowed.

"They'll produce a letter claiming that on the fifteenth of last month, you leaked the capital's defenses to the rebels. It's forged. You were at the academy taking your magical examinations that day. Someone can confirm it."

Elena's expression didn't change.

"What else?"

"The prince will say the academy witness is unreliable. Then he'll order the guards to arrest you. If you resist, he'll use that as further evidence of your guilt."

"How very thorough of you."

"I'm not the one arranging it!"

"Then what are you doing? Predicting the future?"

Adrian had no answer.

Elena descended the last two steps.

"Do you know why I ordered you taken to the guards?"

He shook his head.

"Because last night, someone sent me an anonymous letter saying almost exactly what you've just said."

Adrian stared at her.

"What?"

"It warned me that I would be accused of colluding with the northern rebels. The difference is that the sender urged me to flee the capital immediately."

She held his gaze.

"And now a servant I've never met appears, telling me to believe the same story."

His thoughts raced.

There was no anonymous letter in the game.

That had never happened.

"Where is the letter?"

"I burned it."

"Why?"

"Because whoever wrote it knew far too much. They wanted me frightened enough to leave the city. And the moment a Rosenberg carriage slips through the gates at night, what will everyone say?"

Adrian didn't answer.

She was right.

The royal guards controlled the city gates. A panicked escape would hand the prince exactly the evidence he needed: a noblewoman fleeing the capital on the eve of a treason accusation.

Adrian had been planning to tell her to run.

She'd already figured out why that would be a mistake.

This wasn't the villainess from the game, blundering into every trap the script prepared for her.

Elena had seen the first trap coming.

"And you?" she asked. "What is it you want me to do?"

Adrian opened his mouth.

Everything he'd intended to say suddenly sounded idiotic.

"I want you to survive."

Martha frowned. "Mind your place!"

Elena raised a hand to silence her.

"Survive?"

"Yes."

"You make it sound as though I'm certain to die tonight."

"Not tonight," Adrian said before he could stop himself. "Three days from now."

The corridor went quiet.

Whatever expression had been on Elena's face vanished.

"Martha."

"My lady?"

"Watch the door. No one comes through."

The maid hesitated, then obeyed.

Elena opened a small sitting room off the corridor.

"Inside."

Adrian followed her.

The door closed softly behind him.

It might as well have been the door of an interrogation chamber.

Elena remained standing. She picked up a small silver bell from the table and set it within reach.

"Three questions."

"All right."

"First. Who sent you?"

"No one."

"Second. How do you know what's going to happen three days from now?"

Adrian fell silent.

Elena reached for the bell.

"Wait!" He held up both hands. "I can't explain where the information came from. But I can prove I know something you don't."

"Such as?"

"The sapphire necklace the prince sent you. There's a hidden compartment beneath the lining of the box. It contains a receipt bearing the mark of a northern trade guild."

For the first time, something changed in her eyes.

Adrian pressed on.

"The receipt isn't meant for you. Someone planted it to be 'discovered' when your rooms are searched. You can check the compartment right now."

"Do you know where the box is?"

"Your dressing room."

"Have you been inside?"

"No."

"Then how do you know about a hidden compartment?"

"Please. Just look."

Elena rang the bell.

Martha appeared almost immediately.

"Bring me the sapphire necklace His Highness sent. Don't allow anyone to handle the case."

"Yes, my lady."

The wait was unbearable.

Adrian remained standing while Elena sat at the table and opened her account book. She didn't look at him once.

Somehow, that was worse than being questioned.

At last Martha returned, carrying a deep-blue jewelry case.

Elena opened it herself.

A sapphire necklace lay against the velvet, the stones catching a cold glint of daylight.

She lifted it out, turned the case over, and slipped the tip of a letter opener beneath the inner lining.

A thin wooden panel came loose.

Adrian's pulse quickened.

Elena lifted the panel.

The compartment was empty.

Nothing.

Adrian stared.

"No. That can't be..."

He forced himself not to step back. Instead, he leaned closer.

Two fresh impressions marked the inside of the velvet. The small brass tacks securing the panel had been driven in again. One tack head bore an uneven scratch, as though someone had pried it loose in a hurry with the wrong tool.

"Someone got to the compartment before we did," he said.

Elena glanced at him. "And you know that how?"

"Look here. Dust has settled in the corners, but there are fresh fingerprints in the center of the panel. And that tack—the edge is still bright."

"The maker could have left those marks."

"Then why does only one tack look as though it's been removed and replaced?"

Elena didn't answer immediately. She asked Martha for a magnifying glass and examined the case herself.

"You don't sound much like a servant whose job is to mop floors."

"Even a floor cleaner has to know who made the mess."

The words slipped out before he could stop them.

Adrian braced himself.

Making clever remarks to a duke's daughter while suspected of meddling with royal property seemed an excellent way to shorten his lifespan.

But Elena merely raised an eyebrow.

"Good. At least you've moved beyond saying 'impossible.'"

Adrian let out a careful breath.

That observation hadn't come from any walkthrough. For once, he'd simply looked at what was in front of him.

Elena slid the case toward him.

"Where is the receipt you promised?"

"I don't know. It should be here."

"According to whom?"

He couldn't answer.

Elena rose.

"Third question."

Her voice went cold.

"Did you deliberately lure me into opening this case?"

"No!"

"If I tell anyone that I personally dismantled a gift from the prince, what will they think?"

"That you're trying to destroy evidence," Adrian said.

He thought for another moment.

"Which means we can't put it back together and pretend nothing happened. We should bring in the duke's steward and two witnesses who don't serve you directly. Record the time we found the compartment and the condition of the box. Otherwise someone could claim we planted whatever turns up later."

Elena stared at him.

"That," she said, "is actually useful. Martha, make a note. Ask my father's clerk to witness the inspection."

Sweat trickled down Adrian's back.

Another change.

The receipt that should have been there was gone.

Someone had removed it ahead of time.

Maybe they'd done it specifically so Elena would be caught opening the case.

"My lady," Martha asked quietly, "shall I call the guards?"

Elena didn't answer.

Her fingertips moved along the exposed edge of the compartment.

Then they stopped.

"Martha. Draw the curtains."

"My lady?"

"Please."

The heavy curtains shut out the afternoon sun.

Elena took a small moonstone from her belt and moved it over the wooden panel.

Pale-blue letters surfaced along the inner edge.

Adrian held his breath.

He'd never encountered anything like this in the game.

Elena read the words. The color drained from her face.

"This isn't a receipt."

"Do you know what kind of magic it is?"

"Delayed-reveal ink. Normally invisible. It reacts to moonstone light." She turned the little stone in her palm. "Nobles use it for love letters. Threats are a less charming application."

"So the words were already there."

"Of course. Magic still needs a medium. It doesn't conjure a document out of thin air."

Adrian filed that away.

In the game, he'd only cared about stats and endings. Here, apparently, knowing the rules behind an insignificant bit of magic could determine whether someone lived.

Elena turned the case so he could see.

In delicate letters, the wood read:

**On September twentieth, at noon, Elena Rosenberg, eldest daughter of House Rosenberg, shall be publicly executed.**

Beneath the words was the seal of the Royal Judicial Office.

Adrian went cold.

Today was September seventeenth.

Why was an execution notice dated three days in the future hidden inside a gift from the prince?

For a long moment, Elena said nothing.

Then she closed the case and slipped the moonstone back onto her belt.

"Martha, prepare the carriage. I'm attending tonight's ball."

"My lady! That's far too dangerous!"

"If I stay away, they'll have an excuse to send soldiers to fetch me."

She turned to Adrian.

"As for you..."

He straightened.

"You're staying close to me from now on."

"My lady, I'm only a servant."

"I'm aware."

"I might not be much help."

"You nearly talked yourself into the dungeon just now."

"...Yes."

"But you knew about the compartment." She paused. "And when you read that inscription, you looked more frightened than I felt."

"Being scared doesn't mean I'll run," Adrian said.

"Isn't that precisely what you were considering?"

He blinked. "How did you know?"

"After you told me the execution date, you looked at the servants' passage, then at your shoes. People tend to do that when they're planning an escape."

"Then why keep me around?"

"Because you didn't run."

Elena handed the case to Martha.

"And because, knowing what you believe will happen to me, you still came to warn me."

Adrian found himself at a loss.

She'd said it matter-of-factly, but it made what he'd done feel unexpectedly significant.

"I just don't think someone should have to die because an ending was written for them," he said.

Elena's fingers stilled.

"That," she said, "I'll remember."

She came a step closer.

"So, for the moment, I believe you don't want me dead."

Relief barely had time to reach him before she added:

"Don't misunderstand. That doesn't mean I trust you."

"Understood."

"And if you run tonight, I'll have the guards bring you back."

"...Understood."

At the door, Elena paused.

"One more thing."

"Yes, my lady?"

"You said I would die in three days."

"Yes."

"But the anonymous letter mentioned only tonight's accusation."

Adrian looked up.

"How did you know the execution date?"

Cold sweat spread across his back.

He couldn't tell her.

Not yet.

Elena didn't seem to expect an answer. She opened the door and addressed Martha.

"Find him a clean set of livery. He's coming to the palace with me."

Before leaving, she added, "And find out who he is."

"Yes, my lady. How far back should I investigate?"

"From birth."

Adrian stood motionless.

If someone had already changed the events of this world, then the route he'd memorized might no longer be a way to survive.

It might be the trap waiting for him.

He looked again at the blue jewelry case. The glowing letters had faded, leaving a barely visible impression in the wood. Elena had ordered it sealed until the clerk could witness the evidence.

All he'd wanted was the safest possible escape route.

Now he was going to the palace with a villainess who might be executed in three days.

Martha called from outside.

"Adrian, your livery is ready. Lady Elena expects you at the carriage in fifteen minutes."

"Coming."

He slipped his hand into his pocket and touched the unfamiliar scar.

A detail from the game surfaced in his mind, something he'd skipped over without thinking.

Whenever you loaded a save, the characters' memories reset.

But the scars remained.

He brought his left hand into the light.

The mark on its back was a narrow crescent.

In the game, Elena had held someone's hand shortly before her execution.

That person had the same scar.

Adrian swallowed.

**He remembered that person dying.**
