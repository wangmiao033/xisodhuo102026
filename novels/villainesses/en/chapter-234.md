# Chapter 234: The Last Division Wasn't Empty

On April 2, Adrian received his proposed result notice back. Three words were circled: ready for expansion.

The academy hadn't denied yesterday's success. The reviewer wanted to know which materials, what load, how long, and on what evidence. Three words occupied very little paper. The missing answers could cover a table.

He took the sheet to Luna, who was studying a chess position. Her right palm rested on its cushion; her left hand didn't touch the pieces. Her assistant moved a white rook.

“I may have written the excitement too quickly,” Adrian admitted from the doorway.

“Don't apologize yet. You're blocking my knight.”

He moved. Seeing the diagonal, she requested the rook returned and admitted that her proposed move was illegal. Only then did she invite him to sit.

The game wasn't an elaborate lesson prepared for him. She disliked losing and had genuinely overlooked an inconvenient angle. Should they start again? No, she said. She hadn't finished losing this one.

He offered the rejected notice without demanding a pleasing limit. She asked why B had risen faster than estimated. Transfer waiting might contribute, he said, or material differences. Measurement was needed, not selecting the easiest explanation.

“Then examine the narrowest part first.”

The metrology room was quieter than yesterday's test room. The disconnected apparatus had been unloaded. After inspection, the shield went back under its agreement; its owner received the actual object and condition record, not another extension request. The suspect clamp remained a separate job, absent from the return cart.

Today involved retained data and unpowered dimensions, not a third run. An independent examiner laid out both local instruments' calibration sheets. Which number did Adrian most want to use to persuade people?

He indicated the effective quantity received at B.

“Then look at the division beside it.”

That column contained the permitted measurement error. It hadn't been invented afterward to spoil good news. It existed before the instrument entered use. Yesterday's delight occupied an actual range, not an infinitely precise point.

Using an ordinary glass measure to explain effective cross-section, the examiner compared two apparently identical connections. Their internal receiving surfaces differed slightly. A receiving specimen's capacity wasn't the instantaneous capacity of the narrow path supplying it. Adding the capacities at both ends didn't produce a quantity safe to send through whenever one pleased.

“Two granaries with one small cart between them,” Adrian said.

“Helpful for explanation. Don't calculate our limit from the cart's dimensions.”

Adrian smiled and wrote the analogy beside his plain-language draft, not in the technical calculation.

They checked the local peak, rate of rise, handover interval, and termination reserve. Specimen dimensions and material certificates were traceable; instrument errors had defined ranges. After allowing for those uncertainties and the margin needed to stop, the narrowest connection left a smaller number than Adrian had imagined.

They hadn't burned apparatus until it broke to find a limit. The examiner explicitly identified a conservative operating ceiling for this configuration. Beyond it, current evidence failed—not necessarily immediate rupture, and certainly not permission to try. Different material, duration, or extra connections required new justification. A ceiling supported by this examination was not a universal constant discovered in the nature of fate. Adrian couldn't borrow the authority of the number while discarding the apparatus and interval that gave it meaning. The conclusion belonged to the whole checked description, not to whichever line looked most hopeful on a public notice.

Luna attended only the first part. Her assistant entered the confirmed figure in the technical attachment. She added that a brief success couldn't establish the same fraction of pain safe for a person. Simulated heat and actual calamity still lacked a verified correspondence.

A materials liaison frowned. Would nobles consider the contribution worthwhile if its result was described so narrowly?

Isabella arrived in time to hear him. She didn't answer for every noble. She requested the lender notice, struck ready for expansion with her right hand, and substituted one successful trial in a limited configuration; further scope under assessment.

“They can decline another loan. We can't decide in advance that they only want a large result.”

Her special coordinating authority hadn't returned. She corrected her own communication, while technical staff confirmed the attachment. When the liaison asked for a conclusion an ordinary reader could understand, she invited Adrian to use his granary analogy—and finish the limitations too.

Halfway through, he realized he'd described the two granaries without explaining where the cart might stick. Isabella raised an eyebrow. He supplied the missing half. Two assistants laughed, and he joined them. Correcting the page no longer felt like public humiliation.

The independent conclusion confirmed a current ceiling slightly above yesterday's input. It didn't support doubling, or lining up four sets and multiplying by four. Shared paths, waiting, and termination capacity would need assessment again. An application citing the report couldn't take only its largest number.

Adrian considered the final division. It wasn't vacant ground for courage to occupy. It belonged to uncertainty and the time needed to recognize trouble and stop. Using it to prove conviction would consume someone else's opportunity to call a halt.

At midday he accompanied the administrator delivering the shield's return acknowledgment. On a slope outside the academy, the return cart stood with one wheel sunk in rain-softened earth. The driver unloaded lighter objects to reduce strain while bystanders suggested more men to push.

Adrian rolled his sleeves. The administrator first asked the driver whether help was wanted and where they should stand. The man indicated dry ground and told them to wait while he placed a board. Adrian and another adult worker pushed the ordinary frame on his signal. The wheel climbed slowly. More hands hadn't made its trapped position irrelevant.

Afterward, mud marked Adrian's hand, his scar still covered. Offering water, the driver asked what the academy was researching.

“How not to count every remaining bit as available.”

“Useful work. One more crate and I wouldn't have chosen this slope.”

Adrian didn't take the man's remark back as scientific corroboration. He remembered it because movement hadn't convinced the driver that every road was suitable. He'd wanted yesterday's success to travel everywhere more quickly than a man who moved ordinary goods every day.

He washed the mud away and explained the delay without calling it important unexpected business. The little incident had consumed actual time. He still ate a warm piece of bread before the afternoon's argument.

A Temple reviewer came to inspect the result. He didn't call yesterday false or allege a concealed human connection. He noted the enormous gap between the model ceiling and the burden faced by existing protection. A fine small trial couldn't justify switching that protection off.

Adrian wanted to argue. Luna was resting; nobody beside him would arrange his anger into technical language. Reading the table again, he conceded the point. They weren't proposing immediate shutdown.

Then the reviewer changed the proposed use to incapable of any practical assistance.

Adrian held his own copy flat, without touching the other man's page.

“That doesn't follow. Limited sharing occurred. We established a boundary. Not enough to replace something isn't the same as never useful.”

They argued over the phrase for some time. Eventually the reviewer retained his adverse risk opinion but removed any. The work was insufficient to support replacement at the current scale. That was narrower than the encouragement Adrian wanted and narrower than the dismissal the man had first written. Both differences mattered. An unfavorable opinion could remain in the file without swallowing the actual finding. The reviewer signed his own conclusion, not Adrian's desired future. He hadn't become their ally. Adrian hadn't expelled him with a clever sentence either.

Isabella let Adrian speak. Outside, she asked why he hadn't wanted her rank to outweigh the objection.

“His first statement was right.”

“The second?”

“I had reasons against it. I didn't need your name.”

Her expression shifted. “Should I enjoy being needed less?”

“I needed you to stay. Not to make him silent.”

She stopped for a moment, then observed that his compliments resembled applications refusing to exceed their scope.

“Return it for revision.”

“I'll accept this one. Not the same text every time.”

They smiled without turning their shared walk into a date. She had communications to handle; he had papers for Luna. Before they separated, Isabella pointed with her right hand at a misplaced coat button. Before his next technical discussion, he might inspect his own connecting arrangement.

After her nap, Luna heard the findings without the disappointment Adrian expected. She had the confirmed figure placed beside a blank in the old drawing. Did he know why that passage had originally been designed so narrowly?

“Material limits?”

“Possible. A guess. There's a revision reference here. The original statement of purpose belongs to another custodian.”

Adrian remembered Evelyn's explanation of authority categories. She'd identified the record trail, not opened the core. Now they needed evidence about why the early design contained sharing branches, not a key to operate them.

Old diagrams and present restrictions couldn't answer that alone, Luna said. If an early purpose sheet was merely an unused draft, they couldn't proclaim an altered original intention. Acceptance and issuance records had to establish whether it had actually governed the design.

Adrian recorded a scoped request for purpose and revisions, excluding private marked-person details. He sent it through the independent archival liaison to check existing catalogs, not summon every distant original before nightfall.

Before he left, Luna invited him to make a move in the unfinished game. He studied it and pointed, hoping at least not to lose immediately. Once the assistant moved the piece, Luna laughed. His rook was going too.

“Did you invite me so someone could lose beside you?”

“I invited you because I wanted to play with you. The losing was your contribution.”

He bent over laughing. Her hand hadn't healed behind the joke; the assistant collected the pieces. She didn't interpret his company as an answer matching Elena's. The next game could wait until she could move her own pieces, whenever her body—not her reluctance to lose—permitted it.

That evening the liaison reported matching entries in the academy's old acceptance catalog and independently retained municipal engineering records. A bounded comparison could be requested for tomorrow. The reply confirmed existence and a reading slot, not what the contents established. A catalog entry was a place to begin, not the answer. He would have to go there, read them, and let their actual wording disappoint him if necessary.

Adrian entered both references on tomorrow's page. His result notice no longer claimed readiness for expansion. He studied it without finding it useless.

The final division remained. They hadn't filled it today. They understood why they shouldn't hurry to. Outside, someone pushed an empty cart across the porch, its wheels bumping lightly over a joint between stones.
