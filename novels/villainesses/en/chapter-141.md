# Chapter 141: After They Take Away Her Chair

The first thing Isabella lost on the morning of January 16 was her nameplate.

The clerk welcoming her removed it from the council table and placed it beside a smaller table against the wall. A candidate under review must sit there rather than participate in the vote.

It was not yet a ruling. It already told everyone whose position others would decide.

Adrian stood in the place where he had been authorized to observe the proceedings. He did not put the nameplate back. Isabella did not erupt. After glancing at the smaller chair, she set down her reply and sat.

Elena had no right to speak inside and waited outside. Cecilia was at her office handling the festival explanation; Luna kept her medical appointment. Viola had prepared a route suggestion at the residence. Involvement did not make any of them attendants Isabella could summon to a hearing.

The chair confirmed the grounds delivered yesterday and asked whether she needed more time.

“I reserve the right to supplement my answer to new material. I'll answer the listed matters today.”

Lord Crowe sat opposite, his cane beside his chair. He did not smile at her changed position. Submitting his proposal, he first confirmed that the proxy draft would not be treated as evidence of her agreement.

The draft containing the false witness statement had been blocked. It could not return under another name.

Isabella looked at him.

“You know that statement was false.”

“Which is why I won't use it. Other questions remain.”

Adrian tightened his grip on his papers. He disliked Crowe, but could not insist that the man oppose Isabella only with arguments already disproved.

Security management came first. Ellery's written account recorded incomplete final verification of a temporary replacement. Dacre's report described the response, care of the injured, and continuing inquiry. The intruder's identity chain was not fully established. Isabella was not accused of wielding his knife. Nor did promptly evacuating guests erase management failures.

She acknowledged wanting efficient reception, but had never ordered temporary staff exempted from checks. She asked for each actual duty to be examined, rather than ending every inquiry once someone at the top apologized.

“Do you still wish to direct the old project?” Crowe asked.

“Yes. With defined limits and independent review.”

“You cited an insufficiently checked summary, then published inquiry material before the dispute was settled. Now five sets of records are being discussed together. Your caution seems to follow your expansion of influence.”

Her face tightened.

Adrian remembered the attractive sentences she had crossed out herself. Those changes were real. They could not make earlier choices unhappen.

She explained the scope of the missing-person notice, the private details withheld, and the originals retained in custody. She accepted a specific review of her authority. She had not brought people who supplied leads to testify to her good intentions.

One member asked whether, if publicity was beneficial, she would reveal the unpublished names to observers now.

“No.”

“Then we must trust only what you've selected?”

“Authorized reviewers can examine the full retained record. Agreeing to a search doesn't mean agreeing to make one's family my argument.”

Her words were hard rather than graceful. She knew she was relinquishing a possible appeal to sympathy.

Adrian relaxed his hands.

When the fiscal question arose, a clerk read out the hundred and twenty silver coins. Isabella corrected the characterization: an expense lacked independent support. Its ultimate recipient remained unverified. The recorded balance had not changed.

Crowe did not therefore accuse her of stealing it. He asked why, if authorized offices still had to establish the facts, a succession candidate should continue coordinating oversight resources.

That was the division he had come to make.

They debated through a second round of water. No one rose with a secret that changed every vote. Adrian's permitted contribution concerned only the records he had personally examined and the matters he had witnessed.

After he sat, the chair required the candidate to leave while members considered provisional measures.

The outer room was cold.

Elena offered Isabella the more sheltered chair without asking whether she had lost.

Isabella did not sit down in the chair Elena had offered her.

“They'll call it protecting the investigation.”

“Some of them may mean that,” Adrian said.

She turned sharply enough to tighten his throat.

“Will you speak for them now?”

“I want to know which point each supports. If we make them one enemy, we won't know what to challenge later.”

She finally sat, her skirt brushing dryly against a chair leg.

Elena placed warm water before her. No second cup collided with it.

“Let's hear the decision first.”

Another quarter-hour passed. A clerk came out with a less severe alternative: Isabella might retain nominal eligibility if a designated supervisor approved her future public statements about the old project.

She accepted the paper, not the offer. Reading to the end, she found that the supervisor could demand removal of disputed content. No procedure preserved her objection.

“What if they reject a fact supported by a source?”

“Further discussion would be possible.”

“Until then, only their version could be released?”

After hesitating, the clerk admitted that was the current wording.

Her fingers tightened around the sheet. This was a real route to losing less, not an absurd trap. Accepting could keep her in the candidate's chair tomorrow. Its supporters could reasonably call it necessary supervision.

She sent back an amendment. Disputes could be identified and rebuttals attached. Deleting disagreement in advance could not be the price of retaining her place. She accepted independent examination, not surrendering her voice to someone yet to be appointed.

When the clerk left, she asked quietly whether she had made everything harder again.

“Yes.”

She closed her eyes.

“But I don't want you guessing which sentence preserves your chair every time you speak,” Adrian added.

She opened them. Without smiling, she kept the alternative proposal's copy.

It was nearly noon when the council's provisional decision was finally brought out to them.

Isabella was suspended from exercising her succession eligibility. She could not participate in the next candidate ballot or related proxy appointments. Special coordination powers attached to that eligibility were suspended too. Existing materials would remain with their original custodians; limited review would be reassigned. Reasons, endorsements, and a reconsideration deadline accompanied the decision.

The king still reigned. This neither deposed a monarch nor gave Leon the throne. Those distinctions did not make the paper lighter for a woman still seeking that position.

Isabella required the clerk to read every restriction.

Ordinary travel, private correspondence, and objections remained permitted. She could submit evidence lawfully, but issue no further requests under her former special authority. A reconsideration request must be registered within three days. Whether it would receive a hearing was a separate decision.

She entered her objection and signed to acknowledge receipt, not agreement.

On her way out, the clerk who had moved her nameplate called her back.

“Your Highness, the old project's special dispatch token must also be surrendered.”

She took the little metal badge from her sleeve pocket. Its edges were warm from her hand. It made one small sound against the tray.

Her fingers remained suspended for a moment, as though expecting something.

Ellery could not simply take over everything she still needed. His duties belonged to the chamberlain's office; other instructions required attention. He explained which ordinary window would receive her correspondence. Two clerks previously waiting for special assignments returned to their departments. People who had agreed to provide explanations no longer automatically appeared on her schedule.

The loss did not mean everyone suddenly betrayed her.

Hands she had taken for granted simply withdrew to their ordinary duties.

Before she left, a new request for additional records was returned at the window. The special capacity cited in the request was suspended. A competent office could submit a fresh application.

Isabella stared at it.

“I could ask yesterday.”

“Previously authorized material remains properly preserved,” the clerk said carefully.

“I'm asking about today.”

He had no answer to give her.

Adrian did not invent one.

An ordinary carriage waited outside. Viola's suggestions had accounted for available vehicles, steps, and guard arrangements. She was not on duty and had not used her injured arm to clear anyone's path. Assigned staff provided the escort. The return journey followed open, familiar roads.

There was one problem: two facing seats.

The driver explained that Viola had circled the light two-person carriage for its easy turns and low step. She had not realized Elena would join those waiting outside.

Isabella glanced at Adrian, then Elena.

Usually, she might have made a pointed joke of the arrangement. Today, she merely asked wearily whether it could be changed.

Another carriage could be summoned. There would be a wait.

Elena did not volunteer anyone to be left behind in the name of courtesy. She and Adrian could use their original carriage. Isabella could take this one to the sitting room they were borrowing at the residence and meet them there. Escort and destination were reconfirmed. No one squeezed into an unsuitable seat.

Isabella nodded. Before boarding, she told Adrian she did not want to sit alone.

He suggested asking her accompanying maid to join her, if the woman wished. The maid had been preparing to wait outside. Startled by the question, she then gathered her skirt and took the opposite seat.

Before the door closed, Isabella thanked her quietly.

Adrian stepped back. Elena took his arm without reproaching him for not accompanying the princess.

“You were upset.”

“I thought we might gain more time.”

“You helped keep the facts on record. Time isn't yours alone to grant.”

Her expression was troubled too. Her fingers remained steady.

Back in the residence's sitting room, Viola froze upon hearing about the carriage.

“I calculated the wheel spacing.”

“No one's accused you of calculating a marriage,” Elena said.

“I didn't!”

Adrian laughed. By the window, Isabella gave a brief laugh too. The pressure of the entire morning finally found an outlet.

Viola promised to put passenger numbers before routes next time, then asked whether Isabella still wanted travel advice. The princess nearly said of course. Instead, she added, if your health allows.

“Seated route planning, yes. Active guard duty, no.”

“Then only the first.”

When lunch arrived, Isabella barely touched her soup. Looking outside, she finally asked the question she had kept since leaving the chamber.

“If I can no longer offer access, funding, or positions, will you still come?”

Adrian did not answer for everyone.

“I came today. We'll have to discuss what happens next.”

She looked up. He had not made this day a pledge of permanent loyalty. Nor had he asked for something else now that she had lost power.

At last, she picked up her spoon.

“Then let's discuss it.”

Her place at this table was an ordinary chair.

This time, no one moved it away.
