# Chapter 235: Ordinary People in the Purpose Column

On April 3, a notice on the municipal engineering archive directed visitors around water damage. Adrian followed the arrows upstairs and found ordinary bridge-repair drawings spread out to dry.

He nearly walked straight past them. The attendant pointed to a cord on the floor: readers must take the longer way, keeping dust away from the damp sheets.

The original purpose of the Crown of Destiny would share a building with a small bridge today. Adrian took the proper route. Somehow that made evasion harder than a secret underground chamber would have.

Elena had arrived with two sourced catalog extracts. She hadn't opened unregistered material. She had reserved him a chair facing away from the window, where he couldn't read the titles clearly.

“I remembered you dislike glare. Forgot you need to see the words.”

“One local parameter in your kindness needs adjustment.”

“Return it if necessary. Don't throw it away.”

He moved the chair until both could see. This one he intended to keep.

The academy examiner began with its historical acceptance catalog. The reference concerned an adopted engineering purpose statement, not a designer's private notebook. Its abbreviated name differed from today's Crown of Destiny. Drawing references, component records, and adjacent revisions needed to establish the connection. A circular shape alone proved nothing. The present name was familiar enough to tempt him into skipping that work. Familiarity, however, wasn't a chain of identification. Until the references matched, he had an old engineering document in front of him, not necessarily the beginning of the system whose effects they were living with.

The municipal custodian produced an independently retained acceptance counterpart with a continuous custody record, matching joins and attachment references. Old paper established age, not credibility by itself. The examiner compared project batches and revisions before admitting the authorized pages.

Adrian looked first for five marks. He found maintenance sections, bearing media, and emergency changeovers instead. Reaching for the next page, he was reminded it contained private records outside today's scope. He requested the approved masked version of the relevant reference.

His fingers stayed on his own notebook instead of the forbidden page. The restriction was inconvenient precisely because he wanted this answer so much. He could request the relevant reference in a permitted form; wanting the whole story wasn't permission to read a stranger's private one. He wanted a sentence that would overturn everything. The paper declined to arrange itself around his urgency.

The purpose statement described distributing brief calamity peaks among replaceable bearing media, buying time for sectional evacuation and repairs. Maintenance changeovers were to prevent irrecoverable accumulation at any single bearing point. It didn't promise calamity would vanish forever. It didn't identify five lifelong designated people as ordinary maintenance material.

Adrian read that last thought twice. It wasn't a quotation. It was his interpretation of an absence. Omission alone couldn't prove that no people had ever been involved.

The examiner found the accepted operating restrictions. Bearing media were replaceable external components; personnel duties concerned inspection, evacuation, and replacement work—not bodily reception. Another maintenance counterpart recorded renewed acceptance after a medium had been exchanged. Together the records supported an actual purpose of distributed buffering and maintainable changeover, unlike the later lifelong sacrifice of designated marked people.

The comparison wasn't seamless. A counterpart's final reference digit looked like seven; the academy catalog said one. Adrian's heart sank. Perhaps he'd found a connection only because he wanted it.

Instead of dismissing the problem as age, the custodian compared neighboring figures written by the same clerk, then the full reference across the attachment join. It was a one with a long tail. Even after explaining the handwriting, they checked the batch, drawing citations, and sequence of receipts to exclude a different project.

Adrian recorded the initial doubt. Agreement at the end didn't erase the minutes spent checking.

“Afraid people will see we misread things?” Elena asked.

“A little. More afraid I won't stop if something genuinely fails to match.”

She offered an unfinished sheet and told him to leave space for disagreements.

“An empty field isn't a disgrace.”

That was particularly difficult today. He left one, submitting it with the resolved handwriting query to the examiner.

“Does this establish that the original people were good?” Elena asked.

“No. What they approved and accepted at this stage. Intention and execution still need separate examination.”

Adrian didn't need a past populated by saints to object to living people being entered as components now.

Before noon, the independent liaison delivered Evelyn's supplementary statement. She hadn't appeared personally or explained all history on one sheet. She identified where later revisions had been issued, linked to the common sacrifice protocol already examined. She could explain catalog practices she'd handled, not certify everything implemented before her birth.

Within existing permission, the custodian retrieved matching revision pages; anything beyond it required another request. Evelyn's cooperation didn't transfer the whole archive. The revision replaced exchangeable distributed bearing with fixed marked bearers, assigning accumulated maintenance deficits through designated succession and termination procedures. Its stated reasons cited material shortages, interrupted upkeep, and increasing external shocks.

Oswin wasn't the handwritten author of every ancient revision. Adrian could distinguish those changes from his present responsibility for continuing their use and restricting alternative applications. Evelyn hadn't executed every historical page either. The distinctions didn't acquit either person of what they had actually done. They prevented a vast, easy accusation from replacing the particular decisions already supported by records. It would be harder to explain that way. It would also leave something another examiner could check rather than merely agree with. This was no fable invented in one villain's sitting. Many ordinary decisions had gradually pushed the burden toward bodies.

“First they kept using what couldn't be replaced,” Adrian said quietly. “Then made people who couldn't refuse take the load.”

The examiner requested that he label that as interpretation, not original wording. Elena aligned the pages so he could identify the passages on which it depended.

At midday, the attendant needed to lock the room and attend to the wet drawings. Adrian nearly requested another quarter hour. Beyond the door, someone held a damp bridge plan with visibly aching arms. He closed his notebook and let the restricted material return to custody.

They bought hot hand pies downstairs. Elena cut hers open and found one side thinner than the other. Did that require a sharing proposal too?

“We could exchange halves. You must explain which one you want.”

She laughed. “The half with filling.”

“Thank you for being honest. I decline.”

He moved his own pie closer and offered smaller pieces from each. The result wasn't mathematically equal; both wanted it. For a few minutes they were two people eating lunch in a building where other people still had work to do. Neither needed to turn an uneven pastry into a declaration about the whole kingdom. After tasting hers, Elena praised him for not turning every joke into tomorrow's research project.

When the laughter faded, he admitted wanting to proclaim a return to the ancient right path. But they didn't possess its materials, scale, or maintenance conditions. Their model wasn't a reconstruction. A purpose other than human sacrifice didn't guarantee success from copying its plans.

“You can still be pleased,” she said. “You needn't win the future first.”

What he most wanted to say was that she didn't belong in a fixed sacrificial position. She knew, she replied. She hadn't come merely to hear him refuse it for her. She wanted evidence that gave others grounds to refuse too.

He kept the correction rather than claiming he'd meant that all along.

Back at the academy, Luna received the permitted technical extracts for a short comparison. Her assistant laid out purpose, revision, and new model separately. They weren't stacked into one diagram. The early bearing specification had weaknesses, she observed: distribution wasn't limitless. Failed maintenance still crowded danger onto surviving points. Neither age nor original purpose saved their proposal from testing.

But the evidence changed something important. Permanently concentrating calamity on five people could no longer be called the device's only natural function. It was an institutional alteration with versions, resource reasons, and people who kept choosing it.

Adrian proposed a conclusion that could actually be challenged. Which records established the same engineering lineage? Which wording established changed purpose? Which present responsibilities still required independent inquiry? A dazzling heading about the system's truth would invite readers to fill in everything the evidence didn't yet say. He would rather name the three claims and show the pages beneath them. If someone found a mismatch, they could argue about that mismatch instead of being asked to choose whether to believe Adrian as a person.

Isabella agreed, provided they retained the maintenance shortages and genuine calamity reports. Publishing only the most enraging revision could suggest that the danger itself was invented. Cecilia wanted enough releasable evidence for people to judge, without exposing private identities alongside technical proof.

They disagreed about timing. Viola feared that publication might bring questioning down on people not yet protected. She wanted independent custody first and a bounded release. Adrian didn't give automatic victory to whoever had suffered most. He asked each woman to identify concrete risks and steps they could alter.

They settled on submitting an independently verified conclusion with its sources and limits. Custodians would review the public version page by page; identifying material would stay concealed. It neither required the public to wait forever nor turned their shock into consent on everyone else's behalf.

At the end of her session, Luna indicated the separate drawings with her chin.

“Don't combine them into one repaired system after I leave.”

He promised. She had her assistant collect her own copy, leaving the authorized working duplicate. Liking him hadn't reduced her need for a record of her own.

By dusk, the independent finding had been issued: original buffering and maintainable sharing purposes, the later change to fixed sacrifice, and the supporting references. It wasn't operating permission, judgment on every historical responsibility, or discovery of a manual route into the core.

Adrian set it on the table. The five women read the parts each could assess. Nobody removed a support or forgot her body because of a document. Viola first asked what happened if tomorrow someone invoked real calamity to demand that each decide privately, alone. How did they prevent this evidence from remaining only paper?

Cecilia looked up. Elena looked toward the others before looking at Adrian.

They weren't asking him to promise again that he would save everyone. They had different questions and hadn't decided whose should come first.

He swallowed the reassurance already forming. Moving the chairs apart, he gave them space to see each other. The bridge plans were dry and stored by now. The purpose they'd found today wouldn't fit back into an unexamined line of small print.
