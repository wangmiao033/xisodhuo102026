# Chapter 229: The Bill Couldn't Confess for Him

When Adrian entered the repair shop on March 28, the proprietor offered him a seat.

He declined, then stood unnaturally straight, as though judgment required locked knees.

The man glanced at his shoes. “You're blocking the drawer.”

Adrian moved and sat.

Yesterday's broken substitute remained sealed at the academy. He hadn't brought it for casual repair. Authorized copies described dimensions, material, and damage; technicians were separately examining the borrowed clamps. The shopkeeper could estimate a replacement and insulating pad, not declare the rig reusable from a few sheets.

Adrian spread the description and said he'd take responsibility.

“For asking the price, or paying it?”

The question stopped him.

“Asking first.”

The proprietor showed ordinary sample pieces of different thicknesses. The cheaper material didn't meet their specifications. The suitable one wasn't in stock and would follow existing orders. Adrian's sincerity didn't entitle him to displace other customers.

He recorded the estimate's conditions without obtaining credit in House Rosenberg's name. The man dated the quotation and pointed out earlier delivery commitments. Urgency wouldn't erase those customers.

As he put the samples away, an apprentice asked why research meant to change destiny worried about so little money.

Adrian almost said he worried about failing, not money. The proprietor closed the drawer. However grand the research's name, ordinary pieces paid the apprentice's wages.

The young man lowered his head. Adrian didn't pretend he'd understood all along. He admitted that coming to compensate people had briefly become a matter of courage in his mind. The shopkeeper was conducting actual business.

If they needed the material urgently, the proprietor suggested another shop across town, possibly at a higher price. Adrian requested the address, not treating a suggestion as guaranteed stock. The unsuitable cheaper option was clearly struck through so nobody could later quote only its attractive figure.

At the door, the apprentice caught up with Adrian's forgotten glove. He hadn't meant to despise small sums, he explained. Destiny sounded like something that ought to escape everyday rules.

“I often wish it would,” Adrian said.

“Will you keep trying?”

Looking at the glove's worn patch, Adrian offered no grand ending. “First account for what we borrowed yesterday. Then see what we can apply to do next.”

Disappointed, but apparently understanding that no miracle was due today, the apprentice returned to smoothing an ordinary wooden edge. Adrian secured his quotations. Part of bearing consequences was letting other people continue their days, not stand around watching his distress.

Elena waited in the academy's inventory room with yesterday's explicit loan list, not a bag of money to close every embarrassing question.

“You look as though you want to repay everything before explaining.”

“That obvious?”

“You've checked your empty purse twice.”

He put it away. The household's existing shortfall hadn't vanished. Nobles disclosing some available resources hadn't given the researchers ownership of everything. Compensating with money that didn't exist would create another gap by another promise.

The administrator separated damaged, awaiting inspection, and unused. The broken substitute and scorched pad required action. Clamps under examination couldn't already be declared ruined. The spare hadn't been fitted during the trial; being in the same room didn't damage it. The works employee's attendance allowance followed the agreement, not success.

Adrian requested inclusion of the discussion page containing his proposal for a short confirmation interval.

Elena asked whether he intended to make himself the entire cause.

After a pause, he shook his head. “That judgment was mine. I can't pretend I made a shared design alone. I can't remove my words from it either.”

She placed the pen before him without writing for him.

The first report was still poor. Adrian wrote negligence. Reading it, Luna asked what precisely he'd neglected. He explained his overreliance on a steady aggregate and underestimation of what a transfer wait could impose on one branch.

“Write that. Negligence sounds like admitting everything while giving anyone else nothing to examine.”

She read during a short session, right palm resting, left hand spared a pen. Her assistant distinguished observation from inference. Luna added her own responsibility for review: the model hadn't made the difference between total and local capacity sufficiently clear; design scrutiny needed to establish why that weakness hadn't been identified beforehand.

Adrian looked up. “You needn't make me feel better—”

“I'm not confessing for company. It's my model.”

He flushed. The interruption wasn't gentle. It respected them both more than a companion apology provided because he was sad.

She requested the original observations. The crack's exact sequence remained uncertain and stayed uncertain. Material examination was pending. The most plausible explanation didn't become a final cause merely by arriving first.

The engineering lecturer brought the initial clamp report. No structural crack had been found in the outer frame; one jaw needed further measurement. The return time required renegotiation. Its owner needed it that afternoon and couldn't collect as arranged.

The loss extended beyond a piece that money could replace.

Adrian visited the lender's steward, explaining the inspection and delay. Admission brought no instant forgiveness. The steward produced the reserved work period and asked who would explain this to workers already waiting in the yard.

Adrian couldn't promise an immediate clearance. He asked the steward and academy administrator to assess available ordinary substitutes directly, instead of declaring tools interchangeable when he didn't know. An alternative could accomplish some preparations, not the whole scheduled operation. The delay remained a recorded loss, with compensation referred to the people entitled to decide it.

“Shall we say we voluntarily extended the loan?” the steward asked.

Adrian understood the test. The man wasn't requesting a pleasant phrase. Would he hide inside a convenient one again?

“No. We failed to return it at the agreed time.”

The steward left the line blank for the clerk to enter those words.

Adrian felt no lighter returning. Admitting precise things lacked the heroic finality of confessing to every possible wrong. He had to ask about measurement again tomorrow and have estimates checked item by item. His regret couldn't settle an account.

Cecilia met him with warm water and asked whether he'd eaten lunch.

“Half.”

“Where's the other half?”

“On the table.”

“Good. At least the missing portion has a location.”

Laughing, he followed her into the side room. She didn't pray that nothing had broken. She put the cold bread beside his soup and left him to decide whether to soak it. He complained that soaked bread scarcely seemed like bread. She suggested eating it hard, then. A saint couldn't simultaneously solve hunger and every preference in texture.

Afterward he resumed checking the report without interpreting ordinary care as a declaration of innocence.

Luna's sole work session after rest involved no restarted apparatus. She and the technicians examined the disconnected design on paper. Any later proposal needed separately visible changes in both branches, independent limits not entrusted to the aggregate, and actual allowance for transfer confirmation rather than hoped-for synchronization. Amendments still required review. No second attempt was granted today.

Adrian nearly asked whether she considered the path impossible. She first asked whether he meant yesterday's configuration or every possible distribution.

“I'd made them the same thing.”

“I went from wanting it to succeed very badly to fearing it all had failed.”

Her admission didn't reduce the failure. It showed him that rigor wasn't the absence of desire. She was refusing to replace readings with it.

She requested a small box of old chess pieces unrelated to the trial. She'd considered a game later, but was too tired. Would he find the cracked ordinary piece so she wouldn't catch its sharp edge next time?

He found it without suggesting glue for the experimental wafer. The two cracks weren't one problem. Setting the piece aside, he offered to ask a woodworker, not promise restoration to its former condition.

“At last,” Luna said, smiling. “You aren't preparing to rescue everything on the table.”

“Or take away your game.”

“That sentence sounds better than your report.”

“The report still stays.”

“Unfortunately.”

She admitted she'd wanted to hear Don't worry, I'll win. Now she preferred him closing the chess box and suggesting they discuss a game tomorrow if she'd rested enough. He didn't turn their closeness into promises matching Elena's. Luna didn't use failure to claim his entire evening.

Before leaving, he praised her stopping judgment yesterday. She corrected him: she noticed the anomaly; the safety officer and operator executed the stop.

“Then I admire that you didn't wait for a prettier result.”

After considering him, she accepted that particular praise.

An answer arrived from the independent contact office that evening. Earlier questions about the old protocol and external routing authority had reached Evelyn Vale. In a bounded meeting with an adviser and independent recorder, she would explain categories of authority she'd held and identify records available for verification.

The notice conveyed no new control method and proved none of her forthcoming statements. She hadn't demanded immunity for speaking. Adrian couldn't cancel an inquiry into her conduct anyway.

Elena asked whether he wanted to attend.

“Yes. But send yesterday's failure report first, by the agreed route.”

He wouldn't wait for a new secret and then use it to explain his own error.

The clerk confirmed the destination and took the report. Repairs and compensation remained unsettled, the spare stored, another trial unapproved.

At the door, the porter asked him to confirm a short update for the lenders. The draft said the experiment had been completed and results were being organized. Individually, the words weren't false. Together, they might suggest that yesterday had gone reasonably well.

Adrian changed it: this simulation had not achieved its intended result; one substitute was damaged; borrowed clamps remained under examination; their delayed return had been discussed separately. He included neither an unverified cause nor private medical details about participants.

Would that discourage future loans, the porter asked.

“Possibly. They need to decide knowing what happened.”

Beside him, Elena prepared no more favorable version for noble readers. The checked message went through the responsible contact within its existing scope, not onto every city noticeboard. Leaving, Adrian wanted one more look, to make sure he'd not made it sound too bad. He let the sheet remain in the folder meant to leave.

Over dinner at home, Adrian watched Elena move his preferred portion closer. He said that today's hardest part was nobody requiring him to put himself into the compensation.

“You're used to treating that as the quickest payment.”

He nodded. She didn't prohibit him from ever making another mistake. She wanted someone who could explain tomorrow and return for dinner, not a receipt that disappeared once signed.

He asked to kiss her.

“Yes. Put down the soup spoon first.”

He did. The ordinary action made them laugh together. Tomorrow's questions remained. So did the accounts. Neither required them to leave tonight's dinner uneaten.
