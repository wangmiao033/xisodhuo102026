# Chapter 260: A Request Is Not a Handover

On the morning of April 22, the first thing Adrian recognized on the long table was an empty space.

Not yesterday's gap. The recorder had left a stand empty for an upstream original not yet available. They possessed verified copies; they wouldn't display a similar object to pretend the original was present.

The newly retrieved acknowledgment strips and February's relay-yard copies rested on differently colored cloths. Two examiners had compared provenance, shared timing marks, and identifiers. Worn sections retained their warning slips. Today concerned why receiving capacity had never become ready in one historical accident, not declaring every loop the product of one cause.

Adrian placed his drawing of the copper lever beside them.

“Keep my action here too.”

Nobody suggested waiting for a more respectable conclusion. The drawing remained beside the other evidence, neither covering it nor disappearing beneath a newly favorable explanation.

The engineer indicated the earliest mark. It recorded a request for alternative capacity and acknowledgment of receipt—not an actual connection. The old summary had merged this into an arranged column, using the same short stroke for the confirmation that hadn't returned.

A long stretch followed. Before the main bridge's disturbance, the completed-receiving column still had no corresponding mark.

“So nobody connected it and secretly closed it again?”

“These records don't support a completed connection. We can't invent the worn section. But the state before your shutdown has corroboration.”

It matched the earlier inspection sheet: bypass reconfigured, awaiting retest, connection permission outstanding, isolation maintained. Those weren't newly discovered sentences. The acknowledgment now showed how they had become an apparently dependable promise while traveling through successive summaries.

Evelyn's fingers moved without reaching for the original. Current reviews had retained this merging of categories. Whether she'd personally copied this entry required the relevant signature. Saying she was guilty couldn't authenticate every handwriting sample.

The examiner recorded the specific points open to further questioning and continued.

The next mark resembled a folded-back arrow. It didn't reverse time. Under the old distribution rule, burden returned to the still-connected original route when the alternative hadn't actually accepted it. An operating attachment described the delay conditions. Timing aligned with increased load at the main bridge. The engineer moved ordinary beads along a diagram, identifying where each piece of evidence ended.

Adrian could finally see the sequence.

Upstream trouble and damage to the western route already existed. An alternative had received a request on paper while remaining physically unready. Returning burden stayed with the original route. He had then bypassed a stop and severed the main bridge before receiving capacity existed, removing part of the western passage's protection.

Gray mist hadn't been conjured by one evil button. Other people's errors didn't mean his hand hadn't moved.

“I wanted to stop them sending more people. I left what couldn't be caught for the next moment.”

Luna listened during her limited consultation, right palm supported, left hand in her lap. Her assistant recorded his words separately from the strips' technical interpretation. Motive wasn't measured data; physical sequence wasn't an argument excusing motive.

A young assistant asked why people hadn't simply waited longer if they knew the burden would return.

Viola moved the old evacuation map forward. A walking slope had become an entirely clear area in the summary while stretchers still waited. Waiting had costs. Changing routes demanded more than courage. Today's safe room couldn't answer for everyone there.

The young man admitted he'd spoken too easily. Instead of humiliating him, Viola asked him to use separate words for passable and already evacuated. He took fresh paper.

This time the story would not end with exposing an old mistake. Adrian watched the assistant begin again, carefully distinguishing a usable path from people who had actually reached safety. It was an ordinary correction, but nobody hurried him past it.

Before the break, the examiner asked everyone to identify one thing they still couldn't claim. Not to weaken the discovery, but to prevent the next notice outrunning the evidence.

Evelyn couldn't attribute every outstanding permission to one person's instruction. Isabella couldn't dismiss the old system's real protection as entirely fraudulent. Viola pointed out that missing victims remained missing. Numbers couldn't fill themselves in for a complete conclusion.

Adrian looked at his lever drawing. He couldn't claim their new plan avoided the same error.

Only after speaking did he understand why he'd avoided the document bag beside his chair. Finding a period omitted by dead officials was easier than recognizing one absent from his own diagram.

He put the bag on the table. Yesterday's meal had left a little flour on its fastening. Danger didn't always carry an old Temple seal. Sometimes it traveled home in papers he believed were helping people.

He opened their new proposal. Voluntary sharing remained on its cover, words that had made him proud. In the withdrawal section, one clean arrow connected a stop request to the reserve node.

“There's no time here.”

“The full technical attachment specifies delay, independent cutoff, and residual handling,” Luna said. “You weren't given permission for instantaneous transfer.”

He turned back to the attachment and found the restrictions exactly where she said they were. She had not omitted them. The words had been available to him before he chose the simpler explanation, which made blaming an incomplete document impossible. His organizational summary compressed them into reserve capacity would take over. He remembered explaining it that way to reassure prospective participants.

Had anyone consequently accepted undisclosed exposure? Cecilia asked. Existing signatures covered conditional assessment and preparation; nobody had undergone powered human operation. Withdrawal rights hadn't formally disappeared. Nevertheless, absence of injury didn't justify continuing to use that diagram.

Adrian marked his copy withdrawn, preserving it for review. The explanation team would stop using it and send corrections. Quietly replacing the old page would leave people who'd asked questions believing their own memories were wrong.

“I thought reassurance was part of the work.”

“It is,” Cecilia said. “It shouldn't depend on hiding the next square.”

Isabella unfolded yesterday's relocation plan. An available room didn't mean a wagon had arrived or its occupant had chosen to move. She was still learning that. Other people's earlier mistakes didn't place her beyond explanation.

At midday, she and Adrian visited the couple who hadn't agreed to move together. The wife had reached the temporary lodging; her husband still wanted to watch their shop. They argued on a courtyard bench without either becoming a villain. He feared the distance from their business. She feared another night without safe lighting.

Isabella didn't flatten that into one satisfactory answer. The receptionist confirmed how long the woman's existing bed remained available. Her husband received the ordinary daytime route back to the shop. No extra wagon existed; no royal escort would remain all day.

If she really was a princess, he said, surely she could command something.

“I can decide whether to report you as already relocated. I choose not to.”

He stared, then studied the map. His wife asked whether she would lose her place if he didn't come tonight. Told his absence wouldn't cancel it, she finally said calmly that she intended to stay.

Outside, Isabella leaned against the gate and smiled wearily.

“I once thought not ordering people around would at least make me look generous.”

“And now?”

“Quite often it makes me look useless.”

Adrian did not immediately praise her. She had not said it to earn assurance that everything was right. He let the admission remain between them without trying to improve its appearance. Would she still walk with him to buy lunch? If this was compensation, she warned, she'd choose the most expensive dish. He was hungry too; he could only afford the ordinary portion.

She laughed and went with him rather than wait for a carriage that might restore the appearance of royalty.

At the academy that afternoon, technicians had turned the new concern into a limited test proposal. New materials and an independent energy unit would simulate two empty loads withdrawing at different times on an isolated bench. They would observe the buffer within approved limits. No person, destiny mark, or ancient core energy would be connected. It tested their missing arrow, not the safety of the entire kingdom.

The first proposal was returned. The shield's borrowing period excluded tomorrow morning, and one listed technician had another assignment. Adrian remembered reserve capacity would take over and didn't say someone would manage. The responsible staff found another qualified shield and checked its coverage. A qualified replacement technician accepted specified hours. Courage wasn't missing. Actual people and equipment had to arrive.

Late that day, they completed unpowered assembly checks. They examined the independent stop chain and proposed buffer separately. Total energy was restricted to what the isolation equipment could contain. The node itself could still fail; that risk belonged in the test's purpose. A shield couldn't promise an undamaged node.

Only after reviewing the final configuration and staffing did the site supervisor authorize tomorrow's defined test.

Approval did not keep Luna working late. The limited session had ended, even though everyone around her now had another reason to want an immediate answer. She arranged for her remaining objection to travel without requiring her body to stay. Her assistant delivered one final objection: a limit measured for a single branch couldn't simply become a safe limit for two branches withdrawing together. The test supervisor included that as the question under investigation and reduced initial input. An unresolved issue wasn't an absent one.

Adrian walked Luna to the door.

“I wanted to thank you for noticing. But you'd already written it there.”

“You can thank me for not throwing the attachment at your head.”

“That would hurt your hand.”

“I'd ask my assistant.”

He laughed, then confirmed his diagram was withdrawn. Did he expect her to forgive the omission immediately? No. He hoped she'd still object tomorrow when necessary, without worrying that disappointment would cover his face.

“It will,” she said. “I'll object anyway.”

That sounded more like her trust than an intimate reconciliation would have. She settled into the carriage with her arm supported. Promising to remain beside him couldn't remove fatigue from her body.

When Adrian assembled the evening findings, he separated supported historical causes from unanswered questions. The summary had treated a request as completed receiving; real delay and return had burdened an already vulnerable route. His wrongful shutdown remained part of the chain. Upstream trouble and distorted evacuation reports hadn't vanished.

The discovery supplied one specific mechanism. It issued no universal pardon, either to the person who had moved the lever or to those whose decisions had made that moment more dangerous.

He signed the correction for which he was responsible and looked toward tomorrow's bench. No woman stood there waiting to prove loyalty. Two empty loads awaited power.

If that arrow couldn't hold, this time they had to see it before asking anyone to stand on it.
