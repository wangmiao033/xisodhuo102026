# Chapter 211: The Door Had No Answer From Them

On March 10, the Temple gatehouse lent Adrian a pencil so short he couldn't find a way to grip it without covering its point.

Elena's mouth twitched.

“You may laugh,” he said.

“I'm assessing how many words it has left.”

“Enough for yes, perhaps. Not for an explanation.”

“Then it isn't suitable for today.”

She laughed and took an ordinary spare pencil from her document bag, setting it on the empty table. He thanked her and returned the stub. The attendant found its extension holder in a drawer and realized he'd forgotten to provide it. All three laughed again. The small morning interruption pushed the stone door slightly further from Adrian's thoughts.

Today's authorization added one operation: works staff could open the exterior dust covers and document the structures underneath. No power, lock testing, or crossing the core threshold. A custodian checked yesterday's seals before engineers inspected the supports. When someone tried arranging everyone for an unobstructed view, Viola pointed out the narrow passage. They needed room to turn around.

Her left arm remained supported. Her station was on the level outer gallery; somebody else had the evacuation bell rope. Important discoveries couldn't heal her arm. Her advice didn't depend on being able to wield a sword.

Luna stayed in the upstairs reading room, her right palm cushioned, awaiting the draftsman's copies. Last night she'd been furious that she wanted to see the door more than anyone. This morning her displeasure hadn't changed the medical limits.

“Tell them to draw straight lines,” she'd told Adrian. “I can't go down and criticize every crooked one myself.”

“I'll repeat that exactly.”

“Not the last part.”

He'd agreed too quickly. She narrowed her eyes, recovering a little of their familiar contest.

Belowground, ordinary lamps supplied the light. No old apparatus powered them. Adrian stopped at the engineer's boundary while two workers supported a cover, released its fastenings, and transferred its weight to a stand. Nothing awakened at a spoken name. Dust fell onto a cloth; old fittings scraped.

The first mark emerged.

A narrow metal mounting surrounded it, fine teeth around the edge. Instead of a keyhole, a bent groove occupied its center. Adrian recognized something without trusting recognition enough to name anyone. The next cover revealed a different outline. Workers supported and removed the remaining three circular covers in turn.

Five.

Elena didn't move closer. Cecilia lowered the hands she'd clasped at her chest. Isabella tightened her right hand around her folder, her left wrist still supported. Nobody's mark glowed. No voice welcomed them into their appointed places.

Their silence disturbed Adrian more. All five grooves were clear, but none could answer whether the people resembling them wished to approach. He could imagine someone standing here with a list, assigning each person a place, then describing the arrangement as though the stone itself had chosen. The absence of a miracle left the human decisions harder to overlook.

The works examiner inspected the joints under angled light. Connections extending into the door couldn't be dismantled. The draftsman recorded visible wear, old numbers, and corresponding inspection marks. A maintenance directory described the same number under an old heading: FIVE MARK LOCKS. That supported the name, not a conclusion about what opening them would do.

“Do you need us closer?” Cecilia asked.

No. Drawings could be brought to them. Nobody had authorization for human testing.

Adrian kept his scarred hand inside his sleeve. No new images came, only dampness in his palm. The impulse to touch something for a quicker answer remained. He stepped back, leaving space for the workers moving the stand.

“Let's take up what we can actually see.”

Upstairs, the draftsman spread five scale drawings. Luna immediately asked about a gap in a cross-line. He produced his second field sketch: rust obscured that section. He hadn't omitted something visible.

She changed her intended correction to an uncertainty note.

“At least your lines aren't crooked.”

“Thank you.”

“That's the minimum. Try not to look so moved.”

Adrian laughed. She glanced at him and smiled too. Her left hand started toward the drawing, hovered, then returned beside its support. She asked the assistant to move the sheet. Frustration showed, but she didn't demonstrate recovery with one graceful gesture.

Before comparison, the custodian asked each woman whether her existing mark tracing could be produced. Elena agreed first, then Cecilia. Viola established that it wouldn't enter an unauthorized public notice. Luna wanted the qualification about her indistinct edge retained.

Isabella kept her folder closed.

“Who will receive the comparison?”

The clerk named the repository and associated examiner. Nobody argued that the princess's public support obliged her to unlimited disclosure. Isabella requested notice of any proposed additional circulation, then released the fastening. Waiting hadn't made any lock more dangerous. Nobody watched the clock.

Only after all five had decided did Adrian move the empty display board over. He remembered Isabella's hand on the folder. Once, agreement to cooperate would have seemed broad enough to cover everything that followed. Her hesitation gave their shared decision five separate shapes.

The custodian produced existing tracings. No undressing, blood, or fresh measurement. They compared one turn in an outline with the corresponding turn on a drawing, then the position where another line divided. Bends, branches, and marginal numbers supported correspondence between the five positions and their registered marks. Rust still left one detail uncertain. That space remained empty rather than being filled with what everyone now expected to find. A resemblance could support the comparison without making every hidden part visible.

Elena's tracing lay beside the first drawing. After a while she asked for more space between them.

“That makes comparison harder,” a clerk observed.

“I know. Put them together again afterward. For a moment, I'd like to distinguish the record of me from the thing they made.”

A narrow strip of tabletop appeared. Adrian didn't turn her request into a speech. That gap solved no technical question. Placing two sheets together nevertheless couldn't make a person's destination inevitable.

Isabella frowned over a damaged sentence concerning opening.

“If the threshold records describe a real danger, people outside can't wait until we've answered everything.”

Elena looked up. “And therefore?”

“We need surface precautions and the missing transfer information at the same time. I'm not proposing we test the locks. Nor will I dismiss every warning as intimidation.”

Cecilia stopped before answering. She thought of people at the meal hall who waited for prayers without ever seeing technical evidence. Declaring that the Temple had lied about everything wouldn't protect them.

“I agree about the danger. But these tracings cannot prove we've agreed to anything. That door doesn't get to state even what I believe.”

Viola sat far enough from the stove to keep her arm support from becoming stifling. “My willingness to stay doesn't let me sign for the other four. Even if someone calls it our honor.”

“What if one day there really is only one choice?” Isabella asked.

Silence followed. She hadn't made the hypothetical today's conclusion. She also wouldn't pretend she never thought about ordinary people above them, bearing no marks.

“Then we must establish that,” Luna said. “We don't yet know whether all five locks govern the same thing. I won't tell you that smashing them is harmless merely to contradict Oswin.”

Through the window Adrian watched a mason move an empty bucket for a lunch cart. People outside weren't shadows waiting for his heroic declaration. They grew hungry, detoured around closed streets, and made decisions he hadn't thought to ask about.

“I don't want you to die.”

Elena watched him without supplying an easy reciprocal promise.

“And I don't want others to suffer for us. I can't turn those wishes into one beautiful answer today. I can refuse to treat five lock positions as permission to send you through the door.”

Elena looked from the drawing to his face.

“If I make a decision you dislike, will you decide to take me away first and let me be angry once I'm safe?”

Of course not nearly escaped him. Too quickly; it hadn't passed the brass handle or the old courthouse door. He held it back.

“I may still want to. But I'll tell you what I'm thinking instead of secretly changing your way out. You can ask somebody else to stay and hear us finish.”

She nodded, awarding the promise no greater weight than it presently possessed. There was still space between them left by old harm. Today it could hold an uncomfortable truth.

“And you can't send yourself through first,” Luna said.

“Including me,” he agreed.

Nobody pledged to live and die for everybody else. Isabella still wanted actual responses about surface precautions. Cecilia wanted ordinary believers' access to information preserved. Their disagreements went to the responsible staff; sharing a table hadn't dissolved them.

The afternoon supplementary drawings brought a smaller discovery.

Yesterday's long shallow groove outlined the inspection face of a narrow side case. Works staff supported and removed the thin plate, exposing its opening. The plate was only a dust cover, unconnected to the structure inside the door. No visible linkage connected the case to the locks. Comparing the exterior maintenance diagram and accessible inspection opening, works staff identified a separate mechanical records compartment. The disconnected old interface and stop were visible; anything extending through the core door remained off limits. The custody directory separately listed an outer-records maintenance key. It wasn't a key to the core.

The case's locating fields made little sense when read from outside. The draftsman copied legible short references without interpretation. Luna studied them twice and declined to guess. An old annotation put the recording direction at the cabinet's rear, unlike the entrance view.

“Perhaps we can read something without fitting people into locks,” Adrian said.

“After the works examiner verifies separation and the custodian verifies the key,” Luna reminded him. Then, more quietly, “I hope so too.”

The authorized next step concerned only those exterior records. Nobody turned a mark lock today. When the drawings were sealed, the custodian didn't list five women as keys awaiting issue. Each had agreed only to this comparison of existing tracings.

Lunch had long lost its best warmth. Cecilia opened the soup container and said eating now remained preferable to declaring themselves not hungry. Viola solemnly agreed. Isabella found no need for another committee. Luna asked why Adrian always finished discussions standing furthest from the food.

“I may still be poor at locating the true core entrance.”

Even Elena laughed. She waited until he had bread before asking what had happened to her spare pencil.

Adrian checked his pockets, then searched his folder. It hadn't fallen underground or been sealed as an ancient artifact. It lay perfectly intact between two stiff backing sheets. Nevertheless, he hunted more intently than he'd examined one of the lock grooves.

“I thought you'd entered it as borrowed and overdue.”

“Not yet. Another minute and I might have.”

He returned it and asked why she hadn't reminded him earlier. She glanced at where he'd been standing. He'd been a long way from the soup; getting him over here had seemed more important.

“So this was a way of making me eat?”

“If you miss lunch looking for a pencil, I'll have to reconsider my opinion of you.”

He sat immediately. Luna laughed hard enough to need the water her assistant offered. She claimed she merely appreciated a clear, comprehensible instruction. Elena put the pencil away and finally picked up her own bowl.

The five locks remained silent below. Upstairs someone complained about cold soup, someone said it was still worth eating, and a discussion began about asking the kitchen to use less salt tomorrow. Adrian broke the crust of his bread and finished his late lunch among those familiar voices.
