# Why Do All the Villainesses Want to Marry Me?
## Volume One: A Betrothal Written in Blood
### Chapter 23: I Only Asked You Two to Check the Records

At breakfast on October third, Elena proposed a perfectly reasonable division of labor.

"You're going to the academy today to work with Miss Bell. Bring me a summary this afternoon."

Adrian looked up.

"You're not coming?"

"I need to review the estate's accounts."

"But we only found Evelyn Vale's name yesterday."

"I know." Elena pushed a signed authorization across the table. "The Old Records Preservation Office and Saint Rose Academy used the same transfer forms at one point. Nora knows those formats. It's more productive to consult her than to march straight into the Temple."

Adrian read the authorization and nodded.

"That makes perfect sense."

Elena seemed to wait for something else.

"That's all?"

"What else should I say?"

"Nothing."

She picked up her teacup rather more forcefully than usual.

Martha arrived with the eggs.

"My lady, you truly won't accompany Mr. Reed?"

"Of course not. My administrative aide can examine ordinary records without my supervision."

"Didn't you tell him yesterday that he mustn't go off alone?"

"I was talking about dangerous places. The academy has archivists and a loan register."

Adrian raised his hand.

"I have Hale's permit. I'll leave my itinerary at the gatehouse."

"Excellent. Do your work."

He thanked her cheerfully and left.

When the dining room door closed, Martha glanced at Elena's untouched breakfast.

"You've arranged everything very sensibly, my lady."

"Naturally."

"Then perhaps you don't need to turn the same page of your ledger three times while asking whether Miss Bell still wears that apricot-colored scarf."

Elena looked up.

"I didn't ask what she was wearing."

"You asked about the scarf."

"That was polite conversation."

"Of course." Martha nodded. "Entirely professional."

The academy's west wing still smelled of old paper and dried insect-repelling herbs.

Nora Bell had prepared two registers and three brass paperweights.

"Adrian! You're right on time."

"I've developed a habit of taking the west stairs."

"That's the Rosenberg house, isn't it?"

"Apparently I need retraining."

Nora laughed.

She didn't seem disappointed that Elena hadn't come. Instead, she spread a diagram across the table.

"Your B-17 reference and the Old Records Preservation Office aren't in our public case texts. But we have samples of the old transfer system. When the same file moved between institutions, its receipt sequence should tell us which office handled it first."

"So the Judicial Office stamped it, then the Temple received it?"

"Not quite. There were three copies. The sending office kept the first. The temporary custodian issued a sealed acknowledgment. The final recipient returned a confirmation. If even one was missing, the transfer couldn't be verified as complete."

Adrian remembered the evidence page lost in the warehouse fire.

"If someone destroyed one copy, no one could establish what happened."

"Or someone might simply have misplaced it." Nora picked up her pencil. "Let's identify which copy is missing before we assume why."

They began with a guide to the old forms.

The academy didn't have the original case file for R-074, but it kept training materials from a historical records-transfer course. Several examples used real case numbers with the personal information removed, so students could learn to identify seals.

On page three, Adrian saw a familiar number.

R-074.

He resisted the urge to celebrate.

"Why did they keep the actual case number?"

Nora read the introduction.

"At the time, the teaching authorization required names to be concealed, but the archive number could remain."

"So we can at least examine the routing?"

"After checking the seals."

She moved a lamp over the three sample forms.

The first was a preservation request from the old Judicial Office.

The second was a temporary receipt from the Temple records department.

The third, however, wasn't a completed final handover.

It carried one handwritten line.

*Temporarily stored in the sealed western courtroom of the old courthouse, pending verification.*

Adrian stared.

"The old courthouse wasn't even operating then, was it?"

"The building was still used for examining historical material. The public maintenance books wouldn't tell us what was stored inside." Nora paused. "But remember, this is a training copy. We can't claim the original R-074 was definitely kept there."

Even with the qualification, Adrian could see the next step.

If a document had been stored temporarily in the sealed courtroom, there ought to have been a formal access or custody record.

"We should apply for its sealed inventory."

"We can. But first we need the provenance of this sample. Otherwise they can reasonably refuse us access to original files based on classroom materials alone."

Nora leaned over to copy the reference number. Her silver hair clip had slipped, and a few strands fell beside her glasses.

Adrian held down the page when a draft stirred the papers.

"Thanks," she said.

"No trouble."

"Elena chose a capable aide."

He blinked.

"She said that?"

"She wrote to Mr. Fenn yesterday. She said you can distinguish what you've observed from what you're merely guessing. She also asked us not to skip authentication just because an old case has your name attached."

Warmth spread through Adrian's chest.

"She never told me."

"Then make sure you thank her."

Someone cleared her throat by the door.

"Thank whom?"

Adrian looked around.

Elena stood at the entrance, holding a purchasing reconciliation sheet.

"My lady? I thought you had accounts to review."

"I finished them."

Nora glanced at the clock.

"It's only ten-thirty."

"Efficiency isn't a crime," Elena replied.

Nora covered her mouth.

"Certainly not. Especially when you're worried about someone working with your administrative aide."

"Miss Bell. We're discussing archival records."

"Of course we are."

Adrian had the distinct feeling that he'd wandered into a trap being operated jointly by two exceptionally clever women.

He hurried to intervene.

"My lady, Nora found an R-074 transfer sample."

Elena joined them, her expression sharpening at the words *sealed western courtroom*.

"Do we know whether the original is still there?"

"Not yet." Nora indicated the disclaimer on the training copy. "We need the actual storage number."

They compared the sample's footer.

Elena examined the old Judicial Office stamp.

Nora verified the academy's training authorization.

Adrian copied the initials beside the temporary transfer.

A sealed courtroom handover required two signatures from different institutions.

One partly visible name had been reduced to two initials.

M. H.

"Who is that?" Adrian asked.

Nora consulted the historical personnel list.

"Matthew Holt. He was a junior judicial clerk back then. He now works in the old archives transfer office."

"Can we speak to him?"

"Through a formal request. But he's also attending tomorrow evening's Noble Archives Preservation Society dinner. The guest list is posted outside."

Elena's eyes brightened.

"House Rosenberg received an invitation."

Adrian couldn't help himself.

"Apparently aristocratic dinners do have practical uses."

Elena gave him a look.

"Your last one was rather memorable."

His mind immediately supplied the scene of her dress caught beneath his shoe.

He returned to the documents.

Nora found another teaching sample, this one designed to help students recognize suspicious seals.

One old Judicial Office stamp showed a notch along its decorative rim.

"That's strange," she murmured.

"A fake?"

"Could be a rubbing taken from a worn original. The training notes merely say it requires closer examination."

Elena brought out a certified tracing of the seal on the blue jewelry case from September seventeenth.

The notches appeared in remarkably similar positions.

"That might still be normal wear," Adrian said.

"So we'd need the stamp's issuance and maintenance records," Nora agreed.

Someone might have been reusing an old seal template.

Without supporting witnesses and tool records, it wasn't proof of who forged the royal letter.

By noon, the three had compiled a source table identifying every document they were allowed to cite.

Adrian listed Nora as a research collaborator.

Elena checked the entries and signed as the household's witness.

Nora surveyed the paper.

"How charming. Two ladies have signed either side of Adrian's name."

Adrian looked up.

"And?"

"Nothing. I'm sure the newspapers would love three names lined up like that."

Elena closed her pen.

"Then let's not send it to a newspaper."

"Relax. The academy isn't in that business."

Nora returned the originals to their protective sleeves.

Before they left, she insisted they check the amendment index too.

"One final point. What we examined was an anonymous training sample. The academy borrowed the original materials to create it. We don't know whether the copy omitted anything."

"So we can't submit it as the original in court," Adrian said.

"Exactly. But the educational reproduction was authorized. The loan register retains the original court delivery number. You can use that number to support a request to inspect the real transfer file."

Elena copied it.

"Who approved the sample?"

Nora turned the page.

"Three academy instructors and one reviewer from the Judicial Office. The reviewer later transferred to the old archives office."

Her pencil stopped on the name.

Matthew Holt.

"He didn't only handle the file. He reviewed the teaching copy too?" Adrian asked.

"Yes. He may know what differs between the sample and the original."

"Or he may have only signed the routine approval," Elena pointed out.

"Exactly." Nora nodded. "When you meet him, ask whether he remembers the review before you accuse him of forging anything."

Adrian carefully noted both ways of framing the question.

Finding a name was easy compared to persuading the person behind it to talk.

Nora looked toward Elena.

"By the way, Elena, I'm not helping him simply because he's handsome."

Elena went pink.

"I never said you were."

"You wrote three times in yesterday's letter that research staff should concern themselves with research."

"I was clarifying professional roles."

"I know." Nora smiled. "I genuinely love old archives. Discovering what happened to Augustus Wynn matters to me more than any romantic scandal."

Elena was quiet for a moment.

"You're right. I'm sorry. I can be overly sensitive."

"A little sensitivity won't hurt. But please don't ask me to write my research notes like a formal dissolution of an engagement."

Adrian struggled so hard not to laugh that his shoulders shook.

Both women glared at him.

On the carriage ride home, he finally asked Elena, "Why did you really come?"

"I finished the accounts."

"Only that?"

She was silent a while.

"I wanted to know whether my fear of losing your attention was interfering with your work."

Adrian hadn't expected such a candid answer.

"You're not interfering. Nora's helping with things she knows. You're helping in a different way. Those aren't competing achievements."

Elena turned toward the window.

"I know. Knowing something and acting accordingly aren't always the same."

"Then just tell me next time," he said. "I'll answer honestly. You won't have to guess."

She turned back to him.

"You're becoming rather mature."

"Wasn't I mature before?"

"You couldn't even tie a bow tie."

"Those are completely different skills!"

She laughed.

Their carriage passed the old courthouse. Both looked toward the sealed door at the end of its western wall.

Tomorrow evening, they'd have a chance to speak with Matthew Holt.

And the last traceable destination of R-074 was the courtroom that door had kept closed for years.
