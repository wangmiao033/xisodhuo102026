# Why Do All the Villainesses Want to Marry Me?
## Volume One: A Betrothal Written in Blood
### Chapter 24: This Is Not a Date. Really.

On the evening of October fourth, Adrian stood in front of his mirror, studying his left foot with an intensity usually reserved for life-threatening evidence.

"Left foot forward. Right foot back. Turn."

He made it through the sequence three times.

On the fourth, he stepped on a shoe brush.

The brush shot across the floor and struck the door.

Martha poked her head inside.

"Mr. Reed, are you planning to destroy another door?"

"I'm practicing."

"Dancing?"

"Yes."

"Didn't you dance at the palace?"

"That was a catastrophic technical failure."

Martha looked him up and down.

"If kissing a duke's daughter is what you call a technical failure, half the young gentlemen in the capital would pay you for lessons."

Adrian retrieved the brush.

"Please. I only want to attend one dinner without an incident."

Elena chose that moment to appear in the doorway.

She wore a dark-green evening gown, with none of the heavy jewelry she'd worn to the royal ball. A small gold leaf pinned her hair in place.

Adrian forgot about his feet.

"Well?" she asked.

"That color... it suits you."

"Look at that. You've learned how to give a proper compliment."

"I prepared three. The second one might get me killed."

"Then save it for another evening."

She handed him the guest list.

The Noble Archives Preservation Society was holding its annual dinner at a private club in the old district. Most of the guests were lawyers, academy scholars, collectors, and judicial clerks.

Matthew Holt appeared on the seventeenth line.

"We're not going to pick his pockets," Elena reminded him. "First, we'll ask whether he'd be willing to speak to us with a lawyer present."

"Understood."

"And if he declines?"

"We record his refusal. We do not chase him into the washroom."

"Good."

"I've made remarkable progress."

"Let's hope your feet have too."

Adrian glanced at the innocent shoe brush and kept quiet.

The club was smaller than the royal palace, but felt much more like a gathering of people who actually wanted to be there.

Pine logs burned in the fireplace. Old maps hung beside a modest portrait exhibition. Guests carried books and glasses of aged wine rather than letters accusing one another of treason.

Still, Adrian couldn't entirely escape the whispers.

"Isn't that Rosenberg's new aide?"

"The one who kissed her at the ball?"

"Think he'll be dancing tonight?"

Elena walked beside him without changing her pace.

"Nervous?"

"I'm considering moving to another country."

"Choose one after we've finished the case."

They found Matthew demonstrating how to repair the fibers of an old document.

He was approaching thirty, with round spectacles, ink stains on his cuffs, and a habit of pinching his tie between two fingers when he spoke.

At Elena's introduction, he straightened at once.

"Lady Rosenberg. It's an honor."

"Mr. Holt. We were hoping you could help us understand a few historical transfer records."

His eyes moved to Adrian.

His smile faltered.

"Mr. Reed?"

"Have we met?"

"No." The answer came a shade too quickly. "I've seen your name in the papers."

"I hope you skipped the illustrations."

Matthew gave a strained smile.

"I read the account of your hearing."

They moved to the club's library, where the music was little more than a murmur through the walls.

Hale remained nearby, ready to join any formal discussion. Elena showed Matthew the academy's authorized teaching-sample catalogue for R-074, not the protected original loan page.

"Your name appears as a reviewer," she said. "We'd like to know whether that's your signature."

Matthew accepted the copy.

His fingertips trembled.

"It is."

"Do you remember what you reviewed?" Adrian asked.

"The reference number. Not every detail." Matthew glanced at the door. "My superiors wanted some sealed files adapted into training examples. We were supposed to remove personal information while preserving the procedural details."

"But the borrower's signature survived in the R-074 material."

Matthew closed his eyes for a moment.

"That shouldn't have happened."

"An oversight?" Elena asked.

"Possibly. Or someone might have substituted an older page after the sample was prepared. I can't settle it from memory."

Adrian didn't press him for a more exciting answer.

"There's something else we'd like to understand. The third copy says the file was held in the sealed western courtroom. Why isn't there a final recipient listed?"

Matthew studied the reproduction for a long time.

"An exception under the old system. When two institutions had to preserve the same file, the court kept a temporary transfer copy. One officer from the court and one from the Temple had to acknowledge custody."

"You've seen that temporary copy?"

"Yes."

Hale entered quietly.

Matthew stiffened.

"I haven't agreed to testify."

"And we aren't asking you to accuse anyone of a crime tonight," Hale said. "We can discuss the procedure informally. If you decide to give a statement, an independent clerk can record it."

Matthew sat back.

"I know what you're looking for," he said at last. "The original R-074 file."

Adrian waited.

"The transfer should have been completed, but the final acknowledgment never came back to the old archives office. A few years later, I was told to add a receipt number to the catalogue as though it had. I refused."

"Who told you?"

"My superior said it was an old administrative problem and ordered me to leave it alone."

"Did you keep the correspondence?"

Matthew looked at Adrian's gloved hands.

"Mr. Reed. Are you certain you don't remember meeting me before?"

The question tightened something in Adrian's throat.

"I don't."

"You sound very sure."

"Because making up a memory would only make things worse for both of us."

Matthew stared at him a moment longer.

Then his shoulders lowered.

"Tomorrow morning, I'll let Mr. Hale and an independent notary examine my private work notebook. Provided you keep my home address out of the newspapers."

"Of course," Elena said. "We won't release your notes without your consent either."

Hale wrote the location and time of the meeting on a card.

Matthew tucked it into his inner pocket. His hands were still shaking.

"I may be a little late. I need to collect the notebook from my locker at the archives."

"If anyone interferes, don't confront them alone," Adrian said. "Contact us first."

Matthew gave a tired smile.

"You sound like you've worked in this field longer than I have."

"I've had a very intensive introduction."

Before they left the library, Hale asked Matthew to clarify what he was agreeing to. Matthew signed a note confirming only that he would make the notebook available for examination. He did not certify its contents in advance.

Halfway through the signature, he stopped.

"I have a younger sister. She works at a clinic in the northern district. I don't want anyone dragging her into this."

Elena didn't ask for her name.

"We'll contact you through counsel. If you need protection, we'll help you apply for it through the independent judicial authorities."

Matthew's smile was humorless.

"Some of the people who want me quiet work for the Judicial Office."

"Then we'll use a court-appointed protection process," Hale replied. "We won't ask you to hand your sister over to the Rosenberg household."

Matthew looked from one to the other.

For the first time, he seemed to understand that they weren't recruiting him as an expendable witness.

He nodded and put the appointment card away.

Back in the corridor, a silver-haired nobleman recognized Elena and approached with two younger companions.

"Lady Rosenberg! Congratulations on rescuing your reputation, at least for the time being. Is this the servant from the newspapers?"

"He's my administrative aide now," Elena said.

"A new title doesn't change a man's family."

Adrian smiled politely.

"That's true, my lord. I can't do much about who my grandfather was. But I can make sure the documents we're discussing have been properly checked."

Someone nearby laughed.

The old nobleman had been preparing another remark, but the sound robbed him of his enthusiasm.

Elena's voice remained calm.

"The Archives Preservation Society exists because people believe the work of preserving records matters. If you think the people doing that work lack standing, perhaps you should raise it during the society's next meeting."

The man excused himself.

Adrian looked at her.

"You didn't have to offend a guest on my account."

"I didn't. He was insulting everyone in this room who earns a living with a pen. That's not a problem you created."

Adrian couldn't argue.

He slipped his notebook back into his pocket.

Later, an attendant approached as the society's musicians prepared the final waltz.

Under conventional etiquette, the attendant explained, a duke's daughter was expected to dance with a man of corresponding rank. An administrative aide might be more comfortable at the edge of the floor.

Elena thanked him.

"I'll choose my own partner."

The attendant hesitated, then withdrew.

Adrian had originally practiced dancing to avoid repeating the humiliation of the royal ball.

Now he understood that inviting Elena would mean accepting the attention that came with standing beside her.

He chose not to swallow the invitation.

A light waltz began.

Elena noticed him glance down at his shoes.

"Considering another demonstration?"

"Checking my footwear."

"It doesn't leak today."

"You remember that?"

"You were practicing in front of a mirror for half an hour. I heard the shoe brush hit the door."

Adrian flushed.

"Martha told you?"

"I was outside."

He took a breath.

"My lady. If you'd like to, may I have this dance?"

She stopped walking.

"Now?"

"Yes."

"What are we investigating?"

"Nothing." He met her eyes. "I just want to dance with you."

For once, he didn't try to disguise the invitation as work.

Elena studied him.

Then she placed her hand in his.

"All right."

This wasn't an excuse to approach the side entrance.

They weren't trying to draw the prince's attention.

He'd asked, and she had chosen to accept.

Adrian led her onto the dance floor.

The first step was with his left foot.

He managed it.

The second was a little uncertain, but he didn't catch the hem of her gown.

Elena watched him with amusement in her eyes.

"You've been practicing."

"Ten minutes a day."

"Who's teaching you?"

"My mirror."

"Is it expensive?"

"It occasionally charges a shoe brush."

She laughed.

They turned slowly beneath the warm lights. Adrian discovered that looking at Elena rather than anxiously counting every other pair of feet made the dance easier.

"You told me I couldn't dance."

"You've improved."

"Enough to pass?"

Her hand tightened gently around his.

"Ask me when the music ends."

He smiled and let the question wait.

When the final note faded, they stopped without mishap.

No torn hem.

No stumble.

No accidental kiss.

Elena didn't immediately release his hand.

"That was very good," she said quietly.

"Thank you."

"Thank your mirror."

"I'll bow to it when I get home."

She laughed and finally stepped away.

A touch of pink lingered at her ears.

Adrian was considering asking whether there might be a next dance when Hale appeared beside them.

"Matthew Holt has left early."

Adrian frowned.

"I thought he'd agreed to speak to us tomorrow."

"He received an urgent letter. The doorman says he looked unwell when he left."

Hale held out an unsigned note found in the club's receiving box.

It listed Matthew's home address and the exact time of tomorrow's appointment.

Beneath them was one sentence:

**Sign that statement, and your family pays first.**

Adrian's fingers tightened around the paper.

Someone knew Matthew had agreed to speak.

Someone had prepared to threaten him before he could even leave the building.

Elena glanced toward the open side door.

Matthew's coat was still in the cloakroom.

So was his hat.

"The doorman also saw an unregistered gray carriage pull away with two people inside," Hale said.

Adrian didn't run for the door.

He handed the note back to Hale and asked him to preserve the club's visitor and vehicle records.

Then he turned to Elena.

"This time," he said, "we find him together."
