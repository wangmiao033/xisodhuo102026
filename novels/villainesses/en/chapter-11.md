# Why Do All the Villainesses Want to Marry Me?
## Volume One: A Betrothal Written in Blood
### Chapter 11: An Extra Chair at Breakfast

On September twenty-first, Adrian Reed received an invitation to breakfast.

Under ordinary circumstances, that would have been wonderful news.

Yesterday he'd been wondering whether he'd be spending the night in a prison cell. Today, the judge had rejected the Crown's request for immediate detention, and Elena was alive on the morning after her scheduled execution.

He'd even slept properly.

The sunlight filtering through the Rosenberg corridors looked positively friendly.

Then Martha told him breakfast would be served in the family's private dining room.

"Sorry. You mean the servants' dining hall?"

"The private dining room."

"Have the cooks run short of staff?"

"Her Ladyship requested that Mr. Reed join her for breakfast."

Adrian glanced down at his threadbare shirt.

"Should I wear the suit from the ball?"

"Only if you'd like the entire household to start talking about the dance again."

"Right. No suit."

Martha handed him a charcoal-gray coat.

"New clothes. The accounts office has put the cost under your name."

"Under my name?"

"Her Ladyship said you're no longer simply an ordinary servant."

"Then what am I?"

"You'll find out."

Adrian had grown suspicious of good news at the Rosenberg estate.

The prince had offered Elena a chance to escape trouble. That chance turned out to be a possible treason conviction.

The Judicial Office had merely wanted Adrian to testify. Somehow he'd ended up receiving a summons himself.

A private breakfast sounded less like a reward and more like an interrogation without a lawyer.

Outside the dining room, two maids were polishing silverware.

They stopped when they saw him.

"Good morning, Mr. Reed."

"Morning."

One whispered, "That's him."

The other whispered back, "Is he really going to sit there?"

Adrian stopped.

"Sit where?"

Both women looked down.

"Nowhere in particular, sir."

The people of this kingdom were alarmingly fond of speaking in riddles around him.

The room was smaller than he'd expected. A long table stood by the windows, covered in white linen. Fresh roses filled a vase, and the smell of warm bread drifted from beneath a silver cover.

Elena sat near one end in a pale-blue morning dress, her golden hair loosely gathered behind her shoulder. Two documents lay in front of her.

No prince.

No armored guards.

Adrian found the sight strangely unfamiliar.

"Good morning, my lady."

"You're two minutes late."

"No one told me which corridor leads to the private dining room."

"Take the west stairs next time."

He blinked.

"Next time?"

"Are you planning to be late every morning?"

Martha nudged him gently forward.

"Please, have a seat."

That was when Adrian noticed an additional chair beside Elena.

Not the little stool servants used against the wall.

A proper dining chair with deep-red upholstery, matching the seats reserved for the family. Someone had even set it with cutlery and a folded napkin.

He hesitated.

"Isn't someone meant to sit here?"

"Not currently," Elena replied.

"Your father, perhaps?"

"At the other end."

"Prince Leon?"

Elena set down her teacup.

"Do you honestly think he's in the mood to visit?"

"I'm checking."

"Sit."

Adrian lowered himself into the chair as cautiously as possible.

Footsteps hurried away outside.

He could already imagine how fast the news would spread.

Yesterday, the duke's daughter accidentally kissed a servant at the royal ball.

Today, the same servant was sitting beside her at breakfast.

By tomorrow, the tabloids would probably be drawing them with three children.

"What are you smiling at?" Elena asked.

"Nothing."

"Your mouth is practically reaching your ears."

"I'm worried about the reporters."

"Then stop writing their stories for them."

She slid a plate toward him.

"Eat. The bread is good today."

Adrian had barely picked up a piece when someone cleared his throat in the doorway.

Duke Edmund Rosenberg stood there with his head steward and Mr. Hale.

The duke's eyes went first to Adrian.

Then to the extra chair.

The room fell quiet.

"Elena."

"Good morning, Father."

"Unless I'm mistaken, our family had one fewer member yesterday."

Adrian's bread stopped halfway to his mouth.

"Your Grace, I can explain."

"You seem to have quite a lot to explain these days."

Elena spoke before he could continue.

"I invited him to sit here."

"I gathered as much."

"He needs to review the case with Hale this morning. It would be awkward for him to stand while we work."

"At breakfast?"

"Didn't you always say breakfast was the best time to discuss family business?"

The duke was silent for a moment.

"I'm delighted you've remembered that lesson."

Hale busied himself with his papers.

His shoulders appeared suspiciously unsteady.

Adrian started to stand and offer the duke his seat.

Elena tapped the table.

"Sit down. You're not on trial."

The duke frowned.

"I know he's not on trial. A judge refused to detain him yesterday."

"Then you needn't look at him as though you're questioning a defendant."

Father and daughter stared at one another.

Adrian wanted to climb into the breadbasket and stay there.

Finally, the duke took his seat.

"Hale. The papers."

Breakfast became a working meeting.

Hale laid out the previous day's ruling. The Crown's emergency punishment request had been dismissed, but the treason investigation remained open. Evidence Item 714 required independent review, and Edwin Clay could no longer handle it without supervision.

"So," Adrian said, "we won yesterday but haven't won the case."

"Precisely."

"Could the Crown simply produce another letter?"

"We can't exclude that possibility. Any new evidence would still have to be verified."

Elena nodded.

"The estate's relevant records have been inventoried."

"We also need to know who handled them," Adrian said.

The duke looked over.

"Another prediction?"

"No. Someone was able to replace the evidence because they had access to its custody arrangements."

"Go on."

Adrian put down his bread.

"Henry's account has to be checked. And Clay might not be the only person involved. A clerk can alter a date, but that doesn't mean he could also change the palace guest list, arrange for a guard to recognize my name, and make the prince announce the broken engagement early."

The duke was silent.

Adrian realized, a moment too late, that he'd reduced the duke's daughter's broken engagement to an item of evidence over breakfast.

"My apologies. I didn't mean to be disrespectful."

Elena remained composed.

"You're not wrong. Continue."

The duke finally set down his knife.

"Elena tells me you'd like to remain in our service."

"Yes, Your Grace."

"Because of the investigation?"

"Partly."

"What else?"

Adrian glanced toward the window.

He could have told the duke that he needed to understand how he'd crossed into this world. That he wanted to know where the scar on his hand came from. That he hoped to avoid waking up one day and finding himself dragged to another execution platform.

None of those sounded like especially good breakfast conversation.

"I've met people here," he said instead. "When things got dangerous, they helped me. I don't want to leave them yet."

Elena's fingers stopped on her napkin.

The duke regarded him for a long moment.

"That's not an especially clever answer."

"I'm not sure I'm an especially clever man."

"At least you're honest about being afraid of dying."

"Extremely honest."

The duke snorted.

Then he accepted a document from the steward.

"As of today, you're appointed temporary administrative aide to House Rosenberg. You'll work with Hale to organize evidence, review outside archives, and assist Elena with paperwork relating to this case."

Adrian blinked.

"An administrative aide?"

"One-month probation. Paid employment, no noble status. You may not deploy guards, open sealed archives, or make commitments in the Rosenberg name without authorization."

"That sounds more complicated than being a servant."

"It is."

"And the salary?"

The duke looked at the steward.

"Three times a regular footman's wage."

Adrian's eyes lit up.

"I accept."

Elena turned toward him.

"What happened to all those touching reasons you gave a moment ago?"

"Having touching reasons doesn't mean I don't need a salary."

Hale finally laughed.

Even the duke let out an amused breath.

"Well, that makes you sound like a perfectly ordinary young man."

Adrian picked up his cutlery again.

He had somehow managed to hold the knife backward.

Elena turned it around for him.

"Wrong end."

"Thank you."

"You can challenge the Judicial Office in court but can't manage a breakfast knife."

"I'd appreciate it if you didn't compare those particular skills."

Several maids lowered their heads.

None of them were fooling anyone.

After breakfast, Hale took Adrian to the study.

The old index card containing the word *Substitute* was kept in a protective sleeve. What they'd brought home was a verified transcription; the original remained in the Judicial Office's archive.

Adrian examined the faded writing.

"How old is it?"

"Hard to say." Hale lifted a magnifying glass. "This index format hasn't been used for at least twenty years, although some old records may have retained it longer."

"And if there was simply another Adrian Reed?"

"We would need to compare birth records, family relationships, and the case file itself."

Adrian stared at his own name.

Yesterday, it had nearly stopped his heart.

Now it looked like a particularly terrible employment contract.

*Position: Substitute.*

*Duties: Unspecified.*

*Pay: Probably none.*

*Benefits: Potentially fatal.*

"Can we find the original file?"

"Only by applying to the old judicial archives. The more immediate problem is that this index number doesn't belong to the current judicial classification system."

"Meaning?"

"Decades ago, certain academies, judicial offices, and religious institutions shared archival numbering conventions. The complete cross-reference hasn't survived, so I can't yet tell which institution produced this."

"Academies?"

"Saint Rose Academy keeps some old transfer indexes. We'd need its authorization to examine them."

Adrian remembered the academy library from the game.

One of his least favorite locations.

Walk inside, get an unavoidable lecture on historical background, then discover the book you needed was on the third shelf behind a locked door.

He'd never thought he would want to go there voluntarily.

Elena entered the study.

"Then we should visit the academy."

Hale looked concerned.

"My lady, the Crown may still be watching you."

"Which is why we'll submit an application first."

Adrian turned to Elena.

"Do you know the archivist?"

"I studied there."

"Can't you just ask them to help us?"

"We'll follow the procedure."

"You're a duke's daughter."

"Which is even more reason not to pretend rules don't exist."

Adrian had to admit she had a point.

It was simply unfortunate that everyone in the household seemed determined to expose how little common sense he possessed.

Hale drafted a request for access to the historical judicial index and a copy of its blurred seal. He avoided including Adrian's private identity details.

By evening, a messenger returned with a stamped acknowledgment.

"Approved?" Adrian asked.

"Received," Hale corrected. "The application for access can be considered tomorrow."

Adrian turned the page over.

A line had been added by hand.

"What's this?"

Hale looked closer.

"A catalogue reference from the archivist."

The note read:

*Old-Style Transfer Ledger. Cabinet Three, Volume Seven. Subject: Substitute.*

Adrian felt his heartbeat pick up.

Yesterday, the term had appeared on a forgotten Judicial Office index.

Today, the academy's catalogue contained the same word.

Elena read the receipt.

"Cabinet Three. Volume Seven."

"Can we see it tomorrow?"

"If the academy grants access."

"And if it doesn't?"

"We apply again. You don't have to climb through a window every time a door is locked."

"I wasn't going to climb through a window!"

"Your eyes were already looking for one."

He laughed despite himself.

The ink on the receipt was fresh. The word *Substitute* looked as though it belonged to something very old indeed.

"Could we preserve this receipt too?" he asked Hale.

"Certainly."

Night gathered outside the windows. A servant lit another lamp in the study.

Elena moved a chair closer to Adrian's side of the desk.

"Don't be late for breakfast tomorrow."

"Am I sitting in the same chair?"

She looked away.

"You're an administrative aide now. We have work to discuss over breakfast."

"Oh."

"Don't tell me you've changed your mind."

"No. I'm happy to."

He grinned.

"I just hope His Grace doesn't ask whether we've acquired another family member."

"Then you can explain it to him."

"I'll come early."

Elena laughed softly.

Adrian looked back at the academy receipt.

Cabinet Three. Volume Seven.

This time, he didn't rummage through his memory of the game's quests for a shortcut. Instead, he asked Hale which documents would be needed for the archive visit and arranged them in order.

Elena had reached the door when she noticed he was still working.

She came back and lit another desk lamp for him.

"Don't take all night. I'm going with you tomorrow."

"Don't you have family business?"

"It can wait until afternoon."

"But this might not even be a real lead."

"Then we'll find that out."

She reached the door again and paused.

"One more thing, Adrian."

"Yes?"

"At the academy, don't go telling every girl who helps you that you're nothing but a servant."

"Why?"

She opened the door.

"Because you're not one anymore."

The door closed.

Adrian looked down at his appointment letter and smiled.

Triple pay.

A mystery in the old archives.

And a duke's daughter who expected to have breakfast with him again tomorrow.

This world was beginning to feel a little more reasonable than it had yesterday.
