# Chapter 273: Show the Ugly Page First

On the morning of May 2, Adrian placed his new explanatory drawing before Luna. She glanced at it and asked why the arrow was wider than the route.

“So people at the back can see.”

“They'll see a shortcut that doesn't exist.”

She indicated the elegant curve between two buffers. It meant that completion of one stage allowed a request for the next. On paper, residual energy seemed to leap across the waiting interval by itself. She didn't reach with her injured right hand. Her assistant moved the sheet closer while she dictated the changes.

Adrian crossed out the curve. The drawing resembled an excessively stitched wound.

“More honest,” she said.

“Shall I put my face beside it?”

“The risk statement has a length limit.”

Elena arrived with tea in time for that. Setting her cup away from the drawings, she asked whether they intended to prove artistic ability unnecessary for reform.

Adrian laughed, then laid his withdrawn diagram beside the new one. Its omitted withdrawal delay couldn't remain merely a lesson they'd discussed privately. If only the corrected drawing survived, nobody could establish what he'd previously asked people to believe.

“This too?” the copying clerk asked. “It isn't current guidance.”

“Mark when we withdrew it. Include the correction. It mustn't guide anyone again, but it mustn't disappear from what I did.”

Luna studied him before requesting the April 23 failure record. Original strips, damaged-component inspections, and stop-command logs had separate custodians. A speech wouldn't bring everything into the hall. They had to decide how others could actually examine it. The names of the records meant little if a questioner could never get beyond the person describing them. Each needed a real place to be found, rather than an assurance that somebody trustworthy had already looked.

Before noon, an independent verifier and an archivist arrived. They didn't accept a box labeled publish everything. Working interfaces, facility access routes, and prospective participants' private information were identified separately. Each omission needed a reason, a way to question it, and a record of who could inspect the complete material.

A clerk suggested removing participants' names from the failed trial to prevent harassment. Adrian pointed to his own.

“Is this private information too?”

“You were responsible for the proposal and its explanation,” the verifier said. “Protecting a list doesn't entitle you to anonymity.”

Adrian nodded. Elena exhaled quietly beside him, then asked whether noble families could classify their contributors as protected people too. Necessary responsibility information and irrelevant home addresses required separate consideration, the verifier said. A surname couldn't seal an entire page.

That applied to Rosenberg as well. Elena had asked without removing her own family from the answer. Adrian heard the breath she released and understood that the boundary offered neither of them a convenient exemption.

An academy teacher arrived carrying a much-folded notice. Since the maintenance cylinder had been borrowed, her students had moved desks between two classrooms. Would this presentation borrow the same handlers again?

“No equipment moves today,” Adrian said. “Later, we'll exhibit only preserved pieces checked for hazardous residual energy. Transport will have its own slot, without displacing your confirmed booking.”

“I'm not trying to stop you.” She flattened the notice. “But when you said borrowed, I heard back tomorrow morning.”

He found the loan guarantee. It specified possible consumption; the spoken message had omitted that qualification. She hadn't misunderstood her life. She'd received part of the truth later than they had.

Adrian added the incident to the account of actual costs, identifying who owed her an answer about replacement classrooms. She didn't want her students made into an applauding audience or asked to forgive on the spot. He asked neither.

Afterward, the phrase losses covered by guarantee looked very thin. Paying money wouldn't return a postponed class to its original morning.

“Don't replace that with we achieved nothing,” Elena said, passing his tea.

“I'm trying not to erase her.”

“Keep her question. You needn't supply her ending.”

At lunch, Martha removed two piles of paper attempting to occupy the dining table. Luna protested that the top one was reading matter. Martha turned it over, found nothing but scales, and wondered which author's adventure contained so few characters.

“The characters are waiting for lunch,” Luna said.

“Then let them eat.”

Viola's supported left arm rested beside a plate positioned for her right hand. Hearing about written questions, she asked what people unable to write would do. Cecilia proposed the existing oral-inquiry staff, then asked the clerk to verify they would actually be on duty. A new responsibility couldn't be assigned to an imaginary afternoon.

Isabella opened the venue confirmation with her right hand. Half the larger royal hall she'd considered was housing displaced residents. They would use an ordinary reading room at the independent adjudicating body, with limited capacity and staggered attendance. Her urgency didn't entitle her to remove existing occupants.

“The bad news is no carpet,” she said.

“The good news is I won't tread on a skirt?” Adrian asked.

Elena lowered her fork. “Why turn something that happened once into a risk requiring nationwide precautions?”

“Five witnesses are sitting beside me.”

“Four heard about it,” Cecilia corrected. “I hadn't arrived yet.”

Her solemn precision finally made Elena laugh. Adrian remembered their freely chosen half-circle in the side hall two days earlier. Laughter needn't wait for the crisis to end, or prove that it had.

That afternoon, three people uninvolved in the design tried reading the explanation. A craftsman understood the containers but asked whether independent stopping meant everyone must wait for Luna's command. Another reader mistook two empty loads for two enrolled people.

Hearing this, Luna briefly closed her eyes.

“Draw the loads. Objects, not little people.”

Adrian drew ordinary test pieces and specified that no people, marks, or old-core energy were connected. Independent stopping became a plain explanation: observers could call a stop upon detecting trouble; a separate chain executed it without awaiting the proposer's permission. Access to the underlying records remained. Plain language wouldn't replace complete evidence.

The third reader didn't criticize the drawing. Having withdrawn from future assessment, would attending count as enrolling again?

Cecilia showed her the question form. No participation intention, no medical information. Attendance and copies required no experimental involvement. Still uneasy, the woman asked about someone collecting signatures outside.

They changed the arrangements. Staff would state at the entrance that there was no recruitment. Any separate expression of interest couldn't be tied to seating, food, or access to questions. The woman still didn't agree to come. She took a draft to read slowly in her own room.

Returning from seeing her out, Adrian found the space intended for a bold conclusion filled with awkward questions instead.

At the agreed evening hour, Luna ended her review. Remaining technical answers would follow in writing the next morning. Exhaustion wouldn't buy a falsely complete signature. Adrian put down his pen too and walked with Elena to the open bakery.

Hearing their request for a table without archives, the owner indicated a window corner. Only yesterday's sugar remained there.

“Can that be verified?” Elena asked.

Adrian raised both hands. Laughing, she chose bread with darker edges. He asked whether she wanted him beside her family representative tomorrow.

“Sit where you want. But when I come out, I'd like to see you.”

“I'll wait. If you don't want to talk, we can walk first.”

She broke off some bread and eventually offered it. “A little talking is allowed. This place has better pastries than the hearing room, for instance.”

He accepted, promising to remember. Her fingers lingered beside his; he gently held them. Tomorrow's possible humiliation remained unresolved. Her hand was warm in his, but warmth couldn't settle the questions her family would face. They'd agreed on somewhere she could return to, and on the ordinary conversation that might be waiting there.

Before the preliminary inquiry on May 3, Adrian delivered the signed disclosure plan. The chair stated that he had participated in neither the old orders nor the parking decision. Either side could raise objections to the reviewers. Registration guaranteed nobody a favorable finding.

Oswin opened the page omitting interface details. Was the proposer once again controlling what others could see?

“The custodians and independent verifiers review reasons for omission,” Adrian said. “Complete records stay in controlled reading facilities. Qualified reviewers can apply to inspect them. The public can see where material was withheld and question why. I can't put failures behind that protection.”

Would the flawed diagram genuinely be published?

Adrian took it from its folder and laid it on top.

The chair didn't immediately authorize unrestricted copying. They established which pages could be made available, how originals could be checked, when oral questions could be asked, and where erroneous editions would be corrected. Later presentations would use existing records and inspected, unpowered preserved items. Attendance wouldn't substitute for approval of a new trial. Private pages stayed protected; dangerous interface instructions wouldn't circulate in the street.

As approved sheets went for binding, Adrian noticed two table columns had slipped out of alignment. A limited test-rig capacity appeared beneath maintenance-cylinder losses. Read that way, it suggested two academy test pieces had received that night's accumulation.

He stopped the messenger and showed him. Sweat appeared on the clerk's forehead. People were waiting outside. Could they issue the written conclusion now and add figures after lunch?

“Tell them why they're waiting,” Adrian said. “Don't give them half a table that leads them wrong.”

The chair suspended distribution of that page. Bound copies remained inside. The verifier reread against the custody references, restoring the test rig and maintenance installation to their separate columns. The delay caused no catastrophe, but some waiting visitors missed their lunch break. Staff explained afternoon collection and established another ordinary collection time for those leaving. Nobody was asked to feel grateful for the correction.

Standing nearby, Adrian realized waiting could be public too. They could show what wasn't ready rather than send a handsome page outside to defend appearances first.

By noon, the first approved copies reached the reading table. Some people selected the stable-result chart; others opened the losses or went directly to Adrian's earlier words. The teacher hadn't come. Her written question was included without being exchanged for praise.

His fingers ached as he signed for the last copy. There was no promise that everyone would understand. Someone could read every available page and still oppose the decision; someone else might start with the drawing he most wished he had never made. Neither possibility was grounds to withdraw it now. At least those opposing him would no longer need to believe his explanation before finding the ugly page.

The clock in the next room struck. A custodian wheeled in the sealed material for the afternoon. Approved noble-contract documents occupied the top shelf.

Seeing her family crest, Elena touched her cuff. Then she rose and approached a table that reserved no dignity for Rosenberg.
