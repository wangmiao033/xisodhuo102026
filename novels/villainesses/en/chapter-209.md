# Chapter 209: They Went Down Together

There was no stained glass in the Temple's eastern maintenance room.

On March 8, morning light entered through a narrow window and fell across six borrowed protective caps. Adrian chose one. Its straps were more complicated than his neckcloth. After two attempts, the brim had managed to tilt in two different directions.

“Here is something you really shouldn't face alone,” Elena said.

He offered her the cap, then hesitated. “Just the cap?”

“I could criticize your scarf too, if you like. Let's save something for later.”

She adjusted the straps and let him fasten them. Cecilia, her braid already neatly tucked away, asked whether she might inspect the back fastening. Elena said the buckle wasn't Rosenberg property.

“I was asking him,” Cecilia reminded her.

Adrian agreed. The rear buckle was twisted. Once straightened, it stopped pulling his hair. Nobody had won anything, though Luna regretted that a cap could settle arguments more effectively than many betrothals.

“It's easier to remove, too,” Isabella observed, adjusting her brim with her right hand.

The physician had reviewed the three injured women's arrangements. Luna would join only the short initial inspection, her right palm supported and her left hand carrying nothing. Viola would sit at the verified broad landing below, her left arm in its support. Isabella wouldn't brace herself against walls or carry papers with her left wrist. Each had chosen her own permitted distance and could return upstairs to rest.

The custodian unfolded yesterday's viewing order. Seal numbers were checked against the duty register before witnesses observed the outer door being opened. Engineers had already inspected ventilation, lighting, and walking surfaces, marking both existing exits. Today's lamps were ordinary portable lights, drawing nothing from historical equipment.

Beyond the door, broad shallow steps descended through successive landings. More ordinary than Adrian had imagined. Deeper than he'd hoped.

An engineer went first, followed by the custodian. The others kept their spacing, with medical staff and assistants accompanying them and an attendant remaining above. Adrian walked slightly ahead of Elena, listening to her soles brush the stone. Every turn still made him glance back.

She didn't ask whether he feared her falling or himself being left below.

A cleaned old armchair waited at the third landing. Luna sighed as she sat. At least this antique was receiving more useful attention than the treasures they couldn't touch. Her assistant erected a drawing board. None of the old originals had accompanied them. Neither had the new projection film. Today they were reading rooms, not reading Adrian again.

Repairs interrupted the outer gallery walls. White plaster met older dark stone, with damp following the join like an incompletely erased line. No mysterious singing greeted them. Wind passing through a ventilation pipe made a low, uneven sound.

Adrian stopped in it.

Ahead, the engineer reached for a small brass-colored handle. Adrian's palm went cold. No historical image appeared; no new voice told him what to do. He recognized a lamp-stand fastener. His feet still refused to move.

“Wait, please.”

The engineer withdrew his hand. Everyone stopped within the inspected landing. Elena didn't seize Adrian or explain to the others that he was merely nervous. The physician asked what he needed.

“Let me see what it's connected to.”

The engineer turned the stand sideways. The handle tightened an exposed support rod. Nothing entered the wall or joined any old conduit. After explaining, he waited rather than turning it abruptly to demonstrate its harmlessness.

Adrian slowed his breathing. Then he said they could continue while he sat by the wall.

His pause had cost the group time. Luna's assistant crossed out one interval on the schedule. An apology almost escaped him, but Luna remarked that she could now devote proper attention to this ugly chair's armrest.

She didn't pretend there had been no effect.

Cecilia brought water. Adrian said he knew it wasn't the earlier handle.

“Knowing and not being afraid aren't always the same.”

“Do you think bringing me makes things harder?”

“It can. It just did.” She sat on the stool beside him. “I still want you here. Both are true.”

He drank and finally noticed the water wasn't cold. She didn't turn her wish into a demand for an equal promise. She had another reason to be below: determining whether the storage directory had wrongly classified an old medical route as reserved for ceremonies. When he no longer needed comforting, her work remained.

Two tables stood beside the broad landing. Viola sat at one while her assistant spread the approved route diagram. The stairs broadly matched, but the innermost section was half a room shorter than drawn. The engineer initially suspected an inaccurate old scale. Isabella pointed out that the original northern baseline couldn't be measured from today's rebuilt wall.

“Like using an old royal year as a current date,” Adrian said.

“Excellent. One student remembers.”

“I thought this was an adventure.”

“Adventures aren't exempt from measurement.”

The engineer started again at an original foundation stone. Luna warned against following the damp seam. It marked later repairs, not the foundation's boundary. A second measurement narrowed the discrepancy without closing it. Nobody announced a secret palace behind the wall. The measuring uncertainty went on the drawing too.

Then an attendant brought down a directory attachment retrieved that morning and delivered it to Elena, who was authorized to examine it. At the family emblem on its first page, she called Adrian back.

“This has an old Rosenberg donation mark.”

“What?” His voice came out sharper than he'd intended.

She placed the paper on their shared table, covering only a private address not authorized for copying.

“I've just received it. You can read this portion. I haven't investigated it yet.”

House Rosenberg had funded repairs to this maintenance gallery. The attachment described replacing a small arch with a flat lintel, adding an exterior storage recess, and establishing a new measuring point afterward. A donation couldn't establish what the family knew of the core. It couldn't clear every other payment either. It could explain their stubborn discrepancy.

Adrian watched her open hand.

“You didn't look for a more flattering explanation first.”

“No. I'll investigate who approved the money and where it went. You can ask before I do.”

He wanted to say he wasn't suspicious every time she received a paper. But he'd been suspicious just now. Only seeing the attachment had eased it. A pleasant denial would require her to pretend she hadn't understood his tone.

“I thought you'd decided to wait before telling me again.”

Elena nodded without making his suspicion an injustice against her effort.

“You have it now.”

Not a demand to be satisfied. He heard the difference and continued reading. She had placed what she'd actually received before him. He didn't have to guess whether another sheet remained in a drawer.

The old measuring point lay behind the storage recess's doorpost.

The custodian thought the recess contained only empty wooden frames left by recent cleaning. The engineer explained that these wedged the door open. They had to be removed from outside before anyone could inspect the wall's foot. Together the men moved the frames, which weren't sealed historical objects, revealing an overturned tin pail.

Faded blue paper clung to its bottom. Adrian crouched where he was allowed, expecting another reference number. After considerable scrutiny, he read: NO SALTED FISH.

Cecilia held out for a moment before asking why the Temple underground needed that particular prohibition.

Workers had kept their lunches there, the custodian explained. Cats had followed the smell into the ventilation duct. Isabella inspected the dark opening more solemnly than she'd studied the engineering plan. Luna immediately declared that an expired prohibition would not diminish her expectations of underground discoveries.

Adrian laughed aloud at last. Then he noticed a shallow puddle beneath the pail and returned to dry ground while the equipped workers dealt with it. Seepage had followed the wall to the recess. No ancient power had summoned a gray haze.

Once the water was cleared, the engineer found paired nail holes in the old doorpost. Their spacing matched the measuring plaque drawn on the attachment. The plaque was gone. The holes and an adjacent unchanged stone footing remained. The custodian drew those separately rather than restoring a plaque that now existed only on paper.

Following the old reference point, the engineer found a positioning cut half-covered by plaster. Oblique lamplight revealed it without cutting the wall. Comparison with the unaltered footing at the opposite end finally placed the old diagram's inner boundary correctly.

The storage reference for the central comparison baseline table didn't point to their worktable. It didn't point to the western relay court either. It led to a narrower turning passage at the end of this gallery, labeled CENTRAL ANTECHAMBER.

They couldn't see the antechamber yet. Beyond a removable wooden partition lay flooring that hadn't passed structural inspection. Their permit allowed viewing from outside. Giving the destination a significant name didn't authorize walking there.

At the boundary, Adrian could glimpse a sliver of pale wall through the partition. It looked close enough to make an absurdly reasonable suggestion: he was thin; two steps, one look, then back.

Viola was checking that section's exits. Unable to stand beside him, she spoke from her table.

“There's no second confirmed usable exit beyond it yet.”

The engineer could survey beneath the floor from the safe side and add support before considering passage. He couldn't promise completion by afternoon.

Luna's initial time allowance had ended too. She stared at the gap with undisguised disappointment, then asked her assistant to pack the board.

Adrian didn't want everyone to retreat in the same elegant manner. Did she badly want to stay longer?

“Very badly. So this would be an excellent time not to ask me a technical question.”

He swallowed the question already forming and smiled. “Tomorrow, then.”

“Ask whether I want to tomorrow.”

She returned upstairs with medical accompaniment. Viola had her assistant continue the comparison but left at the time she'd agreed. Two empty chairs weren't entered as a weakening alliance.

The others completed their permitted copying without crossing the boundary. Cecilia identified a medical-route classification requiring further review and submitted a separate request. The core's name didn't consume every minute of her work. Isabella took an authorized copy of the measuring instructions. The custodian resealed the original attachment.

At the exit Adrian asked Elena to wait. His cap strap tightened each time he pulled. Eventually he admitted he needed help.

“May I criticize the scarf now?”

“Once I've removed this strangling evidence of my competence.”

She laughed and released the loose buckle without pulling his hair. He accepted the cap, thanked her, and indicated the bench outside. Would she sit with him until the carriage arrived?

She would.

A little space remained between their shoulders. He hadn't stormed the underground alone. Neither had an old name been washed clean. They had actually gone down together, each retaining a way to leave.

On the corrected plan, the central antechamber no longer floated somewhere in an uncertain direction. Tomorrow the engineers would first establish whether the floor could carry them there.
