# Chapter 259: Coordinates Cannot Choose for You

At breakfast on April 21, Adrian put his left hand on the table.

Elena moved the hot pot away without holding him down. He'd slept in stretches, waking once. The hall's smell had faded, but the door in his dream still closed slowly. He would tell the physician, not replace that account with “I slept well.”

“I want to know what's on that plate.”

“So do I.”

“Do you think I want it too much?”

She set down her bread. “Yes. But being afraid you'll be hurt doesn't let me decide you don't want it.”

If conditions weren't met, she would object. If he stopped, she would help him stop. Whether to undertake this limited examination belonged to him after hearing the risks.

“You haven't a single comforting thing to say.”

“The bread's still warm.”

He smiled and finished eating before leaving.

The evidence store produced no luminous treasure chest. The ordinary box had old wormholes; its replacement lining was dated in the transfer record. A thin plate rested between boards. One corner was missing, but the central pattern survived. A calibration sheet recorded reference holes and reading orientation, distinct from the insert's possible later repositioning.

The examiner checked the box number, deposit history, and present condition. Evelyn couldn't certify provenance simply by recognizing it. She was elsewhere answering supplementary questions, not sitting where her expressions might suggest what Adrian ought to remember.

Luna had reviewed the permitted copies. Her right palm rested on padding, her left hand held no pen. This was a short consultation, not another expenditure of magic. Recent color-vision disturbance still mattered. Feeling better didn't abolish the restrictions.

“You're looking at me like a leaking bucket.”

“I wondered if you were about to say you only needed a little longer.”

“Today doesn't depend on me. An academy storage unit supplies limited energy. The duty technician operates it.”

He apologized for assuming. She didn't promise never to err again. Her assistant marked what lay beyond her review. She could offer an opinion, not certify checks nobody had yet performed.

The old plate wouldn't touch Adrian. Its custodian had authorized one isolated comparison with a newly made trace sheet, using the previously limited-contact reading method. The original rested behind a transparent barrier. No old energy chest was in the room. An ordinary sample tested alignment and stopping response; another technician checked where residual energy would go. February's supply ceiling wouldn't rise because they urgently wanted coordinates.

The physician examined yesterday's reaction and heard about his sleep. Permission covered one short contact. Confusion, nausea, or a stop gesture meant ending immediately. Permission wasn't an order. Adrian placed the release marker where his right hand could drop it, practiced, and saw the technician disconnect before choosing to proceed.

Beatrice sealed the calibration details. His account would come before comparison, not after an answer had been planted in his memory. He knew they were examining an old receiving layer, not its complete division code.

Elena stood where he'd requested, clear of the medical attendant. What if he said something ugly?

“We stop first. Then decide whether an apology's needed. This isn't a farewell ceremony.”

Luna laughed. At the end of her allotted consultation, she left the operating room and her assistant took the observer's place. Not witnessing everything wasn't proof that nobody trusted her.

At first, Adrian saw his hand.

Today's hand. No copper lever. Fine lines marked the plate behind the barrier; his scar didn't unfold into a floating map. Then came the familiar pulling sensation. A corresponding trace darkened slightly. The independent gauge moved slowly, still within the stated range.

Damp plaster rose into memory.

A past hand pressed against the side of a platform, at a different moment from the lever operation. The skin bled, but blood powered no miraculous device. He was holding down a narrow locator sheet, as though afraid of forgetting what he needed to leave behind.

Someone said the main volume would be removed.

He couldn't identify the voice. He didn't give it Evelyn's face. His former self indicated three positions on the sheet, then turned the final square toward an asymmetric hole.

“Not the door,” his own voice said. “Find the acknowledgment. Don't trust what I remember again.”

His breathing quickened. He could still hear today's technician ask whether he wished to continue. A fragment remained. He reported the words: old western division, receiving column seven, third tier, rear hole facing the short edge.

The final item wasn't a number. A short stroke crossed a small square, like a corrected direction. The scar stabbed; gray gathered at the edge of his vision.

“Stop.”

His right hand released the marker. Power disconnected. He neither reached after the last line nor requested another heartbeat. Plaster retreated into memory. Cup, barrier, and chair regained their separate places.

Beatrice asked where he was. He named the examination room attached to the evidence store, then requested a moment before any read-back.

Everyone waited.

His fingertips remained cold after drinking. No fresh break marked his skin, but that didn't mean no cost. Contact was finished for the day. He would stay for observation, however important the documents became.

Only after sealing his first account did they open the calibration sheet. Its asymmetric reference matched the original. Western division, column, and tier formed a storage-location system, not city streets or a command to unlock the Crown of Destiny. A short trace on the new sheet corresponded to his drawn square, allowing the previously missing orientation to be resolved.

An examiner objected: Adrian already knew the hall's numbering. Could yesterday's information have been rearranged into a story?

Silence caught in the room. Adrian instinctively wanted to protect the distress he'd endured, as though pain made an answer reliable. Beatrice laid out yesterday's record. Western division wasn't new. Neither was the receiving-layer concept. The previously undisclosed column, tier, and rear-facing mark had independent comparative value.

“Write it that way,” Adrian said.

The examiner looked up at him and asked whether he needed to rest before they continued. He did. But needing a rest was not the same as needing them to rewrite the conclusion until nobody questioned it. Pain couldn't guarantee those numbers. If someone later found a different direction, he didn't want disagreement treated as denial of what he'd suffered.

The technician checked whether the alignment frame could have made the trace. Unexposed sheets from the same batch lacked it; barrier fixtures were elsewhere. That supported preserving the mark, not declaring every remembered sentence part of one moment.

Account, trace, and calibration remained separate sources, not three incomplete pieces painted into a perfect map. His disappointment didn't immediately vanish. He nevertheless agreed to preserve them unchanged. A real address should withstand another person's search after he left.

They still had to consult the catalog. Agreement among words and marks would not conjure the object into existence or establish that it had survived its transfers.

Column seven, third tier identified a rear-facing box containing an old acknowledgment record. A warehouse move had assigned it a new number. The transfer table mapped old to new. Nobody needed to dismantle a third layer of stone in the old building.

Adrian laughed once, then felt his eyes sting.

“I thought we'd have to go underground again.”

“Today we begin with the next storeroom,” the examiner said.

Complete coordinates finally rested on a checkable page. They located a historical anchor record, not every missing person. They couldn't decide the next step for anyone.

While the item was requested, Elena sat with him. Was she relieved? Partly. His hand was still cold, and she still hated that recovering stolen things required this.

“But I didn't hide the paper from you.”

“I know.”

He offered his right hand, asking first. She held it gently, leaving the monitored left untouched. He wanted to go somewhere with her that had no archives. Then not a library, she said: he'd inevitably ask about the cellar.

Their brief laughter did not conceal the fear preceding it. He could still feel the chill in his other hand. Neither had to pretend that laughing together meant the examination had cost nothing, or that wanting another ordinary outing had become too small to mention.

Isabella arrived after midday for a permitted result extract. First she handed him a relocation acknowledgment. Yesterday's households had arrived. The elderly man insisted on recording his birdcage's condition because the driver had taken his concern seriously. The other household disliked being so far from their shop. Their dissatisfaction couldn't be rewritten as gratitude.

“Did the bird sign?” Adrian asked.

“It pecked the paper. The clerk declined to accept that as consent.”

A woman had asked Isabella to agree to a move on her husband's behalf. She wanted to leave; he remained at their shop. Isabella reserved a place the woman could use independently, without entering the husband as relocated.

“Was she pleased?”

“Angry. She said I obviously knew how to compel someone.”

Isabella looked down at her supported wrist. Once she might have thought a beneficial result justified making people obey first. Now a real room was ready, but that hadn't neatly resolved their disagreement.

Adrian wanted to reassure her. It would have been easy to promise that the couple would eventually understand, as though their anger were simply another delay before the proper ending. He nearly said it, then stopped. Instead he asked whether she would return to hear what they actually lacked.

“Yes. Not to answer for them again.”

She lifted the extract and seriously asked if the coordinates required her to demand the entire old archive. He shook his head. First, the specifically identified acknowledgment should be retrieved within its permitted scope. She looked both disappointed and satisfied, and told him not to record that expression.

Before dusk, the box arrived. The custodian checked its seal edges and old receipt in front of them. Inside lay two parallel recording strips. One registered requests for receiving capacity. The other marked receiving actually completed.

Between them stretched a gap longer than Adrian had expected.

The examiner didn't improvise the strips' entire story. Their timing marks needed comparison with the old relay yard records. Adrian wouldn't fill the gap with memory today. Relevant copies had already been obtained in earlier verification, so tomorrow's comparison needn't begin with rumor.

He looked at the empty stretch and remembered the needle marked unconnected beside the copper lever. He'd known something wasn't ready and moved his hand anyway. Today's material couldn't render that hand innocent. Perhaps it could finally show why so many people had been driven toward a door for which nothing was ready.

He let the custodian secure the original. Rising took him longer than usual. Elena waited until he was steady before they left together.

The complete coordinates stayed on paper. They offered no shortcut around the past—only an address at which they could finally knock on the right door.
