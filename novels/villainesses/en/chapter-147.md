# Chapter 147: A Borrowed Key Does Not Open a Throne

Cecilia's breakfast really was only breakfast.

On the morning of January twentieth, she offered Adrian a perfectly cooked egg. She had asked the kitchen to spare him another shared defeat with the prince.

“Who told you?”

“You did. Yesterday.”

He remembered. Leaving the Temple, he had mentioned Leon's mangled egg, forgetting someone might remember a small thing unworthy of any archive.

There were no document bags on the table. Cecilia asked how he'd slept, then described a girl hanging three paper lanterns together and insisting they were a dragon. She hadn't corrected them into a sacred emblem, or asked Adrian whether the incident proved the Temple still deserved trust.

Near the end of their half hour, he folded his napkin. She looked a little disappointed but didn't claim affairs always mattered more than she did.

“Could next time be longer?”

“We can arrange more time beforehand.”

She wrapped a remaining piece of bread and jam. This wasn't breakfast extended, she said, only insurance against his forgetting lunch.

He accepted it seriously. Her fingers lingered at the packet's edge, but she didn't use the exchange to capture his hand.

“I'll ask whether you ate it.”

“Then I'd better not give it to Leon.”

Laughing, she saw him to the door.

The actual order arrived later that morning.

The royal document office read a time-limited commission approved by the king. Isabella could coordinate retrieval, preservation, and submission of specified old-project records concerning the identified personnel liaison, transport, and maintenance references. It lasted until sunset on January twenty-sixth. Any extension would depend on the reports delivered.

It did not restore her exercise of succession eligibility.

She could not search private residences, command troops, detain witnesses, or strip privacy from unwilling families. Originals under another body's custody still required that body's proper procedures. Expenses went through the existing clerical budget, not whatever balance she chose to draw upon.

At the end Isabella asked whether her own old orders belonged in the report.

The official said every directly relevant action must be explained.

“I accept.”

She signed for responsibility, not a recovered crown.

Leon stood by the window without announcing a victory won for his sister. Yesterday's questions, specific unanswered matters, and preservation needs together justified the commission. Nobody's affection could simply restore suspended power.

Her new credential was smaller than the old one, edged in silver rather than gold. She held it briefly, then put it in an ordinary document folder.

“A borrowed key.”

“Some doors, not others,” Adrian said.

“And it must go back afterward.”

She looked up without humiliation, only the tired relief of finally knowing what she could do that afternoon.

First came the external liaison advances archives.

Its staff had agreed to produce the returned-document index for examination by two authorized offices. Martin Veyr's memory gave direction, not a substitute for the index. Isabella would coordinate documents, not march him there to confess more.

Before leaving they passed Luna's rest room. She pushed a page toward them with her left hand. Her right palm remained cleanly bandaged. Even her cup stood where she needn't reach across herself.

“You got borrowing permission?”

“Limited coordination of retrieval.”

“Congratulations. The sort of limited most easily praised into unlimited.”

Luna wrote more slowly than usual but clearly. Binding layers must not be counted as original registry leaves. If they obtained an impression or old registration insert, photograph it, measure it, record its surroundings. Don't use injured people, or living bodies at all, for verification.

“Who is that last sentence for?” Isabella asked.

“Everyone who thinks they can simply endure a little.”

Adrian glanced at her hand.

“Including me. Don't look so pleased to have caught me.”

Smiling, he pushed her pencil back from the edge toward her left side. She began to reach with her right, stopped halfway, and used her left instead.

Isabella didn't praise her obedience. She simply moved the water closer.

“When can I read your report?”

“The one on five positional forms on paper is ready. The one on whether you're the fifth won't acquire a conclusion because you ask prettily.”

“I meant the one you've written.”

“So did I.”

They looked at each other until Luna smiled first. Adrian wisely declined a contest over who sounded more like official correspondence.

They did not take her to the archives.

The weather had warmed. Water dripped from the old building's eaves. Two buckets stood in the corridor; the archivist said repairs had been requested and the assigned tables were unaffected.

Isabella didn't step across the cordon because she possessed a credential. She waited while permission was checked, then joined Adrian at the designated table.

They found the index.

A returned entry read: Contact status unclear; returned for completion of closure wording. Its date and project reference broadly matched Martin's account. A later resubmission number required the next volume. Not every receipt had been destroyed, and one entry didn't prove all eight people followed the same process.

Isabella studied it.

“Between unclear and completed, there should at least be new evidence.”

The archivist nodded and had the needed volume reference recorded.

Then came a small crash outside.

Not a blade or a spell. A water-softened ceiling panel fell beside a bucket, tipping half its contents. Water ran along floor joints toward boxes awaiting transfer against the wall.

Adrian rose, almost reaching for the nearest case. The archivist stopped him: don't drag it; there were two distinct transfer bundles beneath it.

He withdrew his hand and moved an empty chair instead, blocking people still entering. Isabella sent a guard for the building's maintenance staff. She didn't turn a leak into an attack on the archives.

The archivist and two colleagues moved boxes by number to dry, elevated positions. Adrian passed clean support boards as directed, touching only the outer boards he was permitted to handle. Isabella recorded the moving sequence at the door, keeping lids and inventories from becoming separated in the hurry.

One thin case had a wet bottom corner. The examiner did not tear open its seal to save time. He marked the damp area and sent it for controlled treatment. One item was therefore unavailable today.

“Is that the resubmitted document?” Isabella asked.

“The index suggests relevant annual material may be inside. We can't confirm before opening.”

Her hand paused over the credential's pocket, then dropped.

A key couldn't dry wet paper.

Half an hour later, the water had been diverted temporarily and the wall-side area taken out of use. Maintenance explained the ceiling problem. Custodians recorded affected boxes. Nobody called it destruction of evidence merely because it happened on an important day.

A young clerk stood pale beside the door. He had been watching that row of boxes but moved his chair first when the water came. He feared being accused of protecting his belongings.

Had he touched any seals? No, he said quickly. The day's receiving lists had been under the chair; he moved it to retrieve them.

“Then write that,” Isabella said.

“Won't it sound like an excuse?”

“I don't know how people will read it. Don't remove what happened to make it look better.”

Adrian found a dry cloth for the clerk's hands. The young man's fingertips still shook, making his first line crooked. He tried to discard the sheet. The archivist stopped him: retain the original record and add a clear correction.

Isabella didn't write him a polished report that concealed all panic. When her turn came, she explained that she hadn't initially known which box was wet or what should be moved. This wasn't assigning everyone the same fault. It prevented her report from always making her the first person to understand everything.

When the clerk finished, an older worker replacing the bucket joked that fortunately nobody had used its contents to wash the whole room. It was a rough joke, but someone laughed. Anxiety didn't vanish. At least people stopped scrambling quite so hard to appear flawless.

The preservation risk remained real. Using her new remit, Isabella requested priority information on treatment of affected material and coordinated an already-approved dry examination table. She wasn't expanding a search, only preventing a bucket from ending work already authorized.

Waiting in the courtyard, she brushed dust off her sleeve.

“I really wanted to say open it immediately.”

“I nearly carried off the closest box.”

“So our most useful actions were stopping.”

“Not entirely. I did move the chair.”

She looked at him and laughed, tension gradually leaving her shoulders.

What could be delivered that afternoon was a certified copy of the undamaged index and the custodian's incident statement. The resubmission needed further treatment. The payment ledger was still incomplete. Isabella kept these separate in her first report, not turning a found reference into a recovered full text.

When Adrian remembered his bread, she asked whether it came from Cecilia.

“Yes. She told me to eat it myself.”

“Then you'd better obey.”

She took a biscuit from her own box rather than demanding half of something carrying a clear personal intention. Adrian poured her water from the shared pot. She accepted, apparently a little pleased by that distinction too.

At dusk another custodian sent tomorrow's viewing arrangements. A specified old registration insert would be displayed with its backing board for comparison of project mark positions and document references. The original could not leave custody. Nobody was required to touch it or cast magic; custodians controlled the viewing.

Could Luna work from images, Isabella asked?

“We can obtain permitted records first, then ask whether they're sufficient.”

“Then she won't have to overdo it.”

“Nor will anyone else.”

Isabella knew whom he meant and didn't argue.

Back at the house she secured the credential before writing to the families' office: they had found the return index; treatment continued; no search would be suspended because of damp or clerical delay. She didn't ask anyone to thank her for recovering work.

Elena collected a copy. The two women exchanged tomorrow's arrangements at the door. There was no handshake announcing a stronger alliance, no dispute about which side Adrian occupied. Isabella bore her own commission; the duke's daughter remained free to question her responsibility.

Before parting, Isabella set the small credential beside her work list.

“It opened an archive door today.”

“Not all of them.”

“Tomorrow, then.”

Adrian secured a slipping sheet inside her folder. She noticed a trace of today's dust on the edge and didn't ask for a spotless new cover. It wasn't a medal. It could remind her that this work dirtied cuffs and brought difficulties rank could not instantly remove. She placed the report on top, ready for whoever first asked about progress tomorrow.

She said it calmly. There was no throne on the table. Only a key whose holder needed to know when to stop and when to return it.
