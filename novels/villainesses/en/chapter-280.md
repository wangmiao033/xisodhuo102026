# Chapter 280: Who Takes Tomorrow's Shift?

Early on May 8, Elena spotted an oil stain on Adrian's cuff.

Instead of asking whom he'd rescued, she pinched the cloth and asked whether he'd been wiping tables with his sleeve again. He'd moved a chair for a maintenance worker last night; its back had been oily. She examined his papers. On top lay an unfinished shift handover, not a speech.

“You didn't take over until dawn?”

“No. I can't read that gauge. His supervisor called in a qualified replacement.”

“Good.”

She released his cuff, then frowned. “This fabric is difficult to wash.”

He'd prepared something about enduring responsibility. Suddenly, it didn't need saying. She handed him a clean coat and told him to eat first, then accompany her to the academy classroom that remained closed.

The borrowed maintenance cylinder had undergone its first inspection. The storekeeper passed the results to the responsible staff. Remaining capacity didn't meet its original rotation requirements. A cool casing couldn't make it usable again. Losses were still being accounted for through the previously guaranteed channels; ordinary replacement parts also awaited delivery. The classroom couldn't reopen today.

Outside, a teacher pinned a timetable to a temporary wooden stand. Did they still have to walk to the other building? a student asked. Yes. Bring the books, and avoid the loose brick on the side steps. Nobody asked whether the students supported suspension. The detour already lay beneath everyone's shoes.

The storekeeper showed Elena the delivery notice. Two gaskets had arrived under the same name but with different outer dimensions. A qualified maintenance worker measured them before installation, so they remained boxed. The supplier admitted sending the wrong stock. Replacements couldn't arrive before the next regular delivery.

“Would paying more make it faster?” Elena asked.

“The cart, yes. Not the parts. The next batch hasn't been inspected.”

She slowly put her purse away. Asking that first was a habit, not always arrogance. Often money genuinely did speed things up. That made accepting today's exception harder.

Adrian accompanied her to tell scheduling staff about the actual delay, without telling students the duke's family was fixing everything. The teacher showed them the spare room: narrower, smaller blackboard, but lessons could proceed. A tall student volunteered for the back row. His companion pointed out that seeing was harder from there. After arguing, they fetched stools of different heights.

Watching, Elena asked whether another colleague could handle delivery confirmation while the storekeeper rested. He named someone available that morning and actually went to find them.

One considerate question hadn't reduced his workload. At least they finally understood that waiting required someone on duty too.

Before noon, Adrian returned to the hearing hall. The oily sleeve was gone; the blanks on his paper remained. He changed yesterday's heading. Not how a new system would save people, but what an alternative needed if people were to keep doing the work.

“There is no kingdom-wide replacement system yet,” he began. “Today concerns the conditions it must face. Existing main-bridge staffing, maintenance, and evacuation arrangements continue. They aren't waiting for us to finish speaking.”

Oswin leaned back and waited.

Adrian didn't take over the engineers' work. Actual responsible staff explained consumable lifetimes, inspection, and replacement. Those handling expenditure distinguished confirmed funding from uncommitted shortfalls. When their dates differed for the same supplies, they compared receipts before everyone.

One date meant dispatch; the other included travel and estimated arrival. Fluent speeches wouldn't shorten those intervening days.

A young assistant raised his hand. Could he be listed for independent duty next month? He'd shadowed his master for a long time and wanted him to sleep through one night. His master neither mocked him nor accepted merely to ease the shortage.

“You can read normal gauges. Yesterday, when two readings disagreed, you trusted the lower one first. More practice.”

Red-faced, the youth offered to study on rest days. Viola watched without letting enthusiasm become a name in the duty book. The trainer agreed to arrange feasible practice periods. Independent duty still required an actual assessment.

How many shifts did the current plan lack, then? Adrian asked. Two more than that morning's draft had shown: it had optimistically counted the assistant already.

He crossed out the entry publicly, leaving an ugly blank. The youth lowered his head as though he'd broken something.

“The gap already existed,” Adrian told him. “You haven't enlarged it today. You've helped us see it before a post is actually left unattended.”

He couldn't guarantee successful training. He asked that the revision's reason be recorded, rather than portraying the youth as withdrawing at the last moment.

“You want people to accept a method that will never stop needing repairs?” Oswin asked.

“I can't sell them a promise of never needing care again.”

Adrian looked toward yesterday's man with the roof tile. Today he'd brought only his written question.

“If distributed sharing is eventually adopted, actual burdens still need review, residual receiving capacity must remain after withdrawals, and replacements need training. Equipment ages. Money runs out. People change their minds. When any of those requirements is missing, we cannot put somebody's body in the empty field and call it complete.”

Then how was their method different from the old installation? the man asked.

Adrian indicated maintenance records both sides used.

“Needing maintenance isn't our unique flaw. The difference isn't who claims greater convenience. The old arrangement counted people who couldn't refuse as reusable conditions. If we refuse to count them that way, we must acknowledge the other work and costs instead of hiding them.”

“Where does the money come from?”

“Existing guarantees cover only their stated commitments. Long-term funding still requires discussion. We can't proclaim universal willingness today and charge people who never agreed tomorrow. Nor can relief grain become experiment funding. I'll support public discussion, but I can't sign amounts for every district.”

That wasn't a comfortable answer for donors. A merchants' association representative asked whether businesses paying fixed maintenance fees should gain priority in selecting receiving sites and people. Luna's submitted advice already stated that funding didn't alter the separate assessment and personal consent required for human connection.

“Who'll keep paying, then?”

Isabella looked up. “That's another disagreement requiring discussion. We cannot sell other people to make the money willing.”

She announced no new tax and didn't make her personal support stand for local agreement. The chair retained the question for later discussion. What today could establish was that funding couldn't be treated as already present.

A local observer asked whether, if one maintenance worker were injured, others would be expected to volunteer longer shifts until recovery.

Viola's right hand tightened beside her chair. She knew those supposedly temporary replacements with no visible end.

“Ask how long, who is qualified to take over, and what happens beyond the limit. ‘Between comrades’ cannot tie someone to a duty post.”

The observer shook his head. His station often had only two people. If both left, a bell couldn't read the gauges.

“No. So when minimum staffing is unavailable, we must admit some work cannot be done and arrange specific load reductions or evacuation. Someone's reluctance to leave cannot count as a third person.”

The engineer explained that appropriate measures depended on the particular installation. A hearing couldn't issue one blanket shutdown instruction for every site. Adrian noted it carefully. They were finally debating a person who tired, not a virtue that was always available.

Before the afternoon break, he asked the clerk to read his conclusion back.

“I acknowledge that even a successfully verified alternative will need continuing maintenance. No single success can sign for the next shift. I accept the recording, explanation, and preparation I've already promised, but I can't promise that my assurance will keep every place safe when I'm not there.”

Saying it didn't relieve him. Boundless assurances had once been easiest to offer precisely because offering them didn't require finding a real, capable pair of hands first.

What if people no longer wanted to listen? Oswin asked.

“Let them propose other approaches, and allow them to say no. Danger won't disappear. Refusal, and failure to deliver after agreement, must both be treated as real possibilities.”

The chair accepted the explanation, authorizing no human trial and announcing no completed long-term fundraising. Existing installations remained under their actual supervisors. The next discussion would involve local representatives already in the capital and those able to submit opinions through established channels, to clarify whose willingness was being invoked.

Afterward, Adrian found himself standing motionless by the door. Elena folded her copy. Was he waiting for someone to issue a guarantee that he'd never make another mistake?

“If they did, I'd probably inspect the back first.”

“Good. Paper like that usually charges by the page.”

Catching his sleeve, avoiding the oil stain no longer there, she led him to the bakery they'd confirmed was open. This time he remembered not to ask the oven's number, even when its door creaked.

The owner carried out the last tray. Sweet rolls needed cooling; savory pastries were ready. Elena considered both and let him decide. He chose savory. Her mouth immediately twitched.

“You actually want something sweet.”

“I didn't say so.”

“You're looking at it as though reviewing an objection.”

Laughing, she told him to buy his savory pastry; she'd wait for a roll. Two things needn't be baked to the same temperature to demonstrate shared purpose. At their narrow table, they watched a cart negotiate potholes while people complained about the street.

Would their next date begin with inspecting every maintenance station? she asked. No. He'd leave once a qualified replacement knew where he was going. What if that person unexpectedly couldn't come?

“Tell you what happened and discuss it again. Not leave you waiting and then make my being busy your fault.”

She tapped her saucer with a little spoon.

“Sometimes you can just be tired, too. Not every cancellation needs a disaster attached.”

Looking at her, he suddenly wanted her hand and offered his between them. She didn't immediately take it. First she wiped icing from her fingertips.

“I wasn't testing you just now.”

“I know. And I'm not promising every future answer will be that good.”

“Then we'll argue when you get it wrong.”

He laughed. So did she, blushing as though she'd realized how much she wanted the future she'd just admitted imagining. They didn't make those words a marriage contract. They finished their late pastries.

As he reached for payment, a corner of Viola's slip emerged. Elena read “No guarding doors” and raised an eyebrow. He explained honestly.

“Very suitable. You've looked at the oven door three times.”

“I was judging whether we could take the rolls home.”

“Please don't investigate their ancestry.”

She carefully pushed the slip back into his pocket, without tearing up someone else's ten minutes. He secured it and took the pastry bag she willingly handed him.

Back at his lodging that evening, a small stack of local representatives' opinions awaited him. The top sheet pledged support. Its reverse added a request that the estate be allowed to answer collectively for all its tenant farmers.

Adrian studied the back for a long time, then laid out both sides together.

Maintenance needed people. Before filling in their number, they still had to establish whether those people had spoken at all.
