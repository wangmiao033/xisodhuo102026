# Chapter 267: Opening the Window Again

The northern equipment-room door opened in the morning light on April 28.

Adrian didn't enter first.

The enforcement officer checked the overnight seals with the current custodian. Engineers carried lamps inside and verified that yesterday's cleared return route remained open. From her assigned position, Evelyn identified a groove left by old repairs, then stepped back beside the recorder. Knowing the way didn't mean everyone must follow her footsteps.

Argument sounded at the console's front entrance. Oswin's representative demanded a stay because the High Priest had filed for reconsideration. The officer produced the still-effective interim ruling and asked him to appoint a qualified technician to attend jointly.

The representative didn't remove the people blocking the doorway.

Isabella stood in the front corridor, her left wrist securely supported, the ruling in her right hand. When someone called for the princess to give orders, she directed him to the actual enforcement officer.

“Today isn't about making anyone kneel to me.”

She ensured each front-door exchange reached the rear, preventing uninformed teams from pushing the same door simultaneously. Messengers carried updates. Negotiations continued in front; authorized entry continued behind.

The service corridor was narrower than Adrian expected. Lamplight caught an old scrape, as though a toolbox had once hesitated there. Viola sat at the entrance where she could see both sets of signals, left arm supported. Her right palm held arrivals back at the wider section.

“Room for two ahead. Don't add your anxiety to the queue.”

Adrian gave his short toolbox to the designated assistant and withdrew into the recessed waiting space. His scar neither glowed nor identified secrets beneath stones. His useful contribution was hearing a lamp stand touch the wall and yielding that extra width.

Engineers opened the ordinary maintenance fastening behind the cover, exposing a mechanical transmission rod. Intact isolation still separated it from the five old locks. Nobody could reach through and touch the core. The removed selector wasn't there; the remaining shaft, however, no longer matched yesterday's recorded position.

The technician stopped touching it immediately.

“Someone moved the front dial after removal.”

A messenger brought the overnight entry: Temple staff had manually checked the dial position and recorded it in their duty book, without notifying the displaced independent custodian. Not an untraceable assassination attempt, but enough to make yesterday's position unsafe to reuse unquestioningly.

In the safe outer room, Luna received front and rear readings and measured positions. Her right palm stayed padded. She cast no spell, studying only the mechanical diagram. An independent engineer compared it with the equipment rather than awaiting a genius's pronouncement instead of measurement.

“The shaft shoulder's notch, not the red line on the front dial. Paint may have moved with the dial. The fixed reference is on the rear mounting.”

Her assistant carried the suggestion back. Two engineers established the current position from the original reference, removing the risk of incorrect installation. Luna exhaled and leaned back. Her contribution had gone ahead without requiring her to crawl after it.

The real obstruction waited in the storeroom.

Elena accompanied the custodian to recover the selector. Yesterday's narrow box already sat on a cart. A carrier held its handles while a Temple guard announced its transfer to a safer inner store.

“The served ruling prohibits moving equipment to obstruct enforcement,” the officer said.

The guard placed his hand on the lid. His instructions required preservation. Two orders collided over one wooden box. The carrier looked between them, knuckles whitening.

Elena didn't seize it. A new rope obscured yesterday's identification number; she asked the custodian to verify that the specified component was inside. The guard refused to untie it. The inner store would inventory the contents.

Elena produced yesterday's deposit copy, pointing out a recorded damaged corner of felt. The item had a full entry, but the lid lacked the agreed dual-witness seals. If it moved now, nobody could demonstrate that its contents remained unchanged in transit.

The officer ordered the cart stopped.

Instead, the guard pushed its side. A wheel crossed the low threshold; the box lurched. The carrier instinctively pulled the handles, his heel slipping toward the door.

“Let go! Step back first!” Viola called from the corridor entrance.

She saw where he would be trapped. Adrian immediately cleared his retreat along the emptied return route. Two enforcement guards steadied the cart frame; one chocked a wheel with a prepared wedge. They didn't pull at the man's wrists.

The cart stopped, box still aboard. The Temple guard resisted removal from its side. Officers controlled his arms and escorted him into the wider space rather than pinning him against equipment. Someone cursed him. Cecilia stopped the shouting.

She asked whether the carrier's foot hurt. Breathing hard, he shook his head, then nodded, finally admitting the side of his shoe had been scraped. She seated him nearby, summoned the accompanying medical attendant, and poured water. Qualified duty staff took over the carrying. Nobody required the frightened man to prove courage by returning immediately.

“I'm not on their side,” he said.

“Sit steadily first. Your foot doesn't need to choose.”

Watching, Adrian felt his urge to rush the front dial cool. Recovering a console wasn't worth leaving an ordinary worker's foot beneath a wheel.

News of the delay reached the front entrance. Isabella asked the officer to record the obstruction and medical examination, then transmit a clear prohibition on further movement to the Temple's on-site supervisor. He sought her personal assurance that no executor would later face proceedings. She refused.

“I can promise not to prejudge unexamined matters. I can't trade somebody else's complaint for a box.”

The Temple technician volunteered to witness opening it—the man who'd asked about his mother's medicine yesterday. He joined no banner. Responsible for equipment, he needed to see what would be reinstalled.

They untied the rope. The selector's old number and shaft opening matched. A fresh scratch was recorded and examined; it didn't impair the verified mechanical fit. Under joint observation, the custodian received the component. He didn't hand it to Elena as a trophy.

Returning to the rear, she found Adrian in the recess. Was she all right? She nodded. Seeing him remain where he'd been assigned softened the tension in her eyes.

“You didn't rush inside.”

“You were doing your work. I didn't want to obstruct it.”

After studying him, she briefly clasped his right hand. Her fingers were cold, their pressure unmistakable. For that moment, he could feel both the chill of her fingers and the decision in their grip. Neither of them tried to make the other promise more than being there. The work behind the cover still waited, and they would return to their separate places to help finish it. The touch didn't mean fear had vanished. Before continuing, they simply confirmed each other's presence.

Reinstalling the selector took longer than fighting over it.

People at two positions checked every fit in turn. The corridor couldn't hold everyone. Adrian delivered designated short pieces to the handover point and withdrew. Viola controlled return traffic. When an assistant breathed heavily, she sent him to the wider section rather than equating slowing down with weakness.

Luna's consultation ended. The replacement engineer continued from the checked drawings. Before leaving, she required the unresolved stopping-procedure page preserved separately. Finally reinstalling the console didn't justify stamping that finished too.

During assembly, Viola quietly asked Adrian to move water within reach of her right hand. The request seemed harder than ordering someone out of a narrow passage.

He complied without using it to suggest she go home. Her real work remained. The cup had simply been in the wrong place.

“I wanted to stand just now.”

“I saw you move.”

“I wanted that man to know I wasn't afraid.”

Adrian looked at the place where the cart had stopped. Had she rushed forward, the carrier's retreat would have been blocked first. He said so, not replacing the judgment with reassurance about her courage.

Viola drank slowly, then smiled. “People can hear me without my standing.”

“Extremely clearly.”

Setting down the cup, she said their future tea still counted. Today's routes wouldn't consume that afternoon. He remembered, he assured her. When she looked up, something besides the concentration of checking a completed task showed in her eyes.

Passing, Cecilia heard tea and asked whether somebody had finally found a table without drawings. Adrian was still searching. She seriously recommended a round one: at least nobody would instinctively take the command position.

Viola pictured it and laughed. Quiet laughter, not drowning out engineers' readings, but the corridor became more than a place to await bad news.

Everyone held their breath when the first mechanical position indication returned to the front panel.

The independent custodian watched the front needle; the Temple technician checked the rear mounting. Read-backs matched. Local observation then rejoined its original recording branch, compared successively with previous duty data. They had restored recording and communication. No academy buffers had been fitted to the Crown of Destiny. No bodily receiving had been requested from the five marks.

The paper strip began moving.

Separate lines registered preparation drive and basic shielding. Adrian recognized the names already divided on the diagram, but his heart still struck hard. Their states could indeed be observed separately. Whether they could be treated separately under present load remained another judgment.

The engineering supervisor consulted the maintenance index, requesting the current stopping instructions and actual condition records for the maintenance absorbers as soon as possible. The April 26 test's two buffers couldn't guarantee unchecked equipment here.

The enforcement officer read the handover result: joint attendance restored at the outer console; independent access to local readings returned to the custodian; abnormality reports and operating requests again deliverable to their respective authorities. Original core keys, five-lock isolation, and the compulsory-movement dispute remained unchanged.

They had recovered a place from which to see, question, and genuinely propose a different action.

When Oswin arrived, the strip already held its first complete record. He read the ruling, required preservation of his objection concerning authority, and approached the readings.

“Now you can see too.”

No sarcasm. That made Adrian's back grow cold.

The High Priest indicated the slowly rising preparation-drive line, then a fresh observation report. Aligned timing showed a steeper trend in the final divisions. The duty technician checked again and reported the same change to the independent custodian.

Oswin stayed. He still believed five people would ultimately be necessary and retained influence over core decisions. Today hadn't removed all his authority, or made real danger applaud their achievement and disappear.

Elena came beside Adrian. Cecilia returned from the frightened worker: examination found no injury requiring immediate transfer; rest and further observation were arranged. Viola still watched the outer route. Luna's assistant carried away the last drawing. Isabella gave the new abnormality report to the actual messenger.

The five women hadn't lined up to await somebody announcing their salvation. Each continued work somebody still needed to do.

Adrian looked through their reopened window.

“I'm more afraid now,” he told Elena quietly.

She held his hand without concealing the rising line.

“Then we'll look together.”

The console stood under shared observation again. No dawn waited beyond its window. Danger was approaching from somewhere they could finally see.
