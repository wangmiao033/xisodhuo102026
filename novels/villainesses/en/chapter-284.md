# Chapter 284: The Knight Recruits Someone Who Can Say No

Nothing Viola brought to the district office belonged in a parade.

Two coils of rope, a reused noticeboard, six task descriptions, and a teapot she was reluctant to carry even with her right hand. Adrian took the pot and asked whether it counted as protective equipment.

“It encourages people to sit down before arguing.”

“Powerful. I've seen military orders fail at that.”

Light rain had left the street wet on May 12. Studying the slippery entrance steps, Viola crossed out the proposed assembly point and chose the level side yard. People helping those with limited mobility shouldn't first confront an entrance a wheeled chair couldn't reach.

The district safety coordinator allowed them the yard, subject to conditions. They could assist journeys, deliver sealed receipts, and report obstructions. No searches, detention, or entering homes under the name of protection. Actual security and threats remained the duty officers' responsibility.

Viola added those limits to every task description without putting her old rank at the top.

Two dockworkers Devon had introduced arrived. Their earlier agreement covered transport alone. Only after hearing today's separate explanation did each choose a role. One offered morning material deliveries and, after training, short carrying-chair journeys with a caregiver present. He would return to his regular job that afternoon. The other disliked going near the hearing hall but would provide directions at the street corner. Viola recorded each separately instead of counting two full days.

Four local adults joined them: a cobbler familiar with every alley, a seamstress who could read notices aloud, and two warehouse hands. Did reading private papers count as work, the seamstress asked?

“Only if someone specifically asks and it's within what you can undertake. Never open sealed submissions. Accompany people to reception for complaints or testimony. Don't decide their cases on a street corner.”

The cobbler reached for a rope. With crowds, he said, the easiest method was to cordon off the passage and admit only people holding receipts.

Viola asked him to put it down.

“Other people live here. We can't turn a complaint into an obligation for them to establish their identities to us.”

Annoyed, he wondered what made this protection. He'd expected at least an armband that would make his noisy neighbors listen.

Adrian poured tea rather than answering for Viola with a joke. She turned the board over to reveal her route: reception office, vegetable awning, gutter, outward-opening warehouse door, carriage stop.

“Sometimes it means giving someone afraid to come outside a way to get where they want.”

The cobbler studied it. The warehouse door was on the wrong side. Deliveries also blocked that corner every afternoon.

Viola didn't require agreement with her principles before accepting his knowledge. She handed him the pencil. Their first useful correction came from the man most eager for an armband.

They tried the route with an empty carrying chair. Two adults assigned to lifting carried an ordinary cloth bundle on its seat; nobody's body would test whether it probably fitted. Viola stayed seated by the yard, respecting her injured arm rather than inaugurating the group with bravado.

The first attempt stopped beyond the vegetable awning. Avoiding the gutter, the front carrier moved inward without seeing the rear legs. One struck the stall. The bundle fell, muddying a clean sheet. The stallholder emerged, hands on hips, asking who would pay and how often they intended to collide with her business.

Adrian collected the bundle and covered it with clean cloth. He'd meant to request another half foot of room. The woman indicated the wall behind her: moving back would obstruct the stove's ventilation.

He swallowed his appeal for a favor and returned to ask Viola not to mark the route usable. The coordinator found a longer outside path and arranged a short period without warehouse unloading. Good intentions wouldn't cost somebody else their livelihood.

On the second attempt, the carriers agreed that the rear person could call a stop. At the corner, he did. The front carrier took another half step anyway, saying they were almost through.

Viola's smile vanished.

“Set it down safely. No passengers today.”

Nothing had struck anything, the man protested. He wasn't trying to be brave. He simply disliked letting someone who couldn't see ahead decide when to stop.

“He can see things you can't,” she said.

Looking at his hands, he admitted he'd heard the instruction and considered it unnecessary. They practiced stopping and lowering until everyone could do it without an argument. The organizers accepted the actual cleaning cost, and Viola told waiting residents why they wouldn't be carrying anybody today.

After practice, one warehouse hand approached. He was withdrawing.

“I thought this was ordinary lifting. With a real person sitting there, I'd be frightened.”

Would he prefer transporting empty equipment? He still shook his head. Viola removed him from the later schedule and arranged payment for today's agreed practice hours without demanding another explanation. Once he'd gone, someone wondered whether they'd recruited the most cowardly man available.

“We recruited someone who knows what he can't do.”

Adrian studied the missing line. Her answer didn't replace two hands. The afternoon plan still had to shrink. Instead of three journeys a day, they could offer one short trip, with an appropriate caregiver, the passenger's consent, and the actual route checked. They couldn't promise a second without enough staff.

Later, Viola and the coordinator confirmed tomorrow's duties. The five remaining participants had distinct agreed tasks. Coordination belonged to the current district officer; Viola would help improve training, not remain indefinitely on watch. The board finally read Civilian Access and Protection Group, followed by actual working hours.

Existing district assistance funds covered only two initial short trips, including labor and equipment hire. Viola removed uniforms from her wish list, retaining cleaning, inspection, and paid practice time. The money wasn't meal-service funding. A new name didn't authorize future expenditure.

“Would borrowing under my family name make it quicker?” she asked, then stopped.

Adrian didn't hurry to congratulate her insight. The coordinator said private guarantees could be discussed separately. Tomorrow's residents mustn't mistake this for House Blackwood's company or believe assistance created a debt of political support.

Viola pocketed her old family pin. She wasn't repudiating her origins. She didn't want visitors guessing whether their help had an owner they couldn't refuse. She recorded who borrowed the equipment and its return date, leaving unresolved long-term costs for actual district discussion.

The cobbler returned with an old plank for the shallow side-door gutter. The coordinator examined its dimensions and support. It could back a notice, not carry weight. Disappointed, the cobbler donated it anyway. Viola thanked him without proposing that tomorrow's passenger test it to spare his feelings.

“Not a very imposing name,” Adrian said of the board.

“Excellent.”

She reached for tea and wrinkled her nose. It had gone cold. Yes, he could replace the water; no renewed energy-supply record required.

Over hot tea, she admitted wanting to ask the departing worker to try again. Not for the staffing numbers. She feared he'd decide he was useless.

“But I can't prove otherwise for him. I used to think if people endured a little longer, they'd discover they were braver than they knew.”

“Sometimes they discover it's better not to tell you next time.”

She watched him before nodding. “That's rather unpleasant.”

“Would you like something pleasant?”

“Yes. Without withdrawing what you just said.”

He considered. She'd looked lovely today, listening seriously while a cobbler corrected her route.

Her ears reddened. What kind of lovely?

The kind he'd seen. It wasn't a knight's performance assessment.

She lowered her face toward her cup, finally smiling. “Accepted. Don't use my good mood to add another shift.”

On May 13, the five helpers didn't line up before their first actual journey. One checked that unloading had ended. Another confirmed the passing time with the vegetable stall. Two examined the carrying chair. The seamstress remained at reception, reading ordinary directions. Yesterday's departing man wasn't present, and nobody treated his absence as disgrace.

Their passenger was an adult woman whose leg injury hadn't healed. She wanted to collect her own document copy at the inquiry office. She didn't want a neighbor to collect it or to explain its contents to this little team. After checking the assistance method, her caregiver asked where she wished to go and how she'd report discomfort.

She wanted only her paper. Above all, she feared being taken into a room where leaving depended on finishing her story.

Seated near the door, Viola explained that she could change her mind at the entrance. Reception would ask her separately. Preparing a chair didn't create a debt of testimony. The woman requested to see where her return ride would wait. The cobbler showed her both stopping places, neither blocking the street.

They set out according to the agreed plan. Before the corner, the rear carrier called another stop. This time the front carrier halted immediately, waiting while an old man slowly pushed his vegetable cart past. Nobody demanded gratitude for their charitable mission or blamed the passenger for the delay.

At reception, she declined the reserved interview room. She verified her identity and collected her copy at the ordinary window. Someone wished to ask about a separate matter. When she said she wouldn't discuss it today, the invitation was withdrawn.

Returning through the yard, she looked up at Viola.

“They really brought me back.”

The knight sat a little straighter. This wasn't praise for a short route. Someone could go out, accomplish only what she'd chosen, and return to where she wished to be. That was what today's journey had actually tested.

The coordinator accepted the report: necessary travel details, duration, and problems, not the collected document's contents. The next empty journey slot stayed empty until a replacement worker could be found. The helpers knew their pay arrangements and when another shift would be confirmed. “Thank you for your service” wouldn't become an indefinite obligation.

Viola put the route into her thin folder. Her arm had begun to ache; she finished at the agreed time. Walking her toward the resting area, Adrian asked whether she still wanted an impressive name.

“I considered one. Then I realized a name couldn't hear 'stop' for us.”

He didn't tease her again. Passing a window bench, she said she wouldn't sit together today. Tomorrow brought another care assessment. He agreed, remembering her words rather than recording time owed to him later.

That evening, reception forwarded a question from prospective technical reviewers. Civilian helpers needed to know which areas were dangerous. Without dangerous interface details, how could anyone establish that experts hadn't hidden risks behind an attractive diagram?

Luna read it and asked her assistant to invite qualified researchers who hadn't worked on her design. She didn't ask people simply to trust her.

Today a cobbler had corrected a route. Next, the witch would put her own drawings into someone else's hands.
