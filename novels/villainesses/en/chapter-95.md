# Chapter 95: The Light Stops. The Trace Doesn't.

After the third afternoon bell, Luna finally opened the equipment cabinet.

There were no skulls or eyes floating in jars. Its most conspicuous feature was a sign reading *Please return to the correct place after cleaning*. The sign itself was in the wrong place.

“The true enemy of the Forbidden Arts Research Tower.” Luna returned it to its hook. “Colleagues who can't read.”

Seated at the table, Adrian couldn't help laughing. The surface was much less crowded than the diagram. The lamp was secured on the left, the receiving frame stood in the middle, and the properly clamped red handle waited beside his right hand. Their original notes, the historical betrothal copies, and the Greymarch drawings remained in another room. Nothing had been added to make the result more exciting.

Dr. Hensley asked whether he had developed any new discomfort. He hadn't, and still wished to proceed. Luna repeated the time limit, then held the sealed energy cell where he could see it.

“Only this one. When it's spent, all the secrets of the universe can queue outside and take a number.”

Viola checked the passage and the door latch. Her sword rested quietly in its sheath, and her left arm had no task requiring a sudden pull. Cecilia sat beside the timer, responsible for counting and keeping a separate record, not supplying the apparatus with her own magic. Elena sat where Adrian could see her. She took the scarf from his lap so it wouldn't hang near the support.

“You'll have it back afterward.”

“Can I ransom it sooner?”

“With an honest answer. Are you cold?”

“No.”

She glanced at the physician without adding a rule that only she could determine whether he was telling the truth.

The administrator turned the visitor sign outside to *Please wait*, left another staff member in the corridor, and withdrew. Room temperature and lamp distance went onto the record. Before inserting the cell, Luna asked Adrian to try his stop handle again. The shutter closed with a crisp click. The box didn't move.

“I'm resetting it now,” she said before doing so.

His right hand returned to its place. He removed his left glove and rested his hand on the cushion. A clear gap separated the barrier from his scar. Luna checked the support in ordinary light without touching him.

“If this is a date,” she said, “your experimental agreement makes it the least romantic one I've ever seen.”

“Do your dates usually come with a physician?” Adrian asked.

Hensley didn't look up. “My fee doesn't include certifying a romantic atmosphere.”

Elena laughed aloud. Luna looked regretful, as if she'd forgotten she had given everybody instructions for handling her today.

When the laughter faded, the room didn't turn into an execution ground. The fire still rustled on the far side. Someone in the corridor moved a chair as quietly as possible. Watching Luna check each position, Adrian remembered her first look at his scar. The eagerness remained. Now, however, other people's choices stood between it and the next step.

“Shall we begin?” she asked.

“Yes.”

The cell went into its socket, and a point of cold white light appeared behind the lamp. Cecilia started the mechanical timer.

“One.”

A fine line passed through the filter onto the receiving plate. The transparent barrier kept the beam away from his skin. His left hand remained in the ordinary room light. Two short lines appeared as expected on the manufactured reference. The blank branch showed only a faint rim of light.

“Two. Three.”

No voice came from the scar. Nobody called his name. No scene of death arrived to answer for him. He heard only his own breathing, slightly faster than before but steady.

At the fifth count, a very fine gray line appeared along the receiving plate's lower edge.

Luna's pen paused for half a beat and resumed. She didn't brighten the lamp. Elena leaned forward slightly when she saw the line, then stayed in her chair.

“Six.”

The back of Adrian's left hand grew faintly warm. It didn't hurt. It felt like being near a cup of hot water. He couldn't tell whether the lamp, his nerves, or the old scar was responsible. His old habit would have been to wait until everybody finished looking, then mention that it was probably nothing.

This time, he didn't wait.

“Stop a moment. My hand feels warm.”

He pulled the red handle. The shutter dropped, and the power contacts sprang apart.

Cecilia spoke the final count. “Eight. Stopped.”

Luna had already reached her own handle. She didn't reopen anything. Hensley asked him to lift his left hand from the cushion into ordinary light and tell her whether the sensation was getting stronger.

“No. It's fading.”

Elena stood to give the physician room. She didn't reach across the examining hands to seize his. Viola kept the passage clear so nobody would have to squeeze past.

“The receiving plate hasn't cleared,” Cecilia said.

Luna's eyes snapped back toward the table.

The power indicator was at zero. The blank branch's rim had disappeared within the first count. The manufactured reference had faded within its known interval too. But beside the original gray line on the central receiver, a second shadow remained, offset by a very small distance. They withdrew in sequence. The last trace lasted until the third count after closure.

Luna's fingertips reached toward the fine-adjustment dial and stopped halfway.

Adrian saw it.

Slowly, she drew back and pressed her hand flat on her notebook.

“We don't reopen it. Record what's there.”

Cecilia used a different pen to mark the two disappearance times. All visible light was gone before the agreed ten-second reading limit. She continued watching until that limit, then stopped the timer. A dark table wasn't a reason to leave out the final part of the record.

Hensley confirmed that the warmth was fading. There was no new redness or broken skin, no chest tightness or dizziness. She still asked Adrian to remain seated and rest so she could check again shortly. Nobody said he'd stopped too early simply because he appeared unharmed.

Luna removed the disconnected cell and put it in a box marked *used*. She closed the nearby filter box as well and pushed it out of reach.

“Eight seconds,” she said softly.

“Did you need twelve?” Adrian asked.

She looked up, her gaze moving from his hand to his face.

“I wanted twelve. We got eight. The report says eight.”

Elena's shoulders slowly relaxed.

Before sealing anything, Luna had them confirm that the receiving plate hadn't been exchanged. Then, in ordinary light, she traced the faint remaining marks. They formed no picture and contained no writing, only two fine edges visible from a particular angle. The corresponding blank and manufactured reference went into separate paper sleeves, their identifiers and exposure times linked to the same record. Adrian read his copy and applied his own seal.

“What does it mean?”

Luna didn't answer immediately. She placed the timer before her and asked Cecilia to describe what she had seen without reading Luna's words.

The saint spoke slowly. She separated what she knew from what she couldn't be certain of: when the second shadow appeared, when the first withdrew, whether the last trace still counted as light. Luna added the appropriate allowance for visual uncertainty. She didn't add the eight seconds of exposure to the three residual counts and pretend they had conducted an eleven-second experiment.

“We can say one thing,” she said at last. “With this arrangement, during this observation, the receiver near your left hand showed delayed layered traces that the blank and known reference didn't.”

“Because of the scar?” Elena asked.

“Associated with the position. We can't establish the cause yet. Local differences in the receiver's coating, reflection from the support, environmental changes, even old contamination I've missed would need checking. We haven't compared the other hand or repeated the observation. We can't claim to have proved it came from his skin.”

“Then why did you look as though you recognized it?”

Luna was silent for a moment. She took a published instrument-restoration manual from the shelf and opened it to a diagram. The page included its printing date and references. This wasn't an explanation written to fit what had just happened.

“There's a class of behavior in older recording media called temporal residue.”

Adrian's fingers moved.

Luna laid the book flat, refusing to let those words grow into an enormous story by themselves.

“It means the medium retains part of the sequence of earlier states after stimulation ends. Ordinary afterglow often fades together. These traces withdraw in layers. But materials and apparatus can imitate similar effects. The name doesn't tell us what year anything came from. It certainly doesn't prove you've lived another life.”

“Or that anyone was married to anyone,” Cecilia said.

“Correct. There isn't a name on the plate.”

Adrian studied the manual. Back in front of a screen, he'd never watched someone carefully preserve so much uncertainty around a word that looked like the truth. He found it easier to breathe than he would have after a spectacular revelation.

“So today we found a small sign of temporal residue worth investigating.”

“A sign consistent with that class of behavior,” Luna corrected. “That's the statement the evidence can carry.”

“Did you remember anything?” she asked, then immediately reconsidered the question. “Sorry. That points you toward something to find. Were there any other sensations you'd like recorded?”

Adrian looked at his hand.

“No pictures. No sounds. Just the warmth, and the nervousness I already had.”

“We record that too.”

She didn't look as though half her discovery had gone missing. Her pen left a real negative answer on the page, not merely an empty space. Adrian knew that a little more expectation in her eyes might have tempted him to mistake the fragments already in his mind for something newly experienced.

Viola opened the door for the administrator. She asked him to bring the sealed equipment to the designated cabinet, where Luna would register it herself. A worker in the corridor wanted to know when the temperature-controlled cupboard would be available again. Luna checked the remaining power arrangements and directed him to another storage room already confirmed usable. An unusual shadow wasn't allowed to become a reason everyone else had to work late.

“Do you want to investigate further tomorrow?” she asked Adrian.

“I'd like to sleep on it.”

“All right.”

Those two words granted her nothing for tomorrow. They did let him pick up his glove.

Hensley checked on him again. The warmth had gone completely, but he remained nearby to rest as arranged. When Luna went to wash her hands, Elena moved closer and offered her right hand instead of simply taking his.

“No timer for this?” he asked.

“Tell me when you want to let go.”

He placed his right hand in hers. Her palm was warmer than the metal handle. Her thumb rested along the side of his finger, nowhere near the scar.

“You actually stopped,” she said.

“Did you think I'd try to endure it?”

“I was afraid you'd think everyone had waited so long that you owed us the full twelve seconds.”

He looked at the closed materials box. “I almost did.”

Her grip tightened a little, then eased, leaving him room to withdraw.

“Eight seconds was quite enough to worry me.”

“Could we spend tonight eating dinner instead of counting seconds to prove something?”

“Yes. You can ask.”

“Elena, would you have dinner with me tonight? No diagrams.”

“I'd like that. You've learned to include the important conditions.”

Luna cleared her throat by the door. “Must I separate the kitchen from the research tower before I receive an invitation like that?”

Adrian looked up, smiling. “You should have dinner too. But I asked her to this one.”

Luna blinked, then raised an eyebrow. “Clear enough that I can't pretend to misunderstand. How disappointing.”

She didn't put herself between them. She took her cup and reminded them that the post-observation contact instructions were in the folder.

Before leaving with Adrian, Elena helped stack the unused paper at the edge of the table. Beneath Luna's restoration manual, the cover of an application came into view. It was dated before their arrival at East Ridge. Its title also contained the words *temporal residue*.

Elena's hand paused. She didn't open somebody else's papers.

From the other side of the table, Luna drew out the cover and slipped it back into her notebook. The movement wasn't hurried. It was simply quieter than any joke she'd made that afternoon.

The receiving plate was locked away. The lamp was dark. Elena watched the notebook close and realized that Adrian's old scar might not be the only thing they had yet to see clearly.
