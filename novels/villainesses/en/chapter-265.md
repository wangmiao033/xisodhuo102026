# Chapter 265: The Window They Took Away

On the morning of April 27, the bell outside the control room rang a quarter-hour early.

A narrow wooden box already stood at the door when Adrian arrived, felt-lined and exactly large enough for the outer console's selector component. Two Temple guards watched it. Another attendant was removing the wall clip used to circulate readings.

These weren't intruders breaking into the core. They carried a sealed preservation notice. Behind them stood an unhappy facilities custodian.

“I've lodged an objection. They say that doesn't prevent depositing the equipment first.”

The console stood outside the five old locks. Existing custody arrangements allowed duty engineers to read local instruments, check preparation status, and forward operating requests for inner review. Actual core keys remained sealed with their respective custodians. This outer window had never let Adrian's group alter the Crown of Destiny at will. At least somebody could see the facts before decisions were made.

Now even that light was being taken away.

Elena examined the removed clip. The first readings of the day remained on top, but the following sheets had been stacked inside the box too. She stopped the carrier and asked the custodian to check the deposit's scope.

“Custody of the selector may be disputed. Why take duty records that haven't been archived?”

The man looked toward the leading priest. The priest reached to close the lid. The custodian blocked its edge, insisting the papers be separated first.

A small movement. Everyone fell silent.

Two paces away, Adrian heard boots scraping stone. He didn't grab the box. The easiest victory would be snatching it into his arms. The easiest defeat would also be reducing every argument to his public seizure of property.

“Let me explain.” Oswin's voice came from the corridor.

The High Priest wasn't hurrying. He carried a real report from the northern observation post, not a prayer book. Damp stained its edge. The rider who'd delivered it was still drinking in the yard.

Oswin gave it to the duty technician and waited for provenance and scales to be checked.

“You accomplished something yesterday,” he told Adrian. “I haven't asked anyone to call it a failure.”

“So today you take away our view of the larger danger?”

“You demonstrated that two small loads can stop. What's changing outside won't wait for you to grow merely because you honestly admit your size.”

The technician confirmed a rising reading, still needing alignment with local timing. Oswin let him keep a copy. The danger wasn't a deliberately crumpled forgery. Disliking the person holding the paper couldn't erase it.

Isabella asked why outer-console staff had to be removed.

“To prevent divided commands. Five marked women participate in alternative research while demanding a window onto final preparations. If interpretations differ, who ensures the staff obey?”

“Staff obey lawful, verifiable instructions. Not us—and they needn't see fewer readings to obey you.”

He glanced at her supported left wrist. “You've become willing to entrust power to procedure.”

“I used the unrestrained kind. It hurt people.”

She didn't raise her voice. His potential insult stopped before a truth she'd chosen to bear herself.

Cecilia asked the displaced copying clerk whether he remained responsible for reporting abnormalities today. Uncertain whether to look at her or the High Priest, he said his order only required surrendering the clip.

“Abnormalities don't stop when a paper clip leaves.” She turned to Oswin. “Who replaces him?”

A Temple technician produced proof of his assignment. He was qualified and physically present. Cecilia nodded, refusing to treat everyone in similar clothing as an enemy. Could the independent custodian stay beside him and read the same gauge?

Oswin refused.

The room seemed colder. That refusal clarified more than every explanation about unity. He wanted more than attended equipment. He wanted outsiders to know its condition only through him.

Luna waited in a more distant resting room for a short technical consultation. Hearing the arrangements, she requested the dismantling drawing. Her assistant quickly returned with a warning: they must establish the selector's position before removal. They couldn't lump the display and mechanical linkage together as an insignificant part.

Engineers checked, recording the original reading and resting position beside the box. Oswin permitted the necessary preservation and even ordered his attendants back. Watching, Adrian understood increasingly clearly that the man wouldn't fall through one crude mistake.

“I won't let the city collapse while you argue,” Oswin said.

“I won't let five people disappear when you announce there is no choice.”

“Then tell me where equivalent receiving capacity exists now.”

The question settled between them.

Adrian couldn't hold up yesterday's small buffers. He knew their measured capacity. They weren't the kingdom. Nor could he point to his scar as though revealing undisclosed reserves.

“No equivalent alternative has yet been demonstrated.”

Oswin didn't look triumphant. He seemed to have heard a fact they could finally share.

“But that doesn't entitle you to withhold records needed to examine necessity,” Adrian continued. “Put the decision to remove independent observers under your own name. Don't describe it as equipment failure.”

After studying him, Oswin took the sheet and signed.

His hand was steady.

Adrian had imagined villains fearing demands for their names. Here stood someone prepared to bear what he considered a necessary crime. The signature left Adrian with no comforting hesitation to point at. Oswin had not dropped his eyes, snatched back the paper, or asked somebody beneath him to accept the responsibility. Opposing him would require more than exposing a coward behind the title. Willingness to face judgment still couldn't substitute for another person's consent to die.

The negotiation preserved a custodial copy of today's records, but not the selector. The independent custodian signed the objection field. The box went into an adjoining Temple storage room; the guards changed. Every old lock remained sealed. Oswin hadn't received the inner core keys. Instead, he had choked off the place from which their keepers might hear different questions.

Viola watched traffic from her chair near the side door. Two ordinary workers arriving for relief stood trapped behind the box cart, carrying tools. She asked the duty guard to move the obstruction before the dispute turned a narrow passage into a trap.

One worker quietly asked whether requests were all she could make now.

“Yes. You can see whether they work. You needn't pretend I still command.”

The guard moved. The workers thanked her as they passed. Her right hand tightened and relaxed. Not drawing a sword didn't mean it had accomplished nothing.

The new Temple technician caught Cecilia in the corridor. He didn't want to hurt anybody, he said. His mother still collected medicine at the clinic where Cecilia had worked. He spoke urgently, as though proving his mother's goodness could remove today's choices.

Cecilia didn't ask him to pass a key in secret.

“Your mother will receive her medicine as usual. Record as usual, too. If a reading is abnormal, don't omit it because it doesn't suit either side's account.”

“What if they tell me to submit only one copy?”

“Record that instruction in the duty book. You don't owe me allegiance first.”

He stared, moving his hand away from his pocket. Adrian didn't know what it contained and didn't ask. Cecilia watched him return, her expression troubled.

“You want him to help us,” Adrian said.

“Very much. That's why I mustn't trade on his mother.”

Adrian looked again at the closed door. Somebody had removed the window; others still had to work behind it. They shouldn't recover that place by first making everyone inside fear losing their families.

The afternoon hearing brought no immediate victory. The facilities supervisor acknowledged a conflict between the preservation notice and independent evidence-custody arrangements. Judicial review outside Oswin's command chain was required. Pending review, the dispute couldn't open the five core locks. But the outer console was already sealed. A receipt stamp wouldn't turn the morning backward.

Before the supervisor, Elena surrendered today's copy to the independent custodian. More reliable preservation meant abandoning the comfort of keeping it within reach herself.

Isabella formally requested restoration of joint outer-console attendance and uninterrupted abnormality reports. She signed no royal command. Only her name filled the applicant's space, beside the facilities personnel's existing objections.

Luna's assistant brought a question from the dismantling record: after removing the selector, could its mechanical position still be independently confirmed from the rear maintenance dial? Their copy lacked that face. They needed actual repair documentation.

Evelyn looked up.

She had remained in the waiting area beside the recorder responsible for questioning her. She didn't claim to know everything. Could they retrieve another maintenance attachment from the catalog she'd provided? It might describe the console's rear.

“Might?” Adrian asked.

“I signed a record withdrawing outside copies once. The workers entered from the other side.”

Her voice was quiet. That past wasn't a convenient secret door into redemption. It also opened questions about why she'd kept silent.

The examiner agreed to check the catalog, not to let anyone open a door from memory.

Adrian went into the courtyard to wait. A bird landed on the wall. He remembered the carefully accommodated birdcage outside the execution hall. Their achievement that day had been tiny, but real. Today he'd watched a box carried away and still had to admit he possessed no equivalent receiver.

He stood with his right hand against his forehead for a long time.

When Elena came, he first said he wasn't preparing to sneak inside.

“I didn't come to search your pockets.”

“I know. I don't want to make you guess again.”

She stepped before him and asked to hold him. He nodded.

Her embrace was firm, not the cautious lifting of something about to shatter. Adrian lowered his head until his temple touched her hair. He didn't cry. He could finally admit how badly he wanted to smash that box.

“You can want it. Then tell me what you intend to do.”

“Wait for the maintenance documents. Preserve what they did today. Let people authorized to enforce the decision do it tomorrow.”

“What else?”

He drew a slow breath. “Go home tonight. Not write a letter you'll find after waking.”

Her arms tightened slightly.

“You don't have to save everyone alone, Adrian. I don't need you to win every time before you come back.”

He held her in return, knowing this wasn't absolution. Many small actions still had to repair the old cracks between them. Today she had simply chosen to stand with him where he hadn't won.

When they separated, he asked whether she would walk home with him. Yes, but they must eat on the way. He'd choose an ordinary street, he promised. No archives. She smiled at last and straightened the collar her embrace had crumpled.

“The problem isn't my neckwear.”

“I know. But I can do this now.”

Back in the waiting room, the retrieved catalog lay before Evelyn. Her finger stopped at a faint line.

The maintenance attachment wasn't missing. It had been filed inside instructions suspending copies after an accident.

Evelyn had signed those instructions.

The bell sounded again. Oswin had taken away a window. The woman who'd once drawn its curtain for him was finally pointing out the first nail behind it.
