# Chapter 263: The Empty Place Is Not for a Hero

On April 24, Elena brought two soup basins.

Adrian had laid out last night's blank paper. Watching her put the basins on the table, he wondered whether the kitchen had finally decided to punish people who held meetings. Water marks remained inside them. Beside Luna's ugly chair stood a bucket of ordinary cold water.

“If this is breakfast, I object,” Luna said.

“The cheapest lesson a noble warehouse can offer.” Elena spread two cloths. “Space inside a building doesn't mean the floor by the entrance can support every wagon. My steward once scolded me with that. You may borrow it.”

She didn't look at Adrian. Precisely because she didn't, he knew this wasn't a lecture prepared solely for him. She, too, had wanted every delivery before dawn and believed her steward would somehow manage.

The technician laid the damage drawings behind the basins. The broken receiving layer had its own local limit; the external absorber had genuinely protected people. What needed changing was the physical arrangement between them, not the size of the word STOP.

Adrian reached for a cup. Elena handed it to Cecilia.

“Let the person asking questions pour first.”

Cecilia rolled up her sleeves. Could the cup represent somebody requesting withdrawal? For discussing sequence, Luna agreed. Water didn't affect materials like residual burden, however. Nobody could use these basins to justify powering anything.

“You've finally approved a date without magic,” Adrian said.

“I approve of nobody using my hand as a jug.”

Her right palm rested on padding, her left hand idle. An assistant turned the drawings. Adrian withdrew the hand he'd offered to write for her and asked whom she wanted taking notes.

“Him. You listen. Sometimes you write so quickly that the parts you dislike become particularly small.”

Cecilia poured half a cup. Another followed immediately. Water overflowed the shallow basin and soaked Adrian's sleeve. He didn't move away; he was busy imagining a remedy.

Viola pointed with her right hand at his fingers on the rim.

“You're trying to hold it up again.”

He let go. Water fell onto the waiting cloth. This time nobody needed an injury to reveal his habit.

“Two basins.” Elena moved the other closer. “If the first delivery hasn't cleared, the next wagon can't crowd in. Could each residual load go somewhere separate first?”

“A possible design,” Luna said. “But queuing for one basin would leave whoever asked to stop still carrying the burden. We'd only hide failure inside a waiting period.”

“That was my objection.” Cecilia set down her cup. “People must be able to withdraw at any stage. We can't tell them it's somebody else's turn, so be polite and endure until the bell.”

Neither raised her voice or yielded. Elena frowned: warehouse goods couldn't vanish either. People weren't goods, Cecilia replied. Precisely because they couldn't vanish, an exit must already exist.

Adrian almost said they meant the same thing. Remembering last night's note, he remained silent. They didn't. If calling everyone right solved the problem, fragments wouldn't have struck the shield yesterday.

Using only her right hand, Viola moved two chairs slightly apart.

“Stop pouring. Suppose this person has stopped. Where do they go?”

An assistant carried an empty box through the narrow gap. A rolled drawing caught its edge; two sheets fell. Their original plan stationed the residual-handling worker there.

“You've considered energy leaving, not workers leaving. I need a route that won't collide with the next shift. Better one fewer cycle than everyone crowding together.”

“So I can't always stand closest?” Adrian asked.

“You can if you have actual work. Looking extremely worried doesn't count.”

Luna's shoulders shook with laughter. Viola's ears reddened.

“That includes me.”

Adrian looked at her supported left arm. Those words had been harder. She excelled at approaching danger; today she was moving herself away from the front. She wasn't asking for a pass marked courageous.

Isabella finally opened her bundle. Inside were three wooden pieces, a confirmed work-hours sheet, and an unopened quotation for materials.

“That envelope is still a quotation. Don't count it as a fourth piece.”

She placed the three pieces: two small buffers they could actually purchase, and an external absorber for the separate stopping arrangement. Money was tight. Canceling the enlarged display covered part; research funds required separate approval for the rest. Timber promised to relief stations would stay there.

“Two receiving points mean less throughput,” the technician said. “Slower within the same hours.”

“Then admit it's slower. I can't count one person's sleep as another person's shift, then ask him to thank me for his important position.”

A cart squeaked outside. Adrian studied the increasingly crowded table. For once, complexity didn't necessarily feel like failure. Simplicity might merely mean somebody had hidden the cost for him.

Luna had her assistant draw two separate short paths. Each small buffer would receive only its own branch's residual load. Another stage couldn't enter before the previous one genuinely cleared. If both branches stopped together, supply would be cut upstream and each handled separately—not passed to whoever remained willing.

“We divide admission into stages, not withdrawal rights into five portions.”

Cecilia lifted her cup again. When the next stage began, would previous agreement automatically carry forward? Isabella shook her head. So did Elena, who then frowned at the princess.

“Why are you answering for me?”

“I'm answering for myself.”

“Good,” Cecilia said. “At least two people remember consent isn't a chorus.”

Laughter could finally touch the edges of the argument. Adrian crossed out the total headcount at the center of his page, replacing it with present, currently available, and actually cleared. He didn't add his own name.

“Where does my work belong?”

Elena returned the water-stained summary. “Tell us what we actually said. We'll interrupt when you get it wrong.”

Cecilia interrupted his account of waiting between stages. Luna reminded him the two-buffer arrangement still needed technical validation. His face warmed, but he didn't fight for the last word. Listening meant allowing their correction to remain larger than the explanation he wanted to give for himself. He waited until each woman had finished before beginning again. Watching him, Viola slowly returned her hand from the chair to her lap.

Isabella gave the quotation to the purchasing officer. Their discussion had produced a reviewable proposal. It wouldn't bring materials through the door by itself.

What truly halted the discussion was a small sheet Cecilia took from her pocket. Yesterday's departing candidate had asked whether offering to proofread notices in future would make him seem inconsistent.

The greeting had been corrected twice. Adrian's chest tightened. The man feared refusal might mean they no longer wanted him near.

“I'd like to answer personally.”

“Yes. But don't bring a repaired diagram to persuade him. He's asking whether he can still be a friend.”

Elena moved the basins aside, clearing a dry space. Adrian wrote a short letter inviting him to ordinary proofreading whenever convenient, and saying declining remained equally acceptable. He didn't add that next time they would certainly succeed.

Cecilia smiled over it.

“Shorter than your last invitation to me.”

“You asked seven questions.”

“One was whether you enjoyed eating with me. You answered with three paragraphs about risk.”

Adrian looked up. She spoke so frankly that the surrounding silence became conspicuous. He answered seriously: he enjoyed her company and would like another meal together. But he couldn't describe that closeness as already choosing the same future.

“That's enough for today,” she said.

Luna sighed: apparently the most effective questioning required no instruments. Elena put an empty cup before her and suggested shelving her own research for a moment. Cecilia folded away the letter without announcing victory.

At the break, Adrian was assigned to empty the basins.

“I hoped for an important position.”

“Custodian of two soup basins,” Elena said. “An excellent match for your present abilities.”

At the door, she told him to go slowly. Not because he carried the whole world. The floor was slippery.

Viola waited in the corridor when he returned. She wore no armor; her coat allowed room for the left arm's support. If they eventually reached the core perimeter, would he want her with him?

“Yes. I don't want to turn that wish into an obligation.”

She looked down, smiling. “Then a private answer: I want to accompany you. How I actually do that depends on the route and what my body can manage.”

Could they someday have tea without discussing routes? She considered it seriously. Should she choose the place? Certainly, he said, but three prearranged escape routes weren't necessary.

“Two?”

“One ordinary door.”

She laughed aloud, admitting that might require practice.

Luna's allotted time ended. She insisted on leaving the unresolved drawing behind while she departed. As her assistant opened the door, she beckoned Adrian over.

“You didn't ask me to examine one last thing.”

“You left the last thing here.”

“Disappointed?”

“Yes. I want you to stay, too. Neither wish matters more than your rest.”

The teasing faded briefly from her eyes. She abandoned her prepared joke and requested a book unrelated to experiments later—preferably one in which neither romantic lead plugged cracks with their body.

“A comedy?”

“A less ugly chair would suffice.”

The duty technicians continued the practical discussion that afternoon. Examination hadn't found a preexisting fracture sufficient to explain the accident alone. Independent measurements confirmed that local concentration and the original gauge's lag both required attention. Support dimensions needed recalculation; near-branch measurement would no longer pass through the node's general indicator. The inspected shield could serve within its original rating, with its impact history preserved. By evening, the purchasing officer returned with two component options genuinely available for delivery, not merely an assurance that matters were settled.

Adrian left the test date undecided. Elena studied the blank for a long moment.

“I want a short walk tonight. Not after you've filled every space.”

He gave his pen to the person taking over. Could they go now?

They walked beside the low trees beyond the courtyard wall. The wind was cool. Elena hadn't offered her hand, and he didn't exchange last night's apology for closeness. After a while, she moved half a step nearer.

“I'm still angry with you.”

“I know.”

“But I saw you put the cup down.”

Too small an action for a triumphant report. Adrian remembered cold water running between his fingers. He asked to hold her hand. She offered it, her palm warm.

“Put it down tomorrow too.”

“Not only when you're watching.”

She looked up and finally gave him a genuine smile.

Academy lamps lit in the distance. The washed soup basins had returned to the kitchen; the materials that must actually hold residual burden remained with the craftsmen. Five women hadn't chosen who most deserved to accompany him to death. They had removed from the new drawing the empty place nobody ought to occupy. That did not mean the work waiting inside the academy was finished. It meant the next drawing would have to account for real materials and real limits, without quietly reserving the missing answer for whoever loved the others enough to step into it.
