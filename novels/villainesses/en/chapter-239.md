# Chapter 239: The Blank on the Receipt

On the morning of April 7, Adrian submitted the alternative work proposal and existing evidence through the channel confirmed after the April 5 meeting. He obtained an intake receipt. Independent review had not reached the necessary decision; the limited simulation had not met the conditions for actual replacement. Receipt did not suspend Oswin's next procedure. He left a copy with the liaison before helping at the relief hall, remaining available for a formal reply.

By the time a covered carriage arrived outside, the noon bells had long fallen silent.

Adrian stood in the yard with a sack of dried beans. Cecilia asked him to take it to the kitchen and went to meet the men carrying papers. There were no shackles in the carriage, only two layers of clean cushions. Somehow those chilled him. Someone had already imagined her sitting there.

The messenger produced identification from the Temple's current dispatch office. He was serving an assembly order on a designated bearer. It invoked the compulsory conscription branch Oswin had activated after the deadline and required Cecilia to report to an external registration office the next morning. It claimed to serve the Crown of Destiny's emergency procedure. A person still had to read it, another had to deliver it, and others would have to enforce it.

Cecilia didn't immediately take the pen. She had the hall's attendant check the messenger's identity and asked whether her shift must end now.

“The carriage is ready. You may come now.”

“May isn't must. The order says tomorrow morning.”

He conceded that today was delivery, not authority to transport her early. She had the recorder note that beside the acknowledgment. Then she arranged the established relief for her duties while she dealt with the papers.

Adrian handed the beans to a kitchen worker instead of carrying them over as a useless barrier.

The second page contained five designated numbers. The public copy omitted body locations and private examination information; this delivery identified Cecilia's entry. Orders for the others used their respective addresses and channels. This document didn't prove that all had already arrived.

Above her name was designated bearer. Adrian remembered old records described as fate's arrangements. The paper didn't glow. The carriage wheels didn't move by themselves. Coercion was becoming real through ordinary steps.

“You may record that I've received the demand,” Cecilia said. “Not that I've agreed to participate.”

The messenger showed her the receipt. Its single signature space followed informed of and accepting the arrangements.

She pushed it back.

Adrian expected anger. The man merely sounded tired. Every copy was printed that way; he couldn't alter the form. Without a signature, he would have to record refusal of delivery.

“I've read it here, in front of you. Refusal of delivery isn't what happened.”

They reached an impasse beneath the porch. People gathered while the kitchen's soup still needed tending. A woman with a child hesitated on the steps, afraid to pass between them. Cecilia had the attendant clear a route. Resistance to a document shouldn't obstruct someone's lunch.

Could an independent witness record delivery while Cecilia attached a separate refusal of participation? Adrian asked. The messenger needed to consult his superior. They waited while his assistant went to a nearby liaison point. Lack of an immediate answer wasn't permission.

When Elena arrived, she first saw the woman carrying her bowl out of the kitchen. She asked where the actual papers were and whether anyone was attempting early enforcement.

Cecilia gave her the copy but continued speaking for herself. Elena gathered what a lawyer would need. She didn't say, I'm taking you away. Otherwise the personal answer Cecilia had insisted upon would become ornamental.

“I rather want to block that carriage with a Rosenberg one,” Elena murmured.

“Then the kitchen's grain cart couldn't get in.”

“I know. That's why I'm telling you instead.”

Cecilia gave her a brief smile. “Thank you for not putting the wish into practice.”

The reply authorized a separate explanation. The original receipt would state delivery only, with acceptance inapplicable; Cecilia and a witness would check the attached page. She read everything before acknowledging receipt and attaching her objection to designation replacing consent, with a request for independent review.

It didn't cancel the reporting order or guarantee that nobody would enforce it tomorrow. Adrian swallowed at least you're safe and noted the next time an actual answer was needed.

The carriage left without a passenger. Its clean cushions stayed empty.

During the afternoon, reports accumulated. Elena received hers through her household registry. Isabella used the ordinary contact channel; being a princess hadn't exempted her. Viola's copy reached her lawful residence, not as a military recall. Luna received a limited delivery, with a physician's separate statement of movement and work restrictions. Appearing on the same list didn't suddenly enable her to walk all day.

The five times and destinations formed an interlocking arrangement. Their numbers matched the external organization in the verified old protocol. An independent academy examiner reported that several maintenance offices had also received orders suspending new alternative-configuration applications and reserving designated transfer positions. This didn't establish that the core had opened. It showed that conscription was more than an isolated threat.

The old institution was clearing paths before requiring people to fill them.

Adrian proposed printing every development behind yesterday's declaration. Elena refused to turn addresses, tomorrow's movements, and medical arrangements into a tracking list. They could publish that conscription had begun and cite verifiable document numbers. Personal responses remained each person's decision.

Cecilia agreed, but objected that everything is being handled through procedure would sound like acceptance. Together they composed a brief statement: compulsory assignment demands received; delivery does not constitute consent; objections submitted; ordinary protection and relief continue.

Neither woman abandoned what mattered to her for the appearance of unity.

“You noticed whom the itinerary could hurt before I did,” Cecilia told Elena.

“You heard what received could be turned into before I did.”

There was no grand reconciliation. Elena copied the references clearly; Cecilia caught one missing digit. They handed the results to the actual recipients. Adrian read the short statement again. Elena had not sacrificed privacy to sound defiant; Cecilia had not allowed careful wording to conceal their refusal. Their agreement was useful precisely because it still contained both objections, rather than requiring one woman to be silent for the other.

Before filing the objections, a clerk asked whether all five could use one declaration. Fewer pages would be quicker. Luna's reply, brought by her assistant, was precise: agreement against designated sacrifice didn't authorize another person to describe her ability to travel. She could check wording only within her short work period. Urgency hadn't removed her right-palm injury.

Adrian separated common facts from individual attachments. Viola didn't decide medical limits for Luna. Isabella didn't make her own travel conditions universal. Each checked her section before submission.

By the time the last page returned, the planned messenger's shift was changing. Adrian explained the sealed packet and destination to the replacement instead of saying the previous man understood. He waited for the correct receiving office to be repeated before letting it leave the desk.

Later, an academy attendant brought another problem. An outside equipment office had requested transport priority for conscription supplies. Someone assumed Isabella had agreed to coordinate and withdrew an ordinary cart from tomorrow's flour delivery. Her name appeared in an attachment. Her consent did not.

Isabella requested the original for checking rather than verbally ordering every cart restored. The issuer confirmed that the demand concerned listed specialist transport, not this civilian vehicle. During allocation, priority had been expanded into everything.

A correction went to the owner. Restoring the flour order still depended on whether the cart could reach loading in time. The driver had accepted another half-route and wouldn't erase the hours the disruption had cost him. Academy liaison staff checked schedules and charges. Nobody called him selfish for wanting his working time accounted for.

Adrian considered the crossed-out flour order. Compulsion wasn't confined to five names. It spread into people, vehicles, jobs, and time no one had asked about. Correcting one cart hadn't stopped the institution. It might keep tomorrow's kitchen from losing an arrangement that belonged to it.

At a short meeting, Viola reminded them to retain verifiable destinations for their objections. She would contact existing civilian witnesses to ask who wished to observe document exchanges. Their earlier consent to watch a trial couldn't be repurposed as willingness to confront guards.

Her left arm rested securely. With her right hand she turned an entrance diagram toward the staff.

“If someone comes tomorrow, don't surround them or block exits. Ask the leader for the current authority to act. Notify the lawyer and the person authorized to receive it. Record what you actually see.”

What if they wouldn't listen? a young guard asked. Viola didn't promise to protect everyone with one injured arm. She pointed out the local duty officers already contacted and the emergency medical destination. Witnesses and assistance weren't guarantees against danger.

For once, Adrian didn't hear can't guarantee as retreat. He made his own assignments smaller and more definite. He could identify a receiving office, keep a record of what had arrived, and make sure someone knew where to seek help. He could not honestly write that those preparations removed every possibility of force.

Cecilia returned to complete her relief-hall handover at dusk. Elena accompanied her to the porch without requiring Adrian to follow as proof of whom he cared about more. He stayed at the registry, waiting for the lawyer's actual reply about tomorrow's assignment.

Outside, someone remarked that if the five ladies would simply go, many other people could be spared trouble. The speaker wasn't snarling. He was a repairer carrying a broken lampshade, with children in a poorly protected district. He was genuinely afraid.

Adrian asked whether he'd received notice that ordinary maintenance would stop tonight. No, he'd heard all workers would be reassigned tomorrow. Adrian had the contact officer verify actual shifts and brought back confirmation that ordinary maintenance continued. Any later change would need examination of its specific scope.

The man still hoped they wouldn't delay until everyone suffered. Adrian couldn't promise the new model would be ready. He acknowledged the fear without conceding that designated people therefore lost their right to ask why.

After the repairer left, Adrian sat on the bench for a long time. Making someone an enemy was easier than listening to his fear. Exhaustion wasn't a reason to choose the easier task.

Before nightfall, the urgent objection received a formal acknowledgment. Movement and assignment enforcement were stayed until the following day's examination concluded. Existing lawful residences and medical arrangements remained in place. The underlying conscription decision was still under review, not withdrawn. A second notice confirmed that reset preparation would open its timing record on April 8.

One page let them remain where they were tonight. The other brought tomorrow closer.

Adrian sent the precise result to everyone before returning to Elena. Had he eaten? No. She stood and suggested dinner, neither a victory feast nor a last meal.

He followed her. Behind them, a field on the registry receipt remained blank: consent to bear the risk.

Tonight, nobody had filled it for them.
