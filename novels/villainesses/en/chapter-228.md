# Chapter 228: The Main Gauge Stayed Below the Line

On March 27, outside the academy's test room, Adrian discovered he'd prepared a sentence for a celebration.

It was barely a sentence: This time, it works.

He hadn't seen today's apparatus. He had already reserved a place for those words.

Elena held his ordinary coat and asked why he was staring at the doorplate.

“Deciding what to say first if it succeeds.”

“First ask whether everyone else saw the same success.”

She handed the coat to the attendant without supplying a more modest celebration. Adrian secured a loose sleeve ribbon that might catch a table, then followed the responsible staff inside.

The rig was smaller than its diagram. Nodes A and B were newly made, nonliving substitutes. Independent, limited-energy wafers each had a cap and cutoff, connected through one short transfer path approved for examination. Nothing led underground, to the five locks, an ancient energy case, or anyone's mark. Observers stayed behind the clear line outside a transparent shield.

Luna sat in a high-backed chair, her right palm cushioned, her left hand also spared operation. She would dictate and observe, not become an exception allowed to rush at equipment. A professional operator controlled the supply; an independent safety officer could stop it separately. Adrian read timing cards and recorded visible actions. His scar remained under his sleeve, part of no circuit.

The civilian works employee had previously agreed only to observe preparations. Today he separately received an explanation of operating risks, the observation position, and the recording scope, then freely agreed to witness this one operation. Anyone declining that additional role could leave. He took his designated place. The witness willing only to inspect records would read them later. Their different agreements retained different jobs. Viola checked her exit and departure time before sitting. Isabella supported her left wrist and used her right hand to move her pen out of another observer's sightline.

During setup, she noticed that her nameplate was larger than the others. The attendant explained that it was customary for royal visitors and easier to read.

She turned it over with her right hand and requested an ordinary one. She wasn't disowning her name. The plate obstructed the side witness's view of Node B. A polite custom had nearly become an unexamined equipment condition.

At Viola's request, the works employee checked the position himself. Seated there, he could see the main gauge but not the small local window. Rather than demonstrate trust by writing everything visible, he accepted a side chair inside the staff-designated safe area.

Adrian moved his own timing table instead of asking anyone to cross the line. This put Elena farther away, beyond the easy glance that would find her nodding. He hesitated, then steadied the table legs.

She didn't cross over to fill the space. From her position she indicated that she could see the exit and hear the safety officer. Accompanying him didn't require crowding beside him.

Setup took longer. The administrator amended the actual start time without automatically extending the end limit. Luna's departure remained unchanged. The remaining window accommodated this single approved brief trial, not an improvised repeat. Reading the permit again, Adrian stopped considering those minutes wasted.

Before the start, the works employee asked whether a poor outcome would make his attendance allowance not worth claiming.

“The agreed terms stand,” Adrian said, embarrassed.

“I know. I wanted everyone at the table to hear it again.”

Luna requested that confirmation recorded. The operator read back the total cap, maximum duration, and stopping conditions. Independent cutoffs had been checked within their approved scope before assembly; those records lay nearby. Today allowed one small transfer simulation, not revised parameters and another attempt if the first numbers disappointed.

“Further questions?” the safety officer asked.

Adrian wanted to ask whether, with the main gauge below its limit, they could wait another moment to see the transfer clearly. He'd expressed that tendency during planning. The approved duration allowed no extension. He wouldn't hide desire inside an apparently reasonable question now.

He shook his head, then raised his hand and admitted the thought, asking that they keep the original stop.

“Record the wish,” Luna said without smiling. “Don't execute it.”

Cecilia had left before preparations ended to keep her meal-service shift. Her questions remained with the clerk; Adrian wouldn't declare her satisfied on her behalf. Someone suggested waiting for her return to make a joint attendance record. Viola pointed out that five simultaneous presences weren't required, and a later arrival couldn't be listed as witnessing the start. The administrator recorded separate attendance periods rather than letting a complete list replace who had actually seen what.

Looking at those uneven times, Adrian set aside his wish for everyone to welcome good news together. If there was a result worth knowing, absent people could learn it after work or rest without surrendering an entire day to this table.

A faint light appeared in Node A's groove. No revelation, no sudden pain in a human body. The operator announced readings; Adrian marked the indicated times. The main needle rose and settled below the limiting line.

They entered the approved transfer sequence.

Node B's confirmation window lagged by a beat.

Adrian saw the delay, but his thoughts went first to the main gauge. Steady. In discussion he'd suggested that a brief wait might matter little while the total remained unchanged. The approved plan retained a waiting limit without fully establishing every branch's transient behavior. He wasn't the designer. His suggestion had nevertheless entered their discussion and helped make a smooth diagram reassuring.

“The edge color at B,” Luna said suddenly.

The operator checked the local indicator. An unplanned darkening marked one side of the substitute wafer; the local line had crossed its limit first. The safety officer called an immediate stop. The operator cut supply, and the separate cutoff was confirmed down too.

The main gauge still hadn't crossed its own line.

A delicate crack sounded inside the shield, like a piece of dry sugar snapping. B's brittle substitute split along an edge. Fragments dropped into the tray, leaving a scorch on the insulating pad. Nothing escaped the shield. Nobody was hurt. Neither did anything permit them to rename the failure a success.

Adrian stopped reading time.

“Keep recording,” Luna said.

His grip on the pen had tightened. Nobody could open the shield until the safety officer confirmed zero residual energy and a safe handling temperature. They waited while an ordinary bell sounded outside. Time hadn't slowed for their missed beat.

Viola reminded him to mark where he'd broken off, not reconstruct a seamless recital afterward. He wrote the gap and cited the independent recorder's timings separately.

“I can read it again.”

“Read. Not rerun,” Luna said.

Her voice was steady, her complexion less so. Asked by the medical observer whether she needed to end, she chose the few remaining allotted minutes to describe what she'd seen, then leave without extending.

A local anomaly preceding any main-gauge limit was an observed fact. How delayed confirmation related to heat at B required inspection and comparison. Their proximity didn't assign every cause at once. An aggregate reading had never guaranteed that every narrow path remained within its own capacity.

Adrian looked at the lowered needle and remembered his celebration sentence. It persisted as an embarrassing empty place in his mind.

The works employee insisted on retaining his recorded beat. From the side he'd seen one confirmation window change later than another, but couldn't place the crack precisely before or after the main cutoff fell. The clerk didn't demand an answer convenient for assigning blame.

Luna had her exact words, edge color, recorded. She hadn't fully diagnosed the failure as it happened. Wanting the alternative desperately didn't make her observation cleverer than it had been.

“I thought I'd comfort you first,” Adrian said quietly.

“Later is possible. For now, don't defend the broken wafer.”

He nodded.

Before leaving, her assistant placed the diagram folder in plain sight. Luna asked Adrian for her shawl, not another page to extend her work. With permission he settled it over her shoulders, unusually careful. If he kept looking at her like a fragment, she said, she would require a separate note confirming that she hadn't shattered.

He managed a brief, genuine smile.

“I know you're unhappy.”

“Very. Please preserve that too.”

She left with her assistant. Adrian remained, passing her technical questions to the responsible staff. Affection and respect couldn't erase this morning from her failure record.

The afternoon inspection added no energy. Safety staff opened the shield, documented fragment positions and the scorched pad, then sealed them separately. The substitute was damaged; localized heat discoloration marked the branch. A possible material defect at the connection remained to be examined. Borrowed clamps needed checking before return, even without a crack visible to the naked eye.

Nobody immediately wrote a final cause. The engineering lecturer separated main and local readings, identifying inadequate observation of brief local burdens during transfer. Any later proposal required better monitoring and controls. Shortening today's wait alone couldn't be called a repair.

Adrian produced the discussion page where he'd advocated a shorter confirmation interval, thinking it would reduce the unsupported gap.

The lecturer read it. That judgment did require examination, he said. The design and its approval also carried separate responsibilities. Adrian standing up and saying everything was his fault couldn't make actual engineering and inspection vanish.

It hurt and left him nowhere to hide. He'd almost offered to take everything so others would stop asking questions. Now he had to describe accurately what he'd actually done.

At the final inventory, the administrator asked whether the unused spare should stay mounted.

Adrian nearly said, Since it's ready. Instead he requested its return to controlled storage as unused material. It wouldn't be used today.

Failure didn't grant an extra attempt. Resources contributed yesterday weren't deposits requiring a favorable outcome today.

Elena waited on the porch that evening with his coat. He told her first that nobody was injured, then that the model had failed. The order wasn't meant to soften the second fact. She needed the first.

She nodded without embracing him and declaring he'd done wonderfully.

“Would you rather be alone tonight?”

“No. I might not say much.”

“We can eat together and say less.”

Taking his coat, he found the sleeve ribbon still secured where he'd put it that morning. Some things hadn't moved all day. The wafer couldn't be used again. The clamp's owner would have to wait.

On the way home he asked the driver to pause outside the repair shop. He only asked when the estimator would arrive tomorrow, not for a reassuringly small figure now.

Elena waited in the carriage. When he returned, she asked whether he'd decided to cover every loss privately.

He shook his head. “First inventory it. Explain who proposed what, what we borrowed. Count only money actually available.”

“Tomorrow, then. Tonight, eat.”

He held the hand she willingly offered, without making comfort an exemption from explanation.

This time, it works finally yielded its place. He needed to remember not the gauge that had stayed below its line, but the real broken piece behind the shield.
