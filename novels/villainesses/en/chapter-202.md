# Chapter 202: She Didn't Withdraw the Name

Elena arrived early on March 1 without collecting Adrian's file.

He found her by the judicial repository's window, looking into the rain-washed yard. Separate collection slips rested on the empty chair beside her. When he took his, she said the handwriting examiner had arrived.

“Have you already looked?”

“No. Only checked today's permitted scope.”

He nodded. She didn't insist she'd changed and shouldn't be questioned. He didn't apologize for asking. Their new arrangement had to work under strain, not only when they felt gentle.

An additional high table stood inside. A handover counterfoil lay secured in a shallow frame beside the duty receiving register and material-comparison report. The entire missing circumstances attachment hadn't suddenly reappeared. The technical report still lacked pages; the damaged detention duration hadn't healed overnight.

After explaining those limits, the custodian opened the actual new page.

A folded return counterfoil had once belonged between the old application and intake. Early cataloging had separated it with other receipts without clearly cross-referencing its destination. Following the original transfer subnumber located it in the same batch's return folder. New to their eyes, not newly produced from nothing.

Half an impressed seal along its edge corresponded to the old receiving page. The custodian also displayed original numbering and accession entries from two independent directories. A neat fit couldn't replace provenance.

Transferring party present in person.

Elena Rosenberg's name followed, with a signature confirming handover. The person delivered was Adrian Reed. Not a request to locate him. Not an opinion mailed to court. He had arrived there and been handed into receiving custody by her.

Elena said nothing. Her thumb pressed against the knuckles of her other hand. Adrian noticed, then looked back at the paper. He didn't want his own hurt accompanied by the duty to measure whether hers was worse.

The examiner compared the old testimony, handover signature, and verified routine household correspondence from the same historical grouping. Those samples had independent continuous dispatch records. Today's signature wasn't certifying every century. Joined strokes, pressure changes, and short correction signatures supported a common writer; material layers showed no sign of present-day replacement. The precise old year remained unconverted.

Handwriting wasn't infallible. Combined with independent attendance and handover records, however, the evidence no longer reasonably supported explaining everything as a coincidental name or later copying mistake.

“Please read the duty register too,” Elena said.

The receiving officer recorded Lady Rosenberg accompanying the person to be questioned and requesting judicial custody. That person objected to continued restraint and asked to leave. Acting under a separate intake authorization, the officer moved him into the waiting room and required the transferring party to confirm completion on the counterfoil.

She had confirmed it.

No entry said she'd locked him up herself or ordered execution. But when he wanted to leave, she hadn't withdrawn the handover request. Her choice subsequently joined testimony and a technical report among grounds for continued detention.

Adrian's gaze caught on objected.

His earlier self had said no.

He heard no recovered voice. His scar didn't heat; no courtroom unfolded around him. Paper finally preserved an action previously hidden inside the abstraction of judicial intake. Someone brought him. He wanted to leave. Someone else still finished signing.

Elena requested a pause without leaving. Once her voice steadied, she asked whether the transferring party had issued the custody authorization. No: a judicial officer had signed separately. She asked that distinction be retained, without seizing it as proof that another person's custody decision erased her delivery of him.

“My application, her testimony, this handover.” She corrected herself. “The application and testimony made under my identity in the old file. Please explain their connections together.”

Her face paled at the slip. Adrian understood the correction. She had no memories. Pain couldn't quietly stitch today and that earlier day into one.

The custodian read back the relationship. Different offices' records supported the actual transfer. They didn't establish that she knew every subsequent measure or the full technical material available to her. Intake wasn't blanket approval of all requests; handover wasn't a final judgment.

The handover had nevertheless happened.

The first excerpt carried the heading Assisted Appearance. Adrian read it twice without accepting a pen.

“This makes it sound as though I agreed.”

The copying clerk explained that the classification didn't mean everyone arrived voluntarily. The objection remained in the body. Nothing had been deleted.

Adrian saw it there. He also remembered the street notice: a confident heading read first, qualifications treated as inconvenient details. He wanted judicial transfer in the heading so readers wouldn't begin with the opposite impression.

The clerk hesitated. Standard format. Elena requested the same treatment for her excerpt. Not because a noble preferred particular wording: the original handover included an objection, while assisted risked combining incompatible intentions.

A supervisor inspected the material and permitted the neutral Transfer and Handover, placing the objection reference beside the quotation. Today's request didn't erase the old classification from the original register. Their excerpts no longer allowed one broad term to swallow the difference.

Adrian waited for the ink to dry. He didn't thank her as though this act could cancel another Elena's recorded choice. She didn't look for thanks, merely asking the clerk to recheck her name. Accurate wording would still carry unfavorable facts about her. She couldn't value it solely for protecting him.

As the last page turned, her fingers approached its edge without crossing the custodian's boundary. Her name could remain while the paper stayed outside her grasp. Acknowledgment didn't require taking everything into her hands again.

During the break Adrian stepped into the gallery. A draft lifted a registration sheet. He held it until the attendant brought a weight. Only after completing that tiny action did he realize he hadn't been breathing properly.

Elena stopped several steps away and asked whether she could speak.

“Yes. Don't begin by saying she must have meant to save me.”

“I won't.”

She watched the strip of light beside the window without a prepared defense.

“I haven't suddenly remembered. Reading those lines wasn't remembering them myself. But enough independent records now agree that I can't keep offering the possibility that someone borrowed my name.”

She looked at him.

“In that cycle, I handed you to the court. You objected. I still completed the handover. Later I testified, and those words helped keep you there.”

Footsteps continued in the corridor. Someone approached the wrong counter; an attendant redirected them. The world didn't isolate the couple in silence for her admission.

A chair stood along the gallery. Elena asked the attendant whether she could use it, then sat and gathered her own skirt. She looked pale enough that Adrian instinctively moved a step closer before stopping.

“Water?” he asked.

“Yes. I'll ask for some.”

She didn't refuse care as punishment or wait for him to provide it. The attendant brought a cup; she thanked him and drank slowly. Adrian still worried about her steadiness. That concern hadn't compelled him to pronounce forgiveness. Both feelings could inhabit him at once, harder than choosing only love or only hatred, closer to what they actually possessed today.

She put the cup down and waited for him to speak again.

Adrian withdrew his hand from the sill.

“Are you saying that so I'll tell you it wasn't you?”

She answered slowly. “I hoped you might. But acknowledgment can't become a way of making you comfort me.”

“I know you don't have those memories now.”

“Knowing can still hurt.”

It hurt more to hear that. She hadn't given him an obviously wrong answer he could simply fight. Here was the woman who reminded him soup was cooling and laughed at his crooked scarf, bearing a name that couldn't easily be torn out of history.

“The objection is the worst part. I don't know how much he said. Perhaps he'd explained he wasn't refusing questions, only refusing to be held that way.”

Her hands tightened, then loosened.

“I can't answer for him. Or fill the missing words with certainty that he was unreasonable.”

“What about you? Did she hear him?”

“The officer recorded it, and she was there. Beyond that, I don't yet know.”

He looked into the yard. Somebody opened an umbrella, realized the rain had stopped, and awkwardly closed it. On another day he might have told Elena two people could share an umbrella in sunshine; they'd simply look foolish. The thought remained, unable to cross the few steps between them.

She didn't use his silence to close the distance.

“What I can answer for now is not selecting material to make myself comfortable. And my delay on February 15. That belonged to me in this timeline. The heavier old event can't become somewhere to hide it.”

He nodded. He didn't need her to carry everything she couldn't remember in compensation for the delay she knew she'd chosen. Keeping the responsibilities separate didn't prevent either from hurting them.

“I don't know how to face you immediately.”

Her eyes reddened. She didn't ask whether that meant he no longer cared.

“You don't have to know immediately.”

Called back inside, they separately signed for authorized excerpts. Elena's supplementary assessment accepted that current material supported actual transfer by her historical self. It retained questions about what she'd known, the scope of custody decisions, and later procedure. She didn't turn her statement into Adrian's consent or today's legal judgment against herself.

Adrian requested retention of his earlier objection and the surviving gaps. Neither signed for the other.

Outside they encountered Cecilia returning from an unrelated ordinary submission. She noticed the distance without demanding to know who should apologize first. Elena asked her to walk to the coach with her. Adrian wasn't required to share it.

He wanted a short walk and time to settle his papers.

“When we're home, I'll tell you whether I want to talk,” he said.

Elena agreed. Neither the announcement of an ending nor a guarantee that trust had returned.

A stall sold the first spring branches, their blossoms not fully open. Adrian passed without buying something to make the room awaiting them easier to enter. A gift couldn't substitute for unfinished words today.

He reached the dry path outside the house and sat on a bench facing the entrance. His earlier self had wanted to leave a doorway; the old Elena had finished signing. Today she let him decide when to leave and return. The difference was real. Reality didn't make it an immediate remedy for pain.

On the back of his paper he finally wrote the first question for tomorrow: What had she believed the court could do, and what had she ignored?

Not an answer he would supply for her today.

By dusk her coach had returned. Nobody came to hurry him indoors. He put the paper away, stood slowly, and went to his own room.

She had acknowledged what that name had done. Now they had to decide how to speak the next sentence without erasing it.
