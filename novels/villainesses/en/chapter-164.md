# Chapter 164: She Brought No Bargaining Chip

Isabella did not put the letter before Adrian and require him to prove that he objected first.

On the afternoon of January 29, she agreed to answer its sender at their appointed meeting place. He wanted her to bring an expression of negotiable intent. She brought only his letter.

“Empty hands are lighter.”

Adrian glanced at her bandaged left wrist. “If you need me there, I can answer for myself.”

“He needs to know this is my decision. Not that you're preventing me from obtaining support.”

After a pause, she asked whether Adrian would have tea with her afterward. Without conditions attached, the invitation sounded less confident than her politics.

He agreed to wait nearby after delivering his own papers. Viola had offered to check part of his carriage route. Told of the change, she explained that she had only fifteen minutes before rest. They narrowed the question to what actually needed checking instead of keeping her seated indefinitely for another person's meeting.

Two noble representatives awaited Isabella. One conveyed his family's position; the other understood parliamentary procedure. Neither knelt. Her temporary coordination badge had been returned. A meeting could not restore expired powers.

Three cups stood on the table. A narrow envelope contained the proposed arrangement.

No valid betrothal had been made for her. This was a request for preliminary intent, trading the possibility of a future marriage for support of procedural reform and continued investigation. Candidates and property could be negotiated. Submission to the common interest, however, appeared beyond discussion.

“No candidate has been settled,” the first man emphasized. “We aren't forcing a particular person upon you.”

Isabella turned to the last page with her right hand.

“It asks me to accept beforehand that someone may decide for me at the end.”

The second man called it traditional wording. Nobles paid for their birth too, especially royal women. Why treat an unidentified future partner as a present injury?

She remembered how naturally she had once offered terms that could not be refused. If the exchange was generous enough, refusal seemed merely poor arithmetic.

“I used to think that too.”

His expression softened, anticipating compromise.

“Which is why I cannot simply say that this price is unsuitable.”

She returned the envelope to the table without tearing it. They could retain their proposal. She would retain the letter and her reply.

“I authorize no one to exchange my marriage for political support. Nor will I treat Adrian's consent, or anyone else's, as an attachment to be supplied later. The coercive marriage bargains I once advocated stop with me.”

Outside, a wheel crossed a gap in the paving. A cup lid rattled in the quiet room.

Refusing this route could close another, the representative warned. Investigation needed funding, people able to secure hearings, and supporters willing to leave their names on controversial proposals. Personal preference could not open every door.

“The missing people's families cannot wait while you demonstrate a principle.”

Her fingers tightened around her cup. Eight missing adults, six families: not numbers she could decorate a position with. He had found precisely what she feared.

“Will your family's previously offered ordinary document checking be withdrawn because I refuse this expression of intent?”

He avoided her eyes. He would have to ask again.

“Then please record that change clearly too.”

The procedural representative objected to turning a private meeting into an accusation. She wasn't doing that, she said. They could withdraw support they had not committed to provide. They could not imply that the families had refused help or treat searches already accepted by existing institutions as her private possessions to surrender.

She had no silver badge to strike against the table. She could only finish asking what they were prepared to answer.

How would she explain this to people expecting royal help, the second man asked.

“First, by not announcing support I haven't obtained.”

He produced a draft introduction stating that preliminary understanding had been reached. She requested that sentence be removed. They certainly understood each other, but not in the sense his wording implied. Irritated, he said the draft wouldn't be sent directly. Then this was the easiest time to correct it, she replied.

She watched him cross it out before marking her own notes not accepted. The empty space looked meager. No two parties were joining hands through a crisis; no royal promise deserved a headline.

“You may not receive these terms again.”

“I understand. Don't record today's visit as willingness to wait for another offer.”

Her voice stayed level, but her right hand pressed against her coat. Adrian was not in this room to share the silence that followed. She waited for the letter to be put away and bore it herself.

Ultimately, they retained one opportunity for ordinary document explanation, without promising votes or help expediting the original ledger. The additional introduction was canceled with the marriage proposal.

Isabella requested an accurate written response. She had made no new reformist friend. No more generous ally immediately appeared to prove that refusal always paid.

At the door, she discovered she was still holding the empty cup.

“I seem to have taken something I shouldn't.”

“Cups are easier to retrieve than promises,” the attendant murmured.

Her brief smile was real.

Adrian waited in the agreed tea shop, an empty chair beside the window. He had ordered nothing for her. Asked how long he'd waited, he said less than expected: the delivery office had not demanded a third copy.

“Congratulations.”

“Should I hear the bad news first?”

“I lost an introduction.”

She unfastened her coat with her right hand. A larger movement tugged at her injured wrist. Stopping, she asked him to free a fold caught against the chair so it no longer pressed on the bandage.

He followed her directions carefully, freeing the fabric rather than pulling on her arm. He helped without using the moment to question her about medical details or asking her to demonstrate how much it hurt.

Finishing her account, she realized it sounded like a report. Adrian poured half a cup of warm tea and asked how she felt.

“Like going back to argue again. Preferably until I prove I wasn't being willful.”

“Do you want me to say you lost nothing?”

“No. I'm afraid you'll say it will all be worth it.”

He put the pot down without saying so.

After studying her tea, she admitted that she still wanted political influence. Rejecting forced marriage did not mean surrendering ambition or deciding to stand quietly behind him.

“I know.”

“One day I may cancel tea for a proposal.”

“Tell me beforehand.”

She looked up. That answer was too small for her prepared debate.

“That's all?”

“If you cancel three times, I may stop leaving the same afternoon empty. Respecting your work doesn't mean I have no plans.”

Isabella laughed, faintly stung. People generally promised to wait or accused her of ambition. He was simply preserving a place in his own afternoon.

“Have you been consulting Viola?”

“She very clearly told me she needed rest after fifteen minutes.”

“The knight may be the best negotiator among us.”

An assistant mistakenly brought the next table's sugar. Isabella returned it and asked for something savory. The bread needed another baking; her presence would not make it appear instantly.

She chose to wait. At a neighboring table, the smaller of two children kept asking when he could eat. His mother divided an old biscuit rather than demanding a separate oven. Isabella kept her own impatient question behind the rim of her cup.

The proprietor brought a handbill recently distributed along the street. Someone had turned her rejection of political marriage into an announcement that she had pledged her heart to a servant. The reasons she had just given had not even been heard, yet the conclusion was already printed.

Adrian's ease disappeared. Without snatching the paper, he asked to borrow it long enough to copy the printer's identification and exact words.

Isabella nearly said this misunderstanding was preferable to coercion. She stopped.

A useful misunderstanding for her was not necessarily fair to him.

“My response will state that I am announcing no relationship with anyone.”

“Don't announce whom I don't like either.”

“I won't.”

She requested no genuine declaration as repayment for correcting the false one. Adrian finally leaned back again.

The proprietor permitted the copy, not promising to recover every handbill. Isabella asked to display her written explanation beside it, leaving replacement to him rather than commanding a shopkeeper. After reading it, he agreed to two days.

The savory bread arrived slightly broken, with a small extra piece from the cook. Isabella studied it.

“Is this today's first compensation that doesn't require me to consider a marriage?”

“The sugar was a mistake too.”

“That doesn't count.”

Laughing, she kept the extra piece on her own plate instead of insisting on feeding him to demonstrate generosity.

On the journey back, Adrian raised an unwelcome question. If safety, investigation, and travel always depended on borrowing a noble's name, everyone would keep being pushed toward the same bargain. Could they define the small things they were actually willing to do without promising a relationship in exchange for protection?

“A contract?”

“Let's not claim powers it can't have. An ordinary agreement. Who contacts whom. Who holds copies. No reply doesn't mean permission to enter someone's bedroom.”

She watched the street. That was weaker than a royal guarantee. It couldn't compel evidence or ensure a safe journey.

“But it needn't pledge you as security.”

“Or any of you.”

At home, Viola had sent a correction: tomorrow's medical review had moved to the morning. Their proposed short midday meeting needed confirmation afterward. She hadn't promised an attendance she couldn't guarantee or left anyone wondering whether she had forgotten.

Isabella read it and quietly observed that an inelegant notice could be reassuring.

Before dinner, she completed her personal statement, withdrawing her own advocacy of political interests compelling someone to accept a betrothal. It governed her proposals, not the kingdom's unchanged law or other women's choices. She kept letter and reply separately, including the lost introduction.

Elena offered help finding alternative supporters.

“A list, please. Not conditions promised before I see them.”

Elena nodded and struck out a guarantee she had begun to write. They did not embrace to celebrate the end of rivalry. Isabella still noticed Elena's intimacy when she looked at Adrian. She could still resent it. She simply refused to turn that resentment into something someone else had to sign.

At the dining table, Adrian spread out ordinary paper. He began not with who belonged to whom, but: If someone needs help tomorrow.

Luna glanced over. “That may be harder to write than a contract to protect the world.”

“Not the world tonight. First, what are you willing to do?”
