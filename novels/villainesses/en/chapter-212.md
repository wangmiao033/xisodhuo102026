# Chapter 212: Reading the First Line Again

On March 11, Adrian brought three sheets of scrap paper and scissors borrowed from the gatehouse into the reading room.

Luna asked whether he intended to rule the breakfast table with a paper Crown of Destiny.

“If I manage it, my first decree will prohibit bread crumbs.”

“A short-lived kingdom,” Isabella said, brushing a crumb off her folder with her right hand before settling her left wrist more comfortably. Bread had already announced its opinion of royal authority.

Today's papers included the cabinet's rear maintenance view. Works staff had checked the accessible connections and maintenance instructions, establishing separate custody and operation for the outer records compartment and the five mark locks. The maintenance key belonged to the repository. Yesterday afternoon's supplementary authorization permitted its own mechanical shutters to move, with both an examiner and a custodian present. It excluded the five locks.

Adrian left the scissors upstairs. Only today's paper needed cutting.

Five locating windows appeared on the narrow case, each with three smaller fields below it. The old writing was reasonably legible. Read from the doorway, however, its references didn't match the surviving directory. Reversing everything made two positions plausible, but the third pointed to a space marked EMPTY.

“Two convenient answers don't entitle us to call the third a copying error,” Luna said.

She remained upstairs rather than compensating for yesterday's absence with an overlong visit. An assistant positioned the drawings within view. Her right palm stayed supported. Belowground, staff had numbered each visible field without repeatedly turning the mechanism to see what happened.

The custodian opened instructions retrieved from the historical maintenance folder. An associated issue slip identified this particular case; nobody had borrowed a diagram of a merely similar cabinet. Its text specified numbering from the rear. The later drawing showed the entrance view. Both directions were real, but the copy lacked the conversion between them.

That explained reversed positions. It didn't explain EMPTY.

Adrian arranged five paper pieces, copied the front-view numbers, and walked around the table. Luna's amusement faded as she watched.

“Where are you starting?”

“At what I thought was the first field.”

“That no longer sounds particularly dependable.”

He knew. He'd automatically written the three little fields as the top, middle, and bottom of one column. The original instructions hadn't joined them with a vertical line. The later drawing had.

He remembered the old judicial return folder. The counterfoil had been filed separately among receipts, without a clear cross-reference from the main volume. Following the order on the page before them hadn't revealed where the paper was actually stored. He asked to see the original page's marginal instructions as well as the extracted half-diagram.

Three abbreviations appeared: gate position, storage compartment, volume number. A final line folded toward the binding. Under the ordinary reading lamp, angled light revealed a small outward-turning arrow. Rather than flatten the crease, the custodian asked the conservator to support the page on a shallower stand.

Those three fields weren't one number continuing downward.

Gate position followed the rear view. Storage compartment and volume number belonged to the custody directory, read normally from the front. Reversing all three reversed information that should have stayed unchanged. The later entrance drawing had tidied them into a column without preserving their different sources.

“Reverse only the first reference,” Adrian said. “That's a guess. Don't touch the cabinet yet.”

He cut out the first field from each column and reversed only those five pieces. The other two fields stayed in their original positions. The reference that had landed on EMPTY recombined to identify a directory on the upper outer-records shelf.

The clerk didn't immediately write down a triumph. She requested a known location first, to test whether the method worked beyond the answer they wanted.

The maintenance instructions included the storage reference for blank servicing sheets. An independent custody inventory recorded their actual location. Adrian rearranged the paper in the same way. The positions agreed. Luna requested a second check, using an old seal-replacement entry unrelated to the five marks. That also matched, without using today's target to work backward.

Adrian stepped away from the table. When playing *Crown of Fate*, he'd expected a correct option that wouldn't be questioned after he selected it. Here, paper creased. Copyists omitted explanations. His first idea had been half wrong. Without the clerk's demand for a known-position test, he might already have hurried downstairs, excited.

“Include what you asked,” he told her. “My thinking of it didn't prove it.”

She looked up, then added the cross-check request. Nobody had stolen his discovery. Nor did everyone else's work need to shrink into applause behind him. The scissors lay beside discarded paper; his scar hadn't warmed or delivered a memory of somebody reading this cabinet.

“I imagined deciphering secrets would look more impressive.”

“You were impressive walking around the table,” Luna said. “You nearly hit the chair twice.”

Isabella indicated the chair and asked an assistant to move it inward. Her practical response made composure harder to pretend. They retained the original order, failed reading, and both checks. The paper pieces were an illustration, not ancient evidence.

Near noon the custodian took the maintenance key below. Two duty staff verified its seal before issue; it would be returned immediately after use. Adrian neither carried it nor touched a mark lock.

He followed to the authorized observation point. Elena and Cecilia accompanied him. Viola remained on the level outer gallery, checking people in and out with her left arm still supported. Luna would receive the agreed short written report. Her contribution didn't require proof through physical attendance.

The examiner rechecked the disconnected old interface and independent stop. Today's movement concerned only the outer compartment's shutters. The custodian inserted the key into a side opening and turned it gently. A thin copper strip slid right.

No light spilled from the core door. The five lock positions remained unchanged.

Three narrow shelves appeared behind the locating window. The upper one matched their paper arrangement. Instead of feeling blindly inside, the custodian used the prescribed short support fork to draw out the case resting at its front edge. A small metallic sound made Adrian tense.

Elena noticed. She didn't tell him this differed from the brass handle, therefore he couldn't be frightened.

“Further back?”

“Yes.”

They moved clear of the workers. He wouldn't miss the result. He needn't stand on the foremost available brick to demonstrate improved courage.

The case held a slim directory and separate mechanical reading instructions. The custodian recorded its position, orientation, and condition before bringing it to the outer-gallery table. They'd solved the retrieval sequence for this first record, not the operation of everything beyond the door.

The opening line read: GATE POSITIONS AND NAME REGISTERS KEPT SEPARATELY. READING DOES NOT OPEN THE MAIN DOOR.

Adrian read it twice. Yesterday he'd feared that learning more could only mean sending people toward those locks. Here, at least, they had found another route that had actually existed.

The next column contained volume locations, not personal names. The first volume, NOBLE REGISTER, pointed to a sealed item in a shallow outer shelf. The custodian checked that today's authorization covered external examination there. Using the short support fork, he retrieved the intact wrapper by its reference, recording its original position and seal. The pages remained enclosed. Nobody opened them.

The wrapper's title matched the directory. A further notation concerned burden registration, but its fold concealed the rest. The custodian didn't tear it open for an audience.

Elena immediately asked to read the volume.

“The directory and outer wrapper fall within today's examination,” the custodian said. “The inner volume contains personal material. Reading needs separate registration. We can move the sealed item to the upstairs repository, rather than open it belowground.”

“I want to apply.”

She spoke quickly. Adrian's first impulse was to tell her to wait.

She noticed. “Are you afraid I'll find Rosenberg?”

“I'm afraid you'll decide to carry everything inside before you've read it.”

She looked back at the yellowing wrapper. NOBLE remained unmistakable.

“I want to know where my family stands. Not only in the place you're afraid of. Perhaps in another I'd rather not admit. If we always investigate what happened to others, then suddenly need rest when our own names appear, that isn't caution.”

He didn't agree immediately. She didn't claim family interest entitled her to silence him. Water dripped into the gallery drain while they waited. Finally he asked whether the custodian could explain the scope before she chose how much to read. He wasn't asking her to seek his permission.

“Yes. And you don't have to accompany me.”

He nodded. That still hurt a little. Not being obliged to carry everything also meant she would make decisions that didn't require him beside her. He couldn't value freedom only when it belonged to him.

When the news reached the upstairs reading room, Isabella insisted that the retrieval request accurately identify the noble volume, rather than disguise it as miscellaneous old papers. She also opposed immediately publishing the wrapper's image so strangers could guess guilt or obligation from its title.

Cecilia smiled wearily. “Writing everything out and stopping people from inventing more seem to be the two hardest things to do together.”

“At least you haven't been asked to pray that paper won't be misread,” Luna said.

“I'd rather ask its readers to slow down.”

By Viola's return, the maintenance key had been handed back. The lock covers had been restored according to custody requirements, with new seals documented. After hearing about the directory, she first asked whether the next visit's evacuation route remained reserved. Names that might soon emerge couldn't displace surface safety arrangements.

Isabella then received a response to the risk inquiry. Relevant staff were still checking supplementary material. No new conclusion justified promising there was no danger outside. She placed the unwelcome slip beside the directory copy instead of declaring today's success a refutation of everything Oswin had said.

Adrian studied the two sheets. His excitement settled into something firmer. They genuinely knew one more thing. Much remained beyond it.

That afternoon the upstairs repository formally received the sealed item. Nobody opened the inner volume. Elena's limited reading application was scheduled for the next morning, when staff would first explain its scope and the handling of private material. She folded her own receipt rather than giving it to Adrian to keep.

Before leaving, he gathered the five cut paper pieces. Luna asked whether he intended to preserve his kingdom.

“For the next person who mistakes three kinds of reference for one.”

“Then perhaps leave off Long Live the King.”

He laughed and filed the illustration with today's working papers instead of taking it home as a private trophy. The scissors went back to the gatehouse. Scraps went into the wastebasket. Finding a secret still left somebody to clean the table.

Elena waited at the doorway. Would he walk to the repository with her tomorrow?

“Yes. Once we're there, you can decide whether you'd like me beside you.”

“All right.” She paused. “Tonight, hot food first. Your successful decisions today don't include postponing lunch until the afternoon.”

“Will that assessment appear in the directory?”

“In my own memory.”

Something stirred quietly in him. No fragment recovered through an old object, no earlier record left by somebody else. She remembered today. Tomorrow's decisions would still belong to her.

They had finally read the first record correctly.

Whatever had been written beneath the names waited inside unopened pages.
