# Chapter 237: He Gave Them a Date

On April 5, High Priest Oswin had a bowl of incense removed before the meeting began.

Adrian expected an explanation of some ritual. Oswin merely said that people needed comfortable air; a smell needn't establish the Temple's presence. An attendant carried the bowl out and opened the window a little. An ordinary bell could be heard beyond it.

That didn't make him harmless. It reminded Adrian that the man opposite could recognize another person's discomfort.

The independent recorder checked their scope: current protection, evidence for alternatives, and proposed deadlines. No agreement to bodily risk, no examination, no consent inferred from attendance. Each person confirmed participation separately. Luna's session was short, her right palm cushioned and left hand spared writing. Viola and Isabella hadn't removed their supports to look stronger before the High Priest.

Oswin surveyed the seats and began without demanding a preliminary confession.

First came no list of five names, but actual maintenance reports from outer protection stations. Each identified observation periods, instrument condition, and local reporting staff. An academy examiner had permission to compare available counterparts. Several rising burden records were corroborated; two remained delayed. They supported worsening danger, not uncertainty disguised as precise prophecy.

Oswin accepted that qualification. Using an ordinary wooden pointer, he indicated stations progressively losing maintenance margin. His voice didn't supply missing measurements.

“You don't believe designated sacrifice is right. I know. Do you believe these places are bearing something real?”

Adrian acknowledged the supported danger. He placed the April 1 trial and April 2 ceiling finding on the table without using a small success to conceal the other reports.

“We don't have an apparatus that can replace all existing protection.”

Oswin nodded, as though hearing something long awaited.

“And if there isn't time?”

Adrian didn't immediately answer. His fingers rested on paper thin enough to anger him. The reports were not heavy objects, yet he couldn't move them aside as though they were only an opponent's argument. People outside this room would continue living beneath the conditions those observations described. His anger at Oswin didn't entitle him to forget them, or claim that an unproven machine was already protecting them. Genuine danger didn't lose weight because its speaker supported sacrifice. Refusing cruelty couldn't make the people elsewhere unhurt.

Cecilia asked what time meant: which procedure, what moment, whose decision? Oswin requested the proposed notice.

By noon on April 7, the project must submit an alternative work plan and existing evidence fit for independent examination. Without the relevant acceptance and necessary decisions, the Temple would advance compulsory listing and attendance procedures for marked bearers under existing emergency provisions. External timing for reset preparations could begin April 8. Final action could occur no earlier than noon on April 20, and still required the specified burden thresholds, review, and authority conditions. This wasn't a prophecy of automatic destruction at that hour. Nor could Adrian stop it merely by submitting a pile of paper.

“Your deadline?” Isabella asked.

“I approved the submission and preparation arrangements under present authority. I haven't decided when calamity arrives.”

She required those statements separately recorded. He agreed without withdrawing the dates.

Did attendance already mean bodily connection, Viola asked? The clerk described staged notices and remedies. The actual served document defined current obligations; today's invitation wasn't an execution order. Oswin personally confirmed that nobody would be detained or required to connect because they'd attended this meeting.

Adrian felt relief, then resented needing relief over freedom that shouldn't be a bargaining item.

“Compulsory listing remains compulsory,” Viola said.

“Yes.” Oswin didn't soften the term.

He explained: existing protection depended on long maintenance and bearing capacity. Their model established only a limited simulated quantity. He couldn't wager the capital on a hope lacking field-scale evidence. Shutting down the old system would impose consequences on strangers too. The calamity reports hadn't been fabricated simply to frighten these women.

“So you may designate us?” Elena asked.

“When no usable alternative exists and danger approaches, I consider continuing established arrangements less harmful than permitting it to spread.”

He didn't claim to enjoy it. Adrian almost wished he did. That would have been easier to argue against. Instead, he faced a man prepared to identify the coercion and defend it as the smaller injury. Adrian couldn't defeat that position by discovering that Oswin had failed to notice it hurt. He had noticed. He was choosing it anyway, and calling the choice responsible.

Luna had the earlier independently verified finding passed across: original purpose, later fixed-sacrifice revision, present continuation, separately identified. Would Oswin still call designation of five people the device's only original function?

He read, paused, and conceded that the records didn't support only ever one way. But original replaceable media and maintenance resources were insufficient now. Historical purpose wasn't a working present-day apparatus.

“We've included that objection,” Luna said. “It doesn't erase your preceding admission.”

The recorder read both back. Oswin didn't request a more favorable combined summary.

Adrian asked neither faith in his victory nor suspension by personal promise. Separate alternative assessment from the sacrificial procedure, he proposed. People uninvolved in designation should examine technical evidence and actual preparations. Receipt of evidence mustn't masquerade as approval; failure to approve mustn't prove the designated people had consented. Any coercive document should name its current stage and objection route, not combine listing, movement, and connection into one action.

Independent technical checking could enter the receiving process, Oswin replied. That didn't give anyone Adrian nominated authority to suspend emergency procedures. Competent bodies would consider objections; he wouldn't promise every request success here.

“Then name who decides. Don't write to be handled separately,” Isabella said.

Her sharpest sentence today didn't demand final authority for herself. It demanded that authority become visible. An unnamed later procedure could swallow a person's objection as effectively as an explicit refusal. They needed an office capable of receiving the request, an actual body able to consider it, and a record of which temporary measures could be sought. None guaranteed the outcome. They made the next step something other than asking the same person for kindness again. The clerk entered the receiving and review offices and available applications for interim measures under existing provisions. The independent recorder checked them. Isabella hadn't invented new law.

Luna's allotted time ended. Oswin offered to let her stay longer. She declined. The importance of the subject didn't manufacture usable time in her body. Her assistant secured her copy. Before leaving, she specified that additional matters required later notification; companions wouldn't accept them for her.

The closing door gave Adrian an unexpected steadiness. She hadn't remained to prove she cared. Leaving hadn't removed her from the matter either.

They spent the remaining period checking dates and consequences. April 7 noon was a submission point, not a sacrificial execution time. April 8 was the earliest start for external preparation timing. April 20 noon was the earliest permitted final action, still conditional. It didn't guarantee safety from other calamity before then. Nobody had been given two safe weeks. Nobody could honestly announce tomorrow that final reset had already been irreversibly performed.

Adrian repeated each distinction. His voice wasn't as steady as on entering. He heard the difference himself and continued, giving the recorder time to preserve the distinctions. He preferred admitting fear to mixing three dates into panic dramatic enough to unite everyone.

Before closing, he asked what Oswin would do if independent evidence truly supported an adequate alternative without designating five people.

“I would examine whether it could bear the consequences for which I'm responsible.”

“Not first whether you proposed it?”

“No.”

Adrian requested that answer retained. It wasn't surrender or future cooperation already won. It was a specific answer someone could ask him about later.

The formal notice was issued afterward. Receiving staff checked its independently retained counterpart against the meeting record. Everyone received the version they were entitled to collect; Adrian didn't sign for the group. No recruitment was executed today. No old energy was connected, no five locks opened.

At the door, a clerk requested acknowledgment. A printed clause joined receipt to understood and accepted. Cecilia would confirm receipt, not acceptance of designated sacrifice. The clerk consulted the responsible officer and returned with receipt-only wording, her objection attached to the same record. Oswin hadn't erased it. The dates hadn't vanished either.

Adrian watched the small delay. Accurate paper wouldn't lighten danger, but it would make it harder for someone afterward to say the women had agreed.

On the steps, Isabella stopped. Two days couldn't put these documents before everyone in the kingdom. Even her former special authority couldn't do that. What would she do?

“Say clearly what I can say publicly. Send verified, releasable grounds through the channels that exist. We can't wait for everyone to occupy one room before acknowledging this isn't five people's private affair.”

She would request established publication and regional distribution channels and prepare a declaration she personally owned. Administrative orders required their proper issuers. A princess wasn't the whole nation by herself. Adrian offered to check his technical passages, not choose her capacity for speaking each sentence.

In the carriage, Elena noticed his tight knuckles and offered her hand. He wanted it. Had he said too little today?

“Some things could have been faster. You didn't agree for me. You didn't refuse every future possibility for me either.”

“I wanted to.”

“I saw.”

He managed a small smile, then admitted what frightened him most: those outer records genuinely worsened, while they had one very small success. She didn't promise that love would produce a solution. Her hand stayed in his while the carriage crossed uneven stones.

At the academy, they sent the three distinct date explanations through agreed channels. Absent people received an accurate record. Isabella prepared tomorrow's public wording, Viola checked lawful accompaniment, Cecilia returned to service duties. Luna's assistant placed the remaining account beside her according to her rest schedule, rather than waking her to manufacture an immediate response.

Adrian organized the evidence for an alternative, placing its weaknesses first. No field-scale operation. No human-safety finding. No materials sufficient to replace all protection. Not reasons to surrender to the deadline, but the place he had to begin. If he put the successful little trial on top and buried every limit beneath it, he would be asking someone to supply the missing scale from trust. Today had shown him too clearly what happened when trust was made to carry a burden the evidence couldn't.

That night the porter asked whether tomorrow's carriage should run as usual. Adrian needed Isabella's actual publication arrangements confirmed; an uncertain time wasn't an instruction. Then he looked toward the calendar.

Someone had really given them a date.

He didn't crumple it or write certain success beside it. He set down the pen and went to Elena, who was still waiting to eat with him.
