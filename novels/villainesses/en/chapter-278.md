# Chapter 278: Please Move the Crown Aside

On May 6, the first thing Isabella brought into the hearing hall was a small cushion for her left wrist.

The ceremonial officer inspected it twice before asking whether she wanted the one embroidered with a crown.

“This one's softer.”

“But royal clerks will be attending.”

“If they can only recognize a crown on a cushion, perhaps they shouldn't be checking documents.”

Adrian lowered his head and coughed. Did he object to royal etiquette? she asked. No, only to the prospect of a cushion being promoted to clerk. She smiled, but her right hand stayed around her much-read statement.

There was no newly discovered secret letter. The joint order verified in March, and the expenditure and clearance records linked publicly on April 18, were already in custody under the catalog confirmed yesterday. Today they would actually be brought together somewhere the public could ask questions and both sides could challenge the evidence.

The royal secretary sat to her left. He made no attempt to cover the documents, but passed her a separate page containing his objection: the signing official's conduct should first be termed an individual's unauthorized action. Isabella asked what established the lack of authority.

“One person's ink cannot represent the entire royal institution.”

“Then investigate his authority at the time. Don't choose a conclusion with no place for the institution before investigating.”

Tight-faced, he reminded her that merchants were already questioning royal guarantees. More than a family name was at stake. She acknowledged the practical consequence without pretending her courage would pay those merchants' bills.

At the opening, a clerk moved the gilded crown-shaped nameplate aside to accommodate the comparison sheets. Someone laughed. Isabella didn't ask him to put it back.

“Today, let's read the words it was covering.”

The custodian displayed authorized verification copies. Originals remained at the adjacent inspection table under their respective custodians. Readers could trace reference numbers, issuing procedures, and receipt counterparts without trusting the princess to recognize her crest. Private rosters weren't displayed alongside public pages.

The royal representative indicated the final line, “Verification authorized.” Perhaps the order intended to move endangered people somewhere protected, he suggested.

Isabella asked the clerk to read the preceding sentence too.

Transfer first, verify afterward. Pending review shall not delay execution.

The room quieted. The receiving location Viola had shown yesterday matched the accompanying page; references to the nobles' bearer roster connected too. This wasn't one document corroborating itself. Royal retained records, Temple receipts, and military acknowledgments came through separate custody chains.

“Emergency relocation can save lives,” Isabella said. “This order didn't let people opposing transfer remain outside while verification proceeded. Please don't reverse the sequence.”

“How can you prove the signatory knew the final purpose?”

She turned over the next public set. Royal accounts had paid for revisions to the transfer patterns. The clearance explanation cited the established five positions and explicitly said the bearers needn't be consulted again. Corresponding numbers had already been checked against verification plates and retained records, not conjured today from indistinct ink.

“These support institutional royal involvement in maintaining designated bearers. What each signatory knew requires separate questioning. The king's own handwriting isn't here. That doesn't make the money ownerless.”

The secretary opened his authorization catalog. The chair gave him time, refusing to let impatient spectators dictate his speed. The deputy official had indeed been authorized to issue such documents jointly. Whether he had violated other restrictions remained another question. The representative had not yet supplied sufficiently complete material to explain that point.

His objection remained recorded. Institutional involvement already established wasn't erased by an explanation still pending.

Then came something Isabella hadn't anticipated. The secretary produced a draft notice proposing that disputed maintenance expenditure be suspended until responsibility was clarified, preventing new money from sustaining old wrongs. Someone called that genuine repentance.

A shielding-maintenance clerk stood. That account still included this month's wages. His colleague had replaced a worn lighting connector last night without ever signing a sacrifice roster.

“Separate those items for investigation, then.”

“What do they buy bread with before that happens?”

Isabella took the draft, fingers resting over a column of crossed-out figures. Agreement would easily earn praise for breaking with her past. But the clerk by the window had dark circles under his eyes.

“List disputed funds requiring preservation separately from verified ordinary maintenance, then refer them to those actually responsible. We cannot prove ourselves innocent today by making tomorrow's duty staff go hungry.”

She lacked authority to reallocate funds. She returned the draft and objection to the chair, requesting that her attendance not be recorded as support for withholding payment. The secretary didn't again tell her to consider royal reputation first. Their disagreement remained, but lunch couldn't be resolved by changing its name.

A woman representing affected families raised a question written on narrow paper. Without charges against each individual, why call this collusion?

Adrian watched Isabella. Once, she had wanted a powerful word to steady her. Today she laid her paper flat.

“I mean the coordinated arrangement we can establish, not identical guilt for every royal member. Authorizing transfer, receiving rosters, funding the five positions: different people did those things. If all we retain is ‘the royal institution is guilty,’ the actual signatories may hide behind it.”

The woman did not nod in agreement or put away the narrow paper bearing her question. Instead, she pressed on: had Isabella herself enjoyed the security this arrangement provided?

“Yes.”

Quieter than any reference number, the answer came as her right hand released her statement.

“Why should I believe you?”

“You can begin with traceable material. Check my words against it too.”

Adrian heard someone push a chair back, its legs scraping against the stone floor. No applause came from the audience to help her bring that answer to an end. The chair called for another question, and she looked up again.

Before noon, the royal representative requested removal of “without renewed consultation” from the public explanation. Family guarantees might have adequately represented the bearers.

Elena opened her copy. A family guarantee could not simply stand for a personal answer. The previous hearing had shown draft wording under which either answer led to protection being withdrawn.

“If you claim they're equivalent, show us where the person's consent was recorded. An institution receiving a roster isn't the listed person agreeing in their own words.”

Was she also denying parents' responsibility for their children? Her eyes chilled. Adrian expected her to stand immediately. Instead she remained seated and returned the question to the paper.

“These records concern adults. Don't borrow children you haven't brought here to consent on behalf of the people before us.”

Isabella glanced at her and continued. Neither existed simply to speak while the other was protected. Each had words she couldn't surrender.

During the break, Adrian found Isabella by a window, attempting to untie a pastry packet one-handed. Pulling only tightened the knot.

“May I help?”

“With the string. Not my feelings.”

Inside lay two thoroughly flattened pastries. Apparently she'd sat on them.

“A new royal standard?”

“Convenient for transport. Don't include it in your testimony.”

She broke off their exchange long enough to give him one of the pastries. At last, her smile looked as though it required a little less effort. Would printers make her “yes” larger than all the documents? Possibly, he said.

“You're terrible at comforting people.”

“You didn't authorize that part.”

Her smile faltered, then returned. “One sentence is now permitted.”

He considered. “I didn't hear you claim to be cleaner than the evidence.”

It wasn't sweet, but her shoulders slowly relaxed. She was about to ask him to stay until afternoon when she noticed another person's note protruding from his pocket.

“An appointment?”

“Helping Cecilia move some books. We've fixed the time.”

“Then take her a pastry. Explain that this isn't royal relief.”

She didn't turn disappointment into urgent official business. He didn't promise a matching date later, only asked whether her attendant should adjust her support. Yes, she said. Her left wrist was beginning to ache.

Afternoon arguments weren't easier because the pastries were shared. Permitted to question the records' connection, Oswin noted that the same five positions were maintained because the old installation recognized existing marks alone. The new method still hadn't demonstrated kingdom-wide capacity.

“If danger is real, isn't maintaining a usable method also a responsibility?”

Yes, Isabella said, a responsibility requiring an answer, not a reason to falsify consent never obtained. Where did the documents record comparison with alternatives? Where did they record the bearers' right to refuse?

He couldn't indicate either. Engineering options had been limited, he said, and might remain so.

The chair recorded technical necessity separately from documented involvement. Outrageous documents didn't make disaster imaginary. Real disaster didn't entitle the royal institution to erase its name.

After public verification, complete materials returned to storage; authorized copies remained at the designated reading place. The crown-shaped nameplate rested beside an empty table. Meeting staff would decide whether the next session needed it. Rising with her right hand on her cushion, Isabella no longer wanted to hide that plain little object beneath her shawl.

Adrian reached the borrowed room as arranged. Cecilia had finished collecting bowls with the meal-distribution group and was debating bookshelf placement with a volunteer reader. The church hadn't transformed. Today's room users simply had a chance to complain about an inconvenient arrangement.

Someone collecting the last meal waved off the reading. Cecilia said they could still collect food tomorrow, without stopping them to demand a reason.

“Just in time. These two stacks are general reading; that one needs returning. Don't mix them up to show you care.”

Was carrying books his only job? After serious consideration, she said she also hoped he'd sit and hear a short reading, if he wanted. The invitation hid behind neither rescue, prayer, nor historic marriage.

“I'd like to. After the books.”

Her eyes brightened. Isabella's pastry was cut into pieces; being flat made sharing easier. The volunteer remarked that the princess understood thrift. Adrian resisted explaining the manufacturing process.

When he sat, Cecilia opened an old liturgical text selected for today's discussion. It used migrating birds to speak of hospitality. Some birds returned each year, its first passage said; others didn't. Last year's nest couldn't turn this year's branch into their debt.

“Did you choose that deliberately?”

“Not every sentence is about you.” Her answer was dignified, her ears pink. “This is mainly about not collecting debts at the door.”

He listened, smiling. Before he left, she closed the book herself. Someone else had booked the room. Her new life couldn't always wait for his arrival to begin, and wouldn't end when he departed.

Outside, Adrian received the next day's inquiry catalog. Oswin had submitted measured trends for existing protection and maintenance consumption, requesting independent engineers' attendance. Beneath the questions he had written: Lies exposed today cannot stop the next real surge.

Adrian read it and gave the daytime recorder the catalog to copy. He neither tore it up nor had that sentence concealed behind news of royal collusion.

The princess had moved the crown beside the words. Next, they would have to move hope aside a little too, and see clearly what it hadn't yet kept out.
