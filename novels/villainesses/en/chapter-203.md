# Chapter 203: The Door She Had Trusted

On March 2, breakfast included toast baked rather too dark.

Elena pulled the plate toward herself, but Adrian took the blackest piece. She warned him it might be hard without treating the gesture as reconciliation.

“Good. Something we can establish in advance.”

He paused at his own words. She waited until he tried a corner before mentioning softer bread in the kitchen. He put it down. Passing the table, Martha inspected the faint tooth marks and silently removed the entire plate, apparently deciding neither adult could be trusted with breakfast.

Elena laughed then. So did he.

Yesterday's excerpt remained beside him. Its recorded objection hadn't faded. Laughter and hurt could coexist without either being false.

The repository had authorized supplementary correspondence. Yesterday's examination had identified a cross-reference to a lawyer's dispatch register under the same subnumber. Not the miraculous restoration of the missing technical attachment; another surviving piece of the doorframe.

They traveled separately. Adrian first checked at the independent counter that his questions hadn't been absorbed into a household statement. Elena arrived for her own appointment. Neither presented the small inconvenience as an achievement.

The lawyer's register came from a professional repository. House Rosenberg's retained correspondence sat separately. Matching references connected their exchange to the transfer application and yesterday's receipt. Material assessment supported the same historical grouping, not an exact conversion into the current calendar.

Elena first noticed a request: suspend further transfer before independent questioning; allow the person questioned to address a representative not appointed by the Temple; obtain separate review of the technical assessment.

An urgent addition followed. A report of transmission danger had been received; returning the matter to the original requesting authority was inadvisable.

“She didn't believe there was no danger,” Adrian said.

The custodian qualified that. The letter established her reference to a report, not its accuracy. The incomplete original couldn't place this judicial event on the day of the shutdown they had investigated on February 27.

Adrian left his question mark beside the date. Fear made it tempting to bring two frightening events close enough for one motive to explain both.

The lawyer's reply promised none of her conditions. Requests and objections could be registered, but the representative still lacked permission to meet Adrian. If suspension of transfer was a prerequisite, written acceptance must be obtained. Receipt of a case was not acceptance of conditions.

The warning sat plainly in black ink.

The historical Elena had replied that the receiving chamber had stamped her application. Formal judicial review seemed more controllable than emergency handling by the original authority. House Rosenberg could keep asking questions. This must not happen somewhere without a register.

She asked the lawyer to continue seeking access. She had not waited for explicit acceptance of her conditions.

“Has that acceptance been found?” today's Elena asked.

“Not in the index or this correspondence. That doesn't prove none ever existed. Until one is verified, we cannot say the conditions were accepted.”

At the far end of the table, a copying clerk misread may request as may obtain. The examiner stopped him. Correcting an ordinary slip didn't make the ink look darker, but Adrian drew his copy closer.

“Had requests like these been accepted in other cases?”

That required a separate application for anonymous procedural material. Other people's private files weren't within today's permission. Precedent couldn't establish what happened here anyway.

He had wanted to know whether her trust had been entirely unreasonable. Another convenient division: foolish beyond understanding, or justified enough to forgive. A real decision could contain experience, fear, and a moment when she no longer wished to wait.

Elena didn't press for unrelated files. She asked that the representative's lack of access be copied beside her household's supposed ability to keep asking questions. The two facts shouldn't be separated into different stories.

On her authorized copy she circled received. She didn't write deceived beside it.

Adrian studied the family name. A strong rope tied to people who knew her and answered her letters. If that rope had never tightened around your throat, following it through a door might reasonably feel safe.

He disliked how easily he could imagine her persuading herself.

“I think she believed she could keep fighting once we were inside. If there was a name on a register, they couldn't simply dispose of me.”

“An interpretation,” the custodian cautioned.

“Yes. Not a recovered memory.”

When their short viewing period ended, Elena requested permission to take her copy to the waiting room. The originals remained on the table.

She chose seats away from a draft and left him an empty chair. It was so low his knees almost touched the little table.

“Excellent furniture for discouraging argument,” he said.

“Or ensuring counsel leaves early.”

They both looked at the innocent chair. Then she settled her copy on her lap.

“I can understand why she trusted that door. Names and formal replies still make things easier for me. It's easy to mistake that for protection everyone receives.”

He waited.

“But she'd been warned. A request wasn't a promise. She thought she could arrange the rest afterward, so she accepted the part you couldn't easily undo on your behalf.”

“Do you think she had no other choice?”

“I don't know the full circumstances. Not knowing doesn't let me call her choice the only one.”

Her hands remained in her lap. She wasn't gripping his sleeve or waiting for him to take them. He remembered believing, at the brass handle, that he could address the most urgent danger first and repair the rest later.

The choices weren't identical. His own error didn't disqualify him from being hurt. But he recognized the blind spot: bring one comprehensible danger close enough, and someone else's spoken refusal could become a problem for afterward.

“I understand why she went through it,” he said.

Hope briefly brightened Elena's face. She restrained it rather than claiming victory.

“I still can't accept her treating my objection as something to resolve once I was inside.”

“I shouldn't ask you to.”

He exhaled. “I don't know whether she loved me then. I don't want to guess today just to make the handover seem gentler.”

“Then we won't guess.”

Her eyes reddened, but she could hear him and lift her own cup. He didn't have to close the conversation with an assurance that everything was fine.

Back inside, he added a question: did independent material establish that the person being questioned knew about and accepted those conditions? A request on his behalf wasn't his authorization.

Elena retained the lawyer's warning in her own statement. Misleading conduct by the receiving chamber wasn't yet established. Someone might have deceived her or exploited her confidence. The surviving pages first showed her choosing despite an explicit warning.

Could their statements be combined?

“The attachments can sit together,” she said. “Don't answer for him in mine.”

Outside, half the pavement had dried. Isabella emerged from the ordinary application counter, left wrist supported, a returned envelope in her right hand. She hadn't lost the right to apply. Her papers lacked an organizational opinion she had no authority to sign herself.

Adrian asked whether she'd seek another co-sponsor. Yes, but a guarantee of his innocence wouldn't become the bargaining price.

“Has the baron offered new terms?” Elena asked.

“He suggested removing the name so my support seemed less directed at anyone.”

“And you said?”

“I asked whom we would protect after removing the person.”

Isabella glanced at a pastry shop. Today she very much wanted to purchase something that couldn't be returned for amendment.

Adrian held the door without reaching for her purse. She paid with her right hand, her left resting securely, and hesitated between orange cake and almond biscuits longer than she'd hesitated over the baron.

The assistant asked whether everything belonged in one box.

“Mine separately,” Isabella said, looking between them. “We shouldn't let a pastry settle the matter without permission.”

Elena chose a small cake. Adrian nearly declined, then bought the same. Not an apology, or a paper box announcing who had won.

The assistant tied Isabella's parcel with an elaborate bow. She inspected it, then rested the box on the counter and tried the ribbon with her right hand. The knot slid beneath the base.

“A formidable arrangement,” she observed.

The assistant offered to remove it. Isabella asked for a simpler loop on top instead. She needed to open the box later without putting her left wrist to work. While the ribbon was retied, she asked Adrian to steady the base. He held it where she indicated, clear of the little cake inside.

Elena requested the same plain knot for hers.

“Without the decorative bow?” the assistant asked. “It's usually prettier for a gift.”

“I'm going to eat it.”

“An unusually clear intended use,” Adrian said.

Elena gave him a look over the box. “Are you planning to read yours?”

“Only the ingredients.”

Isabella tried the new loop. It opened cleanly. She thanked the assistant and asked him to leave a little extra ribbon so she could close it afterward. The exchange took no official title, and nobody needed to pretend she could manage two-handed work.

At the door, Elena's paper bag brushed Adrian's. He shifted his own to keep the corners from crushing. She noticed and moved hers to the other hand. Their cakes survived the encounter with rather more composure than their owners had brought into the shop.

Before leaving, Isabella mentioned an afternoon appointment with a representative opposed to her proposal. They needn't abandon their own arrangements to accompany her. She put away her cake and left alone, on time. The proposal still lacked support, and she still had to argue for it.

At home, Elena asked whether he wanted to read the travel book that evening.

“I don't want to talk late.”

“We could read a short passage.”

“Actually short? Not your last page that turned out to contain three folding maps?”

“Maps aren't prose.”

“Precisely why we need to establish the conditions first.”

Her smile lingered before she nodded seriously. A short passage; stop when he wished. One manageable arrangement didn't have to decide every difficult matter between them.

After dinner he found two chairs beside her usual window, enough space between them for a small table. The book remained closed; a paper knife lay beside it. She waited until he chose a seat before opening the unread journey.

The traveler complained about a ferry inn's bed. Adrian laughed aloud, then stopped and admitted he was still thinking about the signature on the handover.

She marked their place with a finger. She didn't ask why, when they had been doing so well a moment ago.

“I know.”

He watched her. “You can continue. Not because I've forgotten. Because I still want to listen.”

She read on. Wheels receded beyond the window; the traveler continued to find the bed too short. Adrian leaned back. The past hadn't become better. But he had words he could use to stop, and someone who hadn't closed the book because he needed them.
