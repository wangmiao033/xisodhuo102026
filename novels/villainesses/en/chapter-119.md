# Chapter 119: A Companion Outside the Experiment

On December 27, Luna arrived punctually at breakfast with an empty trunk.

Adrian moved his bread out of the way.

“It isn't an experiment this time.”

“Then why are you smiling as though you want to put me inside?”

“Because you're clearly the wrong size. I have every reason to be disappointed.”

She sat, settling her right palm on its cushion. Tessa's wooden sleeve was fitted to her pen, making it thicker but easier to hold. Luna had written three pages with it and brought only one. The others consisted entirely of elegant openings she couldn't bear to reread.

She folded the remaining sheet instead of reading aloud.

“I want to join your investigation. When you leave the tower, I'd like to come.”

A spoon touched a cup. Viola stopped spreading jam on a biscuit. Cecilia closed her book. Elena looked up.

Luna didn't fill their brief silence with extravagant promises.

“Records of the old apparatus are scattered elsewhere. Waiting here for deliveries won't tell me why the old warnings failed to accompany the summaries onward, or how my old report was used. I can compare documents, recognize older repair marks, and ask technicians questions. I won't cast before my hand heals. Afterward, traveling won't excuse skipping assessments.”

“What about your work here?” Adrian asked.

“I've discussed it with the director. If you agree, I'll apply for a research visit and send back shareable notes on schedule. Someone can cover my ordinary classes. I'll keep writing the unfinished corrections. If the accident investigation needs me here, I'll return.”

She looked at the empty trunk.

“This belongs to me. I'm not using you as an excuse to remove tower property.”

“Don't treat yourself as something being lent out either,” Viola said.

“Does that mean I can choose what I don't want to do?”

“Yes. I've only recently learned that isn't laziness.”

Adrian didn't immediately welcome her. He first asked about travel and copying expenses. Luna produced the academy-approved allowance. Ordinary costs were covered; personal purchases would be her own responsibility. The paper was more reassuring than a promise to help with absolutely anything.

“And if we think an explanation is premature,” he said, “you must let people object.”

“I'll be annoyed.”

“You're allowed to be annoyed.”

She blinked, then laughed. “More generous terms than the church offered.”

“We must listen when you find problems too. You aren't coming to endorse our guesses.”

This time, she had no immediate retort. She pocketed the folded sheet, as though discovering that she didn't need to package herself as an especially useful tool to keep her place at this table.

Only then did Elena speak.

“I agree to your coming as an investigator. There's something I'd like to discuss privately.”

Luna raised an eyebrow at Adrian. “Investigate if I don't come back.”

“Finish breakfast first,” Elena said.

“Her methods grow more ruthless.”

After eating, they went into the small adjoining sitting room. The door remained ajar. Fire warmed two chairs beneath the window. Elena rested her cloak on her knees without arranging herself like an interrogator.

“I don't like you testing him in the name of experiments.”

Luna's smile slowly faded.

“Measuring his heartbeat, or yesterday's remark about his collar?”

“If you want to flirt, admit it. Making him think he has to cooperate with research is different from asking whether he wants to.”

Luna watched a log split, revealing a bright red seam.

“Is that all you dislike?”

Elena's ears reddened.

“Of course not. I get jealous. I'm not so noble that watching someone approach him makes me delighted.”

“That's easier to believe.”

“But I can't decide whom he speaks to. Caring about him doesn't entitle me to forbid him from helping you.”

Luna looked down at her right palm.

“I used to think that if I could say I was joking afterward, I needn't admit I wanted the answer.”

“And now?”

“I'm discovering that retreating into a joke doesn't make someone else's discomfort disappear.”

Elena didn't declare her reformed. She reminded her that Adrian sometimes worried about others before answering for himself. His hesitation wasn't permission.

Luna nodded, then slowly added, “There's something I dislike too.”

“What?”

“You sit here and make even jealousy sound beautiful. Maintaining my usual composure becomes difficult.”

Elena rose with her cloak and nearly caught its clasp on the chair. Luna's suppressed laughter was worse than laughing outright. Eventually Elena laughed too. They freed the clasp together without undoing anything serious they'd said.

At noon, packing the trunk became the tower's liveliest small event.

Luna put in three authorized personal working copies, two changes of clothing, a roll of ordinary drawing paper, and an enormous magnifying glass. Adrian replaced the last item with a smaller one.

“The large one gives a clearer view.”

“Of why your trunk won't shut.”

She conceded, but insisted on keeping her chipped cup. Cecilia checked that the chip wasn't where she drank, then wrapped it in cloth. Viola supervised the luggage distribution from her chair, refusing to let anyone bury the medical case underneath everything.

Tessa brought a left-handed pen rack that fitted inside the lid. She didn't ask whether Luna would remember them after leaving. She asked who would inherit the mint.

After considerable thought, Luna wrote a note for the pot.

Whoever waters it may pick two leaves.

“Still two?” Tessa asked.

“It's recently been promoted. It's entitled to leave.”

The researchers laughed, then resumed discussing the ordinary support-frame drawing. Luna listened and lowered the left hand she'd begun to raise, letting the young researcher try explaining for himself. The tower needn't stop because she left. Nor would it immediately collapse without her watching.

That disappointed her a little and relieved her considerably.

The recovered button finally returned to her coat. Cecilia threaded a needle. Luna tried pulling the thread left-handed and lost the end twice. After staring at the needle's eye, she handed over the coat.

“Could I ask for help with this?”

“Yes. Hold the fabric for me. Keep your right hand clear.”

They worked beside the window. Luna had assumed the saint could do everything, until Cecilia sewed her first stitch into the shawl underneath. Unpicking it, she readily admitted she usually repaired book pages. Fabric was more inclined than paper to sneak away.

“I won't include this in a research report,” Luna promised.

“You can put it in a letter to a friend.”

The button sat crookedly when Luna put the coat on, but that small patch of cold air no longer got through. Touching the front, she found herself anticipating a long journey in unexpectedly practical terms.

Handing over copies that afternoon, she passed the restricted cabinet room without entering. The administrator held the inventory. C-17 and the accident remains stayed where they belonged. She would take only authorized copies, each traceable to records kept at the tower.

Adrian waited around the corner with her leather drawing tube.

“You look ready for a very serious battle,” she said.

“I'm preventing this from rolling down the stairs.”

“An important front.”

They walked to a sunlit window. Luna extended her left hand, pointing at the tube without touching him.

“There's a sheet inside that I didn't submit. Could you give it to me?”

He did. It bore the opening she'd omitted that morning: I undertake to assist with every ability at my disposal.

She read it and folded it small.

“It sounds as though I must do whatever you want.”

“It does.”

“I used to like sentences like that when writing other people's experimental requirements.”

“You can change it now.”

She pocketed it instead of dramatically tearing up the past. Keeping the wrong words could remind her how to write the next ones.

Outside, two gray sparrows landed on the wall and fought over bread neither could drag away. Luna watched before turning back.

“There's something else I don't want to put into the application.”

Adrian waited.

“I want to come because I like being with you too. Not every kindness is observation. Not every joke means nothing to me.”

Her cheeks warmed. The quips she usually escaped behind remained on her tongue, but she stopped herself from using them.

“You don't have to give me an answer I'll like right now.”

“Luna.”

Her shoulders tightened at her name.

“I care about you. I want to keep being your friend and companion. But Elena and I are seriously courting. Your honesty doesn't allow me to give you a promise I don't have.”

She looked back at the sparrows. They'd finally pecked off a little each, abandoning their struggle for the whole piece.

“I hear you. It hurts more than I expected. It's also simpler than guessing.”

“You can reconsider traveling with us.”

“I spent all morning proving I have other abilities. Don't reduce all of me to one confession now.”

Adrian paused, then nodded seriously.

She smiled, disappointment still showing in her eyes.

“I'll be sad. I may keep liking you. But you don't owe me an answer beyond our research. If I need distance, I'll say so myself.”

“All right.”

Neither immediately moved. They stayed until the birds flew away, then carried the drawings back together.

That evening, Adrian met Elena on the stairs. She'd been checking arrangements for forwarding ordinary correspondence with the administrator. She glanced at his empty drawing tube.

“Finished packing?”

“Almost. We persuaded her not to take the entire bookcase.”

Elena smiled. “And you?”

He understood that she wasn't asking only about luggage.

“I explained that we're courting, and what I can offer.”

She didn't demand every word Luna had said. At the landing, Adrian offered his right hand. Elena took it and held on until they finished the last few stairs.

“I don't want you to be dishonest with others to spare my feelings,” she said softly.

“And I won't pretend you don't matter because I'm afraid of hurting someone else.”

She paused and looked toward the window. The winter light couldn't hide her red ears.

“Then save a little time for me tonight.”

“The travelogue?”

“We'll begin with reading. After that depends on your conduct.”

In the sitting room, Luna held down the trunk lid left-handed and called Cecilia to fasten it. The brass clasp clicked shut at last.

She wrote her own name on the luggage tag. Not a research subject. Not somebody else's possession.

As Adrian passed, she raised her freely moving left hand.

“Companion, remind me to eat breakfast tomorrow.”

“You can remind me too.”

“Agreed. I'll choose the moment you least want to wake up.”

She secured the tag, then showed Viola the respectable handwriting. The knight studied it and offered only one word: legible. Luna looked as pleased as if she'd received a particularly rare high mark.

Outside, daylight slowly faded. Where and when they traveled still required discussion. This time, at least, Luna had clearly said she wanted to come.
