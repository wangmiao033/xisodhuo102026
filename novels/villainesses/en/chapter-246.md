# Chapter 246: Within Reach

On the morning of April 12, the first empty sharing-node housing came through the door while Adrian was making room for a crate corner.

His coat button still scraped the frame. The movers stopped, waited for the man at the other end to regain his footing, and changed their angle together. Calling out careful wouldn't widen the doorway.

From her chair beside the isolation room's window, Luna watched and told her assistant to leave the second crate closed.

“Is that the door you drew last night?”

The measurements were correct. The drawing lacked today's packing crates and the temporary tool rack. Its straight route to the exit worked only if two actual people first pulled in their stomachs.

The academy's separate installation permit hung outside. It allowed unpowered housings and supports in this isolated space, with position checks and mechanical demonstrations. This wasn't the discussion room borrowed until afternoon on April 9. Nobody could be connected today. The old energy hadn't been brought here; two crates hadn't opened a single lock on the Crown of Destiny.

The duty technician checked the seals and set an empty housing on temporary supports. Installers had agreed hours. Attendance was a separate job, not wages replaced by willingness to help.

Adrian had wanted to see a drawing become something solid. He'd imagined fixing the supports first and finishing the surroundings afterward. Surely that beat another morning ending with chalk lines alone.

“Could we install this outer one? The door will be clear once the tools are moved.”

Luna's right palm remained on its cushion. With her left hand, she indicated a small square on the plan.

“From the chair, how do you reach that?”

It marked the stop-request handle intended for a future user. Only ordinary mechanical parts were present, connections capped and unpowered; eventual use still needed safety review. The line on paper made the handle seem close. Here, a housing corner intervened. Adrian could walk around it. A seated person would have to lean far forward.

“There'll be someone beside them.”

“And when that person is opening the door?”

He felt irritated despite knowing she wasn't inventing difficulties. He remembered the dark river lamps and the notice still hanging in the entrance hall. Each condition seemed another piece of time they didn't have.

He admitted his impatience instead of disguising it as everyone's waiting. He had wanted the morning to end with something he could point at, a visible answer to the notice outside. The wish embarrassed him once he said it aloud. A pair of installed supports would look like progress even to somebody who knew nothing of their limits, and for a moment he had wanted that reassurance himself.

“I want it installed more than you do,” Luna said. “It's lived in my head long enough to owe rent. But moving-in day doesn't prove a place fit to live in.”

She stopped and asked her assistant to turn the page. After supporting herself on the paper, her left fingers curled slightly. Adrian nearly pushed her chair toward the apparatus to demonstrate the reach, then checked himself. Her injury wasn't a conveniently available measuring instrument.

Would she describe the problem while the workers positioned things? Yes. She hadn't intended to use her right palm to prove the handle difficult either.

The technician brought a stable ordinary chair. No prospective volunteer was summoned; no mark approached an interface. He sat and demonstrated the unloaded reaching motion, stopping at the guard. A worker occupied the proposed assistance position while a cart passed the door. Without competing, three people still needed the same patch of floor.

“This isn't about leg length,” the technician said.

Crouching outside the chalk, Adrian saw his evacuation arrow passing through the assistant's feet. Arrows could cross each other. People couldn't. Would turning the housing toward the window solve it?

The room supervisor refused. Maintenance clearance belonged there, and empty space beneath a window wasn't a second exit. Rejecting one proposal didn't oblige him to conjure another room.

Sunlight moved across the packing cloth. An installer asked how much longer. He had another booking; extending his hours required agreement. A possible visit from a princess or saint wouldn't extend them automatically.

Luna asked him to perform housing checks unaffected by the layout. Identifying parts and checking transport supports could happen on the floor without drilling. Adrian went to find the tool rack's custodian instead of pushing it into the corridor.

The man was counting old timber behind the building. For how long did Adrian need space? Could tools still be collected? Adrian nearly said only a little room. Instead, he supplied the rack's width and today's return time. They inspected an approved ordinary-tool storage area before workers moved it. Two old boards returned to their proper place. Tidying this room wouldn't mean blocking somebody else's route.

The space gained was modest but allowed different spacing between the housings. On the drawing, moving a rack had required no conversation. In the building, it had meant finding its custodian, explaining when it would return, and leaving another person able to continue his work. Adrian was beginning to understand how much of his straight line had depended on other people disappearing from it. Within the original permitted mounting choices, the technician placed the mechanical handle's support on a visible, reachable side. No energy connection was altered; those remained capped. A different structure required by review would require fresh checks. Drilled holes couldn't oblige later reviewers to accommodate them.

This time they left the fasteners out.

Chair, empty housing, assistant, and door took their places. The technician rested his hands naturally on his legs, then reached. He touched the handle without leaning across the casing. But the worker noticed his sleeve brushed it when passing behind.

“Now it's too popular,” Luna said.

They moved the assistant's position and preserved a clear gap. Adrian walked the exit route without stepping over tools or asking anyone to remove a chair. The assistant recorded each failure rather than retaining only the final attractive diagram.

The ordinary unloaded mechanism clicked.

Adrian relaxed instinctively. Luna asked for a qualification beside the result: in this arrangement, the mechanical request could be made. No burden was flowing, so none had been stopped. Who would disconnect after a request, how remaining load would be isolated, and when immediate departure would be unsafe couldn't grow out of that click.

He nodded. This time it didn't feel as though she'd stolen the achievement. He could see precisely the small thing they'd gained.

The revised distances went on the wall. A young attendant continued frowning. If he stood outside greeting visitors and heard stop from inside, whom should he notify first? His position appeared in the drawing. The questions he was also expected to answer didn't.

To save one person, Adrian had assigned reception and safety assistance to the same hours. It wasn't a question of the young man's reactions. Two jobs required him in two places.

Adrian separated reception from the proposed operating role. Today's installation was unpowered; visitors could wait outside by appointment. Future staffing needed another check by those responsible for safety. The attendant wouldn't agree first and beg somebody to cover later.

“Will it look as though I don't want to help?” he asked, holding his hat.

Luna said that without his objection they would have described their convenient mistake with far too much confidence.

He sat and indicated another delivery duty at noon. He was offering the original attendance hours, not his entire day to a new room. Adrian had reception correct both copies and give him one. No medal for courage—just an arrangement matching what he'd said.

Another vacant position appeared on the preparation sheet. Adrian studied it without copying the young man's name back in.

Near midday, a second crew continued under newly agreed hours. Only checked supports were fixed within the permit. The two housings received separate labels and retained their blank caps. Assistant and technician checked the actual fixed positions together; a successful arrangement on temporary blocks might have changed during installation.

When her break came, Luna left the window. She refused to be recorded as witnessing all the final fastening. She confirmed only what she'd seen. Those present would record the remainder, which she would read during review. Her name couldn't make her an eyewitness for them.

Adrian accompanied her outside.

“Do you still think I slowed things down?”

“Yes.”

Her eyebrow rose.

“The part I most wanted to skip. I'm not used to it yet.”

“Good. I wasn't planning to make you enjoy every minute.”

He held her ordinary folder while her assistant came around. He didn't turn the short walk into a demand for further technical explanations. Luna noticed he'd fastened the folder backward; the papers could fall out underneath.

“Door, chair, folder,” he said, correcting it. “Everything's educating me today.”

“The folder charges least.”

Back inside, he saw the half-erased chalk and no longer wanted every trace scrubbed away immediately. The installation record retained the reasons for changes; chalk needn't become a monument. Until workers finished checking, however, it still had a job.

During a break in her ordinary service handover, Cecilia brought two questions with permission to pass them on. These weren't enrollments. An adult wanted to know whether feeling unwell on the eventual day would allow cancellation after signing. Another wanted only to guide people, without being counted among risk bearers. She gave both to the person responsible for explanations rather than answering for the reviewers herself.

From outside the door, she examined the housings.

“At last, something's there.”

“Nobody connected. No power.”

“I can see the caps. I meant something people can point to when explaining what's wrong.”

He laughed. Had he arranged lunch? Yes: ordinary bread and soup. She needn't save a portion.

“Good. Mine can finally stop being mistaken for yours.”

“Do I look that much like a patient requiring constant feeding?”

“Patients usually wait for the bowl to be put down.”

She sounded perfectly serious, except for her eyes. Then she left according to the handover time, without delaying another worker's meal to stay with him. Adrian didn't enter her joke into the work record alongside her questions.

The packet prepared for that afternoon’s review contained the installation permit, actual layout, unfinished matters, and reasons for today's changes. It offered neither proof of human suitability nor names already signed. Independent inspectors would examine the room, not merely a drawing Luna and Adrian both wanted to praise.

He checked that the chair, restored beside the wall, obstructed no door before collecting his lunch.

Two new housings stood quietly in their places. They hadn't relit a river lamp or moved the date on the notice.

At least when someone eventually sat and asked how to request a stop, the first answer wouldn't have to be: stand up and walk around to where we can see you.
