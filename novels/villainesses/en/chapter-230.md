# Chapter 230: She Didn't Hold the Key

On March 29, Evelyn Vale placed an empty leather sleeve on the table.

Its size made Adrian think of a key.

“A document badge case,” she said.

He looked away. She hadn't read his mind; she knew the question that shape invited. Pale fibers showed through worn leather. Its fastener was gone. No light emerged from the empty space.

They met in an independent reading room. Her adviser sat opposite; custodians brought originals; no party could alter the record alone. Isabella attended as an applicant, not a royal authority deciding which confession canceled which responsibility. Luna's session remained short. Yesterday's easier conversation hadn't extended her medical allowance.

Evelyn began with no story about helplessness. She asked the custodian to open her current receipt record and identified the badge category she'd used, what instructions she could transmit, and what she couldn't decide.

The badge had already been returned through the proper process. Today they saw an authorized copy. It identified a registered executor, not a sacred object that commanded every mechanism when brought near a door.

“Could you stop an operation?” Adrian asked.

“I could transmit a scoped suspension and confirm execution within my external procedure.”

“Write your own reason?”

“Request one. Not turn the request into approval.” She paused. “I've also spoken in ways that let people mistake it for approval.”

The recorder asked whether she meant a specific incident. She indicated the register associated with the old log. That event required its own examination; today's admission wasn't a complete confession covering every case. Her adviser didn't erase it. His presence didn't reduce all her answers to no comment either.

The authorized directory distinguished external routing, maintenance borrowing, and core operation. The five locks remained sealed. Knowing categories conferred no entry. Nor could an old title simply become a working present-day button. Some current rules retained historical terms whose effect required separate verification.

Luna had the question noted, then requested the issuance trail rather than a recital of impressive names.

The examiner initially produced a summary ending with Oswin's name but no signed original. Adrian asked whether the name sufficed. Evelyn shook her head first: it might identify the person to whom a document was submitted. The volume's column definitions mattered.

The preceding page said submitted for approval, not approved. The summary alone established an intended submission. The issued original belonged in another box, requiring its storage entry checked before opening.

Waiting, Adrian wanted to ask Evelyn whose side she was on. He resisted, then admitted the unfair thought to the recorder. Evidence disappointing him shouldn't disqualify someone from correcting it.

Looking at the empty stand, Isabella said it could stay empty until the original arrived. She wasn't defending Evelyn or welcoming uncertainty about Oswin. She didn't want the answer they most needed to decide the order of this inquiry.

When the new box arrived, one custodian matched its storage number and another examiner read the corresponding issuance entry. These supported a specific approved restriction. They didn't transform the earlier submission summary into an approval too. The clerk preserved the different statuses.

Adrian changed a small line in his notes: not finding a name, but finding who decided what at which stage. Then he looked up again.

The custodian produced an original counterpart for an external-routing suspension. It cited a scope approved by High Priest Oswin. Below stood an executor's countersignature field and a requirement for an independent custodian's duplicate. Orders didn't emerge as voices from the core. Identifiable people, recorded date layers, and receiving offices stood between them.

Evelyn had confirmed execution for part of that process.

“Was your countersignature approval, or acknowledgment?” Adrian asked.

“More than receipt. It confirmed that I'd checked the document's scope and stopped the relevant procedure.”

She didn't describe herself as merely the last pen.

A separately retained register tested the claim. Matching numbers connected an execution report with a correction: a borrowing suspension excluded previously approved ordinary maintenance, but the original execution extract had combined both. Evelyn's name appeared on that extract and at receipt of the correction.

It didn't prove every suspension had expanded wrongly. It showed that she knew expansion was possible and had participated in one documented correction.

“Did the correction reach the people whose work was stopped?” Isabella asked.

“The register shows receipt at the custody office. The individual applicants' copies still need retrieving.”

“Then don't say their problem was solved.”

Evelyn nodded. Finally speaking earned nobody unusually generous trust today.

The restriction connected to the old protocol's number. Independent issuance records, ordinary signature specimens, and custody receipts jointly supported Oswin's authorization. It barred external operators from adding new substitute configurations themselves and referred such changes for central review. That explained how an unapproved option could come to be treated as nonexistent. It didn't technically prove an alternative impossible forever.

“You turned not permitted into no other way,” Adrian said.

Evelyn didn't seek a gentler answer from her adviser.

“The distinction disappeared in several summaries I handled.”

“Why?”

“Leaving it there meant people kept asking.”

Silence filled the room. The reason wasn't grand like fear or loyalty. That made Adrian's palms colder. Wanting fewer questions was ordinary enough for anyone. On these pages, it connected to bodies and destinations.

Evelyn added that the institution had genuinely feared untested changes endangering existing protection. That concern wasn't invented. It didn't explain away her omission of the route for requesting examination.

Luna asked whether independent research using new materials, entirely disconnected from active protection, still required the same person's approval.

This old order couldn't decide today's situation, Evelyn said. Academy permission and Temple borrowing conditions separately governed current research. The custodian could dispute access to Temple authority records. That didn't give the High Priest ownership of every academy table.

She described boundaries to examine, not a passage ready to exploit.

Luna's assistant signaled the time. She left a final question: should borrowing disputes and equipment safety review be handled separately? Actual decision-makers should answer each. Others couldn't accept new restrictions for her after departure, or treat her written question as an answer received.

After watching her leave, Adrian saw Evelyn push the empty case toward its box. An ordinary object beside weighty authority made it tempting to think taking the object would finish everything.

If she was speaking now, he asked, would she help them open the core?

“No.”

He was disappointed. Knowing he shouldn't expect it hadn't prevented the expectation.

“I can say what I did and identify places to check. Authority I don't have isn't compensation I can give you.”

She didn't announce permanent membership of the correct side. Her earlier failure to return the log remained a matter for accountability. Today's materials didn't cancel it. Her adviser requested that the new account remain within the authorized meeting scope, private third-party pages stay concealed, and alleged breaches go to the relevant inquiry rather than being privately exchanged for cooperation.

Isabella agreed, then requested preservation of what they'd seen and verification requests to the actual custodians and issuing offices. Oswin's office would receive formal questions about authority and instructions, not a princess's threatening letter.

Arranging copies produced another small difficulty. The form offered to copy the entire volume. The clerk said that was the usual entry; it saved applicants a second journey when they discovered they needed something else. Adrian had picked up his pen before he noticed Evelyn's adviser keeping a hand on the covered third-party pages.

“Only the pages we've checked,” Adrian said, putting the pen down.

“Separating them requires an itemized entry. You may not receive every copy today.”

Beyond the window, the sky promised rain. The driver was waiting, and Luna needed a copy of the permitted discussion. Adrian wanted to promise that nothing would be circulated. Keeping something private himself, however, wouldn't supply another person's permission to give it to him.

Isabella dictated the page numbers to the recorder. Her right forefinger stopped at an attachment. Did it contain someone else's address? They checked rather than accepting her guess. Only the line relevant to the execution trail would remain visible; the rest stayed concealed under the existing authorization. She wasn't deciding, by personal favor, which stranger could keep a private life.

They received the portions ready for release. Missing page numbers and the expected processing interval went on a separate receipt. Adrian didn't sign for material Luna hadn't received. Outside, the driver asked whether he could feed the horses while they waited. Adrian agreed and asked the attendant to hold the document box until the carriage returned. His urgency didn't require someone else's ordinary work to disappear without a word.

Before the meeting ended, the independent recorder read it back. Adrian heard his words summarized as deliberate concealment of all alternatives. He corrected that to the distinction he'd actually raised between unapproved and impossible. Evidence supported specific compressions, not the lazy conclusion all.

Evelyn glanced at him without thanking him. He didn't need gratitude for accuracy.

Rain began as they left. Isabella's umbrella catch stuck despite two attempts with her right hand. With permission, Adrian took it and found tangled thread. He freed it without joking that at least he'd opened one lock today.

“You almost made a joke,” Isabella said.

“How did you know?”

“When you feel clever, you first check whether everyone is ready to laugh.”

He laughed, caught. An assistant took the opened umbrella so her left wrist bore no weight. They walked side by side to the carriage porch, far enough for her to ask what had disappointed him most.

“She had no key to give us.”

“I used to think the right person with the right key would make everything faster.”

“You don't want faster now?”

“I do. I don't want to pretend speed must mean only I get to decide.”

Her carriage came first. She didn't invite him to cancel someone else's dinner. Would he join her to examine replies tomorrow, at a time to be confirmed? He agreed to check his arrangements, without turning work into an unspoken romantic answer.

That evening Luna received the permitted meeting record. Adrian read the passages he needed to explain. At authority I don't have isn't compensation, he paused.

“What is it?”

“Yesterday I nearly compensated someone with money I didn't have.”

“Similar kindness. Different coat.”

He agreed. Luna didn't announce that she wanted him instead of money. She asked when the borrowed clamp would receive further measurement. Tomorrow he would inquire; a return time was still unknown.

She kept that answer without making it prettier. Then she requested half a page of the travel book, no model discussion. Adrian checked the time. Half a page was possible. She hoped the author hadn't lost another hat.

When it was read, the assistant secured the records. Formal verification requests had gone through separate routes. No response from the issuing office had yet returned. Adrian didn't call Evelyn's speech a victory or imagine that the next unopened door already belonged to them.
