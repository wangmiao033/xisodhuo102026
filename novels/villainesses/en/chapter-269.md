# Chapter 269: Nobody Is the First Piece of Packing

When Oswin proposed positioning the first marked bearer, Cecilia was putting away a cup with its chipped edge facing outward.

She turned it before looking up.

“Who chooses the first?”

April 28's dusk had sunk beyond the outer console's view. Lamplight touched the high priest's face. The old procedure specified an order, he said. He sought renewed review of the stay on compulsory movement and the inner activation requirements. Those able to attend should begin taking their verification positions. He wasn't claiming one person could replace all five marks and complete a reset.

“Without the first, nothing afterward can begin.”

Adrian looked down the corridor toward the old locks. No altar had suddenly appeared, waiting for a victim. There were sealed doors, the key custodian, and duty officers. Yet if he volunteered Cecilia now, the next document would quote his voice.

“I won't agree for her.”

“I'm not asking only you,” Oswin said. “But you keep asking them to believe another way can be ready in time.”

That struck something real. Adrian's back still remembered Elena's embrace. He wanted everyone to remain alive with an intensity that almost hurt. He had indeed offered assurances larger than his evidence.

He offered no new one.

“I don't have an alternative installation large enough. Today we can stop this preparatory branch competing for maintenance reserves, retain protection, and gain time to keep responding to the disaster. I can't guarantee tomorrow with that.”

“Then on what grounds do you refuse?”

“You can't use my fear to find someone who'll sign for them.”

Cecilia stepped between them, not behind Adrian.

“I won't take that position. Tonight I'll care for injured people coming off duty here. That's work I've chosen and can do.”

Oswin's clerk quietly mentioned the historical marriage record linking her to Adrian. Perhaps it could support an intention to share the burden. Her expression turned cold.

“That record's present legal effect was settled long ago. You've read the findings.”

The clerk couldn't answer. Cecilia didn't reach for Adrian to prove anything.

“I brought it when I first came to him. I hoped the past could reserve a place for me. Later I refused, in my own words, to misuse it. Your urgency won't make me become someone who never learned those things.”

Her voice softened slightly. “I have loved, and I still care. That isn't an interface you may use.”

Adrian thanked her seriously. He wasn't accepting a marriage contract or promising Elena's future to someone else. He thanked her for refusing to surrender her choice to dead paper. She understood, without demanding a sweeter addition.

Elena came to the other side and stated her own refusal. At the liaison desk, Isabella recorded her personal objection rather than signing collectively as a princess. Viola sent a brief answer from her passage station. Luna heard her written statement read back: she still opposed any unevaluated human connection.

Five voices did not become a chorus offering themselves.

The legal officer told Oswin that the present material didn't justify lifting the movement stay now. Inner conditions remained unmet. His new request would receive formal consideration; the keys would not be handed over here. The officer then required the emergency parking operation to remain within its newly approved limits.

Oswin stayed, moving his chair where he could see the main bridge gauge.

“Then I'll watch.”

“Watch carefully,” Luna's assistant relayed. “And let us see clearly too.”

The first step was stopping new input to preparation, not shutting down the Crown of Destiny. The engineer broke the specified wheel's lead seal and passed it to the custodian in an evidence bag. Another technician watched the independent gauge on the protective system. Adrian sat at the recording position, aligning actual reports and readings with their times.

“Preparing to cut input.”

He repeated it. The engineer turned the wheel only to its first stop. A long, fine scraping came from the metal. The thin needle trembled without immediately falling.

Adrian's stomach tightened.

“The accumulation's still there,” the technician said. “Wait for local confirmation.”

The thick needle maintained its earlier rate of ascent. No sudden jump. The preparation inlet gauge fell first; only then did the thin needle's rise slow. Adrian almost felt the missing acknowledgment from the April 22 recording strips in his body. This time, speaking a request wouldn't make him enter it as complete.

“New input closed. Confirmed locally.”

He recorded the time. His voice had stopped shaking.

A trolley struck a door outside. The second maintenance cylinder was passing through a narrow doorway when a wheel caught in damaged stone. A handler began pushing harder from behind. Viola stopped him: a securing strap was pulling sideways. More force could tip the load.

The supervisor had two people steady the frame and fetched a bridging board. Viola indicated placement with her right hand, her left arm held against her chest. Hearing the commotion, Adrian nearly stood. His relief hadn't returned. He stayed and sent the liaison to ask for an actual progress report.

Elena brought packing blocks. She avoided residual-energy interfaces, handing safely movable wood to the officer. When a handler asked her to step farther away, she did. Sharing responsibility didn't require occupying the space he needed to turn.

With the board secured, the trolley crossed the crack. The engineer rechecked its restraints before allowing it onward.

“Does the door support Oswin too?” Elena murmured.

Adrian laughed despite himself. “The door supports a repair budget.”

“Put it down for tomorrow.”

Those two sentences across the desk's corner were all they managed before the copper lines called them back to their stations.

The first cylinder connected with its inspection window plainly visible. A flow limiter drew a short portion of residual energy from preparation. The cylinder's temperature color deepened slowly along its scale. It received accumulation from the drive branch, not the kingdom's entire disaster burden still striking old protections. Those pressures could still damage homes. People still needed evacuation, repairs, and care.

Watching the local gauge, the engineer closed this stage's input below the limit. Color nevertheless continued creeping along the cylinder.

“Delayed tail,” the assistant reported.

Adrian's pen stopped. He didn't declare the first cylinder empty or hurry the second into the same interface. The physical divider kept the branches separate while they waited for actual reception to finish.

Word came from the rear observation room that Luna's short consultation had ended on time. Her recommendations were already with the responsible technician; the next stage belonged to him. Adrian asked if she needed to add anything. The assistant shook his head. She had answered. Everyone's anxiety didn't make her rest a dereliction.

Adrian nodded and asked him to tell her she needn't stay until the needle fell.

The assistant returned with a tiny note.

“She says you needn't exchange every good deed for praise either.”

Elena finally laughed. Adrian placed the note outside the technical record and said he understood. It wouldn't enter the findings, but it loosened something in his tightly held shoulders.

The first cylinder's indicator settled. The residual gauge reached the permitted range for changing stages. The engineer left the hot cylinder on its isolated stand rather than removing it and turned to the rechecked second branch. As reception began again, the thin needle finally moved distinctly downward.

Then the protective system's thick needle jumped another division.

“Hold!”

All shifting stopped. The second branch remained in its current safe position; nobody panicked and closed everything simultaneously. Adrian looked up. Oswin had risen too. That movement wasn't his conspiracy. The external peak had arrived.

A report came from the corridor: riverside patrols had brought two households out. Falling grit had cut one resident. The cat remained missing in the roof beams. Hearing that his sister was outside, the apprentice gripped his chair and began crying with his head lowered.

Cecilia went to him. She didn't promise everything would come back. Offering a clean cloth, she waited until he could speak, then asked which actual receiving post his sister should go to.

Isabella reassigned two places still able to receive people through the liaison staff. Seats already filled by the first wagon couldn't be offered to the second. Nor would serving a princess justify extending a driver's shift. His replacement was still traveling. She allowed a real waiting interval.

Slowly, the main bridge gauge stabilized. It didn't fall, but its steep rise stopped. Only after checking two independent protective-side readings did the engineer permit the second stage to continue.

While they waited for that recheck, Evelyn was called to the maintenance drawing. A worn inscription allowed two readings that could affect the final backing-down sequence. She studied the copy and shook her head. She remembered one version, but wouldn't let memory authorize their hands.

The custodian removed the original appendix, already delivered to the site, from its rigid holder and unfolded it along the crease. Evelyn identified the passage she'd once excerpted. Here, she admitted, she'd omitted the wait required on the return sequence. Her voice stayed level until it caught on the last word.

“Did you know that would suggest one could pull it straight through?” the engineer asked.

“I knew my extract was shorter. I didn't ask who would remember what I'd left out.”

Nobody answered immediately. Adrian saw the corner of his own withdrawn diagram in its document bag and recognized the shame. Familiarity didn't entitle him to explain her omission as harmless. Nor could his earlier error prove hers deliberately malicious.

“Read the whole line,” he said.

The original's two waiting conditions were finally matched word for word. The engineer marked his operating sheet in red and gave it to another person to check. Evelyn stepped aside and requested that her acknowledged responsibility for the extract be recorded. She sought no exchange of this help for the old questions against her.

Oswin had watched throughout. Did she think this would redeem everyone? She lifted her exhausted eyes.

“No. What I can do now is stop them acting on a page with another line missing.”

Paper and instruments filled the room again. No pardon followed, no sudden embrace of universal acceptance. Someone moved her water within reach. She thanked them quietly and waited for the next question she could answer.

Before sitting, Oswin asked Adrian, “What if it won't stabilize next time?”

“Then we stop according to the actual conditions and evacuate whoever needs to leave.”

“Including them?”

“Including all of us working here. We don't choose one person to stay inside so everyone else can have a tidier ending.”

He didn't look toward any woman for an emotional reward. Elena held the desk rail. Viola remained at the corner. Luna was resting. Cecilia sat beside the crying apprentice, while Isabella briefed the new liaison on the last wagon still outstanding. They were doing their own work.

The second cylinder's temperature color finally stopped advancing. One last low-level accumulation remained on the preparatory side. It had to be released during the next backward shift, and the mechanical interlock hadn't yet seated.

Adrian signed for the two receptions he'd actually witnessed. He didn't write success beneath them. When he looked up, Elena shook a small bag of cold bread at him.

She wasn't waiting to become his reward for rescuing her.

She was reminding him that the next handover had arrived. He'd promised to come out.
