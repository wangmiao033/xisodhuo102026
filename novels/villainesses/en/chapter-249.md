# Chapter 249: Broken Before the Border

The report reached the gate before they did.

On the evening of April 13, Adrian stepped out at the suburban relay on the border supply route, carrying permission to observe. It was a short carriage journey from the capital. Border described the destination it served, not a miraculous crossing of half the kingdom in an afternoon.

The actual border station still required a courier. What had broken first was part of the route toward it.

Burned adhesive scented the air beneath the station windows. Staff had disconnected the local branch and roped off the work area. Oil lamps stood clear of the conduit. A newly fitted sharing-node housing leaned on its support, its unconnected buffer plate cracked. A priority dispatch tag remained clipped to the old transfer assembly beside it.

Adrian stayed outside the rope. Permission to examine wasn't permission to touch everything.

“Anyone hurt?”

A worker's hand had been cut by flying debris and treated. Nobody had carried a pilot load. The halted handling crew wondered about wages; the stationmaster told them to record actual standby time. Broken apparatus didn't automatically erase their hours.

First, the academy technician requested the original April 12 work order. The station works office had issued it and the duty staff acknowledged receipt. It permitted an unpowered housing fit in a disabled outer position, not human connection or movement of the old linkage; the academy premises permit did not extend here. Fitters had treated supply disconnection as sufficient, and the work-order check had missed mechanical coupling to the old lever. That real omission required investigation alongside the subsequent reassignment. Only then did the technician show Adrian the tag from the safe side. It was no spontaneously appearing curse, but a reassignment issued by the old maintenance dispatch office through established channels. The line was to reserve a transfer position for reset preparation. Issue and receipt were recorded. Its precise scope still needed comparison. The station held a received order, not simply a rumor about what somebody important wanted. That made the sequence easier to investigate, but no easier to dismiss. Whoever examined it would need to distinguish what had been authorized, what the operator had understood, and what the machinery had actually done. None of those questions could be answered merely by pointing at the damaged plate.

Following the old transfer drawing, an operator had moved a linked lever. An outer clamp tightened with it. The new isolated support wasn't on that drawing. The clamp trapped its foot, carrying pressure through the metal frame into the unpowered housing. The material hadn't been struck by divine judgment. An old mechanism had crushed it.

“We thought disconnecting supply meant isolation,” the operator said.

The technician asked which section had actually been disconnected and required the scene retained, removed parts stored separately. No energy flow didn't mean no mechanical movement. Luna's isolated academy installation couldn't be copied by appearance onto another transfer assembly and declared identical.

Adrian heard his own name. Someone had said he organized the alternative, and since the princess sought coordination, the old priority reservation should take precedence. He'd never said that. He could see how it had helped push two incompatible arrangements onto one support.

An assistant wanted to tear off the tag so it wouldn't look effective. Adrian asked the stationmaster to mark the branch stopped while the appropriate authority dealt with the order. Tearing up evidence wasn't stopping a hazard.

After measurements, the technician boxed a fragment. Fresh compression marks matched the old clamp's scrape. Two workers separately described the lever sequence without coaching each other. Observation supported their accounts. It didn't prove the issuer intended damage. It did establish that this dispatch action had damaged the node.

The institution hadn't swung a hammer. It used apparatus still within its reach and squeezed what wasn't on its drawing out of place.

Removing the new support might restore the old branch faster, the stationmaster suggested. Adrian asked its purpose. A reserved transfer for reset preparation—not tonight's water deliveries, and not all border protection. Ordinary maintenance used another branch still undergoing checks.

Adrian nearly agreed to dismantle first. Making something move again would show they weren't simply holding up work. Then he looked at the cracked buffer and remembered the people awaiting answers after signing.

“Assess immediate safety first. I request that both interfaces and the clamp remain for independent examination. If danger requires movement, record it and let the technicians decide.”

They would miss tonight's last planned freight departure. Alternative transport needed costing; tomorrow morning's completion wasn't guaranteed.

Adrian signed as the applicant rather than hiding delay behind everyone agrees. The coordinator should explain the actual interruption, not announce a defeated conspiracy. He chose to preserve evidence while acknowledging that a justified delay still cost something. His name on the request would show which choice he had made when a quicker option was offered. He couldn't honestly promise the workers that the delay would disappear by morning, or ask them to regard a good reason as payment for their time.

While they waited, the driver demanded an answer. His loaded cart couldn't remain all night because one new device was broken. His horse needed water; another morning order awaited him, and his daughter expected medicine in town.

The stationmaster asked for another quarter hour. The driver pointed to the departure time already altered twice. Each quarter hour came with the same request.

Adrian checked the load sheet. Ordinary packing timber unrelated to the damaged node belonged on the functioning branch. Holding everything meant two destinations waiting needlessly. He asked the duty technician to identify what could safely be unloaded and what should continue, rather than deciding from unfamiliar item names.

The driver agreed to rearrangement within existing unloading time, not an unpaid extra trip to another warehouse. Adrian withdrew that request and had liaison staff seek transport actually willing to take it.

Beneath the first bundle was a small iron chest absent from this station's list. An assistant suggested emergency additions could be recorded afterward. The driver objected. Those lock fittings belonged to another customer. Sharing a cart didn't make them station property.

Adrian stopped the handling. The seal number matched the driver's other delivery note. The chest stayed aboard. Its contents might have saved them time tonight. But taking even that small box because they needed it would make their demand for an explanation of the crushing lever rather hollow.

The driver remained unenthusiastic after watering his horse. He'd follow the confirmed route and promise nothing else. Adrian accepted the unlovely, dependable answer. The cart left carrying someone else's chest and one convenient way they might have filled the night.

Rearranging what remained exposed an inspection gap behind the damaged support. From outside the danger area, the technician illuminated it and measured the missing physical stop between support and clamp. Tomorrow's repair now meant more than replacing a plate. The lever needed a boundary it couldn't cross unnoticed.

Viola arrived later without heavy shoulder armor, her left arm supported. An ordinary guard carried her plans. Her permission concerned personnel routes and temporary safe positions, not command of the station.

A knight tried to hand over his duty arrangements on seeing her. She told him to retain the current supervisor and identify conflicts instead of replacing authority merely because she'd appeared. Adrian accompanied her short permitted inspection. At the third corner she rested her right hand on a rail.

“Pain?”

“Tiredness. You needn't combine the words.”

He neither ordered her to sit nor pretended not to notice. After the final exit, she requested rest herself, leaving remaining comments for her assistant to read back.

The narrow changing room was available for a defined interval, provided the adjoining clothes cupboard stayed accessible. Viola asked a female attendant to help remove her remaining light protective gear. Adrian waited outside until she requested water.

Without the collar strap she looked younger and more tired. She found him standing straight and asked whether he intended to guard the door all night.

“Until your rest period ends. There's a shift system. I'm not replacing it.”

“Good. If you stand like that, I won't sleep.”

He found a chair. She noticed its unstable leg and made him exchange it. Was that her last inspection before lying down? he asked.

“I don't want to dream about you falling over.”

She closed half the door.

A young messenger brought a route note Viola had already seen. Adrian asked whether it concerned a new emergency. No; he wanted her personal confirmation again. Adrian directed him to the current supervisor, with a clear arrangement to wake her if circumstances actually escalated. A resting person shouldn't be the final stamp every paper needed.

“Some people can sleep even during danger,” the messenger muttered.

“She handed over what she could do. Your paper names the person who received it.”

He looked and went to find that person. Adrian didn't pursue him with a lesson. From inside came one soft movement, then quiet.

He wasn't guarding an intimate moment he could claim. Only time she had chosen to make unavailable to work.

When Viola woke, ordinary lamps lit the yard. She thanked him and admitted she wasn't used to others seeing her without protective gear.

“I'm afraid they'll think I can do less.”

“You did less today. I think that's true, not a misunderstanding.”

She glared. He hastily added that doing less still left her herself. She didn't owe every unused minute to everyone else.

Then she laughed. “Your opening attempts at comfort invite duels.”

“At least wait until your left arm improves.”

“I meant chess.”

“Then you may threaten me immediately.”

She didn't turn the closeness into a relationship obligation. He didn't please her by promising the future he'd discussed with Elena. She asked for a game another time. He agreed they could arrange one, and waited now for her assistant.

The independent examiner arrived that night. The damaged node and support could be removed together into custody. The old clamp remained out of use while its manager received the specific defect report. Any wider suspension required further determination. The priority tag was copied for the case; the station retained the original under its procedure.

No answer had come from the distant border. One suburban failure wasn't proof that every border node was destroyed. Silence wasn't confirmation that all remained sound. Adrian watched two inquiries depart and accepted that answers must travel real roads back. Anxiety could accompany the courier, but it could not replace him.

The technician handed him tomorrow's repair requirements. Restoring safe separation on the ordinary line needed suitable supports, available insulating material, workers familiar with the old transfer assembly, and permission for staged checks. Returning the new node to the same position wouldn't constitute repair.

Could Adrian organize people and transport while the designated technician controlled specialist work? The stationmaster agreed to check the proposed crew and materials. Enthusiastic helpers weren't to arrive and start dismantling conduits.

Adrian returned to the capital without a victory announcement. Outside the carriage ran an ordinary usable road. Inside lay a repair request whose requirements needed to become actual objects and actual people.

The old system had cracked a node. Tomorrow they must repair more than that fracture. They needed a gap the same lever couldn't quietly close again.
