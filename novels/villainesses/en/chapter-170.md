# Chapter 170: The Doors She Could Close

The afternoon reading table was narrower than the morning's.

Adrian tucked his elbows in and nearly upset the ink anyway. Opposite him, Luna moved her cup to a low stand with her left hand. Any spill today, she announced, was not a new experiment.

This was one scheduled document visit, with rest arranged and a carriage waiting. Her right hand couldn't handle apparatus. None awaited her here.

The supervising official stopped them putting their old contact register inside the volume.

“Beside it, please.”

Adrian complied. The paper was remarkably similar. Had he continued, the next reader might have wondered when the extra sheet arrived.

They were allowed certified copies of the current appointment, execution scope, and present forwarding instructions—not Evelyn's entire protected working history. Other people's names were covered, with reasons attached. Luna didn't lift the coverings. She asked whether omissions affected the provisions they needed to understand.

Addresses weren't relevant, the official said. Extracts could be provided for two obscured authorization categories. While they waited, wooden crates thudded against the steps outside. Adrian glanced at the door.

“I want to know what's in every box too,” Luna said. “That doesn't turn them all into evidence.”

He smiled and returned to the first page.

Evelyn wasn't simply a messenger. Under an issued decision, she could mark designated historical records held pending verification, arrange approved transfers, and require missing acknowledgments. For specified custody irregularities, she could temporarily halt that transfer, subject to supervisory review within the stated period.

“She can keep something behind a door.”

“A particular record,” the official corrected.

Adrian added the words.

Nothing authorized her to revive a discontinued betrothal by personal preference. Holding a record didn't detain the person named in it. She couldn't decide who bore a risk, sign for someone else, or extend authority indefinitely because a problem remained.

Luna turned a page and stopped at an annex concerning conversion of historical indexes.

“This.”

Once conditions and specified checks were satisfied, the execution liaison could move an old index entry into a technical-review queue. That didn't establish a valid current relationship. It could, however, bring an ended historical reference back into a present administrative process.

Adrian clenched his left hand beneath the table. The scar didn't glow or interpret the sentence.

R-074 had lapsed, yet remained in risk indexes. Here was a possible procedural route for that, rather than merely a number that refused to leave.

“Does this prove she did it?”

No. It established what the appointment permitted when conditions were met. A particular action required its event record, authority, and acknowledgment. Luna shook her head too, refusing to let his urgency stamp the blank.

He slowly relaxed his hand and read the conditions.

Source version, supervisory approval reference, and recipient confirmation were required. Without confirmation, the entry could say delivered, awaiting response—not complete.

“So what did clean mean when the assistant was told to make a clean copy?” Luna asked.

The official couldn't answer for another person's experience. They could compare the public format with the fields he was willing to describe. Whether a draft survived, who requested recopying, and whether awaiting response became complete were concrete questions.

Smaller than she controls the records. Small enough to grasp.

Luna had Adrian note the page and checked the reference again. Discovering a route didn't settle the harder question of whether it had been used.

The supplementary extracts arrived. Adrian noticed that the current execution instruction required confirmation of specified volumes by ten on January 30. The premises-preservation decision came later.

“Someone knew beforehand?”

He stopped almost as soon as he spoke.

The official directed him to the preceding page. The confirmation request originated in a routine transfer check on January 29. A subsequent problem didn't turn an earlier check into advance planning.

Luna didn't rescue his inference.

“You've put what we know afterward on her desk beforehand.”

“Yes.”

He struck through his suspicion, retaining both it and the reason for correction. Anxiety remained, but no longer rested entirely on that time.

The genuine gap lay below. A transfer summary said receipt complete, yet the delivered page range omitted an attachment. Perhaps the summary hadn't been updated. Perhaps the attachment had separate custody. Perhaps incomplete material had been called complete. One execution signature couldn't select among those possibilities.

The official asked whether they would accept a narrower correction check concerning the six named people. Less material might permit an explanation before tomorrow.

Adrian looked up. This didn't require choosing a betrothal partner. It wasn't the earlier priority form; being quicker didn't make both proposals identical.

Luna asked whether it prevented other applicants submitting statements. It didn't, though their checks would be scheduled separately.

After considering it, Adrian agreed to examination of the part he could request, retaining his question about shared intake procedures. He couldn't withdraw Beth's request. He needn't obstruct an accurate answer to his own merely to appear selfless.

“Not all six agreeing. Only my part.”

Luna defined her scope separately. She wasn't against speed. She objected to speed quietly replacing the question.

The official recorded them apart. Adrian realized how long it had been since he'd allowed a less heroic answer: accept some help, without pretending the unresolved things no longer existed.

He requested preservation of the summary's versions, corresponding confirmations, and corrections for the reviewing officer. The official could receive and forward that request, not instantly authorize every internal document's disclosure.

Quick footsteps sounded outside, followed by an argument. A man carrying a paper tube had been stopped at the door. He wanted copies described as fully returned.

“They say returned. I don't have them!”

Asked to clear the passage, he struck the tube against the frame. It contained only an inventory.

Luna's right hand shifted on its cushion and stopped. Adrian didn't charge out to testify for a stranger. He asked the official whether a dispute involving a similar completion status could be registered separately at the temporary counter.

A colleague took the man next door to sit down. Still angry, he objected to being treated as an unstable visitor. This was his third trip; he needed to reopen his shop.

Through the open doorway, Adrian heard questions about the acknowledgment and promised delivery date, not a demand that the man become quiet enough to deserve service. Eventually the tube stopped hitting the door.

Their own reading time didn't expand. Luna's left hand ached by the last page. She asked Adrian to note two remaining references rather than forcing herself to copy the volume.

Authorized extracts were provided before they left. Restricted technical annexes remained sealed. Missing information wasn't permission to invent an ending. If historical reading equipment proved necessary, its condition and authorization needed separate examination. No activation was scheduled today.

Luna wrote that down without adding I can fix it.

Outside, she immediately sat in the carriage. Adrian waited for the step to be raised before asking about pain.

“My left hand aches. My right dislikes me forgetting the time.”

“You can go home. I'll deliver the request.”

She nodded, then called him back.

“When I corrected you, I sounded as though I was mocking you.”

“You didn't laugh.”

“I enjoyed having the advantage a little. Two days ago you caught me out.”

He didn't immediately say it was fine. Looking at the window frame, Luna continued. His inference did need correction. She didn't want today's correct answer to cancel her earlier apology.

“Please correct me,” he said after thinking. “I want that. Just don't make every mistake a turn at losing.”

“All right.”

No cleverer joke followed. She waved with her left hand as the carriage moved; he waved back and walked to the counter.

Evelyn's young assistant was separating misdirected receipts outside. Adrian asked no questions about her private life. He wanted to know whether the man seeking copies could obtain an independent acknowledgment of his complaint.

He already had one. He hadn't received everything requested, but the dispute was no longer recorded as no objection remaining.

“Does it seem too slow?” the assistant asked.

“Yes.”

The young man had apparently expected a polite smile. Instead, he nodded. Calling a process slow didn't authorize tearing open sealed archives. Nor did a valid seal make slowness admirable.

Adrian waited while the assistant found the correct tray. On the counter lay three pieces of string, a blunt knife for opening envelopes, and a cup with a chipped handle. Nothing there explained the vast fear the room had acquired for him. Someone could do harm using ordinary objects; ordinary objects could also be used to correct it. Neither possibility announced itself by looking sinister.

When the assistant offered to carry his request inside, Adrian asked for the usual receipt instead of treating a friendly conversation as proof of delivery. The young man didn't take offense. He counted the pages, made one correction to the total, and read it back.

Outside, Adrian unfolded the receipt to check the time. For a moment he wanted to return and ask when there would be an answer. The time for forwarding was already written. He folded it again and bought a roll from the stall near the corner. He was hungry, not disloyal to the inquiry.

At home, Martha asked whether he had eaten. He showed her the empty wrapper. She inspected it with exaggerated gravity, then said evidence of one roll did not establish that he needed no supper. For once, he welcomed the additional requirement.

That evening the six of them examined the extracts. Elena wanted Evelyn's name at the top. Cecilia reminded her that tomorrow concerned intake and limited access. Focusing only on one person's reputation could let the representative avoid ordinary applicants' difficulties.

Elena didn't immediately agree.

Adrian placed two sheets together: specific questions about execution acts on one; procedures still possible under preservation restrictions and people shut out by complete on the other.

“I don't want to let her go,” Elena said quietly.

“Then don't trade answerable questions for a title we can only be angry at,” Cecilia replied.

It was blunt. She braced for a retort. After a long silence, Elena moved her first line to the separate sheet.

Luna sat beside the fire, right palm cushioned. When Adrian passed, she didn't call him over with paperwork. Could he reread the passage about the captain losing his hat tomorrow after supper?

“You've heard it.”

“I like knowing what happens this time.”

He agreed, with another time to be arranged if the review ran late. Reading together wouldn't become punishment he must fulfill. She smiled and nodded.

At the table, Elena and Cecilia were preparing different things to say. Night settled outside; the registration building remained shut. At least tomorrow Adrian wouldn't be asking about a mysterious universal key. He would ask which hand, under which piece of paper, had closed which door.
