# Why Do All the Villainesses Want to Marry Me?
## Volume One: A Betrothal Written in Blood
### Chapter 21: The New Death List

On October first, Adrian found himself wishing breakfast would last forever.

Not because the eggs were particularly good.

But as long as Elena sat across from him, he could postpone answering questions about the letter he'd received last night.

She pushed a plate of toast toward him.

"You've put sugar in your tea three times."

Adrian looked down.

Three empty sugar spoons lay beside his cup.

"Maybe your biscuits were so sweet they damaged my sense of taste."

"You said they were good."

"They were. The blackest one would make an excellent knife sharpener."

Elena glared at him.

Normally he'd ask for another biscuit just to hear her complain about his lack of taste.

Today, he didn't smile.

She set down her cutlery.

"Didn't you sleep?"

"I was up late organizing the accounts."

"Hale said yesterday's reports were already finished."

"I thought of something else."

She studied him without pushing.

"Then don't spend the whole day locked in the study."

Adrian nodded.

He couldn't meet her eyes.

Late the previous night, after Hale had gone home, a Judicial Office archivist had delivered a notice.

Because old case R-074 listed Adrian as a possible related party, the old archives department was required to notify him of a redacted historical risk register. He had two days to decide whether to petition for correction of his identity information.

When he first signed the receipt, he'd expected another tiresome form.

Then he read the attached page alone in his room.

One column bore the words:

*September 20 — Disposition Proceedings Terminated.*

The person named was Elena Rosenberg.

In the next column was a freshly added entry.

*Adrian Reed.*

The heading wasn't an execution warrant.

It read:

**Substitute Risk-Bearing Order.**

But in Adrian's memories of the game, lists ranking people as possible sacrifices had never been a good sign.

The lower corner carried a fresh blue amendment stamp.

September twentieth.

The day the judge had prevented Elena's immediate punishment.

Adrian had spent the entire night hiding the notice.

He'd told himself he was protecting her.

If she didn't know the risk had shifted toward him, she could at least enjoy breakfast and continue investigating the Judicial Office without worrying.

Watching her carefully peel a little orange for him, he realized how ridiculous that sounded.

"My lady."

Elena looked up.

"Yes?"

"I need to visit Hale this morning."

"All right. Shall I come?"

"...I'd rather go alone."

Her hands paused.

"Is it a work matter?"

"Yes."

"Then leave your itinerary with Martha."

She placed the orange on his plate.

No more questions.

Somehow, that made Adrian feel even guiltier.

On his way to the lawyer's office, he unfolded the notice again and again.

There was a delivery number on the reverse, and instructions for requesting access to the original register.

At least the paper hadn't materialized out of thin air like a magical prophecy.

Someone had written it.

Someone had copied it.

Someone had altered whatever original it came from.

There had to be a trail.

Hale frowned as soon as he saw the notice.

"You received this last night?"

"Yes."

"Why didn't you bring it to me then?"

"I was afraid of putting everyone else in danger."

The lawyer removed his spectacles.

"Mr. Reed, delaying legal advice doesn't make danger disappear."

"I know."

"Let's start with what this actually is. A redacted notice to a related party. It is not a judgment ordering your death."

"Then what does 'risk-bearing order' mean?"

"An obsolete term. I don't pretend to understand every provision of the historical scheme." Hale pointed to the footnote. "It cites regulations from a discontinued system of court assistance. We must request the legislative history before assuming any of it remains operative."

Adrian let out a long breath.

"So it doesn't mean I'm going to die?"

"No lawful order could sentence you to death on the strength of this page alone."

"You said *lawful*."

Hale didn't pretend to miss the point.

"Which is why we'll establish who created it and why it was amended on September twentieth. A name written on paper isn't fate."

Adrian looked down.

He wanted to believe that.

But he remembered the way Leon had known his name at the palace.

Someone had been waiting for Adrian Reed to arrive.

And perhaps that someone had finally decided it was his turn to appear on a list.

Hale turned over the notice and examined the stamp.

"The delivery number is valid, and the office named here is the old archives department. We still can't confirm which original register the text came from. But as someone listed in it, you're entitled to request the basis for the entry and instructions for correcting it."

He drafted two separate applications.

One requested the history of the Substitute Risk-Bearing Order.

The other requested independent verification of who submitted and approved the September twentieth amendment.

"Why two?" Adrian asked.

"So they can't dismiss every question at once under a single, vague claim of confidentiality."

Adrian nodded.

He remembered yesterday's discovery.

"Could the Fate Maintenance Fee be connected to this list?"

"Possibly. At present, the terms are similar. That's all. We don't know what the money maintains. If we write speculation as fact, we'll make their job easier."

"I understand."

Hale looked at him.

"Does Lady Elena know?"

Adrian fell silent.

"Not yet."

"Will you tell her?"

"Tonight."

"That's your decision," Hale said. "But don't mistake concealment for protection. She respected your right to choose when the Temple came. You should let her decide how she wants to face a risk that concerns you."

Adrian stared at his hands.

He'd told Elena he wasn't her property.

Now he was deciding, without asking, what dangers she was allowed to know about.

That wasn't much of a partnership.

When he left Hale's office, the sky was still bright.

He'd planned to visit the lane behind the old courthouse and see whether B-17 appeared on any other public register. He had even drawn a route designed to avoid the Rosenberg carriages.

At the street corner, he stopped.

The baker's wife from yesterday was cleaning her front window. She recognized him and waved.

"Mr. Reed! No young lady with you today?"

"Official business."

"Bring her next time. She took the leftover turnovers home yesterday, and she asked where to buy the spices for baking apple pastries."

Adrian went still.

So Elena had even gone looking for the ingredients herself.

The baker's wife said something else.

He barely heard it.

The solitary route he'd drawn suddenly looked like a very poor idea.

He didn't go to the courthouse.

He went back to the Rosenberg estate.

Martha was clearing the remaining biscuits from the tea room.

"Mr. Reed. Her Ladyship's been looking for you."

"Is something urgent?"

"No." Martha looked at him. "She just asked the kitchens to save you some lunch."

Adrian stood in the doorway.

"Where is she?"

"The greenhouse."

Condensation dotted the glass ceiling. Elena stood beside a rack of plants, trimming dead leaves, her cloak hanging near the door.

She didn't scold him when he arrived.

"Did Hale help?"

"We filed two applications."

"For what?"

Adrian drew out the notice he'd folded so often the paper had gone soft.

"This arrived last night. I didn't tell you."

Elena set down her shears.

"Why?"

"I thought if I understood it first, you wouldn't have to worry."

Her eyes cooled.

Not in the playful, intimidating way he knew.

"May I see it?"

He handed it over.

She read the entire page, then looked up.

"Why didn't you tell me last night?"

"Because you only just survived September twentieth. I didn't want you to hear someone had moved the next risk onto me."

For a long time, Elena stared at the paper.

"Do you think I was happy yesterday because it meant nobody else would ever be in danger?"

He said nothing.

"I was happy because we could finally investigate together. And this morning you were planning to leave me here while you handled everything alone?"

"I know. I was wrong."

"You don't have to turn every sentence into an apology."

She gave the notice back.

"I only want an answer to one question. If I were in danger, would you want me to tell you?"

"Of course."

"Then that's what I want too."

Wind stirred the vines against the greenhouse glass.

Adrian looked at her.

This time he didn't insist that she had no reason to worry about him.

He didn't smile and pretend everything was fine.

"I'm scared," he said. "When I saw my name, I thought someone had already decided the day I was going to die. I couldn't sleep. I was afraid to close my eyes."

Elena's face softened.

"I'm scared too. But you stood beside me when I faced that date. Let me stand beside you now."

Adrian wanted to take her hand.

Instead of simply reaching for it, he asked.

"May I?"

She held it out.

"You don't need to guess my answer."

He took her warm hand.

No accidental collision.

No orchestra.

No crowd waiting to turn the moment into a newspaper illustration.

Only the damp air of the greenhouse and two people whose breathing hadn't quite settled.

After a while, Elena glanced at the document again.

"We'll keep it with Hale's applications. First we establish whether this is another forged notice designed to lure you out of the estate."

"He verified the delivery reference, but hasn't examined the source register."

"Then we keep looking. And why does this page show only your name?"

Adrian unfolded it.

"I was looking at the new entry."

Elena pointed toward the other columns.

The blacked-out areas weren't random strokes. They followed the grid of the old register, one line at a time.

Adrian had been so terrified of seeing his own name that he hadn't counted the other entries.

Four more spaces remained.

Each carried an old case code and a vague identity category.

The names were entirely concealed.

Hale had made no attempt to expose them. Those details belonged to other people.

But why retain those four spaces on a notice addressed to Adrian?

Elena moved the page toward the light.

"We can't uncover names we're not authorized to see," she said. "But we can ask the archives why a notice about you includes four other protected individuals."

Adrian nodded.

For once, he didn't suggest running off on his own to find the answer.

He laid the sheet flat between them.

Elena's entry at the top had been marked *Disposition Terminated*.

Below it, the next listed person was Adrian Reed.

**And beneath him were four blacked-out names.**

Someone had tried to make him believe this was a danger meant for him alone.

But that list had never been designed for just one person.
