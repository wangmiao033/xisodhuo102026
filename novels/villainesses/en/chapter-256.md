# Chapter 256: She Didn't Ask for a Pardon First

On April 19, Evelyn Vale arrived half an hour early.

She wore no concealing cloak. Her former recorder's coat had faded with washing, though the hard case beside her was carefully cleaned. Asked to wait, she sat by the corridor while an apprentice returning a borrowed volume searched his bag for a missing insert.

By the time Adrian arrived, the apprentice had found it and was apologizing. Evelyn hadn't hidden his error or assumed a senior's right to lecture. She'd only shifted along the bench to make room for the spread papers.

That didn't cancel her past. Adrian realized he was trying to find an easy answer in every small gesture and stopped guessing.

The meeting was not a private conversation from which Adrian could later select whichever promises he preferred. Someone responsible for custody would receive the objects; someone else would record what each person actually said. That separation made the room less comfortable, but it also made the handover something other people could examine. An independent custodian and recorder attended the meeting. Evelyn first supplied an inventory. Some operational copies had been lawfully held in her former post; others were indexes she'd transcribed later. She identified original locations. No complete truth appeared from her sleeve, and Adrian wasn't asked to sign a declaration of belief.

“If you put my words straight down as facts, I came here for nothing.”

Elena looked at her. “What should we put down?”

“Testimony requiring verification.”

Evelyn opened the uppermost page. Its number corresponded to the old catalog entry beside Luna's trace reading, followed by maintenance references for contacts beneath the execution hall. These weren't rescue instructions. They related external permissions to mechanical-lock handovers. One page was missing between references. She marked it with blank paper rather than filling it from memory.

The custodian checked earlier transfer marks. Some old receipts could be verified; other details rested on Evelyn's recollection. They were listed separately. She'd delivered documents by such indexes and knew how something called temporary at one desk could become irreversible execution authority at another.

At irreversible, Adrian's left hand contracted. The scar didn't shine or supply an answer. His body remembered something lost before language caught up.

“Why now?” Elena asked.

“Yesterday's publication showed me you weren't planning to put every responsibility inside one dead person's name.”

“Or it showed you we'd reach you eventually.”

“Both. I'm afraid.”

The silence was more credible than a heroic confession. She feared former colleagues calling her traitor, families refusing to see her, and a new system proving that what she'd called no alternative sometimes meant she'd stopped asking. Fear didn't make her innocent or oblige anyone to comfort her.

She offered formal testimony, materials, and cooperation, requesting that ordinary copyists' addresses remain private while evidence was secured. Did that protection include herself, Elena asked. The fact of her willingness to testify could be public. Responsible staff should determine specific arrangements; not everyone who'd touched a page needed to be pushed into the street.

Adrian had prepared a promise of safety and left it unsaid. Available personnel could escort her between this room and a designated questioning place, with proper custody of materials. They couldn't guarantee nobody hated her or promise a future court's decision. Reception explained what it could actually provide. Evelyn chose to accept.

A Temple clerk arrived carrying a formal notice rather than a secret threat. Material concerning old execution facilities, if used for an alternative proposal, required confirmation of its applicable scope by the original system's custodians. Tomorrow's conditional review wouldn't automatically disappear because new material had been submitted.

Elena frowned at the opportunity for those custodians to delay. The recorder acknowledged some current basis for the notice. It couldn't simply be discarded. Confirming scope, however, didn't grant a custodian exclusive judgment of its own behavior or permission to remove unconfirmed material from the file.

Evelyn asked the clerk to leave the notice. One line used completion of external preparations to call subsequent contact handover routine. She'd written sentences like that. Reading this one, she saw how routine swallowed conditions not yet established.

She didn't seize a pen to alter an official notice. In her own statement she explained that the old execution hall registered external preparation and actual activation in separate volumes. Completing the outer volume didn't establish the inner conditions. She could attest to transfers she'd handled, not swear every present page remained identical. An independent examiner must obtain and compare current records.

Would that be enough to stop tomorrow? Adrian asked.

No. It showed them where to ask. An old recorder's memory wasn't a ruling.

Nothing in her answer offered the relief he had imagined on seeing the case. The missing page stayed missing. The meanings of marks still required people qualified to examine them. A woman who had once delivered orders could tell them how a delivery worked, not magically erase what every delivered order might do. For an instant, the limited help angered him. Time was short; he wanted a key fitting every lock. Then he remembered Luna saying stop when white turned gray. Needing an answer didn't entitle him to demand that someone become one.

At lunch, Elena drew him into the courtyard and offered a hard pastry. It was the kitchen's last unsent portion. He mustn't look as though she was feeding him evidence-sealing wax.

“I haven't bitten it.”

“Your eyebrows have.”

He tried a bite and admitted the eyebrows had judged correctly. She moved her cup nearer so he could dip it. She hadn't chosen it simply to watch him manage embarrassment. Wind stirred her hair. He asked to brush it aside; she nodded, then waited until his hand withdrew before naming the day's hardest part.

Not hearing Evelyn admit wrongdoing.

“You still want to find everybody a road without a cost.”

He didn't argue. Elena didn't tell him merely to trust love. She wanted him to keep searching, but not discover someday that he'd quietly written his own name in a final column nobody else had seen.

Watching pastry soften in the cup, he asked her to call him on it if he began again, without first proving she could speak gently. That ability, she assured him, had never been in doubt. They laughed briefly. The closeness didn't remove the afternoon's problem; it let him return to the table carrying his own fear.

Luna hadn't come. Resting under medical instructions, she sent one technical question through her assistant: did the old contact index specify a retreat and where residue went after stopping, rather than merely how to send power in? Evelyn placed it beside the missing page. Her present material couldn't answer completely.

Could the assistant take every paper back for Luna's judgment? Adrian requested only verified copies and explicit questions. First ask whether she wanted to hear them during a permitted non-casting period. Not using magic didn't mean not tiring. The assistant agreed and removed half the contents of the bag she'd meant to fill.

Sorting actual materials took longer than talking. Two apparently duplicate indexes differed by a corner symbol. The custodian nearly grouped them together. Evelyn pointed it out, knowing only that they'd been used in different folders—not inventing technical meanings. The recorder copied each separately and had her read them back. Ink stains were noted, not tidied into attractive lines.

Somebody suggested awarding her a formal role in the new proposal so cooperation wouldn't look mistrusted. Adrian refused. Verified evidence could be used; she could testify about experience. New authority needn't reward cooperation. Evelyn lowered her eyes, then said that sounded more like a road she could continue on than the bargain she'd expected to accept.

Near closing, a former attendant's daughter arrived. The public notice had told her she could submit old-case leads. Hearing Evelyn was here, she stopped in the corridor. Her father once supplied night lighting to the execution hall. An unpaid bill remained at home, but somebody had warned that submitting his name would mark him as an accomplice.

Evelyn came to the doorway without requiring the woman to acknowledge her recent change. Had she known her father? After checking the dates, Evelyn said perhaps, but a surname alone wasn't enough. She could search the material. She couldn't promise he bore no responsibility, or assume delivering lamps meant knowing whom they illuminated.

Eyes red, the daughter said that wasn't reassuring at all.

Evelyn admitted it and stepped aside. Actual intake staff explained submitting a bill copy. Her presence wouldn't become a reconciliation the woman must accept. The daughter left only a way to inquire, deciding to speak to her father first. No testimony meant no testimony; the recorder didn't fill the blank for her.

Adrian saw Evelyn gripping the case until her knuckles whitened. Once, she murmured, she'd have regarded somebody refusing immediate cooperation as obstructing important work. Now she understood that leaving also required someone not to block the door. She removed the family's private circumstances from the intended public statement and referred them through a separate closed inquiry route. That wasn't completed atonement. It was one delivery she could actually change today.

Before the meeting ended, she signed her statement. Her hand stayed steady, then rested a long time. Elena asked whether anything needed correction.

Yes. Change I only passed the documents along to I chose to keep passing those documents along. The first made it sound as though the hand had never belonged to her.

Her correction did not enlarge her knowledge or reduce anybody else's loss. It only made the sentence describe a choice she could answer for. Elena waited while the recorder changed it, and Evelyn read it back before accepting the final wording. Not every responsibility was settled. Materials were separately sealed, she received custody receipts, and formal questioning had its own appointment. The apprentice had packed his papers. Seeing her emerge, he moved aside. She waited for a paper trolley before following her escort. The testimony was no longer in her arms to withdraw whenever she wished. Its copy remained hers.

Adrian didn't announce them companions or turn away as she left. She nodded and went toward another door where questions would still be asked.

At dusk, the independent examination office reported receipt of current external-preparation pages. Inner activation conditions remained under review; the original volume would be requested tomorrow. Evelyn's index was registered as a comparison lead, not an operating manual authorizing the core to open.

The report included Oswin's application. He would personally attend the conditional review at noon on April 20, requesting progression from preparation to final execution and a physical-key handover.

Adrian laid it on the table instead of concealing it in his sleeve. Elena stood beside him and read every line.

Tonight, nobody had signed another person's life away for tomorrow. Tomorrow they must preserve that fact before a hand actually reached for the key.
