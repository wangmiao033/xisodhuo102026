# Chapter 233: When the Other End Lit Up

On April 1, the equipment attendant placed two apparently identical instruments before Adrian and asked which he wanted.

Adrian didn't choose the shinier one.

Last night's range specification remained inside his application. He asked the attendant to compare the plates and calibration records. The instrument on the right displayed long averages. It could hide exactly the brief change they'd missed before.

“The left. Subject to the assembler checking it, not my thinking it looks right.”

The attendant put the other away. Adrian hadn't become an expert. Asking two extra questions outside this door last night had helped.

Materials hadn't miraculously assembled themselves overnight. The academy had two recorded surplus insulating pads suitable for the work, released by the instructor who controlled the class stock. The custodian verified the unused spare. Another specimen came from the east-side shop at its separately checked price, covered by an actual approved materials allocation. Adrian's empty purse performed no imaginary generosity.

The suspect borrowed clamp remained under examination. Today's academy-owned set had separately checked dimensions; it wouldn't secretly replace the damaged loan. The shield passed inspection, and its owner explicitly extended the loan until tomorrow morning, subject to another check before return. Different people signed the blanks belonging to them. The date wasn't permission by itself; neither was the fact that yesterday had finally produced a conditional decision. Someone had actually checked each missing item this morning. Adrian carried the completed sheet carefully, aware of all the work concealed by those small signatures. He could deliver the application. He couldn't sign another person's inspection for them.

Before noon, the delivery boy brought the new specimen packed in wood shavings. A note on the box explained whom to contact about a dimensional discrepancy. Adrian recognized the repair-shop apprentice's handwriting. He had guided the delivery, not pretended another shop's work belonged to his master.

Could he stay and watch? The administrator explained that the operating observers had already received their briefing. Delivering material didn't mean knowing the room's risks. The boy could wait outside.

He frowned, picked up his box, and asked to hear if it worked.

Adrian nearly promised. Noticing a small splinter mark on the boy's hand, he asked where a result should go—and whether he wanted a failed result too.

“To the shop. We can read it over supper. Not like a proclamation. I don't know all those words.”

“I'll make it clear.”

“Not as though I know nothing.”

Adrian agreed. Satisfied, the boy swept up spilled shavings. His little broom knocked twice against the threshold. The ordinary noise steadied Adrian. People outside this room waited for news, then still had wooden edges to smooth.

He noted the request without adding a delivery boy to a list of supporters. Staff retained the chipped label with the specimen's acceptance record. Knowing the messenger wasn't its entire provenance.

Their brief slot came after lunch. Luna found Adrian studying his timing board.

“Trying to wear it thin by looking?”

“It matters less today.”

“Congratulations to the board.”

He smiled. Separate professionals continuously watched the local indicators. They didn't require his voice to notice a change. The independent safety officer hadn't designed the model. Required cutoff checks after complete assembly were recorded; those limited checks didn't connect the transfer route or smuggle in a preliminary simulation. The safety reviewer signed today's operating permission only after checking them.

Luna sat with her right palm cushioned and left hand resting. Her assistant opened the revised drawing. She could explain the design, not operate the supply or sacrifice rest to catch a permit window.

Isabella initially occupied the best view of A. When an independent observer arrived, tilted his head, and stepped back, she yielded the position.

“You can't see the fine markings from there, Your Highness.”

“He needs to see. I need to hear.”

She didn't enjoy sitting behind everyone. Rather than pretend otherwise, she asked to inspect the result afterward under the same rules. This wasn't a symbolic chair she surrendered. It was a useful view.

The previous works witness had his own job today. Another adult, separately briefed and freely agreeing to this operation, took the designated place without inheriting his judgments. The records-only witness would read later. Viola checked the doorway and left to rest. Cecilia's shift ended at dusk. Nobody completed the five-woman attendance list for appearances.

The operator read back total and local limits and maximum duration. One approved small transfer only: no staircase of increasing loads, no second run because the first looked good. The apparatus remained disconnected from bodies, marks, ancient energy, the core, and active public protection. It carried a preset, limited thermal burden in new inert specimens.

Adrian felt a ridiculous flicker of disappointment. He wasn't trying to save a piece of ceramic.

Seeing his glance, Luna spoke softly. “Don't make it become a person for us.”

He nodded and returned to his position. His scar stayed covered beneath his sleeve. It supplied neither the energy nor a reassuring answer about what the instruments would show. The room contained enough people hoping for an answer without asking his body to become another unapproved part of the apparatus.

A thin light filled A's groove. The local observers' calls no longer waited neatly for his timing. B stayed low while the connection awaited confirmation. The old missing beat seemed to return inside his chest. His body remembered the cracking sound before his mind did.

“A within limit.”

“B awaiting confirmation.”

Adrian wanted to hurry the next words. He announced only his assigned time.

Confirmation came. The operator made the approved change. A's local reading eased downward as B's rose. The connection observer reported without waiting for the main gauge: below the prescribed line. The little preset burden no longer remained entirely at A.

B rose slightly faster than the paper estimate.

Adrian inhaled. The safety officer requested the value; the observer repeated it and the remaining margin. Both value and rate remained within the prior stopping criteria. Nobody extended the duration to celebrate. Nobody prohibited a question to prove courage.

At the maximum time, supply stopped. The independent cutoff was confirmed. Residual energy passed through its designated isolated termination, not back into A. The window darkened. The pendulum moved again.

No crack.

Adrian loosened his fingers. “I've recorded termination here. Please check the other records.”

Isabella briefly closed her eyes, then opened them. She didn't start applause. People were still writing; the shield hadn't been cleared for opening. Happiness could leave them room to finish.

Once handling temperatures were safe, examiners opened the shield. The specimens and pads retained their shapes, with no cracks or scorching found in this inspection. Supply, receiving, and isolated-termination readings reconciled within the instruments' stated uncertainty. The recorder separated the effective transfer interval from preparation rather than counting everything as sustained success.

The drop at one end, rise at the other, connection readings, and termination record together supported limited sharing. One side dimming alone wouldn't prove a burden had disappeared. Luna kept the faster-than-estimated rise at B in the record.

“Did it work?” someone asked from the back.

She let the operator answer first for his work. The apparatus completed its single permitted transfer without a stopping condition or the specified damage detected afterward. Then she gave the interpretation: this configuration had redistributed a little simulated burden at this quantity and duration. It established neither human safety nor a replacement for the Crown of Destiny.

Adrian smiled anyway. He'd thought qualifications would shrink success out of existence. Instead they gave it a size he could actually hold.

“Still borrowing every adjective?” Luna asked.

“One for now. Good.”

“For now, I agree.”

Her assistant signaled departure. She didn't bargain for another page. Technicians would continue unpowered examination; her part ended with her dictated account. Adrian handed the travel book to her assistant and promised not to demand technical appendices tonight.

Its hero had a dangerous sense of direction, she pointed out. That wasn't an entirely risk-free arrangement either.

Adrian didn't follow her out and leave his own work for someone else. The result would still exist when she had rested, and the remaining staff shouldn't have to reconstruct his page because he preferred the pleasure of bringing good news. He stayed to check his timings. Water had blurred one entry. He marked that and compared the independent time record rather than producing an immaculate original in which he'd never been nervous.

At dusk Cecilia arrived for the authorized result. She washed flour from her hands before accepting it. Hearing success, she smiled openly, then asked whether anyone had been counted beyond the participation they'd agreed to. Adrian showed the attendance periods without treating her question as an attack on celebration.

“May I be pleased?” she asked.

“You don't need permission.”

“Good. I already was. Nearly an unauthorized pleasure.”

He laughed. His shoulders finally dropped. She'd brought ordinary little cakes for those present, not an announcement canceling old accounts. A good result didn't turn a late return into a voluntary loan. Nor did today's intact specimens repair the one cracked in the first run. The separate accounts still described different objects, different work, and different obligations. Clamp assessment and compensation remained unresolved. The administrator left those records where they belonged before everyone ate in the next room.

Elena came late, carrying the street's chill on her coat. She hadn't seen the operation; she'd read the signed result. Would he like a walk?

“Yes. Not so you can comfort me today.”

“May I also avoid being the prize?”

He paused. Success had slipped a little expectation into his voice, as though a satisfying evening should follow automatically. Of course, he said. He wanted to walk with her. She offered her hand, leaving him to take it.

They stopped at a cobbler's lit window, watching a small boot's sole being sewn back on. Adrian admitted that for a moment he'd wanted to ask whether the next attempt could double the load, before this result was even examined.

“Did you?”

“I wrote it as a question for discussion. Not an arrangement.”

She squeezed his hand. “Tonight needn't be doubled.”

He watched the cobbler draw the thread tight. An ordinary repair continued behind an ordinary window. He didn't have to carry tomorrow's proposed experiment into every quiet minute they had agreed to spend together.

He agreed, smiling. At home, a technical note waited with the porter: the effective quantity was recorded, but its allowable wider range remained unknown. Tomorrow they should examine the capacity boundary. A successful test value wasn't a maximum.

Adrian put it with the morning papers instead of turning their evening into another meeting. As they entered the dining room, a distant bell sounded beyond the window. The sound was unchanged from yesterday. Today they had one thing worth telling people without making it larger than it was.
