# Why Do All the Villainesses Want to Marry Me?
## Volume One: A Betrothal Written in Blood
### Chapter 14: Hands Off the Archives

On September twenty-fourth, Adrian learned a valuable rule of aristocratic life.

When you wanted access to a locked room, you didn't start by picking the lock.

You filled out three forms.

When Hale slid the third form across the desk, Adrian couldn't help asking, "Couldn't all this fit on one sheet?"

"The first establishes your identity, the second states your research purpose, and the third acknowledges that permission to read something doesn't give you permission to take it away."

"Then we can skip the third. Lady Elena tells me that every day."

The corner of Hale's mouth twitched.

He made Adrian sign it anyway.

Access to the restricted archives at Saint Rose Academy had finally been approved. The permit covered only the old transfer records handled by Augustus Wynn and their corresponding loan slips. All original documents had to be viewed under the supervision of a researcher and the chief archivist.

Adrian put the permit in his pocket and examined his left hand.

Since the burning pain on September twentieth, the crescent scar had been quiet.

Ordinary.

That didn't make it feel any less strange.

"Investigating your hand again?" Elena asked from the doorway.

"I'm beginning to wonder whether it visited the academy before I did."

"You haven't proved anything yet."

"I know."

She crossed the room and tugged his cuff down far enough to conceal the scar.

"Then stop passing sentence on yourself."

Adrian remembered what she'd said under the awning yesterday.

*Neither of us needs to get wet.*

"You're getting better at comforting people, my lady."

"I'm trying to prevent you from clutching your hand and shouting about past lives the moment we find an old document."

"I'm not that dramatic."

"I should hope not."

Nora Bell was waiting outside the west wing with a stack of cloth document sleeves in her arms.

"Welcome back. Your permit came through faster than I expected."

"Because three of us signed our names nine times between us," Adrian said.

Nora laughed.

"Get used to it. This isn't even the most difficult collection."

Elena walked beside him.

"Is the chief archivist here, Miss Bell?"

"He's waiting in the restricted section. But you should know something first. The academy's inspection office is reviewing Wynn's old accident. This morning it requested temporary access to some of the sealed catalogues."

"At whose request?" Elena asked.

"The academy administration forwarded it under the heading of missing-property reconciliation. I haven't found the authorizing officer yet."

Adrian thought of the approving seal hidden beneath ink on the printers' guild paperwork.

Another official request with a blank space where a person ought to be.

"Could they remove the records we're looking at today?"

"Not without the chief archivist approving the transfer. But you should document whatever your permit allows before anything changes."

The chief archivist, Mr. Fenn, was a white-haired old man who wore leather finger guards when he handled fragile paper.

He placed a brass key on the reception counter.

"You're not here on an adventure."

Adrian nodded at once.

"Understood."

"Some of those documents are older than your grandfathers. You will not turn original pages or touch sealing wax without permission."

"Understood."

"Do not move bookmarks. Do not press your fingers over faded lettering."

"Very much understood."

Fenn studied him.

"That degree of cooperation makes me more suspicious, not less."

"Don't worry," Elena said. "I'll keep an eye on him."

Adrian was beginning to feel like the most dangerous historical artifact in the building.

They passed through a heavy oak door into the restricted section.

The room had no open windows. Lamps with protective shades illuminated the reading tables. The aisles were barely wide enough for one person, and the air carried the faint medicinal scent of dried herbs used to keep insects out.

Nora set yesterday's amendment register on a padded support.

"Wynn handled the transfer of Volume Seven. After his death, the academy never completed the process. Under the old rules, any unfinished transfer should have been returned to the original archive."

She indicated a reference number.

"And we have that number here."

Adrian leaned closer.

The heading read *Old-Style Loans and Transfers — Supplementary Register*.

The reference was two digits shorter than the Judicial Office index.

"Two digits are missing."

"The final two identify the department handling the case. The supplementary register uses the common number without the department suffix."

Elena placed their verified transcription alongside it.

"So it belongs to the same set of records?"

"The numbering comes from the same source," Nora said. "Whether we're dealing with the same original file depends on the contents."

At Fenn's instruction, she brought out a flat wooden case.

There were no mysterious glowing sigils inside.

Only several thin pages and a little gray book whose cover had lost most of its color.

Adrian found that oddly reassuring.

At least paper still yellowed with age in this world.

Ink still faded.

Nora opened the transfer register first.

"Augustus Wynn's last recorded transfer was two days before his accident."

"So he didn't come back afterward?"

"Not according to the attendance book. But the accident occurred inside the closed west clock tower. We still don't have an adequate explanation for that."

Elena opened her notes.

"We're not determining how he died today. First, let's establish what records he consulted before the incident."

Nora nodded.

Fenn was just reaching for the supplementary loan register when someone knocked sharply at the door.

"Mr. Fenn? The inspection officers have arrived."

An assistant spoke quietly from the hallway.

"They want to examine Wynn's surviving papers and remove the old loan records marked 'no longer required for teaching.'"

Fenn frowned.

"Who signed the transfer order?"

"They have a letter bearing the administration's seal. No authorization from you."

"Then let them wait in the visitors' room. Without a proper inventory and independent witnesses, I won't release a single page."

Nora's face changed.

Adrian looked at her.

"That supplementary register is on their list, isn't it?"

"I'm afraid so."

Fenn turned toward Elena.

"You'll need to pause your examination. When custody is disputed, the material has to be inventoried before either party can continue."

Elena put down her pen.

"Of course."

Adrian was less pleased.

The gray register was right there.

Yesterday he'd found the word *Substitute*. Today someone wanted to carry off the book that might explain it.

Would there be another convenient warehouse fire tomorrow?

He took half a step toward the case.

Elena caught his sleeve.

"Don't."

"I only wanted to see the cover."

"I know precisely what you were thinking. Come here."

She led him away from the table to a narrow aisle along the wall.

Adrian expected to be scolded where no one could hear.

Instead, she leaned closer and whispered, "Tell me the number you saw. We don't have to steal the book to establish that it was here."

They stood shoulder to shoulder between the shelves.

There was barely room for Adrian to move his foot.

"The first four digits were seven-two-one-nine," Elena murmured. "And the supplement?"

"Volume Seven. Loans, Group Three."

"Good. Now stop staring at the case."

"I'm worried they'll take it away."

"Mr. Fenn has cared for these records for forty years. He doesn't need a newly appointed aide telling him how to protect them."

From beyond the door came a man's voice.

"Mr. Fenn, the request is stamped. Why can't we transfer it now?"

"Because you don't have an inventory or independent witnesses. Come back tomorrow."

"This is an urgent administrative instruction."

"Urgent doesn't mean anonymous."

Adrian lowered his voice.

"Do you think they came specifically for this register?"

"Possibly. Or it might be a genuine inspection. We can't assume everyone following procedure is our enemy."

"I'm afraid they might be better at using procedure against us."

Elena didn't mock him.

She loosened her grip on his sleeve.

An assistant passed them with an empty document trolley, hardly noticing they were there.

Adrian instinctively drew back.

"Should we hide?"

"We're here legally. Why would we hide?"

Elena tried to pass him, but the shelf left less than half a step between his shoulder and the wall.

"Move left."

"There's a wall on my left."

"Then lower your head."

He did.

Instead of passing him, Elena came closer.

One hand rested against the shelf. The other reached past his shoulder.

She was so near he could see the faint shadow of her eyelashes.

Adrian's heart slammed against his ribs.

He remembered the ballroom.

The accidental kiss.

"My lady..."

"Don't move."

Her voice was barely above a whisper.

He held his breath.

Was she really about to—?

Metal clicked behind him.

Elena drew a brass key from a hook at his shoulder.

"Got it."

Adrian stared.

"You were reaching for a key?"

"What else?"

"I thought you were trying to pass."

"Did you?"

"Of course."

"Then why are your ears red?"

"It's stuffy in here."

"This room is temperature-controlled. It's cooler than the corridor."

"...I must be overdressed."

Elena finally laughed.

"Don't bring last night's tabloid stories into the archive."

"You're the one who came so close!"

"Because you were blocking the key."

Adrian made a solemn decision never to have anything to do with brass keys again.

It opened a numbered drawer in the side of the shelf. Fenn had instructed Nora to hang the spare nearby for authorized staff.

Elena didn't open the drawer herself.

She returned the key to Fenn.

The chief archivist had already refused the inspection office's immediate transfer request. The visitors could leave their application and return for a formal inventory the following day.

"Apologies for the interruption," Fenn told them. "Nothing is being moved today. Your permit remains valid. You may continue."

Nora reopened the gray supplementary register under his supervision.

At last Adrian could see the full loan page.

Three entries, written in different hands.

The first two belonged to Augustus Wynn.

The third was unfamiliar.

**Date: September, seven years earlier.**

**Material: Cross-Reference to Old Judicial Indexes.**

**Borrower: Adrian Reed.**

A dull buzzing filled Adrian's ears.

Nora pointed to the line.

"Same name as the old court index."

"Could someone have added it recently?" Elena asked.

Fenn moved the page beneath the lamp.

"The ink has penetrated the paper fibers, so it's unlikely to have been written in the last few days. But its age and authenticity require examination. I won't certify a date by looking at it."

Adrian stared at the signature.

The last letter ended in an unnecessary inward curl.

Nora misread his silence.

"Would you like me to copy it for you?"

"No."

Adrian removed the academy application he'd signed yesterday.

His name appeared on the bottom.

The last letter ended in almost the same little curl.

He covered half the older signature with his thumb.

The resemblance remained.

"This isn't proof," he said quietly. "It's only a feeling."

Elena didn't finish the argument for him.

"Then let's request proper analysis."

Fenn instructed Nora to prepare an authorized tracing. Two staff members witnessed that the page was unchanged and resealed it afterward. Any photograph of the original would require additional approval before leaving the academy.

For once, Adrian was grateful for the tedious paperwork.

When the book was finally closed, he realized he'd been clenching his hands.

Nora handed him a stamped reading receipt.

"Not a complete disaster, then."

"If someone signed my name here seven years before I arrived, I'd certainly call that interesting."

Elena looked up sharply.

"Before you arrived?"

He realized he'd said too much.

She didn't press him.

Instead, she passed him the receipt.

"First, let's find out who made that signature."

They emerged into the afternoon sunlight.

Adrian glanced at the crescent scar on his left hand.

He was no longer sure what connection—if any—existed between him and the man he'd seen die in the game.

They were approaching their carriage when Nora hurried after them.

"Wait! One more thing."

She carried a publicly accessible catalogue summary.

"That loan record was later cross-referenced with the old portrait collection. Apparently an image of the person concerned was retained for identification."

"Where is it?" Elena asked.

"In the academy's old portrait room. We can't get permission until tomorrow."

Adrian accepted the sheet.

Someone had borrowed a judicial record under his name seven years ago.

Apparently, there was even a portrait.

He turned to Elena.

She didn't tell him not to be afraid.

She simply said, "I'll go with you tomorrow."
